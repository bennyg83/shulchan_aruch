Created, etc. kosher. Rambam wrote in chapter 8 of Hilchot Shechitah law 25: created with one kulya — kosher; and I wonder, for in Bechorot 39 a Baraisa teaches: "and the two kidneys" — not one who has one kulya and not one who has three — to disqualify for the altar; and another Baraisa teaches: "you shall remove them" — to include one who has one kulya — permitted; and the Gemara challenges them and resolves: they disagree whether a creature is created with one; one Sage holds there is a creature created with one, and one holds there is not; and certainly it is deficient; and the one who permits holds there is a creature with one created with one and there is no deficiency and kosher; and R' Yochanan said: all agree there is no creature with one, only here it lacked before slaughter, etc.; in any case we hear from this Baraisa "two kidneys" and not one with one kulya or three — there is no creature with one; and Rambam in chapter 2 of Hilchot Issurei Mizbeach copied this Baraisa, and so Rif brought it; therefore he must hold there is no creature with one; how did he write in his book of Shechitah that created with one kulya is kosher — it does not exist in reality; requires study at first glance. And what one may say: Rambam and poskim explain opposite Rashi's explanation of this Baraisa "and the two kidneys" — it appears to him there is a creature with one; therefore the Torah specified two kidneys, certainly excluding one and three, and two specifically even if created lacking, for the similarity of three is as explained there in the Gemara that we require one similar to three; if so, how can the other Baraisa say "you shall remove them" includes one with one kulya — behold in that verse "two kidneys" was excluded; why need a verse to include deficiency afterward — all the more so when created lacking it is disqualified, all the more so when removed; and it is simple; therefore "you shall remove them" is for another exposition; but the other Baraisa holds there is no creature with one; if so, why "two kidneys" to exclude one, for it does not exist in reality; and for deficiency there is no need, for we require similarity to three; and see Rashi there entry "Mar disqualified," etc., what Rashi wrote in name of his teacher and Rashi strained in the text — and for us it is unnecessary. And therefore "two kidneys" does not come to exclude one; only "one who has three"; though not in the plain meaning of the language, nevertheless it was strained and established; and if so we expound "you shall remove them" to include one with one kulya even if lacking, for there is no internal deficiency; and one may say: since we hold the Baraisa "two kidneys," etc., as Rambam wrote, we also hold there is a creature with one. And Pri Chadash end of siman 41 challenged: "every excess is considered as removed" is only where there is examination; why is one with three kidneys disqualified for the Most High, for there is no deficiency here until the name of imperfection applies; and the resolution: Rashba and supporters as stated there — reason disqualified due to perfect; but in conclusion we expound "two kidneys," etc., to exclude one created without a kulya — not from reason of deficiency, for in the sugya one created without a kulya is not called deficient, only by scriptural decree; and likewise for one with three — by scriptural decree, not from deficiency; and it is correct.
