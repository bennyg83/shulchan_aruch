Baer Heitev; and in Machatzit HaShekel he elaborated and raised: puncture by needle even without deficiency, if it reached the loven and the flesh is not healthy — be stringent even without double doubt; and if it reached the loven and there is no deficiency and the flesh is healthy and good and there is only the puncture — permit with double doubt if the puncture did not reach the loven and there is no deficiency and the flesh has a flaw and also the flaw is not in the loven — permit; all this when it is known the puncture was by needle. But if it cannot be clarified that it was by needle and there is no flaw in the flesh — if there is no deficiency, even if it reached the loven — permit — see there; and see Machatzit HaShekel in name of Maharash Halevi: needle found in kidneys reaching the loven, the needle recognizable as from one tree that the shepherd takes with a little iron on it like a needle; the thick end found outside the animal's skin and the thin end inside — there was great mixture in animals and in kidneys; he answered widespread law: the kulya was punctured — kosher. And one may challenge: since it passed through the esophagus, etc. — this does not exist, for it is recognizable this needle entered from the skin to the kidneys, as a shepherd of sheep strikes near the kidneys; it is recognizable its thick end is outside, etc. — see there.
