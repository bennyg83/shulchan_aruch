The loven. Shach wrote it is proven in the Talmud and several poskim that we require it reach inside the loven — see there. And even if the dissolving began from inside — treifah, since the essence of laketah is only in the place of the charitz; if so when the blow reaches the loven it is a deficiency from outside, for the loven under the loins enters inside the kulya and afterward exits from the kidneys outside and is exposed — Maharshal.
Sword. Taz wrote that a sword-blow means it split in two; and Shach disagrees and wrote that a sword-blow is different from being cut — a sword-blow is with force, namely laketah of the kulya; but cut with a calm cut — kosher.
That they reach. Shach explained: namely on the loven. And in clear water — even from the side of the loven — kosher; however this is specifically per the Mechaber's view; but many poskim hold that if laketah occurred even in one kulya in murky water or its flesh became like flesh of the dead — even if they did not reach the place of the charitz — treifah; and it implies even in a small amount of putrefaction far from the place of the charitz — treifah {Rama: (for the Gemara says laketah in the kulya is treifah, and it is when the affliction reaches until the place of the charitz — implying only in laketah but not in putrefaction and murky water; and see Taz and Pri Chadash who disagree and rule like Shulchan Aruch that putrefaction must reach the loven in it. An incident in an animal: putrefaction was found in the kulya that was rotting and they did not manage to examine whether it reached the place of the charitz before a dog ate it — Maharil, Rashdam, Maharshach, and Shai declared treifah. See Kenesset HaGedolah daf 161: many people were in the slaughterhouse; some said they saw the kulya and did not see putrefaction, and some said they saw putrefaction — there is no contradiction and treifah. Two testified they saw the kulya and there was no putrefaction, and two say there was putrefaction but they did not know if it reached the charitz — this is contradiction and two against two, and the animal stands in presumption of permission. They saw water in the kulya and smelled a rotting odor — do not hang and say perhaps the rot came from elsewhere and the water in the kulya are clear — Kenesset HaGedolah in name of Mahari Azulai, see there daf 161)} And so Maharshal and Bach; and Taz wrote that so one should rule for practical law; and Shach wrote: but certainly in a sword-blow all agree we require the affliction reach the place of the charitz, unlike Bach who forbids even in that; and Bach is further stringent in several laws in this siman that are not essential in Talmud and poskim. And Maharshal wrote: although the Gemara says full of putrefaction is treifah — one may distinguish: if the blister was not from outside but from inside, the putrefaction must reach the place of the charitz; therefore it said "full"; but where the blister protrudes outside and there is putrefaction — treifah even if it did not reach the charitz — end of his words.
