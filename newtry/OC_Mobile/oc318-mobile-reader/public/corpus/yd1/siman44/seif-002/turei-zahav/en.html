And Maharshal wrote: at first glance it appears we require the dissolving begin also from outside, for otherwise it is not worse than lung, and we do not find laketah in kulya should be worse than lung; and all the more so it is like lung that internal deficiency is not called deficiency; but it is not so, since the essence of laketah is only in the place of the charitz; if so when the blow reaches the loven of the kulya this is not internal deficiency but external, for the loven under the loins enters inside the kulya and afterward exits from the kidneys outside and is exposed; therefore it implies from this that regarding spleen we say properly it is kosher when afflicted from inside; and I already wrote from this end of siman 43.
Sword-blow. At first glance it is astonishing, for this means cut, and this litigant is Hagahot Ashiri, and it is part of cut too, and he holds treifah — for only in puncture is it kosher; and Maharshal wrote on this and it is not clear — from where do we divide between puncture and cut, for certainly wherever kosher in puncture, kosher in cut per Rosh's view; nevertheless if cut in two or split — Raavyah ruled treifah; if so Rama should have brought "there is one who says if cut — treifah"; and one must say Rama explains this sword-blow means split in two specifically, per Raavyah whom Hagahot Ashiri brings.
And it is when they reach the loven. This is Rashba's and Tur's view; and it depends on variant readings in the Gemara: Rashi reads kosher in lung, disqualifies in kulya — meaning putrefaction only; and Alfasi reads disqualifies in this and in this — laketah; and therefore laketah and putrefaction are two matters; and therefore they explained laketah means dissolving as copied here at the beginning of this seif; and it is also in the Gemara: laketah in the kulya is treifah when it reaches the loven. Now per Rashi putrefaction is laketah — automatically in putrefaction we require reaching the loven; but per Rif laketah is a different matter, and since in laketah alone we say it must reach the loven — automatically in putrefaction we do not say this, and therefore treifah even without reaching the loven; and so Beit Yosef in name of Sha'arim of R' Shmuel ben Chafni; and Maharshal wrote this is implied from Rambam's words, etc.; and so appears to be the essence, not like Rashba and Tur who wrote even in putrefaction and murky water we require reaching the place of the charitz — from where do we lenient in Torah prohibitions without proof from the Talmud — end; and so Moharach z"l; and so one should rule for practical law. And he further wrote: although we say in the Gemara full of putrefaction is treifah — apparently "full" is required and it is not treifah until reaching the place of the charitz; one may distinguish: if the blister was not from outside but from inside, the putrefaction must reach the place of the charitz; therefore it said "full"; but where the blister protrudes outside and there is putrefaction — treifah even if it did not reach the charitz; and he brings proof on this from Or Zaru'a.
That they reach the loven. Meaning: also in the loven there is this blow; and so at the beginning in dissolving, as Beit Yosef wrote in name of Orach Chaim that the city's sages agreed wherever it does not reach the loven itself — kosher; and Beit Yosef brought in name of Orach Chaim that affliction must be specifically in the place of the charitz; and I already wrote in name of Maharshal that in putrefaction we do not require the place of the charitz.
Even if they reached the loven, etc. It implies even in the loven itself — difficult from what Beit Yosef wrote in name of Hagahot Ashiri regarding a ram of Shafir'an where there was no flesh at all, only clear water, only in the place of the loven it was whole and he declared kosher — end; it implies if clear water reached the loven — treifah; and this is not so, for why be more stringent in clear water than in puncture and cut, for we proved above that kosher even in the loven itself; and here in clear water they required the entire loven be whole — behold kal vachomer: what puncture or cut that disqualifies in lung nevertheless is kosher in the loven's loven — why should clear water that is kosher in lung be forbidden in the loven even in part? Therefore it appears in that incident of Hagahot Ashiri they did not declare kosher specifically because the entire loven must be whole, but the incident was thus and not precise; rather even in part, since they were clear.
If blood was found in it, etc. Beit Yosef in name of Hagahot Ashiri in name of Bahag likewise; and he proved from the fact blood is not counted among murky and rotting; and Maharshal wrote: wonder if we should infer the other side stringently — only clear water is kosher and not blood; also it is not reasonable blood should be better than murky, for there is no greater laketah than if full of blood or a blister outside full of blood — end; and Orach Chaim wrote the reason for blood being kosher: this is not laketah for it is entirely blood — end; and accordingly Rashba's challenge is resolved why he did not count it among clear water — all the more so kosher in blood.
