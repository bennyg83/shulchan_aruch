That the loven in the kulya is laketah. And Maharshal wrote there end of siman 87: although it disqualifies only until affliction reaches the loven in the kulya, which is the place of the charitz — how is it recognizable that it was afflicted, behold it is already white? One may say: since it cannot stand upon it — treifah, for it is a Torah-level doubt — end; and Bach brings it.
And any change, etc. And Tur wrote if it is green — kosher; and so Orach Chaim end of general rule 23; and so Maharshal there, Bach; and they explained this is specifically green like a leek (called in foreign tongue "bleu," as above siman 38); and so Perishah seif 4; but in Turei Chadashim there end of law 6 he wrote: this is specifically green called "jaune" that also leans to redness; but green like a leek and such called "bleu" — treifah — end; and so in Sefer Efi Ravrevi; and requires study, for we hold beginning of siman 188 that appearance of green "jaune" does not lean to redness even for leniency, and all the more so for stringency; and likewise above siman 38, a lung that reddened is kosher and green "jaune" is treifah — behold there is a distinction between red and "jaune."
Disqualifies it. And although in all organs except lung change of appearance does not disqualify — the kulya is different, since laketah disqualifies in it and change of appearance is laketah, as stated in the Talmud chapter 1 of treifot (daf 47b), and as written in siman 38 in s.k. 8.
