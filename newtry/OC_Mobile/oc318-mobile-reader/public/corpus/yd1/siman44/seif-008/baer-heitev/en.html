That the loven. Maharshal wrote: although it disqualifies only until affliction reaches the loven in the kulya, which is the place of the charitz — how is it recognizable that it was afflicted, behold it is already white? One may say: since it cannot stand upon it — treifah, for it is a Torah-level doubt — end; and Bach brings it.
Appearance. Maharshal wrote: since change of appearance in the kulya is not from the Talmud, therefore one forbids only when it reaches the loven; and Taz disagrees and holds that change of appearance even if it does not reach the loven is treifah, for in the end it will ruin everything; and it is in Tur: if it is green — kosher; and poskim disagreed whether this is what they call in foreign tongue "bleu" or "jaune"; and Shach wrote: in siman 188 we hold that appearance of "jaune" does not lean to redness even for leniency, and all the more so for stringency; and likewise above siman 38, a lung that reddened — behold there is a distinction between red and "jaune"; and so Taz: one may not permit appearance of "jaune," and in appearance of "gris" one may permit — see there {Rama: (Pri Chadash wrote: on the loven in the kulya there is a thin membrane above, and sometimes that membrane is found black leaning to greenish, and beneath that membrane they extract the loven white like other kidneys — kosher — see there. Turei Chadashim wrote: appearance of "jaune" is kosher for it leans to redness, and "gris" and "bleu" are treifah — end. And Taz and Shach disagreed and ruled that appearance of "jaune" is treifah and appearance of "bleu" is kosher; and Pri Chadash declares treifah in every case — see there)}.
