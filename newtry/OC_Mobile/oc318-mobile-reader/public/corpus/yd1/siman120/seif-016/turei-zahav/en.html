He gives it to a non-Jew as a gift, etc. It appears to me this remedy is only temporary — for that Shabbat, or on a weekday while he has no mikveh; but after Shabbat or when a mikveh becomes available, since it is permanently settled in Jewish hands, it is certainly like purchased in his hand and no better than a non-Jew's pledge in a Jew's hand with intent to sink — tevilah is required. And furthermore regarding a borrowed tallit we find it is exempt from tzitzit for thirty days and afterward obligated, for it appears his own — so here too it appears tevilah is required after he can immerse, even with blessing; but since this is not explicit in poskim, one should be careful to immerse another vessel with it because of concern for a doubtful blessing:
