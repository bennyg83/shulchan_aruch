And only, etc. See above siman 202 seif 6 in the gloss: be careful, etc.; and likewise, etc.; and Mordechai likewise at the end of Avodah Zarah: when one places glass full of water in a bucket full of water and sinks in a pit, etc.; and as written in chapter 3 of Chagigah (21a) that the vessel's weight is a chatzitzah; he challenged there from what is written in chapter 7 of Mikvaot: he immersed a bed in it, etc.; and he resolved: here the water in the bucket is detached, therefore it does not help; and he wrote: one should also be careful from what is written there, one who holds, etc., as above; and this is why he wrote "and only," etc.; and the rabbi's words require study, for even when moistening in mikveh water they become detached on the hand; and Mordechai's words are thus: there mikveh water preceded under the bed, unlike here where detached preceded and through pouring into the mikveh it purifies, as Rosh wrote there and below siman 198 paragraph 25; and if so they must be loose so mikveh water will reach them, therefore it does not help; and likewise here the place of the vessel he holds must be in mikveh water first and afterward he holds there; but the mishnah's language "and if he rinsed his hand," etc., does not imply thus; and one must say for his words that there he deals with that he is not pressing and holding with force, only he need not loosen — all this is forced, for the mishnah states plainly; and Mordechai's main law requires study — whence do we distinguish between detached and attached; and chapter 3 of Chagigah where there is no water at all in the outer vessel — and the essence is there is no distinction, as the mishnah states here "and if he rinsed" — detached they are, as above; and likewise in the gloss in siman 202 there he did not mention water in the outer vessel; and even with force it helps from what sinks, as above, and as written in siman 198 seif 31:
