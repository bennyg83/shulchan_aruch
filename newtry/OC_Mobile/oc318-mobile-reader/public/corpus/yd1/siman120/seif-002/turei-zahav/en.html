And specifically if he moistened his hand in mikveh water, etc. The rabbi left room here to defend himself, for his honor is in its place — he did not analyze this gloss at all, although they follow Mordechai at the end of Avodah Zarah, as we will quote shortly in the name of Darkei Moshe; nevertheless one must understand Mordechai's words and explain them as we will set forth, with God's help. First we will mention the essence of this matter that Tur and Shulchan Aruch mentioned — that rinsing one's hand in water helps — and this is what we teach in the mishnah, chapter 8 of Mikvaot: one who holds a person and vessels and immerses them — they are impure; and if he rinsed his hands in pure water, R. Shimon says he should loosen his hands so water enters them. And Rashi explained: he rinsed his hand before holding — pure, because moisture on his hand connects to mikveh water and there is no chatzitzah here. And likewise Tur brought in siman 197, and likewise at the end of Tur HaAroch. This is according to what we teach in chapter 6 of Mikvaot: one draws and moistens even a hair's breadth — meaning there one wishes to purify invalid water from valid mikveh water, and by moistening them a hair's breadth all invalid water is purified; and this halachah is widespread that moisture of a fingerbreadth to moisten helps to connect to a valid mikveh, as stated in chapter 2 of Gittin (daf 16). This is the intent of the mishnah we mentioned, that one who holds a person or vessel should rinse his hands in water — meaning before holding the vessel he should moisten his hands, even in detached water, and through their connection to the mikveh the water on his hand becomes actual mikveh water through hashakah; therefore the tanna and later poskim used the language of rinsing generally, like all rinsings that are in detached water — not like Rama, who explained Tur's words specifically in mikveh water; rather detached water also helps through hashakah. And what Darkei Moshe wrote in the name of Mordechai at the end of Avodah Zarah, that specifically mikveh water is required — this is Mordechai's language there: he also taught that one should be careful to immerse a glass vessel full of water and place it in a bucket full of water and sink the bucket in a pit, not to set it on its rim lest the weight of the vessel be a chatzitzah, but tilt it on its side and roll it in the bucket in the pit; and one asked: what is different from a person's foot spread on the ground in a mikveh, where there is no chatzitzah, because we say the water precedes the place before he puts his foot on the ground, as we teach in chapter 7 of Mikvaot: they immersed a bed in it even though its legs sink in thick mud — valid, because the water precedes. One might say: only in a complete mikveh where they are attached; but when one puts glass in a bucket, even full of water, it does not help because they are detached; and one should also be careful from what we teach in chapter 8 of Mikvaot: one who holds a person and vessels, etc. — end of Mordechai. From this Rama learned to explain here that the rinsing Tur mentioned is in mikveh water. In truth this is another matter: Mordechai deals with the weight of the vessel that interrupts the mikveh when it lies, and it is not applicable to say the water under the vessel is purified by hashakah from mikveh water unless there were already valid mikveh water under the vessel; then even though the vessel's weight interrupts so one might say those waters were completely cut off from the mikveh, nevertheless they remained connected and have connection on every side to remain in their validity — unlike detached water in a vessel, which you must purify through hashakah and create purity from the outset; for this a good connection is required, and since the vessel has weight it is not called a good connection. But in truth one who immerses a vessel in his hand in a mikveh does not need mikveh water on the hand; since there is no weight of a vessel, hashakah to detached water already on his hand with mikveh water is fine; nevertheless the person immersing must not grip the vessel forcefully, for then it is like the vessel's weight we mentioned — rather moderate contact, as they said regarding a niddah immersing in several places regarding her eyes and lips. This is Mordechai's intent: he also wrote one should be careful from what the mishnah says, one who holds, etc. — he asked: does the mishnah come to inform us? Rather it teaches that one must be careful not to grip too forcefully; for although the first tanna argues with R. Shimon, who holds he must loosen his hands, nevertheless moderate contact is required even for the first tanna, not with force — this is simple. And I saw in Mishmeres HaBayis of Rashba daf 190: we rule like the first tanna that he need not loosen his hands, only that he rinsed his hands first and does not press hard — tevilah is valid. And it appears to me Tur holds that if he grips moderately in his hand it is permitted even l'chatchila, and we do not decree lest he grip with great force and mikveh water connection not help; and this is why he wrote "however if he moistened, etc.," there is no concern — he asked: why "there is no concern" — he should have said permitted or it is fine; rather it teaches there is no concern lest he grip too hard. And Rosh is even more lenient, for he does not hold the vessel's weight interrupts and nullifies the connection of detached water on his hands with mikveh water; for he wrote in Hilchot Mikvaot: because the water precedes and touched the bed's legs before touching mud, and those waters are connected to the mikveh — therefore there is no chatzitzah, as we teach in chapter 8 of Mikvaot; and for this reason one need not lift his legs during immersion because the water precedes — end of Rosh. Behold he equated this law to rinsing hands, where the rinse-water is detached; nevertheless since Mordechai distinguishes between vessel weight or not, one should be stringent; therefore one who wishes to immerse a vessel should moisten his hands even in detached water, hold the vessel with moderate contact, and immerse it. One who wishes to immerse a vessel full of detached water in a bucket should draw water from the mikveh and place it with the vessel in water; then he must turn it on its side, for this is like a person gripping a vessel forcefully and immersing it — the person holding is voluntary and can hold without great force, unlike a full heavy vessel — unless the bucket is sunk in water and he lowers the vessel into it; then we are not concerned for vessel weight, since there is already valid water inside, as we said. And Levush writes that inside the water he passes it hand to hand; although that is a good method, for in that way there is no prohibition even if he grips hard, as Issur VeHeter wrote at the end of general rule 58 law 96 — nevertheless this is not the mishnah's method, which stated generally that he rinses his hands in water first; and Tur and Shulchan Aruch are not explained at all in that method, but in the method we said — and even detached water suffices, since there is no vessel weight and no gripping with force, tevilah is proper even l'chatchila; and even according to Mordechai we mentioned, who holds vessel weight is a chatzitzah, even if hands were moistened first in detached water, there is no concern here at all lest he grip with force, for it is a fixed halachah that we are not concerned for this even for niddah impurity, as will be explained in siman 198 seif 28, with Heaven's help:
