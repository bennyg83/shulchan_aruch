Eating vessels. Chochmas Adam wrote general rule 73 law 13: the large cauldrons in which they brew beer — it appears to me they require tevilat kelim; and even though they fix them and attach them to the ground, nevertheless their law is like a vessel and they have no remedy except that one immerse them or pierce in them a large hole that nullifies them from the category of vessel, and afterward a Jewish craftsman repairs them. But according to what I heard, that the beer they brew in them is not fit for drinking at all until they filter it, it is possible that on this basis the world relies that they do not immerse them; nevertheless requires study, for they are no worse than a slaughter knife below seif 5, since it is fit to use for another food — see in his book Binas Adam more on this:
Glass. See She'eilat Yaavetz vol. 1 siman 67, who wrote that vessels coming from overseas countries called porcelain and similar to glass vessels do not require tevilah, since it is known that their making is from sand and earth, and metal vessels are spoken of in the parashah; and even though they are thinner, we have no concern for this, for glass vessels are thinner than they, and if not for the fact that when they break they can be repaired by melting — for this reason they equated them to metal vessels — they would require tevilah, and these do not have that remedy — see there:
To immerse them. Chochmas Adam wrote there law 15: one must insert the entire vessel in water at one time so that none of it remains outside the water, even any amount; and if it is large and one cannot immerse it all at once, it appears to me it is forbidden to immerse in halves, similar to immersion of a niddah, and it is not comparable to hag'alah, which is to expel the prohibition — and once he hag'alah part, he has expelled — but here it is because of impurity, and since part of it remains outside the water, impurity returns and spreads through the entire vessel — end of his words:
In a mikveh or in water. Chochmas Adam wrote there law 19: it appears to me that in a time of pressing need it is permitted to immerse a glass vessel in snow if it is in one place (see below siman 201 seif 30), for its tevilah is certainly d'rabbanan; but not metal vessels, for according to many poskim it is d'oraisa — see there.
