Eating vessels. For it is written: every item that comes into fire; and there is no way to use fire except for needs of eating. Of metal, for it is written: only the gold, etc., and all mentioned in the verse is called metal. Glass too has the law of metal, since if it breaks it can be repaired by melting. And the reason for tevilah is so that they emerge from the impurity of the non-Jew to the sanctity of Israel:
Or a spring of forty se'ah. Beit Yosef challenged what Tur wrote — it is not clear, etc., for regarding impurity too he ruled in siman 201 that we require forty se'ah; and Darkei Moshe resolved that Tur challenges according to the view of Sefer HaMitzvos, who wrote: even though a spring, etc. (end of his words), that according to him it suffices with less than forty se'ah for impurity, but he is stringent in this tevilah over that — our rabbi wondered why the difference. And it is not pleasing to me from Tur's wording, also from the law, for certainly Semak too holds that for a woman it does not suffice in a spring less than forty se'ah, but for an impure vessel it suffices with ten. And it appears to me the explanation of Tur's words is thus: this tevilah of vessels of a non-Jew we derive in the last chapter of Avodah Zarah from what is said "and it shall be pure"; and in truth there is no implication of "and it shall be pure" that it be through water — let us say through something else. Also what they said there that we derive from "only in the waters of a niddah" — there is no resolution in this, for the plain meaning of the verse refers to waters of sin-offering, as Rashi explained in the Chumash; rather we must interpret this "and it shall be pure," which is through water, since it is written regarding impure vessels "in water it shall be brought and it shall be pure" — from this we learn: in what do we find that this "and it shall be pure" regarding vessels of a non-Jew also refers to water? And after it is clarified that "and it shall be pure" refers to water, we interpret "waters of a niddah" as waters in which a niddah immerses, and it teaches us that it does not suffice with less than forty se'ah like a niddah, as stated in the Gemara. And in Tur we read "more than tevilah of impurity," and he means impure vessels; and this is what he says: since we do not know that purification is in water for vessels of a non-Jew except from tevilah of impure vessels, we do not be more stringent than them regarding a spring, except in what the Torah revealed regarding a mikveh, that it should be like a niddah:
