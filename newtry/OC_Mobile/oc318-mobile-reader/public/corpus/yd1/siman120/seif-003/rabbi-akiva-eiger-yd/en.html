(There in the gloss) And so is the practice. In Keneset HaGedolah (glosses to Beit Yosef letter 18) he wrote: one should investigate our large earthenware barrels coated with lead in which we place wine — in which mikveh can one immerse them, for certainly the mikveh water cannot contain them because of their size; and I found in responsum of Keneset Yisrael to the sage Mahari Za'avi that there is no remedy except through artifice — to give them to a non-Jew as a gift and borrow them back; and the sage Mahari Milada wrote on this that it appears to him that immersion is not required, for only eating vessels require immersion, and these barrels are only for placing wine for preservation; and his teacher agreed with him. But in Ohr U'Me'ir I found that large metal vessels in which wine is stored require immersion; and perhaps one may say that Ohr U'Me'ir meant only metal vessels, for everyone requires immersion, but for earthenware coated with lead, where there is dispute whether immersion is required — for these large barrels one may rely on this to store wine without immersion; and accordingly, those coated with lead inside and outside, which are like pure metal, require immersion — end of his words.
