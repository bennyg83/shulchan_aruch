And if not, etc. Meaning he does not know whether the non-Jew intends to sink it in his possession or not; but if he knows clearly the non-Jew does not intend to sink it, it is obvious no tevilah is required — all this I proved in my book from Tosafot and Tur and Maharil's laws of Pesach, who wrote a pledge requires tevilah only when decided in Jewish hands; and in responsum siman 60 he wrote we are in doubt in the Gemara and uncertain, and tevilah is required out of doubt — unlike Bach — see there; however in any case where the non-Jew does not intend to sink it, Maharil wrote one may not use it without the non-Jew's permission because of deceit, forbidden even regarding a non-Jew's property; we do not practice thus, etc.; and it appears he holds there is no deceit of the non-Jew in using it:
And if in the end it sank, etc. Thus it should read. And Issur VeHeter wrote there law 88; nevertheless, if he added money to the non-Jew and bought the pledge from him after they immersed it out of doubt, he requires tevilah again with a blessing, for they did not immerse initially with this intent — end of his words:
