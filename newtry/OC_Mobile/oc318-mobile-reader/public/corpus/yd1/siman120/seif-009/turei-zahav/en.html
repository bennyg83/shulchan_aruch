In the Gemara we say: Mar bar Rav Ashi said: Father pledged a silver chair from a non-Jew, immersed it, and drank from it; and we do not know whether because he holds a pledge is like a sale, or because it appears the non-Jew intends to sink it; therefore poskim ruled: if we clearly see he intends to sink it, tevilah is required; and if it were not a doubt, it implies even if certainly he does not intend to sink it, there is doubt whether tevilah is required from pledge-as-sale; only Tur wrote: if one does not know whether he intends to sink it, we are in doubt, etc. — implying if certainly he does not intend to sink it, certainly no tevilah. And Beit Yosef writes: where he does not intend to sink it, we are in doubt, etc. — such is primary; and it appears Tur teaches a leniency that even whenever it is not known for certain that he intends to sink it, no tevilah is required, and all the more so when certainly he does not intend; and that he did not teach the reverse leniency that even when he does not intend to sink one may immerse without blessing — this is only a general stringency. And Maharshal wrote: when he does not intend to sink it, a Jew is forbidden to use it because of deceiving the non-Jew; he brought proof from Maharil's laws of Pesach, who wrote it is not proper to use any non-Jewish vessel before it is secured in Jewish hands unless permitted — therefore Tur did not permit here without intent to sink. I say: if there were prohibition of deceit, certainly intent to sink would not help, as pledge-as-sale — for with a Jew's pledge these things certainly do not help; and if there is prohibition with non-Jews, it would be like a Jew — why did Rav Ashi use a non-Jew's pledge without permission from pledge-as-sale? Also Tur and other poskim did not speak of one who transgresses; perhaps Maharil explained Rav Ashi used it with permission — then the question returns: why did Tur not write where certainly he does not intend to sink, for even when unknown there is prohibition from deceit per this view. Nevertheless it appears clear to my humble opinion there is clear permission to use a non-Jew's pledge without permission — it is no worse than canceling his loan in Choshen Mishpat siman 248, which was permitted [meaning in their days, since gentiles then practiced canceling Jewish loans, and Jews measured them as they measured them]. In Bava Kama (daf 113) Rashi explained this is not actual theft, for there is no desecration of God's name, for the non-Jew does not know explicitly that he lies, etc.; and what we say in chapter HaNezikin, Rav Kahana said "peace unto you, master" — Rashi: when he gives peace to a non-Jew he does not intend to bless him but his master (God). Tosafot wrote: requires study whether it is permitted because of deceit; likewise in Pe'ah; and above end of siman 117, forbidden to sell him nevelah as if kosher — that is not comparable here, for there he utters falsehood to deceive and receive good reputation or waive money for this lie, like theft from his purse — forbidden with non-Jews as with Jews; unlike here, where even if he could act justly and keep it all for himself there is no prohibition, since it is already in his hand permissibly for the loan; and see further siman 292 seif 2 regarding deceit of non-Jews if it is passive, as the non-Jew said.
