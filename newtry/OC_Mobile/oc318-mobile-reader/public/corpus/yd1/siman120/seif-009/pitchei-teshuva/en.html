Baer Heitev; and see Binas Adam, gate Issur VeHeter siman 65 regarding what they practice at large feasts, to borrow coated earthenware vessels and glass vessels from a Jewish shopkeeper; and he wrote that even though Taz and Shach are concerned for the view of Issur VeHeter to immerse without a blessing when the first bought for cards or commerce, nevertheless since Rama did not bring the view of Issur VeHeter at all, and Peri Chadash also disagrees with him — if so, for Shabbat or guests or a large feast where there is concern they may break if immersed and there will be loss, or there is no time, one may rely to use without tevilah, for tevilat kelim of these is only d'rabbanan according to all; and it is possible that Issur VeHeter also did not write thus except regarding metal vessels, for according to many poskim tevilah is d'oraisa, and so one should be stringent with metal vessels, for it is a doubt of d'oraisa — see there. And see responsum Chinuch ben Yomo siman 52, who wrote on this: a Jew who borrows a glass vessel from a Jewish shopkeeper and agrees with him that he will pay him the value of breakages during the meal and the remainder returns to him if he pays for breakages only according to the seller's principal and gives him a fixed sum for the loan — he is considered a renter, and his law is like the law of a borrower who does not require tevilah (and according to Taz one should immerse it without a blessing); but if he pays breakages according to what he profits on it, he is considered a purchase, for borrowing appraises how much it is worth in the market for any person, and therefore he must immerse — see there; and it appears he means with a blessing:
