But if he pledged it, etc. Even if he intended to sink it in the non-Jew's possession — no tevilah — likewise Issur VeHeter there law 88; and likewise Bach and Perishah in the name of Maharshal, who wrote in his name: if initially he pledged with intent not to redeem and changed his mind and redeemed — tevilah is required:
In partnership — no tevilah. I already wrote the reason in s.k. 22; and from that reason it implies while part of the vessel is still the non-Jew's; but if the Jew returned and bought the non-Jew's share — tevilah is required:
A Jew whom they stole, etc.; but a minister, etc. Taz wrote the reason: a minister or governor who certainly despaired of them — it is as if they sank in non-Jewish hands, etc.; therefore if theft and robbery are in a matter we heard he despaired of them, tevilah is required; and it is not clear, for in Issur VeHeter from which the rabbi brought this law (general rule 58 law 89 on the gloss) he wrote the reason: for a thief and robber, even though they despaired completely, since they are not permitted to hold it publicly, the non-Jew's name is not called upon it and no tevilah is required; Darkei Moshe brings it; and perhaps Taz also agrees to this, only he comes to say that even a minister or governor does not require tevilah except because presumably he despaired and it is as if sunk in non-Jewish hands; but if he did not despair — tevilah is required:
