Their tevilah was valid. Bach wrote the main reason of Rashba is because he ruled like Rav in chapter 2 of Chullin (daf 31) that a niddah who immersed without intent — her tevilah was valid; but for us who rule below end of siman 198 like R. Yochanan that a niddah requires intent — likewise here their tevilah was not valid — end of his words; and it is difficult, for Rashba ruled like R. Yochanan regarding niddah and Beit Yosef brings it end of siman 198; rather Rashba's main reason is that tevilah here is different, as he brought in this siman his words that he distinguishes; and likewise Terumat HaDeshen siman 257: even those who require intent for niddah — who says this tevilah of vessels, which is not impurity to purity and does not forbid what entered before tevilah and is only a decree of Scripture — requires intent? — end; and so the rabbi's view appears, who ruled plainly here like the Mechaber (and below end of siman 198 regarding niddah he ruled another tevilah is required); and Darkei Moshe ruled plainly like the Mechaber; and further, since he did not write in seif 14 regarding a minor that the adult must teach him to intend to purify, as Darkei Moshe brought in the name of Terumat HaDeshen — evidently he holds like Terumat HaDeshen's conclusion there that for tevilah here intent is not required; and wherever intent is not required, one need not teach him at all, as proven in Tosafot chapter 1 of Chullin daf 12 beginning; and Taz also distinguishes between niddah tevilah and tevilah here — see there; and therefore if vessels fell into water before tevilah, no tevilah is required, as Maharam's responsum — unlike Bach who rejected his words from what he holds intent is required, for this was already explained to be incorrect:
