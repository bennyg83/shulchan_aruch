Their tevilah was valid. At first glance the wording implies b'dieved only, but in truth not so, for in the source of this law — responsum to Ramban — it is written: you asked whether to immerse vessels through a non-Jew — I have not heard this, but it appears permitted, for we do not say intent is required except for washing hands for eating, which is because of terumah concern — end; implying even l'chatchila permitted. That responsum is Rashba's; and in that responsum above he wrote regarding washing hands intent is required, or the washer's or giver's intent — nevertheless he was lenient for tevilat kelim even l'chatchila. And Maharshal wrote Rashba was lenient per his view that a niddah who immersed without intent — but for us who are stringent with niddah, we should be stringent with tevilah by a non-Jew; and through distraction in the text he did not look at siman 198 in Beit Yosef, where Rashba holds niddah requires intent; and behold Rashba is stringent even for washing hands for eating, yet lenient for tevilat kelim — all the more so more lenient than niddah. But I wonder how he can bless on what the non-Jew immerses while the Jew does nothing — therefore it is not proper l'chatchila; and therefore Shulchan Aruch wrote b'dieved language here; but if the Jew also immerses some vessel at that time while the non-Jew helps immerse other vessels, the blessing refers to what the Jew does — it is fine; and this is the responsum to Ramban we mentioned.
