Disagree. Shach wrote that Bach challenged Rama on this and his words are not plausible; the essential law is as Rama wrote, that even with Jewish silver, since the non-Jew makes the main vessel, immersion is required — whether the non-Jew makes an entirely new vessel or it was pierced and did not hold a revi'it, since the non-Jew stabilizes it, immersion is required even for Jewish silver, for a craftsman acquires through enhancement of the vessel; likewise Taz — see there (one should immerse without a blessing or with other vessels).
Immersion. With a blessing — Shach; but Taz ruled to immerse without a blessing.
Blessing. Because we are uncertain whether we follow the craftsman — and here immersion is not required — or we follow the one whose silver it is — Shach.
His own. Shach wrote that it appears to him from Rama that when a Jew made it, even according to the view that we follow the one whose silver it is, it is possible where part of the silver is the Jew's that he agrees immersion is not required; but Taz wrote that it appears there is a scribal error here, and this "or he gave part of the money" belongs earlier; but if etc., and it teaches us two matters: first, that if the non-Jew gave all the metals, nevertheless he does not bless; and if he gave only part of his own, nevertheless immersion is required — end of his words (and see Peri Chadash).
