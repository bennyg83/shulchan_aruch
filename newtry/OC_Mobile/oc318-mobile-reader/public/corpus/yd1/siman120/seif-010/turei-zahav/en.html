A Jew who gave silver, etc. Rama brought "and some disagree" — these are two views in Tur and Beit Yosef; Beit Yosef strained to explain, as he testified, that he explained everything follows the stabilizer, meaning the non-Jew stabilized the vessel — this is not "follow the stabilizer" in Gemara or poskim; and Maharshal explained Ritzba means the non-Jew gives silver needed for repair and disagrees with the law emerging from the first view, not the law itself; from this he wrote Rama was imprecise here — all this does not matter to me. What appears clear: this dispute is itself what Semag's gloss author disputed with Semag — Semag wrote the reason: the non-Jew's name is not called upon it, as Tur wrote; the gloss author wrote: it appears he means even if it did not hold a revi'it before; and Tosafot explained in Avodah Zarah because the vessel's essence is not the non-Jew's; therefore when it did not hold a revi'it before, the vessel's essence is the gentile craftsman for those who say we follow the stabilizer; and Ritzba explicitly wrote tevilah is required where it did not hold a revi'it — end of his words. Tur's intent is also thus: this is obvious if the non-Jew gives silver for the whole vessel — tevilah is required; if he gives a little in a way that stabilizes the vessel, this depends on the dispute of following the stabilizer, as Tur explained above in Sefer HaMitzvos; here he only comes to explain the dispute according to those who say a craftsman acquires through enhancement of the vessel, as Tur ruled in several places in Tur. The first view holds exemption when a non-Jew repairs depends on the reason the non-Jew's name is not called upon it — we require "his name is called upon it," as the mishnah's law; therefore no difference whether it held or not — even without holding a revi'it before repair, the non-Jew's name is not called after repair; and Tur teaches a leniency: if a Jew gave a non-Jew to repair a broken vessel and gives silver to repair, the non-Jew acquires enhancement of the vessel, that repair being slight — obviously no tevilah, for his name is not upon it. But even if a Jew gave silver to a non-Jew to make a new vessel with great enhancement and the gentile craftsman acquired it — one might have said tevilah is required; he teaches: since his name is not upon it, no tevilah. Ritzba disagrees and holds: since the gentile craftsman acquires through enhancement, whether he made a new vessel from Jewish silver with great enhancement, obviously tevilah is required; but even if the non-Jew has only a small repair, he acquires that enhancement and tevilah is required, since that small amount stabilizes — it is as if he gave silver for repair from his own, since he acquired it, and the vessel's essence becomes his — tevilah is required, as Semag's gloss author we mentioned; and we do not care whether the non-Jew's name is called upon it or not. This is a broad explanation with Heaven's help, without stumbling from all Beit Yosef's challenges; and Rama well ruled to immerse without blessing a vessel a Jew gave silver for, etc.; and that he did not bring even giving a broken vessel not holding a revi'it that Ritzba mentioned — because Rama did not decide like him, and perhaps this is included in making a vessel that Shulchan Aruch mentioned, that whatever does not hold a revi'it is not a vessel. And Maharshal wrote in vain that he was imprecise — rather he was precise. And what he wrote "and if part of the silver," this is from Mordechai chapter HaSho'el, brought by Beit Yosef: however if pierced below a revi'it and did not hold because of the hole, and he placed a piece of metal, tevilah is required, for we establish everything follows the stabilizer — end; and Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe explain it means the non-Jew gave the metal piece from his own — such are Rama's words here in this case only; and according to Ritzba we mentioned, the law is so even from the Jew's own, since he acquires enhancement and stabilizes; only Mordechai does not hold thus, therefore he required metal from the non-Jew. I wonder: Mordechai's words are regarding a broken Jewish vessel that did not hold a revi'it — when the non-Jew places his piece, he causes the vessel's essence; but when making a new vessel from Jewish silver and the non-Jew gives a little from his own, who says that little comes where it stabilizes? Rather say it is nullified in the majority of Jewish silver and is like all the Jew's, for everywhere we follow the majority — therefore this tevilah without blessing is meant; and so Rama's wording implies afterward "and likewise a Jewish craftsman," etc., immerse without blessing; and it is further proved Mordechai tied this ruling to what he ruled everything follows the stabilizer, and we already explained Tur in Sefer HaMitzvos concludes that because of what stabilizes one immerses without blessing — how can we say Rama ruled like those who follow the stabilizer to bless? Rather it is clear: since it is a dispute, one does not bless because of stabilizing, per "some are stringent" in Tur I brought above seif 7; therefore tevilah without blessing is required for outer silver above and here for part of the silver that stabilizes:
He immerses it too without a blessing. Question: Issur VeHeter general rule 58 law 94 wrote: a non-Jew who gives silver to a Jew to repair a vessel for him, and the Jew buys it from him — even before it reaches the non-Jew's hand requires tevilah — end; implying even with blessing. And Rama's reason appears: Issur VeHeter's main reason obligating tevilah here is we do not say in this case "craftsman acquires through enhancement," as written there before — only regarding enhancement we say so, not the vessel itself, for the craftsman may not keep it; therefore the craftsman's name is not upon it; and since Rama saw other poskim hold "craftsman acquires through enhancement" as Ritzba we mentioned, he ruled here immerse without blessing. But what Rama wrote afterward "or he gave part of the metals from his own, no tevilah" — on what does this apply? If relying on "Jew makes for himself," obviously even if the non-Jew gave all metals he said first no tevilah; if on the previous "craftsman makes for non-Jew," it is difficult: since we are concerned perhaps here "craftsman acquires through enhancement," it becomes the non-Jew's vessel — what help that the Jewish craftsman gives part of the silver, since most of the vessel is the non-Jew's? This resembles buying a vessel from a non-Jew with a hole and a Jew repairs with Jewish silver — does that exempt non-Jew obligation? Now we hold the non-Jew's name is upon it — a fortiori from Rama shortly: if part of the silver from which the vessel was made is the non-Jew's, tevilah is required because we follow the stabilizer who is the non-Jew, even though most is the Jew's — all the more so if most is the non-Jew's and minority the Jew's. And further, even a stabilizer name is not here, for he makes a new vessel and is already nullified in the non-Jew's majority, as we mentioned — whence do we uproot tevilah obligation entirely? And even if you explain the non-Jew gives only part, it is still difficult: why above is tevilah required without blessing and here tevilah obligation is uprooted entirely? Therefore it appears there is a scribal error here, and this "or he gave part of the silver" belongs earlier; but if, etc., and it teaches two matters: first, if the non-Jew gave all metals, nevertheless he does not bless; and if he gave only part from his own, nevertheless tevilah is required. And so it appears primary to include this law in "requires tevilah without blessing," and there is no obstacle:
He should also immerse it without a blessing. This is difficult: in Or Zarua, kuntres 58, din 74, he wrote: a gentile who gives money to a Jew to repair a vessel for him — and the Jew purchased the material from him — even before it returns to the gentile's hand, immersion is required — end of quote; implying with a blessing. And the reasoning of Rama seems to be that Or Zarua's principal reason for requiring immersion here is that we do not say "the craftsman acquires through the enhancement of the vessel" — as he wrote there before this — for that principle applies only to the enhancement of the vessel, not to the vessel itself, since the craftsman is not permitted to retain it for himself; therefore the craftsman's name does not apply to it; and since Rama saw that other decisors hold we do say "the craftsman acquires through the enhancement," as I mentioned in the name of Rizbah, he ruled that one should immerse it without a blessing. But as for what Rama wrote afterward — "or if he [the Jew] contributed some of his own metal, no immersion is required" — where does this clause apply? If it refers to the adjacent case where a Jew is making [the vessel] for himself — obviously [no immersion is required], since even if the gentile had contributed all the metal the first clause already states no immersion is required. And if it refers to the preceding case where [the Jew] is making [it] for the gentile — it is difficult: since we are concerned to say perhaps we cannot apply "the craftsman acquires through enhancement," and thus the vessel belongs to the gentile — how would it help that the Jewish craftsman contributed a portion of the silver? Since most of the vessel belongs to the gentile, this resembles buying a vessel from a gentile that has a hole in it, and a Jew repairs it with Jewish silver — would that remove the obligation of immersion arising from the gentile's ownership? And now we take the position that the Jew has no share in it from his craftsmanship — it is as if the gentile's name applies to it — and there is a fortiori reasoning from what Rama wrote nearby: if a portion of the silver from which the vessel was made belongs to the gentile, immersion is required, because we follow the "stabilizer" which belongs to the gentile — even though most of the vessel is the Jew's — all the more so when most of the vessel belongs to the gentile and a minority to the Jew, immersion would be required; and moreover even a "stabilizer" is absent here since an entirely new vessel is being made and the minority is already nullified by the gentile's majority, as I noted nearby; and we have no basis on which to remove the obligation of immersion entirely. And even if you interpret it as the gentile contributing only a portion, it is still difficult — what is the difference that above immersion is required without a blessing, while here the obligation of immersion is removed entirely? Therefore it seems there is a scribal error here, and the clause "or if he contributed some of his own silver" belongs before "but if," etc. — teaching us there two novelties: first, that even if the gentile contributed all the metal, no blessing is recited; and second, that if he contributed only a portion of his own, immersion is still required. And so it seems to me in principle to classify this law under [the category of] requiring immersion without a blessing — and in this way there is no stumbling block before us.
