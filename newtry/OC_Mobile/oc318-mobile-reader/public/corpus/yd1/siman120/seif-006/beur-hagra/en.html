Eliezer, etc.; meaning not only regarding the Temple table that Scripture calls wood and the altar, as Rashi wrote there — rather in all vessels, as written in chapter 11 of Kelim: a lampstand is impure and coated is pure; and there "the rim," etc., and coated are pure; and Rosh strained there that it refers to impure iron — but it is not so, for it does not deal with this, for it says "the rim," etc., of metal are impure — meaning they receive impurity; and likewise the rims before it; and this is what the mishnah in Chagigah means "because they are coated" — meaning R. Eliezer defiles because of the metal in them, and the rabbis purify because the metal is coating; and as written "the rim," etc., and coated are pure; and likewise Rambam there and in his composition chapter 4 of Kelim — only his view is that even for a receiving vessel that receives impurity, the coating nullifies the vessel and it is pure; and the sugya does not imply thus, for it asks on the contrary, etc.; and even for the other answer there is no difficulty, only the coating is nullified to the vessel and as if not coated, and he retracted from what he wrote there "let it be derived from coating," etc. — all this is the first answer; and in the Gemara there, and what he brought proof from Torat Kohanim "every vessel," etc., "I might add coating," etc. — it does not deal with metal, for only wood and leather are written there; and as written in chapter 97 of Kelim: this is the rule — all for coating are pure; and Raavad already challenged; nevertheless his words here are correct that coating is nullified to the vessel; and as written in chapter 11 of the five primary sources of impurity, halachah 11: their coatings are nullified to them; and here we follow the coating because use is in the coating inside:
