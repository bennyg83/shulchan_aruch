By Torah law it is permitted to lend, etc. In Tur he wrote the reason — the reason for interest is as written "and your brother shall live with you" and a gentile you are not commanded to sustain him — end quote; some challenge from what is stated in the chapter (71a) Rav Nachman bar Yitzchak said — is it written "do not take from them"? "From him" is written — from Israel; and Rashi explained — is ger toshav written regarding "and your brother shall live with you" that you are commanded to sustain him, and "do not take from him interest" refers specifically to your brother and not a ger toshav — end quote; learn from this that interest and sustaining him are not dependent on each other; and it seems to resolve — since it is written a second time "and your brother shall live with you" after it wrote "do not take from him interest" — learn that regarding taking interest the Torah linked it to his sustenance — with this you sustain your brother and not a gentile or ger toshav; but regarding his other sustenance the Torah included also ger toshav at the beginning of the verse "and your brother shall live with you."
And nowadays it is permitted. The reason in Tur — because we cannot profit in any business if not with them; if so it does not apply lest he learn from his deeds in interest more than other business — end quote; and one asked him — why did they not also decree in Talmudic times on all business that it not be with a gentile lest he learn from his deeds; and he answered it was an uncommon matter and the Rabbis did not decree on it — for if so why did they decree on lifnei iver in business; and it appears that in business that was also with Israel we do not say he will learn from the gentile's deed more than from Israel's — unlike interest which was only specifically with a gentile; and therefore now when all business is with gentiles and it applies lest he learn from his deeds — nevertheless they did not decree also on interest — they did not decree.
