(Siman 159, Shulchan Aruch, seif 3) The Karaites, etc. See what the Shach wrote regarding the Karaites. In Tiferes L'Moshe he challenged him from what is stated in Even HaEzer, siman 44, that their kiddushin are valid kiddushin. Infer from this that as a stringency they were treated like non-Jews, but not as a leniency. The Beis Hillel had already preceded him in this challenge. See also Toldos Adam, where R' Zalman of Vilna, of blessed memory, challenged the Shach from what the Rambam wrote in Hilchos Mamrim. See also Tosafos Yom Tov, chapter 6 of Niddah, which likewise implies that for purposes of leniency they cannot be treated as non-Jews, because even though they sinned they are still Jews. Only regarding the Cutheans can this be done, either because the Sages have such authority or because he holds that they were converts motivated by fear of lions, which does not apply to Karaites. In Atzei Levonah he challenged this from words of Torah in Bava Metzia, daf 72, where it is proven that the Sages have the power to be lenient. One can answer that there it is different, because since they have authority to enact monetary rules, their enactment is effective also regarding prohibition, as I wrote in my novellae.
