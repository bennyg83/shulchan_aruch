And some are stringent. Responsa Tiferet Tzvi, Yoreh De'ah, siman 63, entry 5, writes that even according to the poskim who rule stringently that it is forbidden to lend with interest to an apostate to idolatry because he remains “your brother,” if the lender collected interest from him, he need not return it. Although he violated the prohibition, returning the money would fulfill the positive commandment, “and your brother shall live,” and he is not commanded concerning this person. The same applies to an apostate who sins to provoke. Although he may have worthy descendants, the lender need not return the interest and may retain it. I was uncertain, however, about one who collected interest from a Jew and the borrower afterward became an apostate to idolatry. Must the lender return it to rectify the earlier prohibition that he already violated, or perhaps the prohibition cannot now be rectified because he is presently not commanded to sustain him? See there. He reached no clear conclusion and left the matter requiring further study. See also Responsa Chacham Tzvi, siman 43, and Responsa Shevut Yaakov, volume 2, siman 73, concerning an informer.
