A mumar — permitted to lend to him, etc. For you are not commanded to sustain him; and do not challenge — behold he transgresses lifnei iver for the mumar commits a prohibition in taking interest; Sefer HaTerumah resolved — he too does not commit a prohibition in taking interest, for Israel is forbidden (in taking) [in giving] interest from "and to your brother you shall not charge interest" — meaning you the lender shall not cause a prohibition that your brother charges you interest; and the mumar whom the lender is not warned to take from him — automatically he too is not warned to give interest; but to borrow from him and give him interest is certainly forbidden for he commits a prohibition in what he takes from you; if so it applies in our giving because of "do not charge interest to your brother" — for even though he sinned he is an Israelite; and Tur wrote and one who gives him to him transgresses because of lifnei iver, etc.; and the intent is he also transgresses on this; and he cited this prohibition because the essence of the prohibition even Israel from Israel — namely lo tashich — its reason is lest he cause sin to the lender as we mentioned; and in Perishah he challenged — behold nowhere is it mentioned regarding an Israelite borrower that he transgresses lifnei iver except the other verses regarding borrower; and he omitted an explicit mishnah at end of the chapter where they said he transgresses lifnei iver with the other prohibitions.
