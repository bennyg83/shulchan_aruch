An apostate. Shach notes that this wording implies that one may not lend to Samaritans at interest. He questions this, since the Sages treated them as complete non-Jews for all matters. It is strained to say that this status applies only stringently and not leniently; indeed, stringency itself can sometimes lead to leniency. Shach therefore rules that they are full non-Jews for all purposes and may be lent money at interest.
That it is forbidden. Shach writes that this applies specifically to the Karaites of Rambam's era, who possessed several positive qualities.
Forbidden. Even according to those who permit lending to an apostate, that applies to one who knows his Creator and consciously rebels. One raised among non-Jews is considered inadvertent. Nimukei Yosef cites Rabbeinu Tam as permitting interest, while Darkei Moshe leaves the matter unresolved. The child of an apostate father and non-Jewish mother follows the mother and has the law of a non-Jew. See Even HaEzer 16:2. Shach.
