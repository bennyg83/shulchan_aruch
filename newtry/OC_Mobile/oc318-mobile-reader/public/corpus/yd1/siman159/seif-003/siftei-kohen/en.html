Samaritans have the law of a mumar for idolatry. It implies forbidden to lend them at interest; and so it implies even more in the Eliyah Rabba; and it requires study, for we rule in many places that they decreed upon Samaritans and made them like complete idolaters [a covenant was made in Israel according to the Torah that they would form a mutual-aid society and no man would take interest from his fellow; and a gentile who is not a member of this society, or a mumar who separated himself from it — they do not have this right and it is permitted to take interest from them]; and it is forced to say specifically for stringency — that their slaughter is forbidden and their wine is yayin nesech — but not for leniency; but since they made them like complete idolaters, it implies they made them entirely like complete idolaters; and moreover, certainly even a stringency that leads to leniency from another source — and this is clear. And so below siman 266 seif 47, the Rif, Rosh, Rambam, and Mechaber wrote that bills of divorce and writs of manumission are invalid nowadays when witnessed by a Samaritan, since we rule in the first chapter of Chullin that they made them like complete idolaters — even though it is a leniency that if another betroths her she is not betrothed to him and does not need a get from him; and likewise regarding manumission the slave remains his slave and is exempt from the commandments; and so is written there seif 80 in the name of the Rambam, Ran, and Beit Yosef that a Samaritan nowadays has the law of a gentile regarding selling his slave who goes free; and I wrote there that so is the Rav’s opinion, and it is a leniency — permitted for a freewoman; and even for the Bach there who wrote it is uncertain — that is as he explained the reason there that since it is only a fine they did not penalize the Samaritan, unlike other matters — see there and examine. Rather, certainly they are complete idolaters in every matter, and if so it is permitted to lend them at interest as well.
And therefore a female apostate etc. But a male apostate who has a son from a gentile woman — the son follows her — Mordechai there; and Darkei Moshe brings it; and so the Rav wrote in Even HaEzer siman 16 seif 2.
That the son etc. it is forbidden to lend to him etc. Even according to one who permits lending to a mumar — that is specifically the mumar himself who knows his Master and intends to rebel against Him; but one who was raised among gentiles is like one who acts erroneously; and so somewhat implies in the Mordechai — end of Darkei Moshe. And Nekudot Yosef chapter Ein Nochri wrote in the name of Rabbenu Tam that it is permitted to lend to him etc.; and Darkei Moshe wrote since Beit Yosef wrote regarding Karaites unlike his view, the same applies here; and the matter requires study given what I wrote nearby that even regarding Karaites Beit Yosef’s words are not precise.
