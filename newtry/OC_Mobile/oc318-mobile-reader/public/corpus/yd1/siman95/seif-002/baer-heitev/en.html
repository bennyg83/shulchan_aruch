Egg. Shach wrote: even if it is peeled, if the pot is well washed.
In its shell. Rashba wrote that the egg shell is clearly perforated, and when a person cooks it he finds the egg itself colored from that color; therefore the Sages forbade an egg of a chick and permitted a forbidden egg, for that one emits broth of a chick and this one emits only mere broth, end of his words. And Taz wrote and requires study from what Rama wrote in siman 86 seif 5 that if the egg is pierced its law is like peeled, for every egg shell is perforated — so why distinguish a hole that developed or did not develop — and he left it requiring study. (And it appears to me that this is certainly if the egg was cooked all day — we see it does not emit at all but rather certainly hardens moment by moment until because of this those fine holes closed; therefore regarding an egg of impure fowl it does not forbid until it is pierced with a large hole; but here if it was cooked with meat then it absorbs well through those holes with difficulty, and therefore it is forbidden to eat it with kutach. Proof for this from Rashba himself who wrote in siman 66: if blood is found in an egg that one placed on hot coals to constrict — it is plain that the dish is not forbidden since there was no sauce — it forbids only the measure of a peel, and the egg shell is no less than the measure of a peel; and here he wrote that the egg shell is perforated — but certainly it does not emit anything through those small holes, investigate.)
To forbid. L'chatchila to eat it with kutach; but b'dieved if he placed it in kutach it is permitted; but to cook them l'chatchila in a meat pot in order to eat them with kutach is forbidden even for Mechaber.
In a vessel. Shach wrote: it appears that even to place them by way of pouring is permitted, but it is difficult, for he wrote in seif 3 that if one pours from a kli rishon of meat onto a dairy vessel its law is like a kli rishon and it forbids if it was ben yomo; and one must say that here he deals only with permitting to place them without pouring. Nevertheless regarding the halachah requires study, for it appears that if one pours from a meat vessel onto a dairy vessel it is permitted since it is b'dieved; and some say regarding what we forbid meat bowls that were rinsed in a dairy cauldron — there it is different as Ran wrote: who will tell us that emission of meat and emission of milk will not mix in themselves without the intermediary of water — therefore it is not noten taam bar noten taam; therefore it appears regarding fish that were cooked, etc. And further, what we permit as noten taam bar noten taam is not because they hold the first taste does not spread until the third — for if so, in other prohibitions too we would say so for one who does not hold chein nafsho, and we permit only in basar b'chalav and the like where there are two tastes of heter; therefore specifically when the second taste stood in heter without mixture of milk do we say so; but here where the second taste did not stand by itself at all but as soon as it came into existence it mixed with the taste of milk — it is reasonable and certain that it is forbidden, as Semak wrote, end of his words. And all this does not apply to pouring; and so is the view of Issur VeHeter that if one pours from a kli rishon of meat onto one of milk it is permitted; and even the Rav in Turei Chayim there din 12 wrote that all his words appear to me to be the halachah since they are matters of taste; but it appears to me they did not practice to be lenient in such a case; however in great loss or time of need one may rely on him. And also in responsum of Masat Binyamin siman 2 he ruled so and wrote that all who are stringent in this are only among those who wonder, see there; and see s.k. 17.
Sharp. The sharpness of spices makes it as if it is visible, and specifically the food itself that is sharp is made for improvement; but it does not make the pot for improvement; and see above siman 96 and siman 103 seif 6.
The absorbed. Explanation: we do not say regarding spices that since they are made for taste they are not nullified, since their prohibition is not because of themselves; and see above siman 98 seif 8. And Taz wrote: if the absorbed amount is unknown, one needs sixty against all the spices.
