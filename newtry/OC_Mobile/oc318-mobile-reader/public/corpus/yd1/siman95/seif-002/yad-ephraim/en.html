But if one pours boiling water. He wrote in responsum of Pri Megadim manuscript that they asked: if they poured boiling water from a dairy vessel onto hot meat — what is its law; and he wrote that even though in the law above that pouring onto a meat vessel — one may say the water was not forbidden since pouring forbids only kedei kelipah and it cannot be peeled — nevertheless in this case it would be forbidden since the meat is hot, if so the lower prevails and the water becomes forbidden and returns and is absorbed in the meat; but one may challenge: who will tell us that the vessel from which he pours imparts taste at the time of pouring to the water poured outside the vessel — one may say the taste applies that the poskim wrote regarding vessels that were rinsed in a dairy cauldron, etc., that both tastes emerge at once to the water, etc.; and if we come to forbid in pouring one must say the vessel imparts taste in the water poured at the time of pouring, and then taste emerges from the meat too and the tastes mix and the water becomes forbidden — and where do we have this from; and he brought proof from Maggid Mishneh in name of Maharam that Beit Yosef brought end of seif 8 in poultry slaughtered by nikur in a dairy bowl, etc., and explains that what he wrote: but if they poured the water onto the poultry — meaning they poured onto it from a dairy kli rishon, and proves from this that tastes emerge at once, for otherwise it would be noten taam bar noten taam; and he holds that forbidden water requires sixty against the peel; and if so even though here we do not establish so and it is reasonable to us that the water is not forbidden because of pouring since peel does not apply in water — nevertheless in pouring onto hot meat the lower prevails and imparts taste throughout — one may say it is forbidden until there is sixty against the water poured onto the meat, for we estimate throughout we do not know how much it absorbed — so too; and this is a novel matter to say in pouring that the taste emerging with the water strikes the taste emitted from the meat and they forbid one another and the water becomes forbidden; for in pouring from a meat vessel onto a dairy vessel we do not say so, and what he wrote that there the water is not forbidden since emission from the vessel through pouring forbids only kedei kelipah and peel does not apply in water — behold the crux in the poskim who permit did not come for this reason, as explained in Issur VeHeter chapter 34 who wrote that taste of a dairy cauldron is not considered taste b'dieved — it is no worse than if they placed in pewter, etc., and explained that because the water is noten taam bar noten taam they are permitted even with meat and he was not concerned for meeting of tastes regarding pouring; and so explained in Shach and other poskim; and also what he explains Maharam's words in Mordechai — meaning they poured from a dairy kli rishon — is explained from Shulchan Aruch siman 68 and those who came after who explain as its plain meaning that the poultry was placed first in a dairy pan and they poured water from a kli rishon onto the poultry into the pan; and the Gaon, and these are his words, also explains Shulchan Aruch's words on this basis that they poured from a dairy kli rishon, and he carried on in two views that Shulchan Aruch wrote on this; and see what he wrote in my Yad Ephraim on Orach Chaim siman 467 in Shulchan Aruch's words; and I found in Sefer Tiferet Lemoshe who also separated as the Gaon wrote — however in my view it is a great forced explanation, and I toiled and found the source of Maggid Mishneh's words taken from the small Tashbetz that Maharam's student composed, and these are his words: there in siman 34 it is stated — poultry slaughtered by nikur in a bowl (dairy) is permitted even if it is ben yomo, for it is a kli sheni and does not cook; and he wrote a gloss on this (and it is Maharaf's gloss as explained in Kol Bo in copying laws from Tashbetz), investigate: when they poured the water in the vessel first and afterward placed the poultry in it; but if they placed the poultry in the vessel first and afterward poured water onto the poultry — if the water is boiling they forbid the poultry the measure of a peel, end of his words; and also what he wrote there in Hagahot Maimoniyot: and one may not return the poultry, etc., is also from Tashbetz there; and also what he wrote there an incident with udder and meat, etc., is also from Tashbetz there siman 141; and according to the plain meaning it appears that what he wrote: but if they placed the poultry in the vessel first — means that vessel, namely the dairy bowl he spoke of at first; and what he wrote: if the water is boiling they forbid the poultry — meaning they emit from the vessel and absorb in the poultry kedei kelipah, and then the second noten taam is from the vessel to the poultry without intermediary of water, since the poultry is on the edge of the bowl literally, and it is a second noten taam in prohibition; and according to the Gaon's words one must explain what he wrote: but if they placed the poultry in the vessel first — does not refer to the dairy bowl above but to a vessel in general, and afterward poured water — meaning water from a dairy vessel; and accordingly the language boiling water forbids — means the water itself that becomes forbidden because of meeting tastes of pouring water and emission of poultry together; and proven is his law in the Gaon that meeting of tastes also applies in pouring; but in my view it is forced to explain thus in Tashbetz's gloss, see there who wrote that even according to his explanation one may say Maharam holds when cooked it is forbidden, therefore they forbid pouring water well — unlike for us who establish when cooked it is permitted; and he again wrote to prove that even roasted Maharam holds permitted as explained in responsum of Maharam Bava Batra siman 213 in honey cooked in a pan, etc., who concludes there and further he would permit because noten taam bar noten taam; and even though in Mordechai he wrote the responsum in name of R' Yehudah in a responsum — he only mentioned they asked according to R' Yehudah, and perhaps that one and his student who arranged the responsum wrote this, and perhaps in Mordechai there is a scribal error from R' Yehudah to R' Yitzchak; and one must say according to R' Yehudah; and if so, since Rabeinu Yehudah permits roasted, all the more when cooked; and until here what forbids in that case of poultry slaughtered by nikur is because of meeting of tastes — until here his holy words; and in my innovations Mordechai's words and ben yomo and Rashal and Minchat Kohen in that incident of honey, etc., and there I wrote that this responsum of Maharam that they asked according to R' Yehudah refers to R' Yehudah as the written questions there siman 214, 215, 216 see there — Rabbi, and he is R' Yehudah, as explained in ShaDa siman 482 that R' Yehudah answered that Rabbenu Tam was particular, etc.;
