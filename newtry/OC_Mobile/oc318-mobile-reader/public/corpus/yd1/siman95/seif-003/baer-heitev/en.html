Permitted. Shach wrote: this is b'dieved; but l'chatchila one should not rinse them even for Mechaber, as Mechaber wrote in Orach Chaim siman 452.
Fat. Explanation: even if there was only clinging fat on one of them, all is forbidden, because the second taste immediately imparts taste into the substance and it is not a second noten taam in heter; and also the fat touches the second vessel and the vessel becomes forbidden and again all becomes forbidden. And accordingly, even if one of them is not ben yomo and the clinging fat is on the one that is not ben yomo, all is forbidden; but if the clinging fat is on the one that is ben yomo, that one which is ben yomo is permitted and the one that is not ben yomo is forbidden, end of his words Shach.
They forbid. For who will tell us that emission of meat and emission of milk will not mix in themselves without the intermediary of water — Ran. And Taz wrote: and nevertheless, if water or vegetables were cooked in a dairy ben yomo pot and one stuck into it a ben yomo meat spoon and there is not sixty against the spoon, they forbid one another even if both were well scraped; and he also wrote what Rama wrote: all the vessels are permitted — this is because the one that is ben yomo is permitted since it receives spoilage; and the one that is not ben yomo is also not forbidden from the one that is ben yomo, for it is noten taam bar noten taam, and it is still heter after it entered this vessel since it is not ben yomo there is no prohibition on it. And even though Rama wrote in siman 94 seif 5 to forbid the vessel that is not ben yomo as a stringency, it appears that specifically there he holds one should be stringent because of marit ayin since one pours food into a vessel that is as if it were ben yomo — therefore one forbids the vessel that is not ben yomo, and this does not apply here, end of his words. And Shach wrote in name of Issur VeHeter that there is no difference whether one rinsed bowls with bowls or spoons with spoons or ladles with ladles, and we do not say that what is used in a kli sheni does not forbid, end of his words. And it appears to me that specifically from stam we are concerned lest he used a spoon or ladle in a kli rishon, as we are concerned in such a case regarding hagalah of vessels; but if it is clear to him that he did not use a spoon or ladle, only a kli sheni within twenty-four hours, certainly it does not forbid b'dieved, and so is practiced (and Peri Chadash wrote on this and the law is not correct, but anything used while yad soledes bo applied within twenty-four hours is forbidden).
L'chatchila. Since it is not food but mere water, it is like l'chatchila and we forbid it since it absorbed from spoiled prohibition.
Together. Shach wrote and it teaches us: some say that if one rinsed meat bowls in the dairy cauldron itself all is forbidden; rather, even if one rinsed a meat vessel and a dairy vessel together in one vessel, all is forbidden, for the water in the kli rishon emits and absorbs and becomes nevelah.
Second. Taz wrote: even if they were soiled, as he wrote afterward regarding pouring; and all the more so pouring is stricter than kli sheni for us. And Maharshal disagrees on this and wrote that if there was grime from meat and milk it forbids even in a kli sheni, for there is concern the grime will dissolve in the water and be considered as the substance of the prohibition; however since this is somewhat a stringency, the teacher need not ask about this if they were soiled until it becomes clear to him automatically; but regarding pouring one must ask if the rinsing was by way of pouring, since stam they are accustomed to pour on them from a kli rishon by the fire — if it is not known to him that it was not by pouring, then one need not ask if there was food remnant, end of his words. And it appears to me for practical halachah to decide that regarding pouring one must forbid both if there were food remnants from both; and if one is soiled and the second clean, then the clean one becomes forbidden; but in a kli sheni food remnants do not forbid, for we establish that even yad soledes bo in a kli sheni does not absorb and does not emit.
Its law. Taz wrote: one may challenge — behold it is noten taam bar noten taam; and it appears that since we forbid above as those who forbid whom Rama brought in this seif, because it is like taste of meat and milk mixed together — that is, since the two tastes mix in one vessel and chein nafsho — it is comparable to our considering the splash of pouring as if it were in the vessel from which he pours; therefore he forbids the vessel onto which he pours since it too is ben yomo; but if this vessel onto which he pours is not ben yomo, it is not forbidden, for it is no worse than that which he wrote nearby that the vessels are permitted if one is not ben yomo.
They will absorb. Shach wrote: and even though in siman 68 seif 6 it appears from Rama's words that pouring cooks kedei kelipah — one must say that although it cooks so that poultry emits and absorbs, nevertheless a hard vessel does not absorb, for it does not emit and absorb at once like a hard vessel; however regarding the halachah it appears as Hagahot ShaDa wrote that they are forbidden, since we establish that pouring cooks, as will be explained in siman 105; if so it is considered actual cooking and absorbs even in a vessel; however all this is when clinging fat is on them, but if they are clean certainly there is room to permit because of noten taam bar noten taam; but where it is a vessel of prohibition where noten taam bar noten taam does not apply, it appears that even if clean it is forbidden, and requires study in this, for perhaps even cooking does not cook so much that it emits from the vessel and absorbs into the vessel; and accordingly also in meat and milk vessels where one is clean and one not clean — the clean is forbidden and the not clean requires study whether we say that since the clean is nat bar lichtmile it returns and forbids the second each time he pours onto it, or we say pouring does not cook so much and one should be stringent with both where there is no great loss.
Were rinsed. In Issur VeHeter it is written: even if they are accustomed in that house to rinse in a kli rishon they are permitted through a double doubt — a doubt lest they were not rinsed with them, and if you say they were rinsed, a doubt lest it was not ben yomo.
