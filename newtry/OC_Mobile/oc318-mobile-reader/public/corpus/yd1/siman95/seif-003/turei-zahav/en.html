It is clear to me that there was no fat at all. One may not challenge from what Tosafot and Rosh and Ran that I brought in siman 97 seif 2 wrote — that they hold like Rabbeinu Tam who holds an oven is not cleaned well — one may say that there it deals with an oven that cannot be controlled so much by hand and requires great wiping needed for earthenware vessels, which is not so here in a bowl; and so too in a pot one controls it well with great wiping. And Maharach wrote: because of this Rosh here and Ran in chapter Kol HaBasar and in chapter Kli Sheni disagree with Rabbeinu Tam and hold it is cleaned well; and incidentally in his preoccupation with the text he did not look at Rosh and Ran chapter 25 who explicitly wrote like Rabbeinu Tam.
And there are those who forbid even if there is no fat, etc. Their reason is explained in Tosafot — for fish that came up in a bowl, this case is different here, since the bowls touch the pan and taste is emitted from one to the other and it becomes a second taste in prohibition; and further, when the second taste of meat and milk enters the water they are immediately forbidden and return and forbid the bowls and the pan, end of his words. And in Ran the second reason of Tosafot is explained further — this case is different in fish that came up in a bowl, etc., for kutach did not receive taste at all from the first taste, that is the bowl, but from the fish which is a second taste; but here the taste of milk that fell into the water receives taste from the first taste of meat in the bowls — for who will tell us that emission of meat and emission of milk will not mix in themselves without the intermediary of the water, end of his words. And nevertheless, if water or vegetables were cooked in a dairy ben yomo pot and one stuck into it a ben yomo meat spoon and there is not sixty against the ben yomo spoon, they forbid one another even if both were well scraped; and even though this is the actual law of bowls that were rinsed in a cauldron that Shulchan Aruch mentioned, nevertheless I wrote this because it is most common.
And then all the vessels are permitted. The one that is ben yomo is permitted, for it receives spoilage; and the one that is not ben yomo is also not forbidden from the one that is ben yomo, for it is noten taam bar noten taam and it is still heter even after it entered this vessel, since it is not ben yomo automatically there is no prohibition on it, end of his words — permitted even for the first reason of Tosafot above that they touch the pan, etc.; and according to what Rama wrote in siman 94 seif 5 to forbid the vessel that is not ben yomo as a stringency, he should have written here too, for it is one reason; and it appears that specifically there he holds one should be stringent because of marit ayin, since one pours food into a vessel that is as if it were ben yomo — therefore one forbids the vessel that is not ben yomo, and this does not apply here; so it appears to me to be Rama's intent.
And the water — they treat it with prohibition. For it absorbed now from spoiled meat and milk, and b'dieved does not apply in mere water as in a dish and pot which are significant items, and the Torah had pity on the money of Israel — Issur VeHeter general rule 34.
But if they were rinsed one after the other. Even in a kli rishon, for this is noten taam bar noten taam since the tastes did not mix.
Or in a kli sheni, etc. — all is permitted. Explanation: even if they were soiled, as he wrote afterward regarding pouring; and all the more so, for pouring is stricter than kli sheni for us. And Maharshal in chapter Kol HaBasar siman 64 disagrees on this and wrote that if there was grime from meat and milk it forbids even in a kli sheni, for there is concern the grime will dissolve in the water and be considered as the substance of the prohibition; however since this is somewhat a stringency, the teacher need not ask about this if they were soiled until it becomes clear to him automatically that they were soiled; but regarding pouring, which absorbs and emits up to kedei kelipah, food remnants are certainly forbidden, and one must ask if the rinsing was by way of pouring, since stam they are accustomed to pour on them from a kli rishon by the fire — if it is not known that it was not by pouring, then one need not ask if there was food remnant, end of his words. And it appears to me for practical halachah to decide that regarding pouring one must forbid both if there was food remnant from both; and if one is soiled and the second clean, then the clean becomes forbidden; but in a kli sheni food remnants do not forbid, since we establish that in a kli sheni even yad soledes bo does not absorb and does not emit, as Turei Chayim wrote general rule 57. It is written in Issur VeHeter that there is no difference if one rinsed in a kli rishon bowls with bowls or what they call in foreign tongue ladles — we do not say that what is used in a kli sheni does not forbid; since they place hot meat on it by way of pouring, if so through many pourings the prohibition is absorbed in all of it; kli sheni — the spoons that are accustomed to stick in a kli rishon; but if it is certain he did not use a kli rishon within twenty-four hours, it does not forbid b'dieved. And Maharach wrote: he agreed with Issur VeHeter.
Its law is like a kli rishon. One may challenge — behold it is noten taam bar noten taam; and it appears that since we forbid above as those who forbid whom Rama brought in this seif, because it is like taste of meat and milk mixed together — that is, since the two tastes mix in one vessel and chein nafsho — it is comparable to our considering the splash of pouring as if it were in the vessel from which he pours; therefore he forbids the vessel onto which he pours since it too is ben yomo; but if this vessel onto which he pours is not ben yomo, it is not forbidden, for it is no worse than that which he wrote nearby that the vessels are permitted if one is not ben yomo; so it appears to me.
We are not concerned lest they were rinsed in it. In Issur VeHeter it is written: even if they are accustomed in that house to rinse in a kli rishon they are permitted through a double doubt — a doubt lest they were not rinsed with them, and if you say they were rinsed, a doubt lest it was not ben yomo.
