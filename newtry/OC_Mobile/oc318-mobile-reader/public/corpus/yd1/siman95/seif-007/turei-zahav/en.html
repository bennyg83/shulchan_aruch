And one who is stringent also in this — blessing shall come upon him. This he wrote following Tosafot and Rosh chapter Kol HaBasar and these are his words: one may learn from here that pure salted and impure unsalted do not require hagalah — cheese molds of gentiles, etc.; and the wood does not become hot through salting; and they act every day that salt placed in a bowl used for meat — they take from it to place into milk and are not concerned lest it absorbed from the bowl; and one who is stringent — blessing shall come upon him, end of his words. The Rav understood that what Tosafot concluded "and one who is stringent — blessing" also refers to what he wrote "and they act every day," etc.; and this is very astonishing — for if he wrote "and they act every day," etc., he brings important learned men — are they among those who exclude themselves from blessing? Rather it is clear that "one who is stringent — blessing" refers to cheese molds of gentiles where we find stringencies in some poskim; but regarding this salt there is no reason to forbid even l'chatchila; and what Tosafot wrote "and they act every day" they wrote only as proof for the previous matter. And I also found afterward in Rashal chapter 25 siman 81 that the law of salt is permitted even l'chatchila; and he brought in Turei Chayim from Orach Chaim that clean gentile vessels — it is permitted to place there spices and other sharp things that do not emit a hard thing when dry, end of his words — I brought it in siman 91 seif 2; and Rashal concluded: and nevertheless I praise the Ashkenaz custom of having vessels made for salt, one for meat and one for milk, for sometimes during the meal one's hands are soiled from meat and he takes salt there and afterward will take for milk, and likewise the reverse, end of his words.
