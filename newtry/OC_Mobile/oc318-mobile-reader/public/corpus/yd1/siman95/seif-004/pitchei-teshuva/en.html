See in responsum of Makom Shmuel siman 89 who expanded greatly to refute this ruling, that in Rashba's responsum there it does not appear at all, and from Issur VeHeter's words too there is no proof, for what required sixty against all the spice is not because in basar b'chalav we do not say noten taam bar noten taam, but because there are only two noten taam (this is like roasted that I wrote above), and accordingly when cooked where there are three noten taam, even in a sharp item it is permitted and not as Rama compares the law of cooking sharp items to the law of spice that was ground; and he concludes that in a fully sharp item one should not deviate from Rama's ruling since so the custom spread, but in a place where there is doubt whether it is called a sharp item (as in Eretz Yisrael where Taz and Beit Yosef disagreed, as Beit HaTevah wrote siman 96 s.k. 10) and the like — there is room to permit, see there; and likewise agreed in responsum of Pri Tevuah siman 22 see there. And one may question whether sharpness that improves is d'oraisa or d'rabbanan, with practical difference to be lenient in its doubt; and I saw in responsum of Knesset Yechezkel end of siman 24 he wrote it is d'rabbanan, see there; and see in responsum of Rabbi Akiva Eiger Panim Me'irot part 1 siman 64 who brought proof it is d'oraisa, see there. I again saw in responsum of Shivat Tzion siman 32 he wrote it is not relevant at all to say law of d'oraisa or d'rabbanan — sharp food imparts taste, and since the Sages stood on this through experimentation and knew that in a sharp item it makes it for improvement — it is plain it is d'oraisa, see there; and as I wrote above end of seif 110 in laws of end of seif note 19 that so is the view of Rabbeinu Akiva Eiger.
