It appears to me that if they placed, etc. — see Shach what Peri Chadash wrote, and he is correct; and in truth it was not hidden from Shach's words the view of Orach Chaim HaArukh that water for washing that they place ash in it — hagalah does not help. However nevertheless he challenged well: just as for R' Eliezer one must say they purged twice, so too for R' Tam one may say they first purged in water with ash to spoil the absorbed matter, and afterward purged in hot water — for the absorbed matter already became spoiled and the water will not become nat bar lichtmile. But certainly if we say what Tosafot wrote in the challenge, and we say the Torah forbade only a pot of that day and did not challenge without qualification whether the Torah forbade — we learn it is forbidden in any case — their intent one may say: what is the challenge from hagalah — behold noten taam lefegem in general is permitted, only in purgings by law of scriptural decree to forbid; if so how would it become nevelah — therefore Tosafot wrote this explanation is not because of scriptural decree, for according to one who holds noten taam lefegem is permitted they truly did not purge except ben yomo, and if so they challenged well; if so there is no place for Shach's challenge who challenged Shach's explanation that they purged in water with ash, and challenged according to one who holds nat bar lichtmile is forbidden how they purged — one need not say that according to one who forbids he holds like one who holds it can be squeezed out and permitted, for in the Gemara in Avodah Zarah for Rav we are uncertain whether nat bar lichtmile is forbidden and the halachah is permitted, or Rav holds nat bar lichtmile is permitted. However I did not understand at all Shach's view that a meat pot and they cooked water with ash in it — the absorbed matter becomes spoiled that absorbs water with ash and spoils the meat — if so if afterward they cooked milk it does not forbid, for it is nat bar lichtmile; and likewise a vessel absorbed from prohibition; and accordingly why did the Torah forbid something that comes in fire — meaning it absorbs through fire requires libun, for if all absorbed matter emerges through hagalah it is difficult why libun is needed — one should purge the spit in water with ash and then it spoils the absorbed matter inside and afterward one can roast in it, for even though the absorbed drop emerged, it is spoiled from the outset; and one must say the Torah does not deal with this, investigate, for these matters are clear to one who understands.
