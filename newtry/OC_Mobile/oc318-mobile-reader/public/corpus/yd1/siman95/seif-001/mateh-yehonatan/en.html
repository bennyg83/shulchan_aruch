(Siman 95 in Shulchan Aruch seif 1) Fish that were cooked, etc. — permitted to eat them with kutach, etc. This is because he holds according to the view of Tosafot and Rosh that they came up, and from this it is proven that Tosafot also holds unlike Rashba who holds that if one cooked in a vessel of something else it is hagalah where it is heter it absorbs — unlike Shach who wrote that Tosafot also holds like Rashba. See above siman 93 where I wrote the dispute of Reish Bet Yosef and Shach in explanation of Tosafot's words in Chullin daf 111 s.v. halachah. And at first glance one may challenge on this that it is stated in Avodah Zarah: every day purging is made from one to the other, and the Gemara challenges: it works out for peace offerings but a sin-offering eaten at night and day — for they cook in it today a sin-offering and it becomes leftover because they cook in it tomorrow peace offerings or a sin-offering — let leftover sin-offering of today in peace offerings or sin-offering of tomorrow not be needed, for when they cook today's sin-offering they again cook in it today's peace offerings of tomorrow's sin-offering and yesterday's peace offerings complete their times together, and again they cook tomorrow's peace offerings, etc. And one may challenge why they needed to cook first yesterday's peace offerings — why not cook in it immediately on the first time tomorrow's peace offerings? And one may say that Tosafot wrote there in another manner: that which we say regarding a sacred vessel — we establish it on a Torah basis; if so, whatever we can do as a matter that does not nullify prohibition in the majority we do as much as possible; accordingly if one cooked in it immediately tomorrow's peace offerings, immediately he would be nullifying the majority of two prohibitions — one, that a sin-offering is forbidden to non-priests, and further, if the absorbed matter of the sin-offering in tomorrow's peace offerings were not nullified, that absorbed matter would become leftover tomorrow; therefore for this matter too we had to establish it on a Torah basis that it is nullified in the majority; therefore he had to cook first yesterday's peace offerings — if so he nullifies only one prohibition, namely what is forbidden to non-priests, but he does not nullify the prohibition of leftover, for the time of sin-offering and peace offerings is one time. Behold, it is impossible that they came up specifically, and nevertheless noten taam bar noten taam of heter is permitted does not require all this, for he nullifies only prohibition to non-priests, unlike leftover prohibition which needs no nullification at all since it is noten taam bar noten taam; only if you say according to Beit Yosef's explanation of Tosafot's view that hagalah does not help in such a case except in the Temple, since such hagalah does not extract everything absorbed — and that which in the Temple hagalah applies, this is because in the Temple we establish it on a Torah basis; meaning, since it is absorbed in the pot up to the majority it nullifies it to absorb within the vessel — if so, he had to cook yesterday's peace offerings, for the absorbed matter was only the first taste, for after it changed we say it is noten taam bar noten taam, but before the absorbed matter emerged it was not noten taam bar noten taam; if so, if he cooked tomorrow's peace offerings he would nullify two prohibitions, etc., and he spoke well that he needs to cook first yesterday's peace offerings so as not to nullify two prohibitions; accordingly it is proven unlike Rashba, for if like Rashba who holds it is full hagalah, then also within the pot there is no absorption of leftover; if so, the challenge in practice why he needs to cook yesterday's peace offerings at all — all the more so as Beit Yosef wrote to Tosafot — requires study.
