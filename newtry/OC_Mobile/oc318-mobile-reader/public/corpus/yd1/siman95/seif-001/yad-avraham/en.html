(Siman 95 seif 1) Fish that were cooked, etc., in a meat pot — if it was not well washed it is forbidden to eat them with kutach. One must say that because of the opening he mentioned kutach and not specifically — for likewise it is forbidden even by itself if there is not sixty against the grease of the meat because of danger, as below siman 116 seif 2, see there; some say even sixty does not help, for danger is stricter than prohibition. And see what he wrote in siman 89 that if one ate them by themselves it is permitted to eat cheese immediately afterward even if there was not sixty.
(There) And if a dairy bowl is found among meat vessels — we are not concerned lest they were rinsed together in the forbidden manner. Even if they are accustomed in that house to rinse in a kli rishon, they are permitted through end of seif — doubt they were not rinsed with them; and if you say they were rinsed, perhaps it was not ben yomo — Issur VeHeter, Taz; it appears to me for practical halachah that here he speaks after they removed the vessels from the cauldron in which they were rinsed inside it — the bowl is found at the end; for there is concern perhaps it was also beforehand when it was rinsed with the vessels, for from time to time we establish prohibition; and for this reason one may not permit except through end of seif mentioned; and if there is only one doubt, such as that it is certainly ben yomo, one must forbid the vessels, lest it was already in the cauldron; but if the bowl is found among the vessels after they removed them from the cauldron and moved them elsewhere — even if it is certainly ben yomo it is permitted, for we do not establish prohibition from place to place to say that in the cauldron it was rinsed with them; rather we say: here it is found and here it already was, and it was not rinsed with them, only that afterward it became mixed with both of them; and such a case Orach Chaim siman 467 seifim 11 and 12 in the gloss and in Magen Avraham; and with this the wonder of Tosafot in Tractate Taharot chapter 7 is resolved; and so in Tosafot Chadashim; and see what he wrote siman 104. And in vain Peri Chadash and Peri Megadim were forced in this, as in Sefer Tanya siman 104; and so I found in Sefer Binyamin Zeev who wrote in name of Hagahot ShaDa the reason we do not establish from place to place, and he wrote that for this reason even if it is ben yomo one need not forbid, but nevertheless he was stringent to purge them where possible and in every case to wait until twenty-four hours pass: however requires study in this, for what is end of seif — the second doubt lest it was not ben yomo — is not effective, for nevertheless stam it was soiled until there was sixty against it until one says it is clear to me it was not soiled, as Shulchan Aruch wrote seif 3 and Rosh and Darkei Moshe in name of Issur VeHeter, and so Shach in this siman; and doubt of accustomed practice is close to certainty as proven in Tosafot first chapter of Pesachim (daf 9) s.v. ve'im and first chapter of Ketubot daf 11 s.v. veAva; and see siman 84 and Taz end of seif 122 — more likely to say it was soiled, for otherwise why rinse, as Shach wrote; and it appears to me nevertheless it is end of seif doubt they were not rinsed with them, and doubt it was not ben yomo, and all is noten taam lefegem; and even though stam it was soiled — perhaps something that spoils was cooked in it while visible, and it is also nat bar lichtmile, for it is a full doubt that stam vessels are not ben yomo, as the poskim and Taz siman 122 seif 6 wrote; and further one may say perhaps ash that spoils while visible was placed in the vessel, as Shulchan Aruch wrote seif 4; and I again saw that Maharam in Lublin end of general rule 58 sensed this, and he wrote Issur VeHeter speaks when it is known it was clean; and according to what he wrote, likewise in stam it is end of seif, and in such a case also doubt of accustomed practice is considered doubt, as Maharam in Lublin wrote general rule of taste, and for this reason they counted sixty also lest not ben yomo, investigate; and so he himself in Salt for Minchah general rule 66 that when not ben yomo it is permitted for stam there was sixty — and until here his meaning is as he wrote, investigate. And requires study, for in siman 122 seif 10 Mechaber wrote that if an Israelite sent a vessel with food to a non-Jew — if the food he sent is not recognizable it is forbidden, for we are concerned lest it was rinsed inside a forbidden vessel; and it is astonishing, for this contradicts what the gloss wrote here — how is it worse if it was found with forbidden vessels, that according to the gloss here one may not forbid, for it is end of seif doubt it was not rinsed at all; and if you say it was rinsed inside a forbidden vessel, perhaps the non-Jew cooked in it something that spoils while visible, or perhaps the non-Jew placed ash when rinsing — and the gloss did not gloss anything there; however Turei Chayim end of general rule of taste explains that there it is considered certainly rinsed with forbidden vessels.
