Noten taam. Be'er Heitev; and see in Peri Megadim in Meshivtzot s.k. 1 who wrote in name of Shaarei Dura that noten taam bar noten taam applies only in a vessel but not in food; and see in responsum of Pnei Aryeh siman 48 and 49 who expanded to prove that this version in ShaDa that Peri Megadim brought is a corrupted version, and in truth the law is that in food too we say noten taam bar noten taam, and it is a fortiori from a vessel; nevertheless he wrote regarding a barley dish into which butter was placed about one in forty of the dish that they stirred with a spoon, and afterward the spoon was stuck into a meat pot that does not have sixty against the butter that fell into the barley dish — forbidden; not only for butter placed in the barley lump and the like where we do not judge noten taam bar noten taam in it, for it is impossible that all the butter entered inside the barley without any remaining on the surface of the barley outside, and if so the spoon absorbed from that filth that is in substance; but even if the dish were all gravy it is forbidden, for they did not permit noten taam bar noten taam except in a vessel or in a piece that absorbed meat or milk where the absorbed matter clings and adheres to it, but in gravy where absorption does not apply but only mixture — there is no noten taam bar noten taam there, see there his proofs; and it appears to me to permit that meat pot, and not because of noten taam bar noten taam but because Taz wrote in siman 92 s.k. 24 that one need not estimate except according to calculation — the diluted does not dilute, etc.; and if so here one need not have sixty against all the butter but against one part in forty of the spoon, and this is simple. And see in Chokhmat Adam who also wrote that we do not say noten taam bar noten taam at the time of cooking — such as if milk fell on the pot in which they cook fish against the gravy and afterward they cooked the fish with the meat — even b'dieved it is forbidden if there was not sixty in the meat against the milk that fell first on the pot, and we do not say it is noten taam bar noten taam, for since it fell at the time of cooking and cooking spreads throughout the vessel stringently and the absorbed matter is bound with the dish — we consider it as if it fell into the dish and it is one taste, see there; and see in responsum of Beit Efrayim Yoreh De'ah part 2 siman 37 in the middle of the responsum who challenged him and raised that even at the time of cooking we say noten taam bar noten taam, see there; and see what he wrote below siman 97.
