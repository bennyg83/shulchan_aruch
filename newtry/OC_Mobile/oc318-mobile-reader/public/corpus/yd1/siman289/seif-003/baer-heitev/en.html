The hinge. Taz writes that where it is uncertain which side is considered the right for entry and which for exit, we follow the side indicated by the hinge. From this one can derive the law of a doorway from a house into a courtyard. If the courtyard is enclosed on all sides and has no opening other than from the house, the courtyard is treated like a room and the hinge indication does not control. The mezuzah is placed on the right when entering the courtyard from the house. But if the courtyard also has an opening to another alley or to the public domain, then we follow the hinge indication: whichever side contains the hinge is treated as the interior, and the mezuzah is placed on the right when entering that area. Responsa Maharil further discusses a winter room and a large room, each opening to the courtyard, with a small room between them having one doorway to each. For the small room's doorways we follow their respective hinge directions. If the hinge of the doorway to the winter room is in the winter room, and the hinge of the doorway to the large room is in the large room, one places the two mezuzot respectively on the right when entering the winter room and on the right when entering the large room from the small room. If both hinges are within the small room, both mezuzot are placed on the right when entering the small room, whether from the winter room or the large room. If the large room had no opening to the courtyard, we would not follow the hinge indication for the small room; rather, the small room would be treated as part of the large room. One would then place a mezuzah on the right when entering the small room from the winter room and on the right when entering the large room from the small room. Levush writes that for the doorway through which we enter from the house into our winter room, the mezuzah is placed on the right when entering from the house into the winter room, because our principal use is in the winter room. We disregard the hinge entirely. Whether it is outside or inside, the mezuzah is always placed on the right of entry. Shach writes that this is obvious. See siman 40 and siman 50.
