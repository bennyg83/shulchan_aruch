To freedom. The Shach wrote in the name of the Perishah: it appears that the same applies if others gave money to the slave on condition that the master have no authority over it, but that he eat and dress from it; the master also does not acquire it, as above siman 222 regarding one who gives money to his daughter on condition that her husband have no authority over it. According to the Rambam, who ruled there that if he said, "on condition that your husband have no authority over it, and whatever you wish you may do with it," this is effective, the same applies here by a slave. But the Taz wrote that it is not comparable, for a woman acquires the principal and the husband merely consumes the fruits, whereas a slave has no hand at all, neither in the principal nor in the fruits. If so, immediately when he says to the slave, "I hereby give you a gift," it is as if he said it to the master himself, and he can no longer retract from the master. Therefore these formulations do not help with a slave, except specifically when he says, "on condition that you go free with them." See the Shach.
And from his master. The Shach wrote: it appears to me that there is a scribal error in this gloss, and this is how it should read: but he may receive a gift from another for his master, and from another for another; but from his master for another, since the hand of a slave is like the hand of his master, the recipient does not acquire it.
