On condition that he use it to obtain freedom. When the slave gives the money to his master, he thereby goes free. Tur. This is explained below, seif 26.
That he use it to obtain freedom. In that case the giver does not grant the money to the slave outright, for he said only that it be used to obtain freedom. This excludes a gift “on condition that your master have no rights in it,” for since he first gives it to the slave and merely limits the master's rights, the condition is ineffective, since a slave's hand is like his master's. It appears that the same applies when others give a slave money on condition that the master have no rights in it and that the slave use it only for food and clothing: the master does not acquire it, as above, siman 222, regarding money given to one's daughter on condition that her husband have no rights in it. According to the Rambam there, if the giver adds, “and do with it whatever you wish,” the condition is effective; the same applies to a slave, because the gift is not fully placed in his possession until he chooses how to use it. See the Perishah and Even HaEzer there.
And from his master to another. Bach writes in his later glosses that this is difficult, because a slave's hand is like his master's. If the master gives a gift to another through his slave, it has not left the master's possession and the recipient does not acquire it. This is explicit in Choshen Mishpat 243:14 and in the Mishnah at the end of Eruvin, chapter Chalon. The text of the gloss must therefore contain a scribal error and should read: “He may receive a gift from another for his master, and from another for another.” This is explicit in Semag, positive commandment 87, in the Yerushalmi, and in Hagahot Maimoniyot, chapter 5 of Hilchot Avadim. The text there also requires correction. See there.
