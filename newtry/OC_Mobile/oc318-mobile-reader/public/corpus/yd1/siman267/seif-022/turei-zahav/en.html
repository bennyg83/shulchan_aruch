On condition that your master have no authority over it, etc. If the donor first says, “Acquire,” the slave's master immediately acquires the gift, and a later condition that the master have no rights contradicts the act and is ineffective. The condition is effective only when the donor says that the money is given so the slave may purchase his freedom, for the slave never acquires it for himself; it is given to the master as redemption money. This differs from a gift to a married woman. A woman has an independent capacity to acquire the principal, while her husband ordinarily receives only the fruits. The donor may therefore define the gift as being solely for what she places in her mouth or for whatever use she chooses. A slave has no independent hand against his master, in either principal or fruits, and his master is not obligated to support him. Expressions effective for a wife therefore do not automatically work for a slave. Only an express condition that the money be used for his freedom is effective.
