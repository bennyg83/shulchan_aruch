On condition that your master has no authority in him etc. For when he first said "acquire" — his master acquired and the condition "your master has no authority" no longer helps, for it is as one who gives a gift to a friend and says on condition it not be yours — he should have said "acquire"; for any condition contradicting the act is not a condition — so Rosh first chapter of Kiddushin. And that which helps when saying on condition you go free with them — they say in gemara the reason is also for him he did not acquire except "on condition you go free with them," meaning he will give them in your redemption money; and it appears even though in siman 222 we say also if giving money on condition the husband has no authority it does not help, nevertheless there it helps if saying "what you give is for your mouth" and for Rambam it also helps if saying "whatever you wish to do with them, do" — here these expressions do not help except specifically "on condition you go free with them," for a slave is different who has no hand at all without the master in principal or fruits; thus immediately when he said to the slave "behold I give you a gift" it is as saying to the master himself and he cannot retract from the master — as Rosh wrote. If so, what helps "what you give is for your mouth" — behold it contradicts what he first said it be the master's and afterward says it be the slave's; not so when saying on condition you go free with them at the end — he does not want it be the slave's but the master's only that for this he releases him to freedom. But regarding a woman she indeed has hand — whoever gives a gift to a woman she acquires the body only the husband eats fruits as Even HaEzer siman 85; therefore "what you give is for your mouth" helps, for if he said on condition your husband has no authority it does not help since the husband already acquired rights in fruits when she acquired principal — implying fruits remain only husband not enjoy them — which does not help; not so "what you give is for your mouth" hinting at what he said "behold yours in gift" that she enjoy and not make fruits. All this does not apply to a slave who has no hand at all — therefore they did not say in gemara one expression for woman and slave. And know further distinction woman vs slave: husband's right only if father gives money to daughter, not maintenance — as Ran Nedarim (88) brought siman 222, because husband is obligated in wife's maintenance and clothing; therefore "what you give is for your mouth" needed there specifically money; but for slave certainly no distinction for master is not obligated to feed his slave — if so what helps "what you give is for your mouth" when even what he puts in his mouth or covers himself the master can take? Therefore it does not help for slave except saying he goes free with them per gemara; also Tosafot first chapter of Kiddushin (24) distinguish woman and slave in name of R' Yitzchak; even Rabbenu Tam who does not distinguish there and brings proof from woman to slave — each gets its expressions as Tosafot wrote: learn Ran holds "on condition you go free" helps like "what you give is for your mouth" — end their words. But expressions said regarding woman do not help for slave — no posek ever thought otherwise per what we saw; Derishah wrote from his own reasoning those expressions helping woman in siman 222 also help slave — he was not precise.
