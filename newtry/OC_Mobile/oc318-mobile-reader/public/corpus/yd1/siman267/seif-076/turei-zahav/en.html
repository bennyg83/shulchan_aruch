When the master says, “Acquire this bill for my slave,” the recipient acquires it for the slave through ordinary lifting or possession, even without a hand-to-hand transfer. Hand-to-hand delivery is required only where the master says, “Give,” according to the view that “give” is not fully equivalent to “acquire” unless the document is actually delivered to the agent.
He cannot retract. The Rambam holds that the word “give” is equivalent to “acquire” at least to the extent that the master cannot retract, even regarding a gift. The same ruling appears in Choshen Mishpat, siman 125.

In emancipation, however, if the master physically hands the document to the agent, “give” functions like “acquire,” because freeing a slave resembles repayment of a debt. A master ordinarily frees a slave only in return for some benefit previously received.
