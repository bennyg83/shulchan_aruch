Even if she is his maidservant, the child is a slave. This is where the circumstances are unspecified. If his conduct indicates that he had relations with her for marriage, the child is free, for he certainly intended to release her; otherwise he would have engaged in a prohibited relationship. This is implied by the Maggid Mishneh.
And some say, etc. We presume that he had relations with her for marriage, because a person does not ordinarily make his relations promiscuous. His intention was therefore to release her, for otherwise he would have committed a prohibition.
And there is one who says, etc. Although we follow an assessment of a person's intent to remove monetary ownership, we do not rely on such an assessment in a matter of prohibition to permit her without a bill of release. Tur in the name of Ramah.
