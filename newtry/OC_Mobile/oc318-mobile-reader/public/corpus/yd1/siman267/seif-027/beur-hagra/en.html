And he requires, etc. See Be'er HaGolah. Although it is stated there that “those who arbitrate, etc.,” it is stated in chapter 3 of Shabbos, folio 40a, and see Tosafos there, s.v. “perhaps,” Rashba there, and Tosafos in Menachos, folio 31b, that the halachah follows an arbitrating opinion only in a Mishnah, not in a baraisa. This is the view of the Rif and the other decisors. See Tosafos there, folio 40a, s.v. “the Tanna, etc.” So too in the Yerushalmi there, cited by Rashba; see the Rosh.
(Addendum) And he requires a writ of emancipation. The halachah follows Rabbi Akiva against his colleague, for Rabbi Tarfon was his colleague, as stated at the conclusion of chapter HaKosev, Kesubos 84b. The halachah does not follow those who arbitrate, because the rule that the halachah follows an arbitrating opinion was said only regarding a Mishnah, as stated in chapter 3 of Shabbos. This is the view of the Rif, and the Rosh and Ran likewise wrote this. They added that it is not called arbitration unless the distinction is mentioned in their own words, as in the case there where they mentioned hot and cold, and in the case of cloth patches where they mentioned a frame and a door. This accords with Tosafos in Shabbos, folio 40a, s.v. “the halachah, etc.” See Tosafos there, folio 29a, s.v. “between, etc.” Likewise, an unattributed Mishnah follows Rabbi Akiva, as Tosafos wrote and as stated there. So too in the Yerushalmi: Why did we not teach that he goes free for a tooth, an eye, or exposed limbs that do not regenerate? Rabbi Yochanan bar Mari said: Because of the dispute. Some teach that he requires a writ of emancipation, and some teach that he does not. Rashba and Ran cite this. Ran wrote that nowadays even the opinion that ordinarily does not require a writ of emancipation agrees that one is required, because ordained judges are needed, since this is a law of a fine, as stated in chapter Merubah, folios 74b to 75a, that one who admits it himself is exempt. It is stated there that he goes free upon appearance before beis din, not from the moment of injury. Nowadays the master is compelled to emancipate him, as stated in seif 40. End of addendum.
