The slave's consent is unnecessary, etc. He goes free even against his will, according to the Tur and the other authorities. Ran writes that Rif and Rambam hold this only where the slave does not consent, but not where he expressly objects. With a document, all agree that he cannot be released against his will, as stated below, seif 41.
And some say that it also effects acquisition. Tosafot and Rashba write in the name of Rabbeinu Chananel and Rambam that exchange effects acquisition only when given as payment. Tur cites Ramah: exchange removes monetary servitude but not the ritual prohibition, unless the exchanged object is worth a perutah and is given as payment. Regarding ordinary exchange, it is uncertain whether it is treated as money and removes the prohibition, because a gift on condition that it be returned is considered a gift, or whether it is unlike money, just as exchange does not effect kiddushin even when the object has value. We therefore rule stringently and compel the master, or his heirs if he died, to write a bill of release, as in the case of one who declares his slave ownerless. See Choshen Mishpat, simanim 202 and 203, regarding acquisition by exchange.
