He has said nothing. If he says to a pregnant maidservant, “You are free and your fetus remains a slave,” the statement may be understood as freedom taking effect for the mother when she gives birth, and it is therefore valid. If he says, “Your fetus is free and you remain a slave,” the fetus cannot acquire freedom independently before birth, and treating it as acquiring through its mother would amount to freeing half of the mother. The distinction is not that a fetus is sometimes considered part of the mother and sometimes not, but that the first statement can operate upon the mother at a future time, whereas the second attempts to transfer freedom to an entity not yet independently capable of acquisition.
