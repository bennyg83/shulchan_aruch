And his master cannot remove him. In the Tur it is written "and his master cannot redeem him," which implies at first glance that there is an obligation to redeem him if possible — and this is puzzling, for he cast himself into the non-Jew's hands on his own. Rather, one must say the Tur spoke in the ordinary manner, meaning: if the Jew is unable to profit from this and give some money for him — for certainly he would not give his full value, since he can buy another in the market — but specifically for less than his value; and then certainly the Jew does so willingly. In such a case he says: if it is not possible for him [to redeem for less], and he therefore wants to receive his full value — it is permitted.
