But she may not acquire male slaves, etc. The Gemara makes clear that certainly she may not acquire a Hebrew slave, because he is regarded as discreet in her eyes and, if they sin together, he will not boast and reveal the matter. Bach writes that although the law of a Hebrew slave does not apply today, we learn from this that because of suspicion a woman may not hire a Jewish male servant for a fixed term. It appears that the Sages prohibited this even for a married woman, for the ruling speaks of any woman, just as they prohibited every woman from teaching children, as stated above, siman 245, seif 21.
Even minors, etc. Since the Mechaber does not distinguish, he evidently follows the Tur that even one younger than nine, who is not fit for relations, is prohibited, lest she retain him after he matures. This is unlike the Rambam. Bach writes likewise.
