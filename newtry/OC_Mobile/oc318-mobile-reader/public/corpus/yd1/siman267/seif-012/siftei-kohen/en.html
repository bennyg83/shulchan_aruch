And afterward he recites the blessing, etc. Perishah writes in the name of Maharshal that this blessing requires a cup, as above, beginning of siman 265, regarding circumcision. Bach agrees and adds that because this blessing stands in place of “to bring him into the covenant of Avraham Avinu,” the slave's master should recite it. Perishah, however, explains that the Tur's generic phrase, “the one reciting the blessing says,” means that whoever recites the blessing over the cup may say it. See there.
And to draw from them blood of the covenant. Were it not for the blood of the covenant, the ordinances of heaven and earth would not endure, as it is stated, “If not for My covenant day and night, I would not have established the ordinances of heaven and earth.” “Blessed are You, Hashem, Who establishes the covenant,” is the text of the conclusion. Bach writes that since Rosh did not record this conclusion, he apparently did not have it in his text, and in practice, due to the dispute among the authorities, one should not conclude with that blessing. Perishah writes that Rosh omitted it because it was obvious. This also resolves Bach's question why the Mechaber below, siman 268, seif 5, did not record the conclusion; it was self-evident.
