And likewise a maidservant, etc. This wording implies that even an ordinary maidservant who is treated licentiously must be released. The Tur, however, implies that coercion applies specifically to one who is half maidservant and half free woman, who is fit neither for a slave nor for a free man, but not to an ordinary maidservant who is fit for a slave. The Gemara, Gittin 43b, likewise recounts specifically a woman who was half maidservant and half free woman and was treated licentiously. If licentious treatment alone compelled release even for an ordinary maidservant, the Gemara should have stated that essential fact rather than her half-free status. Rather, the coercion was specifically because she was half free. Ravina's ruling in Gittin 38 that an ordinary maidservant may be released means only that her master is permitted to release her, not that he is compelled. Rabbeinu Yerucham in Meisharim, netiv 13, follows Rambam and the Mechaber.
