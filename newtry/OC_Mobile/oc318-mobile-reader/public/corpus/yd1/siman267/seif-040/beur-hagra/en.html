Release of, etc. As written at end of chapter 5 of Bava Kamma and at end of chapter 3 of Ketubot "and he seized," etc.; and slave seized in himself.
And there is who, etc. As Rif wrote in chapter 8 of Bava Kamma; and as written in Choshen Mishpat end of siman 61.
And the same applies, etc. Meaning, if there are witnesses, even though he initially admitted. Although we rule in Bava Kamma, folios 74b to 75a, that one who admits a fine and witnesses later come is exempt, this requires an admission before ordained judges who adjudicate fines, as stated there: Rabban Gamliel is different, because it was not before, etc. Nowadays there is no ordained beis din. See the Rosh at the end of the first chapter of Bava Kamma. But through his admission alone, without witnesses, no obligation for a fine exists at all and the slave does not go free, as stated there: “for you no longer have witnesses.” Although he admitted outside beis din and could not free him through an admission before two or three unordained judges, the Yerushalmi at the end of chapter 3 of Kesubos asks whether they tell him, “Fulfill your obligation before Heaven.” It proves this from an incident with Rabban Gamliel, who knocked out the tooth of Tavi his slave. He came before Rabbi Yehoshua and said: Tavi my slave, I have found a pretext to free him. Rabbi Yehoshua said: What proof do you have? Fines are imposed only by beis din and witnesses. Should they tell him, “Fulfill your obligation before Heaven”? This teaches that they do not tell him to fulfill it before Heaven. Rashba likewise wrote this in Bava Kamma there, and it appears there that even with witnesses he is not obligated even to satisfy Heaven, from the comparison there to rabbinic interest. See Choshen Mishpat, siman 87, seif 26; the Shach likewise wrote this there.
