Since the release through loss of a limb is a fine requiring ordained judges, contemporary courts do not adjudicate it or receive testimony for that purpose. Nevertheless, neither side may compel the other. The master cannot continue enslaving him merely because the court lacks authority, for the slave may present witnesses and say that the court's inability to hear them does not restore the master's right. Conversely, the slave cannot compel the master to write a bill of emancipation. The proper course is nonaction: the master does not enslave him and the slave does not compel a document. If the master admits the injury, the Raavad holds that the admission is effective nowadays, while the Rambam disagrees. The practical details follow the rules governing admissions in fines.
