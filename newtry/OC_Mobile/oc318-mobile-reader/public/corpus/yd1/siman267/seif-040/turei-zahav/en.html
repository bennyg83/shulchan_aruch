That they excommunicate him, etc. It does not appear thus Tur's opinion who wrote it is a fine and requires expert court; therefore they do not judge him now since they do not accept testimony to judge him; and if he admits himself, etc. — seemingly his language wonders and is superfluous — why need he give reason since they do not accept testimony after he already wrote experts are required; rather it appears he intended not to say that which the slave in himself is like other seizures in fine laws as written at beginning of Choshen Mishpat if the injured party seized they accept testimony; and so arose on Beit Yosef's mind who wrote a slave since he is seized in himself they need accept testimony to know whether his master will enslave him or not; and Tur does not hold thus — different there testimony acceptance is to return the permitted from damage, for in this we do not judge fine laws as written there in Choshen Mishpat in Rosh's name — and this is not relevant here; and further there is distinction between slave-release laws and other fine laws — in other fines when we do not judge we mean we do not extract money from damager to injured; unlike here the damager wants to extract from injured — meaning the slave is established in himself and this comes to enslave him because we do not judge fine laws — this is impossible the slave will say behold my testimony before you and shows them only you do not wish to accept — do not compel me to be enslaved gratuitously; and likewise conversely the slave cannot compel his master to write him bill of release — for this too is called extracting from another; therefore we say here sitting and not doing is preferable and one has no coercion on the other — this one will not be enslaved and this one will not write him bill of release; and this is well intended in Tur's language "therefore they do not judge him now" — and lest we err to say he is obligated to be enslaved to him as initially — for this he said behold they do not accept testimony to judge him — meaning they do not hear testimony to make Torah judgment to extract from another what he is established in; unlike here one has no power to extract from another — the slave will say behold I have witnesses and master will say I will not accept since there are no experts, only if master admits then exempt and slave must be enslaved — so Raavad holds; and on this Rambam disputes there in Tur and holds admission does not help now since there are no experts; and he brought proof from what R' Yehoshua said to Rabban Gamliel "your words are nothing," etc. — and Rabban Gamliel's admission was outside court as Beit Yosef wrote; and Beit Yosef resolved certainly Raavad too knew this Gemara of Rabban Gamliel and R' Yehoshua — rather he says admission helps when there is expert court and seizure of injured party does not help, etc.; and I say it escaped the rabbi Beit Yosef Nekudot Yosef's words in chapter Merubah who wrote explicitly in Raavad's words he is exempt only in our time — see there. And it appears to me to resolve Ramban's challenge — Raavad's reason admission helps: that which Gemara says admission outside court does not help — that is in Rabban Gamliel and R' Yehoshua's time when they were experts and could obligate in fine laws — then admission not before court did not help since he could confess before experts and did not do so; unlike now the master is exempt he can say since there are no experts in the world you have no permission to leave me for tooth and eye as Torah law since I am exempt also according to Torah law one who admits to fine is exempt — and what shall I do that there are no experts from my side; it would not be prevented if there were experts I would also confess before them — as above in Raavad's reason; and it is correct in my opinion; and similarly I wrote in Choshen Mishpat siman 1 — see there.
