Although it is a fine. Monetary fines are not adjudicated today, because they require ordained judges, as explained in Choshen Mishpat, siman 1. Nevertheless, the slave's freedom applies today, because it is explained there that if the claimant seized the amount, it is not removed from him even today, and the slave is considered to have seized himself, since his body is in his own possession.
If witnesses came regarding the matter, etc. Since no distinction is made, this implies that even if the master admitted before the witnesses came, we do not apply the exemption of one who admits to a fine, because today's courts are not expert courts and his admission before them has no legal force. Bach discusses this at length. His conclusion is uncertain whether the admission is treated as outside beit din or as before beit din; accordingly, the master cannot be compelled to write a bill of release. See there and my discussion below of Rambam's view.
