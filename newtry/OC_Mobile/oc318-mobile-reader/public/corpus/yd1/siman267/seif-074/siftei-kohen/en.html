But if he wrote. The Bach wrote: it appears that in practice we follow stringency in a dispute of the great authorities. If he wrote these inferior expressions only in a document, his master cannot force him to serve, but we also do not force his master to write him a bill of emancipation, and he is forbidden to a Jewish woman, unlike what is written in the Shulchan Aruch, that he goes free.
But a shekhiv mera, etc. This is the language of the Derishah: the Beit Yosef wrote, of blessed memory, that it appears to speak where the shekhiv mera recovered, etc. But this is difficult, for in the Shulchan Aruch the Beit Yosef himself wrote, “But a shekhiv mera, etc., he goes free and requires a bill of emancipation to permit him, and we compel the heirs to write it for him.” Thus his words explicitly contradict each other and require study; end quote. The Bach wrote: one should wonder at the Beit Yosef, for it implies that he holds that when the shekhiv mera died, no bill of emancipation is needed, but this is not so, as is explicit in the Rambam’s words in chapter 9 of Hilkhot Zekhiyah, where he wrote that we compel the heirs to free him; I brought it nearby. The Beit Yosef himself also brought the Rambam’s words on this here in Sefer Bedek HaBayit, etc., and so he ruled in the Shulchan Aruch, retracting from what he wrote in the Beit Yosef and in Bedek HaBayit; end quote.
