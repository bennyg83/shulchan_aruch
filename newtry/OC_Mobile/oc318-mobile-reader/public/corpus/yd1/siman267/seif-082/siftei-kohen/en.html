And he loses the money. Even if the purchaser has not yet paid, but acquired the slave through pulling, lifting, or possession, he must pay the seller despite being required to release the slave. Tur in the name of Ramah. The reason is that where a prohibition is involved, the Sages penalize the party in whose hands the prohibition presently rests. Since the purchaser already acquired him, the prohibition is in the purchaser's hands, and he is penalized. The purchase price is a debt that he must pay.
We do not heed him. We are concerned that he may persuade the slave to follow him outside Eretz Yisrael. The Gemara asks about a resident of the Diaspora who married a woman in Eretz Yisrael, intended to return, and she brought slaves into the marriage: is this considered as though she sold them outside Eretz Yisrael? The question was unresolved, so we rule leniently and the slaves do not go free. Rambam and the Tur write likewise.
