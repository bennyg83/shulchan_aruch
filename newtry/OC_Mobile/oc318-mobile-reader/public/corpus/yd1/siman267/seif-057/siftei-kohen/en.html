One who writes all his property, etc. Ran writes that the word “all” is essential. Although a slave is called property, when a master gives property to his slave, the language does not imply both a bill of release and a gift unless he explicitly says, “all my property.”
Whether he had other property, etc. If he retained a house or field, it is obvious that the slave does not go free, because we say that just as he retained that house or field, he also retained a share in the slave, and “all my property” refers to the remaining property. Even if he owned only that house or field, so that the exclusion can refer only to the slave, he still does not go free, because the document is not one of complete severance.
And even if he wrote, “yourself,” etc., except for a certain field, etc. Tosafot and Rosh hold that in such a case he does go free. Both opinions are explained in Even HaEzer.
