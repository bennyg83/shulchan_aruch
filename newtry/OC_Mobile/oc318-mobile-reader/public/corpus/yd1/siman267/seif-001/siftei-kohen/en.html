And they are circumcised only by day. Even those who are circumcised on the first day may be circumcised only by day; this is implied by the Rambam. Bach. He further wrote that it requires study whether three judges are required for the circumcision of slaves or for their immersion. After a lengthy discussion, he concludes that initially the immersion should always take place before three, both for immersion into servitude and for immersion upon release, in accordance with the Rambam; likewise, circumcision should be before three, in accordance with the Tur, for circumcision too requires a judicial proceeding.
If one acquired, etc., he is circumcised on the first day. This applies even though his mother immersed before giving birth. Tur. Bach writes that the same applies even if she immersed before becoming pregnant, such as an Israelite's maidservant, because the reason is that the case is not comparable to the phrase “for you,” since the purchaser has no share in the mother.
How so? If one acquired a maidservant, etc. This implies that even if she did not immerse, the child is circumcised on the eighth day, since she gave birth in his house and the child is comparable to one born in the household. This is also written by the Ateret Zahav.
If he acquired a maidservant for her offspring. He has no ownership in the body of the maidservant, only in her offspring.
And if his mother immersed afterward, etc. The Kesef Mishneh writes that this does not refer back to the case of one who acquired a maidservant for her offspring. In that case, whether she gave birth and afterward immersed, or immersed and afterward gave birth, the child is circumcised on the first day, because the reason is that ownership of the produce is not equivalent to ownership of the body. Rather, it refers to the immediately preceding case, where one acquired a maidservant on condition not to immerse her. This is also implied by the Beit Yosef.
