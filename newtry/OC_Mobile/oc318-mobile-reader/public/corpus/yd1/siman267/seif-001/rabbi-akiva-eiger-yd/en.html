(siman 267 s.k. 1) It is a mitzvah on the court to circumcise him. Since he did not write "and if the court does not circumcise him he is obligated to circumcise himself" — it implies there is no obligation on the slave; for just as a woman is not obligated, a slave too is not obligated (and even for Rambam chapter 12 of Hilchot Avodah Zarah that a slave is warned regarding rounding the head — one can say here since she is exempt from circumcising her son even though it applies to her, it is like other mitzvot a woman is not obligated and a slave too is exempt). And behold from Tosafot Bava Kamma (88a) s.v. sheken einah b'milah, etc., and some explain she is not commanded to circumcise her son — if so it implies what we say "say regarding a slave who is fit to circumcise" means he is commanded in milah mitzvah; however one can say on the contrary it is proven inversely from that which they rejected — for if so what does it say "a minor will prove" and the challenge would be "just as a woman who is not in mitzvot" — no need "a minor will prove"; therefore a slave too is not in milah mitzvot but is fit to circumcise; and Perishah's words are thus and some explain she is not commanded in her son's milah; and let us say truly the Gemara holds a slave has milah mitzvah upon him and they rejected — for if so what does it say "a minor will prove" — meaning a slave too is not commanded in milah but the obligation is only on the master — end. And truly I wonder at that Rambam — why do we not challenge from woman and minor "just as those who are not commanded in the mitzvah" — say regarding a slave who is commanded (later I found somewhat in Sefer Sha'ar HaMelekh Hilchot Milah — see there who brought from Gufei Halakhot from Tosfei Rosh on what Tosafot Bava Kamma wrote "if so what does it say minor will prove" — Maharam answered still to challenge thus "just as woman who is not commanded to circumcise her son" — no need minor will prove; and one can say a slave too is not commanded to circumcise his son — end quote); and simply it greatly wonders — for if so how did they want to explain the Gemara's challenge is woman is not commanded to circumcise her son — behold a slave too is not commanded to circumcise his son; or as our words above that Tosafot themselves were uncertain whether a slave is commanded to circumcise his son, therefore they wanted to explain the Gemara's challenge "just as woman who is not commanded to circumcise her son" — and it was truly necessary a slave is obligated to circumcise his son and they rejected "if so what does it say minor will prove" — therefore the Gemara's challenge is only that there is no milah mitzvah in woman herself; and if challenged nevertheless why do we not truly challenge "just as woman who is not commanded" — it is proven from this truly a slave is not commanded to circumcise his son — end.
