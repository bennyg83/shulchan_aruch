One born in the house, etc. A “homeborn” slave is one whose mother conceived and gave birth while owned by a Jew. An “acquisition of money” is a slave purchased by the Jew, whether already born or still in the mother's womb. The Torah mentions both categories twice concerning circumcision. One passage requires circumcision on the eighth day, like a Jewish infant, while the other requires circumcision immediately. The distinction depends upon whether the child was born under circumstances comparable to “for you,” meaning that the mother was fully within Jewish ownership and obligated in mitzvos. Thus, a purchased pregnant maidservant who gives birth in the owner's possession produces a child circumcised on the eighth day. If both mother and already-born child were purchased, the child is circumcised immediately. A homeborn child may also be circumcised immediately where the owner acquired only the future offspring but had no ownership in the mother, or where he purchased her on condition that she not immerse. R' Chama adds that eighth-day circumcision applies only if the mother had immersed before birth and was subject to the laws of birth impurity. The Rambam and Tur rule stringently in both directions: such infants are not circumcised before the eighth day, yet if the eighth day is Shabbos, the circumcision does not override Shabbos because perhaps the law follows R' Chama and this is not a circumcision at its proper time.
Even though he acquired the fetus separately. Since the fetus is in its mother's womb and the mother also belongs to him, it is regarded as born in his possession.
If its mother immersed after giving birth, etc. This formulation is from the Rambam. Once she immerses, the original condition that she not immerse is effectively nullified, and the purchase is treated as though it had been made without that condition.
