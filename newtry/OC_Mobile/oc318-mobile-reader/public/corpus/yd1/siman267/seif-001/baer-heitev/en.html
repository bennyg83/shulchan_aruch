By day. Bach questions whether three judges are required for a slave's circumcision or immersion. He concludes that initially both immersion into servitude and immersion upon emancipation should be before three, following Rambam, and that circumcision too should be before three because it has the character of judgment. Shach.
The fetus. Even if the mother immersed before giving birth, the child is circumcised on the first day rather than the eighth. Tur. Bach extends this even to a maidservant who immersed before conception.
And he acquired. Shach implies that even if she had not immersed, a child born in his household is circumcised on the eighth day because he is considered homeborn.
For her offspring. Meaning he acquired no ownership in the maidservant herself, only in her children.
She immersed. Kesef Mishneh explains that this clause does not refer to one who bought a maidservant only for her offspring. It refers to one who bought her on condition not to immerse her. Once she immersed, the condition was nullified and he is treated as having acquired the mother without restriction. Taz.
