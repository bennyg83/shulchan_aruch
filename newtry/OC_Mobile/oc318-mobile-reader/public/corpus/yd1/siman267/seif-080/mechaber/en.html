One who sells his slave to a gentile or ger toshav — he goes out to freedom, for if he flees from the gentile the master cannot enslave him again; and if he did not flee, they fine the owner to redeem him up to ten times his value and write him a bill of release to permit him to an Israelite woman; and this fine is not collected, judged only by expert court; and if the seller died, heirs are not fined to return the slave for freeing (there chapter 8 of Laws of Slaves): {Rama: If he borrowed money on him for a term that if not paid by a set time the slave is his — if he delivered him immediately to a gentile's hand, he goes out to freedom immediately; and if not delivered immediately to a gentile's hand, when the time comes and he does not pay, he goes out to freedom; but if gentiles collected the debt or violence came and he redeemed himself with it, he does not go out to freedom. If sold to the king's slave who regularly take slaves and animals from wherever found and give their value to owners — he goes out to freedom. If sold on condition to return after time, or except his labor, or except mitzvot, or sold to a Sadducee or apostate — doubt whether he goes out to freedom, and we follow leniency for rabbinic prohibition and he does not go out (all in Tur from Gemara); and some say seizure helps — if he seized from his master's property enough for his value to go out to freedom, gentiles do not remove from him (there in the name of Rambam halacha 8).}
