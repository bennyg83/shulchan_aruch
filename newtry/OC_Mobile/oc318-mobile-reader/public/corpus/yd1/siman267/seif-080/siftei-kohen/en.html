This fine is not collected, etc. Rosh questions what connection this has to fines that are not collected in Babylonia. Those are Torah-imposed fines that require ordained expert judges, who were not present in Babylonia. But if the Sages instituted a fine as a safeguard for the Torah, why should it not be collected in Babylonia? The Tur cites this question.
If he delivered him, etc. According to Rashi and the Tur, the same applies if the master did not place the slave in the non-Jew's possession but borrowed money against him on condition that, when the due date arrived, the creditor could seize the slave. If the time passed and the master did not pay, he is penalized and the slave goes free, even if the creditor did not actually seize him and he remains in the master's possession.
But if the non-Jew collected him for his debt. If this occurred against the master's will, or a violent person threatened to kill the master and he bribed him by giving the slave, the slave does not go free. There is no basis to penalize the master when the act was coerced.
Or except for his labor. Meaning, the master retains rights to his labor.
Or except for the mitzvos. Meaning, that the non-Jew not prevent him from observing any mitzvah.
To a Sadducee. The Tur and Rabbeinu Yerucham read “a Samaritan.” The Rama follows Ran, Rambam, and Beit Yosef, who explain that this applied only in Talmudic times; today Samaritans are regarded as complete non-Jews in every respect. He therefore writes “a Sadducee,” before the decree. Bach suggests that because this is a penalty, perhaps it was not imposed for sale to a Samaritan even after the decree, just as it was not imposed for sale to an apostate Israelite. His words are not compelling. It is more likely that Tur and Rabbeinu Yerucham merely stated the Talmudic law incidentally and relied on their rulings elsewhere that Samaritans later became equivalent to complete non-Jews in every respect. Similar usages are common among the authorities.
For it is a rabbinic prohibition. Meaning, the slave's freedom because of the sale is a rabbinic penalty imposed upon one who sells his slave.
And some say that seizure is effective, etc. The Rama uses the phrase “some say” because Raavad disagrees and holds that seizure is effective only where the claimant suffered a monetary loss.
