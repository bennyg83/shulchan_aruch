And this fine is not collected, etc. The Rosh wondered at this: what connection does this have to fines that are not collected in Babylonia? Those are fines imposed by the Torah, and they are not collected in Babylonia because expert judges are required and there is no semikhah in Babylonia. But if the Sages enacted a fine to make a fence for the Torah, why should it not be collected in Babylonia? The Tur brings this.
If he handed him over, etc. According to Rashi and the Tur, the same applies if he did not bring him into the non-Jew's domain, but borrowed against him on condition that when that time arrives the non-Jew would take the slave as collateral, and the time passed and he did not give him the money. Even though the non-Jew did not draw the slave, and he remains in the master's domain, they fine him and the slave goes free.
But if the non-Jew collected him for his debt. Against the master's will, or if a violent person rose against him to kill him and he bribed him by giving the slave, the slave does not go free, for there is no reason to fine him when it was done under compulsion.
Or except for his labor. Meaning that his labor remains for him.
Or except for the mitzvot. Meaning that the non-Jew should not prevent him from mitzvah observance.
To a Sadducee. In the Tur and Rabbeinu Yerucham the text says Kuti. The Rav followed the Ran, Rambam, and Beit Yosef, who wrote that this applied specifically in Talmudic times, but now that Kutim are like complete non-Jews for all matters, the same applies here. Therefore the Rav wrote Sadducee before the decree. The Bach wrote that the view of the Tur and Rabbeinu Yerucham is that since this is a fine, perhaps they did not fine regarding a Kuti even after the decree, just as with an apostate Jew. His words are not compelling. It seems more correct that even the Tur and Rabbeinu Yerucham speak of the Talmudic law and mentioned it only incidentally among other laws, but in truth they rely on what they wrote in many other places, that Kutim became like complete non-Jews for every matter. We find the same in several places in the poskim.
For it is a rabbinic prohibition. Meaning: the rule that he goes free because of the sale is a rabbinic fine, for they decreed that one who sells his slave causes him to go free.
And some say that seizure helps, etc. He wrote this as "some say" because the Raavad disagrees, holding that seizure helps only for one who lost money.
