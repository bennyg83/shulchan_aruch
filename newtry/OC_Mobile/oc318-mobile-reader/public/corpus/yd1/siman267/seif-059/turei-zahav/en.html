They did not acquire themselves. The verse says, “Freedom was not given to her,” in the singular, excluding one document written jointly for two slaves.
And so-and-so, my slave. The correct text includes the word “my slave,” which applies to both individuals, as though he wrote “my slaves.” Where one person receives two separate documents for two slaves, both must be transferred together when the wording would otherwise cause the first slave to acquire everything and leave the second enslaved to him.
But if he wrote half, etc. Rashi, the Rosh, and the Tur explain that two documents each granting half may refer to the same half and therefore leave a remainder. The Ramban explains that a gift of half of one's property does not necessarily confer half of every object, and the slave's own person may not be included. The Ran gives another reason: a slave becomes free only through the document delivered for him, and each individual document contains a remainder. The Shulchan Aruch follows the Rambam and Ramban rather than the Ran, for it validates language granting all the property jointly and also validates a simultaneous transfer of the other half.
