And if he seized we do not remove, etc. The reason — it was a question in the Gemara in chapter HaSholeach (Gittin 42) regarding one detained with a bill of release — meaning he was already fit to go out a freeman because a limb was missing but his master had not yet written him release; and in the meantime another injured him — does he give a fine and the fine to his master, or does he not give a fine at all; and we resolve from that which knocked out his tooth and blinded his eye he gives him eye's value; and if one thought he has a fine — if another injured him and gives to master, now another injured him the master receives; and if the master injured him how can we say he gives fine to the slave; and we reject it — perhaps that comes according to one who says he does not need a bill of release if a limb is missing; but according to what we rule he needs a bill of release — say likewise he does not give him eye's value; and the question was not resolved — therefore Rambam ruled chapter 4 of Chovel if the slave seized we do not remove from him by judgment "knocked tooth and blinded eye"; and in chapter 5 of Hilchot Avadim he ruled explicitly he gives him eye's value; and Raavad already challenged this there; and therefore Beit Yosef wrote Rambam did not specifically say thus in Hilchot Avadim that court rules thus, rather if he seized he speaks; and so one must say according to Tur who wrote here he gives him eye's value; and in Choshen Mishpat siman 424 he ruled it is a doubt. And behold in Maggid Oz he wrote to resolve — different here for in one act he struck his tooth and blinded his eye — with this it is obvious he gives eye's value; and when we are uncertain it is when there was interval between; and Maharshal praised and said here Baal Maggid Oz stood on the primary; and therefore he disputed Shulchan Aruch that he did not distinguish; and I say this primary is uprooting and damaging the exalted table — for if there is reasoning to say thus, why did the Talmud need to reject that "knocked tooth and blinded eye" comes not like halachah he does not need bill of release — it should have said different there he did both in one act as he explains Rambam's language — behold one language literally; only there is no scent or taste in this distinction as primary; and as Beit Yosef and Shulchan Aruch words — so is primary; and Beit Yosef did well not to bring Maggid Oz's words at all for they are in his eyes rejected matters and need not respond; and also what Beit Yosef wrote what Rambam wrote he gives eye's value — that is when he already wrote him bill of release — seemingly one can challenge why not establish in Gemara thus — this certainly impossible the tanna of mishnah need not say this for certainly he was like another; and Beit Yosef did not write it except hastily in Rambam's words and relied on the second resolution.
