One who acquires, etc. As written in Yevamos 48a.
And likewise a non-Jew, etc. Yevamos, folio 46a.
Or who sold, etc. As stated in the Torah: “and from their families whom they begot, etc.” Rambam. In Toras Kohanim: “From them you may purchase,” their sons and daughters, etc. From where do we know that they themselves may be purchased? The verse states, “whom they begot, etc.”
Rather, etc. The Gemara there. This means before he immersed; if he immersed for the sake of freedom, the law is as stated above in seif 9.
And in all of them, etc. Kerisos, folio 9a.
And after he immerses him. Meaning, together with circumcision, in accordance with the law of a convert.
And he is obligated, etc. The beginning of Chagigah, and this is understood there.
And through this, etc. In Torat Kohanim "you shall not rule over him with rigor," etc. ; lest you say since you forbade us all these things with what shall we use ; it teaches "and your male and female slaves from the nations," etc.
And even though the law, etc. As written in Yerushalmi; and Rif and Rosh brought in chapter HaChovel 66; and Maggid Mishneh there ; R' Yochanan would eat caper and give his slave two measures and give his slave and call upon him "did not he who made me in the womb make him," etc.
And he shall not shame him, etc. Niddah, folio 47a.
And he shall not increase, etc., but rather, etc. This is Rambam's wording. It is likewise explicit in the upright ways of Iyov, for which he praised himself: “If I rejected the judgment of my male servant or maidservant when they contended with me; did not He Who made me in the womb make him, etc.?” As stated in Bereishis Rabbah at the beginning of Parashas Vayeira: Rabbi Yosei's wife was arguing with her maidservant. He contradicted her before the maidservant. She said to him: Why do you contradict me before my maidservant? He said to her: Did Iyov not say, “If I rejected the judgment of my servant”?
