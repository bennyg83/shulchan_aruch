One who purchases a slave from a non-Jew. Even though he acquired him through the modes by which a slave is acquired.
His body is not acquired by him. He owns only the right to his labor. See below, seif 17.
Until he immerses him for the sake of servitude. Until then, his body is not acquired by him, and the slave can still make himself a free man. Thus, if he immersed for the sake of becoming a free man, he is free and requires no bill of release. The same applies if, before immersion, the master told him, “Go,” for no bill of release is required since the master has no ownership of his body. The Maggid Mishneh writes this in the name of Ramban and the French sages, and Beit Yosef and Darchei Moshe cite it.
He is a free man. There is no distinction whether he immersed in his master's presence or not, as implied by the Tur, following the reading of Rashi and Rosh. According to them, regarding a convert whose slaves preceded him and immersed before him, if they immersed before their master converted, they acquire freedom even without specifying their intent. If their master converted first and they immersed afterward, they become free only if they expressly immersed for freedom. Rif and Rambam distinguish even regarding an Israelite's slave: he becomes free only if he declares at immersion, “I am immersing before you for conversion.” If he immerses in his master's presence, however, he need not specify; an unspecified immersion releases him. Semag, negative commandment 117, writes likewise, as does Rashba in the responsum cited by Beit Yosef at the end of page 311.
