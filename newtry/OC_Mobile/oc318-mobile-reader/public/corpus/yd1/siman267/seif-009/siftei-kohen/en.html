One who purchases a slave from a non-Jew. Even though he acquired him through the modes by which a slave is acquired.
The body is not acquired to him. Rather, only his labor is acquired to him. See below seif 17.
Until he immerses him for the sake of servitude. For his body is not acquired to him, and thereafter he can no longer make himself a free man. And what is written "and if he immersed for the sake of freedom — behold he is a free man and does not need a bill of release" — and likewise if he said to him before immersion "go" — he does not need a bill of release, since he has no acquisition of the body. So wrote the Maggid Mishneh in the name of Ramban and our French rabbis; and Beit Yosef and Darchei Moshe bring this.
Behold he is a free man. And there is no distinction whether he immersed before his master or not before his master — so implies the Tur, based on the version of Rashi and Rosh. According to them, regarding a convert whose slaves preceded him and immersed before him, there is a distinction: if they immersed before their convert-master immersed, even without specification they acquired themselves as free men; but if their convert-master immersed first and afterward they immersed — if they specified that they are immersing for the sake of freedom, they acquired themselves as free men, but if they immersed without specification — not. But Rif and Rambam distinguish even regarding a Jew's slave — they do not acquire themselves as free men except when they declared at immersion "behold I immerse before you for the sake of conversion"; and if he immersed before his master he need not specify, for once he immersed without specification he was freed. And so Semag, negative commandment 117; and so Rashba in responsum which Beit Yosef brought at the end of daf 311.
