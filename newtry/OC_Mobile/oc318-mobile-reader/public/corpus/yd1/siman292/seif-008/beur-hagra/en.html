Was crouching, etc. — a question there, and it was not resolved. And see in the Gemara R' Elazar's dispute, etc.; and Rif omitted it and Rosh wondered about him — see there. And his words are strained in explaining Rif's words. Also Yesh Shenim strained himself and explained that certainly these two versions of R' Elazar disagree: in the first version where he says "but with female korah" meaning liable, implying that with an ordinary female meaning exempt, since they do not dispute regarding other birds. And in the later version where he says "but with an ordinary male" meaning exempt, implying an ordinary female meaning liable — and it follows the later version; and so the plain meaning of the mishnah "impure bird," etc., implies so: that the Gemara's variation "perhaps with korah of evasion" means korah is pure, and with impure they do not know its counterpart, etc. And this is why Rif omitted the first version; and also the question mentioned, since it says "but," etc., implies an ordinary female is liable, and all the more so female korah. And this is why he brought the later version, even though ostensibly there is no difference since it is according to R' Elazar. But Rambam, who omitted "female korah is liable," is surprising, since certainly there is no dispute, as Tosafos said "impure bird" [and not like Rosh]; and it must be because it is not common at all, and therefore he omitted also in siman 7 to say even male korah. And Beit Yosef strained here, and all his words are strained and contradict themselves. (Likut) Was, etc. — Rambam, and holds that these two versions of R' Elazar are not disputes, and R' Elazar also doubts an ordinary female (and also the braita that only mentions female korah can be said because regarding an ordinary female he doubts — and we find such in the Gemara in many places). And it is difficult on Rosh why he omitted the question (end).
