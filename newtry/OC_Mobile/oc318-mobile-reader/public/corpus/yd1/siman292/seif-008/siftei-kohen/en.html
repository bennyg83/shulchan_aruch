And they are kosher. Meaning that both the mother and the eggs are kosher, but they are not of the same species. Since no distinction was made, it implies that even with a female koreh one does not receive lashes. This is Rambam’s view, as Beit Yosef writes, and it is also Rif’s view according to Rosh’s understanding of his words. Rabbeinu Yerucham and Bach followed this understanding of Rif. Rosh, Tur, Rashi, and Ran, however, hold that with a female koreh one is obligated even when she sits on eggs not of her species, since both are kosher. Rabbeinu Yerucham likewise writes that most poskim rule this way. According to Beit Yosef and Maharshal at the end of the chapter Shiluach HaKen, Rif also holds this way, and Maharshal rules accordingly there. Regarding the identity of the koreh, Rabbeinu Yerucham writes that it cannot be the bird called kokliyo, as some commentators explain the verse, “The koreh gathered what it did not lay,” because that is a non-kosher bird, and all agree that one is exempt in such a case.
