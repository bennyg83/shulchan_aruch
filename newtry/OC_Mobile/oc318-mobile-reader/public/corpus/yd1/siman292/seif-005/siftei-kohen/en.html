He sent her away and she returned, etc. This implies that even if he sent her away properly, meaning until she left his hand, once she returned, even if he sent her away in this manner many times and she returned before he took the young, he must send her away again. Since he has not yet taken the young and she returned to sit upon them, the verse, “You shall not take the mother together with the young,” applies. This is also implied by the Gemara in the chapter Eilu Metziot, Bava Metzia 31a, where this is compared to the command, “You shall surely return them.” Although one already fulfilled the mitzvah of returning the item, he must return it repeatedly. This also appears to be the Tur’s view. I do not understand the Bach’s words, where he writes that Rabbeinu explains that one must send her until she leaves his hand, and if she did not leave his hand and returned, one may not take the mother but must send her away even one hundred times until she leaves his hand. It appears that even if she did leave his hand, he must send her away one hundred times, until she no longer returns.
He took the young and returned them, etc. Since he already acquired them, they are considered prepared. Accordingly, if they were in his courtyard, once he sent the mother away, even though he did not physically take them, his courtyard acquired them for him. If she then returned to them, he is exempt from sending her away.
