Two layers of eggs, etc. The Atzei Levonah writes that he intends to take only the lower eggs, which follows Rashi’s explanation. In Chiddushei Rashba, however, it is written that this is not satisfactory, for the mother is nevertheless sitting upon the upper eggs. Even if he intends to take neither the upper nor the lower eggs, we learned, as explained in seif 5, that if he says, “I will take the mother and send away the young,” his statement is ineffective. Rather, the case concerns where the upper eggs are from a non-kosher bird, or prepared eggs, similar to a cloth. For example, he reached into the nest and placed his own eggs there so that they would form a barrier between the mother and the eggs. This appears correct to me. In my view, however, the plain meaning of the Gemara follows Rashi, and this is also implied by the plain meaning of Tur, Rambam, and the Mechaber. There is no difficulty from the mishnah concerning one who says, “I will take the mother,” because here he does not wish to take the mother, but only the lower eggs. The Gemara asks whether the upper eggs constitute a barrier, in which case the lower eggs are regarded as lying elsewhere and he may take them before sending the mother away, or whether they do not constitute a barrier and he may not take them. The mother, however, may in any event not be taken, because she is sitting upon the upper eggs, even though he does not intend to take those eggs. It appears to me that this is Tosafot’s view, unlike Sefer Torat Chaim, which questions Rashi and Tosafot. According to what I have written, there is no difficulty; see there.
