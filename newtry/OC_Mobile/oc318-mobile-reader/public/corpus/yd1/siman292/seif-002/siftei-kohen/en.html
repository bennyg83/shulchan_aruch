A prepared nest. For it is written, “If it happens,” excluding one that is prepared.
That nested in an orchard. This refers to one that is not guarded, for once the birds became wild, their nest is not under your control. If it is guarded, however, one is exempt. This is implied by Rashi’s explanation, and Rabbeinu Yerucham wrote likewise in Netiv 15, section 33. The reason is that it is regarded as part of his home.
Provided that the mother was not lifted, etc. For then one cannot say that his courtyard acquired it for him, making it prepared. Since it is forbidden to acquire the eggs while the mother is sitting upon them, his courtyard likewise does not acquire them for him, because where he himself cannot acquire, his courtyard does not acquire on his behalf.
If the mother was lifted, etc. It appears that this means she was lifted sufficiently to leave his hand, as stated below in seif 4. Otherwise, since he cannot acquire her, because she did not leave his hand and he did not fulfill the mitzvah of sending her away, his courtyard likewise does not acquire for him. Alternatively, this case concerns where she rose by herself, and therefore it is sufficient that she no longer touch the nest, similar to the case below in seif 11 where she was hovering.
His courtyard acquired it for him. A person’s courtyard acquires property for him even without his knowledge, as explained in Choshen Mishpat, siman 200; see there.
