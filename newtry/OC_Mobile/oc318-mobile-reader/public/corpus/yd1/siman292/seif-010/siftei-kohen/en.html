This is, etc. We are uncertain whether to say that since, if he were to leave her in this condition, she would become a tereifah, the principle of “for you, and not for your dogs” applies, and he is exempt from sending her away, may take the mother permissibly, and then complete the slaughter. Or perhaps, since he is able to complete the slaughter, the phrase “you may take for yourself” applies, and he must send her away. Sefer Ma’adanei Yom Tov, at the beginning of the chapter Shiluach HaKen, writes that this concerns a case where the interruption did not reach the measure of shehiyah, and according to our practice it is not applicable because even the slightest interruption renders the bird a tereifah. It appears to me, however, that the case is where, while slaughtering, he also takes the mother, such as where he slaughters with one hand and takes the mother with the other, or instructs another person to take the mother. This explains why Rama did not add that according to our practice the case is inapplicable, and why Atzei Levonah and the other later authorities state this law without qualification. Furthermore, even if the case concerns the slightest interruption, many poskim permit it, as explained above in siman 23. Although we are stringent, that stringency concerns eating the bird, not being lenient regarding the mitzvah of sending away the mother. This is clear.
