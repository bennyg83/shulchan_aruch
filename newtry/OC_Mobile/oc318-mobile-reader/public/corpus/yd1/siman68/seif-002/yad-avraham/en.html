(Shulchan Aruch seif 11) Even in kli sheni some say one should not skin; and if he skinned — permitted. This 'some say' is Rashba, who holds that kli sheni, even though it does not cook, nevertheless expels and absorbs — as written in Orach Chaim 105 seif 2. And Shach wrote that for Rashba even b'dieved it is forbidden, and he challenged Rama in Torat Chatat, who wrote that for Rashba b'dieved it is permitted (see there); and it escaped him what Rashba wrote in the responsum Beit Yosef brought, siman 69, and further there regarding if meat was pressed from above without melicha — he wrote explicitly that even though kli sheni absorbs and expels, nevertheless melicha helps, for through melicha the blood absorbed from elsewhere exits through its blood; and it is permitted to place in kli sheni a piece with ice and snow. However, from these words of Rashba it implies that even l'chatchila it is permitted. And see in Magen Avraham general rule 15 s.k. 5; and so in Tashbetz Katan related to Magen Avraham siman 4 in the name of responsum of R' Shimon son of R' Avraham. And to this Rama intended when he wrote that for Rashba it is permitted — not as Shach understood, that Rama meant Rashba who is R' Shlomo ibn Aderet, author of Torat HaBayit, and therefore he challenged him and did not examine well. And see in Laws of Interest siman 176 that there were two Rashbas: one is R' Shimon, author of Tosafot; and the second is R' Shlomo. And see in Magen Avraham there. And likewise in Orchot Chaim general rule 17 it implies Rashba permits b'dieved and forbade only l'chatchila, like liver on meat. And see there in general rule 1, who also wrote Rashba permits as Beit Yosef wrote. And see in Ra'avan siman 21: likewise if he skinned in kli sheni and one is found treifah, the others are not forbidden, nor the vessel — because kli sheni does not cook, and we need not be concerned lest the treifah expel and be absorbed in the kosher, for boiling points meet and do not expel, as stated regarding an olive's bulk of meat that fell into a pot, and regarding liver — that if it expels, even two kosher pieces are not permitted, lest this expel blood and the other absorb; and even one is not permitted, lest it expel and re-absorb, end of his words.
