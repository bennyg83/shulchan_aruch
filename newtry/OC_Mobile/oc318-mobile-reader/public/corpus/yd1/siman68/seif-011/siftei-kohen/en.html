And there is no difference, etc. And regarding irui, Rabbenu Yerucham wrote in Netiv Chayim: if he poured meat into a copper vessel of milk, hag'alah is not needed, for copper does not absorb unless fire passes beneath it; and the poskim agreed and inferred from many places, end of his words; and they were drawn to his view — Beit Yosef in Orach Chaim 93, and the Rav in Torat Chatat general rule 33 din 3; and I did not reach the end of their view, for it appears simple from the Talmud and Jerusalem Talmud and all poskim that copper absorbs through irui. From the Talmud, for we learned in the mishnah, chapter Dam Chatat (Zevachim daf 95b): one who cooked in it and one who poured into it boiling — requires purging and rinsing; and it teaches on this in the Gemara in a baraita: 'that which is cooked in it' — I have only one who cooked in it; poured into it boiling — from where? Scripture teaches 'and that which is cooked in it' shall be broken — hence that which the mishnah teaches requires purging and rinsing in a copper vessel deals with earthenware requiring breaking; and it says afterward in the Gemara: come and hear: one who cooked in it and one who poured into it boiling — absorption without cooking we do not inquire about, etc. — hence it calls it absorption; and it is forced to say the mishnah deals with cooking by fire, for plain vessels in the Temple are metal (and likewise Maharai in Torat HaBayit, Orach Chaim 132, and copper vessels are meant, which they would kasher for sacrifices, etc. — see there); and further, for it teaches 'one who cooked in it'; and further, for it teaches anonymously; and further, for it refers to the verse, which deals with copper vessels; and further, in Torat Kohanim, parashat Tzav (chapter 7), it teaches explicitly: I have only an earthenware vessel poured into boiling; copper vessel poured into boiling — from where? Scripture teaches 'and that which is cooked in it' shall be broken — until here. From the Jerusalem Talmud (chapter 1 of Ma'aserot and chapter Kirah — see there), which asks: and we learned likewise regarding copper vessels? You may say copper absorbs — meaning, you may say it absorbs if it does not cook — hence even if it cooks, and even R' Yosi, who disagrees and holds it does not cook, agrees it absorbs — as Rashba wrote in Torat HaBayit daf 92 and Ran, chapter Kirah; for otherwise how would he resolve the baraita Rabbenu Yonah brought; and likewise Tosafot explained: you may say copper absorbs — in wonder: meaning, if irui does not cook, it did not absorb — hence such is implied from Rosh, chapter Kirah, and Hasagot Semag, and Gemara, and Rokeach, and all poskim who brought the aforementioned Jerusalem Talmud to rule halacha; and I asked this matter of my teacher the great Gaon, the famous Rabbi Yehoshua, may his light shine, and he answered me at length as it appeared to me in Rabbenu Yerucham's view; and nevertheless he also raised for practical law that hag'alah is required:
