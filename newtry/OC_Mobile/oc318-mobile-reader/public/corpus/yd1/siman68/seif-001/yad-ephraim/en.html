However, for honoring guests, etc. And he wrote in Sulam LaMinchah general rule 15 in the name of Torat HaEsh, composed by the third author of Turei Zahav: it appears to me that wherever 'for honoring guests' is mentioned, they are fitting — such as Torah scholars and people of good name and good deeds, or a poor person of good lineage, or wealthy people fit to honor who do not do evil and are not lowly who quickly fall, or misers who transgress for treifah and food — they are in the category of charity and not in the category of honoring guests; and he brought part of his words from collections of Maharil; and what Shibolei HaLeket [brought] from Abraham to Lekh Lekha — there it is different, for he wished to bring them under the wings of the Divine Presence, as written 'and he planted an eshel,' etc.; and he also wrote that they are not called guests unless they lodged in his house, or he invited guests who lodged with others; but when he invited his friend to dine, they are not called guests and it is not a mitzvah meal, as written in Orach Chaim siman 333; and the Mechaber there wrote there is no proof from there to here, since he invited his friend fit to honor and he has nothing to eat — there is no greater embarrassment than this; and see Chullin daf 94 and Bava Batra daf 93; and certainly l'chatchila he should not invite his friend to him when he knows he will cause leniency in rinsing and melicha; but if he already invited, then for their honor one may be lenient, like because of other guests — and great is human dignity, end of his words.
