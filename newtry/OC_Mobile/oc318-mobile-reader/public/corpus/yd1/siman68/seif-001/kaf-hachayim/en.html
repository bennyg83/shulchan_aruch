(1) [Seif 1] One who buries the head to roast it, etc. In Tur it is written: one who buries the head in hot ashes to skin it, and there is no difference between them for practical halacha, for skinning and roasting are equal in this. Taz s.k. 1. And although below in Shulchan Aruch seif 3 he brought a dispute in skinning, it appears Taz's view is to be stringent like those who forbid, and therefore he wrote for practical halacha they are equal in this. And likewise Chayei Adam general rule 33, observation 3; and see below observation 9.
(2) There. He must intend that the beit shechitah be below so the blood flows out through there, etc.; and if he hung it and the beit shechitah is below — permitted, for the blood exits and drips. Rambam, chapter 6 of Laws of Forbidden Foods, halacha 14. And this is to exclude that the beit shechitah not be below and touch the ground — for if it touches the ground, the ground blocks the blood from exiting, and it is like salting in an unperforated vessel; rather he must hang it in air and not touch the ground so the blood flows. However, this is if he roasts it on hard ground; but in hot ashes, which are like fine dust, one need not be particular about this, and even if the beit shechitah touches below in hot ashes — permitted, for it is not called salting in an unperforated vessel. As Kenesset HaGedolah in Hagahat HaTur, observation 5; Zivchei Tzedek observation 1.
(3) There. And if he placed it on its sides, the brain and membrane are forbidden and the rest of the head permitted, as written below in hagahah seif 4; and even peeling is not needed — Shach s.k. 1. Pri Chadash observation 1; Kereti observation 1; and that which we say the brain and membrane are forbidden — it deals even when the head was salted, for since fire has no power to expel the head's blood without cutting, all the more so salt will not expel it. Pri Chadash there; Kereti there. And likewise in Shulchan Aruch below siman 71 seif 3, that in melicha too one must pierce the bone and membrane — see there; Zivchei Tzedek observation 2.
(4) There. Unless he pierced the brain, etc.; and even if he did not pierce the membrane, for since he roasts it, once the bone is pierced the blood no longer gathers inside, and it appears fire draws out — Beit Yosef siman 71 in the name of Rashba; Shach s.k. 2. Pri Chadash observation 2; Kereti observation 2; Shulchan Gavoah observation 3; Shiyurei Berachah observation 2; Chayei Adam general rule 33 observation 3; Beit Yosef Avraham in Shulchan Pitchei 7 observation 32; Zivchei Tzedek observation 3.
(5) Once they brought before me a roasted lamb head that was not cut at the brain bone, but a nail was stuck in the brain bone; and I told them: even though Maran wrote that if the brain bone is pierced the blood flows through there — this is where the hole remained open; but here, where the nail and its head seal the hole — in what way will its blood flow? And even though Maran wrote nearby that where he placed it on its nostrils, if he stuck a reed or other object — it is different regarding nostrils, for there is no bone on that flat line, only hard flesh separates between it and the brain; hence whatever is pierced there, even if he left the reed there, its blood flows through there. But the brain bone is hard, and the nail-head seals it, and no way remains for its blood to flow — Shulchan Gavoah observation 4; Zivchei Tzedek observation 4.
(6) There, hagahah: and l'chatchila they are accustomed to be stringent not to roast any whole head, etc.; rather they regularly remove the brain and salt it first before roasting. Shach s.k. 3. Pri Chadash observation 3; Lehem HaPanim observation 1; Beit Yehudah observation 1; Beit Yosef Avraham in Shulchan Pitchei 7 observation 3; Zivchei Tzedek observation 5. But its membrane they throw outside — Beit Yosef Avraham there.
(8) There. If he stuck a reed, etc. — Rashi explained: pierce in it in the hole of the nostril something so it not seal; but Rif and Rosh wrote: pierce in it something and open it so blood may exit. And it appears there is a difference for practical law: for Rashi, that object he pierced must be in the nostrils at the time of roasting so the hole is open and not sealed; but Rif and Rosh hold he need not have it at roasting time — whatever he pierced and opened the hole, even if he removed it before roasting — permitted, for blood flows through the hole. The true custom is to leave a nail there and leave it during roasting. As Kenesset HaGedolah in Beit Gavriel observation 2; meaning that in truth they are accustomed to be stringent like Rashi so the hole is open all during roasting and not sealed; however, that object must be placed so there is air in the hole of the nostril for blood to flow and the whole hole not seal.
(12) There. Unless there are sixty against the brain and membrane. There are two membranes for the brain: one attached to the skull bone, and one attached to the brain itself; and there is not sixty in the brain against both membranes; and if the brain was cooked with both membranes, everything is forbidden; but against the membrane attached to it there is sixty in the brain against it, and if cooked with this membrane, everything is permitted. Orchot Chaim general rule 19 observations 4 and 6, from what is stated in Shabbat daf 129a; Kenesset HaGedolah in Beit Gavriel observation 17; Beit Yehudah observation 3; Shulchan Gavoah observation 12; Zivchei Tzedek observation 14.
(14) There, in hagahah. The membrane and brain forbidden and the rest permitted. See above observation 3.
