But in singeing, etc. And Maharshal there forbids even in singeing; it appears it deals when he leaves them a while on the fire; but when he passes them over the fire and always changes their place — he agrees it is permitted:
Everything is permitted. Nevertheless l'chatchila it is good to be stringent even in passing-through to remove the tops of the hooves, as Ran wrote in his view, that it deals with passing-through, and even so he wrote that the good custom is to remove them first, end of his words — Darkei Moshe:
But the rest is permitted. And there is no difference whether they lie on their length or stand on their hooves — so in Darkei Moshe in the name of Orchot Chaim, and likewise in Torat Chatat general rule 76 din 16 (and Orchot Chaim wrote at the beginning of general rule 17: one should not compare it literally to salting in an unperforated vessel (below siman 69 seif 8) to forbid even what is above the hooves, for a small amount in the hooves does not block the whole leg from expelling, end of his words); but from the words of Maharai in hagahot of Shach Devarim siman 91, whom Maharshal brings in his book there, it appears to forbid when they stand on their hooves, for he wrote: and it is not similar to salting in an unperforated vessel, where everything is forbidden, for there the place is cramped for blood to exit; but here, plain legs lie in their singeing on their length, and there is room for all the leg outside the shoe to exit through its length — and why is it said that it flows and goes through the shoe, for blood's way is not to flow downward but to spread sideways, end of his words; and likewise Eitan HaEzrachi distinguishes, and likewise in Maharil: legs whose hoof-tops were not cut and were salted — if they lay on their side all the time they were in melicha, only what is inside the hooves was forbidden; and if they stood upright, all were forbidden, end of his words; however, in any case most poskim permit even what is inside the hooves:
