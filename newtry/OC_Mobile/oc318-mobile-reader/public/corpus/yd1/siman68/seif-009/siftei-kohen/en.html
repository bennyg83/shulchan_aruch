And provided he rinse the beit shechitah, etc. And it is implied from Ran's words that if he did not rinse the beit shechitah, even b'dieved it is forbidden even in roasting, for visible blood that was absorbed does not exit even through roasting, as below siman 76 in many places; but he wrote there in the name of Rashba that because of expulsion of heart and liver one need not be concerned for roasting; and the reason regarding expelled blood is that we say as it absorbs so it expels, as below siman 72 and siman 73 in many places — so it appears in Ran, and likewise from Rashba's words in Torat HaBayit Ha'aroch daf 76a, and from the Mechaber's words to distinguish between blood of the beit shechitah and heart and liver; and likewise Rabbenu Yerucham, whom Beit Yosef brings: congealed blood such as in the beit shechitah must be rinsed even for roasting, for all congealed blood on the flesh — fire does not draw it out, only absorbs it, and in slight heat it is absorbed; therefore one should be careful when skinning the head with the neck to rinse the place of the beit shechitah, and likewise in a bird when skinning it, end of his words; and in Darkei Moshe he wrote that Rabbenu Yerucham forbids in roasting — he argues with the aforementioned Rashba; and according to what I wrote they do not argue — only requires further study, for below siman 71 s.k. 9 I wrote in the name of Rashba that even blood of the heart is considered visible blood regarding meat next to it, and removal is needed in roasting; and it is possible that in singeing, which is lighter than roasting, they are not so stringent to consider it visible blood, for specifically in roasting that expels much is it visible blood — not in singeing:

Must remove or peel, etc. Requires further study from where the Rav derived this, for it appears that since we hold regarding visible blood we do not say as it absorbs so it expels, and even in roasting sixty are needed as below beginning of siman 76 — all the more so in burial in hot ashes; and it appears the Rav holds that its blood is not so considered visible blood, for for this reason we hold below beginning of siman 76 that meat roasted without first rinsing is permitted; and although there it is entirely permitted — nevertheless here, since it is blood of the beit shechitah and also in burial, which is weaker than roasting, peeling or removal is needed:
