(9) [Seif 3] Some wrote that to remove hair alone, even if he placed it on its sides, is permitted, etc., for he does not leave it so long; and in such a small measure blood does not go from place to place, and even if there is a crack, through melicha it exits. Beit Yosef in the name of Ran. And 'some forbid' is Rashba's view and Tur, as we wrote above observation 1, and with him, as Beit Yosef wrote, who holds that burial in hot ashes to remove hair has the same law as roasting, and it is forbidden to leave on the sides; and Pri Chadash wrote observation 5 that such is primary for practical law, and likewise above observation 1; and what is written in hagahah 'and the reason is the first,' etc. — see in observations afterward.
(10) There, hagahah: and the first reason is primary, etc. The wording is unclear, for it implies that according to the first reason even burial placed in one place is permitted, since he only wishes to remove its hair and afterward finishes — and it is forbidden only burial. And it appears the Rav in hagahah follows his reasoning, who wrote in Darkei Moshe: and it appears to me Ran's words are like Rabbenu Yerucham, who wrote in Netiv Chayim: specifically burial; but placing on hot coals and always changing place — permitted in every matter, end of his words; and with this we do not find Tur disagrees, and likewise Orchot Chaim general rule 16 permits to singe the head and remove hair on fire even if he places it on its sides, since he does not bury it in hot ashes always in one place — and likewise regarding legs, end of his words. And hence such is the explanation of his words here in hagahah: the first reason is primary, for the second too does not disagree except regarding burial; but in singeing permitted — and so they are accustomed to singe, etc., and forbidden only burial, etc. Shach s.k. 5; and Pri Chadash wrote on this: although it does not appear so from Ran's plain words, nevertheless in the main law it is correct, since, as written in hagahah here, Lehem HaPanim observation 2; Beit Yehudah observation 2; Zivchei Tzedek observation 7; and he wrote: and so they are accustomed in the city of Baghdad, etc., in chickens to pass them through fire and singe remaining feathers on them, and they do not place them in one place but move them back and forth, and we are not concerned blood will be absorbed.
