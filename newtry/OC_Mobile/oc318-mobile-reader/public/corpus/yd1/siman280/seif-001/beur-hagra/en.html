And some say, etc. This is very puzzling, and the Beit Yosef already expressed astonishment at it.
And some say it is permitted, etc. The Jerusalem Talmud states as a halacha transmitted to Moshe at Sinai that scrolls are written on hides and with ink, ruled with a reed, wrapped to the proper measure, patched with cloth, glued with adhesive, and sewn with sinews, with the stitching made in the prescribed form. Some disagree and say this is the view of an individual authority, based on Maseches Soferim at the end of chapter 2. Maharik rejects that objection because the Jerusalem Talmud states the rule anonymously, so the halacha follows it. The Rama's further ruling follows R' Shimon ben Elazar there. Some explain the passage as referring to tefillin, like the statement about wrapping with hair, and Rambam likewise interprets patching with cloth as referring to tefillin, as explained in Orach Chaim, siman 32. Nevertheless, Maseches Soferim itself states that a torn Torah scroll may be patched. See Rivash, siman 31, and Maharik, siman 122.
As long as it is permitted, etc. The same source states, 'they glue with adhesive and sew with sinews.' This applies only where sewing itself is permitted. Where a tear of more than three lines cannot be repaired by sewing, all the more so adhesive does not suffice.
