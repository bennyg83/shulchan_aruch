Worn out. A Torah scroll that has become worn may be buried in an earthenware vessel beside a Torah scholar. Shach explains that burial protects it from rapid decay and disrespect.
An earthenware vessel. This is specifically one that endures. A wooden or metal container that does not serve this protective purpose is not equivalent.
Beside a Torah scholar. Meaning even one who studied halakhot rather than Scripture itself, because all Torah learning gives this status.
And its wrappings. The coverings and accessories used directly for the Torah scroll share its sanctity and require burial. Items used only to protect the protective covering may be discarded.
A Torah scholar. If no Torah scholar is available, it should be buried beside a person known for mitzvot and good deeds.
In a grave. The burial should be arranged so that the scroll is protected from immediate contact with moisture and decay.
