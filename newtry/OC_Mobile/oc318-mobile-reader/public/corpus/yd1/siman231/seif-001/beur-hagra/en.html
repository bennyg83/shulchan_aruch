One may not, etc. As stated at the beginning of siman 228.
For even, etc. Otherwise, what is the Talmud's question in the third chapter on 23b: "If he remembered, he uprooted it," etc.?
And even if he made it dependent, etc. As stated concerning a husband: once he has confirmed her vow, he can no longer annul it. This is the meaning of the statement above that immediately when the vow takes effect, etc. Only concerning a husband did Rava inquire at the beginning of the tenth chapter whether a confirmation may be released, because a woman relies entirely upon her husband's decision. With another person, however, once that person has done what was required of him and confirmed the vow, the person who vowed no longer relies upon him. This is the meaning of the statements, "And even," etc., and, "Thereafter," etc.
But if, etc. As stated concerning a husband, and specifically if he acts immediately, for the husband's law is a scriptural decree limited to "the day he hears it." Thus writes Rivash. See siman 225, se'if 37, what is written there, and the authorities who disagree with this.
All the more so, etc. In this case all authorities agree, as stated there.
Nevertheless, if, etc. As stated throughout the eighth chapter of Nedarim, and there on 60b: "He said to him: if so, let us decree," etc.; and as explained above in siman 220, se'if 3, in the gloss. The same law applies to bans imposed by a court, as Tosafot wrote in the first chapter of tractate Yom Tov, 5a, beginning with the words "Every matter," etc., and in Sanhedrin 59b, beginning with the words "For every matter." They testified that Rashi retracted what he had written in his commentary there, beginning with the same words. Orchot Chayim likewise writes that Rashi stated this in a responsum, though he was uncertain whether Rashi may instead have retracted in his commentary there. He overlooked the cited words of Tosafot, which state that Rashi retracted the position in his commentary. All this concerns bans imposed by a court. Regarding vows, however, all authorities agree that no release is required, as explained above.
