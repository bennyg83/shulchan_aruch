But if the fish is salted, etc. — because the salting thing heats the unsalted so it absorbs; but not so much that it emits — Beit Yosef in the name of Rashba:
And likewise if the fish, etc. — and in doubt one should forbid — Rashal:
After the poultry remained, etc. — and it is not comparable to the practice of Rashi siman 69 seif 20 that we are stringent there regarding moisture after the time of salting — that is specifically when the moisture is in the eye and the meat rests inside it, as above; but for mere moisture on the meat alone we did not practice prohibition, and rinsing suffices for fish. Or Zarua:
For fish do not absorb blood in the vessel. — specifically blood; but regarding other prohibitions by which the vessel became forbidden through salting meat, perhaps fish are forbidden because emission of other prohibitions clings more — Beit Yosef in the name of Rashba; and Rashal wrote Perek 25 siman 73: I say salting does not apply to wood to emit prohibition at all, even absorption in a vessel of Torah prohibition, end of his words:
But if it has not yet emitted its blood, etc. — in this he disagrees with Beit Yosef, who wrote on what the Tur said that fish are forbidden when both are salted, and Beit Yosef wrote: all the more so if the fish is unsalted and the prohibition salted — it is like pure unsalted and impure salted; and his reason: when both are salted each is busy emitting, and nevertheless we say it also absorbs — a fortiori unsalted fish that absorbs from salted poultry. And likewise Rashal: there is a fortiori if the fish was not salted and the meat salted — the fish is forbidden. And Rama in Darkei Moshe and Tosafot Chullin disagrees and holds: since the fish is unsalted it absorbs only blood, and we say since it is still emitting its own blood it will also emit blood absorbed from elsewhere, as stated regarding meat never salted placed with meat already salted, end of his words. And Maharach wrote: one may explain Beit Yosef's words that the fish was already salted and afterward called unsalted; and it is written in Perishah on Rama — requires further study, for at first glance one may distinguish: here the fish are permitted upon them, so how are they permitted when they received forbidden blood; and in seif 6 he ruled to forbid because fish blood is only brine and one cannot say since it emits its blood, etc., end of his words. And in my humble opinion likewise according to those who forbid, for since brine in fish is a slight thing and to emit it in a short time — how can we rely on their brine emission to say that since it emits brine it will emit absorbed blood, lest the time of brine emission finish before absorbed blood emits, and there is a Torah doubt here; not so with meat and meat where there is much brine to emit for a long time — therefore there is much time to emit also blood absorbed from elsewhere. And according to the explanation of R' Yitzchak of Orleans in Tosafot Perek 25 that Beit Yosef brought at the beginning of siman 70, who explained regarding meat and meat that all the time one is open to emit before the other begins absorbing, its emission does not close afterward — one may also permit fish through their salting. But we do not hold that answer as explained there. And further there is reason to forbid, for the Rosh wrote in Perek 25 explaining Rashbam: regarding meat and meat they did not trouble them excessively, since blood is rabbinic, etc., therefore they were lenient there; this does not apply here, for it is not a rabbinic enactment to salt fish with meat — therefore one may not be lenient that one return and salt and emit, as appears to me. And further proof from what Teshuvot HaRash wrote: perhaps fish, since their skin is soft, absorb much and blood coming to them from elsewhere clings in them more and salt has no power to extract it, end of his words. And further proof from what Or Zarua general rule 5 wrote: unsalted fish placed with brine of meat are forbidden — no worse than if they were salted with them, end of his words.
