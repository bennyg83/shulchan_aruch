Shach wrote: it appears explicitly that they touch one another; and even though in seif 3 regarding treifah meat unsalted and shechitah meat salted we forbid a peel's thickness when they touch one another — one may say that there the kosher was salted boiling such that it emits brine from treifah, and treifah brine is forbidden; nevertheless it is not considered so boiling that blood would emit — and here it is forbidden only because of blood. And he wrote further: even if the fish were not yet salted, nevertheless it is forbidden, and salting afterward does not help it, for regarding fish we do not say since they emit their own blood they will emit another's blood, as with meat — for fish are different because their skin is soft — such is the Mechaber's view:
On. Maharshal wrote: specifically when it is known clearly that the fish were not heated from above; but if it is not known clearly — forbidden, for ordinarily in salting the upper turns to the lower. Or Zarua wrote: likewise even side by side — if the fish pieces are large and higher and rise against the meat, what is above the piece that does not touch it is permitted even in that same piece, for it is no worse than half a piece falling into brine.
