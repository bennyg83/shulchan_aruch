A piece that one salted twice. Without rinsing between them, and specifically if one salted; but l'chatchila it is forbidden to do so — rather one must rinse it well before the second salting; and so appears in Tosafot Chullin general rule 15 din 20 — see there:
And we are not concerned that the second salt absorbs the blood, etc. And that which we forbid in siman 69 regarding meat salted without first rinsing — because salt absorbs the blood on it — that case is different, for there is blood in the eye; alternatively, this case is different because since it was salted first and not rinsed afterward, it is still standing to emit brine, and therefore we say the second salt does not absorb the blood:
And if one transgressed and salted them together, etc. Whether one salted them together side by side, whether one salted poultry on fish or fish on poultry — it always applies to say the fish are forbidden a peel's thickness and the poultry permitted, for even though blood does not seep upward, and as the Rav wrote in seif 2, nevertheless peeling is required, as will be explained there and as the Mechaber himself wrote in siman 69 seif 18 that what is outside the brine one cuts a peel's thickness; and poultry are always permitted because since they are busy emitting they do not absorb; and so is the view of many poskim — not like Bach who ruled as an individual to forbid from the poultry until sixty — see there, for his words are automatically refuted; also what we mention to be stringent in Torah prohibition does not apply, for we hold blood in salting is only rabbinic, as explained in many places:
And if their scales were not removed when they were salted — permitted. Because scales are in place of a peel, and so is proven in the Tur — see there; and also Rabbenu Yerucham appears plainly to agree that scales are in place of a peel, etc., except he needed the reason soft skin for what we forbid fish at sixty; and practical difference if fish were salted with another prohibition not of blood — even though we do not say there since it is busy emitting, etc., the fish are permitted, for even though we hold all salting at sixty, nevertheless Rabbenu Yerucham wrote in siman 105 seif 9 that in a prohibition with no fat at all, such as chametz on Pesach and the like — for all opinions salting forbids only a peel's thickness; and see there; and if so in such a case here the fish are permitted without a peel, for scales are in place of a peel:
