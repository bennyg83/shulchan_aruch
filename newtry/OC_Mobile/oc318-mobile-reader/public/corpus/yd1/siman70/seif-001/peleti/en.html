For they do not emit, etc. — likewise the Rosh: scales constrict like poultry; and the Tur wrote: since it forbids only a peel's thickness, if scales were not removed — permitted — implying their reason is scales are in place of a peel; and likewise Shach and Peri Chadash that the Tur did not write like the Rosh because there is no practical difference since salting forbids only a peel and they are in place of a peel — forced to say why did the Tur change his father's language, especially since the Rosh himself can be understood: why need the reason they constrict — since salting forbids only a peel and scales are the peel. One must say if he took scales after they salted together and cooked them in a dish — for the reason they are in place of a peel, sixty is needed against scales for absorbed blood — see below in laws of mixtures that something forbidden by a peel needs sixty to nullify; but according to the Rosh's reason that they constrict and did not absorb — nothing is needed, and the Tur teaches much. Therefore it seems poultry salted and fish unsalted — nevertheless forbidden, and we are not told if fish are salted that since they emit their blood they emit poultry blood; and Rama's view is not so — see Peri Chadash s.k. 7. One may infer from the Gemara that challenges: in a non-perforated vessel why fish and poultry — even poultry with poultry; the challenge is poultry with poultry when both salted, but if one unsalted it is not forbidden — what of fish and poultry when both unsalted — nevertheless forbidden; this is not a challenge, for the Gemara teaches us the case of soft skin; but in the conclusion we know nothing — the challenge stands well. But Tosafot, Rashbam, and Rosh inferred that it teaches fish and poultry that they salted together, not fish salted and, etc. — if it taught fish salted, one might say only if salted, but unsalted permitted through emitting their blood, etc.; therefore these authorities hold like Rama that in truth permitted, and one should not be stringent.
