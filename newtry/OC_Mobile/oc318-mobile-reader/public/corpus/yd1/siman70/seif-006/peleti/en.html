Emitted all its blood and was not rinsed — later authorities hold that since it was rinsed the emission holes closed and even second salting does not help; the Rav HaTzedek challenged from where we know second salting does not help, and Peri Chadash agreed. I found support from what Ran wrote that even after the time of salting there is still blood in the piece — and Rambam requires scalding before the pot because when rinsed the holes close; if so meat salted and rinsed should be forbidden to salt again in a non-perforated vessel before scalding — we do not find this for Rambam. One must say when rinsed the holes closed; nevertheless in great loss one may rely on presumption — requires further study.
A full day — Taz emended that boiling suffices to place on fire; Shach and Peri Chadash wrote certainly it deals with a perforated vessel — then it is not pickling, therefore from time to time is needed; and still not settled even in twenty-four hours. What does Rama teach here regarding brine — the same in water as Taz wrote. One may say Rama doubted whether pickling in a perforated vessel is called pickling, and whether pickling in vinegar is like fully cooked so second salting does not help — therefore two doubts, and we require both; and in water some hold it is not pickling — therefore require brine and twenty-four hours which have both.
Meat, etc. — if unsalted pieces mixed with pieces salted in a non-perforated vessel, Shach extended to permit — see there; in truth if we want to forbid because of non-perforated vessel it is obvious to permit. But for me it is difficult: the piece salted in non-perforated vessel was forbidden because blood separated place to place — forbidden cannot forbid — perhaps some moisture goes with it; if so all forbidden. Perhaps since forbidding for separated blood is mere stringency they were lenient with forbidden cannot forbid; but l'chatchila be stringent — perhaps Mordechai thus — requires further study.
