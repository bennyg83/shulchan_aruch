There are those who forbid placing meat that was never salted at all, or that was salted and emitted all its blood, together with meat that was salted before emitting its blood, because the unsalted meat, or meat that salted and emitted all its blood, returns and absorbs what its fellow emits. And there are those who permit through salting — that one salt it afterward, for then it will emit all blood it absorbed. And there are those who permit in all this; and l'chatchila one should heed those who forbid. {Rama: And even b'dieved the custom is to forbid if meat that already emitted all its blood and brine fell next to meat that has not yet remained for the time of salting (Maharai in Hagahat Shaarei Dura). And there are those who say that all from time to time after it was salted it emits brine, and if within this time it touched meat that was salted and has not yet remained for the time of salting — it is not forbidden — and such is the custom (Shaarei Dura in the name of the Geonim, Maharai, Sefer HaTerumah, and Mordechai in the name of R' Yitzchak). However, where there is no great loss one should forbid it after twelve hours; and before that one need not be stringent at all. And all the more so if it was never salted — even if it fell into actual brine one need not forbid it, for we say that by emitting its own blood it will also emit what it absorbed from elsewhere. And even if the meat was salted and has not yet remained for the time of salting and fell into brine — there is room to permit (Or Zarua general rule 5) if it was not pickled in it for a full day; but if it remained for the time of salting and fell into brine — some forbid it even though the time of brine emission has not yet finished (Rashal in his Or Zarua). Nevertheless, for the need of great loss one may permit this too as long as brine is still being emitted — that is, within twelve hours — by rinsing (Hagahat Shaarei Dura) and returning to salt it. And likewise if it fell into brine before it remained for the time of salting — one rinses and returns to salt it; however, if one salted without rinsing after it fell into brine — it is permitted b'dieved (Or Zarua thus). And all this deals only with brine that is like boiling, as explained above siman 69; but actual blood that fell on meat that is within its salting time and is considered like boiling, as explained below siman 91 — the meat is forbidden, for regarding blood in the eye we do not say that since it is busy emitting it does not absorb, nor as it absorbs so it releases (there). And that which we forbid it when it fell into brine — that is specifically what is inside the brine; but what is above the brine is permitted. However, what is inside the brine is forbidden immediately and there is no measure to the matter. Meat that fell into brine on the ground — its law is as if the brine were in a vessel (Beit Yosef in the name of Shaarei Dura and Or Zarua there). Meat that touched a piece that was salted in a non-perforated vessel and became forbidden — its law is as if it touched brine (an individual opinion). A doubt whether brine or water — permitted, for brine is rabbinic and a doubt is resolved leniently (Mordechai end of chapter 25). Brine mixed with water — even if the water was scant, it is no longer considered boiling and does not forbid (Beit Yosef in the name of Agur). The law of pickling — see below siman 105.}
