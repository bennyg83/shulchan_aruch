There are those who say regarding meat that was salted, etc. — in Beit Yosef it is written that this law depends on the law in siman 69 beginning of seif 20 — see there; and in Tosafot Chullin general rule 14 din 4 he wrote that even for those stringent above to forbid a peel's thickness — that is because there we see salt extracted more brine, and since it rests in a non-perforated vessel it is forbidden; but here where meat always rests in a perforated vessel we are not concerned the meat will absorb from salt on it, for even if it emits blood it slides off — therefore permitted b'dieved, end of his words (and see in s.k. 11); and even though if meat that already emitted blood and brine fell next to meat that has not yet remained for salting time — forbidden, as the Rav wrote beginning of seif 6, and it is obvious — that is even in a perforated vessel, and we do not say blood slides off; one may say this case is different, for what left the meat during salting time while boiling and actual blood already left the perforated vessel, and what remains on it is only moisture that left after it remained for salting time, which is only rabbinic moisture according to many poskim, as explained in siman 69 there; if so, although they are stringent to consider it blood, nevertheless there is room to rely in such a case on the reason blood slides off; and likewise many poskim hold salt after it emitted blood has no power, as explained there; therefore here we rely on blood slides off — not so below:
