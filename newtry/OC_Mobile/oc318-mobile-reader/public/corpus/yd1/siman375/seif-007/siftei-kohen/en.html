One who drowned in water that has an end, etc. It appears he used water that has an end specifically to exclude water that has no end, for one does not mourn for him at all. The Beit Yosef wrote in the name of the Itur in the name of a responsum of the Rif: one who drowned in water that has no end, since there is no permission for his wife, should not observe mourning. The baraita in Evel Rabbati that says "drowned in a river" can be explained as where he fell and died, or where he fell into water that has an end. According to this, what is written, "or a report emerged," etc., or "an animal dragged him," etc., also means in a case where his wife is permitted to remarry. Although from the words of Rav Hai and Rav Sherira brought by the Tur it implies that even if it was not clarified that he died, but only a report went out, they mourn for him, since their words were rejected by the poskim, as stated in the Tur and Beit Yosef, they were rejected in this too. So the Rav wrote in Even HaEzer siman 17 seif 5, that his wife is forbidden to eulogize or wear black as long as there is no testimony fit to permit her to remarry; see there.
The relatives need not mourn. So wrote the Tur. This astonishes me: since it is no worse than gathering bones, then the relatives too should need to mourn, as below siman 403 seifim 1 and 5. It appears that he inferred this from the Rosh in the name of Maharam, who wrote that if they buried him in his son's place and his son knew of his burial, it is no worse than the day of gathering bones, etc. He understood from this language that specifically his son does so. But the matter is clear: Maharam there was addressing the incident of Rabbi Elikum, where someone was killed on the road, his son searched for him, and afterward he was found; only his son was mentioned there, so he refers to that. But the same applies to other relatives, for he brings proof from the baraita in Evel Rabbati: all for whom one tears at death, one tears for at the time of gathering bones. See there. Rabbeinu Yerucham's words in netiv 28 part 1 are also confused, for he wrote that if the killed person was later found and buried, Rabbi Elikum ruled that they do not mourn for him, even his sons; but Rabbi Meir wrote that his sons mourn that whole day like gathering bones, etc. One can settle his words by saying he mentioned sons and the same applies to other relatives; or he came to exclude those who mourn together with the mourner, as above siman 374 seif 6, who need not mourn here. But the words of the Tur and Shulchan Aruch cannot be settled this way, and this requires study.
If they are there, etc. He used the usual case, for presumably if they are not there they did not hear that day. But certainly, even if they are not there, if they heard that day they must mourn the entire day, as below siman 403 seif 5. According to what I wrote nearby, the same law applies to relatives as well.
Whose law is to begin mourning from when they turn their faces back. This is where the head of the household did not go with them to another city, as above in seif 2. So wrote the Beit Yosef, brought by the Darkhei Moshe. Therefore the Rav wrote, "if they sent their dead to another city," etc.; but with a cemetery in the same city, even those who return do not mourn until they are told that he was buried, as I wrote above in s.k. 1.
