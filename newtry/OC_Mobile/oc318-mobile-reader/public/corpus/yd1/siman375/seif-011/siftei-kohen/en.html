One who prayed Ma'ariv. And he too prayed. So it is in the Mordechai and in Mahari Weil responsa siman 81. The Bach copied the text as "one who prayed Ma'ariv," etc., and so too Terumat HaDeshen siman 241. But if he himself has not yet prayed Ma'ariv, Maharshal and the Bach wrote there that that day counts for him, even though the congregation prayed Ma'ariv. Even according to the view above at the end of siman 196 regarding niddah that she is drawn after the congregation, a woman is different, since most women do not pray and are drawn after the congregation; therefore even if she is accustomed to pray they did not distinguish. Here it is different. So wrote Maharshal there. So too my father and teacher, the Gaon z"l, wrote in a responsum, that if he himself did not pray, even though the congregation prayed, that day counts for him, for in such a case he has not adopted two contradictory leniencies. Even according to the one who is stringent above in siman 196, mourning is different, for it is rabbinic and we rule that the halachah follows the lenient view in mourning. Alternatively, one can distinguish: above it speaks where the congregation prayed Ma'ariv and she wanted to be lenient for herself, to make it day and wear white; she has no power to remove herself from the congregation. But here, mourning was sent to him from Heaven while it was still day, and he had not prayed; he can say, for me it is day. Maharshal in his responsum distinguished differently, but in my view his distinction is weak, and what I wrote seems better. These words are correct. According to this, if this mourning occurred to a woman who had not prayed, that day counts for her, unlike Maharshal; and so is primary.
And that day does not count. The Atteret Zahav wrote: according to what I wrote above at the beginning of siman 196 regarding wearing white, that one who is lenient loses nothing, it seems to me that all the more so in mourning, one who is lenient to count the day on which he heard as day one, even though the congregation prayed Ma'ariv, loses nothing. This does not appear correct to me, for the law here is taken from the Mordechai in the name of Maharam, brought by Terumat HaDeshen siman 241, and from it he derives regarding niddah. He wrote there that one of the great authorities wrote to him in a responsum that one should not compare niddah to mourning, because the hour when one prays Ma'ariv is only an attachment and addition to the following day, not the essence of the day. Therefore regarding mourning it is not considered day, since it is an attachment and addition to the following day; but regarding counting, we are particular only about the essence of the seven days and not the addition. These are his words. If so, we rule like this great authority, and so it is implied by the Bach and the other later authorities, who are lenient regarding niddah but not here. Perhaps the Atteret Zahav also meant only where the congregation prayed Ma'ariv but he himself did not, in which case that day counts for him, as I wrote nearby.
