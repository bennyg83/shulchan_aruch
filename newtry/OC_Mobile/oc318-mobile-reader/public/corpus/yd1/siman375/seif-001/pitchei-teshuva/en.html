Another. The Gaon of Lissa writes that if the mourners removed their shoes and began mourning before the burial party returned from the cemetery, shortly before dark, and the buriers later returned at night and reported that the grave had been closed while it was still day, the mourning already observed counts toward the seven and thirty days, even though they acted only out of doubt. This follows from the Rama's case where one later learns that his earlier assumption was mistaken: if it is later established that he mourned correctly, the mourning counts. [Citation 210.]
