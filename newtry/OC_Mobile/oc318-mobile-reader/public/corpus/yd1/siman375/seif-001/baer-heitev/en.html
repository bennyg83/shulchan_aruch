Another. But those who bury in the cemetery near the city and return from the city gates do not begin mourning until they are told that he was buried. This has a practical difference for one who buries his dead on the eve of a festival. End quote from Shach. There is another practical difference: above in siman 374 it was written that the custom developed that parents do not follow after their first children. If such a case occurred on the eve of a festival, they do not count from the moment they turn back from accompanying him, but also only from when they are told he was buried. Thus, if they are told before bein hashmashot that he was buried, they need not count seven after the festival; but if they are told at bein hashmashot, they must count seven after the festival. Beit Hillel. No measure of “another distant place” is mentioned here except that it is so distant that they do not know when he will be buried; this is the Rambam's view of the measure. The Ramban wrote generally that when they take him from city to city, once they hand him over to those who will take him and turn their faces back, they have despaired of him, and despair is like closure of the golel, etc. He then wrote that so too appear the words of the Rambam, who wrote that those whose practice is to send the dead to another country for burial and who do not know when he will be buried begin counting from when they turn their faces back. He afterward brought the view of Bahag, who distinguished only that once he leaves the city and they forget him from the relatives one hour before the festival, mourning falls upon them and the decree of seven is nullified. The Beit Yosef decided like the Rambam, which is why he wrote here “when it is so distant that they do not know when he will be buried.” Radbaz, question 63, also wrote like the Rambam and Ramban, but concluded that where he was buried at bein hashmashot on Friday, mourning falls even upon those who did not accompany the bier, and it counts for them as one day. Likewise if it was the eve of a festival, the festival cancels the decree of seven for them, even though the relatives did not return until after Shabbat or the festival entered, although on other days mourning does not fall upon them until the relatives return, following the Ramban's view.
