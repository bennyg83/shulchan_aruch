Another. See Baer Heitev in the name of the Shach. In the prayer book of the Gaon of Lissa it is written that if the mourners removed their shoes and began mourning before the burial attendants returned from the cemetery, near nightfall, and the attendants later returned at night and reported that the grave had been closed while it was still somewhat daytime, then although the mourners did not know this at the time and observed mourning only out of doubt, that day counts toward the seven and the thirty. This is also implied by Rama in seif 1: where one mistakenly thought the burial had occurred and later learned that he had erred, the implication is that if he later learns that his mourning was in fact halachically proper, the mourning already observed counts.
