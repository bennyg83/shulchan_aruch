To another city, etc. This implies specifically when they send him to another city. But those who bury in a cemetery near the city, those who turn back from the city gates do not begin mourning until they are told that he was buried, as the Ramban wrote and as the Beit Yosef wrote. The practical difference is for one who buries his dead on the eve of a festival, as stated in the poskim in the name of Baal Halakhot.
And if the head of the family, etc. It implies that we follow the head of the family specifically as a stringency, so that they count only from burial. But if the head of the family is with those who remained here, that does not help; nevertheless those who go with the dead count only from burial, like the view of Tosafot brought by the Beit Yosef. So Tosafot wrote in Moed Katan 22a, beginning of s.v. mahadritu apeikhu, that those who go with the dead count from when the golel is closed, whether the head of the household is at home or goes to the cemetery, and so is the custom. But the Tur wrote that everything follows the head of the household: if he remained here, both groups count from when the dead is taken out. This also appears to be the Ramban's view in Torat HaAdam, p. 47c, and the Rosh's view in chapter Elu Megalchin from his bringing the Yerushalmi there; see there. I do not understand the Beit Yosef's statement that the Ramban's text in the Yerushalmi is confused, for his text is letter for letter like the Rosh's. Nevertheless, according to the Yerushalmi text brought by the Beit Yosef, the law is proven like Tosafot's view. It seems to me that this text is primary, for the Yerushalmi asks: one teaching says these here count from when the dead leaves and those there count from when the golel is closed, and another teaching says both count from when the golel is closed. Rabbi Simon answers that everything follows the head of the family. If it were otherwise, why is it so fixed? It should also have said both count from when the dead leaves, namely when the head of the household is at home. Rather, we follow the head of the household only to be stringent. It seems to me this is the view of Raavyah brought by the Mordechai, chapter Elu Megalchin, who wrote that regarding those who went with the dead to bury him, it appears from Raavyah's words that they count from when the golel is closed; while according to Rabbi Avraham, they count from when the dead leaves the doorway together with the head of the household. He then wrote that if the head of the household was in the cemetery, in this the great authorities disagree: Raavyah explains that all count with him from when the golel is closed, etc.; see there.
Even those here, etc. From the words of Raavan, p. 38c, it is clear that this applies specifically if he can come to the grave within three days. But if he cannot come within three days, those at home count immediately and the head of the household in the cemetery counts from when the golel is closed; they are not drawn after him. He explains the Talmud's phrase "if he came within three," etc., this way. Perhaps the Mechaber and those with him also agree to this, for it is not reasonable at all that if the dead is buried in a distant place, those here should sit many days and not begin mourning until he is buried. Although Raavan's interpretation of the Talmud is forced, the reasoning is correct.
