עוכרתן ברגליה. it requires investigation דOrach Chayim siman ע"ד ruled בש"ע שהנשים יכולות להתפלל whenהן לבושות החלוק אע"פ שאינן מפסיקות למטה מהלב והם דברי א"ח שbrings ב"י שם ולמה הצריך כאן עforרת המים ובסמוך יתבאר דאפי' מים צלולים הוה כלבישת החלוק:
Until after the immersion. Their reason is that since for a convert's immersion one cannot recite the blessing before the immersion — as he is still a non-Jew — the sages did not distinguish between the immersions.
She should cover herself, etc. In Derisha he elaborated and wrote that our women who recite the blessing during immersion while naked in the water and do not make the water turbid are not acting properly — even though they clasp their arms over their bodies; for according to Maharam's reasoning that the water must be turbid so that men not see their nakedness, clasping arms would help to separate the heart from the nakedness; but for a woman there is no concern of men seeing her nakedness, since her nakedness is far below, as Beit Yosef wrote in Or Zaru'a in Orach Chayyim siman 74. In any case, his conclusion is that the prohibition of standing naked exists because of honor of the Divine Presence, and clasping arms does not help for this. In my humble view: women who do so by clasping their arms are doing well, even ab initio; and even without clasping there is support for them — for what he wrote, that there is a prohibition in standing naked due to the honor of the Divine Presence, is puzzling: why seek this reason when there is an explicit biblical prohibition: "there shall not be seen among you a shameful thing" — as written regarding the laws of separating challah in Yoreh De'ah siman 328, that a man is forbidden to separate challah and recite the blessing when nakedness is exposed; and in all cases they prohibited prayer and sacred speech when facing another's nakedness, and the same applies to one's own nakedness when exposed. But if one is within the water, even if it is clear, there is no concern about exposure — and this is obvious even to young students. For Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Rashba in Orach Chayyim siman 206: "and is the heart seeing the nakedness" — meaning both the heart and nakedness are within the water — "but as to the eyes seeing the nakedness, there is no concern, for since one's eyes are outside the water looking outward, one does not see the nakedness." We see that even the challenge of "heart sees nakedness" only applies when the heart too is within the water. And so it is proven from R' Tam cited by Beit Yosef in Orach Chayyim siman 74: when bathing in hot clear water and wishing to drink, he would cover himself with a cloth from below his heart so the heart not see the nakedness — end of quote. And if standing naked in clear water constituted exposure, what did he accomplish by covering only his heart, since he himself could see his own nakedness? Hence it is certain that the prohibition of "there shall not be seen a shameful thing" only applies to nakedness exposed outside the water — then one may not pray or speak words of holiness facing it unless one turns one's face away, as clarified in Orach Chayyim siman 75; but within the water there is no concern about exposure — it is like turning one's face. Only the concern about the "heart seeing nakedness" remains — resembling one who is dressed without a separation between heart and nakedness. And thus a woman immersing and standing in water after the immersion: the separation she makes with her hands between her heart and nakedness is certainly a complete leniency according to all opinions. And although it would be better that her heart not be in the water so that both heart and nakedness not be within the water together — as I noted from Rashba — nevertheless it is more modest that her whole body be covered up to her neck in the water, and afterward she should clasp her hands to create the separation — since the Shulchan Arukh in Orach Chayyim siman 74 seif 36 ruled this constitutes a separation. And those who stand in the water during the blessing without clasping their hands as a separation between heart and nakedness also have support — namely the Or Zaru'a I cited, that for a woman the heart does not see her nakedness since it is far below. And my father-in-law of blessed memory held that for a standing woman there is no prohibition of exposure at all according to Or Zaru'a, and she need not have her face pressed against the ground — only her buttocks covered. Therefore he had difficulty with the Talmud's challenge in chapter Mi She'Meto regarding challah — why must she be seated? According to Or Zaru'a this implies even when standing there is no concern of the heart seeing nakedness, so why must she be seated... But in any case, since Or Zaru'a and the Rosh hold that for a woman the heart does not see her nakedness — and the Shulchan Arukh has already ruled thus in Orach Chayyim siman 74 — who can object to any woman who relies on this? And all that is within the water, even if it is clear, has her nakedness covered as though she wore a garment in front and behind — and there is no concern about exposure, as we proved. There is only the concern about the heart seeing nakedness; and the best practice is to clasp her hands as a separation between heart and nakedness while in the water — and then she has fulfilled her obligation according to all opinions. And even though Rema wrote here that the custom is to cover with her garment or dress — possibly in his days that was the custom; but now women do not observe this, as the author of Derisha testified; and we have also heard that women are not careful even about clasping their hands as a separation between heart and nakedness — and it seems this too is halachically acceptable; but the best practice is to clasp the hands as we said. So it seems to me clearly.
