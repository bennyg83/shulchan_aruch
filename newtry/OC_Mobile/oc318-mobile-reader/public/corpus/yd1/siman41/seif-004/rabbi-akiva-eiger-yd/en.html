(There) It was not explained whether this is specifically when the entire olive dried, but something of it remained that did not dry — kosher, since the rest of the liver exists and also something of this olive; or whether one requires specifically that the entire olive exist, and if something of it dried — treifah. Afterward I saw Peri Megadim was uncertain about this. And it appears to me to infer from what Taz wrote s.k. 2: the intent of Rashba in that which he wrote the law in "they dried" two olive-volumes and did not cite the law in "removed" the two olive-volumes — he goes according to his view that removed liver means also the sinews and simfonot; if so, in removal of the two olive-volumes also the sinews and simfonot — it is obvious treifah, since all its hanging was removed, the entire liver was removed anyway; and likewise in the place of the gall the gall was removed — see there. It is clear he holds it is not treifah except when the entire olive was removed and nothing remained; for if removed — all the more so treifah — you find the law well in removal. Also one may infer from s.k. 6 that so is Kereti's view from what he wrote in this law "they dried" two olive-volumes — they are stringent that an olive is a third of an egg; and if we say one requires the entire olive to exist, and automatically half an egg is a stringency — all the more so if something remained it is kosher; and on this we say stringently that one requires something to remain of that third of egg — that is called an olive; but if the third of egg dried and afterward something remained — the entire olive does not help. And this is clear. Further, I still have a place of doubt in the foundation of an olive in the place of the gall: if the thickness of the place of the gall is two olive-volumes, and in the place where the gall adheres the thickness of an olive was ruined, but nevertheless in thickness an olive remained intact from outside — if this is called "there remained an olive in the place of the gall," for all the thickness is called the place of the gall; or whether one requires the olive that is adjacent to the gall specifically. And from Kereti's words above one may infer that specifically in the very place of the gall is required; for if all the thickness is called place of the gall — what stringency is there in the matter that we judge an olive as a third of an egg, namely as we explained that one requires something to remain of that third; behold all thickness is called place of the gall and something from anywhere in the thickness helps; perforce the very place of the gall is required adjacent to it; and this requires study. And nevertheless for practical law there is doubt.
