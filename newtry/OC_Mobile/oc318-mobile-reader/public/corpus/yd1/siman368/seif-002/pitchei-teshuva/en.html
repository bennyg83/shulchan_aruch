Chatam Sofer 327 writes that where the living all know the cemetery's custom and, in anticipation of death, waive their honor, the uses described are permitted, since the issue is respect for the dead rather than a prohibition of benefit. This applies even according to those who prohibit something detached and later attached. Thus burial societies customarily sell cemetery grasses for the needs of the dead and their shrouds, or for medicines for the sick and poor; established Jewish custom has halachic force.
