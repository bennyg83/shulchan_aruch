Chatam Sofer, siman 335, writes that even land not directly over a grave but designated as a public cemetery acquires a sanctity comparable to a synagogue, as Sheyarei Knesset HaGedolah explains at length in Perek Nigmar HaDin. Anything permanently designated for a mitzvah, whether prayer, Torah study, or burial, is set aside for that mitzvah and should not be used for personal benefit. The story in Berachot of a pious man sleeping in a cemetery can be explained because Torah scholars and their students may use sacred premises in ways permitted to them, as in a synagogue. It appears, however, that this applies once at least one burial has actually occurred in the field. If no one has yet been buried there and the land was merely designated for a cemetery, perhaps it has not acquired sanctity, comparable to a synagogue before prayer has actually been held there. Yet Sheyarei Knesset HaGedolah's language may imply otherwise, and the matter is lengthy.
