See Chatam Sofer responsa siman 335, who wrote that this applies even where it is not on top of a grave: a place set aside as a public cemetery becomes prohibited with the sanctity of a synagogue. He explained this well at length in Shiltei Gibborim, chapter Nigmar HaDin. The reasoning supports it: anything designated for its mitzvah, whether to pray in it, learn in it, or bury in it, is set aside for its mitzvah and one may not derive benefit from it. The incident of the pious man in Berakhot who slept in a cemetery may be explained that sages and students are permitted, just as they are permitted in a synagogue, see Orach Chayim siman 151 seif 1. It appears, however, that this is specifically where even one dead person has already been buried in that field; then the entire field is forbidden. But if no dead person has yet been buried there and it was only designated as a cemetery, it is not forbidden, as written in Orach Chayim siman 153 seif 8 regarding a synagogue, that it is not sanctified until they pray in it; see there seif 5 and the later authorities there. In truth, the language of Shiltei Gibborim there does not imply this; see there. The matter is lengthy, and this is not the place to elaborate.
