And there are those who permit with a sign or any crack, etc. And Maharshal there wrote: and I say like the words of Rambam, and according to my view even Rashi agrees with him, for what does a sign or crack help? Perhaps there is a deficiency in an ona; it is fine where it resembles a sponge that is entirely attached — then where there is some sign we see that thus they grew; but for one or two missing onot one may not permit with a sign or crack unless there is a distinction of the measure of an ona — that is, like tarfa de-asa — end; and in the words of Rashba and Ran and Mordechai and Hagahot Ashiri and Tur it appears explicitly one should not distinguish between entirely attached and one or two onot; and so from the words of Beit Yosef and from his words here; and so from the words of the Rabbenu in Darkei Moshe and in the hagahah; also according to what Ran wrote that Rashi's reason is since there is a sign it appears there is no deficiency but they stuck in order — it is explained there is no distinction; and now there is no difficulty from what Maharshal wrote "what does a sign help — ultimately there is deficiency in an ona," for since there is a sign it appears the onot were as they should be but stuck in order and there is no deficiency; also there is no logic at all to distinguish thus — examine; and so Bach ruled:
The lower ona, etc. And Maharshal there disagrees with all this and wrote: and I say that no sign helps to prove that the onot are attached — what of it if they are attached, and why is it invalid? It resembles a sponge and does not leave the category of sponge unless with a sign of crack and cut of the onot; and all the more what the expert examiners say, that each ona has a furrow (simpun) by itself — if so we see after the furrows whether they are as they should be and in their place they permit even without a crack; and again I found thus in a gloss in the name of Maharach — and it is not clear, even though the experts say they received thus as he explained — end; and perhaps Maharshal spoke only when the entire ama is attached; but when the lung is as it should be except two onot are attached — such may be permitted through simpun, for now we do not come on it from the reason it resembles a sponge but from the reason of missing, and since there is a simpun it appears there was an ona by itself but stuck in order; and so Maharam in Machazik Brachah: the words of Maharach apply specifically to two onot, but not to an entire attached ama — see there; and this is what the Rabbenu wrote: through the furrow usual to be in an ona; and so in the words of Maharach brought in Darkei Moshe; and Ran states: two attached onot, etc., if they have two furrows, etc.; and it is possible that the sign of long and thick that Mahariu and the Rabbenu mention is also in such a case, for the entire lung is as it should be except the lower ona is attached; and even though Maharshal's words appear to forbid in every matter, for his language implies he challenges even Maharach who deals with two onot — nevertheless since in this his reason is hidden and the Rabbenu holds thus and so practiced all examiners and so is found in all later examinations, one should not be stringent; but when the entire lung is attached — in this certainly one may not permit through simpun, only through sign of crack and cut, as explained:
Some say: in kids and lambs, etc. And Machazik Brachah wrote: it must mean specifically when it has no sign of onot at all; but if one side has and the other does not — it appears it has the law of sponge like adults; and it appears to me they are not called tender for this purpose except within thirty days of birth; and also they follow the mother who nurses them, for we find regarding firstborn that they are not called lambs to graze with their owners until given to priests only until thirty days; but after thirty days, even though they follow the mother and she nurses them, nevertheless they are considered as adults — so it appears to me — end; and from the simple sense we say this is after thirty days, for this itself is a great leniency in a matter not found in Talmud and poskim; and they gave it lest we add upon it except when we know certainly it is within thirty days, but from doubt — forbidden:
