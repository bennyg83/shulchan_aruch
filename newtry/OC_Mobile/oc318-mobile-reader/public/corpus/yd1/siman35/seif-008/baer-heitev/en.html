Treifah. And Taz wrote in the name of Maharshal: there was an incident in Rinus where they slaughtered a fat cow and when they examined it they found no lung — neither ama nor ona nor varda nor any crack, only it was smooth like one piece of meat; and there was one examiner who was prominent among the sages of Austria; he took a sharp knife and cut the upper membrane — that is, the upper garment and covering that had formed on it — and opened it gently and peeled it gently and separated it all from above; then they saw the lung like other lungs and permitted it; and he said he saw such a case in Austria — see end of this section 39.
Kosher. And Shach wrote: and specifically when the crack stands in its proper place; but other cracks do not help — end Maharam and Maharshal; and if the ona next to the ama is missing somewhat and the ama is larger than usual and when they inflate the lung it fills the deficiency and will be equal as it should — kosher; but if it was smaller than usual — treifah — Darkei Moshe daf 108 — see there more of these laws.
To declare treifah. And Maharshal divides and holds: if entirely attached, then where there is some sign we see that thus they grew; but for a missing ona we do not permit with a crack, only when it is of the measure of tarfa de-asa; and Shach disagrees and agrees with Rama's words that there is no distinction — one always permits with a crack — and so Bach.
And kosher. And Taz wrote: the same between ona and ona these signs also help.
Sign. And Maharshal disagrees with all these signs and does not permit if a distinction like tarfa de-asa is seen; and Shach wrote one should not be stringent except when entirely attached like a sponge; but when the lung is as it should be except two onot are attached together, one may permit through simpun; and in Hagahot HaRav he divides: specifically from ona to ama simpun helps and other signs, but not from ona to ona — in this other signs do not help, not even simpun; and other hagahot disagree that their law is equal, and such is primary — Taz.
With them. And Shach wrote: one may not permit unless within thirty days of birth with certainty; and also they follow the mother who nurses them — both are required; but after thirty days, even though they follow the mother and she nurses them, they are considered adults — Pri Chadash wrote one should not rely on these views; but from doubt we say it is after thirty days. And he also wrote: if on one side of the lamb it has onot as usual and on the other side it does not — one should declare treifah even in kids and lambs.
