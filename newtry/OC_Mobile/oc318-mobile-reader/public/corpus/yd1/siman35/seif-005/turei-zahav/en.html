That specifically above half, etc. In the examinations of Mahariu he brought on this two explanations: one — the half is toward the tip of the ona; but if found toward the root of the ona — treifah; and accordingly the measure of half is in the width of the ona. And some say one also measures in the length of the ona — that half in width that we permitted is not all of it but specifically half its length — that is, opposite the windpipe, but not the other half toward the lung — end Mahariu's words; and so I saw Maharshal explained, and one should be stringent like the latter view.
In each side three or four, etc. Maharshal wrote: what he wrote in Likutim that one may not permit only up to ten onot on both sides — that is, five on each side and no more — is the view of one who startles, and if it comes before me I will buy the finest from it.
Combines. In the hagahah of Hagahot Ashiri: an incident where on the right were two large onot and the third was recognizable by a cut to permit through it, and there was another change that on the left there were three onot; and Rav Achai forbade and R' Yosef answered: I do not know why he forbade unless he received from his teachers, for it appears to me complete permission — end; and Maharshal wrote: it appears to be stringent like Rav Achai's teachers, and it appears Rama is also stringent here, for there are two suspicious matters.
It is called a lump and kosher. And if above or below sharp like a cap — treifah — Beit Yosef; and Magid Commentary wrote: if it is wide below and round above — kosher.
And equal to the rest of the lung. It implies that if it was shorter than the hollow — treifah, for then it is not a split but extra.
But from the front, etc. Meaning: if there is a split with a hollow beneath it, we say since when it began to split it will eventually come apart entirely; but if there is no hollow there, it was explained above that our custom is to permit extra from the front. If extra is found in a bed above the onot adjacent to the spine inside the lung — it is kosher; and it appears to me treifah, for this is certainly included more in "on its back" mentioned in the Talmud than what is included inside.
