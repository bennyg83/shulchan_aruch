Below the varda is called not in the row of onot. At first glance one should forbid also that of seif 4 for this reason; but Rama's language does not imply any distinction; and the reason appears: there it is kosher either way — if it were in the row of onot it is obviously kosher; and if not in the row of onot, because the varda divides and completes the count of the onot, nevertheless kosher because it is not on its back, as Beit Yosef wrote; unlike when there is clearly a missing ona before you and you need to complete it with a clear replacement — perhaps it is called not in the row of onot and there is no valid replacement; and so I saw Maharshal ruled here to forbid and that of seif 4 to permit. My master may he live long wrote: a case came before him several times — a lung with no suspicious matter except on the left a protrusion from the ama at the cut between ona and ama exactly where the varda usually stands opposite on the right; that protrusion had a complete pouch, was placed in it, had no varda form at all, and was equal to the ama on the front as if cut from it; he permitted because that protrusion was neither extra from the front nor extra varda and there is no suspicious matter except from the pouch — end. And it appears to me even if it had varda form there is no prohibition, for it is called extra from the front and kosher for us; prohibition of two vardot is only when both stand on the right in their proper place and are not like finger-thickness between them, as written above; and the pouch certainly does not forbid, for we do not find that because of a pouch extra there is prohibition, as explained. Maharshal wrote: if the lung's extra stands in the row of onot and leans slightly outward through inflation — treifah; but Beit Yosef wrote: specifically protruding extra, but if bent and goes upward — kosher since rooted in a permitted place — end.
