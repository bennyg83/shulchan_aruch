(Siman 129 in Shulchan Aruch seif 8 in Hagah.) Since the gentile did not know, etc. See Taz seif kaf-dalet and Shach seif kaf-alef, who wondered greatly at Rama, for in the responsum of the Rosh he wrote the opposite — even if he informed that he sailed it is also permitted; and it appears to me: Rashba wrote regarding one who brought wine in a wagon or ship, etc., if he informed that he sails to the bathhouse or synagogue it is forbidden; and what he wrote that we say "he heard the sound of a shofar blown for Shabbat" — if there is a roundabout way it is permitted, for we are not in the presumption of Shabbat observers as befits in the eyes of the gentile, and he trembles every hour lest he come now — so too here the gentile trembles every hour lest he come now; and he resolves: there regarding the sound of a shofar he trembles lest he remember the wine and come immediately, unlike regarding the bathhouse where certainly if he remembers the wine he will not come immediately from the bathhouse, for in any case he must stay a while to dress; and Raah wrote to resolve: granted there regarding the sound of a shofar that he went because of panic — we properly say he trembles lest he remember the wine and come; unlike here where he informed he sails with settled mind — he does not tremble lest he come now, since from the outset he went with settled mind. If so, according to Rashba who holds wherever he is not compelled to stay we say the gentile trembles — in our case even if he informed he sails it is also permitted, since there is no compulsion to stay; unlike according to Raah, who holds even where he is not compelled to stay, wherever there is no panic he does not tremble — here where he informed he sails it is forbidden, since there is no panic, for Israel thinks he locked with a key. According to this one can say the Rosh holds like Rashba — therefore even if he informed he sails it is also permitted; unlike Rama who holds like Raah and forbids if he informed he sails. Requires study.
