Since the gentile did not know that the Jew had gone far away. The master was not precise in this and did not examine the responsum of the Rosh itself, which writes explicitly as follows: “And not only if he did not inform him that he was going far away, where it is permitted because he says to himself, ‘He may come at any moment,’ and he is afraid; but even if he informed him that he was going far away, the gentile says to himself, ‘When the Jew remembers that he did not lock with a key, he will return home and lock it,’ and he is afraid, etc.” End quote. The master did not examine this, only the Beit Yosef, who brought this responsum but did not copy the phrase “and not only, etc.” Although above we said that one who leaves on his own and informs him that he is going far away makes it forbidden, here it is different, for you must say that he forgot to lock with the key, since he inserted the bolt and began closing the house but did not finish it. Even if he had not yet begun, we say that presumably he forgot to close it, for otherwise how would he leave his house open without a guard? This is like one who leaves in panic above. So it appears to my humble opinion as obvious; the master’s laziness caused the teaching not to be said from his mouth.
