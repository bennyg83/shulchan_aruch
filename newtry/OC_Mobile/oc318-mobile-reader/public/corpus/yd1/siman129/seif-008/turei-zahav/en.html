Since the non-Jew did not know that the Jew had gone far away. The Rav was not precise in this and did not examine the responsum of the Rosh itself, which explicitly states: It is not only where he did not inform him that he was going far away that it is permitted, because the non-Jew says, he may come at any moment, and is afraid. Rather, even if he informed him that he was going far away, the non-Jew says, when the Jew remembers that he did not lock the house, he will return and lock it, and he is afraid, etc., end quote. The Rav examined only the Beit Yosef, who cited this responsum but omitted the words, it is not only, etc. Although we said above that whenever one leaves of his own accord and informs the non-Jew that he is going far away it is forbidden, this case is different. You must necessarily say that he forgot to close the lock, because he inserted the bolt and began closing the house but did not finish. Even if he had not yet begun, we assume that he forgot to close it, for otherwise how could he have left his house open and unguarded? It is comparable to one who left in alarm, as above. This appears entirely obvious in my humble opinion. The master's laziness caused him not to state the teaching accurately.
