Closed — Taz wrote: for us, since we hold above siman 128 seifs 3–4 that there is a prohibition only if the non-Jew himself closes the door from inside, and on a doubt we do not say perhaps he himself closed it with a lock — therefore we should permit here even without a Jew having closed it first — end of his words. And see below siman 130 toward the end of seif 8 in the gloss.
That was explained. The Shach explains that this means that there it was explained that we do not suspect that she touched it for nesech unless she touched it for her own benefit. Even if she intentionally removed the tap, it is permitted, for since her intent was not for nesech, it is contact by a non-Jew through something else and without intent. We do not suspect that she herself touched the wine in the barrel, or touched what came out as she drew it, which would then prohibit what remained inside through nitzok, since she has no need to do so. Likewise, what the Rama wrote above that the non-Jewish woman is not believed refers to a case where she cannot take the wine without touching it with her hand, such as where there is no tap in the barrel and she must draw from the barrel by hand. But where there is a tap, even if it is known that she took wine from the cellar, it is permitted for us nowadays in a case of loss. The Taz, however, prohibits if she certainly removed the wine through the tap even nowadays. I have already written the reason for their dispute in siman 124, and in Nekudot HaKesef he disagrees; see there.
