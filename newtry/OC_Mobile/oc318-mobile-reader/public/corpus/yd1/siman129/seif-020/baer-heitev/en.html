Closed. Taz writes that nowadays, since we rule in siman 128:3-4 that prohibition applies only when the non-Jew locked the door from inside, and we do not assume this from doubt, the wine should be permitted here even if the Jew did not first lock it. See the gloss at the end of siman 130:8.
As explained. Shach explains that there it was established that we do not suspect touch for libation unless the non-Jew touched for his own benefit. Even if she intentionally removed the spigot, because she did not intend libation this is indirect unintentional touch. We do not suspect that she inserted her hand into the barrel or touched the outgoing wine, which would forbid the contents through a connecting stream, because she had no need to do so. Rama's statement that the maidservant is not believed refers to a barrel without a spigot, where she must draw by hand. If there is a spigot, even where it is known she took wine from the cellar, nowadays it is permitted in a case of loss. Taz forbids where she certainly drew through the spigot. Nekudot HaKesef disagrees.
