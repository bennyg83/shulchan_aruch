To draw water from the cistern, etc. Meaning, if so, he has an excuse for why he entered. Since he would not be apprehended as a thief merely for entering, the wine should consequently be forbidden. Nevertheless, it is permitted because the place was initially locked with a key. He would not forge the Jew's key and open the lock without the Jew's knowledge, for then he would be apprehended as a thief and is afraid. So wrote the Rivash at length, as the Beit Yosef cites in his name. This implies that had it not initially been locked with a key, the wine would be forbidden, because he would not be apprehended as a thief, since he could say, I entered to draw water. This is astonishing to me. Even without the prior lock it should be permitted, since he found the entrance open. We stated above in siman 128, seifim 3 and 4, that the wine is forbidden only when the non-Jew locked the door from inside. Out of doubt we do not say that perhaps he himself locked it, as I wrote above at the beginning of this siman in the name of the Rashba. Accordingly, here too we should permit it even if the Jew did not initially lock it. Possibly the Rivash does not accept the Rashba's ruling above. But according to our established ruling, there is no need to say that the Jew initially locked it. This appears in my humble opinion.
Likewise, regarding a woman who hid the key to her cellar. She did not find it in its place, and she found a jug of wine, etc. This is the correct reading.
And there is an indication supporting the matter, etc. It is explained there in the Mordechai that if there is reason to attribute the sealing to a Jew, it is permitted.
However, nowadays, etc. This refers only to the latter ruling, where there is an indication supporting the matter, etc. The Darkei Moshe explicitly wrote: Therefore, it appears that one should permit such a case only nowadays, end quote. But in the first case, where it is permitted because the maidservant is not believed, it is implied that if she certainly drew wine through the spigot, it is forbidden even nowadays. This is as I wrote in siman 124 regarding one who pierced a barrel with a knife; see there.
