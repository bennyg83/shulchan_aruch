If a Jew has wine in his storehouse, and the opening of the storehouse was closed with one key, and he found the opening open, and one non-Jew admits that he opened it in order to draw water from a pit that is in the storehouse in order to build his house — it is permitted. {Rama: A homeowner who told his maidservant to set the table, and he found wine on his table, and his non-Jewish maidservant says that she took the wine from his cellar where there is much wine: the wine in the cellar is permitted, for the non-Jewish maidservant is not believed, and say that she took it from elsewhere (a responsum in Mordechai, perek R. Yishmael); but that which is on the table is forbidden. And similarly, regarding a woman who hid the key of her cellar and did not find it in its place, and she found a jar of wine hidden in straw, and a non-Jewish maidservant says that she took the wine: the wine in the cellar is permitted and the maidservant is not believed (ibid., and Agudah, and Hagahot Ashiri). But if thieves entered the cellar and drew wine and left the spigots open, and afterwards the spigots were found closed, and a non-Jewish maidservant said that she closed them — since the non-Jewish maidservant was alone with the wine and is not “caught as a thief,” and there are indications that she closed them, the wine is forbidden (Mordechai at the end of perek R. Yishmael). However, in our times, when non-Jews do not pour libations, it is permitted, as was explained above in simanim 123 and 124.}
