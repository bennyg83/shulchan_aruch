For the gentile woman is not trusted. But if it is known she took from the wine in the cellar, it is forbidden — that is according to the Gemara's law; or such as where she cannot take without touching with her hand, such as when there is no tap in the barrel and she must draw from the barrel or from the vat with her hand, and similarly — for even though in such a case she can also be careful not to touch, such as drawing with a vessel and keeping her hand from touching at all — nevertheless we suspect perhaps she touched it incidentally, as above siman 128 seif 4 in Hagah; but where there is a tap and her hand did not touch the wine in the barrel itself except through the tap — even if it is known she took the wine from the cellar, it is permitted for us nowadays in a case of loss, because it is gentile touch through something else without intent, as above siman 124 and below in the next s.k.
But that same vineyard, etc. For it is not customary to steal from this vineyard and hide within it — Tur and Rashba; but without this reason it would be permitted, for even though in evident proximity we follow the majority — here since it was found within the vineyard itself it is more reliable, as written in s.k. 27.
