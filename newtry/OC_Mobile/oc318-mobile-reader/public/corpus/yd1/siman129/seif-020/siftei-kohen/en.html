Because the non-Jewish woman is not believed. But if it is known that she took wine from the cellar, it is forbidden. This follows the law of the Gemara. Alternatively, the case may be one where she cannot take the wine without touching it with her hand, such as where there is no spigot in the barrel and she must draw wine by hand from the barrel or vat. Although even in such a case it is possible for her to avoid touching the wine, for example by drawing it with a vessel and carefully avoiding all contact with her hand, nevertheless we are concerned that she may have touched it incidentally while occupied with the task, as above in siman 128, seif 4, in the Rama's gloss. Where there is a spigot, however, so that her hand itself did not touch the wine in the barrel and she acted only through the spigot, then even if it is known that she took wine from the cellar, according to us nowadays it is permitted in a case of loss. This is because it is contact by a non-Jew through another object without intent, as explained above in siman 124 and below in the following seif katan.
Siman 125. This is the reading in the books and in Olat Tamid, and so it appears in Darkei Moshe. I do not know what relevance that siman has here. It appears to be an error, and the text should read siman 124, or perhaps siman 128. The meaning would then be that in seif 4 there it was explained that we are not concerned that a non-Jew touched wine for the purpose of idolatrous libation unless he touched it for his own benefit. Since contemporary non-Jews do not pour wine for idolatrous libation, here too we are not concerned that the woman deliberately touched it with her hand for that purpose. In siman 124, seif 24, it was explained that nowadays, since non-Jews are not idolaters, even if she deliberately removed the spigot it is permitted, because since her intent was not idolatrous libation it is contact by a non-Jew through another object without intent. The same applies here. We are not concerned that she touched the wine in the barrel itself with her hand, or that she touched the wine that flowed outside when she drew it, which would cause the wine inside to become forbidden through nitzok, because she has no need to do so.
