For the gentile woman is not trusted. But if it is known she took from the wine in the cellar, it is forbidden — that is according to the Gemara's law; or such as where she cannot take without touching with her hand, such as when there is no tap in the barrel and she must draw from the barrel or from the vat with her hand, and similarly — for even though in such a case she can also be careful not to touch, such as drawing with a vessel and keeping her hand from touching at all — nevertheless we suspect perhaps she touched it incidentally, as above siman 128 seif 4 in Hagah; but where there is a tap and her hand did not touch the wine in the barrel itself except through the tap — even if it is known she took the wine from the cellar, it is permitted for us nowadays in a case of loss, because it is gentile touch through something else without intent, as above siman 124 and below in the next s.k.
Siman 125. So it is in the books and in Ateret Zekenim, and so it is in Darkei Moshe; but I know of no relevance there to here. It appears to be an error and should read siman 124, or it should read siman 128. The meaning is that there in seif 4 it is explained that we are not concerned that he touched for libation except when he touched for his own benefit, since non-Jews nowadays do not pour libations. If so, here too we are not concerned that she intentionally touched with her hand to pour libation. For there in seif 24 it is explained that nowadays, since non-Jews are not worshippers of avodah zarah, even if she intentionally removed the tap it is permitted, because since her intent is not for libation, it is contact of a non-Jew through another object unintentionally. If so, the same applies here, and we are not concerned that perhaps she touched with her own hand the wine in the barrel, or touched what went outside when she pulled it, in which case what is inside would also be forbidden through nitzok, for she has no need to do so. This is explicit in Mordechai there, who concludes regarding the incident where the key was hidden, mentioned above: even if the non-Jewish woman pulled it and the tap extended outside the tube, if the non-Jewish woman does not know, it is the force of a non-Jew unintentionally, end quote. Thus we are not concerned that she touched with her hand; he required that the non-Jewish woman not know only because otherwise it is her force. But for us, who hold above in siman 124 that the force of a non-Jew and contact of a non-Jew through another object are permitted nowadays, the same applies here. However, this is specifically in a case of loss, as I wrote there in s.k. 71.
