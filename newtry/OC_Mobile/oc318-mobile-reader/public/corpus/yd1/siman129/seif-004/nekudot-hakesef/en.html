(Ibid., Shulchan Aruch, seif 18) It is unnecessary to say this where the only Jewish vineyard there is that particular vineyard, etc. This requires study. If we know with certainty that a barrel was stolen from this Jewish vineyard and it is found being safeguarded within that vineyard, do we say that since one barrel is known to have been stolen, this is presumably that barrel? Or perhaps we say that a stolen barrel would not be safeguarded in the same vineyard. Likewise, there is room to question a case in which we know with certainty that nothing was stolen from any non-Jewish vineyard, but we do not know whether anything was stolen from this Jewish vineyard. Do we say that since we know nothing was stolen from a non-Jewish vineyard, the consideration that thieves do not ordinarily steal from one vineyard and conceal the item within it no longer applies, and the barrel is permitted? Or perhaps that consideration remains in force, and this barrel came from elsewhere. Nevertheless, where there is no possibility that it came from elsewhere, and we also know that nothing was stolen from the surrounding non-Jewish vineyards, or even from more distant ones, it appears that the barrel may be permitted, because the presumption that thieves do not act in this manner has been weakened.
