Many travelers pass there. So wrote the Rashba. Tosafos in the chapter HaSocher, folio 70a, likewise wrote in the name of the Rashbam to be lenient where it is a public thoroughfare, even if the passersby are non-Jews. They wrote that we are not concerned that someone touched it, because nowadays they do not have an attachment to idolatrous libation. They concluded that this applies specifically where the opening is narrow. But with a vat or cup whose opening is wide, it is easy to touch, and people commonly touch them incidentally, end quote. The Beit Yosef cites them, and the Darkei Moshe wrote that there is a dispute between the Rashba and Rashbam. According to the Rashbam, it need not be stoppered, for if it were stoppered, even a wide opening would be permitted. The Derishah distinguished as follows. Wherever there remains an attachment to libation, as under the law of the Gemara, the Rashba required it to be stoppered even on a public thoroughfare and even when the opening is narrow. Tosafos, however, discuss the present era, when the prohibition is not because of libation but only lest the non-Jew exchange it for his own benefit. In such a case, an open vessel is permitted when its opening is narrow. This appears to me to be the primary explanation. Even nowadays, although there is no attachment to libation, when contact is nearly certain it is forbidden, as the Beit Yosef wrote in the name of the Rashba at the beginning of siman 128 regarding leaving wine secluded with a non-Jew. Therefore, here too, with a wide opening it is nearly certain that he touched it, and it is forbidden even now. The Rama therefore ruled correctly in siman 128, seif 4, to distinguish nowadays between a narrow and wide opening.
