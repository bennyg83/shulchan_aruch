And all this refers to. See the responsum of Shevut Yaakov part 2 siman 72, where the question was posed: a Jew who rented a cellar in the home of a non-Jew, stored wine there with lock and seal as required, and one night thieves came and broke open the door of the house. Not finding what they sought in the non-Jew's house, they also broke open the door of the cellar. The next morning a young maidservant noticed that thieves had been in the house and that the cellar door was open as well; she ran in alarm to the Jew, who went and found the barrels intact and sealed as before — what is the law for that wine? He responded leniently, as explained here: if he found the barrels as they were before, it is permitted. Even though at first glance one might forbid it on account of the non-Jew who lives in that house that was broken into — we might suspect that he and his household went into the cellar afterward and poured a libation on the wine — nevertheless there is no cause for concern in our times, since the non-Jew fears: the Jew rented the cellar and a Jew lives in that city, and if [the non-Jew] is found to have [tampered with the wine] he will lose his rental fee and be seized as the main thief, who robbed the wine — or else he should have immediately notified the Jew that the cellar was broken into, etc. See there.
