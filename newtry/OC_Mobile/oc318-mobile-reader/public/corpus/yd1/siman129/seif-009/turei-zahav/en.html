And specifically that they went out in panic. So is the opinion of the Rambam; and they learned from what we say in chapter HaSocher in this incident: they heard the sound of a merchant — they went out, etc.; since the Talmud mentioned the incident was thus — learn from it the matter depends on this; and I already wrote according to the Tur — and he is the essence — that also in the incident where he heard shofar blowing and heard them calling to synagogue it is specifically so; and the reason here is specifically they went out in panic — Beit Yosef wrote: if not through panic one should concern lest he saw the Jew in the market and left him there and came home to libate and is not afraid, for he left him in the market; but when they go out through panic, sometimes a person leaves his friend in the market and when he comes home finds they preceded him home — from the panic one passes before him and he does not notice him; therefore he concerns lest the Jew preceded him — end; what he wrote that without panic one should concern lest he saw him in the market, etc. — I wonder: from uncertainty do we forbid wine? And what difference from that case siman 128 seif 3 where from uncertainty we do not say he locked with a key even though there is a bolt inside; and as proven also at the beginning of siman 129 as we copied there — except it is clear they did not forbid stam yayin until the prohibition is clear, as proven also from the mishnah where he did not inform he sails and we do not say perhaps the gentile thinks Israel sails as is the truth; and likewise in seif 10 we are lenient from uncertainty; and likewise in many places. Also what Beit Yosef wrote: if they went out in panic even though he saw him in the market he does not notice him — this is excessive leniency, for the gentile's entry was not in panic but the exit alone; and many times a person goes out alarmed from the sound of noise outside and when the noise subsides his mind settles — all the more here where it states the panic was because of a bridegroom or a funeral: when he returns home there is no panic here; and the Talmud's author and poskim should have mentioned that the entry was also in panic — therefore it appears in my humble opinion that what it states "they went out in panic" is because of a negative commandment that the wine is forbidden — the gentile does not tremble that the Jew guards the wine; for if he does not trust the gentile, why did he go outside and abandon his wine and not fear lest the gentile lock the door as he actually did and libate his wine — for he has connection in the house; therefore they required he went out in panic and did not remember his wine at that time and the gentile concerns lest he already remembered his wine and came home; and according to this, if the gentile saw him in the market at the time of his return home — clearly the wine is forbidden whenever the gentile closed the door from inside, even though the exit was in panic; and we do not say the panic causes him to concern lest he did not see well — we have no proof for this leniency even though at entry he was still in panic; and without panic there is no place to be stringent from the side of lest he saw him in the market, for from uncertainty we are not stringent except because the gentile thinks the Jew trusts him — for otherwise why abandon his wine; and even though at the beginning of this siman regarding leaving in a shop and not informing he sails it is permitted — there it is different, for we do not know clearly he closed the door — we say he did not close it and trembles lest he come; unlike here where we have a counterpart before us that the door is certainly closed — only that we should permit because Israel already preceded to the upper story; if there is no panic at time of exit the gentile says: since he went with settled mind he certainly remembered then his wine — why would he precede himself so much; therefore we are stringent since the door is closed before us; therefore they required the exit was in panic and he did not remember his wine and already remembered and preceded himself. So appears clear in my humble opinion.
