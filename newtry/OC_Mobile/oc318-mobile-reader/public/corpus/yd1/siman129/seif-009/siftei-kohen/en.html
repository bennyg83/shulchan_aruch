"Since he did not know," etc. Darkei Moshe writes this in the name of Responsa Rosh, kelal 19, siman 13, and it is likewise indicated in the Shulchan Aruch. This is astonishing, for the Rosh explicitly writes there the opposite, that even if the non-Jew knows that the Jew is going a considerable distance, it is permitted. His words are: "It appears to me that the wine is permitted. A proof is from the final chapter of Avodah Zarah: a Jew and a non-Jew were sitting in a boat and drinking wine. The Jew heard the shofar blasts announcing Shabbos and went up. Rava said the wine is permitted, because the non-Jew says, 'Now he will remember his wine and return.' If you object on account of Shabbos, Issur Giora told me that when I was a non-Jew I used to say that Jews do not observe Shabbos. All the more so in this case it is permitted, because the non-Jew says, 'Now the Jew will remember that he did not lock the door and will return.' Not only where he did not tell the non-Jew that he was going far, in which case it is permitted because the non-Jew thinks he may return at any moment and is afraid; even if he told the non-Jew that he was going far, it is permitted, because the non-Jew says that when the Jew remembers he did not lock the door he will return home and lock it, and therefore the non-Jew is afraid," etc. End of quotation. This explicitly shows that even when he informed him that he was going far it is permitted. It is apparent that the Rama had before him the old Venice edition of the Responsa Rosh printed in 5312, which reads: "All the more so in this case it is permitted, because the non-Jew says, 'Now the Jew will remember that he did not lock the door and will return.' Not only if he did not tell the non-Jew that he was going far is it permitted, because the non-Jew says that when the Jew remembers he did not lock the door he will return home and lock it, and therefore the non-Jew is afraid," etc. These words appear defective, and the Rama emended them to mean that only if he did not tell the non-Jew he was going far is it permitted. But the truth indicates that even in the old edition the intended meaning is as in the later Venice edition, as I copied above. Moreover, the Rosh proves his ruling from the case of the Jew and non-Jew drinking wine, and there even when he informed him that he was going away it is permitted because the non-Jew may think that he will now remember his wine and return, as I wrote in seif katan 19. Further, according to the Rosh's own reason, that the non-Jew thinks that when the Jew remembers he did not lock the door he will return and lock it, it is certainly permitted even when he told him he was going far. The Mechaber's words also appear this way. This requires study.
