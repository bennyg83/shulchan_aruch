(Siman 129, Shulchan Aruch, seif 8, Rama's gloss) Since the non-Jew did not know, etc. See Taz, seif katan 14, and Shach, seif katan 21, who expressed great astonishment at the Rama, because in a responsum of the Rosh he wrote the opposite, that even if he informed the non-Jew that he would be away for a substantial period, it is nevertheless permitted. It appears to me as follows. The Rashba wrote regarding one who brought wine in a wagon or ship, etc., that if he informed the non-Jew that he was going away to the bathhouse or synagogue, it is forbidden. What is different about the case where we say that if the non-Jew heard the sound of the shofar announcing Shabbos and there is an indirect path, the wine is permitted, because in the non-Jew's eyes we are not presumed to observe Shabbos properly, and he is afraid at every moment that perhaps the Jew will come now? If so, the same should apply here, and the non-Jew should be afraid at every moment that perhaps the Jew will come now. He answers that there, with the sound of the shofar, the non-Jew is afraid lest the Jew remember the wine and return immediately. By contrast, in the case of the bathhouse, even if the Jew remembers the wine he will certainly not return immediately from the bathhouse, because he at least needs some time to dress himself. The Ra'ah wrote a different answer: there, in the case of the shofar, since the Jew left in haste, we properly say that the non-Jew fears lest he remember the wine and return. Here, however, where the Jew informed him that he was leaving for a substantial period, and departed in a settled state of mind, the non-Jew does not fear that he will return now, since from the outset he left calmly. Thus, according to the Rashba, who holds that wherever the Jew is not compelled to delay we say that the non-Jew is afraid, in our case even if the Jew informed him that he would be away for a substantial period it is permitted, since there is nothing compelling him to delay. By contrast, according to the Ra'ah, even where there is no necessity to delay, whenever there was no haste the non-Jew is not afraid. Therefore here, where the Jew informed him that he would be away for a substantial period, it is forbidden, since there was no haste because the Jew thought that he had locked it with a key. According to this, one can say that the Rosh holds like the Rashba, and therefore even if he informed him that he would be away for a substantial period it is permitted, whereas the Rama holds like the Ra'ah and forbids it if he informed him that he would be away. There is therefore no difficulty.
