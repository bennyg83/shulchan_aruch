And he left him alone with the wine and went out. Even though in all these cases the gentile has ownership in the wine, it is permitted even if he stayed, etc. Language of the Tur; and Perishah seif 2 wrote the reason: since the gentile knew the Jew knows he is there beside the wine, he is afraid, thinking now the Jew will remember his wine and come and see — as above siman 128 seif 2.
L'chatchila he should not leave, etc. See above siman 118 s.k. 31.
Even though he stayed, etc. Since he did not inform him that he is sailing, as immediately below.
Because he is afraid, etc. As Beit Yosef in the name of Mordechai at the end of the chapter R. Yishmael: a Jew who has a gentile in his house and left wine without supervision — since their master's fear is upon them, they are in the presumption of being kosher, as they would be seized as thieves; however l'chatchila it is forbidden to do so — end of Darkei Moshe; and it is in Beit Yosef in this siman.
That he closed, etc. But if he closed, etc., we are concerned since he has ownership in the house and can excuse himself that he stands there permissibly, for the Jew left him there — Rashba; meaning: if he had no such excuse it would be permitted since he is seized as a thief for the entry, as above siman 128 seif 4.
[The store or that he distanced. Bach wrote: even if he closed and distanced he forbids only when barrels are open or sealed while waiting long enough to remove from the bodies of the barrels etc., as below regarding informing he is departing—and this is plain.]
A crack or a hole. See above siman 128 seif 3.
That there is a winding path. The Ran wrote: that which we require a winding path applies specifically when the gentile holds the barrel in his hand or on his shoulder; but when he left it and Israelites go in and out, we do not require a winding path, for at every moment he fears lest an Israelite now come and see him approaching the barrel — end. And Bach wondered at the Ran, for where there is no winding path, even in his own store one should be concerned that he keeps the entrance open and sees from a distance whether an Israelite is coming or not, and does not fear; and such is the view of the Tur, and thus we hold — end. But he wondered in vain, for as Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe wrote — even the Ran agrees with this, and he only excludes the case where the gentile cannot see whether the Israelite is coming — see there; and nevertheless when holding the barrel in his hand we require a winding path, for otherwise it is impossible that he not see him when nearby and immediately withdraw his hand; but to move away from the barrel one cannot do so immediately — examine.
Or he informed him that he is sailing. This applies to the entire matter above, as is in the Mishnah — unlike the Derishah; and see above s.k. 20.
In a great loss. That is because there are poskim who hold that sealing the stopper does not constitute a seal; but with a genuine single seal, it suffices b'di'eved even without great loss, as below siman 130 seif 2.
