Who closed. Bach writes that even if he closed the door and went away, he forbids only when the barrels were open, or sealed and enough time elapsed to remove their covers, as stated below concerning one who informed the non-Jew that he was departing.
A winding path. Ran writes that a winding path is required only when the non-Jew holds the barrel in his hand or carries it on his shoulder. If a non-Jew is left in a shop and the Jew goes in and out, no winding path is needed, because he constantly fears that the Jew may arrive and see him near the barrels. Beit Yosef explains that the case is where the non-Jew cannot see the Jew approaching. Even so, when he holds the barrel, a winding path is required because if there is only one approach he can see the Jew and immediately remove his hand, though he cannot quickly move far from the barrel. Taz questions this and explains instead that in a house or shop the Jew may have gone only a very short distance and can return instantly, whereas a wagon or ship is in an open field and the non-Jew can see him far away. Taz nevertheless recommends stringency even in a shop or when transporting wine between houses.
Forbidden. Nowadays there is no concern for touch unless the non-Jew obtains benefit, such as by drinking or exchanging, as stated in siman 128:4. Beit Yosef writes that a small hole raises no concern for substitution if one can remove wine through it but cannot insert anything through it. Shach.
A seal. One actual seal suffices after the fact even without great loss, as stated in siman 130:2. Shach. Taz writes that even in major loss there is no permission unless the plaster has dried.
