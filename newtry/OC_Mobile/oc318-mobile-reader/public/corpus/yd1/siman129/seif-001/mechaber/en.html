The law of one who leaves wine in the hand of a non-Jew while a Jew goes out and comes in (and similar). In it are 20 seifim. If one leaves a non-Jew in his shop and went out; or he had wine on a ship or in a wagon and left it with the non-Jew and went to the beit knesset or to the bathhouse; or a non-Jew was transporting for him a barrel of wine from place to place and he left him alone with the wine and went out — it is permitted, even if he remained away for a long time, because the non-Jew is afraid that at any moment he may come and see him. And this is provided that it is not known that the non-Jew closed the shop, or that he moved the wagon or the ship in a way that they cannot see him. (And even if he closed the shop, so long as there is a crack or a hole through which one can see inside, it is considered like it is open.) In what case is this stated? When there is a winding route by which he can come suddenly without being seen; and similarly, when he went to the bathhouse, he went by a winding route in a way that the non-Jew does not know that he went to the bathhouse. But if the non-Jew saw that he went to enter the bathhouse, he knows that he will not hurry to come. And similarly, if he said to the non-Jew whom he left in the shop, “Guard for me,” he knows that he removed himself from guarding. And similarly, if he said to the non-Jew who is transporting the barrel, “Go and I will come after you,” or he informed him that he is departing: if the barrels are open, once they are out of his sight they are forbidden. If they are sealed: if in his departing there is time for him to remove the covers of the barrel entirely, whether of clay or of lime, and to return it and to wipe it dry, it is forbidden.
{Rama: And there are those who say that if they are sealed, they are permitted in every case, for one seal suffices for wine; and one may rely on this in a case of great loss; and see below siman 130.}
