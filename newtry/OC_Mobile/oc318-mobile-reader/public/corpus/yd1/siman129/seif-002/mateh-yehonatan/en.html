(There in Shach seif kaf-chet.) Even if most of the city's thieves, etc. Requires study, for in the Gemara they permit only because of double doubt, etc.; but when most of the city's thieves are gentiles there is no double doubt — see there. According to the quick reading it appears to me the Mechaber holds like the Rambam, who holds that what we hold "majority and proximity follow the majority" is not a certainty like a majority but is a safek — see there; and likewise Rambam in Laws of Yom Tov regarding a dove coop — see there. If so, here it deals when there is also a minority of Jews who are close to the wine, and it is majority and proximity which is a safek — and it is properly permitted because it is double doubt. But on this it is difficult at the beginning, where he said if most thieves are Jews it is permitted: if it deals when there is a minority of gentile thieves who are close, why should it be fully permitted — it should at least be forbidden from the side of safek, for majority and proximity is safek. Therefore one must say the Mechaber holds like the Rosh, brought in Asifat Zekenim chapter 1 of Bava Metzia regarding one who jumped from the counted ones into it — exempt from tithe; and they challenge: we captured them and they moved — it is "everything that separates from the majority separates"; and they answer: what we say "everything that separates" is not certainly from the majority but nevertheless is safek — therefore exempt from tithe, for the Merciful One said "certainly" and not safek. According to this, here since there is a small amount of Jewish thieves, therefore even though "everything that separates" is safek, and here there is another safek with it — it is double doubt. But above at the beginning, where there is one safek, nevertheless since most thieves are Jews, even if a minority are gentile thieves, it is not double doubt, for it is more to rely on Jewish thieves who are the majority than to rely on the minority; and regarding tithe the Merciful One said "certainly obligated in tithe" and this is not certain — unlike here where there is another safek with it: this safek of "everything that separates" is properly double doubt, for alone it would not be double doubt; and when I say another safek with it — there is double doubt. Requires study.
