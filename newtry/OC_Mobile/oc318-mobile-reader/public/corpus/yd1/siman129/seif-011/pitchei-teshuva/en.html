All this refers to. See Responsa Shevut Yaakov, part 2, siman 72. He was asked about a Jew who rented a cellar in a non-Jew's house, placed wine there under lock and seal as required, and one night thieves broke into the house. They did not find what they were seeking in the non-Jew's residence, so they also broke open the cellar door. The next morning a young maid discovered the break-in and that the cellar door was open. In alarm she ran to the Jew, who came and found the barrels whole and sealed exactly as before. He permitted the wine, as explained here, because if the barrels are found as they were previously, the wine is permitted. Although one might argue that because the non-Jew lives in the same house, once the thieves opened the cellar we should suspect that the non-Jew and his household later entered and poured wine for idolatrous use, there is no such concern nowadays. The non-Jew is afraid, because the Jew rented the cellar and lives in the same city. If he is found inside, he will lose the rent and be treated as a thief, since people will say he was the principal thief who stole from the wine. Otherwise he should have immediately informed the Jew that the cellar had been broken into. See there.
