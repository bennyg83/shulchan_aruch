שאין דרך העובדי כוכבים מפסיקתם כו'. כתב הב"ח ובמקום הפסד מרובה יש לסמוך אהרא"ש וטור להתיר אף כשדרך עובדי כוכבים מפסיקתה אם נעולות הן בלילה נ"ל עכ"ל. ולפעד"נ דגם הרשב"א והמחבר מודים לזה דלא הצריכו שיהא אין דרך העובדי כוכבים מפסיקתה אלא כשאין דלתות השכונה ננעלות בלילה והרא"ש (ורבינו ירוחם) דהצריך שיהא דלתות השכונה ננעלות בלילה מיירי כשדרך הרבים מפסיקתה ביום והשתא הרא"ש והרשב"א לא פליגי ודלא כב"י וד"מ שכתבו דפליגי זה נ"ל ברור:

and requires study, for in the Gemara they permit only because of double doubt; and Rashi: safek gentiles safek Jews; and we lean gentiles lest they opened only for money, and when they saw it was wine they left — evidently it deals only with safek gentiles and Jews, namely when gentile thieves and Jews are equal; or when unknown whether majority gentile thieves or majority Jewish thieves; but with majority gentile thieves — no, for there is no double doubt, since majority are gentile thieves; and so appears from the words of Rabbeinu Yerucham; and one can resolve with difficulty — I shortened since this law is not common among us, for it is not customary to hide money in wine barrels — as the Rosh wrote. Taz wrote: and likewise thieves who pierced a barrel with a knife and in their hand a pot and drew wine into it — some say the wine inside should be permitted because of double doubt lest because he is agitated with this pot he did not touch the wine but immediately when the knife passed the thickness of the walls the wine came out and he did not touch it with the knife; and we lean he touched with the knife since the thief did not intend except to pierce so wine would come out and did not concern himself with touching — it is his force without intent and permitted; and one may be lenient nowadays since they do not libate — until here his language. It appears from his words that if it is known he touched with the knife it is forbidden, for there is no double doubt; and his words are astonishing, for never will one find such double doubt, for with that reason alone — since he did not intend except to pierce — it is permitted; and in truth he understood thus from Mordechai's words that Beit Yosef brought, who permits because of double doubt; and it is not so, for his conclusion is to permit from that reason alone even in the time of the Gemara; and likewise the Rav above siman 124 seif 24 permitted from that reason alone — since he did not intend except to pierce the barrel, it is gentile touch through something else without intent and permitted — end; and it was already explained there that gentile touch without intent through something else is permitted even according to the law of the Gemara — see there.
