If thieves entered a cellar and opened barrels of wine: if most of the thieves of the city are non-Jews, it is forbidden; and if most are Jews, it is permitted. And if most are Ishmaelites, it is forbidden for drinking and permitted in benefit. And if the Jews have a neighborhood by themselves where non-Jews do not normally pass through, we follow the majority of the neighborhood even if the majority of the city are non-Jews. And in a place where people are accustomed to hide money in barrels and place them among barrels of wine, even if most of the thieves of the city are non-Jews it is permitted, for perhaps they came only for the money, and when they saw that it is wine they did not touch it. {Rama: And all this is when we saw a re’uta, such as that they were initially sealed and were found opened; but if they were sealed and were found sealed, or they were all open and no re’uta was found in them, everything is permitted (Beit Yosef: Rivash siman 424, and Mordechai, and Agudah, and Ra’avan), for a typical thief does not take wine. And if there was a re’uta in some of them and the others were opened, and we do not see a re’uta in them, everything is permitted (Beit Yosef and Mordechai at the end of perek R. Yishmael; responsum of Maharam). And some say that all this is when there are known thieves who are non-Jews (so it seems in Mordechai, perek R. Yishmael, and the Beit Yosef brought it), but if 1 thief is known to be a Jew and no non-Jewish thief is known, we attribute it to the known Jew; and see above siman 128 for additional laws of this kind.}
