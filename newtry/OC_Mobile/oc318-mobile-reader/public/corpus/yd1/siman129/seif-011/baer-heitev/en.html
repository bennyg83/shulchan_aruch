Ishmaelites. They are not idolaters. The same applies to other contemporary non-Jews.
Majority. Although in general we follow the majority rather than proximity even where proximity is compelling, this case differs because the wine was found within the Jewish neighborhood itself. Rashba. Bach permits in a major loss even where a non-Jewish road divides the neighborhood if the barrels were locked at night. Taz and Shach write that all agree this is permitted even without major loss when they were locked at night.
They touched. Taz writes that nowadays, when ordinary non-Jewish wine is treated leniently regarding indirect touch, even touching with a reed is permitted because the intention was to search for money, not to touch wine. Even direct hand-contact might be permitted, but he hesitates because this is not explicit. Hagahot Ashri writes that nowadays even a locked door does not prohibit unless there is concern that the non-Jew drank or gained another benefit. Shach questions the Mechaber's permission where most thieves are non-Jewish, because the Gemara appears to permit only through a compounded doubt. The gloss answers that the case involves many open barrels beyond what could have been drunk, preserving a compounded doubt.
