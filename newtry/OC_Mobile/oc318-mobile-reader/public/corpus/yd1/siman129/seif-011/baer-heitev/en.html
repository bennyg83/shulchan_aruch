Taz wrote: it seems that now that we are lenient regarding ordinary gentile wine in gentile contact through an intermediary, it is permitted even if they touched it with a reed—since he did not intend wine contact but only to search for money; this is contact without intent and is permitted as above siman 124 seif 14. Even if he touched with his own hand, it would seem permitted to permit—only we should not be so lenient since it is not found explicitly. Hagahot Asheri in chapter HaSocher wrote: nowadays we do not forbid even if he closed the door and was not caught as a thief unless there is concern for drinking or any benefit of his. Shach wrote on what Mechaber wrote "even majority gentile thieves of a city are permitted"—for in the Talmud it implies it is permitted only because of a double doubt; meaning when gentile thieves and Israelites are equal or it is unknown who is the majority; but with a majority of gentile thieves where there is no double doubt—not (and one examining Tosafot and Asheri folio 94 in the hagahah will see practical difference—they deal when many barrels are open more than for drinking—then even with a majority of gentiles there is double doubt—see there). Shach concluded: I shortened since this law is not common among us—we do not regularly hide money in wine barrels—as Rosh wrote.
