Specifically when it is closed with a stopper. The Rama derived this from what the Ran wrote in the chapter Elu Metziyos, that wine found in this manner is permitted even for drinking and no seal is required. This case is different, for if a non-Jew had touched it, he would not have troubled himself to close it again; he would have taken wine and left. Therefore, whenever we see it closed, it is permitted, end quote. This implies that open vessels are forbidden. It appears to me, however, that even if it is closed with a wooden stopper it is forbidden. Above, in seif 12, regarding an army that entered a city, barrels closed with wooden stoppers are forbidden, because people ordinarily replace a wooden stopper, since doing so involves no effort. These are likewise the words of the Ran in the chapter HaSocher. Necessarily, when the Ran wrote in the chapter Elu Metziyos that the closure proves that they did not touch it, he meant a clay seal. This contradicts the Rama's ruling that if it is closed with a stopper, meaning a wooden one, it is permitted, and that only a completely open vessel is forbidden. We have demonstrated that permission applies specifically when it is sealed with clay.
