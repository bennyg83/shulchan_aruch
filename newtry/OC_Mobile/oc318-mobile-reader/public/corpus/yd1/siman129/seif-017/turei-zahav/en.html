And specifically when it is sealed with a cork. Rama learned this from what Ran wrote in chapter Elu Metziot here, that it is permitted even for drinking what he finds, and we do not require a seal — for here it is different: if a gentile had touched it he would not have bothered to return and seal it, but would take wine and go — therefore whatever we see sealed is permitted — end; it implies open ones are forbidden; and it appears to me that even if sealed with a wood cork it is forbidden, for it is stated above seif 12 in Cheil who entered the city that wood corks are forbidden — it is propriety that they return a wood cork there without bother; and these are also Ran's words in chapter HaSocher; except perforce what Ran wrote in chapter Elu Metziot that the sealing is proof they did not touch — namely sealing with clay; and from this contradicts what Rama wrote: if it is sealed with a cork — meaning of wood — it is permitted and forbidden only when fully open, for we proved the permit is only with clay specifically.
