But when transporting, etc. With a wooden stopper, however, it is permitted, as the Tur explicitly wrote in the name of the Ramban and the Rosh. It appears obvious that this applies even when the Jew does not accompany him at all. The Derishah understood that this applies only where the Jew accompanied him but merely diverted his attention, and because of this he entered into a difficulty and resolution. I do not know what compelled him to this. The Tur wrote that Rabbeinu Yitzchak permits even when it is completely open. The Beit Yosef asked how this differs from siman 125, where one who carries a full open vessel renders it forbidden, and he therefore establishes the case here as involving a vessel that is not full. My father-in-law of blessed memory resolves it differently. To me, however, there is no difficulty at all. Above, the non-Jew carries it in his hand, and he can easily touch it, making caution impossible. Here, he carries it on his shoulder in the usual manner of transporting a barrel from place to place, and he cannot touch it so easily.
