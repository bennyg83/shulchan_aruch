(Likut) Without a stopper. Meaning that with a stopper it is permitted even when he is transporting it. This is the view of the Rosh and Ran in the name of Ramban, unlike the Raavad, who wrote that this applies only when they are entirely sealed. See the Ran there. The Rosh and Ran bring proof from the fact that all the clauses were grouped together, indicating that they all discuss the same circumstance. A barrel being transported with a stopper is still deemed open.
But when transporting, etc. As stated in the Yerushalmi, cited by the Rosh and Ran. We also say on 60a that if a non-Jew carries a vessel and a Jew follows behind him, when it is full it is forbidden, etc. Although there a partially empty vessel is permitted, here it is forbidden, as explained later, because we are concerned that perhaps, etc. This appears in the Rosh and Ran in the name of the Raavad, and accords with the Yerushalmi there: This was not said only of full vessels, etc. The Rosh cites it there, unlike the Ran's initial statement that partially empty vessels are permitted as in the case above. (End of Likut.)
