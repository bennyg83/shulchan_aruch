And they are divided by stakes. It is astonishing why a division by stakes was required here, for the roofs are already separate from one another, and the Gemara did not mention a division by stakes in this case. It appears that the phrase, and they are divided, means, or they are divided. This is explicit in Rambam, chapter 13. After mentioning that a courtyard is divided by the tops of stakes, he writes: Likewise, regarding two roofs, where the Jew's roof is above and the non-Jew's roof below, or where they are alongside one another and there is a partition between them, etc., end quote. Thus, even regarding roofs there are cases in which division by stakes is relevant. This is obvious. In all these cases, the reason is that the non-Jew would be apprehended as a thief.
