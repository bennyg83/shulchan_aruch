(There in Shulchan Aruch seif 18) And needless to say when there is no Jewish vineyard there except that vineyard, etc. This requires study where we know with certainty that it was stolen from this Jewish vineyard and it is found guarded in the Jew’s vineyard. Do we say that since it is known that one barrel was stolen, presumably this is that barrel, or perhaps we say that it is not considered guarded in this vineyard? Likewise one may be uncertain where we know clearly that nothing was stolen from the gentiles’ vineyards, but regarding this Jew’s vineyard we do not know whether anything was stolen. Do we say that since we know nothing was stolen from the gentiles’ vineyards, the reasoning that “it is not the way, etc.” has been removed and it is permitted, or perhaps the reasoning that “it is not the way, etc.” remains in place, and this barrel came from elsewhere? However, where there is no concern that it came from elsewhere, and we also know that nothing was stolen from the vineyards of the surrounding gentiles, even from those farther away, it appears that one may permit it, for this presumption that “it is not the way, etc.” has been weakened.
