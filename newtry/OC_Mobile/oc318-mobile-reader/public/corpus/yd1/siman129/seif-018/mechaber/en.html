If a barrel of wine was found in the vineyard of a Jew, and there are other Jewish vineyards nearby and many vineyards of non-Jews around them — even if the non-Jewish ones are farther than the Jewish ones — the barrel is forbidden, for we follow the majority, even though there is closeness that indicates that it was of a Jew. And there is no need to say this when there is no Jewish vineyard there except that vineyard alone. And the same applies to be lenient: for example, if it was found in the vineyard of a non-Jew — even though there are other non-Jewish vineyards — if there are many Jewish vineyards, even if they are far, we go to be lenient; and this is when it is situated between mountains, where there is no passage for passersby. But if not, it is forbidden, for most passersby are non-Jews, and perhaps it fell from one of them. And these matters are regarding a bottle; but a barrel, which passersby do not usually carry such, we do not attribute to them.
