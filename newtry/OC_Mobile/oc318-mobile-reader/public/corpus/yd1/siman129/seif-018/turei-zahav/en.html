And it goes without saying when there is no vineyard belonging to, etc. It is difficult why he wrote, it goes without saying. It is further difficult that the Tur concludes with the reason that one would not ordinarily steal from this vineyard and hide the stolen item within it, end quote. Why is this reason necessary? In any event it is forbidden. [This is the correct reading, and so it appears in the Tur. Most editions read, permitted, which is an error. What he writes, and in the Gemara, etc., means that the Gemara rejected the proof by suggesting that perhaps the permission rests on the fact that one does not ordinarily hide it there. The Gemara discusses wine found in an orlah vineyard, while the surrounding vineyards provide the basis for permission. This corresponds to the latter clause of this ruling, and the same applies to be lenient, etc., because one reason applies to both. One who examines it will find this.] The general principle is that when majority and proximity conflict, we follow the majority. The Gemara wished to explain the ruling on the basis of majority, but rejected that proof by suggesting that perhaps the permission rests on the fact that one does not ordinarily hide stolen goods within that vineyard. Since we in fact rule that we follow the majority, why did the Tur state the other reason? It appears that the phrase, it goes without saying, refers to a vineyard completely isolated, with neither Jewish nor non-Jewish vineyards surrounding it. He did not say merely that there is no Jewish vineyard there. Rather, the main basis for permission in the first clause is that the surrounding vineyards belong to Jews. He then states that if this ground for permission is absent and the vineyard is isolated on all sides, one might have thought that we should follow this vineyard itself, as stated above at the beginning of seif 11 in the name of Mishmeres HaBayis. He therefore teaches that, on the contrary, prohibition is even more obvious for another reason, namely that one does not ordinarily hide stolen goods there. This reason applies particularly to an isolated vineyard. A thief would not hide within it what he stole from that same vineyard, for its owner would certainly notice it when he found it there, since it was taken from him. The phrase, it goes without saying, is therefore well explained.
