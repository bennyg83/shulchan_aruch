And all the more when there is no fence of, etc. It is difficult why he wrote this "all the more"; and further difficult that the Tur concludes with this reason "we lean there is no way to steal from this vineyard and hide inside it" — end; and it is difficult why I need this reason, for in any case it is forbidden. (So it should read; and so in the Tur. And in most books the version is "permitted" — this is corruption; and what he wrote "and in the Gemara, etc." — and he rejected it through rejection lest the permit is because there is no way to hide, etc., because in the Gemara it deals with found in an orlah vineyard and around the vineyards is the permit — and this is the end of that law; and likewise to be lenient, etc., for one reason for both. And the examiner will find:) Because of majority and proximity we follow the majority; and in the Gemara he sought to give a reason in truth because of majority and rejected it through rejection lest the permit is because there is no way to hide inside it; and since we hold in truth we follow the majority — why did the Tur write the other reason? And it appears this "all the more" deals when the vineyard is completely separate and around it is neither Israel's vineyard nor gentile's vineyard; and he did not state there is no Israel's fence, but the essence of the permit at the beginning is because of surroundings that are Israel — he said here that if that permit is not there and the vineyard is separate on every side from vineyards — if so, we follow this vineyard; and as written above siman 11 in the name of Mishmeret HaBayit it teaches us that on the contrary it is more straightforward to forbid for another reason that there is no, etc.; and this reason applies more to a solitary vineyard where there is no way to hide the theft from it, for the vineyard owner will surely notice when he finds it, since he takes from it — therefore the language "need not say" is well settled.
