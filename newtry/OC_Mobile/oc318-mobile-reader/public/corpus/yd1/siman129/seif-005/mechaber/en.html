One who hands over the key to his shop to a non-Jew: the wine that is in it is permitted, even if no Jew remained in the city, even if he informed him that he is departing, since he gave him only the guarding of the key, and the non-Jew is afraid to enter. And there is one who says that they permitted only when the non-Jew does not know that he is departing; and then it is permitted even if one found that he entered. But if he informed him that he is departing — even if he would be “caught as a thief” — it is forbidden. {Rama: And one may be lenient like the first opinion (it is the opinion of R. Chananel, as so is in Rivash siman 424, and Terumat HaDeshen siman 250, and Rashba). And if the non-Jew transgressed and entered there: if he would be caught as a thief, it is permitted; but if he would not be caught as a thief, and he has an excuse for why he entered, it is forbidden — and this is specifically if he locked the door with a lock from inside and there is no crack in the room through which one can see; but if the door is open (Beit Yosef in the name of Rashba), or even if there is only a crack through which one can see him, the wine is permitted (Mordechai, perek R. Yishmael). And even if he told him to enter there (ibid.), if he did not tell him that he is departing and he is able to come, for the non-Jew fears. But if the non-Jew does not fear, such as the governor’s slaves who seized the Jew (and search the Jew’s house), everything is forbidden if he has no sign that they did not touch (Beit Yosef in the name of Tosafot and Mordechai at the end of perek R. Yishmael).}
