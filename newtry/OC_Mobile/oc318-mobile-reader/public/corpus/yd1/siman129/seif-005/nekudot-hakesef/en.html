(Ibid., Taz, seif katan 26) For it is stated above, in seif 12, concerning an army, etc. It appears to me that the cases are not comparable. There, since the barrel is in a lodging place, it is normal for one who opens it to replace the stopper. Here, however, the reason is as stated by the Ran in chapter Ein Ma'amidin: had the non-Jew touched the wine, he would not have troubled himself to reseal the barrel. He would instead have taken some wine and gone away, or carried the barrel to his house. Ateret Zekenim writes similarly. This applies specifically where the barrel had been sealed, or even stoppered with wood. If non-Jews found it and opened it, why would they bother to replace the stopper after leaving the barrel to spoil? The Beit Chadash likewise rules in accordance with Rama.
