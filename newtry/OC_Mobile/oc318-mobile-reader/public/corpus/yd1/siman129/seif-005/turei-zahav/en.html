Morality is key. It is written in the words of the verse that even means the message to which the key is not told by the key, but rather the preservation of the key as it is written from the Rambam, which means that it is not only the preservation of the key:
And he fears to enter it. And it is not similar to one who leaves his wine in a city of gentiles without key and seal — for here other gentiles tremble from the gentile, and he trembles from them lest they tell Israel who entered. Beit Yosef in the name of Ran. And what he wrote in seif 6: and Israel heard the sound of shofar blowing — Rashba in Tosafot HaRosh challenged: if so, why did he forbid above when informing he sails — let us say he remembers his donkey and comes, for when he goes to sit Shabbat and to pray he is like informing he sails; and he resolved: they did not say "he remembers his donkey" except when sailing in matters possible to do here, such as prayer and Shabbat observance — for when he remembers his wine he comes and does here prayer or observes Shabbat; but when sailing in matters impossible to do here, such as sailing to bathe in a bathhouse and the like — forbidden; and further study is needed on this — end; and the Tur disagrees with this and wrote: since the reason is because he remembers his wine, in any matter where one can say so — even though impossible to do here — nevertheless he will not lose his wine on his account — end; and one must resolve Rashba's difficulty according to the Tur; and it appears he resolves the difficulty another way — and it is a clear matter in my humble opinion that in those two cases in the Gemara — namely that where he heard the sound of Shabbat rest and prayer sound in synagogue and went — not for nothing did they mention this, and did not say he went on his own for these matters without arousal by another; but certainly specifically in such a case where he does not go on his own but suddenly by chance, and therefore his wine is forgotten; the gentile sees this and therefore trembles lest he return and remember his wine — unlike those at the beginning of this siman when informing he sails, where he certainly consoles himself before leaving and the gentile thinks he intentionally left his wine with him and trusts him — therefore he does not tremble; and afterwards I found this resolution in Raah in Sefer Badek HaBayit, and Rashba in Sefer Mishmeret HaBayit rejected it with a rejection that has no substance to any viewer of his words in this; and proof to our words from what is written nearby seif 9 "and they both went out in panic" — the essence of the law depends there on this as will be explained nearby; and that we learn from what they said there in the Gemara that the incident was thus — and if so, here too let us say thus, since it was mentioned in the Gemara that the incident was thus — certainly the matter depends on this. And with this I further resolve what was difficult in what he wrote in seif 1: if he said to the gentile "you pass and I will come after you" — it is forbidden; it is difficult: why not say the gentile trembles lest he remember his wine and come quickly after him; according to Rashba's resolution I do not know how this will be settled; and according to what I wrote it is resolved: the beginning of this siman certainly deals when he left it intentionally, and the essence of the matter there is whether the gentile thinks Israel trusted him the wine — and therefore it is forbidden when informing he sails; therefore if he says to him "go and I will come after you," the gentile sees he trusts him the wine since he tells him to go with the wine and not go with him to guard it — why should Israel's mind change afterwards, since he did not leave in panic — change of mind applies only when he leaves not by himself but by others, or leaves unintentionally and possibly afterwards he will remember and regret. So it appears clear in my humble opinion; and I saw other resolutions on this that are not acceptable at all and need not be answered.
