Morality is key. It is written in the words of the verse that even means the message to which the key is not told by the key, but rather the preservation of the key as it is written from the Rambam, which means that it is not only the preservation of the key:
And he is afraid to enter. This is not comparable to the case of one who leaves his wine in a non-Jewish city without a key and seal — for here the other non-Jews are afraid of the [specific] non-Jew, and he is afraid of them lest they tell the Jew who enters. Beit Yosef in the name of Ran.
And what is written in seif 6: "and a Jew heard the sound of the shofar blast" — Rashba in Torat HaBayit raised the difficulty: if so, why did [the Tanna] forbid above when [the Jew] informed [the non-Jew] that he was departing — let us say he [the non-Jew] will remember [the Jew's] donkey and come? And he answered: we only say he remembers the donkey when he departs for things that he could also do here, such as prayer and Shabbat observance — for when he remembers his wine he can come and still do the praying or Shabbat observance here; but when departing for things he cannot do here, such as departing to bathe at a bathhouse and the like, it is forbidden. And the Tur disagrees with this and wrote: since the reason is that he will remember his wine, in every case one may say this — even though he cannot do the matter here, nevertheless he will not lose his wine on its account — end of his words. And one must resolve Rashba's difficulty according to the Tur's view, and it appears to me that the resolution is clear: for in those two incidents in the Gemara — namely one who heard the sound of the [Shabbat] rest and one who heard the sound of prayer in the synagogue — they were not mentioned without reason, and [the Tanna] did not say that he went on his own without the prompting of another; rather certainly specifically in such a case where he does not go of his own accord but rather suddenly — and therefore he forgets his wine — and the non-Jew sees this and therefore fears lest he return and remember his wine; unlike those at the beginning of the siman where he informed [the non-Jew] he was departing — certainly [the non-Jew] thought that he deliberately left his wine with him and he trusts him — and therefore he does not fear; and afterward [when the Jew] thought about it and was prompted, since he did not go in a rush, the non-Jew thinks he will not change his mind after having gone with full awareness. And this is what I found afterward as the resolution of Ra-ah in Sefer Bedek HaBayit; and Rashba in Mishmeret HaBayit rejected it with a response that is without substance to anyone who examines his words there. And proof for my words from what is written nearby in seif 9: "and both of them went out in a rush" — for the entire ruling depends on this, as will be clarified shortly; and this is what we learn from the Gemara's account of the incident as having occurred in this way — and if so here too we should say the same. And by this it is also resolved what is difficult in what is written in seif 1: that if he said to the non-Jew "carry it and I will come after you" — it is forbidden; the difficulty is: why should we not say the non-Jew fears lest he remember his wine and come after him quickly? But according to what I wrote, this is no difficulty — for at the beginning of this siman the case is certainly that he left it intentionally, and the core of the matter depends on whether the non-Jew thinks the Jew trusted him with the wine — therefore it is forbidden when he informs him he is departing, for thus the non-Jew sees that [the Jew] trusts him with the wine and therefore nothing will change [the Jew's] mind, since he did not go in a rush; and so too if he says to him "go and I will come after you" — the non-Jew sees that he trusts him with the wine since he tells him to go with the wine and does not go with him to guard it — why should [the Jew's] thinking change afterward, since he did not go out in a rush? A change of mind only applies when he goes out not by his own initiative but through others, or when he goes not deliberately and it is possible that afterward he will remember and regret — so it seems to me clearly; and I saw other resolutions to this that do not even come close to making sense, and there is no need to respond to them.
