The wine is permitted. The reason given in the Gemara is that although the inclination for immorality overpowered them, the inclination for idolatrous wine did not overpower them. In my humble opinion, this applies only under the law of the Talmudic era, when Jews were greatly distanced from ordinary non-Jewish wine and it was forbidden even for benefit. Nowadays, however, we are not so distanced from it, for benefit is permitted. Furthermore, to our great sorrow we have seen many people breach this prohibition. Even those who are not suspected of immorality are suspected of drinking ordinary non-Jewish wine. This is a well-known daily occurrence, because the prohibition is treated lightly in their eyes. Therefore, those suspected of immorality are certainly also suspected of drinking ordinary non-Jewish wine, and such a person will not guard against his non-Jewish paramour touching his wine. The same principle appears in siman 119, seif 4, that one suspected of a severe prohibition is suspected of a lighter one in the eyes of people. Likewise, in the chapter HaNizakin, Gittin 54a, suspicion follows the prohibition that is more severe before God for purposes of imposing a penalty. Therefore, it appears to me that the wine of those who sin through relations with non-Jewish women is certainly forbidden for us. Their case is worse than the case stated here of a Jewish prostitute, where the wine is forbidden because she follows after them. This appears in my humble opinion.
