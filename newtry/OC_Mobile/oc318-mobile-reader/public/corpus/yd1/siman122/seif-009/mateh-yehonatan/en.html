(There seif 9 in the hagahah) And even if he gave them to a craftsman, etc. See Taz s.k. 8 and Shach s.k. 8, who elaborated on this; and their ultimate intent is to explain Rama's words: if he returned them immediately it is forbidden because there is only one doubt; not so if he returned them after overnight — then there is a double doubt, perhaps they used before overnight; and this is against reason, for certainly that which we establish regarding a gentile's vessel that it is a double doubt, perhaps he did not use overnight — namely the entire doubt is perhaps he will not use at all; only since we see the vessel is old, certainly it was used, we say regarding it that he did not use within overnight; not so here with an eating vessel that they were concerned and decided that certainly a gentile used it — there is no room to say any doubt perhaps he did not use. If so, even if he did not return it immediately, why should it be permitted — perhaps one hour before he returned it the gentile used it? Therefore it appears to my humble opinion to explain: certainly Ran in the name of Ra'ah who was stringent regarding a knife — this is because the gentile craftsman's way when Jews give him a Jew's knife to use, even though he gains nothing, in other things we are not concerned for substitution in such a case; nevertheless here with a knife, since it is available in his hand and he needs a knife to use, he does not trouble after another knife and uses the Jew's; but knives that gentile servants carry in a Jew's house — since he is in a Jew's house, there is no concern in this at all, and it is like a gentile's vessel permitted through double doubt perhaps he did not use at all; and if you say he used, perhaps he cut in it something spoiled visibly; and that which Rabbi Yom Tov wrote: and all this when they wish to use it on the day they gave it — this is on that day the gentile returned it, even though it stood many days in a gentile's hand; nevertheless there is concern perhaps he used one moment before they give it to him; but if he took it immediately from the gentile until a time when we can say even though the gentile used it, it is already spoiled — meaning he knows he did not use it in that overnight; or it stood in a Jew's hand after taking from the gentile overnight — we follow leniency because it is a rabbinic doubt for leniency, not because it is a double doubt — there is no double doubt here at all as written; and that which Rabbi Shalom of Bunburk in Mordechai wrote: if a Jew forgot his pot in a gentile's hand it is permitted from double doubt; and he wrote: even if he used it, it is spoiled that is not bat yoma; and stam vessels we establish thus — behold he holds that we say since it stood in the house a time that it can be, it is not bat yoma, we are not concerned perhaps he used it one moment before they returned it; one may say he deals where he returned it to him the next day, and at night there is no way to use it at all; if so one may say Rabbi Shalom holds thus: overnight spoils; if so there is no room for concern at all; and so the questioner inferred from the language of the She'elah, who wrote: he forgot the pot in a gentile's house until the next day — it implies he took it immediately the next day — investigate.
