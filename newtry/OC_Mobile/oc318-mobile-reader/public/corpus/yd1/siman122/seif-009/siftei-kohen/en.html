And we are not concerned b'dieved. It does not mean that the food used in it is permitted b'dieved — that is obvious, and furthermore he did not yet mention anything about the food; rather it means that since they already gave it to a gentile and took it from him, we are not concerned b'dieved and it is permitted to use even l'chatchila, as Ra'ah wrote and Torat Chayim brings in general rule 59 law 4, and so in the markers of Torat Chayim there — to exclude that l'chatchila it is forbidden to give it to a gentile with intent that he leave it afterward me'et le'et; and even though Ra'ah said only when the Jew left it overnight it is permitted to use l'chatchila, it implies to the Rav that all the more so whenever we can say it stood in a gentile's hand it is permitted, for it is not comparable to a gentile's vessel; and so it appears in the markers of Chokhmat Adam there; and even though in Torat Chayim there it appears that for Ra'ah likewise if he used it the same day without hagalah the food is permitted — that is according to the Rav's view; but Issur VeHeter, who holds the food is forbidden and like the plain meaning of Mordechai's words, one may say both apply: even though the food is forbidden because he used it the same day, but whatever stood overnight is permitted even l'chatchila; and for this the Rav wrote here in the hagahah at the beginning anonymously that we are not concerned — namely when it stood overnight it is permitted to use l'chatchila; and afterward he wrote the view of those who forbid the food; and even though in Torat Chayim there it appears that for Issur VeHeter, who forbids the food, he also forbids using the vessel even if it stood overnight — nevertheless for actual law it seems to the Rav to distinguish as he wrote. And all this according to the Rav; but Beit Chadash in this seif and in siman 137 disagrees and wrote: where it stood in a gentile's hand half a day and he returned it the same day — hagalah is required, and if earthenware — breaking; and standing overnight does not help; and if he used it the same day the food is forbidden; and this is Issur VeHeter's view who explained Mordechai's words in the name of Rabbenu Tam thus; and where it stood in a gentile's hand for an hour, standing helps to use l'chatchila; and with this Orach Chaim deals in the name of Ra'ah; and if he used it without standing the food is permitted — namely what Mordechai wrote in the name of Rabbenu Tam: but for an hour one need not be concerned — end of his words. However what Beit Chadash challenged below siman 137 from what the Tur and Rashi and poskim wrote, that one who leaves a wine vessel in a gentile's hand even one hour requires kashering, etc. — he forced greatly and his conclusion is that Rabbenu Tam in Mordechai does not hold like the Tur and his supporters, and for law there too it is permitted to leave them overnight and use l'chatchila; and where it stood in a gentile's hand a day or days it is forbidden to use l'chatchila in every regard like gentile vessels themselves — not like Rabbi Shalom of Bunburk in Mordechai, who is alone against the Tur and Rashi and poskim who require kashering for wine vessels; and Rabbenu Tam and Ra'ah in Yoreh De'ah agree that for vessels whose use is hot, hagalah is required — not as Beit Yosef wrote in this seif that Rabbenu Tam and Rabbi Shalom of Bunburk agree in this matter — end of his words. And I did not reach the depth of his view, for there with wine vessels standing overnight does not apply, as we establish below siman 137 that wine vessels cannot be distinguished between ben yomo and not ben yomo (and so Ran at the end of Avodah Zarah), and as the Rosh proved in responsum general rule 19 section 16: as long as twelve months have not passed on them there is taste for improvement; therefore kashering is required in wine vessels for Rashi and Tur and their supporters, for Ra'ah's reason does not apply there, that even if they used them it is spoiled after overnight; but in a pot everyone agrees with Rabbi Shalom of Bunburk that it is permitted to use l'chatchila immediately, from what Rabbi Shalom of Bunburk wrote there: establish the pot on its presumption (and therefore some say for gentile vessels forbidden to use l'chatchila — for a Jew's vessel we say establish it on its presumption); and say perhaps prohibition was not used in it; and furthermore even if he used in it, it is spoiled that is not ben yomo; and stam gentile vessels we establish thus, as Rabbenu Tam's words — end of his words. And now there is no dispute among the poskim at all, and this is clear; and so in Mordechai Pesachim he brought Rabbenu Tam's words and Rabbi Shalom of Bunburk's words one after another anonymously; and so Issur VeHeter general rule 33 ruled like both their words anonymously; and this is the Rav's view in the markers of Torat Chayim there; and here in the hagahah: if he left it in a gentile's hand overnight it is permitted to use l'chatchila — and so is essential. And all this in vessels whose way is to use them hot; but vessels whose way is to use them cold — certainly scouring suffices — investigate.
And l'chatchila one should be careful in every regard, etc. And the world is not careful about this now; and the reason is because servants and maidservants are in a Jew's house always, that a Jew goes in and out there — in such a case it is permitted even l'chatchila, as above siman 118 seif 10.
