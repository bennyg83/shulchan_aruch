Hour. Behold Beit Chadash challenged from what is written below siman 136, that he holds like the Tur and poskim that one who forgets a wine vessel in a gentile's hand, even one hour, requires kashering, etc.; and he forced greatly and his conclusion is that for law there too it is permitted to leave them me'et le'et and use them l'chatchila — end of his words; and Shach wrote on him that he did not reach the depth of his view, that in a wine vessel one cannot distinguish between ben yomo and not ben yomo, for as long as twelve months have not passed on them there is taste for improvement — see there, who elaborated on this matter and concluded that so is essential, as written here in the hagahah: if he left it in a gentile's hand overnight it is permitted to use l'chatchila; and all this in vessels whose way is to use them hot; but vessels whose way is to use them cold — certainly scouring suffices — end of his words. And Taz distinguishes: the essential matter depends on what is its way to use, even incidentally, such as a wine vessel that a person is apt to drink at every hour, and there is concern that in the short time it was with the gentile perhaps he drank wine in it; therefore they were stringent in siman 136 even for a short time; not so a vessel in which they cook or an eating knife, whose way is not to use except at mealtime, and a person's way is not to eat except a fixed measure per day — therefore one should not be stringent except when it stood, like half a day; and some say half a day, but a large measure that one can estimate that in that time he used it for eating — end of his words.
B'dieved. Shach wrote: the food that was used in it — this is obviously permitted; rather the Rav Rama comes to inform us that since they already gave it to a gentile and took it from him, we are not concerned b'dieved and it is permitted to use even l'chatchila — to exclude giving it to a gentile initially with intent that he leave it afterward me'et le'et — that is forbidden; and Taz disagrees with this hagahah and elaborated greatly on this case; and his conclusion for the general rule: where there is a double doubt, such as if they did not return it the same day — if he left it intentionally the vessel should be forbidden and requires hagalah, and earthenware requires breaking; and if he forgot, the vessel should be permitted, only if it can be kashered he should kasher it, and earthenware is permitted; but where there is no double doubt, such as if they returned it the same day — it should be forbidden even b'dieved by law; namely if it is an eating vessel, the prohibition is only that it should stand with the gentile part of the day — a large measure that according to estimation of the mind he used it for forbidden eating; and if it is a drinking vessel, like that case of a wine vessel siman 136 — it is forbidden l'chatchila even for an hour — end of his words.
And maidservants. And the world is not careful about this now; and the reason is because the servants and maidservants are in a Jew's house always, that a Jew goes in and out there — in such a case it is permitted even l'chatchila, as above siman 118 seif 10 — end of Shach's words.
