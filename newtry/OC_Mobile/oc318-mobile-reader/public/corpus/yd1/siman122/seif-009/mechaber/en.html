One must be careful not to leave in a non-Jew’s house utensils of a meal, for we are concerned lest he use them. {Rama: And even if he gave them to a craftsman to repair, he must make a sign on them (Beit Yosef in the name of Orach Chaim) so that the non-Jew will not use them. And if he transgressed and did not do so, when he returned and took them from the non-Jew they require hag'alah; and this is specifically if they remained for some amount of time by the non-Jew, i.e., about half a day, but for an hour there is no concern (Mordechai, Perek Elu Metziot). And all this is when he wants to use it on the day he gave it to him, for there is only one doubt: perhaps the non-Jew used it or not. But if he did not take it from the non-Jew until after time, such that we can say that even if the non-Jew used it it is already lifgam and “from time to time” has passed, or that “from time to time” passed in the hand of the Jew after he took it from the non-Jew, we follow the lenient view, and b'dieved we are not concerned (the reasoning of the Rav). And if he took it from the non-Jew on the same day he gave it to him, and he transgressed and used it without hag'alah, some forbid the food, as with a utensil that is certainly ben yomo (Arukh); and in a place of need one may be lenient b'dieved. And l'chatchila one must be careful in every case, even with gentile male and female slaves in a Jew’s house, not to designate our utensils with them, lest they use them with forbidden items (ibid. in Mordechai); and see below siman 136.}
