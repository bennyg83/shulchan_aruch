Some say regarding sharp things. See in responsum Chacham Tzvi siman 75, who ruled to permit if they cooked on Pesach a sharp thing in a chametz pot that passed twelve months, for although we forbid on Pesach nosen ta'am lifgam, and likewise regarding a sharp thing nosen ta'am lifgam does not help — nevertheless after twelve months there is no taste at all, as they said regarding yayin nesekh vessels that after twelve months it is permitted (as below seif 135); and even though in other prohibitions we do not find one who is lenient to cook l'chatchila in vessels that passed twelve months — one may distinguish: regarding yayin nesekh within twelve months even b'dieved it is forbidden — the matter is very proven; therefore l'chatchila permitted after twelve months; but in other prohibitions, whatever is ben yomo is permitted b'dieved — it is easy to err if we permit after twelve months l'chatchila lest they permit also within twelve months l'chatchila; and he wrote that accordingly in chametz vessels according to what we rule that in chametz nosen ta'am lifgam is forbidden and within twelve months even b'dieved forbidden — automatically the leniency emerges that after twelve months l'chatchila permitted like yayin nesekh; and nevertheless he did not have the heart to permit to cook l'chatchila; and the Gaon Rabbi Naftali Katz agreed with him in siman 76 — see there; and see in responsum Avodat Avraham Panim Me'irot vol. 1 siman 23 who disagreed with him; and see in Chacham Tzvi siman 80 who responded to Avodat Avraham and strengthened his words; and see Pri Megadim in siman 103 who wrote that in cold such as through pickling Pri Megadim also concedes permitted and aging helps, for they disagree in hot — see there further. And in responsum Chaim Sha'al siman 38 he wrote that Rashba's words in responsum siman 575 are not like Chacham Tzvi; and he wrote that an incident came to his hand: a Jew was sent a gift of fine metal vessels and it was not recognizable if a gentile already used them, and two years passed; and he wrote that at first glance one should not permit l'chatchila as Chacham Tzvi himself wrote, and also Rashba disagrees; but in this where there is double doubt one may be lenient; and still requires answer — end of his words; and see in responsum Bat Chayim siman 491 who wrote on this that if possible to kasher three times one should do so; and if they are vessels not regularly used boiling one may be lenient — see there; and see in responsum Maor HaGolah Rabbi Akiva Eiger 57 siman 43, asked on such a case: one who bought earthenware vessels from a gentile that are older than twelve months, and there is irretrievable loss whether he must sell them to a gentile; and he sided with the questioner to be lenient according to Chacham Tzvi's responsum; and he answered him that there is no permission here, for behold Chacham Tzvi himself wrote that in other prohibitions it is forbidden l'chatchila, and also in chametz vessels he did not decide to be lenient, and also Rashba's responsum disagrees, and also there is no side to be lenient according to what the Minchat Yaakov wrote that in irretrievable loss one may permit in earthenware to use after overnight, as Ran wrote in chapter 21; and so is Maharalbach siman 121's view. This is not so, for behold in Maharalbach there it is proven that specifically where the vessel itself is irretrievable loss, like the case of a lime-kiln oven, one may be lenient; but where each vessel is not irretrievable loss, only through combining them — one may not be lenient (and see there what he wrote to distinguish between this and what he wrote in his name siman 61); and if so in the case at hand where irretrievable loss is only because he has many vessels, it is not considered b'dieved — see there; and it implies where the vessel itself is great loss like an oven, perhaps one may be lenient somewhat like Maharalbach — investigate. And see in responsum Shiyurei Yah siman 21 who raised to permit b'dieved even after two months — see there; and see in responsum Shemen Rokeach part 1 siman 2 that even after twelve months it is difficult to rely on Chacham Tzvi and be lenient; but regarding a pot lid that otherwise is a stringency without reason, one may be lenient after twelve months, and perhaps even after two months as Shevut Yaakov's words — see there; and see further in responsum Beit Avraham part Yoreh De'ah siman 8 section 4, who wrote on Chacham Tzvi's words that in other prohibitions forbidden l'chatchila after twelve months — one may say this is in Torah prohibition; but if the prohibition itself is rabbinic, one may side to be lenient to permit even l'chatchila; nevertheless it is difficult to rely on this alone unless where there is another leniency — see there further; and this twelve months we do not require complete, only lunar months, each month twenty-nine days.
