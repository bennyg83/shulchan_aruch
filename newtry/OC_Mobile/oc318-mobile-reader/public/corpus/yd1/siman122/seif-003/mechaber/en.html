There are those who say that it is considered lifgam when it is not ben yomo only if he rinsed it well and all the oily issur that is stuck to it was removed; but if he did not rinse it well, such that the issur is present as a substance, it is not rendered lifgam by standing from time to time (Rashba, Tur, and Raavad). {Rama: Nevertheless, if there is 60 only corresponding to what is stuck to the pot, everything is permitted, for what is absorbed in the pot is already lifgam and does not require nullification (so it appears above siman 95). There are those who say that regarding sharp items we do not say the law of noten ta'am lifgam, as was explained above simanim 95 and 96; therefore, if one cooked in a pot of issur that is not ben yomo a sharp food, such as a dish whose majority is vinegar, or spices, or other sharp items, it is forbidden. But the dish is not considered sharp due to the small amount of spices in it (Mordechai, Perek Elu Metziot, and Rashba siman 449). And so is the practice; and see above simanim 95 and 96. But if the issur was a sharp item, and afterwards the pot stood from time to time and one cooked in it heter that is not sharp, it is permitted, for the first sharpness does not render it for improvement (Arukh, general rule 38).}
