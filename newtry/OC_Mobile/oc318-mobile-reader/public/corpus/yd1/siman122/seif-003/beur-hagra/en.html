But not, etc. As written in chapter 6 of Berachot (36b) and chapter 8 of Yoma: this citron, etc.; and they challenged: is it not a sharp thing? And Semag's explanation: regarding a dry thing we do not say its sharpness improves it for the better; and he challenged him in Torat Chayim from a pinch of chiltit; and so in seif 96 seif 3 in the hagahah; and Mordechai answered because they have a designated knife for this; and see below; and another explanation: they do not cut it but tear it; and he challenged: behold they cook it in their kidneys; and he answered: stam gentile vessels are not ben yomo, as in seif 6; and even though it is a sharp thing, since they cook it in honey and the ginger is small, it is not considered a sharp thing; and so Semag; and for all the resolutions it must be so, whether for Semag's explanation or Mordechai's second explanation; and see in seif 6 that Mordechai's first explanation is also refuted — see there [and see Mordechai chapter 6 of Berachot and Torat Chayim general rule 61 seif 7].
But if, etc. So Issur VeHeter; and it requires investigation, for behold in chapter 10 of Terumot we say: all pickled things are permitted except with lettuce that is a sharp thing — lettuce, etc.; and one may distinguish between something that is visible and something absorbed; but from what is written in siman 105 seif 1 in the hagahah: and all the more so if, etc. — it does not appear so. And further Issur VeHeter wrote: and likewise if it is cold we do not say it emits because of the sharp thing within it — meaning even though conversely it emits even in cold like chiltit and radish. And there is proof for his words from what is written in chapter 21 (40b): all vessels, etc.; and Rif's girsa: in which chametz was used cold, use matzah cold; and he explained in Milchamot: even though he used only cold, nevertheless it absorbed as if used hot, because chametz is sharp and strong like yayin nesekh, for every earthenware whose initial use is in a gentile's hand is forbidden; and as written in Avodah Zarah 33b: first time, etc.; leather bottles, etc.; lime jars, etc.; and inserts it for permanence even in metal vessels; and the press and the pestle — what is written at the end of Avodah Zarah: all are forbidden in cold; and likewise chametz that absorbs in cold, for everything that has sharpness absorbs even in cold; and somewhat similar to use in hot, as written regarding chiltit and radish; and if he uses it hot it emits emission; but in cold permitted — evidently it is not absorbed in cold; and even though if he used hot it is forbidden even to use cold, behold he wrote there because of a decree lest he use hot; and like every prohibition whose use is hot — forbidden l'chatchila even in cold; and likewise a bowl in which he salted — forbidden to eat boiling in it, but cold permitted; and nevertheless Rabbi Ami broke it and did not wish to use it cold; and so one should learn the first law from what is written there (in Pesachim 40a): why leave them, etc.; and Tosafot explained there d.h. why leave them: because there are two adverse factors — nosen ta'am lifgam and something; and likewise for the second explanation — because of nosen ta'am lifgam; and permitted because it is considered b'dieved, even though it is a sharp thing — nevertheless nosen ta'am lifgam is permitted when it is not ben yomo; but according to what is written in seif 5 there is no proof; and one should not bring proof to forbid nosen ta'am lifgam of yayin nesekh that is forbidden all twelve months, as written (in the Mishnah of Avodah Zarah 29b): gentile lees and their jugs and Jew's wine, etc.; and they said forbidden to make hides for a donkey lest its jug burst, etc., there 32a; and there 34b: see it does not cry out on me, etc.; and even though its use changes year to year — Rosh already wrote in general rule 19 seif 6: specifically something that was cooked — spoiled from overnight;
