(There, Shach s.k. 2) And in Darkei Moshe, etc., wrote in the name of Orach Chaim: if he cooked a sharp thing in a meat pot, etc. — see there at length. And behold at first glance one should wonder at the Rav Shach from that which we establish in siman 96 seif 1: if one cut a radish with a meat knife that is not ben yomo, even though it is well scoured and he cooked it in milk — sixty is required for what is against the knife; and the reason is because of the sharpness of the radish that improves it for the better, and it is also not nosen ta'am bar nosen ta'am; if so here why do we not consider that when the sharp thing was cooked in it the taste of the meat became for the better, and there are still not twenty-four hours from this cooking? And it appears to resolve: behold the teachers of Rashba wish to prove and say that that which we establish nosen ta'am bar nosen ta'am of heter is permitted — this is even when the second taste is in prohibition, not specifically fish that went up in a bowl, only likewise if they made kutach in the meat bowl itself — then immediately when it emits taste in the meat it is in prohibition, nevertheless it is permitted, for he holds that which we establish taste is like substance applies only when taste was absorbed from the meat itself or from the milk itself; but when taste was absorbed from what was absorbed in the pot in such a case we do not say at all taste is like substance deoraita. And behold all this is when the absorption is in the pot and the like; not so when the absorption is in the food — this does not apply, for behold taste entered the food and the absorption is first taste and became nevelah, and then what emerges from it became a forbidden thing within the food and forbids everything; not so if the absorption is in a vessel — we establish that absorption in a vessel is not nosen ta'am, as the poskim wrote in many places; if so one should forbid only after it emerged from the vessel, then it is second taste that is not forbidden at all. And now it is resolved: above in cold, even according to the teachers of Rashba it is well that the Mechaber forbids; not so here, where otherwise there are opinions among poskim whether it is called a sharp thing, since it is not called a sharp thing at all except a pinch of chiltit; and according to Rashba's teachers one should not forbid at all; if so combining two opinions Shach relied to permit in such a case — investigate.
