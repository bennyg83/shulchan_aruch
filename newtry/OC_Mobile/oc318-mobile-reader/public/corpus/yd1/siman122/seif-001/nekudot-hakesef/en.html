(Siman 122, Shach s.k. 2) (Hagahah) What the Gaon Maharam wrote: and the same applies if he cooked a sharp thing in a forbidden pot that is not ben yomo and afterward it stood me'et le'et from when the sharp thing was cooked in it — permitted. I did not reach the depth of his view — is this not all the more so from Issur VeHeter's law? And furthermore, even if he cooked a sharp thing in a ben yomo pot and it stood me'et le'et — permitted; and even after another sharp prohibition after overnight — permitted, as the Rav wrote in the hagahah. And it is possible there is a textual error and the word "not" should be deleted, and it should read: of forbidden ben yomo. Also in what the Gaon Maharam wrote: and likewise if he cooked a sharp thing in a ben yomo meat pot, if he cooked in it afterward me'et le'et from when the meat was cooked in it, etc. — permitted; and he brings proof from siman 94 s.k. 22; and I challenge this law, for there is different because he did not cook a sharp thing, not so here where the second cooking is sharp and draws what is within the walls of the vessel as if cooked now; but if he cooked a sharp thing after me'et le'et, since its taste is spoiled it does not draw for improvement as if cooked now, only for the sharp food itself and not for a second food; not so in the law if a sharp thing was cooked within me'et le'et, for its taste was not spoiled — one may say we consider as if the meat was cooked now; and so it appears from Issur VeHeter's words that he brings from Maharam — he should not have written in a meat pot that is not ben yomo, it should have been a prominence even ben yomo; rather certainly as I wrote — investigate.
