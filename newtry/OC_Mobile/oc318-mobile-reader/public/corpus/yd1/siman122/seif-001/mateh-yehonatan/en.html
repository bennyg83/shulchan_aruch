(Siman 122, Shulchan Aruch, seif 2) Nevertheless, the Sages forbade cooking in it l'chatchila, etc. The Rashba derived this law from the Gemara in Pesachim, daf 39, where Rav said, "Pots on Pesach must be broken," etc.; see there. If it were permitted l'chatchila to use a pot that is not ben yomo, why must they be broken? Let them leave the pots until after twenty-four hours and use them on Pesach. Rather, it is clear that l'chatchila it is forbidden. One can object that the Rashba holds that wherever even a minute amount is forbidden, nosen taam lifgam is likewise forbidden even b'dieved. If so, nothing can be proven from chametz on Pesach, which is forbidden even in a minute amount, and therefore nosen taam lifgam is also forbidden even b'dieved. By contrast, with other prohibitions one could say that a pot that is not ben yomo may be used l'chatchila. One can say that his proof is from Shmuel, who holds that a mixture of one type with another is nullified in sixty even with chametz on Pesach. It is certain that although the Rashba holds that nosen taam lifgam is forbidden in prohibitions that are effective in any amount and the like, where a mixture of one type with another is nullified in sixty, even if it became mixed with its own type and gives a detrimental taste it also does not prohibit, because once it is detrimental there is no significance of being the same type. Therefore, since Shmuel holds that a mixture of one type with another is nullified in sixty, if with all prohibitions it were permitted to use such a pot l'chatchila, why should it not be permitted to use it for chametz on Pesach, whether with its own type or with another type, since anything that gives a detrimental taste is treated as though it were a different type? Why, then, did Shmuel say that one should keep them until after Pesach and then use them? Rather, it is clear that nosen taam lifgam is forbidden l'chatchila.
