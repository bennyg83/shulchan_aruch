Therefore if he transgressed and used. Explanation: permitted matter that is not sharp; but if it is a sharp thing, certainly forbidden, as above seif 3; and the reason we establish gentile vessels as not ben yomo — the Tur wrote it is because of a double doubt: doubt whether they used it today, and even if they used it today, perhaps they used it in something that spoils; and it is written in the Tur: and according to this the same applies to our vessels — it is a double doubt, we rely to be lenient — end of his words; and so it is written in Shulchan Aruch nearby seif 7; and in Darkei Moshe in Hagahat Shach it is written: regarding a Jew's cooking there is remedy through asking, and if he does not know he is penalized — end of his words. And it seems to me that penalty applies only to that Jew whose pot it is and he should have known; but regarding another, penalty does not apply, as above siman 99 — end of Darkei Moshe. And Rashal wrote in chapter Kol HaBasar siman 65: it seems to me that even though stam gentile vessels are not ben yomo and also presumably one need not ask whether it is ben yomo or what they cooked in it if it is something not spoiled; nevertheless if he asked them and they told him it is ben yomo, he should also ask what they cooked in it; and if it is something not spoiled, or he is in a situation where he cannot know from them what they cooked in it — it is not fit to permit, since there is no double doubt here; and even though we are not concerned for a gentile's words — neither to forbid nor to permit — that is regarding something that was already in presumption of kashrut; but here the presumption that stam gentile vessels are absorbed with prohibition has worsened, only through double doubt it comes to permit; therefore we rely on his word. And furthermore that which we do not rely on a gentile's words — this is where he comes to improve his merchandise, saying they are orlah, as stated in the Gemara — end of his words. And this does not seem so to my humble opinion: what he wrote that after he asked and he told him it is ben yomo there is no double doubt here — behold certainly there is a double doubt here: doubt perhaps he lies in saying he used it today, for stam a gentile is presumed to lie, as stated in chapter Chazakat HaBatim regarding one compelled for his donkey; and from scripture: whose mouth speaks falsehood, etc.; and furthermore what he wrote that here presumption of kashrut worsened regarding the vessel — what do you see that we follow the vessel's presumption, follow the presumption of the dish cooked in it that has presumption of kashrut; and this is exactly like the sugya in first chapter of Chullin: establish the knife on its presumption and say it was not spoiled; on the contrary establish the animal on its presumption and say it was not slaughtered; and we answer: the knife was impaired, the animal was not impaired; and here likewise exactly so regarding the dish there is no adverse factor. And what he wrote that we do not rely on a gentile's words — specifically when he intends to improve his merchandise — it is true that at the end of Yevamot it is so: a gentile who brings fruits to market and says these fruits are orlah, etc. — he is not believed neither to forbid nor to permit, except he intended to improve his merchandise; the Gemara needed this lest you say since he harms himself and says they are orlah, certainly he spoke truth — to this it said on the contrary that he intends to improve his merchandise; but otherwise certainly a gentile is not believed at all, even to forbid, even where improving merchandise does not apply he says so, for even a Jew is not believed to forbid his fellow's property unless it is in his hand — and so it is stated in Mordechai chapter Rabbi Yishmael regarding a maidservant who said the wine found is what she drew; and he wrote: and if because of the maidservant's statement there is no concern, for even a Jew is not believed regarding something not in his hand now — end of his words. However it seems to me: if the gentile says thus innocently by himself without any question, and the matters appear he did not intend regarding a Jew — then one should be stringent and forbid the dish of a Jew who cooks in it; and proof for this from what is written in siman 113 seif 2, when the gentile says unprompted that a Jew commanded him to cut the blemish that is forbidden. And one should still discuss this, as ruled in siman 17: if the gentile is believed to him it is forbidden — likewise one should discuss this even when he does not speak unprompted.
