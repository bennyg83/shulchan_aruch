Stam utensils of a non-Jew are presumed not to be ben yomo; therefore, if one transgressed and used them before kashering, the dish is permitted. {Rama: But if he heated water in a utensil of a non-Jew in order to knead bread with it, it is forbidden to knead with that water, for regarding this it is called l'chatchila, since he has not yet begun to benefit from it, as it was not heated for drinking (ShaD and its hagahot). But something that was made for its own sake and not for something else — even though it is of a non-Jew and a Jew purchases it from him l'chatchila — it is permitted, since it was already made by the hand of the non-Jew, and it is called b'dieved.} Nevertheless, it is forbidden to say to a non-Jew, “Cook vegetables for me in your pot,” and likewise he should not say to him, “Make me a preserve,” for anyone who says “Cook for me” is as though he cooked with his own hands. And it is possible that through perfumers (or other craftsmen) it is permitted, for all craftsmen set aside clean utensils for their work so that their craft will not be spoiled; and a ba'al nefesh should be concerned, for these matters bring to purity and cleanliness.
