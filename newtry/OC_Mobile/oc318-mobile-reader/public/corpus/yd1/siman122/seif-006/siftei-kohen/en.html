Stam, etc. The reason — the poskim wrote that it constitutes a double doubt: doubt whether they used it today or yesterday; and if you say they used it today, perhaps they used it in something that spoils visibly or that is not nosen ta'am; and therefore it is forbidden l'chatchila to cook in gentile vessels, for there is only one doubt; a pot that is not ben yomo is forbidden l'chatchila. Maharshal wrote in his Issur VeHeter siman 59: that stam vessels of gentiles are not ben yomo — that is, one need not ask whether it is ben yomo or not; and if after he asked it became known to him that it is ben yomo, he should ask what they cooked in it today; and if it is something that certainly does not spoil, or that he cannot know — it is not fit to permit, since there is no double doubt here; and even though we are not concerned for a gentile's words — neither to forbid nor to permit (and see above siman 118 end of s.k. 37) — that is regarding something that was already in presumption of kashrut; but here the presumption that stam gentile vessels are absorbed with prohibition has worsened, only through double doubt it comes to permit; therefore we rely on his word. And furthermore that which we do not rely on gentiles' words — this is where he comes to improve his merchandise, saying they are orlah, as stated in the Gemara — end of his words; but from the responsum of Rabbi Levi ibn Chaviv, double doubt 121, it appears clear that even if he says it is ben yomo we do not rely on his word; and see above siman 127 s.k. 20.
That they were not heated for drinking, etc. It implies; but if they were heated for drinking it is permitted to drink them, for regarding this it is considered b'dieved and permitted like other things taken from gentiles that are permitted since it was already done by the gentile's hand — end of Torat Chayim beginning of general rule 59; and see above siman 114 seif 7; and above siman 94 s.k. 19 and siman 95 s.k. 14.
