Permitted. The reason — the poskim wrote that it constitutes a double doubt: doubt whether they used it today or not, and lest they used it in something that spoils visibly or that it is not nosen ta'am (and accordingly the same applies to our vessels — there is a double doubt and we rely to be lenient; and so too in Shulchan Aruch seif 7); and therefore it is forbidden l'chatchila to cook in a gentile's vessel, for there is only one doubt; a pot that is not ben yomo is forbidden l'chatchila. Maharshal wrote: that stam vessels of gentiles are not ben yomo — that is, one need not ask whether it is ben yomo or not; and if after he asked it became known to him that it is ben yomo, he should ask what they cooked in it today; and if it is something that certainly does not spoil, or that he cannot know — it is not fit to permit, since there is no double doubt here; and even though we are not concerned for a gentile's words — neither to forbid nor to permit — that is regarding something that was already in presumption of kashrut; but here the presumption of stam gentile vessels has already worsened, for on the contrary they are absorbed with prohibition only through double doubt it comes to permit; therefore we rely on his word; but from the responsum of Rabbi Levi ibn Chaviv, double doubt 121, it appears clear that even if he says it is ben yomo we do not rely on his word (however this is specifically if he lent it and the dish was already cooked in it; but if he says while still in his hand or after he lent it before it was cooked in it — even if he transgressed and cooked, it is forbidden; and see Pri Chadash s.k. 4; and see responsum of Mahari ibn Lev vol. 1 folio 134 and vol. 2 siman 68); and Taz also ruled thus, unless the gentile speaks unprompted by himself without any question; and the matters appear that he did not intend regarding a Jew — then one should be stringent and forbid the dish of a Jew who cooks in it, as written in siman 113 seif 2; and one should still discuss this, as written in siman 17: if the gentile is believed to him it is forbidden — likewise one should discuss this even if he does not speak unprompted — end of his words.
For drinking. Shach wrote: it appears that if they were heated for drinking it is permitted to drink them, for regarding this it is considered b'dieved [and permitted like other things taken from a gentile that are permitted since it was already done by the gentile's hand]; and see above siman 114 seif 7.
