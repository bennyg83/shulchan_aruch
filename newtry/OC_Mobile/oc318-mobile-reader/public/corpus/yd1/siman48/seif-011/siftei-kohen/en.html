And it is like punctured from one side, etc. Maharshal Orach Chaim siman 61 wrote: even according to Rashba — specifically in kereis, meses, and beit hakosot, where we establish on presumption of puncture wherever we did not examine (see s.k. 23 and 25); but kurkavan whose wall is thick — even for Rashi who wrote in the meses from one side treifah, they concede in kurkavan as Mahari wrote in Tur haArukh — if so, where not examined, even Rashba concedes we establish on presumption of kashrut — and this is clear until here his language. And this is not clear to me — for in Terumat HaDeshen there he wrote that even in beit hakosot Rashi concedes; but certainly one must say: for concern lest it healed we are not concerned, but for concern lest it punctured we are concerned; and so from Darkei Moshe's language that I brought in s.k. 40 — kurkavan is equal to beit hakosot in this:
And if there is no korat dam on it. For if there is korat dam on it — even if you say it came through the face — it punctured outward; for if there is no wound there — whence korat dam:
Korat dam, etc. He wrote in Darkei Moshe siman 49: examination from outside is required if there is no korat dam, according to those requiring examination when punctured from one side, end of his words; and it is implied where rinsed or salted its law is like punctured from one side:
And there is a distinction. The reason is as Orach Chaim haArukh kelal 91 din 23 wrote: since the needle is not recognizable from inside as it is not recognizable from outside — one may suspend and say it came from outside and punctured the esophagus or the dekin, and so all similar cases, end of his words; and it is implied that wherever there is some recognition from inside, such as there is korat dam from inside and not from outside — kosher:
We suspend to be lenient. Because the way of the esophagus is to swallow, and through there food enters — only the puncture was sealed; therefore one of the punctures was sealed — we suspend on what is its way; and further we established this bird on presumption of kashrut regarding these punctures — end of Rashba's words:
