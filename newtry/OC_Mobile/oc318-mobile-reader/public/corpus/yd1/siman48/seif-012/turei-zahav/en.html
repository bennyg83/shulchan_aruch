A minor in whom a needle was found, etc. It is a simple matter that a gentile is not believed at all in this — a minor is preferable to a gentile, as stated in Choshen Mishpat siman 35 seif 7: a minor and an adult are preferable to a gentile who converted. And I saw in a responsum to Maharam of Lublin siman 66 regarding a gentile who did melacha with an Israelite in flaying the animal and removing the innards — he came to the Israelite with a nail in his hand and said: see this iron nail that I found and it was stuck in the animal's kereis; the Jew said: where was it stuck, and he showed the place stuck inside the kereis shaped like peels in a manner the animal would not have been treifah if so; and he ruled not to rely on his words to permit but to forbid — for if the Israelite had come before the gentile removed the nail, it would have required examination whether the needle punctured outward; and now examination is impossible — forbidden from safek; and he elaborated there on being stringent in safek when not examined. I do not believe this ruling came from the Gaon's mouth — for certainly there is no treifah concern; if you do not believe him regarding the place of the nail's sticking, you also do not believe him regarding finding the needle at all — the mouth that forbade is the mouth that permitted, as stated in mishnah chapter 6 of Demai: one who sells produce in Syria and says they are from Eretz Yisrael — he must tithe (explanation: demai); if he says they are tithed — he is believed, for the mouth that forbade is the mouth that permitted — meaning: just as we believe him that they are from Eretz Yisrael, so we believe him that they are tithed; and likewise here. And greater: it appears even if gentiles do not show the place of sticking — such as when they cannot show — one does not forbid in any manner; for even regarding a minor we say here he is not believed to forbid unless he is sharp and knows to aim in the matter; and so below siman 127; all the more a gentile is not believed to forbid at all. And even regarding an adult Israelite we say there he is not believed unless it is in his hand the first time, as stated there — then there is credibility for an Israelite; but a gentile does not enter this category at all. But one may say: if the gentile is believed that he says he found a needle in the kereis somewhat inside the wall of the kereis — it is possible it was also punctured mafpolash, only the gentile did not know to be precise — then one forbids from safek; and proof: Beit Yosef wrote siman 16 in name of Rashba regarding oto v'et beno — if the gentile is believed to him, likewise here. And likewise regarding whether the vessel is ben yomo as siman 123 seif 16 — only when speaking offhandedly; but if the gentile said he found it stuck along the length of the nail inside the folds of the meses called heib — like small chambers in a manner the point of the needle is not opposite the thickness of the wall — one does not forbid even if the gentile is believed, and even if an Israelite said he found so and the kereis is not before us — we do not forbid from safek; for even according to what I wrote above to forbid without examination if punctured from one side — that is when the needle entered its point into the thickness of the wall and there is concern it went further and punctured; not so when lying along inside the skins of small chambers — no treifah concern unless the kereis is before us to examine where possible; and where impossible — one does not forbid from safek in this, as it appears clear and correct.
