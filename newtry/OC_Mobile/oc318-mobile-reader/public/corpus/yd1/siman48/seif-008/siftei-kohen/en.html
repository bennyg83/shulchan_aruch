Bach wrote on this — and this is his language: I wonder, etc., until "and not like Beit Yosef and Rabbenu Yerucham in Shulchan Aruch who thought about Rambam and Ra'ash a law that is not true," end of his language. From his words it is implied his intent is to challenge that examination is required after korat dam; for if only to see whether it punctured outward — Beit Yosef and Rabbenu Yerucham did not mention this at all. If so, what proof does he bring from "seeing" there — one must see whether it punctured outward, and the same here since it is stuck before us — examination is required if it did not puncture on the other side as on this side. But after korat dam — whence that examination is required; perhaps just as there is no korat dam inside, there is none outside. And further wonder: how did he mix Ra'ash's language and challenge Beit Yosef and the rabbi — he omitted Ra'ash's words regarding "a thorn stuck in esophagus" who ruled no examination is required if there is a puncture, and brought proof from beit hakosot; and Bach himself siman 33 seif 8 for Ra'ash's view — see there. Thus in beit hakosot it is obvious no examination is required (and Beit Yosef and Rabbenu Yerucham also omitted these words of Ra'ash — examine well). And one may wonder on Tur who wrote here "they turn over and examine," etc. — and from what he wrote above siman 33 and Orach Chaim in hints it is clear Ra'ash requires no examination for esophagus — all the more here. And even though for lung we permit only with blowing, as Ra'ash and Tur themselves wrote end of siman 36 — see Beit Yosef — one must distinguish: there as long as they do not blow it there is concern lest there is a puncture before us not recognizable; not so here, since according to estimation it is not punctured outward — no need to turn over and examine. And one may say lung is different because its membranes are very thin; and so Maharam distinguished in his book daf 162a; and one may distinguish in several manners — examine well:
