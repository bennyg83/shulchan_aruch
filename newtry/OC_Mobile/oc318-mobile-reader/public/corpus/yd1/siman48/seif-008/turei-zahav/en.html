They turn it over and examine, etc. This is Tur's language — implying examination is obligatory even when punctured from one side alone; and Rama brought nearby "some are lenient" from one side, etc. — namely Rambam and Ra'ash as Beit Yosef wrote in their view, and so in Darkei Moshe. I say: first it appears proven from the Gemara that examination is required — there Rav Safra said to Abaye: see, master, this young scholar from the west said an incident came before Rabbi with a needle in beit hakosot from one side, and he said treifah; he sent to him and he did not come; Abaye went to him and said: tell me the body of the incident — where was it. He said: a needle came before Rabbi in the thickness of beit hakosot from one side; Rabbi turned it over and found korat dam on it and declared treifah, and said: if there is no wound outward — whence this blood. Abaye said: in vain did he trouble to go to him — for there is no novelty here; it is our mishnah "the meses and beit hakosot punctured outward." And if there were no obligation to examine when found from one side — why did he say there is no novelty; behold he informed us of a great novelty that there is obligation to turn over and examine as Rabbi did — for otherwise why did Rabbi turn it over. Rather it is clear to Abaye from the mishnah that punctured outward is treifah, and whatever emerged from one side — certainly we must see if there is korat dam, for then certainly the second side was also punctured, as in any safek treifot where we see a defect. And as they said in the mishnah: it fell to the ground and its innards were crushed — treifah; and it does not teach that examination is required; but in truth examination is required as stated siman 52 and in many places where there is a defect; and as we say in the first chapter of Chullin (daf 17): regarding examination of a knife — it is obvious that since it punctured — treifah requires examination. And this is certainly so — since a knife is commonly found to damage, it is as if a defect is before us. And it appears from this reason that Rambam also holds thus, as Beit Yosef proved from Rambam that he holds no examination is needed from one side — since he mentioned only treifah and did not mention examination from one side. And in what we said for Abaye — from the mishnah it was exactly so in Rambam: he need not mention examination since before us in puncture from two sides it is certainly treifah, and in puncture from one side it requires examination afterward. Ra'ash wrote: if he turned it over and examined, etc. — Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe learned from this language that examination is not obligatory. I wonder — for Tur also wrote this language in hints like Ra'ash, yet wrote here "they turn it over," etc., implying examination is obligatory. Rather it is simple: the language "if" refers to "if found on it blood" — and this is what it says: if when he turned it over he found blood on it, etc. An example: Ra'ash himself explained on "it is known" regarding "it fell to the ground," etc. — this is what it says: when it fell to the ground its innards were crushed, etc. And likewise here: examination is obligatory for everyone according to what I wrote; and so Rashba for halacha and Mahari Chaim z"l. And according to what Abaye learned from the mishnah that punctured from two sides is treifah — we learn that from one side one must examine since there is safek treifot before us. It is further clear: whatever was salted or the needle was lost and examination is impossible — treifah from safek; for we cannot say there is a distinction between one side and two sides — otherwise Abaye's difficulty returns: why did he say there is no novelty in Rabbi's incident — perhaps the novelty is that examination is not required from one side, just as it is lenient when examination is impossible. Rather it teaches there is obligation to examine. It is simply that there is no leniency when found from one side — neither regarding obligation of examination nor when examination is impossible — only that Rama wrote nearby one may be lenient from one side when examination is impossible; and so Rashba; and it appears to me as I wrote and as Shulchan Aruch ruled according to Rashba's responsum siman 360 that wherever examination is required — treifah if examination is impossible; as Rama himself brought nearby — therefore one should be stringent also here, not like Rama's leniency in great loss twice in this siman.
Around the needle. And all the more on it — Beit Yosef answered his difficulty: what difference between one side and two sides — in both, if there is blood — treifah, and without blood — kosher; and it works for him that from one side there is treifah only with blood opposite the needle; and from two sides treifah even if blood is around the needle. And it appears to me to distinguish: from one side there is no treifah when blood is found inside — for even though blood is before us proving it was from life, nevertheless only one skin was punctured — therefore specifically if blood is found outside it is treifah, for it is proven the second side was also punctured. But from two sides there is treifah even if blood is found inside — for here it is proven it was from life.
Found in the meses, etc. It appears that if there is korat dam outside — treifah, as Rama wrote siman 33 regarding esophagus; and so is implied from what is said here "and no examination is required" — implying in any case there is prohibition when korat dam is found outside; and the same for a wound. And see what I wrote siman 49 seif 3.
And one may rely on them if it is great loss and examination is impossible, etc. I wonder what he innovated here — for he already wrote this above. And it appears he did not write it here except to answer why there is leniency when the needle was lost or the flesh was salted and rinsed — for he wrote nearby at the beginning of this hagahah: wherever examination is required, etc., treifah; therefore he wrote that from one side examination itself is not obligatory according to some — therefore kosher when examination is impossible; and such are his words in Darkei Moshe. But it was already clarified above that examination is obligatory — therefore forbidden in every case where examination is impossible, for there is safek treifot. Hagahot Shaarei Dura siman 92 and Orach Chaim haArukh kelal 91 wrote: if a needle is found in the wall of beit hakosot along its length such that one cannot understand which side the pouch faces — treifah, since its cavity is wide; if through the esophagus it should have been found on the secretion — rather it certainly punctured and came, end of his words. Rashba brought this Orach Chaim siman 61 and wrote on this: "along its length" means the needle lies in the wall of beit hakosot along the length of the needle — and these are matters of taste; and one may not be lenient even from one side and from inside. Therefore there is no permit from one side except when the needle lies in the cavity of beit hakosot specifically as food goes — not stuck along the length of the wall. Also Darkei Moshe siman 46 brought this hagahah — I do not know why he did not bring it in Shulchan Aruch.
