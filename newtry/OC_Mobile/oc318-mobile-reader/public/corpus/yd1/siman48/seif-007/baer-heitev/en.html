Mafpolash. For in any case they pour into each other; and some say the puncture goes from this cavity to that cavity — but even if only the meses alone or beit hakosot alone was punctured in the place of their attachment — kosher, because the meses wall protects beit hakosot punctures and beit hakosot wall protects meses punctures.
Treifah. Shach wrote: even if both skins were punctured in the place where it lies on the meses and is stuck there in fat — it does not seal, for it is tame chelev. And what is called meses and beit hakosot — Rashi: end of the kereis called paunch shaped like a hat called beit hakosot; meses attached to it; around their attachment when you separate them there is a wall for each; between them they pour into each other; food enters from beit hakosot to meses and from meses to kivah and from kivah to dekin.
Practice. The reason is in Shach: since its wall is thin — we are concerned lest it punctured and healed — and all this specifically when punctured through thorn or needle (and even examination does not help to see if punctured outward); but when evident it came through illness — kosher even without great loss. And not specifically in the meses — the same in beit hakosot in the thin place. And he wrote further: it is implied in the meses treifah even if there is no korat dam outside; however it appears we require korat dam from inside — otherwise we would say after slaughter it stuck in the meses, and if it stuck before slaughter blood should have flowed (and know: beit hakosot — part thick with two skins, part thin with only one skin; and according to Rashi and those who forbid — in the thin place too treifah like the meses. So too R' Yehudah Zahav).
