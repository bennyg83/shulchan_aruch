Hagahot Ashri and Kol Bo. The explanation is that they pull up grasses together with their clods of earth, and throwing them on their heads is a manner of grief, like “they cast dust upon their heads at the news.” Afterward they wash their hands and say, “Our hands did not spill this blood,” etc., “atone for Your people Israel,” etc., until “among Your people Israel.” Some explain that the reason for the earth is that one's end is earth, and the grass alludes to resurrection of the dead, as it is said, “they shall blossom from the city like the grass of the earth.” Tur.
In this siman the Beit Yosef brought in the name of Kol Bo, etc., that the father may protest. So too at the end of Yoreh De'ah he wrote in the name of Tashbetz, etc., and wrote there: however, the world has the custom, etc. I did not merit to have his book here. Darkhei Moshe.
Mas'et Binyamin wrote that where there is pressure regarding Kaddishes and not every person gets one Kaddish, the son within thirty is pushed aside before the yahrzeit. If the yahrzeit does not say Kaddish that day, he cannot say it tomorrow, for once the day has passed the offering is lost; unlike the son within thirty, who can say it tomorrow. So I have seen practiced. The Rav wrote in chapter 17 that this is a correct distinction. He further wrote there in his name that regarding seven days there is no distinction between adult and minor, nor between resident and guest. See the customs.
