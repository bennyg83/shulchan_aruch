Maharshal wrote that he received from his grandfather that one should say it seven times: the first time until the word “ki,” including the word “ki”; the second time until “ki malakhav”; the third until “ki malakhav yetzaveh”; the fourth until “yetzaveh lakh”; the fifth until “lakh lishmorcha”; the sixth until “lishmorcha bekhol”; and the seventh until “bekhol derakhekha.” There is a secret in this, and the Levush wrote that he too practiced this way.
Within the seven days of their mourning. In the customs it is written that one within thirty days is entirely displaced by one with a yahrtzeit if the latter is a resident for that whole day, while the Rama wrote here that the one with a yahrtzeit has only one kaddish within the thirty. In Sefer Masat Binyamin he raised a contradiction within the customs: first they wrote that with one within thirty days and one with a yahrtzeit, the yahrtzeit displaces the one within thirty days, as it does one within twelve months, implying that yahrtzeit is stronger than one within thirty. Then they wrote that one within twelve months who is a resident, and a yahrtzeit who is a guest, or one within thirty days who is a guest, displaces the resident within twelve months for the yahrtzeit guest in one kaddish and one prayer, and for the guest within thirty days divides with him all the kaddishes, etc.; this shows that one within thirty is stronger than yahrtzeit. He distinguished there that where there is space, such as on Shabbat when there are three kaddishes and one person is within thirty and one has yahrtzeit, then the one within thirty is stronger: he says two kaddishes and the yahrtzeit one kaddish. But where there is pressure in the kaddishes and not everyone can receive even one kaddish, then the one within thirty is displaced before the yahrtzeit. The reason is that if the yahrtzeit does not say kaddish that day, he cannot say it tomorrow, for once the day has passed the offering is void; this is unlike one within thirty, who can say it tomorrow. This is a correct distinction: whenever it is possible to give kaddish to both, the one within thirty has an advantage over the yahrtzeit; but if the yahrtzeit would be entirely displaced, that is not reasonable. He further wrote that with regard to seven days there is no distinction between an adult and a minor, a resident and a guest. It appears to me that one whose relative died on Shabbat or Yom Tov, and it is impossible to bury him that day, should in any event say kaddish immediately after the death, for saying kaddish does not depend on mourning at all. But on a weekday one should not do so, for while he is an onen he is exempt from prayer. So it appears to me.
