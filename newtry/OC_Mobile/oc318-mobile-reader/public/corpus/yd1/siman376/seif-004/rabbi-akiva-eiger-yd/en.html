The Eliyah Rabbah, Orah Hayyim (siman 224), wrote as follows: The homilies of Maharash record that when he went to the grave he would say: "May it be His will that the rest of [so-and-so] be in honor, and that his merit stand for me." One should not walk over a grave twice in the same day. The writings of the Ari; it is written in the Likkut at the end of Ma'aneh Lashon: one who calls [something written] on a gravestone has difficulty forgetting (and there it states that it is difficult to learn, and Rashi explained: it will be difficult to hear — but not that it causes forgetfulness — see there); and one may say: "abundant love" until "in love" — and it is very conducive to forgetfulness. The custom is not to set up a gravestone until after twelve months, because a gravestone connotes importance, and during his twelve months he has anguish; alternatively, the reason is that a gravestone prevents him from being forgotten in one's heart, and the deceased is not forgotten until after twelve months. It is customary not to take anything small or large from a mourner's house throughout the entire seven days of mourning, because the spirit of impurity rests there all seven days. Some also have the custom not to measure the cemetery within seven days of a burial. The ancient authorities placed a ban against taking from the river after escorting the dead, because once non-Jews invented a false charge and killed several Jews. It is customary to be careful not to hand a spade or mattock directly from one person to another when burying — rather they cast them to the ground and then pick them up. At the time of washing the hands, one is careful not to hand the vessel directly from one washer to the next — end quote.
