To alter. The Taz writes that Maharshal rejected this ruling, arguing that King David also said, "Can I bring him back again?" implying that if he could have returned the child, he would have done so. I am astonished by this objection. David said, "Why should I fast? Can I bring him back again?" He meant that once the child had died, fasting would not help. Consolers who say, "Do not distress yourself, for what can you do?" are not speaking about prayer and fasting, but about grief itself. They tell him not to grieve because no other outcome is possible. Their words imply that if another outcome were possible, he should grieve over events not conforming to human desire. This is blasphemous, for it is proper that events conform to the will of God, not to the will of man.
