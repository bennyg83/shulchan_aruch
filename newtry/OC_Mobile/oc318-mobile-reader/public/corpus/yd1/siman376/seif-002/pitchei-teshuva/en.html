To pray. See Baer Heitev, that Tachanun and Hallel are not recited in a mourner's home. Responsa Noda BiYehudah, second edition, Yoreh Deah, siman 215, explains the practice that the mourner goes elsewhere while the congregation recites Hallel. According to this custom, even when they pray in the house where the deceased died, if no mourners are present, Hallel is recited. See there. Responsa Har HaCarmel, Yoreh Deah, siman 20, writes that in a mourner's home that is not the house where the deceased died, because the death occurred elsewhere, Hallel is recited. The mourner himself, however, should in any event not recite Hallel because it contains the verse, 'This is the day Hashem has made; let us rejoice and be glad in it.' Likewise, when he recites Havdalah over a cup during shivah, although he may recite Havdalah, he should omit the verses of joy preceding it and begin directly with the blessings. Anything that a mourner ought not recite may not be recited even if he wishes to be stringent upon himself, because of the honor of the deceased. In the writings of the great Rabbi Daniel, of blessed memory, it is stated that if the seventh day of mourning occurs on Rosh Chodesh, the mourner should recite Hallel after leaving the synagogue, when his mourning has ended.
