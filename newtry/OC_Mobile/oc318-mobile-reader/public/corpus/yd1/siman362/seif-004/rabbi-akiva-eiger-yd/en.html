(Ibid., seif 4) Five handbreadths of earth are permitted. Beit Yosef writes that this requires study, because Terumas HaDeshen writes regarding this Baraisa that this applies specifically where the graves touch. But where one grave lies above another and three handbreadths of the upper grave's earth separate them, it is permitted. Sefer Chasidim distinguishes that where a vertical wall separates adjacent graves, six handbreadths are required, but where one grave is above another, three handbreadths suffice. He further writes that if no vacant space is available, the bones should be removed and the ground deepened so that after the deceased is buried, three handbreadths remain above the lower grave.
