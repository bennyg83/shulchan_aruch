One does not bury. See Responsa Shevut Yaakov II, siman 95, concerning the required distance between graves. Although the basic law is clear in the Tur that the dead should be separated from one another by six handbreadths, observe what the people actually do: they bury one adjacent to another and even one above another. Though this is not in accord with the strict law, the custom apparently spread because we have not been granted broad expanses of land, and in many places great effort is needed to obtain even a small burial area. It is enough that we can maintain our own limited ground. These laws were stated where ample land is available. The Taz, siman 364, seif katan 2, writes similarly concerning a met mitzvah. Nevertheless, in a place newly settled where adequate spacing is possible, the required distance should be maintained; see there.
