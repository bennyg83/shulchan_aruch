They may not bury. Shevut Yaakov, part 2, responsum 95, notes that the Tur requires six handbreadths between graves, yet common practice is to bury people adjacent and even one above another. He justifies the custom because Jews often possess only cramped cemetery land obtained with difficulty. These measurements were stated where ample land is available. In newly settled places where adequate spacing is possible, it should be maintained. See Taz 364:2.
