To pass over. See Be'er Heitev. See Batei Kehunah, Beit Din siman 23, who wrote that the practice of some kohanim to prostrate themselves on the graves of tzaddikim has no basis to rely upon. If they rely on the Midrash brought in Yalkut Mishlei siman 9, where Eliyahu said that there is no tumah in a Torah scholar, this is not valid, for Tosafot in HaMekabel, Bava Metzia 111, and in HaBa Al Yevimto 61, already wrote that he was deflecting him, and his real reason was that it was a met mitzvah. He discusses at length what forced Tosafot to explain this way. And if they rely on what is stated in chapter Mi SheMet, that one may leap over, this refers to coffins, etc.; perhaps here too, since most coffins have a hollow of a tefach, there is no Torah tumah, and the Sages did not decree against prostrating at the graves of tzaddikim. This too is not valid, for those cases are all because of a mitzvah or human dignity; but regarding prostrating at the graves of tzaddikim we have not found a known mitzvah or honor of sages. Moreover, perhaps they were not buried in coffins at all, or the coffins rotted, so it is a sealed grave, which is Torah-level tumah. Therefore those who practice this act improperly; see there at length.
