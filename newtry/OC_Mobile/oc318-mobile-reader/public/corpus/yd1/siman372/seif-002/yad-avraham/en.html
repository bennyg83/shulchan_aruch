(There) It is forbidden to wait until he dresses himself. This appears to apply specifically when the kohen initially lay down in a prohibited situation, such as where the corpse was already in the house or he entered a cemetery. Remaining then is treated as an action, like one who continues wearing kilayim after an initially prohibited act of dressing. If, however, no corpse was present when he entered, and a naked kohen lay there permissibly, and only afterward someone died in the house, remaining is not treated as an action. Tosafot in Yevamot 90b and several rulings of the Rambam imply this. He may therefore remain until he dresses, because human dignity overrides a Torah prohibition when violation is only passive.
I saw that Rabbi Ozer, of blessed memory, questioned the Rama from Tosafot and Rashba in Berachot 20, who derive from an unattended corpse that human dignity overrides a kohen's impurity prohibition. Penei Yehoshua argues that this case also contains the positive mitzvah of burial, and cannot serve as a general source. His alternative explanation of the Gemara is strained. The Lechem Mishneh in chapter 12 of Mourning provides a basis through which the Rambam's position in the laws of kilayim can be reconciled, as I explain elsewhere.
