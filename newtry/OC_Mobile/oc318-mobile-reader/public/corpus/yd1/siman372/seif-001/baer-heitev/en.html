But not on the Tomb of Adar, because of Daidana, all of them are sealed and there is a lot of space in which the Tome is plucked and up-to-grounded and enthusiastled against the empty side in which the void is held, and there is no cultivating space, but against the impurity, and not against the empty side in which the void is sooted:
Specifically. Shach writes that this wording specifically refers to being in the same tent as the corpse. If the kohen is in a neighboring house adjacent to the house containing the corpse, although that neighboring house is also impure under the rule above in siman 371, seif 4, the impurity may be only rabbinic, as implied by Tur and the poskim. By Torah law a separate tent with an opening of a tefach remains pure. Likewise, if the house containing the corpse is completely sealed, impurity in neighboring houses arises only rabbinically because the corpse will ultimately be taken out. Therefore, in neighboring houses under any such circumstance, the kohen may first dress himself before leaving.
To wake him. Shach writes that according to what he just explained, where the kohen is not in the same tent as the corpse but only in an adjacent house subject to rabbinic impurity, one need not wake him. Perhaps this is why Rama carefully writes, "and the corpse is with him in the same tent." See below, siman 373.
