To pass. But not over the graves, even for a mitzvah, because nowadays they are all sealed and there is much hollow space in the graves; therefore the tumah breaks through and rises to the heavens, even opposite the empty side. This is unlike a sealed grave with no hollow of a tefach, which renders tamei only opposite the tumah itself and not opposite its empty side. End quote from Taz.
And specifically. The Shach wrote: it appears he stated specifically “in the tent of the dead.” But if he is in houses adjacent to the house containing the dead, although they too are tamei as above in siman 371 seif 4, nevertheless one can say this is only rabbinic tumah, as found in the Tur in this siman and in the poskim. See Beit Yosef, that by Torah law a tent that has an opening of a tefach is tahor; see there and examine carefully. And when the house containing the dead is sealed on every side, the adjacent houses also have only rabbinic tumah, because the tumah is destined to leave. If so, in adjacent houses in every case he should dress himself first. End quote.
To wake him. The Shach wrote: according to what I wrote nearby, that where he is not in the actual tent with the dead but only in a house adjacent to it there is only a rabbinic prohibition, one need not wake him. Perhaps for this reason the Rav was precise and wrote, “and the dead is with him in the tent,” etc. See below siman 373. End quote.
