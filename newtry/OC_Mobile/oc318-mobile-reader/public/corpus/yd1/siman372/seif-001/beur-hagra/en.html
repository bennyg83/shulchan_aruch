(Likkut) But one who violates, etc.; a kohen who is lying, etc. See Tosafot in Berakhot 20a, s.v. shev, etc., where they write: rather it appears, etc. The Ramban wrote in Torat HaAdam that some texts indeed read this explicitly in the Gemara, and Rav Hai Gaon wrote likewise. According to this, what was said there at first regarding rabbinic tumah is rejected, namely that every prohibition of tumah is a prohibition that is not equally applicable to all. But Rashi's words there, s.v. shev, appear correct: initially, etc., as stated in the first chapter of Yevamot, 3b and 7a, where the Gemara says: perhaps we say that a positive commandment overrides a negative commandment only, etc., and one does not learn from the case of a brother's wife itself, where yibbum overrides it; see there.
