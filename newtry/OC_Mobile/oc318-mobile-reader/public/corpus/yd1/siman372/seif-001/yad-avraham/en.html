(Siman 372 seif 1 in the gloss) A kohen who is lying unclothed and is in an ohel with a corpse. In Shach s.k. 2 it implies that “the end of tumah is to go out” is only rabbinic. Sefer Tiferet LeMoshe asked that although Rashi explained so in the first chapter of Beitzah, from the Talmud at the end of Beitzah it is proven that it is Torah law, as the Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Piskei Maharai, and so Rashi explained in Perek HaDar that it is halachah leMoshe miSinai. The Magen Avraham in Orach Chaim siman 311 also wrote that the Shach was not precise. But according to what Sefer Shaar HaMelekh writes in Hilkhot Eruvin, this is not difficult at all; see there. He writes according to the view of the Rif and the Rambam that according to all it is rabbinic, and that what the second chapter of Beitzah says, that Rabbi Oshaya is stringent regarding it with respect to bereirah as he is stringent in a Torah matter, is because its root is Torah law. He holds that regarding something that has a root in the Torah, we are stringent as with Torah law. But Rabbi Oshaya and Rava in Perek HaDar hold that we say bereirah regarding “the end of tumah is to go out,” and this must be because they hold it is only rabbinic. Although tumah has a root in the Torah, we say bereirah because this matter itself is rabbinic. The Rif and Rambam ruled accordingly, and the Penei Yehoshua in Beitzah wrote this as well. In my work on the Rambam, Hilkhot Issurei Mizbeach, I resolved on this basis the words of the Rambam there, and I also proved from other places that this is the Rambam’s view. According to this, the law is with the Shach here. Later I found that the Penei Yehoshua at the beginning of chapter 3 of Berakhot wrote in a kuntres to resolve the Shach’s words and brought a proof for them; perhaps he intended what I wrote. In his novellae to Sukkah 10 he proved that this is Rashi’s view there, and on 21 he proved that this is the Rambam’s view, that tumah passing from house to house through windows and an opening of a tefach is only rabbinic. In Sefer Mishkenot Yaakov, newly printed, he noted the Shach’s words from Rashi’s commentary at the end of Beitzah, and wrote to resolve that it depends on whether we say bereirah in Torah law or not. He did not mention that this had already been raised against the Shach, and that his words are resolved according to Sefer Shaar HaMelekh and the Penei Yehoshua.
