It is as if they stipulated thus. See in Semag CM siman 72 s.k. 114 who wrote that all this is as long as the non-Jew is alive, for then the first Jew stands in the non-Jew's place to redeem him; but if the non-Jew died, the second Jew acquired the collateral, for since the non-Jew's power departed, so too departed what comes from his power. However, if the non-Jew has heirs who know of this and intend to redeem it, then the first Jew too can redeem it, etc. And see in responsum Tzitz Eliezer siman 20 who disagrees; his view is that always the second Jew must give to redeem the first Jew, because it is not a sale but only for ribbit permission and not to acquire truly — see there.
