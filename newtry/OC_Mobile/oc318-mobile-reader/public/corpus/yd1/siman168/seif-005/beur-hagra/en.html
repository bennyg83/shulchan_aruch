If a Jew, etc. This is from a responsum of the Rashba cited by the Beit Yosef, folio 130b, s.v. "If a Jew," etc. He wrote that this is unlike the case in seif 6, where a non-Jew borrowed, etc., and it is permitted, because here the arrangement is evasive. Furthermore, if the non-Jew placed the second Jew in direct relation with the first, it is forbidden because agency is treated stringently, as the Shulchan Aruch writes there. The Shulchan Aruch did not discuss here a case of such evasion. This is the meaning of the Rama's words, "specifically," etc., and "according to the opinion," etc. Their dispute is explained in seif 6.
And even if, etc.; but, etc. So write the Hagahot Maimoniyot, cited by the Darkei Moshe, seif katan 18. See Mordechai, siman 456, who writes that when a non-Jew borrows money from a Jew, even if the lender knows that the non-Jew is borrowing for another Jew's benefit, he may collect principal and interest, because a non-Jew is not subject to agency. He then cites a great authority who disagrees with Rabbeinu Tam, proving his view from the Tosefta: if a Jew appointed a non-Jew as steward or administrator, it is forbidden to borrow from him with interest. Mordechai answers that a steward or administrator is publicly known to be lending on the Jew's behalf. This is the meaning here of "because it appears," etc. All the other poskim, however, explain the prohibition because the responsibility rests upon the Jew, as the Tosefta states, "This is the rule," etc., and as explained below in seif 24.
(Likkut) And even if, etc. The Hagahot Maimoniyot write this because the Tosefta posed a difficulty to Rabbeinu Tam's ruling. It teaches: if a Jew said to a non-Jew, "Go lend my money with interest," it is forbidden. They distinguish between a case with collateral and one without collateral. See there, siman 33, and Mordechai, siman 447. The poskim, however, distinguish according to whether responsibility rests upon the Jew or upon the non-Jew.
