If a Jew, etc. This is from Rashba's responsum that Beit Yosef brought siman 130 side 2 entry "if a Jew," etc.; and he wrote: I wonder at this, for in siman 66 "a non-Jew borrowed," etc., it is permitted — for here he is scheming; and further, if he presented him, etc., it is forbidden because of agency for stringency as Shulchan Aruch wrote there, and Shulchan Aruch did not write here such a case which is through scheming; and this is what the Hagahah says "and specifically," etc., and "according to the one who says," etc.; and their disputes will be explained in siman 66.
And even if, etc., but, etc. So Hagahos Maimoniyos, and Darkei Moshe brought it s.k. 18; and see Mordechai siman 356 who wrote: a non-Jew borrowed money from a Jew, etc., even though the lender knows the non-Jew borrows for his fellow Jew's need — he collects principal and interest, for there is no agency for a non-Jew — see there. And he wrote: I found the words of a great one who disagrees with Rabbeinu Tam; and his proof from what is taught in Tosafot: a non-Jew who appointed a Jew as overseer or guardian — forbidden to borrow from him with interest; and he wrote: it seems we can answer that overseer and guardian are a well-known matter that his loan is for the Jew; and therefore here because it appears, etc.; but all poskim explain because it is on the Jew's responsibility, as stated "this is the rule," etc., and as stated below siman 64. (Likkut) And even if, etc. So Nimukei Yosef, because he was challenged on Rabbeinu Tam's ruling from Tosafot that teaches there: a Jew who said to a non-Jew, go borrow my money with interest — forbidden; and he distinguishes between with collateral or without — see there siman 33; and see Mordechai siman 347. But the poskim distinguished between on the Jew's responsibility or the gentile's (end quote).
