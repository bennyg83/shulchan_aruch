(Taz s.k. 20) Since both know of the matter. I wonder — if so, where the lender did not know, the permission of acquisition stands; if so, why do we need the reasoning that they are not believed — behold, since initially he did not know, even though it became clear it belongs to the Jew, nevertheless it was permitted through acquisition. And more so, Siftei Kohen wrote in truth (s.k. 26) that even though it is now known to him it is permitted because of acquisition. If so, why did the Mechaber write the permitted case that they are not believed. Also I wonder at the foundation of Beit Yosef and Derishah that for Rabbeinu Tam in any case it is permitted; if so it implies for Rabbeinu Tam, since there is no agency for a non-Jew, we need not the reasoning of acquisition. And since so, also for Rashi's view, where he did not know and it became clear afterward — we need not acquisition reasoning nor the reasoning that they are not believed, for we hold like R. Yitzchak that in unsecured debt without the lender's knowledge it is permitted to take interest even for Rashi's view as above (seif 7). And this is that where he did not know they were not stringent to say there is agency for a non-Jew. If so, automatically where he did not know there is no difference between Rabbeinu Tam's view and Rashi's. And just as for Rabbeinu Tam where he knows it is permitted and acquisition reasoning is not needed, so too for Rashi where he did not know. And see Bach who wrote in truth that the Tur comes to say that even for those who forbid, according to Rashi, even where he did not know — nevertheless the collateral is permitted because of acquisition. If so, for us too, even for Rashi's view acquisition is not needed. Also in this foundation that for Rabbeinu Tam in any case it is permitted — and Taz also wrote thus in Shulchan Aruch (seif 10): if the pledged party did not know, etc., and this is from Mahariik that in this there is no aspect of agency — it implies that without an aspect of agency there is no prohibition with collateral. And Taz also wrote Siftei Kohen (s.k. 26) that for Rabbeinu Tam and his companions it is permitted; behold, even though there is no acquisition there, nevertheless it is permitted for Rabbeinu Tam. And in my humble opinion, from Ramban's words in Be'er HaGolah siman 66 ch. 4 it implies it is not from agency that it is forbidden, but rather that whenever the collateral was not acquired by the non-Jew and its responsibility is the lender's on the collateral — it is forbidden. And Taz forbids also in the second case of agency where he commanded to pledge to the non-Jew and pledged to a Jew, for in such a case he did not acquire for him. And likewise is explained in Nimukei Yosef (and see Darkei Moshe — puzzling); and as Ramban's language there is precise: "and a Jew's collateral in a non-Jew's hand — if a non-Jew borrows on it from a Jew with interest, it is forbidden" — end quote; it implies in every case. And even for Rabbeinu Tam that there is no agency for a non-Jew — nevertheless it is forbidden from the aspect of collateral, for ribbit extends on the Jew's collateral — see there. Examine well.
