Likewise, etc., upon the ground. And he is discharged, as stated in the Gemara, unlike the Beit Yosef, and as explained in Choshen Mishpat, siman 120. The Taz and Shach also write this.
And some say, etc. This refers to the end of seif 1, where the non-Jew placed the second Jew in direct relation with the first. By strict law this is permitted, as taught in the Tosefta and Yerushalmi, that whenever the responsibility remains upon the non-Jew it is permitted, as explained below. Tosafot nevertheless wrote there that since the first Jew had accepted responsibility until then, etc. Their meaning is that this case differs from the Tosefta and Yerushalmi, where the money was never under the Jew's responsibility. Here, however, until the non-Jew lends it to the second Jew, it remains under the first Jew's responsibility, and he is discharged only through that loan. This is the meaning of the words, "And some say," etc.
And if the second Jew, etc. This was written on the basis of the Hagahot Mordechai and cited by the Beit Yosef, but their words are difficult. On the contrary, there it states the reverse, explaining that when the non-Jew placed him in direct relation with the first Jew, he appointed the first Jew as an administrator, and it is therefore permitted. Nevertheless, the Gemara established that case specifically where the money was placed upon the ground, as explained above. The later authorities have already challenged this ruling. See the Shach.
