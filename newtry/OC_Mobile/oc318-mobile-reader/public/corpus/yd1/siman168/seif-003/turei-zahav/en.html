"Place them on the ground." In the Gemara it states on the ground and exempt; and Beit Yosef wrote the Tur holds "and exempt" is not specifically required; and one can question whence the Tur to explain thus — behold in CM siman 120 when he says throw my debt and he threw, the borrower is still responsible until he says throw my debt and exempt; and let us say here too it is specifically required, and whoever did not say so is forbidden since still on the Jew's responsibility — and this is certainly no distinction between "throw" and "place on the ground." And it seems the Tur holds since the main prohibition here is receiving from the Jew's hand — it appears as the first Jew's agent giving ribbit to the non-Jew — and since he does not take from his hand but from the ground, this does not apply; and what they said in the Gemara "and exempt" — they did not mean exempt from responsibility which we do not care about, but to exclude that he not leave them on condition he return and take them — then it would be as if he did not place them; therefore he said and exempt — meaning exempt yourself from here onward and the money shall remain placed; and this is what Tur and Shulchan Aruch wrote "and he did and withdrew" — for apparently "and he withdrew" is superfluous; and per my explanation it is resolved: specifically we require he leave there; but as long as he stands there it is as if still in his hand, for perhaps he will return and take; and if so Tur's words are exactly the Talmud's words and there is no "not specifically required" in the Talmud.
"To receive them from the first Jew through him." My teacher z"l challenged this — behold it was already explained in seif 1 that saying give them to him and exempt and I will stipulate with him, etc. is forbidden; and one can answer: there is different, for there he said give them and exempt — exempt only after giving, so at giving time there was prohibition; unlike here where the non-Jew explicitly tells the first Jew: behold responsibility for the money is from today on me and you shall be my guardian — as I mentioned in seif 2 in Hagahos Mordechai — automatically there is permission at giving time. And this is explained in what Rama wrote "and the first Jew is only like the non-Jew's agent"; and even though in seif 23 he wrote regarding guardian forbidden because of marit ayin — I am here where the Jew made the loan with the non-Jew's consent, only that Baal Hagahos Mordechai wrote this explicitly on the Baraisa in what it said "and if they presented before a non-Jew — permitted" — discussing making the first Jew guardian; and nevertheless we do not find permission in the Gemara in explaining the Baraisa except placing on ground or giving and taking by hand; and Rama wrote here that even if the Jew received from the first Jew's hand it is permitted since he made the first guardian — and there is no one to answer this matter; and this Hagahah is not halakhah; and even though Levush followed him, there is no proof he delved deeply.
