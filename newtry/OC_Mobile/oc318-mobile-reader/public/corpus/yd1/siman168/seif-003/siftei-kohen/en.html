"He placed them on the ground and exempted," etc. So it is in the Gemara and all poskim; and the Mechaber follows his reasoning that he wrote in Beit Yosef: since Rambam and Tur did not mention "and exempt," apparently in every case it is permitted. And to me it appears that "and exempt" is specifically required; and further, for even if he said "throw my debt to me," behold it is on the borrower's responsibility until he says "and exempt," as explained in CM siman 120 — see there. And from Rambam there is no proof at all, for he did not mention this law of placing on the ground at all — one could challenge him why he did not mention "and exempt"; and this is because the Gemara challenges "what is there to say — it is obvious," and establishes it in another resolution of giving and taking by hand; therefore Rambam did not mention the resolution of placing on the ground and exempting for the simple meaning; and on the contrary it is proven apparently the opposite — if Rambam held "and exempt" is not specifically required, he should have written it to inform us it is not specifically required. And from Tur too there is no proof, for he refers to the beginning where he said "give them to this Jew and be exempt" — forbidden; and he says in the conclusion that if he said "place them on the ground" in place of "give them to him," meaning he said place them on the ground and exempt — permitted. And further I found in an old Tur it was written "and exempt," and it is clear to me this is a omission in other books; I again saw in Bach that he wanted to prove from the Gemara that "and exempt" is not specifically required — and his words are puzzling; for what he wrote "since the Gemara does not establish when he said place on the ground without exempt," etc. — on the contrary, to the Gemara it is obvious it is forbidden; and what he wrote "further this is a fortiori," etc. — it is a fortiori challenge; for when he gave and took by hand the first Jew departed and behold it is in the non-Jew's possession; but when he placed on the ground, perhaps he placed them saying they shall be in your possession — he said.
