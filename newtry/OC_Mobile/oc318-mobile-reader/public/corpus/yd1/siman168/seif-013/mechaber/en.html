A non-Jew who said to a Jew, lend me money from a Jew with interest on my collateral — it is permitted for the lender to take interest from the Jew the agent, provided the lender says to the agent: you shall be my agent to bring me the collateral from the non-Jew on my responsibility and to convey the money to him on my responsibility. {Rama: And if the non-Jew gave the collateral to the Jew before the second Jew lent — even if responsibility for the collateral was on the agent it is permitted (the Rabbi's reasoning, unlike Beit Yosef), only that responsibility for the money be on the lender. And likewise at redemption: you shall be my agent to bring my money from the non-Jew and they shall be on my responsibility from when they leave the non-Jew's hand; and likewise you shall be my agent to return the collateral to me. And if he does not trust him he should say so in his heart and need not inform him, or give it to him with a condition that he not be believed to say it was lost or stolen except before witnesses. And if he did so and at redemption the agent says, you did not tell me the truth initially but it is mine — the lender says, I do not believe you except in your first words I believe, but I am concerned for your later words and do not wish to accept money from you; and when the non-Jew comes and gives me the money I will give him his collateral. And even if he wishes to swear he is not believed; and even if witnesses testify it belongs to the Jew it does not help, since he first admitted it is not his. Rama: And all the more so a borrower's oath does not help (Beit Yosef in the name of an Ashkenazic responsum). However, if the matter became known that so is the truth, it is forbidden to take interest from him; and one who schemes to do so is called wicked (Beit Yosef in the name of Rashba). And all this discusses only lending on collateral; but if he gave him money to lend to the non-Jew with interest and afterward the recipient said, I did not lend to the non-Jew but took for my own need — the recipient is believed that he took for his own need; and if he is suspected of lying, he is excommunicated that his words are true; and one who does so is called wicked (responsum of Rashba siman 453).}
