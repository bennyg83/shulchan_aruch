A non-Jew, etc. As stated in Tosafeta perek 5: a Jew who said to a non-Jew, here is your wage, go and lend my money with interest — forbidden; and a non-Jew who said to a Jew, take your wage and lend my money with interest — permitted, but forbidden because of marit ayin. A Jew's money deposited with a non-Jew — permitted to lend with interest; and a non-Jew's deposited with a Jew — forbidden to lend with interest. A Jew who became guardian or overseer for a non-Jew — permitted to borrow from him with interest; and a non-Jew who became guardian or overseer for a Jew — forbidden to borrow from him with interest. This is the rule: whatever is on the Jew's responsibility is forbidden; whatever is on the non-Jew's responsibility is permitted. And likewise in Yerushalmi — the first case means "take your wage," and the last cases of guardian and overseer are on the depositor's responsibility; and the middle case of deposited money is on the depositary's responsibility; and in the last case, since it is well known, even marit ayin does not apply — see below end of this siman. And where a Jew became guardian and overseer — it is permitted to lend to a Jew who became the Jew's agent to borrow from a non-Jew, since responsibility is not on him; likewise he becomes borrower of the Jew to lend to the non-Jew; and therefore "and he should say," etc.; and likewise, etc.; and see Mordechai siman 347 and 348 and Rosh in his rulings siman 56; and a gentile who said, etc.; and Tosafeta, etc.; and in Rosh's responsum and Tur wrote in the name of Ramban that one need not accept except responsibility for the money and not for the collateral; but Rosh disagrees and requires responsibility for the collateral too — see there.
