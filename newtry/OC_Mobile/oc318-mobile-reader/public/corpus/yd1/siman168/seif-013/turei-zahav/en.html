A non-Jew who said, etc. The Jew is the agent of his fellow Jew, even if responsibility for the collateral rested upon the agent. The Darkei Moshe rules this way, unlike the Beit Yosef, who forbids it, for there is no reason that the lender must bear responsibility except after the loan. The Tur’s statement that the lender must say to the agent, “You shall be my agent to bring the collateral from the non-Jew,” means after he gave the money to the agent. Alternatively, since the Jew sent him to lend to the non-Jew immediately upon bringing the collateral, he became his agent at once and the lender must accept responsibility. This is unlike a case where the broker brings the collateral before the lender sent for it, in which case it is unnecessary to return it to the non-Jew and then take it again under the lender’s responsibility. The phrase “Nevertheless, I am concerned for your words” was explained by Maharshal to mean: lest you spread a bad name about me, saying that I took interest from you. Alternatively, it means: although I do not believe your claim that the collateral is yours, I do believe that you came of your own accord to redeem it and not as the non-Jew’s agent. Therefore, if the non-Jew does not come, I will retain it for myself. End quote.
