And he may take ribbis from the non-Jew. This is because the loan is genuinely the non-Jew's obligation and the Jew is not receiving interest from a fellow Jew.
Permitted etc. Even though there is no agency for a non-Jew — since he became the lender’s agent to carry his money to the non-Jew and bring him his collateral; and even if the agent redeemed it with his own money and gave the lender principal and interest — it is permitted, for the Torah forbade only interest that comes from the borrower’s hand to the lender’s hand, and this one is not a borrower but merely an agent — Tur; and it is explained below seif 14 in the Hagahah.
But if the Jew, etc. Where the Jew effectively becomes the borrower or assumes the debt as his own, the arrangement is forbidden because the interest then runs between Jews.
And if the non-Jew gave etc. Meaning: specifically where the lender placed the money in the agent's hand to lend to the non-Jew and take collateral from him — thereby becoming the lender's agent — the lender must accept responsibility for the collateral immediately; but where he had not yet become the Jew's agent, such as the non-Jew who gives collateral to a Jew to lend him money from a Jew, and the Jew first brought collateral from the non-Jew without the lender's agency — he need not return and bring it to the non-Jew for the lender to accept responsibility on it — Darkei Moshe; and Bach likewise.
And if he transfers, etc. The permissibility depends upon the non-Jew's obligation remaining the true source of payment and upon the Jewish intermediary not undertaking the interest-bearing debt as his own.
He is not believed etc. Beit Yosef wrote in the name of an Ashkenazic responsum: specifically if when he lent him he handed the collateral to his hand — for he acquired it by holding it, etc.; and Darkei Moshe brought it; and likewise in Rashba's responsum siman 558 in the name of the Riva.
And this applies specifically, etc. If the arrangement places substantive responsibility for principal and interest upon the Jew, it has the character of a Jewish interest-bearing loan and is forbidden.
If it became known etc. Meaning: if it became known to the lender with certainty that this is the truth — even though by law the court cannot coerce him not to take interest, for he may say "I believe no one except the borrower’s words" — nevertheless it is forbidden for him to take interest since it is known to him it belongs to the Jew; and see above siman 127 s.k. 14.
And the one who schemes to do so. To borrow in a non-Jew's name and says it is his — is called wicked; so it is in Rashba there.
Except in a loan on collateral. And the same without collateral if it is known he lent to the non-Jew, as above s.k. 34 — to exclude one who says “I did not lend to a non-Jew,” etc.
He places a ban on him. Since he claims perhaps; and if the lender claims certainly that he knows he lent them to the non-Jew with interest — the recipient swears heses and is exempt; and likewise Pri Megadim; and it is obvious.
And one who does so is called wicked. Somewhat puzzling, for Rashba wrote only that he left the category of “the remnant of Israel shall not do wrong” but did not say he is called wicked; and even though above he is called wicked — that case differs, for he explicitly states from the outset he is lending to a non-Jew; and it is easy.
