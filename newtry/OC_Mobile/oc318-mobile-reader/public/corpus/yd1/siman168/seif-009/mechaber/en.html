A non-Jew who borrowed money from a Jew with interest on collateral, and at the time of redeeming the collateral a Jew came and said the collateral is mine — the lender is not believed, and witnesses cannot help him testify that those very coins the lender gave the non-Jew returned and were given to the Jew, for he can say he withheld them for himself and lent him from his own money. And if he knows the collateral belongs to the Jew, it is forbidden to lend on it; and even if he does not know for certain, if it is known that it belongs to the Jew, such as a garment that non-Jews do not customarily wear — it is unseemly to take interest. {Rama: And all this is only for one who holds there is agency for a non-Jew for stringency; but for one who does not hold so, it is permitted in every matter (Beit Yosef). Therefore they wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Tam that it is permitted to give collaterals to a non-Jew from the outset, even his slave and maidservant, to pledge them with another Jew, even if they are his books and the lender recognizes them; and even if the lender returns and deposits them with the borrower it is permitted, since a non-Jew's hand is intermediary — there is no agency for a non-Jew (Beit Yosef in the name of Mordechai, perek Avodah Zarah, and in Hagahos Ashiri in the name of Aviasaf, and Tur, and Hagahos Maimoniyos, and Nimukei Yosef). And I already wrote that one should not protest those who practice leniency; and even if the non-Jew goes and the Jew redeems the collateral it is permitted. However, if the lender does not wish to give the collateral to the Jew whom the non-Jew sent, the authority is in his hand, for he has dealings only with the non-Jew (responsum of Rosh klal 108 siman 8, and so is implied in the poskim). Therefore initially the collateral must also be equal to his money so that he should have no dealings with the borrower at all if he wishes (so is implied there).}
