And it is permitted for him to take principal and interest from the Jew, since the lender did not know from the outset; and even though it is now known the collateral belonged to the Jew who gave his collateral to the non-Jew to borrow on it from a Jew with interest — it is permitted, for by default one does not abandon a permitted path and eat forbidden matter; and he transferred the collateral to the non-Jew, and the non-Jew acquired it through meshichah; and all that he gives to the Jew is he gives it on the non-Jew's behalf — Tur.
And if he knows etc. — forbidden. For there is concern that perhaps he did not transfer the collateral to the non-Jew.
The matter is ugly. Since at the time of the loan it is known that it belongs to the Jew.
And deposits them with the borrower etc. The borrower uses them or sells them, but the borrower commits to return to the lender them or their value — there.
However, if the lender, etc., he has permission. This is only in such a case, where the Jew gave it to him from the outset to pawn it. But if the Jew lent his vessels to the non-Jew, or even gave them to pawn temporarily and to return them to him, then when the non-Jew pawned them to another Jew for a term he is a robber, and the second Jew must return them to the first, as Mordechai wrote, that it is like a robber who took from this one and gave to that one, in Choshen Mishpat siman 369. So too wrote Bach. Beit Yosef and the Rav below in seif 11 distinguish in another way.
