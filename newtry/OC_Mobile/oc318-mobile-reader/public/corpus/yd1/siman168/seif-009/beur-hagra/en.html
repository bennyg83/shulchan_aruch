A non-Jew who borrowed, etc. See the Rosh there. He also writes in a responsum, "if he gave," etc. Sefer HaTerumah, the Tur, and the other poskim write that the Ramban disputes Rabbeinu Yitzchak and holds that we do not presume the Jew transferred ownership of the collateral to the non-Jew. The Rosh, however, holds it is permitted, provided the lender does not know that the collateral belongs to a Jew or that the non-Jew is borrowing for a Jew's benefit. If the Jew says, "It is mine," he is not believed, etc. But if the lender knows, etc., the law is as stated here. All this appears in the Tur and is based upon Rabbeinu Yitzchak's earlier ruling, siman 55. Rabbeinu Yitzchak later wrote in a responsum permitting it even if one were to say, etc., as stated in the Rosh's responsa, klal 108, siman 18. All this follows Rashi's view, as the Rosh states both in his rulings and responsa. This is the meaning of "and all this does not," etc.
Therefore he wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Tam, etc. Tosafot, s.v. "Such as," and in the responsum, etc.; and Hagahot Ashiri, siman 47, s.v. "It appears," etc.
And even if he later returns, etc. Mordechai and Hagahot Ashiri there.
And even if the non-Jew goes, etc. Hagahot Ashiri there. The Rashba wrote, etc., as above in seif 6. Even if he said to him, "I," etc.; and according to Rabbeinu Tam, even if the non-Jew placed the parties in direct relation, as explained above.
Nevertheless, etc. For the lender may say, "You are not my litigant." Although it is known that the collateral belongs to him, he removed himself from it so that he could borrow in a permitted manner and gave it to the non-Jew for that purpose. This is stated there in the responsum.
Therefore it is necessary, etc. Meaning that Mordechai's requirement that the collateral equal or exceed the debt was written merely as an added precaution, so that the lender should have no dealings at all with the original borrower.
