"But he cannot acquire for the non-Jew," etc. Beit Yosef brought in Mordechai's name in Rabeinu Yehudah's language: if the non-Jew told Reuven place collateral in my place and the Jew took his collateral and told Shimon this is the non-Jew's collateral or said I transfer to you in his place and Shimon agreed — permitted to receive interest from the pledging Jew who is Shimon's agent to lend to the non-Jew; and likewise when he transfers collateral; and if Reuven denies, Shimon swears and is exempt — end quote. Beit Yosef on what he said "this is the non-Jew's collateral" — the permission is even though collateral is the Jew's because collateral has guarantor law, for he does not sell unless he first demands payment, etc. I wondered how a holy mouth could say this, for certainly the lender's primary reliance is on the Jew's collateral which truly belongs to the Jew — what does demanding the Jew first help; payment comes from the Jew either way; unlike a Jew guarantor for a non-Jew where if the non-Jew demands first payment does not come from the Jew — unlike here. But it appears to me this is not Mordechai's path; so it states there and in Hagahos Ashiri and Rosh's responsum klal 108 siman 5: a non-Jew told Reuven lend me money on my collateral and Reuven went and borrowed from Shimon on the non-Jew's collateral; or the non-Jew asked him place collateral in my place and he told Shimon this is the non-Jew's collateral or said I transfer to you in his place — permitted for Shimon to take interest from Reuven who is Shimon's agent to lend to the non-Jew; and likewise when he transferred collateral — end quote. Beit Yosef and my teacher z"l understood Mordechai's statement "this is the non-Jew's collateral" refers to what precedes — the non-Jew asked him place collateral in his place — and on this Mordechai gave two divisions; and they labored to find a reason for permission when the Jew left his collateral in his place — heaven forbid to say this is a general permission, for it is not like a guarantor — here is prohibition even if the lender thinks collateral is the non-Jew's he automatically has payment in hand without coercion even through the agent on the non-Jew who will say behold you have collateral needing only warning; and all the more truly it belongs to the Jew and the agent transgresses "before the blind." My teacher z"l also wrote even without collateral permitted, etc. — he was not precise, for without collateral it is worse since lender's primary reliance is on the non-Jew — end quote; it appears complete prohibition. And what Ran wrote even without collateral permitted — means non-Jewish collateral; rather it refers to Mordechai's first statement: non-Jew told Reuven lend on my collateral and Shimon did so — Mordechai says two paths: first borrowed on non-Jew's collateral and says this is the non-Jew's collateral without lying; second says I transfer, etc. — in both permitted; therefore concludes permitted to take from Reuven who is Shimon's agent — meaning the first division; but the second has no relevance; and on the second path concludes "and likewise when he transferred collateral" — this is clear as the sun for they did not examine properly in their haste. And on Mordechai transferring for the non-Jew Beit Yosef wondered — we hold there is no acquisition for a non-Jew in perek HaRibbit (71a); therefore he explained at least prohibition for the agent only permission for the lender — very strained; if so poskim would not be silent and why not mention at least prohibition for the agent lest he do so with a Jew. My teacher z"l wrote possibly to resolve: meaning he transfers in a way the non-Jew acquires — such as through admission he admits it belongs to the non-Jew like the case of Isur the convert, etc. — not clear to me; that admission case where he admitted the money is not his does not apply here at all, for certainly regarding money we say a person is believed about his liability and admission of litigant is like hundred witnesses even though we know he lies, as Shulchan Aruch CM siman 113 seif 2; but this certainly does not help to remove prohibition through his lie — consider if a Jew borrows from his fellow, gives ribbit, and says I admit this money belongs to a non-Jew — can permission come through this famous lie. I again saw in Maimoniyos Mishpatim siman 31 regarding selling a non-Jew's debt — admission does not help in the name of Baal HaItur; the initial challenge does not exist per my view here — not that the non-Jew acquired this collateral and the lender acquired from the non-Jew; certainly no acquisition for a non-Jew; rather here the pledgor gives the lender a path of sale for this collateral saying I transfer this thing to be yours on this right that if so-and-so non-Jew gives you principal and fixed interest you return the collateral — this is exactly as one sells to his fellow for a non-Jew and stipulates conditions; likewise here; and there is no loan on the collateral — the pledgor is the lender's borrower and collateral is security. So it appears to me.
