Permitted. Even if the lender knew the collateral was Jewish, since it was transferred to and acquired by the non-Jew through meshichah. Shach.
To acquire for him. If the borrowing non-Jew instructs the Jewish owner to give the collateral to another non-Jew who will borrow upon it, and the non-Jew becomes liable to return it once delivered, the arrangement is permitted. Mordechai permits where the Jew declares the collateral to belong to the non-Jew or transfers it in his place. Beit Yosef treats the collateral like a guarantor; Taz challenges this because the lender relies primarily upon the collateral. Nekudot HaKesef defends the ruling through the owner's binding admission.

A violent person. Taz distinguishes between a non-Jew who became violent only after the loan and one known as violent from the outset. Interest may be collected only until the moment his status deteriorated. Levush permits further interest on the ground that he is violent toward the owner but not toward the lender; Taz rejects this.
And interest. Hagahot Ashri writes that even if the Jew delayed redeeming the collateral, he must pay all accrued interest, because until the lender receives payment the collateral is treated as sold to him.
