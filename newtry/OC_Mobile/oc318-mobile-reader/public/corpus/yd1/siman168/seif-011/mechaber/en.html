If a Jew lent his collateral to a non-Jew to borrow against it with interest for himself, and the non-Jew pledged it — it is permitted for a Jew to lend to the non-Jew against it with interest. And even if the non-Jew went and paid, the Jew the owner of the collateral may pay principal and interest — it is permitted. {Rama: But he cannot enable a non-Jew to acquire collateral through another, according to the one who says there is no acquisition for a non-Jew (Beit Yosef in the name of Bach). And if the non-Jew is constrained and does not wish to redeem the collateral and return it to its owner, the lending Jew must give to redeem the collateral to its owner, for the non-Jew is like a robber who pledged it for him (Mordechai perek Avodah Zarah). But if he is not constrained and the owner of the collateral can compel the non-Jew to redeem (his), the lending Jew can tell him, you are not my counterpart (Beit Yosef deduces from Mordechai). And if the non-Jew is constrained — had he come to redeem it they would have had to give him without interest — he must also give the owner of the collateral without interest (there). A non-Jew who pledged collateral with this Jew, and then sold it to another Jew — when he comes to redeem it, he must give the lender principal and interest (Mordechai and Hagahos Ashiri and Hagahos Maimoniyos perek 6 of Hilchos Malveh and Agudah and Beit Yosef siman 172, who wrote this in the name of Mahari Katz).}
