(There s.k. 45) But he is not called wicked. And in my humble opinion one can say simply: above he is called wicked since the law would be that the lender can say I do not believe you and he would have to pay; and since the truth is the collateral is his, he transgresses "do not charge interest." Unlike here, he did nothing — behold in truth he will not pay, and he is only in the category of "the remnant of Israel shall not do," etc. However, I wonder why he is not completely wicked, for he is misappropriating his fellow's property — he only gave it to lend to the non-Jew, and in what he held for himself he is misappropriating — puzzling.
