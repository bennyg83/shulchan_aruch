Knows. Siftei Kohen wrote: The same applies if he says "I do not know" — he must swear a heses oath, as the Mechaber wrote below siman 177 seif 1; and likewise Bach and Semag in CM siman 82 s.k. 34, unlike those who disagree in the Hagahah siman 177 there. And Taz wrote: Nevertheless one must understand why he is truly obligated to swear in these claims more than in the claim "I took it permissibly," for since if he admitted he took from him he would still have a claim to exempt himself that he took permissibly — and we find in CM siman 93 seif 9 that we administer a heses oath only on a claim where if he admitted he would be liable for money, etc. And it appears that it does not suffice to say "I took it permissibly" without specification, but he must specify the permission — how it was — lest he not know how to make a permit. Therefore if he explicitly says it was done permissibly as we can investigate him, if it is a matter where there is no concern it did not occur to him that he would need a permit — therefore if he says "I do not know" or is silent, there is this concern, and therefore he swears he does not know or is silent — end quote.

Collateral. Siftei Kohen wrote: It implies even if he claims "you lent me one hundred with fixed ribbit on collateral" and the other responds "I acted permissibly with you" — he is exempt without oath; and all the more if he claims "I left collateral with you for interest" and the other claims it was done permissibly from the outset. And that which was written above seif 18, that when one claims "I pledged collateral with you at fixed ribbit" and the other responds "you told me it belongs to the non-Jew" and he denies it — the lender swears, etc. — one can distinguish: here it is permitted because we do not abandon a permitted path and eat forbidden matter, and we say he certainly acted permissibly; but there he did nothing permissibly, only says he relied on you that you said it belongs to the non-Jew — if so the essence of the permit is from the borrower's word, and the borrower denies him, therefore he must swear. And if the lender comes to extract payment, the borrower swears and is exempt — this is puzzling, for here it rules the borrower swears even though he makes himself and the lender wicked; and below siman 177 seif 2 it wrote that even though if he wishes he exempts himself by saying "I paid" or "I do not know" and would swear and be exempt — he is not believed even with an oath, for a person does not make himself wicked; and the matters contradict each other — puzzling — end quote.
The lender. And one can say regarding above siman 162, that there one cannot say "he did not abandon a permitted path," etc., for here there is certainly prohibition; but there it did not enter his mind that wheat would become expensive so he would be careful to have wheat at the time of the loan. And Semag wrote in CM siman 82 s.k. 33: specifically here where he admits there is ribbit but says it was done permissibly — he swears and takes by promissory note as the law of anyone who swears on a document and takes. But if the lender stands on his document and says "I do not know" that there is any ribbit at all — he need not swear before giving money by promissory note, but pays and afterward sues him and administers a heses oath. See in responsum Maharashdam siman 67 on laws relevant here — end quote Siftei Kohen. And Taz wrote that Rama decided thus from his own view that on a document one swears and takes — end quote (and Nachalat Yaakov challenged him — see there).
