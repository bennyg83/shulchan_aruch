A non-Jew. Where the arrangement follows a recognized heter, the lender is believed without an oath because we presume he did not abandon a permitted route for a prohibition. Shach.
Knows. Shach writes that if he says, 'I do not know,' he must take a hesset oath. Taz explains that a bare claim, 'I took it permissibly,' is insufficient; he must describe the heter so beit din can examine it.
Permissibly. Taz likewise requires the lender to specify the heter. Given widespread laxity concerning interest, judges should investigate carefully and administer an oath where the explanation is unclear.
Collateral. If the borrower claims fixed interest on collateral and the lender responds that the transaction was permitted, the lender is exempt without an oath because we presume he chose a permitted route. Shach questions the consistency with siman 177, where a person is not believed through an oath to portray himself as wicked.
The lender. This differs from the end of siman 162, because here there is certain appearance of prohibition and the lender claims a specific heter; there the parties may simply not have anticipated a wheat price increase. If the lender admits interest appears in the document but claims a heter, he swears and collects. If he denies any interest term, payment is made first and the borrower later sues with a hesset oath. Shach.
