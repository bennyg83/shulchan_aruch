And Bach wrote that there is no dispute here; specifically when the non-Jew already redeemed the collaterals and did not give him the interest as the agent stipulated there — then one may not extract from the agent's hand, since he did not explicitly become guarantor for the interest. But when the non-Jew does not redeem except from the agent, and the agent redeems from the lender and says the non-Jew is silent and did not give the interest as he stipulated — the lender is not obligated to return the collateral unless he gives him what he stipulated with him, for he can say I have no dealings with the non-Jew, only with you. And specifically by default; but if the Jew explicitly said you shall be my agent, he must give the collateral without interest, only that he should not suffer loss in principal — end quote. And it is clear from his words that wherever he explicitly stipulated about the interest, obviously he must pay him, for the body of the loan was on the collateral. However, when he mentioned the non-Jew's name and recognized that he is silent, he should not take interest from him even from the collateral, for he knew and waived, and must return the collateral without interest — end quote Siftei Kohen. And Taz wrote: It seems to me, if the agent did not know this non-Jew is coerced and it became known to him afterward, he has no obligation, and the sender must give him the collateral without interest — why should he lose since he did not sin in anything. But if the agent knew the non-Jew is coerced, he is liable under laws of causative damage, for he should have informed the lender that this non-Jew is coerced. And Levush wrote: It seems to me specifically in such a case where the agent brought the collateral with him at the time of the loan; otherwise, if the non-Jew only sent him to bring the money and afterward would give him the collateral — then responsibility for the collateral was never on the agent, for he was only the Jew's agent; the agent can say do not sell it, for it will harm me, that the non-Jew is coerced. And if he sold it and the agent came to harm thereby, the lender must compensate him. Taz disagrees and wrote that whenever the agent knew the non-Jew is coerced and did not inform the lender, he cannot extract the collateral from his hand without interest, for he caused his own loss — end quote. And he further wrote that Rosh explicitly discusses where he sent him to bring collateral from the non-Jew on the lender's responsibility, and nevertheless ruled thus (and Nachalat Yaakov agreed with Levush — see there).
For the need of. That is, he should say he borrows for the non-Jew's need and not say he will pay principal and interest. And even though it is permitted for a Jew to be guarantor for a non-Jew where there is collateral, as below siman 170 seif 2 — that case differs, for the loan's beginning was to the non-Jew. However, I have seen practiced in many places that the Jew says at the time of the loan that he will pay principal and interest, and it appears they hold that specifically where the collateral is not good to lend on it the full loan amount such that it equals principal and interest that will accrue even over a long time — then it appears the lender's reliance is on the Jew, and in such a case it is forbidden to say so. And in such a case I have truly seen practiced that the Jew gives the lender a promissory note for permission as security. But where the collateral is good, the Jew's words in this neither raise nor lower, since he lends for the non-Jew's need there is no concern for this. Nevertheless one should be careful in the matter — end quote Siftei Kohen.
