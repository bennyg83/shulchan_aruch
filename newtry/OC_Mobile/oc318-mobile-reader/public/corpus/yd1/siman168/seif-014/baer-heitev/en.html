He shall repay. If the lender merely suspects that the agent received more from the non-Jew, he may impose a ban because the claim is uncertain. Beit Yosef and Bach.
And some say. Bach explains there is no real dispute. If the non-Jew already redeemed the collateral but failed to pay the agreed interest, the agent cannot be forced because he never expressly guaranteed it. If the agent must redeem from the lender and says the non-Jew is violent and paid no interest, the lender need not return the collateral without the agreed amount unless the agent was explicitly appointed only as agent. If the agent knew the non-Jew was violent and failed to disclose it, Taz treats him as causatively liable; Levush and Nekudot HaKesef distinguish cases where responsibility never rested upon the agent.
For the need of. He should say he borrows for the non-Jew's need and should not personally undertake principal and interest. Where the collateral fully secures principal and long-term interest, common practice is more lenient, but Shach advises caution.
