A Jew who borrowed from his fellow for the non-Jew's need and fixed with the non-Jew interest at a higher rate, and with the lending Jew fixed at a lower rate — the borrower profits what he fixed with the non-Jew more than what he fixed with the lending Jew. But if he fixed with the lender at a higher rate and the non-Jew is silent and unwilling to pay them, the borrower should not pay from his own pocket (Mordechai and Hagahos Ashiri). {Rama: And some say the borrower must pay from his pocket unless he mentioned the non-Jew's name to him initially — then he is exempt from paying from his pocket (Tur and Mordechai and Hagahos Ashiri in the name of Avi HaEzri). And in any case, lekhatchilah it is forbidden to borrow on a non-Jew's collateral and say he will pay principal and interest, for it is as borrowing from a Jew and relending to a non-Jew; rather the loan's beginning must be for the non-Jew's need. If the agent comes to redeem the collateral from his own money, it is permitted to accept principal and interest from him (Beit Yosef in the name of a responsum of Rashba). However, specifically when the lender gives it to him to redeem willingly; but if the agent redeems from his own funds it is deception, for from the outset he lent him on condition the collateral remain in his hand until the non-Jew redeems it (Hagahos Ashiri and Beit Yosef in the name of a responsum of Rosh). And if the agent does not wish to redeem it and the lender wishes them to redeem it, he cannot compel him, as will be explained at the end of this siman (Beit Yosef).}
