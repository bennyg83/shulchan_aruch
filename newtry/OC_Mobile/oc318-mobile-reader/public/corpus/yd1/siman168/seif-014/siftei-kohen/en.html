"Rather the loan's beginning must be for the non-Jew's need." Meaning he should say he borrows for the non-Jew's need; for even though it is known he borrows for the non-Jew's need, he should not say he will pay principal and interest; and likewise is explicit in Rashba's responsum: even if he borrowed it for the non-Jew's need, when he told Shimon he will pay principal and interest — it turns out Shimon borrows and Reuven lends; and even though the collateral belongs to the non-Jew, Reuven claims from Shimon and Shimon is liable to him; and it is close in my eyes that one who says in such language commits fixed ribbit even if the collateral belonged to the non-Jew — for this is as Shimon borrows from Reuven with interest and lends to the non-Jew — end quote; and he brings Darkei Moshe. And even though it is permitted for a Jew to be even a guarantor-contractor for the non-Jew where there is collateral, as below siman 170 seif 2 in the Hagahah — that case differs, for the loan's beginning was to the non-Jew. However I have seen practiced in many places that the Jew says at loan time he will pay principal and interest; and it appears they hold specifically where the collateral is not good to lend the full loan amount equaling principal and interest even over a long time — then it appears the lender relies on the Jew, and in such a case it is forbidden to say so. And in such a case I have seen practiced that the Jew gives the lender a promissory note for permission as security. But where collateral is good, the Jew's words neither raise nor lower; and since he lends for the non-Jew's need there is no concern; nevertheless one should be careful.
