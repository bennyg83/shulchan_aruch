The payment to the non-Jew is made at the lender's direction and resembles a lender telling the borrower to pay interest to a third party. This is fixed interest even if the lender owes the non-Jew nothing.
Without. Since the first Jew bore responsibility for the money when he transferred it to his fellow, it is as though he lent his own money. Tur.
Forbidden. Since the second Jew receives the money from the first Jew, it appears that the first is paying interest to the non-Jew through him. The central principle is that a non-Jew has no halachic agency, either for a Jew or vice versa. Taz. Shach implies this is not fixed interest. It applies where the money left as a loan, but a true deposit is permitted.
