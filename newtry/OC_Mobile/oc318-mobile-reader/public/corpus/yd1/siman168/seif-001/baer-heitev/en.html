The reason is that he gives interest to the non-Jew through his agency, and it is analogous to saying to his fellow "I will lend you on condition that you give the interest to the non-Jew" — which is obviously forbidden; and even if the lender owes the non-Jew nothing at all, since he gives the interest to the non-Jew on account of the loan at the lender's direction, it is fixed ribbit.

Forbidden. Since he received it from a Jew, it appears as though he is giving ribit to the non-Jew as that Jew's agent. The main principle here is that there is no agency for a non-Jew, whether from a non-Jew to a Jew or the reverse. Thus the Taz. The Shach writes that it implies that in such a case it is not fixed ribit, and that this is specifically where it left his hand as a loan, but if it was by way of deposit it is permitted, as below seif 3 in the gloss.
