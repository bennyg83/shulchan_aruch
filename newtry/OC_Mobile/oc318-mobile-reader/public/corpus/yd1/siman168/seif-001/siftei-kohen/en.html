Behold it is permitted. Not only if he lent one hundred for one hundred fifty; but even if he stipulated with him to give him such-and-such interest per month — Beit Yosef in the name of Beit HaTalmud; and Darkei Moshe brought it.
Forbidden. It is similar to one who says to his fellow "I will lend you on condition that you give the interest to the non-Jew," which is obviously forbidden — not only if the lender owes money to the non-Jew and the borrower gives it to him on his behalf as here, but even if the lender owes nothing to the non-Jew at all — since in payment for the loan he gives the non-Jew interest at the lender's instruction it is fixed interest — Tur.
Without interest. Since at the time he gave the money to his fellow the full responsibility for the money was on him — it is as if he lent him his money — there.
Forbidden. Since he received it from a Jew's hand it appears as giving interest to the non-Jew as his agent — Tur. And it implies in such a case it is not fixed interest; and likewise Derishah and Bach; and specifically where it went from his hand by way of loan; but by way of deposit — permitted, as below seif 3 in the Hagahah.
