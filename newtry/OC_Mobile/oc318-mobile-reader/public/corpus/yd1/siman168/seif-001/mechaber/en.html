Simanim 168–169: several laws of interest done through a non-Jew; it contains 27 se’ifim. A Jew who borrowed money from a non-Jew with interest, and sought to repay it, and his fellow said to him, “Give them to me, and I will pay him in the same manner that you pay him,” it is forbidden. And even if the Jew wrote 2 promissory notes in his name for the non-Jew, and gave collateral, and also gave the interest to the non-Jew, it is forbidden. (And all that he gave to the non-Jew, we extract from the first Jew, for it is fixed interest) (Beit Yosef in the name of a responsum of Rosh, klal 108; and so is implied in the Tur and poskim). And the same law applies if the Jew owed money without interest — it has this law. And even if they presented him before the non-Jew, and the non-Jew said to him, “Give them to this Jew and be exempt, and I will stipulate with him that he will give me principal and interest,” it is forbidden.
