A non-Jew who pledged, etc. Specifically regarding a non-Jew who borrowed from a Jew on collateral we say in seif 9 it is permitted because by default he transferred it; but here certainly he did not transfer it, for he took it against his will; and if the Jew redeems it, it is as if he sent his agent the non-Jew to borrow with ribbis ; forbidden according to Rashi above; therefore if the pledged party did not know the pledger would pledge it with a Jew and agency does not apply at all ; it is permitted even according to Rashi.
Debt with interest and he transferred it, etc. This is simple: if he already owed the non-Jew principal and interest he can accept on himself and take from the Jew that interest too; except there in Rashba's responsum it discusses where the Jew owed the non-Jew principal and interest had not yet accrued at the hour the non-Jew transferred to another Jew; and know that if he transferred in a complete sale to another Jew obviously complete prohibition (only) even to take from the non-Jew; rather it discusses transfer as security, and when the non-Jew wishes he can remove the Jew with money; if so it is only like collateral and we should have permitted taking interest even from the Jew since the money is essentially the non-Jew's on this ; he wrote forbidden because of ribbis stringency and concluded with language "and similar" implying; but in truth it is not so; and thereby we understand this Hagahah ; not as it appears discussing complete acquisition which is forbidden even from the non-Jew since from that hour it is Jew's money and it is as borrowing from his fellow on condition the non-Jew gives interest ; certainly forbidden.
