Taz wrote: This is simple — if the non-Jew already owes principal and interest, the Jew can take it upon himself and take from that Jew both principal and interest; but the interest does not apply at the time the non-Jew transfers to another Jew, and one should know that if he bought it in an absolute sale to the Jew he would not even have taken from a non-Jew, but rather he gave it to him as a trust. Therefore it must be prohibited (and in Nekudot HaKesef he wrote that the non-Jew transferred to the Jew in a full acquisition, and even so there is no ribbit prohibition here, for it discusses where the second Jew cannot sue the first in their laws and not in our laws, and moreover the non-Jew cannot return to the first Jew — examine this carefully).
