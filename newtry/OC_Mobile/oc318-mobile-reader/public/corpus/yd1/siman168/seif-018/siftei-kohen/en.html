To return his collateral to him — permitted. And if afterward the first one says to the second “it is not true, it is mine — return it to me for free” — the second swears that this is what the first told him, and he never returns the collateral — Tur from Rosh's responsum klal 108; and Beit Yosef wrote specifically here where he contradicts him by saying “I did not tell you it belongs to the non-Jew” — he must swear; unlike above seif 13 s.k. 38; and see above s.k. 76.
And so the custom is widespread. That is to say: so the widespread custom is like the lenient view, that even with his collateral in his possession by default — it is permitted; he returns his collateral and takes interest from this one. But if he is obligated to return it — there is no custom here, only that they customarily return it when he redeems it; and possibly if he wished not to return it the authority is in his hand per law. And the Rabbi proceeds to explain that possibly he is obligated to return it since the custom is so; and so Darkei Moshe implies — as I explained, see there.
And in credit obligations. Meaning a promissory note he has from a non-Jew — Perishah; and the same if he owes him verbally or by any other obligation.
You shall release the non-Jew etc. Specifically in such a case where he forgives the non-Jew’s debt to the non-Jew and the second Jew extracts from him what he can — this helps; but there is no other remedy to transfer a non-Jew’s credit debt; so the poskim say; meaning even kinyan agav and other acquisitions do not help; as stated in the preceding s.k. in the name of Maharam and Mahariik; and see in responsum Maharam Galanti siman 88.
