A non-Jew's collateral is in a Jew's hand, and his fellow Jew brought him to lend against it, and when the non-Jew comes to redeem it he will take the interest that accrued on it until that day, and the second will take the interest that accrues from that day onward — it is permitted, provided he says to him: I hereby sell you all power, right, and lien that I have on this collateral, and I have no dealings with you and you with me. And if after a time the second wished to return and take principal and interest from the first to return his collateral to him — it is permitted. {Rama: And some say even if he does not sell it to him and did not say to him I withdraw from you, but merely returns and pledges it with him without specification it is permitted, for certainly he intends permissibility and it is as one who said "it is worth [such and such]" (Beit Yosef in the name of Mordechai and responsum of Rosh). And if the first comes to redeem the collateral and the second does not wish to return the collateral to him, the authority is in his hand (Mordechai perek Avodah Zarah), for it is entirely his as though he sold it to him explicitly until the non-Jew comes to redeem it (there and in responsum of Rosh). And thus is the widespread practice. And if they stipulated from the outset that he must return and give to him when he comes to redeem it, everything is according to their stipulation. And it is possible that even without specification it is as though they stipulated thus since the practice is so (the Rabbi's reasoning). And it is permitted for the borrower to fix with the second Jew at less even though the non-Jew gives him more. Or that he can say, I will take a share in the interest that comes from the non-Jew each week (Beit Yosef and so is implied in Terumat HaDeshen siman 303). Also if the borrower and lender wish that the second lender not take the collateral into his hand but leave it in the hand of this borrower who borrowed against it for the non-Jew — it is permitted (Beit Yosef and the Rabbi's reasoning), provided the collateral from now on be on the second lender's responsibility (in my opinion). And if they stipulated with one another that the first lender to the non-Jew — who is now the borrower from the second Jew — will be liable on his responsibility, the authority is in their hands, for a stipulation that he guard unpaid is to be like a borrower (also this is in my opinion and Shlomo Luria responsum 52). And some say even to transfer these collaterals to the second lender he need not do anything but from when the second lender gives him the loan money the collateral is acquired for him wherever it is, for by Torah law money acquires even though rabbinically it requires physical transfer — nevertheless in such a case one may rely on Torah law. However it is good that he transfer to him through another (Beit Yosef). And the borrower is believed to say he has collateral from a non-Jew and need not bring proof, and the lender may lend to him unless he is not established in this — then certainly he lies. Such are the main matters in these laws (in my opinion from above siman 162 seif 2 in hagahah). Even though there are some who question these leniencies (Maharik shoresh 119), the practice has spread to be lenient and one should not protest those who act as mentioned, for it is law. And all this discusses only when the borrowing Jew did not fix principal together with interest on the non-Jew; but if the Jew already fixed principal and interest together on the non-Jew, such as he calculated with him interest that would accrue until a year and calculated it all together — from now all is as though it is the Jew's principal (Terumat HaDeshen siman 303), whether he borrowed against the collateral or without collateral — it is forbidden to borrow against this from his fellow Jew and have interest accrue on the debt as long as the loan stands, for it is as though he gives him interest from his pocket (the Rabbi's reasoning). However he may sell it to him without specification and when he wishes to return let him take his debt he returns and acquires it from him a bit more than he sold it to him according to the value of interest that was fit to accrue on it. And it is forbidden to stipulate this from the outset; rather he should acquire the debt with a complete acquisition and afterward the buyer returns and assures him with true words that when he has money he will sell it back to him in the manner mentioned (Terumat HaDeshen siman 303).} And for credit debts he must say to him: you will discharge the non-Jew thus and thus and in such and such [ways], and in such and such [amount] I buy from you everything I can extract from the non-Jew's hand. (And see below siman 173 on the law of selling debts.)
