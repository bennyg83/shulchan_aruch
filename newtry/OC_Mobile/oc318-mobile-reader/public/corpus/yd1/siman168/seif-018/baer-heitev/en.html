Sells. Levush permits a Jew to repledge a non-Jew's collateral to another Jew for more than the first loan, provided the interest charged does not exceed what the non-Jew pays, treating the transaction as a sale of the collateral and an interest-free excess loan. Taz rejects this as fixed interest; Nekudot HaKesef supports Levush and records the custom as lenient.
To return. If the first Jew later denies having said the collateral belonged to a non-Jew, the second swears and need never return it without payment. Tur from a responsum of the Rosh.
The custom. Common practice permits a lender who received non-Jewish collateral without special stipulation to return it to the first Jew when redeemed and collect the interest. Shach understands this as voluntary resale, not a legal obligation.
They stipulated. Taz and Shach question Rama where the second lender is obligated by express stipulation or custom to return the collateral, because then the transaction is not a sale. Taz rules stringently. Shach defends the custom either as voluntary resale or by relying on the Rosh's ruling that non-Jewish collateral may be repledged at the same interest so long as the non-Jew did not say, 'Acquire it from now if I do not redeem it.'
They wish. Shach requires an explicit complete sale wherever the collateral remains physically with the borrower; otherwise no sale is implied. If the collateral remains with the non-Jew, it must likewise be formally transferred to the second Jew wherever located.
Credit. Meaning a written debt owed by a non-Jew, or an oral debt or other obligation. Shach.
Acquires. A responsum of Maimonides explains that the first Jew should direct the non-Jewish debtor to pay the second Jew and be discharged from the first. Shach permits even without approaching him, if the first immediately releases the non-Jew and the second must collect whatever he can.
