(Likkut) And if they stipulated, etc., for he stipulates, etc. These words are taken from Sefer HaTerumah and are cited in the Rama, siman 177, seif 1, based upon the Yerushalmi. The ruling is incorrect. If it were correct, why did the Gemara state on 73a that when the goods are under the buyer's responsibility it is forbidden? The same difficulty arises from the case of the donkey drivers, as Tosafot explain there, from Rav Chama's deferred sale, and from Rav's acceptance of the risks of the road. This is a grave error. The Yerushalmi permitted the arrangement only where the recipient derives no benefit whatsoever and acts purely as the owner's agent. Whenever the recipient derives any benefit, it is full interest. See what is written in the Rama, siman 177, seif 1. This is also the meaning of siman 167: the lender, etc., and they shall be under the responsibility, etc. Although the lender presently derives no benefit and appears merely to be an agent, as in the Yerushalmi's case of the package, because he acts for the sake of the later benefit, he is treated as a borrower. He must also be paid for his labor. Likewise, in an iska, regarding the half entrusted to the managing partner, the investor must accept the risk and pay wages for the manager's labor.
