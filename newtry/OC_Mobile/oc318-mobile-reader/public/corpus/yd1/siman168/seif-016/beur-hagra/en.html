It follows the explanation above in seif 13 and Sefer HaTerumah's interpretation of the Tosefta: "under his responsibility" means that the money has the legal status of a loan in his possession. If he is merely a custodian, he is an agent without responsibility. See below, seif 21, and the Taz and Shach in the name of Sefer HaTerumah.
(Likkut) And if there is no, etc., but only, etc. Sefer HaTerumah, seif 7, explains that the rule, "Whenever it is under his responsibility," applies specifically when the money became a loan in his possession. Otherwise, why should he be worse than a guarantor? Whenever the guarantor accepted Jewish law, it is permitted because the lender must first demand payment from the borrower, and the guarantor does not immediately become the borrower. All the more so here, where he is liable only if the money is lost through unavoidable circumstances. This is why the baraita in Bava Metzia 71b states, "The lender," etc., and describes specifically a loan. It did not state the greater novelty of a deposit accepted under responsibility, because only a loan is included. See there.
