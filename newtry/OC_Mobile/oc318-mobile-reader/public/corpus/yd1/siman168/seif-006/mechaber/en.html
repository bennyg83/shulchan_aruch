A non-Jew who borrowed money from a Jew with interest, and sought to repay it to him, and a Jew found him and said to him, “Give them to me, and I will pay you in the same manner that you pay him,” it is permitted. And even if he said to him, “I will pay him in the same manner that you pay him,” it is permitted, for he is giving them to him for the non-Jew’s sake. But if he presented him before a Jew, even though the non-Jew gave the money into his hand, since he gave it with the Jew’s consent, it is forbidden. {Rama: And some are lenient even if he presented him before him (Tur in the name of Rabbeinu Tam and Rosh), but the primary ruling is like the first reasoning (Beit Yosef in the name of Nimukei Yosef, who so wrote in the name of Ran). But in a place where they are accustomed to be lenient, one should not protest them (a responsum of Rosh and Hagahos Maimoniyos).}
