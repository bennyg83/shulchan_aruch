Since he gave with the Jew’s consent. Behold the non-Jew is the Jew’s agent for stringency, and it is forbidden rabbinically — this is the consensus of most poskim; unlike the Rambam who wrote it is fixed interest.
And some are lenient. Since there is no agency for a non-Jew at all, therefore since he received the money from the non-Jew's hand — it is permitted. And Tur wrote that even per Rabbenu Tam, if the second Jew says he receives it in the first's agency — forbidden, for he is the Jew's agent. And Bach wrote this is specifically where the first Jew appointed him agent — for otherwise one cannot say he is his agent without his knowledge; unlike Beit Yosef — end. And what Beit Yosef brought as proof from the students of Rashba who wrote: it is found in which by law it is forbidden and not merely by stringency — where the lending Jew said to the non-Jew "give them to this second Jew on my behalf" and the non-Jew said to the second Jew "acquire them" — not on account of the non-Jew's agency but because since the non-Jew wished to transfer the money to him, behold he departs from them and behold he is as one acquiring from ownerless property — end — this is no proof, for there the borrower did not say he receives them in the first's agency; rather there the reason is that since the non-Jew said "acquire them," behold he departs from them and he acquires from ownerless property — it seems to me.
