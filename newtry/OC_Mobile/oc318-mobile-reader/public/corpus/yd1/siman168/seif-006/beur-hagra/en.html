And even if he said to him, etc. The Rosh there, since they prohibited only where the non-Jew placed the second Jew in direct relation with the first, as explained above. The same appears in Tosafot there, at the end of s.v. "They found him."
And if he placed him in direct relation, etc. The Gemara there.
Even though he gave, etc. There the Gemara states, "Granted, the latter clause," etc. See Tosafot there, s.v. "Such as," and Rashi there, s.v. "with that understanding." The poskim accepted Rashi's view. This is the meaning of "and some," etc., "but," etc. Rabbeinu Tam's proof is from the Tosefta and Yerushalmi cited by Tosafot there: if a Jew appointed a non-Jew as administrator, etc.; money belonging to a Jew that is deposited, etc. The latter case is permitted because responsibility rests upon the non-Jew, unlike an administrator or steward, as the Tosefta states, "This is the rule," etc. Likewise here, even though the non-Jew placed the second Jew in direct relation with the first, so long as he did not tell the first Jew, "You are discharged," the money remains under the non-Jew's responsibility and should be permitted. The other authorities rejected this proof, because placing the parties in direct relation is different. The deposited money discussed there was accepted under responsibility specifically so that the non-Jew would borrow it as a borrower, as explained in Sefer HaTerumah. See the Shach, seif katan 68.
