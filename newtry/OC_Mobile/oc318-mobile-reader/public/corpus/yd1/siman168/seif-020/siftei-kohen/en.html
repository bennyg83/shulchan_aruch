Permitted to lend etc. For what difference does it make that the collateral does not belong to the non-Jew — is it forbidden to lend to a non-Jew on a Jew's collateral? And even if the non-Jew lent to the Jew with interest — it is permitted for a Jew to lend to the non-Jew on that collateral with interest and to receive from the Jew the interest the Jew owes the non-Jew; for the second Jew did not lend to the first anything but to the non-Jew, and it is in his place that he receives the interest from the Jew's hand — Tur and the Rosh.
And according to the first view, etc. This is not comparable to above in seif 10, where if a Jew redeems it it is forbidden; there, as soon as the Jew gives him the collateral and borrows on it, it appears that he borrows for the Jew's need. Alternatively, there, since he took it as collateral against his will, he certainly did not transfer ownership to him; but here one can say he transferred ownership to him. So wrote Beit Yosef regarding the law of seif 10. With this it is also settled that there is no difficulty from above in seif 9, where it is forbidden when the lender knows the collateral belongs to a Jew, for there he borrows for the Jew's need. So wrote the later authorities.
