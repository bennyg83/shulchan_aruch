It is permitted to lend, etc. What difference does it make that the collateral does not belong to the non-Jew? Is it forbidden to lend money to a non-Jew upon collateral belonging to a Jew? Even if the non-Jew had lent money to the first Jew with interest, it is permitted for a second Jew to lend money with interest to the non-Jew upon that same collateral and to receive from the first Jew the interest that he owes the non-Jew. The second Jew did not lend anything to the first Jew, but only to the non-Jew, and he merely stands in the non-Jew's place to receive the interest directly from the Jew. Tur and Rosh.
And according to the first view, etc. This is not comparable to the law above in seif 10, where if the Jew redeems the collateral it is forbidden. There, from the moment the Jew gives the collateral to the non-Jew and the non-Jew borrows upon it, it appears that he is borrowing for the Jew's benefit. Alternatively, there, since the non-Jew took the collateral from him against his will, the Jew certainly did not transfer ownership of it to him. Here, however, one can say that he did transfer ownership to him. So wrote the Beit Yosef regarding the law of seif 10. This also explains why there is no difficulty from seif 9 above, where it is forbidden when the lender knows that the collateral belongs to a Jew. There the non-Jew is borrowing for the Jew's benefit. The later authorities likewise write this.
