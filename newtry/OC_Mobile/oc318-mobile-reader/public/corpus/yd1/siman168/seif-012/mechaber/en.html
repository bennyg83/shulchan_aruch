A Jew who lent to a non-Jew with interest on collateral, and afterward said to him, give the collateral to Reuven and he will give you your money — and he did so: he gave the collateral to Reuven to sell, and Reuven withheld the money in his hand after selling the collateral, and the lender demands interest until Reuven brings his money to his hand, and the non-Jew is unwilling to give more than up to the time of sale in a case where Reuven must give the surplus — the lender may take all the interest from the non-Jew. And what difference does it make to him if the non-Jew will compel Reuven to pay the interest because he withheld?
