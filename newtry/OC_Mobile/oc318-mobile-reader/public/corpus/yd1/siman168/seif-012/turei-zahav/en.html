And if the non-Jew compels Reuven, etc. The Beit Yosef cites this from a responsum of the Rosh, whose reason is that since the non-Jew was present when the money was lent, the lender has no dealings with Reuven at all. It appears that this is so even if the collateral was not worth the interest that accrued after the sale. This is unlike the ruling in siman 170, where the Rama writes, “but not the interest that will accrue upon it from this point onward.” There, the Jew pays the lender the interest on behalf of the non-Jew, whereas here the non-Jew pays it. This astonishes me, for it contradicts the Rama’s ruling in seif 4, where the Jew compels the non-Jew to give him interest and knows that the non-Jew will compel the Jew, etc. Indeed, the present case is the stronger basis for prohibition. Here, at least in the end, the Jew has dealings with the lender, for the lender takes the collateral, sells it, and retains the money, and any payment for delaying those funds is compensation for waiting. With a Jew this would constitute fixed ribbis, yet it is permitted because the arrangement began permissibly, and we do not say that it appears like ribbis. All the more so above, where even in the end the Jewish lender has no dealings with the Jew who borrowed from the non-Jew, and the arrangement began permissibly, since there is no agency for a non-Jew even to create a stringency, that we should not be concerned that the non-Jew will compel the Jew, and it does not appear like ribbis. It is clear that the Rosh follows his own view, disagreeing with the ruling cited by the Rama in seif 5 that distinguishes between unsecured credit and collateral, as I wrote there. Other poskim also disagree, as I mentioned there. Thus, the Rama’s rulings contradict one another, as do those of the Levush who followed him. In my humble opinion, the primary halacha follows the ruling here, since the ruling above is the opinion of Maharam alone against the many authorities cited there. The prohibition in seif 5 is only because agency for a non-Jew is treated stringently. Therefore, in a place where the custom is to be lenient, as the Rama writes in seif 7, there is no prohibition in this case. So it appears in my humble opinion.
