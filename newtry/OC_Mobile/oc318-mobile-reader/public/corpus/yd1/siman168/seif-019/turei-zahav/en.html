"Lend me money on these collaterals," etc., "and the money is for the Jew's need." Tur did not write this but "lend me money," etc.; and Beit Yosef wrote this needs explanation because it was challenged what prohibition is in the matter. And in Derishah he wrote Tur's words mean: if he lends on collateral one hundred and wants to pledge in second Jew's hand for fifty — collaterals still in his possession and he returns and borrows on them for his need — forbidden; and he was not precise — Tosafot siman 303 explicitly wrote no distinction between selling entire debt or part; likewise collateral — one matter. And in Perishah he wrote prohibition because collateral was acquired by Jew from non-Jew and must sell specifically to second Jew; therefore if he said lend on these non-Jewish collaterals there is no sale. Darkei Moshe wrote custom is lenient even if he said lend me; and likewise Mordechai perek HaRibbit practical law permitted even if he did not say I remove myself and did not say he sold it — as one who said by default intends for permission; and since he said this collateral stands for me from the non-Jew for such amount lend on it thus and from today accept interest accruing — permitted — end quote.
