And he told him "lend me" etc. And Bach wrote "and it has already been explained to be lenient" — end; and likewise Darkei Moshe. And in my humble opinion, specifically when he told him "lend on this non-Jew's collateral thus-and-such, and when the non-Jew comes to redeem it you take such-and-such interest" — we say by default he intended for permission, and he departs from the collateral. But if he said "lend me" — as Rosh wrote in responsum klal 108 siman 28, the reason being: in such language "lend me money with interest on these collaterals which are a non-Jew's" — it is certainly fixed interest, for he fixed interest with him whether the non-Jew gives interest or not; what difference does it make if the collateral belongs to a non-Jew, since the Jew fixed interest for him — end quote. Obviously in such a case the custom was not to be lenient. And although below siman 170 we say it is permitted for a Jew to be guarantor where there is collateral — here where the Jew lends initially for his own need it is forbidden. However as long as the collateral was removed to the Jew — but when it is in the non-Jew's domain — in every manner it is permitted, as stated above s.k. 55.
This is the scheming of the wicked etc. Meaning this scheme of the wicked is to try to make it permitted because the collateral belongs to a non-Jew — but it does not help since the Jew fixed interest for him; unlike Pri Megadim who appears to explain differently.
