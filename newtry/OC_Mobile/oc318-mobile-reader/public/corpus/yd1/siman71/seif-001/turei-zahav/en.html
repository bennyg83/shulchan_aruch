He cuts it in two. Meaning: some are stringent and say that hair blocks the blood in the flesh of the head from exiting through salting; therefore they salt it first on the hair so it will emit a little, and afterward place it on the fire and remove the hair and return and salt the head. And Rashba wrote on this that according to their view, if the first salting did not enter except a little — why did it come? Rather certainly hair does not block at all and the head is like other meat; and one who wishes to be stringent with the head cuts it in two and salts it on the face side and also on the hair. And in Torat HaChatas HaAroch he did not mention cutting the head in two, only wrote briefly; and likewise it is not from law but in the language "if one wishes, one cuts the head in two," etc. And even though Tur and Shulchan Aruch did not write "if one wishes" but wrote as a ruling — nevertheless it is for a mitzvah min hamuvchar, since Beit Yosef wrote the reason one must cut the head in two because of the brain inside; therefore in truth piercing the bone alone also helps, as written afterward here; and therefore the language "and if he wishes" that Rashba wrote is resolved.
