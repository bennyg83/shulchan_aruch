In the thick head. It appears that even though on the side of the thick there is also somewhat thin in width, nevertheless it is forbidden there — for otherwise he would not have said "thick head" but "part of the thick"; rather everything on that side is forbidden.
A perforation through and through — treifah. It appears simple that even if the skin on the spleen was not punctured there — treifah — for that skin is not included in "spleen" plain, but has another name, namely the membrane on the spleen mentioned in siman 64 seif 10. And as they said: if the heart was punctured — treifah — it is obvious they did not mean that the membrane on it must also be punctured; so too here with the spleen — certainly it does not help to permit that the membrane on it remained intact. And further proof from the words of Orach Chaim HaAruch that I brought in siman 46 seif 1 — see there. I was asked: in a certain district of calves there are perforations through and through in the spleen at the thick head, and around the hole from inside the spleen skin surrounds it and it is recognizable that it was created so; and each time the hole is found in one place, and some people say that thus is the way of most calves there. I answered to permit — for needless to say according to those poskim who hold that deficiency from the body of the lung is kosher, as in siman 36 seif 8 — namely when there is deficiency and crack in the lung and the skin and flesh remain — so too here it is kosher; for there it is forbidden with a hole even if not through and through, yet we say since the skin beneath remains there is no hole on it — so too here with through and through: thus I understand the lung without through and through like the spleen with through and through. And just as in the lung it is not called a hole as long as the membrane remains — so too in the spleen with through and through. And further: there too we find through and through yet kosher, e.g. when there is deficiency at the bottom of the lung and the skin is whole around it — we say there it is kosher, only that in some places they practice to declare treifah if it is like a bent palm; and in many places they permit in every case, as written there in the name of Maharshal. And even for Rambam who forbids the lung, and they wrote in Shulchan Aruch there in the name of "some forbid" — the prohibition is not on account of a hole but on account of deficiency, as they mentioned there in the language of deficiency. And so is proven further from what Rambam wrote that Shulchan Aruch copied siman 50 seif 2: "Every organ we said — if punctured in any amount treifah; so too if all of it was removed treifah, whether removed through illness or created deficient," etc. — behold he did not mention this "whether created deficient" except regarding removal, and they did not mention it regarding puncture that he opened with it; rather necessarily there is no relevance of "hole" if created so, but there is deficiency upon it — therefore Rambam forbade the lung, for a lung that is deficient is treifah; and this too is included in "removed." Not so the spleen — if it is deficient it is kosher, as explained here: what difference to me if all of it is deficient or part of it. If so, this round hole created so is in the category of deficiency and kosher in the spleen, and there is no prohibition except if punctured afterward — for we say the blow of the hole will cause damage to the rest of the body, as Rashi in the Gemara. Not so here where there is no blow but deficiency alone — kosher, in my humble opinion.
Like the thickness of a dinar. It appears that thickness must be specifically from the flesh of the spleen itself, and sealing of the lung or flesh does not help, as in siman 45 in the name of Orach Chaim.
But if the thin end was punctured — kosher. It appears clear that those holes in the spleen that are kosher refer when there is no needle placed before us there — for if there is a needle it is certainly no better than liver or tarfesha of liver, for there is concern perhaps it entered from outside after the esophagus was punctured, as we are concerned with liver in siman 41 seif 6 — specifically regarding lung it was permitted according to Talmudic law since it is close to the windpipe and from there the needle went to it; and so in the stomach, as stated siman 48, that there we say the way of the esophagus is to reach it, even in the outer pouch of food and drink that they forced, as stated explicitly in the Gemara; and so in the crop, as above siman 41 seif 6 — liver is not comparable to all these. And the spleen is certainly comparable to liver in this, for all the stringencies they said regarding a needle in liver apply to the spleen, which is farther from the windpipe than even the liver. And I saw Maharshal brought Rashi's words Piskei Avodah Zarah (daf 55): "And if there remained in the thickness of its wall like the thickness of a dinar and the needle did not come out — and there is a covering opposite it like the thickness of a dinar — kosher." And from this he distinguished: if the needle is found in the thickness of the inner spleen attached to the kereis — we attribute that the way of the esophagus enters the kereis and food and drink forced it and it entered the spleen; therefore if there remained like the thickness of the dinar in the thickness of the spleen toward the outside — kosher, for it is no worse than if punctured — and liver seals its hole and is kosher; so too the spleen attached to the kereis — the spleen seals the hole. But if a needle is found in its loose end toward the outside — even if there remained like the thickness of a dinar in the place attached to the kereis — treifah, like a needle found in the body cavity of the animal, end of his words. And in my humble opinion it is clear that the spleen does not seal a hole in the kereis, as I proved in siman 48 seif 2. And Rashi who cited here a needle found in the spleen refers when there is no concern that other inner organs were punctured, as Mordechai wrote siman 50 and 51 — namely that we saw it came from the outside toward the spleen; but in an unspecified case where a needle is found in the spleen in every manner — treifah, for the Sages did not distinguish to permit a needle only in lung, liver, crop, melting organ, and stomach — and they did not mention anything of the spleen if there were a distinction; certainly they would not have been silent about it — rather certainly in every manner treifah. It appears to me simple in reason that the spleen does not help to seal a hole in the kereis at all.
Afflicted with a change of flesh. Maharshal wrote that a change of appearance does not disqualify at all in the spleen, for this is not found at all in the Talmud. And it appears that Rama here too did not forbid except when the flesh is afflicted on account of the change; but on account of change alone there is no treifah — and so wrote Maharshal.
And if there is water in the spleen, etc. The source of this law is from Mordechai Piskei Avodah Zarah, and this is his language: Rabbi Yitzchak of Vienna wrote: I saw before our teacher Rabbeinu Ephraim that they brought before him a cow's spleen very swollen in its middle until its hilum; he commanded and they cut before him the swelling; when they cut, clear water jumped from the spleen from the place of the swelling and water the amount of a large vessel came out; its appearance inside was like wax; he commanded and they scraped inside with a knife and scraped much and examined inside and it was whole on every side that it was not punctured; and Rabbeinu Ephraim told us that Rabbenu Tam said to Raavan mouth to mouth in every place the Sages said it is treifah through a hole — even if it was not punctured and the flesh around it is bad it is treifah; and here since they scraped it inside and it was found whole on every side — it appears to permit. And he said that if the water were putrid he would also permit, end of his words. And one may wonder why Rabbi Yitzchak needed to write this incident — for even if cut entirely on the thin side it is kosher; why would there be treifah here when the swelling was only until the hilum. And it appears he wrote it to inform us two things: one, that at that time Rabbeinu Ephraim told him what he heard from Rabbenu Tam that if the flesh is bad it is like punctured — from this we learn that if so on the thick side and dinar-thickness did not remain whole it is treifah. Two, that we do not say since it rotted so much until it became water — and all the more if putrid — perhaps in the end it will be afflicted in all of it including the hilum; he teaches that since it only went until the hilum and from there onward there is no affliction at all — kosher. From this we learn that if there is affliction in the spleen on the thick side, i.e. the hilum, it is certainly treifah if above or below dinar-thickness did not remain — this affliction is like punctured or cut, as Rabbenu Tam told Raavan; and even if the water were clear, nevertheless it is affliction and is considered at least deficiency in that place. And what Rabbeinu Ephraim permitted here if the flesh is whole means it remained whole in the hilum in its measure — and so is written in Turei Chayim explicitly; and so is Rama's intent here in what he wrote "if the flesh is whole," meaning in its law in the hilum; and he teaches that whenever there is water in the spleen they do not forbid — even if putrid we do not say perhaps in the end all will rot; nevertheless we require that the whole flesh be in its measure, namely the hilum shall be like a dinar. And behold Maharshal wrote that from Rama's words here it implies that even if dinar-thickness did not remain in the hilum, nevertheless kosher — and he retracts from what he wrote in Turei Chayim; and I do not know who inserted this to separate between the attached parts — but in truth Rama's words here and in Turei Chayim are one, and so is the essence: everywhere flesh is missing in the thick place — even through clear water there is no permit unless dinar-thickness remained from it, as above.
