The body. Shach wrote: provided its place of connection to the body remains like a peg in a hole — otherwise it is as slipped:
Broken. Shach wrote: even if it healed well, fragment on fragment — forbidden, not like Bach and Maharshal; however one may side and be lenient since in any case many poskim permit in a break; and regarding a break far from the body where the limb itself is forbidden if cooked thus — see above siman 55 in the hagahah. (And there it is explained in s.k. 13 one should not forbid even if there is no sixty, for where there is doubt we go leniently; and all the more where there is no defect in skin and flesh and it rejoined — only not joined as at first — the dish is permitted; and the same in doubt of going out in siman 55 seif 5 — permitted without sixty; and likewise even where certainly went out, one need not be stringent in a dish or roast or salting against it as long as there is no sixty in the piece against it to say piece so-and-so is forbidden by adhesion, as we say below siman 72 regarding heart and liver siman 73; since in this limb there is only the mitzvah of separation. And specifically where there is sixty in the dish but in the piece where the limb adheres there is no sixty — one need not be stringent, since in any case many poskim hold even in adhesion prohibition all the pot joins; but when there is no sixty in the dish, forbidden from certain going-out prohibition, end of Shach there. Taz ruled there s.k. 7 to be lenient in salting in every case, whether doubt of going-out or certain; and in a dish and roast to be lenient where there is great loss or for guests or Shabbat since it is rabbinic doubt; and Peri Chadash ruled there like Shach: in doubt of going-out no sixty needed, and certain going-out needs sixty; and we are not stringent in adhesion prohibition; and he disagrees with Taz who is lenient in certain going-out if cooked in great loss even without sixty, see there):
Thumb. Shach wrote in the name of Rambam (chapter 10 of Hilchot Shechitah): thumb-width is the middle finger, and it is seven medium finger-widths pressed together; and in length its measure is two hairs loosely. We measure the thumb apart from what is stuck in the body like a peg in a hole (and in responsum Noda BiYehudah siman 63 he rules: when measuring from the peg one measures with a spacious finger; and if measuring from place of connection to body from outside — with a compressed finger, see there; and our custom: if there is thumb-width even in narrow measure from break to body — kosher; and specifically until the body, not until the head of the chapter stuck in the body):
Concerned. Bach wrote: nevertheless one should be stringent for oneself to look at ribs when the break is close and is only thumb-width — and requires further study; but beyond thumb-width all agree inspection of ribs is not needed (and Taz ruled: with a spur one should look; and Shach copied Bach who ruled one should look at ribs when break is close in less than thumb-width; and I did not understand: if less than thumb-width, in any case it is treifah per Rama's ruling):
The body. Perishah wrote: and if the spur entered within a thumb near the body, even though the break is more than a thumb from the body — treifah; and Shach wrote on this: he challenged — what of it the spur entered within a thumb; after all the place of breaking is far a thumb from the body; and Perishah must mean: even though the main break is more than a thumb, from the middle of the break there is far a thumb; nevertheless since the spur entered within a thumb — treifah; and so is implied in Taz. And if on the broken bone something remains connected to the body as thumb-measure, even if not from outer bone connecting a thumb — since the break was made far a thumb — kosher (and in responsum Mateh Yosef siman 5:6 he elaborated greatly to explain these Shach words in three ways, see there; and he raised for practical law: if broken near the body less than thumb-measure — treifah in every case even without spur and even if healed well, for it is not as at first — behold treifah; and if the break is far from the body without spur, even if the break is diagonal like a pole and a bit of the break is within a thumb — kosher; and if there is a round spur, if diagonal and part of outer bone is within thumb — treifah; and if the break is straight, even if outer bone entered within thumb — one may permit, end of his words, see there; and in responsum Noda BiYehudah question 61 he also elaborated. If a wing broke near its connection to the body before us — break the wing entirely so the lung is not perforated; with that — kosher for all; but in slipped there is no remedy except waiting twelve months or birth, as siman 57 Peri Chadash wrote. Bach wrote: those geese they bring to the slaughterer — they bend their wings to place them under the goose's feet and tie the feet; it is common the wing breaks thereby from life — we hang to forbid, for it was done from life; and the same for a chicken they bend the wing when holding to slaughter — common at start of slaughter when it convulses the wing breaks — we hang to forbid from life; and Peri Chadash wrote on this: this law is not clear, for from any double doubt nothing emerges whether at start or end of slaughter it broke; and if you will say at start of slaughter perhaps lung was not perforated — so Issur VeHeter; and all the more if the slaughterer knows certainly the bird brought before him was whole, only at slaughter it broke from convulsion. Pri Toar: the reason is since wings are tied or in the slaughterer's hand — certainly not perforated, for the main reason is through swinging the wing the lung is perforated — this does not apply here. An animal or bird: a limb broke in a place that makes it treifah — we hold thus — and the limb is forbidden; or broke where it does not make treifah — we hold thus — the limb is forbidden; may one sell the animal or bird alive or after slaughter with the dangling limb to a gentile, for we hold a gentile is warned regarding a limb from a living animal — Ateret Zahav siman 14 permits, especially in a bird, see there; and see R' Moshe Butin 5; Taz 117; Kaf HaChayim 142 and 178; Pri Megadim 24 and Mishna Berurah; Noda BiYehudah 49 and Tzitz Eliezer 100:105; and in Shaarei Moriah 17):
