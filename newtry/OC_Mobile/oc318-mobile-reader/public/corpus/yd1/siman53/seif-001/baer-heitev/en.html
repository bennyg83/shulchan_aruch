Kosher. In an animal, from the thickness of the shoulder and breadth there is no concern for lung perforation as in a bird (Mordechai); and Bach is stringent in broken or slipped or cut even in an animal from concern perhaps its nivin were eaten; and Taz wrote: there are those who want to declare treifah if an extra wing is found in a bird like slipped wing; and Bach already obtained that declaring slipped limb is from concern perhaps lung was perforated — and this does not apply in missing or extra wing, for the lung was not perforated; if so, certainly no treifah in extra wing per his view:
When broken. And when slipped — see above siman 62 seif 3 in the hagahah (and we practice to cut off that entire broken limb — Beit Yosef):
Coagulated. And so in Shach: he found in Tashbetz one should be stringent even if blood did not coagulate across ribs, from its nivin being eaten (and Maharshal is stringent in break like Shulchan De'ah and lenient in slipped; and Bach is stringent even in slipped); and the milk of that animal, and the same for an egg (Pri Toar) — question 53 permits; and see responsum Mabit question 10. Peri Chadash wrote: and it is good to be concerned for their words in a gentile's animal; but otherwise one should rule as Shulchan Aruch ruled, and even if blood coagulated in ribs there is no concern here. Levush is stringent in one broken rib in a bird opposite lung like broken wing; and Taz permits one broken rib and distinguishes rib from wing: in a wing, since the bird swings it, perhaps thereby it entered the lung; unlike a rib lying in one place — even inspection is not needed. Beit Yosef declares treifah in a bird if a rib broke and there is a spur even if fragments rejoined, even not opposite lung; but if doubtful whether there was a spur — kosher from double doubt; but in an animal one does not declare treifah if one rib broke even with a spur — see there siman 54 in Shach s.k. 3.
