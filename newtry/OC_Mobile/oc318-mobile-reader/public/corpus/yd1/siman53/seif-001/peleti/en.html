If an animal's limbs slipped off, etc.; and see Shach s.k. 5 in the name of Shulchan De'ah that he declares treifah in slipped limb in an animal from concern perhaps its nivin were eaten; and Bach testified he saw thus in manuscript; and he wrote it is against the Gemara, which is clear slipped limb in an animal is kosher; and in truth it is astonishing at first glance. It appears they hold not like Tur HaChadash who explained slipped thigh means its nivin were eaten — for anonymously it teaches slipped thigh; and see Ran and Rashba — they have proof, for slipped thigh, limb in animal and bird are taught together; and if the sign of slipped is from nivin being eaten — what relevance to slipped in a bird, where treifah of limb perforates lung; and what difference whether severed or nivin eaten; and accordingly in truth slipped is treifah without nivin being eaten; if so, difficult R' Yosi against R' Yosi who holds above buka d'atma that pushed from its place is kosher (and in Rif's version even with nivin eaten — kosher; see Rashba's novellae); and in our sugya R' Yosi holds slipped thigh is treifah, and all sugyot of treifah, and Rava per our version disagrees and holds kosher in buka d'atma — difficult how he argues on all these sugyot; and why need Tosafot's explanation that slipped thigh means nivin eaten; if so, R' Hoshaya who said slipped thigh in bird is kosher — the same in animal, as Tosafot from Levi; and difficult: R' Hoshaya said 87b deficient and excess in leg — treifah, for all excess is considered as removed; and how do we say R' Yosi permits buka d'atma that pushed, etc. — behold we rule like anonymous mishnah that legs were cut, etc.; if so, mishnayot contradict — R' Yosi adds buka d'atma permitted from excess of that mishnah; and to distinguish between cut and pushed from its place — I had not concluded except finally regarding slipped thigh, etc., which is difficult Rav against Rav; and for R' Yosi nothing is difficult; therefore the Gaon R' Yosi Shulchan De'ah and his supporters hold buka d'atma is a matter common to hand and leg — the head of the thighbone stuck in the hole like a peg is called buka d'atma, and for R' Matnah everywhere treifah, namely with nivin eaten, for there is defect of decay; but R' Matnah who says generally between hand and leg, and does not hold only in leg but permits in hand, ruled halacha: with nivin eaten everywhere, even buka that pushed — both hand and leg treifah; and this written regarding slipped limb in wing — treifah from concern nivin eaten, for we are not expert to distinguish between severed and eaten; and one should be stringent in animals that are not Israel's and heed the stringent view. And behold Tosafot wrote explicitly daf 57 s.v. shemutat, etc.: all slipped in the sugya deals with nivin eaten in animal and bird; and now it is resolved why the whole sugya uses "slipped" and not "pushed from its place" like R' Meir: R' Meir deals without nivin eaten; but here it deals with nivin eaten, so "slipped" applies; and Tosafot concluded the whole sugya deals with nivin eaten. And one must understand slipped wing in bird is treifah from concern lung perforated — why did Shmuel say "and likewise inspect it" — what need for nivin eaten; one must say we do not establish lung defect except when we see nivin decayed; if so, here there is decay without decay and rot, only from defect — we are concerned defect through slipped wing also decayed lung from decay, unlike when not decayed and no defect here — we are not concerned for lung; and so must be said in Tosafot; and so is proven in Agudah Perek Avodah Zarah: slipped near slaughter — and we do not know if before or after slaughter — permitted, for in short time nivin cannot be eaten; and the same law in a bird per Tosafot's explanation — behold Tosafot hold all slipped, even in bird, means with nivin eaten; and difficult what to say regarding slipped wing; if so, also here if no defect — kosher. And these are Teshbetz's view that Shach brought: slipped wing in bird at shoulder connection — treifah from nivin eaten, end of his words; and Shach wrote it is an error, for what relevance does lung concern have to nivin eaten; and on this he sent his hand to emend — and heaven forbid to emend even one letter; and in great loss one may rely if nivin remain and were not severed at all; and so is Beit HaGadol's view as Tur wrote in slipped bird wing — certainly nivin eaten; and we do not concern for lung perforation unless nivin were eaten:
