Broken. Shach explained per the Mechaber: it deals when there is a spur on the break and the lung is not before us to inspect; with this the Mechaber's words do not contradict each other — requires further study:
To permit. Shach wrote: this deals when there is no spur — for then none permit; also if defect is seen in ribs or lung it is simple we do not rely on double doubt; and even inspection does not help, for we are not expert; and Bach wrote: all they permit from double doubt is where prohibition and permission are equal; but where room to hang prohibition more — we hang stringently:
To be stringent. The reason is in Shach: this is not a complete double doubt, for the doubt whether the lung was not perforated is from lack of knowledge that we are not expert; therefore Rama was stringent in slipped (and Maharshal wrote: also in break one should be stringent in doubt when it occurred, for it is not a complete doubt). And what he ruled in siman 110 s.t. that it is permitted in break and slipped from double doubt — there it deals in great loss; and here not in great loss; and Taz answered: there he ruled from the law side, and here from stringency; therefore he was precise in language "one should be stringent."
