But when found dislocated, etc. And in responsum Mabit siman 41 and siman 50 he elaborated greatly and said: even Mahariik does not forbid at all where there is double doubt — implying even in slipped; and though his words are correct in this, nevertheless in the essence of the law he elaborated unnecessarily, for the matter is simple that the Rav holds this is not called double doubt, since the second doubt is from lack of knowledge, as Darkei Moshe wrote (and see below siman 55 s.k. 4 and siman 98 s.k. 9; and in Taz he wrote it is closer to certainty the lung was perforated and here there is only one doubt, see there); and Mabit escaped Darkei Moshe's words; and though he sided to say nevertheless it is double doubt and concludes in great loss one may be lenient even in slipped, see there — nevertheless it is simple the Rav also holds that in a place of great loss and such one may consider it properly double doubt, as Darkei Moshe wrote siman 57 that such a double doubt does not need inspection; if so, lack of knowledge does not harm; and so below siman 110 s.t. in hagahah one may permit slipped from double doubt; and this is what the Rav wrote here; and see above double doubt 110; and in double doubt 110 Darkei Moshe wrote; and see here siman 53; and Ran practically one must be stringent in slipped; but in great loss and such one may permit; and so Bach; however where there is no great loss one should not be lenient at all, for Maharshal in Issur VeHeter siman 45 forbids in a broken wing — doubt before slaughter doubt after slaughter; and all the more here both doubts are from lack of knowledge, for also the first doubt whether before slaughter is lack of knowledge, for if he were wise and expert he could distinguish, as Maharai wrote in Hagahot Shulchan De'ah siman 86; and it will be explained below siman 55; and so Bach:
