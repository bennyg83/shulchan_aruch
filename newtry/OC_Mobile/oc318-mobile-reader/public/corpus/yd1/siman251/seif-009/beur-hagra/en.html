And an Israelite precedes a chalal, etc. This follows the order in which they are listed in the fourth chapter of Kiddushin. A daughter of an Israelite is fit to marry into the kehunah, unlike the daughter of a chalal. A shetuki is of uncertain mamzer status, and an asufi is also of uncertain convert status.
And even a scholar, etc. This is stated in the Yerushalmi cited above.
And whoever is greater, etc. The Yerushalmi there teaches: One who is proficient in ordered learning precedes one who excels in dialectical analysis, etc.; see there.
But if he was, etc. As stated in Bava Metzia 33a regarding a lost object, where one's father, etc. Otherwise, his father takes precedence even if he is not a scholar at all. All the more so if he possesses some Torah scholarship, and all the more so in the present context, whose obligation is more stringent than that toward one's father, as explained above.
