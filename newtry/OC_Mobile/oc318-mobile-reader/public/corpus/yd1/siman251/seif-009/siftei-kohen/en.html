And a Levi precedes an Israelite, etc. See Orach Chaim, siman 201.
And a shetuki, etc. See Even HaEzer, siman 4, for the definitions of a shetuki, an asufi, and a mamzer.
And an unlearned Kohen Gadol, etc. Sefer Be'er Sheva, page 53c, states that this does not mean specifically a complete ignoramus, but even one who is not equal in wisdom to the Israelite. See above in siman 246, seif katan 15.
And even a Torah scholar with respect to clothing, etc. It appears that this is the strict law. In the present era, however, when no person has the status of a Torah scholar even regarding the exemption of a litra of gold, as stated above in siman 243, seif 7, all the more so may saving a life not be deferred for his sake. This is comparable to a case of a man needing clothing and a woman needing rescue, or the reverse, concerning which the Yerushalmi rules that the one requiring rescue takes precedence.
Or his father who is a Torah scholar, etc. This wording also appears in the Tur and in the Rambam, at the end of chapter 8 of Laws of Gifts to the Poor. I do not know their source for requiring the father to be a Torah scholar. On the contrary, the Mishnah at the end of chapter Elu Metziot teaches that if one's father and teacher are held captive, he redeems his teacher and afterward his father. If his father is a Torah scholar, he redeems his father first and afterward his teacher. The Rambam likewise rules in chapter 8 of Laws of Torah Study, and the Tur and Shulchan Aruch above in siman 242, seif 34. This implies that the first clause concerns a father who is not a scholar, and nevertheless only his teacher precedes him. Otherwise, even one greater in wisdom than his father would not precede his father. This is also implied by the Rambam there, the Beit Yosef, and the Rama, who specify that only one's primary teacher precedes. If he is not one's primary teacher, one's father precedes even if the father is not a scholar at all. This is also logical, for if he is not his teacher, why should he precede one's father, whose honor is a Torah obligation? It appears that the statement here, 'his father who is a Torah scholar,' means that in such a case the father precedes even one's teacher who is greater in wisdom than the father. Regarding redemption of lives, the father need not equal his teacher in scholarship, as stated above in siman 242.
