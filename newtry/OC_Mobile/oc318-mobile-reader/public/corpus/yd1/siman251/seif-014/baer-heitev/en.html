And they struck them. The Taz wrote that Maharshal challenged this: do we not conclude in the first chapter of Megillah that Torah study is greater than saving lives? This is not a challenge, for certainly nothing stands before pikuach nefesh. Rather, there it says that there is greater merit for one who merits to engage in Torah and did not have saving lives come to his hand, through which he would need to interrupt Torah study and engage in saving lives. This is unlike the Derishah, who wrote that if it is a case where he cannot fulfill both, then Torah study comes first, etc.; this is certainly incorrect; end quote.
