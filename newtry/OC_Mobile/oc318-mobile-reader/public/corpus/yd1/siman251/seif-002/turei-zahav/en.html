Forbidden to redeem him — in Tur it is written regarding a poor person who transgresses one of all the mitzvot, etc., he is not obligated to give him charity, etc.; and it deals with to provoke, for for appetite he mentions afterward in the conclusion; therefore necessarily to provoke; and Beit Yosef challenged that from the language "he is not obligated" it implies there is no prohibition if he gives him; and the case of R. Ami is clear that it is forbidden to sustain him; and Perishah answered that for provoking once he is not called apostate and there is no prohibition to sustain him; and so an apostate who does many times, even for appetite it is so; but an apostate to provoke — meaning many times — there is prohibition to sustain him; and it is not clear to my humble view that also the first time when he acts to provoke there is prohibition, as implied by Rambam's language that Beit Yosef brings, who wrote he would eat nevelah to provoke; and so Tur's language siman 252; and nevertheless there is prohibition to redeem him, and it implies once; and so proven in Gemara in chapter HaSholeach where it says "sometimes there is permitted and forbidden before him and he leaves the permitted," etc. — language "times" implies once (in chapter HaKotev Ketubot 85a we say "times R. Papa sat before him," etc. — meaning once, as proven there); and what Perishah wrote that once he is not called apostate is true, for so Tosafot in the first chapter of Chullin for one answer in the mishnah "one who slaughters on Shabbat and Yom Kippur," etc., is not relevant here, for there it deals whether his slaughter is disqualified as slaughter of an apostate to desecrate Shabbat — we might say he is apostate to the whole Torah; and on this we say because of, etc. — end.
