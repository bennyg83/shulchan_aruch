One who gives to his adult sons and daughters whom he is not obligated to sustain, in order to teach the sons Torah and guide the daughters in the upright way — and likewise one who gives gifts to his father and they need them — behold, this is included in charity; and not only that, but he must give him precedence over others; and even if he is neither his son nor his father but his relative, he must give him precedence over every person; and his paternal brother takes precedence over his maternal brother; and the poor of his household take precedence over the poor of his city; and the poor of his city take precedence over the poor of another city (so is explicit in Semag and Semak and Tur): {Rama: And those settled in the city are called "the poor of the city," and they take precedence over other poor who come there from other places (Tur, not like R' Yehudah bar Baruch).} And residents of Eretz Yisrael take precedence over residents of chutz la'aretz: {Rama: His own sustenance takes precedence over every person, and he is not obligated to give charity until he has his sustenance; and afterward he gives precedence to sustaining his father and mother if they are poor — and they take precedence over sustaining his children; and afterward his children — and they take precedence over his brothers; and his brothers take precedence over other relatives; and relatives take precedence over his neighbors; and his neighbors over the people of his city; and the people of his city over another city; and the same law applies if they were captives and he must redeem them (all in Tur).}
