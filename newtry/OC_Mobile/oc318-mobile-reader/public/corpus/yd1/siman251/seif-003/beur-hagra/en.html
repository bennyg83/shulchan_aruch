Likewise, one who gives, etc. See Tosafot, Kiddushin 32a, s.v. Oru, etc.
Moreover, etc.; and even, etc. The Sifrei expounds: "Your brother" means your paternal brother; "one of your brothers" includes your maternal brother. This teaches that a paternal brother precedes a maternal brother. "In one of your gates" teaches that the residents of your city precede the residents of another city. Residents of Eretz Yisrael precede residents of Chutz LaAretz. From where do we know that residents of Chutz LaAretz are also included? Scripture states, "which Hashem your God gives you," to include every place. Tanna Devei Eliyahu, at the beginning of chapter 27, expounds the verses, "Is it not to break your bread for the hungry, etc., and do not hide yourself from your own flesh." How is this applied? If a person has food in his house and wishes to distribute it as charity to support others from his own resources, what should he do? First he should support his father and mother. If there is a surplus, he should support his brothers and sisters. If there is a further surplus, he should support the members of his family. If more remains, he should support the residents of his neighborhood. If more remains, he should support the residents of his alley. From that point onward, he should increase his charity together with the rest of Israel, etc. See also Bava Metzia 71a.
And those who are established residents, etc. As stated in the first chapter of Bava Batra, 7b: How long must one reside in a city, etc.? The Yerushalmi there states that for the wheat of Pesach the period is twelve months, both to receive and to contribute.
(Supplement) And those who are established residents, etc. See the Tur there. Likewise, the Tosefta at the end of Peah states: The communal charity fund is for the residents of that city; if a person remained there thirty days, he is regarded as one of the residents of the city. (End of supplement.)
His own sustenance, etc. As stated at the beginning of the fifth chapter of Bava Metzia, where Ben Petura expounded, etc.; and at the end of the second chapter of Bava Metzia, regarding his own lost object, etc. Likewise, Tanna Devei Eliyahu cited above states, "If a person has food, etc.," as explained above.
