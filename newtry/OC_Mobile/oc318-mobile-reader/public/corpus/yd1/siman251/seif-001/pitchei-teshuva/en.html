Taz — here it deals with an apostate for appetite many times; and it implies here even when they seek to kill him; and so is explained in responsum Maharam Lublin siman 15. And I wonder at responsum Chavot Yair siman 139 who wrote regarding a famous Jewish thief who was caught and they tortured him excessively — certainly they must strive for expenses and save him; and one should not say since his way is thus he is an apostate for one matter — for what of it, he is not an apostate to anger; and what some say that since he is a famous thief he endangers the public who may hand him to gentiles — it is not clear to say he endangers the public; for if he is caught in his sin he will die; and even though there is chillul Hashem as some wicked people generalize against all Israel for this — these are only words of the masses not their sages; for also among them are found thousands; and the poskim did not speak of permission to hand him over except one dealing in counterfeiting — for whoever touches this becomes impure and these coins spread among Israel and how many innocent souls come to danger from this — unlike such a thief — end quote; and he did not mention at all Beit Yosef and Rama's words here that even for an apostate for appetite one is not obligated to redeem him — and this is also surprising on responsum Kenesset Yisrael siman 38 regarding a man who sat in the military courtyard near mortal danger and that man was famous for bad ways — adultery and theft — and he extended whether they must redeem him more than his value as I will write in his name below siman 252 seif 4; and he did not mention at all to say he need not redeem him even for his value because his ways are not good. However I again saw in Bechor Shor in his novellae to Gittin 47 that he wondered at Beit Yosef and Acharonim who did not bring Tosafot in Avodah Zarah 26 that implies even one who does so many times for appetite — nevertheless they are obligated to redeem him; and he raised that for halachah one should be stringent even for a habitual one not to stand on his blood and save him per the plain meaning of Tosafot in Avodah Zarah and per Rambam brought above siman 158 that one who eats nevelot for appetite — mitzvah to save him and forbidden to stand on his blood; one should not push aside their words in Torah per Semag in name of Ra'em that he is an individual relative to them — end quote; and it must be said this is the view of those responsa above; and even so it is surprising they did not speak of this at all. And he further wrote in Bechor Shor that it implies in our sugya that even a safek for appetite one must save him — for it says "perhaps for appetite," etc., which is language of doubt — see there. And see further in Chavot Yair s.v. 146 who wrote that one who killed a soul is only like one who transgresses for appetite to satisfy his evil inclination as said in Gemara Shabbat regarding tearing in his anger; and if afterward he was caught for theft or another matter — all the more if he comes to mortal danger — mitzvah to strive on his behalf if he is not constantly wicked — see there further; and see in She'elat Ya'avetz part 2 siman 9 that I brought above siman 232 s.k. 10.
