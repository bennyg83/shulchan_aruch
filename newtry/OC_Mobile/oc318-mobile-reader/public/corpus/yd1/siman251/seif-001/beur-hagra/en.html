One who is, etc. This is the language of the Semag, and it means an apostate who sins to satisfy his appetite, as stated there. Regarding the incident at the end of the fourth chapter of Gittin, 47a, where Rabbi Ami initially wished to redeem him but then refrained because he was an habitual transgressor, the reason is that, since he is a transgressor, he is not included in "your brother." The mitzvos of "from one of your brothers" and "your brother shall live with you" do not apply to him until he receives lashes, as stated in the Mishnah, Makkot 23a. The Semak rules likewise, as stated in seif 2. The Beit Yosef similarly inferred from the Rambam's wording in seif 2, "one who is a transgressor in order to provoke, etc.," that there is no prohibition against assisting one who sins from appetite, but there is also no obligation. This accords with the gloss there, "But a transgressor, etc." The inference is not conclusive. Tosafot, Avodah Zarah 26b, s.v. Ani, write that one is obligated to redeem him and lend to him, and this is the primary ruling.
(Supplement) And they support, etc., together with, etc. It appears that his view follows the Mordechai, that this applies specifically together with the Jewish poor, unlike the Ran there, who writes that the word "together with" is not exclusive. See Darchei Moshe. However, in Shulchan Aruch, siman 151, seif 12, he rules in accordance with the Ran; see there. (End of supplement.)
