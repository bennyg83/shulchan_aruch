One who pledged, etc. As stated in the Tosefta, second chapter of Megillah: An individual who pledged charity while in his own city gives it to the poor of his city; if he pledged in another city, he gives it to the poor of that city, etc. One who pledged charity may redirect it to another purpose before the charity administrators acquired it. Once the administrators acquired it, he may not redirect it to another purpose except with their consent. The same appears in the Gemara, first chapter of Arakhin, 6a.
But before, etc. As explained above.
For we assess, etc. Since the obligation rests upon him, as explained above, it may be presumed that his intention accords with what the Torah commanded. This follows the principle stated in Kiddushin 64b: "A person does not leave aside, etc."
Specifically, etc. For one who pledges charity generally intends that it be distributed immediately.
And all this, etc. As stated in the final chapter of Megillah, 27a: "An individual who went to another city, etc." All the more so does this apply to the residents of his own city. Likewise, as stated above: An individual who pledged charity in his city gives it to the residents of his city, etc.
