Meaning: it has a wide seat — then they do not appear as fragments but as originally made; and there was only this limb alone — no nullification here appears when they worshipped while whole and a fragment broke from it — nullification. Difficulty: at end of siman 146 the Tur ruled idolatry that broke by itself is forbidden — shall we concern here perhaps it broke by itself from it, and why permitted to finder not standing on base — behold Torah doubt? It appears one should say: since the Tur wrote there the prohibition is only for a layman who can restore it — and here when broken and lost, behold a layman cannot restore it. I later saw in Rashi end of chapter "all images" he challenged so and answered breaking by itself is uncommon — we follow majority that gentile broke it; and the Tur wrote in the Rambam's name forbidden even not standing on base; and the Tur challenged him — behold the Gemara established it like Shmuel specifically standing on base; and Beit Yosef answered the Rambam holds Shmuel also holds regarding idolatry broken by itself permitted; and therefore needed this; but for us forbidden — forbidden in idol fragments certainly worshipped and nullification does not help. Well answered; and so Tosafot there Shmuel like R. Yehuda holds permitted when broken by itself. And Maharach disagreed and answered the Rambam distinguishes images from idols — in images we require specifically its base, but in idols forbidden regardless; and investigate — behold explicitly Shmuel permits even idolatry fragments; and Rambam forbade, for since known this form was worshipped, behold it remains in prohibition until known the gentile nullified it; and Beit Yosef brings it — rather certainly ruled not like Shmuel; and as Beit Yosef wrote.
