Permitted. Although an idol that broke by itself remains forbidden, here we do not assume that it broke naturally. A non-Jew ordinarily guards it carefully, so we presume that a non-Jew broke and nullified it intentionally. Shach.
Its base. Meaning its pedestal, a broad base upon which the fragment stands independently, so it appears to have been made originally in that form rather than as a broken piece. The Mechaber therefore forbids it because perhaps it was worshipped. Shach writes that Rambam's formulation appears more correct: all fragments of an actual idol are forbidden because perhaps the non-Jew never nullified them, while fragments of idolatrous forms are permitted through a compounded doubt, whether they were worshipped and, if so, whether a non-Jew nullified them. Shach notes a contradiction with siman 146:11, where the Mechaber himself copies Rambam that fragments of an idol are forbidden.
