One who finds fragments of images. Meaning: it is not known they were worshipped; or even fragments of idols themselves that were worshipped when whole are permitted (and see s.k. 1) — although we hold an idol that broke by itself is forbidden, as below siman 146; nevertheless here for breaking by itself we are not concerned, for a gentile guards it well; therefore we say presumably the gentile broke it and nullified it manually; but if he found a hand or foot form standing on its base — meaning they have a wide seat they sit on alone, not appearing as fragments but as originally made — forbidden, for we are concerned perhaps they were worshipped; this is the mechaber's opinion like the Rif, Rosh, and Tur. But in truth the Rambam's words appear primary in the Gemara — all idol fragments forbidden even not standing on base, for we are concerned gentiles did not nullify them; but image fragments permitted, for there is double doubt — perhaps worshipped or not; and if you say worshipped, perhaps gentiles nullified (and the law of images themselves see s.k. 1). And so Tosafot's and Ran's opinion; and wonder on the mechaber who himself copied in siman 146 seif 11 the Rambam's language — therefore one who finds idol fragments, behold they are forbidden for benefit lest gentiles did not nullify them — end; and here he wrote even finding idol fragments permitted; and so wonder on the Magen Avraham — investigate.
