Shach s.k. 6 wrote: meaning it is known that one did not bow to it etc.; and see in Pri Tevuah siman 51 regarding hats engraved with idolatry forms that there is room to permit them per the Ran's words brought in Shach, who wrote on the Rashba's words — and difficulty, for possibly they do not worship those forms except as mere remembrance; and he explained his words — meaning even a form certainly worshipped, there is doubt perhaps they nullified it; and since there is further doubt perhaps for mere remembrance, it becomes double doubt; and although the Rivash wrote fitting to be stringent in Torah doubt, nevertheless in this case there is room to be more lenient since it is a sunken form; and he further combined the Rambam's words that sale is nullification; and especially that hats eaten from are not called honored vessels, as written in hagahah below seif 63 that the custom is like the first view that even a form certainly worshipped in disgraced vessels is permitted; and further it appears all the essence of the form is craftsman work for mere marking; and the Gaon R. Yitzchak z"l of Krakow agreed with him for the law in siman 52 and 53.
