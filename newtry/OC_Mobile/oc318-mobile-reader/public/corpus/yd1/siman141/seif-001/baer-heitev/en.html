Shach wrote: one can infer a precision from the first part against the end — in the first part he says that presumably etc., behold in doubt they are permitted; and in the end he says that certainly etc., behold in doubt they are forbidden. And it appears that in the first part he states the truth of the matter, that presumably the ways of villages are not to make for ornament but for idolatry; and likewise in doubt they are also forbidden, for we need to know specifically that they were certainly made for ornament, as he says in the end. And so is the Perishah's opinion, and not like the Bach.

Image. Meaning: like the law of an image that has proof it was made for idolatry; and that which is hung on the neck is permitted — meaning it is known that one did not bow to it, for otherwise it is no worse than finding vessels on which is a form of idolatry, for vessels are forbidden if they are honored, since it is customary to worship that form — we are concerned perhaps the vessels were worshipped, as below seif 63; and here too there is concern for this. And in idolatry itself one does not distinguish between honored and disgraced — only regarding vessels do we say that on disgraced ones they were certainly made for ornament of the vessel, not so here. And I found in the Ran, who wrote: the Rashba said that in this time they engrave engravings in vessels and paint forms of idolatry in them — even finding those vessels is forbidden — end of the Ran's words. And so R. Yerucham — if a human form or any other form is found, he disqualifies them as forbidden; and it is possible that these gentiles who are not attached to idolatry — all that they make in those forms in vessels they make only for ornament and are permitted for benefit; but forbidden to leave them because of suspicion if they protrude; and fitting to be stringent in Torah doubt — end of his words. And it is possible regarding a form that it is customary to bow to it for everyone it is forbidden — end of Shach's words.
