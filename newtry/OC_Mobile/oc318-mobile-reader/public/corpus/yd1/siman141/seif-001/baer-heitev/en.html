An image. This means according to the law of an image that has evidence that it was made for avodah zarah. The one hung from the neck that is permitted is where it is known that he did not bow to it; otherwise it is no less than one who finds utensils bearing an image of avodah zarah, where honored utensils are forbidden, because since that image is normally worshipped we suspect the utensils were worshipped, as below seif 3. The same concern applies here. With avodah zarah itself there is no distinction between honored and degraded; only regarding utensils do we say that on degraded items it was certainly made for decoration, unlike here. I found in the Ran that the Rashba wrote that nowadays, when they engrave utensils and draw images of avodah zarah upon them, even one who finds those utensils is forbidden to benefit from them. The Ran writes that this requires study, for perhaps today they do not worship these images and make them only as a memorial. Rabbenu Yerucham likewise writes that if one finds a human form or some other form among their statues, they are forbidden; but perhaps these non-Jews, who are not devoted to avodah zarah, make all such images on utensils only for decoration, and they are permitted in benefit, though one may not keep them because of suspicion if they are protruding. It is proper to be stringent in a doubtful Torah prohibition. Perhaps with an image that people normally bow to, all agree it is forbidden. Thus the Shach.
