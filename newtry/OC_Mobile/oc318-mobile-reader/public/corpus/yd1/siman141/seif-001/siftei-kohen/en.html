All images, etc., because ordinarily they are made for idolatry, etc.; for certainly they were made for ornament, etc. There is an apparent contradiction between the implication of the first clause and the latter clause. The first clause says that ordinarily, etc., implying that where there is doubt they are permitted, while the latter clause says that certainly, etc., implying that where there is doubt they are forbidden. It appears that the first clause merely states the actual ordinary circumstance, namely that in villages images are ordinarily not made for decoration but for idolatry. In truth, where there is doubt whether they were made for idolatry or for decoration they are forbidden, because one must know with certainty that they were made for decoration, as stated in the latter clause. This is also evident from the Tur. See there. This is likewise the view of the Prisha. The Bach, in Kuntres Acharon, challenged him, but according to what I have written his words are correct. It is also astonishing that the Bach did not note that in the Gemara, daf 41b, concerning the statement, 'Rather say: the images themselves are ordinarily forbidden, in accordance with R' Meir,' etc., the entire discussion proves that regarding intact images found in the marketplace we do not say that perhaps they were nullified, since they remain whole. See there and examine carefully.
The province. Meaning a city; and so we find many times in the language of the Gemara and the poskim.
And it was in the hand of the form etc. The mechaber copied from Rambam's language as is his custom; it implies from this language that all forms standing at the city entrance with a staff etc. in their hand are forbidden; and so somewhat appears from the Rav's glosses in seif 4 — see there; and so in Beit Yosef he wonders at what appears from the Tur that only human form specifically is forbidden in such a case (and possibly the Tur did not specifically mean human, and for this reason did not write this law at the beginning of the siman where it primarily belongs — see there — investigate); and the Bach wrote unlike what the Shulchan Aruch wrote “and it was in the hand of the form” etc. — it does not exist; rather specifically in human form — end; and it is wondrous how he disputes the Rambam (and apparently did not know the mechaber's words are copied from Rambam) and the mechaber without any proof at all, especially to be lenient; and all the more since the Tur's words can also be explained that way — see there; and also the plain Gemara sugya appears so — see there.
Form of a staff or a bird etc. See beginning of chapter "all images" — explained in the Gemara that all these indicate they were made for idolatry; and it appears in our time everything depends on the matter, whether in cities or villages — all according to what the things indicate as made for idolatry or for ornament.
Its law is like that of an image. Meaning: like the law of an image for which there is proof it was made for idolatry — forbidden without nullification; and in the manner explained in siman 146 — see there.
But those hung on the neck etc. Meaning when it is known he did not bow to it (and comes to teach one should not think that since they hang it on the neck it is immediately forbidden) — if not so, it is no better than when one finds vessels upon which are idolatrous forms: the vessels are forbidden if they are honored, since there is a customary way to worship that form we are concerned perhaps the vessels were worshipped, as below seif 3; and here too there is room for such concern. Regarding the gentile himself there is no distinction between honored and dishonored — specifically regarding vessels we say that dishonored ones were certainly made for vessel ornamentation, which is not the case here. I found in the Ran chapter "all images": Rashba wrote that nowadays when they engrave carvings in vessels and paint on them forms and images of idolatry, even one who finds those vessels they are forbidden — end; and wonder, for perhaps they do not worship those forms nowadays but make them merely as a memorial — end Ran; and so R. Yerucham 17:4; and possibly these gentiles who are not attached to idolatry, all they do with these forms on vessels is done only for ornament and permitted for benefit; and forbidden to leave them because of suspicion if they are protruding; and proper to be stringent in a doubtful Torah prohibition — end; and possibly regarding the form itself, since there is a customary way to bow to it — forbidden according to all.
