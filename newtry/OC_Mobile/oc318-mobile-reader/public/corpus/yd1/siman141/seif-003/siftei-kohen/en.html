Meaning, images made to represent the sun and moon, etc. Since the Rama writes 'meaning, images made to represent the sun,' rather than 'and likewise images of the sun,' it appears that only this special type of image is forbidden. A plain circle representing the sun or a crescent like the moon itself is permitted when found, because such forms were not ordinarily worshipped. Rambam explicitly writes that 'images of the sun and moon' does not mean merely finding a circle and saying it is the sun, or an arc and saying it is the moon. Rather, the makers of talismanic images attribute particular forms to the celestial bodies, such as depicting the sun as a crowned king seated on a chariot; the prohibited 'image of the sun or moon' means an image conventionally attributed to that body. R' Ovadiah of Bartenura writes similarly, and this also appears from Rashi, who explains 'image of the sun and moon' as their mazal. This is unlike the Levush, whose wording implies that an ordinary circular representation is all the more prohibited. As to the rule below in seif 4 that images of the sun and moon may not be made or retained whether raised or engraved, there the concern is different: making is prohibited by the verse 'You shall not make with Me' regarding what is in the heavens, and retaining such an image creates suspicion even when the simple form was not ordinarily worshipped. The same distinction is evident from the Rama at the end of this seif: nowadays, when non-Jews do not worship these forms, one may benefit from them when found, yet it remains forbidden to retain them because of suspicion.
Or a dragon and it resembles a serpent etc. This form was venerated among them because they attribute it to one of the divisions of the celestial sphere, and it is the form of the lunar node — end Rambam there; and similarly wrote the Mordechai, whom Beit Yosef brings.
If the vessels are honored they are forbidden. And it appears that if they are neither honored nor dishonored but middling, they are forbidden — for if they were honored they would certainly have been made for idolatry, as the Tur wrote; if so there is here only a single doubt. And the Tur wrote below in this siman: it appears all those forbidden when found are forbidden even sunken, since we suspect perhaps made for idolatry, even sunken there is concern — end; but R. Yerucham wrote one authority wrote and so in Tosafot — they forbade only honored ones when protruding — end; brought by Beit Yosef.
That are on silks etc. The Tur's language: some say silk garments and embroideries are considered dishonored since they are for wearing; and they interpret "shira'im" as a bracelet; and the Rambam counted them among the honored; and it is good to be stringent — end; and since the mechaber did not bring any explanation, it implies his opinion is to be stringent.
Kumkumot. Small metal pots — Rashi. Hot-water heater. Meaning a vessel for heating water. Below the water. The bottoms and sides; above the water — meaning the rim of the vessel — Rashi.
But when it is known these vessels were not worshipped. Even though they worship those forms when on the vessels, permitted; and obviously even per this view if it is known these vessels were worshipped, even the dishonored ones forbidden.
And there are some who say etc., even though we do not know these vessels were worshipped. Since this form is certainly worshipped, we say presumably even the dishonored ones were worshipped, unless we know certainly these vessels were not worshipped — then even per this view even the honored ones are permitted. And per this view there is no distinction between honored and dishonored except in the unqualified case — meaning most gentiles are accustomed to worship it; but where it is not known if this form is worshipped there at all, or where it is known it is not worshipped in that place, or it is not known that most gentiles are accustomed to worship it — even honored ones permitted. All this is proven from the Ran.
And the custom is like the first view. Meaning: regarding forms that gentiles worship nowadays, we follow the first view — investigate.
And nowadays, when gentiles do not worship these forms etc. Refers to above — sun, moon, and dragon form — for nowadays permitted for benefit to the finder, even per the last view, since gentiles do not worship those forms; but one must not leave them because of suspicion.
And some are stringent regarding all the forms mentioned, even when we know they are not worshipped. This is very difficult. I have not seen an authority who requires such stringency, nor is there a reason for it. If we know with certainty that the image is not worshipped, what concern remains? Even regarding honored utensils, the final opinion cited by the Mechaber permits them where it is known that this particular image is not worshipped in that locale, despite the fact that most non-Jews elsewhere may worship that form. The Jerusalem Talmud explicitly says that if they are not worshipped, even honored images are permitted. Although the Rama derived a stringent view from the Tur and Tosafos following the Beit Yosef, in my humble opinion neither source proves it. The Tosafos discussion concerning Rabban Gamliel and images made for him addresses making images and suspicion in retaining them, not finding an image known not to have been worshipped. The Tur likewise may have relied upon his earlier statement that where it is known that such images are not worshipped, even honored ones are permitted. Moreover, after the Rama writes that the custom follows the first opinion, which permits even ordinarily worshipped forms on honored objects where it is known these particular objects were not worshipped, how can he then write that some are stringent nowadays even though we know they do not worship them? If we know they are not worshipped, the objects certainly were not worshipped, and no prohibition remains. This requires study.
And it is permitted to make the form of a dragon. This applies even in the era when dragons were worshipped. I found in the name of Maharshal that the reason is that the dragon is depicted as flying in the air and is not among the heavenly beings included in 'You shall not make with Me.' There is, however, a difficulty from seif 7, where the Rama states that the prohibition concerning a human form and a dragon applies only when the figure is complete; a head without a body or a body without a head entails no prohibition, whether found or made. This seems to imply that making a complete dragon is forbidden. The same difficulty applies to the Rosh and Tur. It appears, however, that the phrase 'or made' there refers only to a human image. The Bach cites the Mordechai in chapter Kol HaTzelamim as apparently treating the dragon like the sun and moon, but there too the comparison concerns retaining it because of suspicion, not the prohibition of making it.
But forbidden to leave them with him because of suspicion. Even nowadays; and as the Rav wrote nearby — one must not leave them.
