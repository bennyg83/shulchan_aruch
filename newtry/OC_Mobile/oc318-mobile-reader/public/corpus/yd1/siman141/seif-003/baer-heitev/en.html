For the sake of. Only in such a form are they forbidden. A simple round shape like the sun, or a crescent shaped like the moon, is permitted when found because these are not ordinarily worshipped in that form. Levush disagrees. Making or retaining even these round forms is nevertheless forbidden; the permission applies only to finding them. Shach.
Honored. If the vessels are neither honored nor degraded but intermediate, they are forbidden. The Tur writes that any image forbidden when found remains forbidden even if recessed, because if we suspect it was made for worship, recessed form does not remove that concern. Rabbeinu Yerucham writes that the prohibition on honored vessels applies only to protruding images. Shach.
Silks. The Tur writes that some regard fine embroidered silk garments as degraded and explain shiraim as an armlet. Rambam counts them among honored objects. It is proper to be stringent.
Small kettles. Meaning small metal pots. 'Water heaters' means vessels used to heat water. 'Below the water' means upon the bottom and walls; 'above the water' means upon the rim. Rashi.
When it is known. According to this view, if it is known that these vessels were actually worshipped, they are forbidden even when degraded. Shach.
Forbidden. If it is known with certainty that these vessels were not worshipped, then even honored vessels are permitted according to this view. Shach.
The custom. Regarding images that non-Jews worship nowadays, practice follows the first opinion. The phrase that non-Jews do not worship these forms refers to forms of the sun, moon, and similar heavenly bodies discussed above: when found, they are permitted for benefit, but may not be retained. Shach.
That we know. Shach expresses astonishment that anyone is stringent where it is certainly known that the vessels were not worshipped. Even honored vessels are permitted in such a case. He especially questions Rama, who first writes that even worshipped forms are permitted when known not to have been worshipped, and then cites those who are stringent despite our knowing this. The matter requires study.
Permitted. Even in Talmudic times. Maharshal explains that a dragon flies in the air and is not among the heavenly bodies included in 'You shall not make with Me.' Shach notes, however, that seif 7 appears to forbid a complete dragon form. Taz explains that retention is forbidden because perhaps it was previously worshipped; according to this, even a recessed dragon is forbidden.
