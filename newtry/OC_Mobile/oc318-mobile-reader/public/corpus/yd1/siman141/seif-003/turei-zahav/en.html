And it is permitted to make the form of a dragon. This is an explicit Talmudic passage at the end of Perek Kol HaTzelamim, Avodah Zarah 42: Is making the form of a dragon forbidden? But it is written, "You shall not make with Me gods of silver or gods of gold"; these are forbidden, but a dragon is not. I am surprised at the Beit Yosef, who wrote the source of this law as being that it is not among the things mentioned in the Gemara as forbidden. Maharshal gave a reason why a dragon is not included in "you shall not make" in the likeness of the heavenly attendants: because the dragon above flies in the air and is not in the heavens. My father-in-law z"l objected from the words of the Mordechai in the name of Rabbeinu Elyakim, brought by Beit Yosef at the end of this siman, on the statement that all faces are permitted except the human face: that "all" is not exact, for the forms of the sun, moon, and dragon are included in the prohibition, since they are important and people draw and worship them; end quote. This implies that a dragon too is forbidden to make because of "you shall not make with Me," like the sun and moon. This is not difficult, for there it refers to keeping them, as he explicitly concludes: since they are important, people draw and worship them. This applies to an idolater who made it for this purpose and a Jew found it, as in Perek Kol HaTzelamim, Avodah Zarah 42. Although Rabbeinu Elyakim there speaks according to Abaye, who establishes the case as making, his intent is clear: even according to Abaye, who is lenient and establishes it even as making, so that keeping it would be entirely permitted, nevertheless "all" is not exact, etc. So it must be stated there for one who examines his words. But it is forbidden to keep it. Meaning, perhaps it was already worshipped. For this reason even a sunken image is forbidden; so wrote the Tur.
