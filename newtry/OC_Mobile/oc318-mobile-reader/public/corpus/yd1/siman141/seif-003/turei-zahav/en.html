And it is permitted to make the form of a dragon. This is an explicit Gemara at the end of the chapter Kol HaTzelamim, Avodah Zarah 42b: “If one makes the form of a dragon, who forbade it? Is it not written, ‘You shall not make with Me gods of silver and gods of gold’? These are forbidden, but a dragon is not.” I am astonished at the Beit Yosef, who wrote that the source of this law is that a dragon is not included among the forms mentioned in the Gemara as forbidden. The Maharshal gave a reason why a dragon is not included in “You shall not make with Me,” as a likeness of My servants who serve before Me in the heavens, because the heavenly dragon flies in the air and is not in the heavens. My father-in-law, of blessed memory, raised a difficulty from the words of the Mordechai in the name of Rabbeinu Elyakim, which the Beit Yosef cites at the end of this siman. Concerning the statement, “All faces are permitted except the human face,” he writes that “all” is not precise, for the forms of the sun, moon, and dragon are likewise included in the prohibition, since they are significant forms that people fashion and worship. End quote. This implies that making a dragon is likewise forbidden because of “You shall not make with Me,” just like the sun and moon. There is no difficulty, for there he refers to retaining them. He explicitly concludes, “since they are significant forms that people fashion and worship.” This applies where a non-Jew made it and a Jew finds it, as stated in the chapter Kol HaTzelamim, Avodah Zarah 42b. Although Rabbeinu Elyakim there discusses Abaye, who establishes the teaching as referring to making the form, his intent is as follows: Even according to Abaye, who is lenient and establishes it even as referring to making, so that retaining it is certainly entirely permitted, nevertheless, the word “all” is not precise, etc. This is how one must explain his words upon examining them.
Rather, it is forbidden to retain it. This means that perhaps it had already been worshipped. For this reason, it is forbidden even if it is engraved or recessed. So wrote the Tur.
