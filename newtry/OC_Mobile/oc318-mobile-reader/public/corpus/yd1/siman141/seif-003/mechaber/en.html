One who finds vessels upon which are forms of the sun or the moon — meaning forms that are made for the sake of the sun and moon, as the masters of talismans make forms for the stars; for example, forms attributed to the sun: they portray a crowned king sitting upon a chariot, and the like; and for the moon they have a distinct form (Rambam in his commentary to the Mishnah, and Bartenura there) — or a dragon, which is similar to a snake and has fin-like protrusions between the vertebrae of the neck: if the vessels are honored, they are forbidden, for certainly they were made for elilim; but if they are degraded, they are permitted, for they were made only for ornamentation. And these are honored: silk garments, earrings, and a ring; and degraded: coins, lamps, kumkumim of hot water, and cups from which one drinks, whether they are below the waterline or above the waterline. And all this is in an unspecified case, where we do not know that these vessels were worshipped; but if it is known that these vessels were not worshipped, even those upon the honored [items] are permitted (the view of Ran). And there is one who says that any form that is certainly worshipped is forbidden even on degraded [items], even though we do not know that these vessels were worshipped; and according to this, in our times when they engrave carvings in vessels and draw upon them elilim, one who finds vessels that have elilim in them — they are forbidden. {Rama: And the custom is like the first reasoning. And in our times, when non-Jews do not worship these forms, they are permitted in benefit if one finds them, but one may not keep them (Tur, netiv 16). And there are those who are stringent regarding all the aforementioned forms even in our times, even though we know that they do not worship them (so it seems in Tur and Tosafos, perek Kol HaTzelamim). And a dragon form is permitted to make, but it is forbidden to keep it in one’s possession due to suspicion (Beit Yosef from Rosh and Tur).}
