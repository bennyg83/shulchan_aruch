That we know. Beur HaTur in the name of Shach; and see in responsa Knesset Yechezkel part 1 siman 13 what he wondered on Shach in this. And see there further regarding a parochet used several times for holiness with painted forms in colors, and one arose wanting to cast a blemish through sun form; and he z"l wrote there is no concern, for these forms are painted in color and this is neither protruding nor sunken as written in Darkei Moshe small seif 4 in the name of responsa Maharam — what they forbid in sun form sunken is specifically sunken; however depiction in color is neither protruding nor sunken (also Turei Zahav s.k. 12 wrote this distinction; and Nekudot HaKesef wrote it is not correct — wonder that they did not mention Darkei Moshe's words). And plainly those coats — according to what is heard, cut from a piece of silk and the forms are in printing — certainly not made for idols; and plainly they never worshipped them; and permitted even on honored ones as Rama wrote and the custom is like the first view; and although he concludes one should not leave them because of suspicion — it was already explained that in public there is no suspicion. And secondly, these forms were accustomed in those countries where they were printed not for idolatry etc.; and although R. Yerucham (brought in Beur HaTur s.k. 20) wrote that even though there is no suspicion in public it is ugly — he did not speak of these curtains, for many of them are sold in stores, and all the more when they cut it — and if the cut passed through those forms they are not precise — certainly there is no greater nullification than this even for sages who hold if sold to a Jewish smith it is not nullification — meaning actual idolatry. And not because of nullifying a blemish as in Orach Chaim siman 90 s.k. 23 is there concern, since the parochet was in the synagogue for a while before — similar to a kohen with a blemish in his city permitted to raise his hands etc. However, if the parochet is from a piece made by itself and not cut from pieces but made as a rug by itself — investigation is needed lest suspicion in distant countries (like in Asia and Barbary) they worship the sun and this rug was made to pray on it in their house of prayer — similar to the Rosh's question etc. — see there; and see what I will write below s.k. 7 in the name of Dibrei Yosef.
