Such as the four faces. One might ask: He later states that even a human form by itself is forbidden. The Ran answers that here there is a greater novelty, because he joined the other forms to the human form. One might have thought that it is permitted because it is not a human form. The same applies to ministering angels, because they have wings. Therefore, he teaches us that it is forbidden. This is difficult, for if so, were one to make a human form together with one or two of the other four faces, it should likewise be permitted, because there is neither a human form alone nor the form of all four faces. It does not appear reasonable to permit such a novelty, for the poskim certainly would not have remained silent without informing us that it is permitted. Therefore, it appears that there is a practical difference here. There is a prohibition derived from “with Me,” referring to the heavenly servants, the four faces together, and another prohibition derived from “Me,” referring to the human form alone. Were there only the exposition of “Me,” I would have said that after diminishing the human form it is permitted to retain the object, because the prohibition was only due to the human form alone. Therefore, the exposition of “with Me” teaches that there is also a prohibition because of the four faces. Consequently, all of them were made in a prohibited manner, and correcting only the human form does not help, since a prohibition remains even apart from the human form alone. He must diminish all of the forms. This appears correct. I later saw in the Semak, siman 160, as follows: “The text asks: Let the prohibition follow from the human form itself, for even by itself it is forbidden. It answers: This is needed only where the human form was already made and he completes the four faces by adding the other forms, which are permitted to make by themselves.” End quote. One cannot answer that a human form is forbidden only when it is a complete form with all of its limbs, as the Shulchan Aruch writes in seif 7, whereas the face alone is permitted, while the four faces are forbidden even when only their faces are depicted. For if so, why does the Gemara ask, “Accordingly, let the human face alone be permitted”? In truth, the form of a human face alone, without the entire body, is permitted. Necessarily, all four faces are depicted in their complete bodily form as they appear in the Divine Chariot.

Which are woven into a garment. The Ran's text reads, “upon the garment.”
It is permitted to make them. This implies that all the forbidden forms mentioned above may be made when they are recessed. However, the Tur subsequently cites the Ramban, who writes that every form whose manufacture is forbidden by the Torah because of “You shall not make with Me,” namely, the four faces of the Divine Chariot, the likeness of the angels in the upper abode, the likeness of the sun, moon, stars, and constellations in the lower abode, and the human form because it is stated, “You shall not make Me,” meaning in the likeness in which I show Myself to the prophets, is forbidden whether recessed or protruding. Other forms are likewise forbidden to make, but only rabbinically, and regarding those forms there is a distinction between recessed and protruding. Consequently, regarding those forms whose manufacture is forbidden by the Torah, although retaining them is permitted by the Torah and prohibited only rabbinically because of suspicion, there is likewise a distinction between recessed and protruding. The Ran cites this opinion in the name of the early authorities, writes that it is the primary view, and proves it from the Gemara. If so, it is astonishing that the Shulchan Aruch did not cite the Ramban's opinion. As a matter of practical halacha, one certainly should not be lenient at all against the Ramban, for the Tur concludes in accordance with him, and so does the Ran. My father-in-law, of blessed memory, wrote that according to the Ramban, objects whose manufacture is forbidden by the Torah and whose retention is forbidden rabbinically may not be retained even when recessed. However, the Ran's words that I cited, which express the Ramban's view, state explicitly that they may be retained when recessed. As for the implication of the Ramban's words that there is likewise a rabbinic prohibition to make other forms, the Ran explains that this refers to forms that non-Jews were accustomed to worship. If there is no custom to worship them, it is permitted to make them, as the Shulchan Aruch writes in seif 6.
And the form of the sun, etc. This is because these celestial bodies do not protrude in the firmament. So wrote Tosafos. In the Gemara and the Tur, the constellations are also mentioned together with the stars. If so, it is astonishing that the Shulchan Aruch later writes in seif 6 that it is permitted to make forms of animals. Are not the kid, lamb, and ox among the constellations, and likewise the lion? The Beit Yosef explicitly writes at the end of the siman, in his difficulty concerning the Mordechai in the name of Rabbeinu Elyakim, that a lion is among the permitted forms. Furthermore, if all forms of the constellations are forbidden, it would be forbidden to make the form of a fish, which is the constellation of Adar. Yet we find in the Gemara, in the chapter HaShole'ach, that Rav drew a fish. Were this forbidden, it would be forbidden even when recessed, as the Tur writes in the name of the Ramban concerning every form forbidden because of “You shall not make with Me.” It appears possible that the forms representing the constellations have distinctive designs, similar to what the Rama writes concerning the forms of the sun and moon in seif 3. Therefore, the Shulchan Aruch properly writes that forms of animals are permitted, implying all of them. However, this is difficult, for why did the Gemara not answer concerning Rabban Gamliel, for whom they made forms of the moon, that they did not draw the image of a king seated in a wagon mentioned by the Rama? Necessarily, with regard to making them, every depiction is forbidden, even a plain depiction of the sun. The distinctive image of a king is relevant only to retaining a form that one found. If so, the forms of the constellations, such as a lion and an ox, should likewise be forbidden even in their ordinary form. My father-in-law, of blessed memory, indeed wrote that it is forbidden to make the form of a lion or an ox because they are constellations. This is difficult because of what we cited, that Rav drew a fish. It appears that a drawing is more lenient even than a recessed image, because a drawing has no substance at all. Nevertheless, it appears that those who draw the forms of the constellations in machzorim in the prayer for rain do not act properly, since they intend to make the forms of the constellations.
