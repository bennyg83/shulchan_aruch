Signs of pure domestic animal and wild animal. In it are 3 seifim: The signs of domestic animal and wild animal were explained in the Torah, and they are two signs: chewing the cud and split hooves; and every domestic animal and wild animal that has no teeth in the upper jaw and no "nivin" — which are knobs of flesh like protruding teeth in children — and some say nivin are two teeth above, one from here and one from here at the ends of the jaws (explanation: "and strike him on the cheek" — Targum: on her jaw). It is known that it is pure, and this is recognizing a young camel that has no nivin until it grows. And everything that chews the cud splits the hoof except camel, hare, and hyrax which chew the cud and do not split the hoof — one need not be concerned they have teeth above or nivin; therefore one who finds an animal in the desert and does not recognize it and its hooves are cut off — for he cannot check them — should check if it has no teeth and no nivin above; it is known it is pure, and this is recognizing a young camel. And everything that splits the hoof chews the cud except pig; therefore if one found an animal whose mouth is cut off — for he cannot check it — he should check its legs if its hooves are split; it is known it is pure, and this is recognizing a pig. If it has horns it is removed from doubt of pig and is pure. And no impure animal's flesh under its elbow joint goes back and forth to whichever side one cuts it except the wild ass (explanation: wild donkey); therefore one who finds an animal whose mouth and hooves are cut off — for he cannot check them — should check the flesh under the wing of the elbow joint after slaughtering if it goes back and forth — it is pure, and this is recognizing a wild ass. (Human flesh is forbidden to eat from the Torah) (Ran chapter Acharai Mot and Rambam 22 Laws of Forbidden Foods law 3 and so Korban Aharon Shemini daf 64a unlike apparent meaning in Tosafot Acharai Mot and Rosh that human flesh is not forbidden from Torah — and so Bach).
