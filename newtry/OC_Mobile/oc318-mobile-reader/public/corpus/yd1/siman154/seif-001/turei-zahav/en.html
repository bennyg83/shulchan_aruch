Between herself and the gentile woman. It is clear that nursing and assisting at childbirth follow the same rule. In both cases two conditions are required: other Jewish women must be standing over them, and it must take place in a Jewish home. The reason the ruling concerning childbirth first says only that the Jewish woman should not be alone with the gentile woman and does not also state that it must be in a Jewish home is that ordinarily a woman gives birth in her own house and does not go to a gentile woman's house to give birth. Nursing is different. Tosafot write: accordingly, a Jewish woman traveling outside the city should not leave her infant alone with a gentile wet nurse unless there is a Jewish woman in the city who regularly enters and leaves that place. Even so, from bedtime onward it is forbidden to leave the child alone. This implies that regular entry and exit is sufficient even in the gentile's house. It is surprising that Beit Yosef did not cite Tosafot as disagreeing with Rosh.
