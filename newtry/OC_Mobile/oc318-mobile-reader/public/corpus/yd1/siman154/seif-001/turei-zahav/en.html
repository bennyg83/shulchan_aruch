Between her and [the infant] alone. It is clear that nursing and delivery are equivalent, and in both cases we require two conditions: namely, that other women be standing over them, and that [it take place] in a Jewish home. The reason the law of delivery was stated first [mentioning] "not between her and [the infant] alone" without also stating there that it must be in a Jewish home — is because ordinarily every woman in labor gives birth in her own home and does not go to a non-Jewish woman's home to give birth there, which is not the case with nursing. And in Tosafot they wrote as follows: "Therefore one must ensure that a Jewish woman who travels outside the city does not leave her infant alone in the care of a non-Jewish wet nurse if there is no Jewish woman who regularly enters and exits there; and even [with such a woman present], from the time the infant sleeps onward it is prohibited to leave him alone." This implies that if [a Jewish woman] enters and exits, it is sufficient even in the home of a non-Jew. It is surprising that the Beit Yosef did not cite [this Tosafot] to distinguish [it] from the view of the Rosh.
