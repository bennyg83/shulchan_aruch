And the upper story. The Shach explains that tumah breaks through and rises because the corpse lies directly beneath the opening. The tumah does not spread laterally through the house when it has a direct path upward through the opening, nor does it descend again into the house, since the opening is less than a tefach. If it were a tefach wide, the house and upper story would be treated as one chamber. The Taz adds that the doorway of the house is not rendered impure on the principle that the tumah will ultimately leave through it. Since only an olive-sized portion of a corpse lies there without an ohel above it, it is treated as lying in the public domain and does not convey tumah through the house. We learn from this that when a corpse lies in a room without an ohel above it, a kohen may not remain elsewhere in the house, both because the tumah rises and descends and because it will ultimately leave through the doorway. The leniency here applies only where the opening is less than a tefach.
