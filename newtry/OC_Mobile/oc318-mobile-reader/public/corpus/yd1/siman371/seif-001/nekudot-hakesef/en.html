(Siman 371 in Taz s.k. 3) He wants to prove that the interposition of a door or window does not help whenever it cannot stand without iron hinges or some other iron item, even though they are needed for the ground, etc. In my humble opinion this is not so. If so, wherever the Mishnah and the poskim state without qualification in many places that doors and windows interpose, they should have explained that this applies only when they can stand by themselves without the hinge and key fixed in them. Furthermore, why did the Mishnah in Ohalot chapter 6 teach this specifically regarding those burying the dead, etc., who supported it with a key, that if the door can stand by itself, etc.? It should have taught this in every other place where it teaches that a door interposes, namely only where it can stand without the key in it. Also, what he understood in the Rambam’s words appears to me incorrect, because the Rambam’s language is not as he copied it; he copied it according to his understanding. In truth, the Rambam’s language there is as follows: “And if not, the house is tamei, because it is found that the person is the one who interposed before the tumah, and a person and vessels bring tumah and do not interpose, as we explained”; end quote. Thus he wrote only that the person is the one who interposed, and he did not mention the vessel. Therefore it appears to me that the Rambam’s intent is to say that this person made the interposition, namely that he supported it with the key, and were it not that he supported it with the key, the door would not have been able to stand by itself. If so, the person is the one who interposed, and a person does not interpose. But the Rambam is not discussing the key, because the key is not a vessel once it is attached to the door, as I will write below. And so it is explicitly implied in the Rambam’s Commentary to the Mishnah, where he speaks of a key attached to the door, writing there, “to lock the gate of the house with the key that is in it”; see there. What the Rambam concludes, “and a person and vessels bring tumah and do not interpose,” is a carryover of language that he wrote above, in chapter 13 law 4: “These bring and do not interpose: a person and vessels.” That is why he writes “as we explained.” This appears to me clear in the Rambam’s view. A clear proof for my words is from what the Rambam wrote in his Commentary to the Mishnah: “If the entrance could remain locked as it was and would not open, even if this one holding it removed his hand from the key, the house is tahor. But if, when this one removes his hand, the gate opens, it is tamei, because this person who was carrying the dead was the one who interposed before the tumah, and a person does not interpose”; end quote. This is exactly as I explained: he is not discussing the key’s interposition, that it should not interpose because it is a vessel, but rather the person. If so, on the contrary, it is proven from these words of the Rambam that even if the door could not stand except by means of the key, it interposes, for he distinguishes only whether it can stand without the person. But if it can stand without the person, it implies that it interposes in every case. It also appears that Tosafot and the Mordechai agree with this in law, except that they explain the Mishnah differently, as I will explain. They explain “if the door can stand by itself” to mean without the key, for the reason they wrote, that otherwise it would be a temporary closure. For this reason it appears obvious to me that Tosafot and the Mordechai speak of a key that is not attached to the door, but is now being used to close it temporarily; therefore it is a temporary closure. This is why the Mishnah teaches “and he supported it with a key.” If it were as his words, that it refers to a key attached to the door, it should have said “and he closed it with a key.” But according to the Rambam and according to my explanation it works well, because he is discussing the person, and “supported it” refers to the person, who did not actually close it but supported it. It is possible to find a key that does not receive tumah although it is not attached, such as one lacking completion, meaning it is destined to be smoothed, scraped, inlaid, grooved, or struck with a hammer, as stated at the end of the first chapter of Chullin and in the Rambam at the beginning of chapter 8 of Kelim. But regarding a key attached to the door, which is made to close the door, one certainly cannot say that it is a temporary closure, for it is made for that purpose and is attached there permanently. The door and the lock attached to the door do not receive tumah for this reason, because they are made for the ground, as stated in chapter 11 of Kelim and in the Rambam at the beginning of chapter 9 of Kelim. Therefore, since they are considered like ground, they are considered like the body of the door itself. Accordingly, with hinges in doors and windows, which are made to be attached to the door and are attached to them and considered like ground, one also cannot say that it is a temporary closure. Also, what he wrote from the commentary of the Rash, that if it can stand without the support of the key, the key is as though absent, and if not, it is tamei, because anything supported by a person or vessels does not interpose before tumah, implying from this that the key with which the door is locked receives tumah and does not interpose, etc.; it appears to me that this is not so. Rather, the view of the Rash is that although the key does not receive tumah, since it is a vessel it is not an interposition. See there; this is explicitly proven from his words. This follows the latter explanation that Ri found. It also appears clear to me that when Tosafot wrote, “however, in the Commentary to the Mishnah, Ri found that he explains, etc.,” they mean the Commentary to the Mishnah of Rabbeinu Shimshon; see Tosafot there. Nevertheless, the view of the Mordechai and the Agudah is that even if it is not a vessel at all, it is not an interposition, because we require that it stand specifically by itself, like the first answer of Tosafot. This is also the view of the Rama, and so the Bach wrote. All this is clear; examine carefully.
