Second, the barrier may not depend for support upon an object susceptible to tumah. It is obvious, however, that if one fills a window with enough stones or wood that the window walls themselves hold the material in place, that is a valid barrier and is not called external support. An incident occurred where tumah was present in a house adjacent to a synagogue and could pass through a window of the women's section. One halachic authority instructed that the window be closed with a small curtain that hung there permanently on iron hooks for privacy. I said he erred. Ohalot teaches that a sheet or mat interposes against tumah only when it is made and positioned as an actual tent. If merely stretched flat without sloping sides or walls, it does not interpose. Here the curtain merely covers the window and is neither a wall nor an ohel. Furthermore, the curtain is held by iron rings. Rambam rules that if a door is kept shut by a key or by a person supporting it, the house is pure only if the door can stand closed by itself. If not, the supporting person or utensil is the effective barrier, and people and utensils transmit tumah but do not interpose. Tosafot likewise require the barrier to stand by means of something not susceptible to tumah. The iron rings here function like the key. Even if certain rings are not independently susceptible to tumah when detached from their normal use, when joined to the curtain they clearly function as utensils. There is also reason to say that even a support not susceptible to tumah may fail. Kelim teaches that certain metal door components made for attachment to the ground are not susceptible to tumah. Ohalot nevertheless says that a door supported by a key fails unless the door can stand on its own. Tosafot offer two explanations: either any barrier requiring support is temporary and invalid, or specifically a tumah-receiving support invalidates it. They then cite a commentary holding that even a key not susceptible to tumah invalidates because the barrier is not independently established. Mordechai follows the first explanation and requires the material in the window to stand without any other support. Rama rules this way below. Therefore, a door or window cannot be relied upon as a tumah barrier if it stands only by iron hinges or other iron fittings, even if those fittings are deemed attached to the ground and not susceptible to tumah. According to this, the locking arrangements mentioned later work only where the closure is independently established and does not rely on such supports. One should not rely upon Derishah's leniency of locking the house in order to protect a kohen. The same concern applies to an iron lattice in a window. Since it is iron and not formed as an ohel, it does not create the necessary interposition. Therefore, one should not permit a kohen to remain inside merely by closing the door or window in such a fashion.
