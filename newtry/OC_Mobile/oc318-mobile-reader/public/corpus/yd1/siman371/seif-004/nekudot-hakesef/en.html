(In Taz s.k. 10) It is astonishing regarding the Derishah and regarding him, how they wrote without qualification that the measure of a hole in the cellar door is a handbreadth opening. But we learned in Ohalot chapter 4, Mishnah 3: “A hole in a door has the measure of a full fist,” according to Rabbi Akiva; Rabbi Tarfon says, etc. And we hold that the halachah follows Rabbi Akiva over his colleague. The Rambam ruled likewise in chapter 10 of Hilkhot Tumat Met, law 4. According to their words, one must say that this is specifically with an ordinary hole made originally by a craftsman, but with a hole made for taking things out and bringing them in, which is considered made for use, Rabbi Akiva agrees with Rabbi Tarfon. There is support for this from the words of the Rash and Bartenura there; see there and examine carefully. If so, they should have explained that this hole in the door is specifically a hole made for use. The Rambam there, however, made no distinction in the matter, because he holds that in every case its measure is like a fist, and this requires study.
