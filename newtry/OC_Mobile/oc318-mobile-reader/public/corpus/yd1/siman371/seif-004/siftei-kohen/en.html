A courtyard surrounded, etc. That is, the houses of the courtyard are around the courtyard side, and not on the other side, namely behind the courtyard. Each house has two openings, and likewise windows: one opens to the other side, behind the courtyard, where there are no projections.
If all the openings of the houses and the windows are closed, etc. This is how it should read. It means that when all the doors of the house containing the tumah are closed on every side, both the side open to the courtyard and the side behind the courtyard, then the tumah goes out beneath the projections and porticoes, and everything beneath them is tamei. Since the tumah has no place to leave except by that route, we view the tumah as though it were already there, for the Sages decreed regarding all places through which the tumah will ultimately leave as one, when it is not known by which route it will leave. But it makes no difference whether the other openings of the house are open or closed, for in any event the tumah goes out beneath the projections and porticoes. Alternatively, he teaches us that if the other openings of the house were open, then the open houses would also be tamei, and everything inside them would be tamei, because the tumah goes out beneath the projections since the tumah will ultimately leave, and afterward it goes out from beneath the projections and enters the open houses.
Therefore some are stringent, etc. However, one should not be stringent in the house of tzidduk hadin; on the contrary, that opening comes to make the ohel tamei. So ruled Terumat HaDeshen there. The Ma'adanei Yom Tov, p. 118 at the end of side a, wrote that this reason suffices for a house of tzidduk hadin that has only one opening, through which they bring in and take out the dead. But if it has two openings, through one of which they bring in and through one of which they take out, as exists in the house of tzidduk hadin in the holy community of Prague, one should be stringent that kohanim not enter through the opening from which the dead is taken out.
And if a window or opening was opened on the other side. Behind the courtyard, for through that opening it will leave, since toward the courtyard it is closed.
A window or opening. That has four by four; so it is in the Atzei Zahav, and it is from the words of the Rosh.
And likewise, if tumah is beneath the projections, it enters from beneath them into the house. This is when the house is open.
And likewise roofs that project, so it should read. Maharshal and the later authorities wrote in the name of Responsa Maharam Chaim Or Zarua: in a place where there is an eruv and they made beams whose circumference is a tefach from alley to alley, and the ends of the beams enter beneath the roofs on either side, and the tumah enters beneath them, the beam brings the tumah from roof to roof and it spreads into all the houses on either side. If so, when there is, Heaven forbid, a dead person in one of the houses, the alley is connected by the eruv beams; end quote. The Bach wrote that the beam must be round with a circumference of three tefachim in order to have a width of one tefach, for anything with a circumference of three tefachim has a width of one tefach.
Roofs that project, etc. The Derishah wrote: everything depends on the roof above the entrance of the house in which the dead is located, and from there it comes from the roof before the entrance to the roof of the house. Even if the houses are not adjacent to one another, but there is a wall between them at the side, although there is no small roof over it, if a beam one tefach wide rests on it, then when the tumah comes from the entrance to the roof and the roof surrounds the whole house, the tumah is beneath the roof, which is beneath that wall, and that wall brings the tumah beneath the roof of the house next to it, and it enters inside through openings or holes. But when there is no roof above the entrance of the house in which the dead is located, even if it has a roof on the side and it overshadows the houses adjacent to it on every side, the tumah does not come there. As for what people commonly say, that if it is surrounded by a high wall: that wall makes no difference except regarding the side before the entrance, or if there is no high wall before the entrance of the house in which the dead is located, and there is a wall at the neighboring house on the side before the entrance in such a way that it will not bring the tumah from the roof of the dead to beneath the roof before the entrance of the neighboring house; then even if at the side there is no wall interrupting and the roofs overshadow one another, we have no concern; end quote. But it does not appear so to me. There must be a roof forming an ohel of one tefach from beginning to end; whenever there is an interruption between them by a high wall, even if it is several tefachim thick, since there is no ohel of one tefach, the tumah no longer has a place to go. So many great authorities agreed, and so is the plain custom.
Whose openings are open are tamei. But if they are closed, they are tahor. According to this, even when the tumah comes into some house, if the kohanim intend to close the openings of their houses and their windows and not leave them until after the dead is taken out, they do not need to leave their houses. So wrote the Derishah. According to this, a kohen who is in a house or room that the tumah touches, and it is closed by door and windows, and he learns that the tumah has reached it, is forbidden to leave the room until after the dead is taken out, for as long as the room has not been opened, the tumah has not entered it; and when he opens it, the tumah will enter it.
And if they were one above the other, etc. The Bach wrote that even if the upper roof was much more than three tefachim higher, since its roof has a tefach we say chavot rami, etc.; see there.
The kohanim cannot, etc. The Bach wrote: it appears to me that if it is a miscarriage, where there is no concern for honor, we compel him to remove it from its place so that they may enter their house.
