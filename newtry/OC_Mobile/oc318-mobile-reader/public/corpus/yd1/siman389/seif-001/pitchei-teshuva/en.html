And the custom is that another person. See Responsa Panim Meirot II, siman 138, by my ancestor, who writes that one upon whom two mourning periods overlap may wear on Shabbat a garment washed with ash and water even during the seven days, provided another person first wears it for an hour.
After seven days. See Responsa Lachmei Todah, siman 30, who concludes that the requirement to wait until after seven days applies only when changing clothes for pleasure. If he changes because of need, such as where the shirt is soiled or causes significant discomfort, it is permitted even on a weekday during the seven days if another person first wears it. This is analogous to bathing, which is prohibited only when done for pleasure, as explained above in siman 381. The fact that the poskim do not state this explicitly is only because it is so obvious, being learned directly from the laws of bathing and anointing. See there at length and what I wrote above, siman 381, seif katan 2.
