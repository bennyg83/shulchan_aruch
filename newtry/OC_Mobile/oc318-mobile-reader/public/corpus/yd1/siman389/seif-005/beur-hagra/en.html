(Likkut) Seif 5 and seif 7: all, etc.; after seven, etc. Evel Rabbati chapter 7 says: thirty days for laundering; how so, etc.? It is permitted to send out, etc.
(Likkut) All, etc. The Raavad explained that even new garments are prohibited specifically when they come from the press, and that this condition applies to both old and new; see the Rosh there, s.k. But the Ramban disagreed, for if so it should have said, "provided they come out," etc. Since it says "old garments that come out," it implies that this condition refers to old garments. This is why the words of Rabbi and Rabbi Elazar bar Rabbi Shimon do not mention "coming out from under the press," for according to them the issue applies specifically to new garments.
(Likkut) And this is, etc. See Tosafot 23a, s.v. Rava, etc., and the ruling, etc.
And even if it is, etc.; laundering, some say, etc.; there is one who says, etc. See all this in Orach Chayim siman 551 and what I wrote there.
(Likkut) And even if it is, etc. What is stated in Taanit 29b, that linen garments are not subject to laundering, was said of old garments, because the sweat and dirt do not leave them completely. That is unlike here, where the garments are new. In the Yerushalmi, Moed Katan chapter 3: what is laundering? New woolen garments and pressed white linen garments. We learn that even linen garments are subject to the rule of laundering. So too we say in chapter Arvei Pesachim, 109a, regarding pressed linen garments. Nevertheless, we rule like our Gemara, that even woolen garments are prohibited specifically when they are white.
And for his father, etc. Although in the Yerushalmi at the end of Moed Katan it says twelve months, we rely on what is stated in Evel Rabbati chapter 9: until the festival arrives and they rebuke him, etc., as stated in the Gemara 22b regarding haircutting. They have one law, except that with laundering, if no festival occurs, his friends do not recognize it so as to rebuke him, unlike haircutting. This is from Torat HaAdam, unlike the Rambam, who ruled like the plain Gemara that regarding laundering they made no distinction at all, and ruled that even for one's father and mother it is only until thirty days; see there.
