For it is forbidden to wear. See Responsa Panim Meirot II, siman 123, by my ancestor, concerning a woman within thirty days of mourning who had recently given birth and reached the customary day to go to synagogue. That day was treated locally like her personal Yom Tov, and women wore valuable garments and gold ornaments. He ruled that on that Shabbat she may wear Shabbat clothing, but not festive Yom Tov clothing, so that her mind not become elevated to the point of forgetting her mourning. She also need not change her usual seat; see above, siman 353:4. Responsa Shevut Yaakov II, siman 102, was asked about a woman within mourning whose son had a pidyon haben, where women customarily wear their important garments for the ceremony. He replied that because several authorities hold that "shehasimchah bime'ono" is recited at such a meal, the occasion is for her comparable to a festival. At least during the pidyon she may therefore wear Shabbat clothing; see there.
