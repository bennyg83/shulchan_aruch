Male eggs — as long as the animal had not reached thirty days, they are permitted for a pot without peeling, even if they have something like red veins; and if it had thirty days, if they have something like red veins they are forbidden for a pot without peeling, but for roasting they are permitted: {Rama: And it is customary to devein the eggs even when they are less than thirty days, and likewise to remove from there the large sinew and to cut them into many pieces (Or Zarua and the Long). And it is customary to take from the shoulder the meat called "drize" (the order of deveining) — and there is no prohibition in it, only disgust. Furthermore, it is customary in Lombardy, which they call "lidwitz," to roast it and not to cook it — and there is no prohibition if one cooks it, and even l'chatchila it is permitted (Beit Yosef and the Long). And likewise it is customary to remove the median membrane and discard it and not eat it — and there is no prohibition in the matter (Or Zarua and the Long). And likewise they are accustomed to cut between the hooves of an animal and remove from there thin white meat because of disgust; and if one did not do so, there is no concern (in my humble opinion):}
