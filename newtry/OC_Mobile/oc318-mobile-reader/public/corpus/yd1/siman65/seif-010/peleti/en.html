Gid hanasheh is permitted for benefit — so too Rambam; and the sages of Lunel challenged him: since he ruled like R' Elazar, as there — it is said "you shall not eat," even prohibition of benefit; and the Gemara says in Pesachim daf 22: therefore the sinew is permitted — for when nevelah was permitted, it and its sinew were permitted; and the Gemara challenges: it works out according to the one who holds there is taste in sinews, but according to the one who holds there is no taste — from where? And the Gemara says: whom did you hear — there is no taste in sinews? R' Shimon, for we learned: one who eats the sinew of an impure animal — R' Shimon exempts; R' Shimon truly holds the sinew is forbidden for benefit, end of his words; and therefore we, who establish there is no taste in sinews, should forbid it in a mixture of one in a hundred; and this difficulty the Rosh brought in chapter Gid HaNasheh, and how many exertions did bearers of Rambam's vessels make to settle it. And one must say: apparently, what is this difficulty — perhaps Rambam's difficulty is one prohibition of eating and one prohibition of benefit are included; and we establish there is no taste in sinews — perforce one must say that he holds like one change in the Gemara: the anonymous mishnah "a person sends a thigh" implies it is permitted for benefit; and he holds like R' Elazar, a Torah prohibition in Pesachim daf 23b — regarding milk we certainly say: when nevelah was permitted, it and its milk were permitted; and there is also a verse that milk is permitted for benefit; and if so, there is an a fortiori — what is milk, for which karet is punishable, yet permitted for benefit, the sinew for which there is no karet is certainly permitted for benefit; and so one may say the Rosh's difficulty in chapter Gid HaNasheh — why did he rule like R' Elazar in the place of Chizkiyah? And if we say that R' Yishmael and R' Akiva both hold that prohibition of eating and prohibition of benefit are included — if so, the opposite should be needed: all hold only eating is included, for R' Yishmael said explicitly: were it not for a verse, milk would be forbidden for benefit; and according to the above one may settle it — for Tosafot wrote on daf 113 that even for Chizkiyah R' Shimon holds the sinew is forbidden for benefit, for it is not possible "you shall not eat" on prohibition of eating — it is wood, etc.; and if so, R' Yishmael also holds thus; and if so, were it not that Scripture permitted milk explicitly, we would derive an a fortiori in reverse — what is the sinew for which karet is not punishable yet forbidden for benefit, all the more so milk for which karet is punishable that it is forbidden for benefit; and therefore a verse was needed for milk to permit; and now that we heard permission in milk, one may say the a fortiori in reverse that the sinew is permitted even though it is written "he shall not eat," etc. (see Maharsha in Pesachim daf 23b in his comment "for it is different," etc., who challenged — behold R' Yishmael perforce holds like R' Elazar; and according to what I wrote, it is fine that one may say R' Yishmael holds like Chizkiyah). And one must say that the essential point is like Tosafot's view in his comment "and what," and the verse that they challenged — many prohibitions of benefit for which there is no karet; and explain: thus the a fortiori — what is milk in which there is karet, nevertheless it is in the category of nevelah permission — "it and its milk were permitted," all the more so the sinew which is in the category of nevelah permission; and if so, one cannot say to Rambam this a fortiori, since we establish there is no taste in sinews — if so, what is milk that is in the category of nevelah permission, for it has taste and the name nevelah applies to it, not so the sinew in which there is no taste — the name nevelah is not relevant; and also in reverse one cannot say: what is the sinew for which karet is not punishable and is not in the category of permission — all the more so milk; therefore it is not in the category, since it is not in the category of nevelah, for there is no taste in it; and according to this we stand here. (And one should not challenge in the Gemara that it rejects the a fortiori of milk that was permitted from its entirety with a wild animal, and we do not derive an a fortiori from blood that is punishable by karet yet permitted for benefit, etc. — one may say: blood has permission for its prohibition — blood in its cooking, not so the sinew; and requires further study.) And if so, the difficulty of the sages of Lunel returns to its place; and what he strained in the notes where Rambam ruled in Hilchot Maachalot Assurot chapter 8 halacha 6 — one who eats the sinew of nevelah and of an olah is liable twice; and in halacha 5 he wrote — one who eats the sinew of an impure animal is entirely exempt, for the sinew is not in impurity, since its flesh is forbidden and there is no prohibition of impurity, for there is no taste in sinews; and it is difficult from this — and he asked: why for nevelah and olah is one liable? Here there is no prohibition of nevelah and olah, for there is no taste in sinews; and Rashba added to challenge in a responsum — for we say explicitly in the Gemara, beginning of chapter Gid HaNasheh, we say: gid hanasheh applies to consecrated animals — obvious, etc.; and you would say: there is no taste in sinews, nevertheless there is prohibition of the sinew, and there is no prohibition of consecrated animals, etc. — behold it is clear that when there is no taste in sinews one is not liable because of consecrated animals, yet he rules that for olah one is liable twice — requires further study. And see Kesef Mishneh who also found no answer. Therefore it appears that Rambam holds: even though there is no taste in sinews, this is only regarding eating — it is not called eating; but benefit is called — at any rate one benefits from the sinew; and see Tosafot Pesachim that I brought above, who hold that therefore we do not expound "he shall not eat" for prohibition of eating — it is not relevant, only for prohibition of benefit; and if so, it is difficult for him that which we say: one who eats of nevelah is liable twice, and one is liable only once; and they agree regarding one who eats of olah and of an ir hanidachat that he is liable twice; and the Gemara toiled to establish the good dispute as inclusive and a severe prohibition, etc.; and one should say: here the first tanna holds there is taste in sinews and therefore he is liable; and the Sages hold there is no taste in sinews and therefore for nevelah he is not liable, but for olah and ir hanidachat, which are prohibitions of benefit, nevertheless there is liability for lashes; and see Mishneh Lamelech, Hilchot Yesodei HaTorah — he wrote explicitly for Rambam: one who benefits from prohibitions of benefit in the manner of his benefit receives lashes; therefore Rambam holds this case of one who eats the sinew of nevelah — according to the halacha that there is no taste in sinews it was stated; and nevertheless, since he is liable because of eating the sinew even though it is not fit, and the Torah considered it eating — even regarding nevelah it considered it; and how can one who eats a sinew that is nevelah be said: regarding the sinew it is eating and regarding nevelah it is not eating — and it would be two opposites on one subject; therefore he is also liable because of nevelah, for the Torah considered that it should be called eating regarding the sinew; therefore the sinew of an impure animal is exempt — there is nothing because of the sinew, for its flesh is forbidden; and since there is nothing because of the sinew, also because of impurity he is exempt, for there is no taste, and the Merciful One did not consider it regarding the sinew that it should be called eating — there he is not liable at all because of the sinew; and requires further study. However, according to this one should analyze that which the Gemara says in Pesachim: if there is no taste in sinews, we do not say "when nevelah was permitted, it and its sinew were permitted"; and it is difficult: why not — behold one who eats the sinew of nevelah receives lashes for "you shall not eat nevelah" as well; and if so, when permitted for benefit, the sinew too was permitted, for it is in the category of the Torah prohibition of nevelah; and it appears that in any case one should analyze that which it says: who is the tanna who holds there is no taste in sinews? R' Shimon — R' Shimon holds the sinew is forbidden for benefit; and it is difficult — behold there is R' Yehuda ben Bava, who holds there is no taste in sinews, and we rule like him. Therefore it appears that apparently it is difficult: why specifically R' Shimon who holds no taste — even if there is taste, behold R' Shimon holds that prohibition does not take effect on a prohibition in any manner; if so, nevelah never took effect on the sinew, and one who eats the sinew of nevelah is not lashed because of nevelah; and how can it be "when nevelah was permitted, its sinew was permitted" — behold there is no prohibition of nevelah on the sinew here; and how can it be considered in the category of permission? And one must say: what of it that there are no lashes because of nevelah — nevertheless the sinew in itself is nevelah, and when one eats it one eats nevelah, and it works out well "when nevelah was permitted, it and its sinew were permitted"; but this is if there is taste in sinews; but if there is no taste in it, then in any case in itself there is no nevelah here, for there is no taste — only that we say: nevertheless one who eats it is liable because of nevelah, for the Merciful One considered it eating; even when nevelah was permitted, the sinew, etc. — this is not so for R' Shimon, who holds prohibition does not take effect on prohibition; and if so, he is not lashed because of nevelah, and also in itself it is not nevelah, for there is no taste in it — it is obvious that in no way is "when it was permitted," etc. relevant. And requires further study for R' Yehuda ben Bava — even though he holds there is no taste in sinews, nevertheless one may say "when it was permitted," etc., for one who eats the sinew of nevelah is liable because of nevelah, as the Merciful One considered it, as above; and if so, it applies well "when it was permitted," etc.; but for R' Shimon, who holds prohibition does not take effect on prohibition — if so, one who eats the sinew of nevelah is not liable because of nevelah, and in the sinew itself there is no nevelah, for there is no taste; if so, there is no way to say "when it was permitted," etc.; and therefore for R' Shimon we say it is forbidden; but according to what we establish that prohibition takes effect on prohibition, as Rambam wrote — one who eats the sinew of nevelah is liable twice — if so, it is obvious to say "when it was permitted," etc.; and therefore he ruled permitted for benefit; and Rambam's words are clear and precise in the Gemara that cites R' Shimon and not R' Yehuda ben Bava; and requires further study. And still one must settle the difficulty: Rashba — that which the Gemara says: there is no taste in sinews and prohibition of the sinew exists and prohibition of consecrated animals does not exist — and it appears that there, beginning of chapter Gid HaNasheh, in his comment "but," they challenged: how can it be thought there is no taste in sinews — how does it teach it does not apply to impure animals, for prohibition of the sinew does not take effect on impurity — behold there is no prohibition of impurity here, for there is no taste in sinews; and it appears to me to settle it: Tosafot in his comment "apparently" wrote in the name of Rashi that initially the substance of the sinew was flesh, and in the end it hardened and whitened and became a sinew; and in the Gemara in Avodah Zarah there is a dispute whether we require nevelah fit for a ger or a dog — to exclude putrefaction from the outset; initially it was not fit for a ger — according to all it is permitted; and this is what they disputed whether there is taste in sinews — Rashba wrote that there is weak taste in it, and they disputed whether it is considered full taste or not; and if so, it is clear that for a dog it is fit; and as Tosafot wrote that they throw to cats; and therefore one may say: that which one eats the sinew of an impure animal and is liable because of impurity — not because there is taste in sinews, but initially before it became a sinew and hardened it was flesh; if so, prohibition of impurity took effect on it; and if it hardened and became wood, nevertheless it is fit for a dog, as above; and nevelah that was initially nevelah and afterward became disqualified must become unfit for a dog's eating — this is not so with the sinew; therefore it is forbidden because of impurity; but the sinew itself, when prohibition took effect, had already hardened and is putrefaction from the outset — we only say it is wood and the Torah forbade it; therefore from "it does not apply to impurity" there is no proof that there is taste in sinews; but at the beginning of chapter Gid HaNasheh, where it says that prohibition of consecrated animals takes effect when he consecrated it after the sinew was already made and it is wood — it is putrefaction from the outset and does not apply to consecrated animals; and if so {and it is possible Tosafot did not challenge except those who do not hold like Rashi above}: And now also Rambam's words above come out correctly — Rambam holds the view that since the tannaim disputed whether there is taste or not, this is only weak taste, and they disputed whether it is called taste; and certainly from the beginning when it formed in its mother's womb, while still soft, there is certainly taste in it, and reality testifies; and also a distant dispute — for this there would be taste even in adulthood, and for that there would be none from the beginning of formation — we do not say; and requires further study: that which we say it is wood and the Torah forbade it — this is a decree of Scripture; apparently therefore only when it formed there was taste in it and prohibition of the sinew took effect; and if so, afterward when it spoiled and hardened, nevertheless we require fit for a dog and it is forbidden; and if so, also for one who eats nevelah — regarding nevelah it is putrefaction from the outset, it is permitted, and one cannot say the Merciful One considered it regarding the sinew, for the sinew is different — from the outset it was nevelah automatically; only this is according to the one who holds fit for a dog; but Rambam ruled fit for a ger {and even though R' Meir said the sinew of nevelah, and R' Meir holds fit for a dog — R' Meir truly holds there is taste in sinews, and we rule like R' Meir, not for his reason that we require fit for a ger}; and if so, also regarding the sinew the prohibition ceased when it became wood, and one must say the Merciful One considered it, as above; and therefore he ruled one who eats of nevelah and olah is liable twice, for the Merciful One considered it, as above — not so at the beginning of chapter Gid HaNasheh, where the Gemara toiled to find an establishment to settle it, and the Gemara says: if we say there is no taste in sinews, and it comes like this tanna — fit for a dog, therefore prohibition of the sinew exists and prohibition of consecrated animals does not exist, etc.; and requires further study; and like the detail as R' Meir, who in the anonymous mishnah holds fit for a dog; and requires further study.
