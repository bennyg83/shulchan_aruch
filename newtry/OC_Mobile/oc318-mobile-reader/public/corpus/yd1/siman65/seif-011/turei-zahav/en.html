And tells him that it is kosher. This entire seif is the words of Rambam, except "and tells him that it is kosher" — that is the language of Maggid Mishneh, as he wrote, and these are his words: it appears to me that Rabbeinu Rambam holds that here it is explicit that the thigh is from kosher meat; and in this manner there is concern that perhaps the Israelite who sees is the one who gave it to him with the presumption that it is kosher, and it is cut, and he thinks it is certainly kosher — for if not, the Israelite would not deceive him, for it is forbidden; and he also thinks that its sinew was removed because it is cut, and he will come to sell from it and eat it with the sinew; and Rabbeinu already wrote above that meat found in the hand of a non-Jew is forbidden everywhere; therefore there is concern only in this manner; and in such a manner it is permitted to sell them nevelot and tereifot, end of his words. Here he needed this from the fact that it was difficult for him: behold, no Israelite will buy from the non-Jew — so why not give it to him in the presence of an Israelite? And one must say that he does not hold that deveining of the sinew is a sign; for if not, he would certainly buy from him by force of the seif that when there is a sign, Rambam agrees that one buys meat from a non-Jew, as he wrote siman 63; but according to the Tur, who holds it is a sign, one needs a reason to distinguish between in the presence of an Israelite and not in his presence; and the reason is explained in Tosafot, chapter Gid HaNasheh — in a place where one does not buy meat from non-Jews because of tereifot, this sign does not help to buy deveining cuts from him, lest we say: after it was deveined it became known to the Israelite that it was tereifah and therefore he gave it to the non-Jew; and they challenged this: why is it not forbidden to send a thigh to one's fellow through a non-Jew — perhaps a thigh happened to the non-Jew because it was not known to the Israelite that it was tereifah until after the sinew was removed, and he exchanged it for this; and they answered: all this we are not concerned for, for it is not common that such should happen by a non-Jew; but where an Israelite sends to a non-Jew it does not help even if the sinew was removed, for we say: for this reason he sends it to the non-Jew — because it became known to him after the deveining that it is tereifah, end of his words; and thereby the distinction stated above is explained — that anything given not in the presence of an Israelite, an Israelite should not buy from him, lest the tereifah became known after the deveining and therefore he gave it to the non-Jew; but in his presence there is concern that the seeing Israelite will know that this animal is truly kosher and see the cut of deveining and err, saying the sinew was removed; therefore one must remove the sinew before giving to the non-Jew.
