See in responsa Shevut Yaakov part 2 siman 58, that they asked about what some rabbis instituted in their community to announce that all meat brought from a certain country is as if one eats tereifah meat, and to forbid even the vessels in which the meat was cooked — whether they acted well; and he elaborated with proofs from the Talmud and poskim that it is not proper to do so, lest there be hatred; and further that it is like a decree the majority of the public cannot withstand — for what will travelers do? But nevertheless it is incumbent on every rav and teacher to appoint kosher God-fearing people over shechitah; also it is fit to investigate the slaughterer before eating; and what should be done if the slaughterer transgresses in rebellion and above the collar depends on the neck of that slaughterer, and all Israel are clean, and no iniquity shall befall the righteous; but to forbid altogether the meat of the people of the country and villages is not proper, see there. [See in responsa Chavot Yair siman 5, and regarding the officials and leaders of one city that since imposed a tax on every liter of meat and leased that income to known lessees — and since the people broke out to take meat from the villages in secret and did not pay the obligation — therefore the community consulted to forbid publicly in the synagogue that slaughterers from outside their city, even though slaughtered by the town slaughterer with kashrut, nevertheless they agreed to forbid; but the rav av beit din of that city did not agree to this agreement, and also a few individuals protested the matter; nevertheless they did it and announced the above prohibition, and it was asked whether there is substance to this announcement. And he elaborated on this and concludes: even though it is clear in Choshen Mishpat siman 238 s.k. 8 that where there is an important sage appointed over the public, their condition is not effective without the sage's knowledge — all this is in an enactment that is only an enactment of the public and not fencing a forbidden matter; but where the enactment is to fence a forbidden matter, certainly the officials of the community can and are obligated to stand before transgressors, and we do not need a city member; and even if individuals protest explicitly, the enactment is imposed on them against their will, for also Rashi in Mordechai Bava Batra siman 474 does not disagree with Raavyah there. And since so in our case — the lessees leased from the community the meat tax and are like an Israelite tax collector by law, and one who steals from them is a full robber — if so, the community is obligated to fence before the transgressors, and their strength in their enactment should be approved, and they do not need a city member, and one should not heed individuals who protested the enactment, and even the majority should not be heeded, for it is obvious the robbers will erase them; therefore, apparently, the prohibition the community forbade is a full prohibition, and one may not breach their fence. However, it appears to me that this is if they did not take counsel from the great one of the city and Torah scholar; but since they consulted with him and the enactment was against his will — if so, presumably he knew and understood that it is not proper to enact that enactment, perhaps he feared obstacles would multiply and they would eat meat of carcasses and nevelot; and for this reason Yehoshua was punished through Achan's cherem [or for the reason of Shevut Yaakov above] — be that as it may, since the rav av beit din did not agree and sages have power to uproot something from the Torah in a temporary hour; and if you ask, it appears like lawlessness in what they announced prohibition in the synagogue — and even for Raavyah, who holds that initially the officials were appointed for all they would do all year — if the majority is silent, nevertheless the Torah-scholar officer was appointed from all the public before them, and again they were appointed on condition to heed his voice, and all they did against his will has no substance, and the humble shall eat and be satisfied, see there]:
