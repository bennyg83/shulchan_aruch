And the membrane on the brain. In the Tur it is written: and if one roasted them and did not cut them and did not leave them on the slaughtering spot — the membrane is forbidden and the brain is permitted; and Beit Yosef wondered — behold, in siman 68 the Tur wrote that even the brain is forbidden; and he answered: here it deals with where one removed the brain with its membrane and roasted it by the fire, and therefore the brain was not forbidden; and below it deals with where the head is whole and the blood collects in the pots of the brain, and it is found that the brain cooks inside it; and even though his words are astonishing from the language of the Tur, which wrote here "and did not leave it on the slaughtering spot" — implying that it deals with where one did not remove the brain — nevertheless I copied his words to learn from them another law, namely that it is permitted to roast liver when it is wrapped in paper on the fire, and we do not say that the paper interposes between the liver and the fire and it is like cooking; for here he permits roasting the brain in its membrane on the fire. And regarding Beit Yosef's difficulty — the Tur's words contradict each other — there are answers for some commentators but they have no foundation; and the essential point is as Maharal of Prague wrote: the essential text here is that the brain is forbidden and the head is permitted. In the Tur it is written: and it is a stringency that he was stringent, etc. — implying that it does not refer to Rambam, as Beit Yosef wrote, since he did not say "they were stringent" in plural; and it is difficult: what is the reason? Indeed he did not write thus regarding Rambam, for he too considered what was not mentioned in the Gemara; and it appears that Rambam spoke only of those forbidden due to blood; and it is possible that it does not refer to salting, but rather that without salting it is forbidden — excluding intestines, which he wrote afterward; but in the words of the Gaon one cannot say thus, for he required cutting specifically — implying that salting does not suffice:
And for roasting, peeling suffices. Above siman 22 implies that peeling is a mere stringency; and Rashal ruled that one does not need peeling at all in salting or roasting. And in the Tur it is written in the name of the Gaonim that even l'chatchila it is permitted to roast without cutting.
