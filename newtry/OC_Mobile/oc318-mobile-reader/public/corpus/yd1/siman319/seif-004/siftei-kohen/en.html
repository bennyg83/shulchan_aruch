The majority emerged, etc. See above siman 14 se’if 3 and what I wrote there.
And it is forbidden to sell it to a non-Jew, etc. For perhaps it is considered as born.
And the one that comes after it, etc. This is the language of the Rambam, and so it is in the Tur. According to the Rambam it follows simply — for perhaps it was not considered as born, and he speaks of a case where the half that was inside in the majority of limbs came out by cutting; therefore even if it later emerged completely it does not exempt the one that comes after it, since it emerged via cutting. This is as stated above in seif 1. But according to the Tur who holds that even if he cut it limb by limb it exempts the one after it — since it is still considered as opening the womb — one must say the case here is where the one remaining inside did not subsequently emerge completely, but rather its other half emerged via a cesarean section; or for example where there were twins, and first one half of the first twin emerged in the majority of a limb, then it went back in, and then the second one emerged, and then the first emerged completely — the second is not exempted by the first having initially emerged half in the majority of a limb. But if after the first half emerged in the majority of a limb the complete one subsequently emerged, and afterward she gave birth to another — it is obviously exempt from the firstborn, since regardless, afterward the complete one was born. This is straightforward.
