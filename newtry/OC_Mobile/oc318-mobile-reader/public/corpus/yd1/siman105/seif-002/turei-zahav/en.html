And it is fitting to be concerned to be cautious, etc. And Rashal in the chapter Kol HaBasar siman 71 wrote, and these are his words: to rule the law — although a kli sheni does not cook, even if yad soledes bo in it, and also does not emit and absorb at once, such as forbidden matter already forbidden and permitted matter that came in a kli sheni — they are not forbidden one from another; but forbidden matter that fell into a kli sheni of permitted, or permitted into issur — it properly absorbs and forbids through all of it, since the yad soledes bo in it like in a kli rishon, and all the more in iruy — end of his words. And so it appears to me to explain Tur's words, in what he challenged Rashba here — for I saw various explanations and my mind was not settled on what Tur wrote "heat into heat," for who mentions his name, except in this explanation of Tur, who inferred from Rashba's wording that he wrote "but any heat by fire emits," even if the yad does not soledet bo in it — for he included in kli sheni all its heat in one category to be stringent; if so, as the more severe law in it — namely hot into hot — so should the lighter law be — namely the yad does not soledet bo in it — and in all of them it should be forbidden through all of it; and with this he wrote there is no proof from heat of the slaughterhouse, for there it forbids only as much as a peel — rather necessarily that which Rashba said, that all laws of kli sheni are equal — meaning he holds also in hot into hot, which is more severe, a peel suffices; and on this he directed and said that also when the yad does not soledet bo in it, there is this issur; and on this he said he saw some authors write in kli sheni that the law is thus; and with this Tur's wonder on Beit Yosef and on this is resolved; and Tur said: in truth this is not in kli sheni, even cold into hot, only that the yad soledes bo in it — forbidden in all of them, and all the more hot into hot; and its law is regarding emitting from what is inside to absorb into another, like a kli rishon — even though regarding cooking there is a distinction between kli rishon and kli sheni, as written in Orach Chayyim siman 318 — nevertheless regarding absorbing and emitting there is no distinction; if so, there is a distinction in kli sheni, and Rashba should not have included all his laws in one rule, as appears to me — explanation of his words; and from this emerges that even in a kli sheni in which the yad soledes bo, it forbids all of it, as written in the name of Rashal, and likewise Beit Yosef — Tur's words in conclusion, as I wrote; and if it is hot so the hand is burned in it — then it forbids like cooked — meaning even in a kli sheni; and according to this, all the stringencies mentioned in siman 94 regarding ben yomo apply also in kli sheni, and not as Rama wrote there at the beginning of the siman — see there Orach Chayyim: if the spoon is ben yomo, meaning it was used within me'eit l'eit in a kli rishon. And for practical law one should rule stringently like Rashal, especially since Tur also holds thus — only in a place of great loss and an important matter one may rely on Beit Yosef in siman 68 and Rama in seif 3 here to be lenient b'dieved in kli sheni that it does not emit and absorb at all, even if the yad soledes bo in it; and if the yad does not soledet bo in it, one needs only rinsing, as appears to me; and see further what I wrote in siman 94 seif 14 in the name of Rashal, that in a thick item such as a hot piece of meat, its law is literally like a kli rishon — and what I wrote on it there.
