Heat of a kli rishon, etc. See Shach, who elaborated in s.k. 5 on the laws of iruy and rishon. First Shach's view was stated, that Rabbeinu Tam's view is established stringently, that iruy cooks as much as a peel, and even if boiling stopped, nevertheless it absorbs, as proven in chapter Keitzad; and that which Rama wrote above siman 68, that when boiling stopped it is permitted to pour on the chicken — meaning it does not emit and absorb at once; and I did not understand his words, for behold Rama above in siman 92 seif 7 in the hagahah wrote for practical law, and it is from responsum of Maharam: if boiling went from the pot and boiling stopped — it is not iruy, and even a peel is not required — and this is not like Shach. And it is clear Shach intended that which we say there: hot meat into cold milk requires a peel; and likewise he challenges from dripping from gravy, etc. — behold all boiling stopped; but still it did not help: if we say that sugya that teaches one peels means not from the law of iruy, nevertheless a peel is required, for it does not cook, only absorbs — if so, from where do we further say according to Rabbeinu Tam's views that iruy cooks and nevertheless does not cook more than as much as a peel — behold we did not find mention of this; and on the proof of hot into cold it is difficult to distinguish to say it cooks and nevertheless only as much as a peel — and all the strain is from that which we establish tattei gavar, and nevertheless a peel is required, as Tosafot wrote in many places; and if we say there it is not iruy when boiling stopped — if so, from where do we have this; and as I wrote, in truth Tosafot in Zevachim, as they descended, distinguish between boiling stopped — and they establish that in Pesachim when boiling stopped they ruled that iruy cooks entirely — see there. And also according to Rabbeinu Tam and his supporters there is no distinction between absorbed and cooked, for what cooks absorbs, and what does not cook does not absorb, as appears explicitly from Tosafot in Zevachim and likewise Rashba daf 92, who brings proof from the Yerushalmi that what cooks absorbs — and he who holds all the more absorbs needed to enter strained explanations — see there; and according to Shach's views, even for Rabbeinu Tam when boiling stopped it must be that it absorbs — and this is impossible for Rabbeinu Tam, for according to him there is no distinction between absorbed and cooked; and on Shach's proof from hot into cold I already wrote above in siman 92 that one should say it deals with a solid item; and from dripping from gravy — in truth difficult for Shmuel, and for Rav in truth there is no difference between boiling stopped and not stopped — and so too must be according to Shach's views, for in the Gemara we say for Rav from dripping from gravy, etc., that it heats the earthenware and the earthenware drips to the gravy and it is roasted because of something else — it is clear that it also cooks and not only absorbs; and one must say also according to his view a difference between Rav and Shmuel. However it is difficult to me: from where do we have Maharam's distinction and to be lenient between boiling stopped and not stopped; and as I wrote, all Tosafot's strain was to say for Rabbeinu Tam iruy cooks entirely; and see there the chapter of challah and hag'alah; but according to what is established for me that iruy does not cook more than as much as a peel — and that is as Tosafot wrote from Shmuel — if so, from where are we further lenient between stopped and not stopped; therefore it arose in my mind that this is difficult for Maharam for Rav, who said ila'i gavar and cooks — if so, how do you find a kli sheni mentioned everywhere: if the vessel was cold and he put boiling water from a kli rishon hot — thus ila'i gavar and it becomes for him a kli rishon like Shmuel, who put cold into boiling from a kli rishon — tattei gavar. And strained to say: if that boiling water he put in a cold vessel and the water grew strong and the law of hot is upon them like ila'i, he again poured into a cold vessel — so much we do not say ila'i gavar, and we judge it as kli sheni — this is strained; and also difficult is the mishnah that teaches: into the bowl it is permitted to put spices — behold in the bowl one pours from a kli rishon; and for Rav, who holds ila'i gavar — behold it cooks entirely, as we say regarding dripping from gravy that it heats so much to absorb because the vessel returns and cooks it; and if so, behold here a kli rishon entirely; and therefore Maharam holds: since he holds for Rav ila'i gavar means without boiling stopped; and that which is permitted to pour into the bowl — that is when boiling stopped; if so, we find even for Rav kli sheni. Rather this question from putting into the bowl, etc., is still difficult for Tosafot in Zevachim, who hold Rav and Shmuel dispute when boiling stopped, and nevertheless Rav holds ila'i gavar — if so, the question is in its place for Rav: how does he put into the bowl and ladle; and it would appear Tosafot should say: even though Rav holds ila'i gavar, nevertheless with spices it is different, for R' Yehuda holds even in a kli rishon they are not cooked — if so, even for the rabbis it is not cooked in a kli sheni, even though ila'i gavar — see there. But Maharam does not hold this distinction to divide, and therefore he needed to distinguish between boiling stopped and not stopped; and if so, requires further study, for even for Rav there is a difference — the question from dripping from gravy, etc., is in its place: how do we say for Rav it is resolved — behold when boiling stopped Rav concedes. Therefore it appears to distinguish: there is a difference between stopped boiling — meaning in something cooked in a bowl and a vessel that interrupts between it and the fire, and the walls of the vessel preserve the heat — therefore when one pours and boiling stops, the vessel's heat from the fire ceases and it has no power to cook; but roasting at the fire itself absorbs power and heat of fire — and if so, wherever its heat is disconnected in its interior from heat of fire, it is like moving a kli rishon from place to place; and if so, there on Pesach, which is roasted, it is as if boiling did not stop, for always on the gravy roasted in fire there is power of fire; and if it separates from the meat, it is like two first pots separated from one another with power of fire in it — and this is clear and correct. And if so, also hot that fell into cold one may explain thus — heat of roasting; except if so, he should have explained, and the rabbi should not have concealed hot that fell into cold, that it deals with hot roasted — requires further study. And it is possible this is Rama's intent: one must say adameikar absorbed — a peel is required — this is a general stringency, that it deals with when boiling stopped. However Tosafot and Rosh, who challenged in Shabbos daf 2: one puts hot into cold and not cold into hot — Rashi explained because tattei gavar; and Tosafot, Rashba, and Rosh challenged: if so, difficult for Rav, who holds ila'i gavar, and likewise for Shmuel — behold a peel is required; and they entered strained explanations; and it is difficult: what is the question — perhaps it deals with boiling stopped; rather we learn that even when boiling stopped they hold they dispute; and likewise it appears from many places; and Tosafot and Ran, who wrote thus — that is, Rabbeinu Tam holds without boiling stopped it cooks entirely, as appears from their wording; but according to what is established for us that it cooks only as much as a peel — there is no proof for boiling stopped, and we did not find it for Maharam literally. And likewise one must say in Ran: he distinguishes between boiling stopped and not stopped, and nevertheless he challenged in hot into cold — Tosafot's question — and did not answer that it deals with stopped — and it is clear. And know that above many poskim hold: if it cooked in part of a vessel, hag'alah is required in the entire vessel; in iruy we do not say thus — for from the cutting board it spreads along the length of the vessel and not in the thickness of the vessel until it is forbidden only as much as a peel, or in iruy it does not spread at all — examine. And behold in siman 68 Shach brought Mahariv's view, whom Beit Yosef and Rama followed, that a copper vessel does not absorb unless fire passes underneath; and Shach wondered: behold the mishnah teaches: one who cooked in it and one who poured boiling into it requires purging and rinsing — and therefore in a copper vessel; if earthenware, breaking is required. However because of this it does not apply that one may always say it deals in every case with earthenware, and according to Rambam's views in explaining the mishnah and in his composition, that which in every case requires breaking is in a chatat; but in other kodshim, even in copper, only purging and rinsing; and this mishnah is all in every case and in other kodshim, whose law in every case is purging and rinsing; and in truth Rambam's words are wondrous, as is known, and as I wrote in many places. And the rabbi Ba'er HaZev wrote that Rambam has proof from the Yerushalmi that Semag brought in the laws of hag'alah — and I did not see proof from there as it appears. But from that Yerushalmi in the chapter HaKirah there is proof, for it says that iruy never cooks, only as much as it absorbs; and it challenges this: a baraita teaches: even in a copper vessel thus; and you can say, etc.; and it is difficult: why bring proof from a baraita that teaches even in copper thus; and likewise the first answer, that it means in every case — how was the mishnah hidden from him that plainly teaches purging and rinsing are required — and therefore in copper, if in every case breaking is required; and why challenge from a baraita — and therefore the mishnah is unnecessary; one may say it nevertheless deals in every case and purging and rinsing suffice in other kodshim, and therefore he needs the baraita. However from that baraita Shach properly cut in the Yerushalmi that even in a copper vessel purging and rinsing are required for iruy alone. But not only on Mahariv is his question, but also on Rashba, whom Shach himself brought, for there in Terumat HaDeshen daf 92 side 2 he wrote: and from Ramban's explanation we learn that even a copper vessel into which one poured, whether from a kli rishon or kli sheni — it absorbs and requires hag'alah — for they did not say in the Yerushalmi that it does not absorb except when fire passes underneath, not to require purging and rinsing when pouring consecrated boiling into it, but it absorbs and requires hag'alah — end of his words. And on this too Shach's question: behold plainly it teaches: or he poured boiling into it — purging and rinsing; and explicit in a baraita in a copper vessel; and for Ramban too the mishnah must be not like Rambam, who holds purging helps in every case, as the Yerushalmi explains on the mishnah "one," etc., that it deals with earthenware and absorbs; and it is difficult: why breaking — for Ramban, absorption without cooking does not require purging; and if so, even in every case breaking should not be required; and one must say nevertheless in every case hag'alah does not help, and therefore breaking is required — and this is not like Rambam, for whom hag'alah helps. And likewise one may understand in Pesachim daf 76 in Tosafot s.v. granted, who challenged that the spit should forbid the Pesach — and answer that a wooden spit does not absorb heat so much like earthenware and flour. And it is difficult: behold even in a copper vessel it is clear in the mishnah that iruy into it boiling requires purging — from which it receives heat that cooks; and according to Rabbeinu Tam cooking and absorption are one — if so, how would wood not cook — strained to say this is according to Rashbam, who holds iruy absorbs but does not cook; if so, one may distinguish: even though iruy has power to absorb in a vessel, but that the vessel receive heat that roasts the food — it does not cook at all, only absorbs. And with this one may resolve Maharsha's question, why Tosafot challenged Rav and not Shmuel — for according to his explanation it is resolved in the Gemara for Shmuel from the mishnah what he puts and what he adds — if Tosafot would challenge and answer the Gemara, establishing it in hot earthenware — requires further study: perhaps iruy only absorbs and does not heat, and one need not distinguish between every case and broken vessels — requires further study; but for Rav, who establishes in cold earthenware, and nevertheless we say earthenware receives heat and roasts the Pesach — if so, they properly challenged Rav: is it resolved — behold the spit forbids; to distinguish between every case and wood Tosafot did not hold at all in the question, and answer — see there. Rather this is strained — R' Yehuda is the one who answers thus, and R' Yehuda holds in the chapter HaKirah daf 42 like Rabbeinu Tam, that it cooks, and one should not distinguish at all between absorption and cooking; and therefore Maharsha did not pay attention to the answer I wrote — if so, the above question returns to its place. Therefore it appears Rabbeinu Tam explains in the Gemara that which it says on iruy into it boiling — absorption without cooking — meaning it was already cooked in the first pot and he poured into his second vessel, and cooking does not apply, only absorption; and if so, according to this the verse and mishnah teach: do not say specifically if it cooked in this vessel it requires breaking and purging, but if it already cooked and afterward came to a vessel where it did not cook again in it — behold already cooked and standing, and purging is not required — it teaches that nevertheless it is required, since the vessel absorbs, etc. And see there the Yerushalmi that specifically requires fire passing underneath — otherwise it does not absorb; and the mishnah deals with fire passing underneath; and it teaches even though it cooked before and was not asked. And one must say that which Tosafot proved in Shabbos that iruy cooks, because it teaches "he poured into it boiling" — for according to this explanation there is no proof here, for in the mishnah it deals with fire passing underneath; Tosafot's words to answer the second that Tosafot wrote here — but for the first answer, in truth there is no proof from here, only from elsewhere, as written there — many proofs — see there; and with this all questions are resolved. Further it appeared that Mahariv and Rashba hold: that which was doubtful in our Gemara is plain to the Talmud of the land, as is common in the Bavli — it should not have been resolved; and in the Yerushalmi it was resolved — we follow the Yerushalmi, and he holds that certainly when pouring into boiling copper, even though the vessel does not absorb, since fire does not pass underneath — nevertheless the meat and gravy in the vessel cook from it inward and contract and cook, as Rosh wrote in the chapter Kirah — see there. And if so, it is cooking without absorption — and this is also the question in our Talmud: if so, purging is required. And with this it is resolved: the Gemara wanted to resolve whether cooking without absorption requires purging, from that we taught "he poured into it boiling"; and Rashi explained: he holds that just as absorption without cooking requires purging, so too the reverse; and this is strained: who would balance on one scale purging for what did not absorb at all, and what is the nature of this purging if it did not absorb at all — for that which he argued purging if it did not absorb without cooking — there the language of purging properly applies to remove what was absorbed. But according to the above it is resolved: in the Gemara he holds like the Yerushalmi that copper does not absorb unless fire passes underneath; and if so, that which teaches "he poured into it boiling" — because meat and gravy cook, as written — and he properly resolved that cooking without absorption requires purging; and the Gemara rejected: perhaps like the other view, who holds copper absorbs, and it deals that fire does not pass underneath; and if so, this view in the Yerushalmi holds cooking without absorption requires purging; and if so, one may say copper does not absorb, only meat and gravy cook and contract — and it is cooking without absorption. And with this one could also resolve Lechem Mishneh's question on Rambam, who copied the mishnah "dripped from its gravy," etc., and did not explain hot earthenware and hot flour — for we establish like Shmuel; and I already wrote above on this — see there. And it appears Rambam certainly holds: if we say nevertheless a peel is required — since gravy was forbidden as much as a peel, it absorbed on Pesach as much as removal, and likewise in flour as much as a kometz; only if we say cooking without absorption requires purging — why did the mishnah not teach it, and how did the mishnah omit this great law of Zevachim — one must say that in truth it teaches for him: or he poured into it boiling, that iruy does not forbid at all, even as much as a peel; and therefore because of cooking without absorption; but for the view that it is not required — one may say iruy cooks as much as a peel, and therefore iruy is because of absorption. And if so, it is resolved: that which the Gemara challenges Shmuel: when it absorbed — nevertheless a peel is required — not a natural presumption that perhaps it immediately cools until it does not even absorb as much as a peel (and see in Magen Avraham that it is only a general stringency, and it is difficult from where we challenge that perhaps it is not a stringency) — only the question is from the mishnah "iruy into it boiling," etc., and one need not because of cooking without absorption — for if so, let it resolve, etc.; and therefore one must say there is absorption; but above the Gemara challenges the opposite: if it is proven from the mishnah that nevertheless hot as much as a peel — if so, cooking without absorption does not require purging — for otherwise why did the mishnah not teach it, as I wrote; and if the question is difficult, let it resolve that purging is not required, and the Gemara rejected in hot earthenware; but Rambam, who is uncertain whether cooking without absorption requires purging, as clear in his book — therefore ruled hot into cold one peels; and if so, here too to be stringent, even in cold earthenware and cold flour, removal is required, as I wrote — examine.
