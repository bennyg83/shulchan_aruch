One should be concerned to be cautious l'chatchila in this matter. Meaning even if it is something customarily rinsed afterward — when it is permitted to place it on cold prohibition as above siman 91 — nevertheless here one should be cautious l'chatchila in this matter; and in my books I expanded on the laws of pouring and kli sheni; and here I write in utmost brevity what relates to the roots of the law: Rashbam whom Tosafot brought in chapter Blood of Sin Offering (Zevachim daf 95 side 1) and chapter Kirah (Shabbos daf 42 side 2) and Rosh and Ran — pouring is like kli sheni and does not cook; and likewise does not emit and absorb at once like kli sheni. Rabbenu Tam whom all those poskim brought — pouring cooks as much as a peel like kli rishon; and it is proved that Rashbam nevertheless pouring absorbs as much as a peel — e.g. if one poured forbidden boiling on permitted or milk on meat it forbids as much as a peel — only it does not emit and absorb at once; therefore permitted to pour on the chicken to pluck; and also proved that Rashbam holds kli sheni does not absorb at all even as much as a peel; and Rabbenu Tam whom all those poskim brought — it is proved he holds that just as pouring does not cook in all of it, so it does not absorb in all of it — unlike Maharshal in responsum 62 who wrote that for Rabbenu Tam whom Rosh brought it absorbs in all of it; and in Perishah section 68 he wrote that for Rabbenu Tam whom Rosh brought it cooks in all of it — this is astounding; and also proved from above that Rabbenu Tam holds kli sheni does not absorb as much as a peel; and if so the view of all those poskim above and Semag from them stands in Rabbenu Tam's approach — unlike what Maharshal wrote in Orach Shemesh section 33 that for Semag even earthenware absorbs in kli sheni — all the more so food; and so many Rishonim and Acharonim that kli sheni does not absorb at all; and so the rabbi in Turei Zahav general rule 33 law 1. One might say in hagahah in Orach Chayyim siman 451 it implies he holds earthenware used for chametz in kli sheni is forbidden — perhaps because of chametz stringency it touched it; nevertheless one should be stringent and forbid in earthenware where there is not so much loss, since Ramban and Rashba in Torat HaBayit daf 92 hold even copper absorbs; and there are poskim that even though kli sheni does not cook, nevertheless it absorbs and emits; and in food one may peel where there is not much loss; but in any case one does not forbid the whole thing in kli sheni; and so Maharshal in responsum there did not write it absorbs except as much as a peel. One might question what he wrote in his book chapter 105 siman 71 to forbid the whole thing. And in Bach in this siman he is very stringent that kli sheni emits and absorbs at once and forbids the whole thing — this is astounding; and we hold leniently for pouring that kli rishon cooks as much as a peel; therefore for Shabbos and basar b'chalav it is forbidden; and even in pouring where the stream broke off it nevertheless absorbs as much as a peel for all — as the poskim said and as is proved in chapter 25 (daf 76 side 1); and even though from the rabbi's words siman 68 section 10 it appears that if one poured from kli rishon on the chicken to pluck and the stream broke — one need not even a peel, because it does not emit and absorb at once — nevertheless it absorbs; and this is implied from Tosafot in Zevachim and Mordechai and Hagahot and Ran at end of Avodah Zarah; nevertheless certainly a peel suffices and it does not absorb more — and this is proved from what the poskim and Taz siman 91 section 4 wrote: boiling milk fell on cold meat — peel the meat and it is permitted; and so in the Talmud chapter 25 there. And all heat means specifically where yad soledes bo applies; but where yad soledes bo does not apply, even kli rishon standing on the fire does not forbid at all; and so early and late poskim in many places; and so Maharshal chapter 105 siman 71 — except afterward he wrote he is concerned to be lenient in kli rishon on the fire as long as it is hot even if yad soledes bo does not apply, because they made a distance for kli rishon as in Yerushalmi — I did not reach the end of his view, for in Yerushalmi chapter Kirah it says they made a distance for kli rishon where the hand does not rule — meaning yad soledes bo does not apply; but where the hand rules it is proved there explicitly in Yerushalmi that all permit; and so the consensus of poskim; and so in Turei Zahav general rule 57 law 12 — except in general rule 33 section 3 he ruled like Rashba and Tur that cold into hot where yad soledes bo does not apply requires a peel; and one must say he holds that even though yad soledes bo does not apply it absorbs as much as a peel; and one may be stringent even though from other poskim it does not appear so — requires study.
