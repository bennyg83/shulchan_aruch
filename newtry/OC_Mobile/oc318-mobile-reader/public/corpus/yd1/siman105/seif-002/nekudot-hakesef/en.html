(there s.k. 4) And Maharshal, etc. I already challenged in Shach s.k. 5 Maharshal's view on this and wrote there briefly, and wrote that in my book I expanded on this; and since he follows Maharshal's view in forbidden foods siman 71, therefore I return to raise and show with clear proofs that kli sheni does not absorb throughout — for certainly all agree that irui, even when the stream broke, is stronger than kli sheni, as poskim say in several places; and even irui when the stream did not break — Rashbam and R"T and many other poskim hold it does not absorb more than kedei kelipah, for Rashbam and those who stand in his view brought proof that irui is like kli sheni from that which is in perek Keitzad Tzolin that hot into cold peels because the lower prevails — therefore irui is like kli sheni and does not cook; and R"T and those who stand in his view resolve that which is in perek Keitzad Tzolin — since it peels, one may say it cooks kedei kelipah; if so, for all, that which is in perek Keitzad Tzolin deals with actual irui, and even so it does not absorb throughout; and if so, all the more a poor vessel; and even for some poskim who resolve that which is in perek Keitzad Tzolin when the stream broke — nevertheless it is proven that when the stream broke it absorbs only kedei kelipah, and plainly kli sheni is lesser than when the stream broke; if so, it is proven from the Talmud and all poskim that in any case kli sheni does not absorb throughout; and further, the view of four poskim that kli sheni does not absorb at all, as I will explain; and for all, nevertheless it does not absorb throughout, for Rashbam and all poskim who stand in his view explain that which is in perek Keitzad Tzolin regarding actual irui and hold irui is like kli sheni; if so, it is proven that in kli sheni even kelipah is not required, for necessarily kelipah is not required there in perek Keitzad Tzolin because since he values it, it is impossible that it did not absorb a little, as it says in the six orders there; but when he poured into kli sheni that already cooled, he no longer needs even kelipah; and if so, even R"T and those who stand in his view we do not find they disagree on this with Rashbam that kli sheni does not need even kelipah; and further, behold also R"Meir explains that which is in perek Keitzad Tzolin regarding actual irui and holds the lower prevails, except that since he values it, it absorbs and cooks kedei kelipah; if so, when he poured into kli sheni that already cooled, it no longer absorbs at all — for otherwise the Talmud would not need to say "since he values it, it is impossible that it did not absorb a little" — see there. And behold Tosafot and Rosh perek Kirah, and likewise Tosafot in Zevachim perek Dam Chatat (Zevachim daf 95b) mentioned the dispute of Rashbam and R"T; and likewise Ran in perek Kirah and end of Avodah Zarah; and likewise Mordechai; and likewise Semag negative commandments 65 daf 13a — unstated like R"T's view; and likewise it appears the view of Sefer HaTerumah and Gemara Mei'iri perek 25 laws of Shabbat; and likewise Ra'avan in Sefer Yere'im siman 102 daf 38a; and R"Meir Recanati siman 28; and Agudah perek Kirah like R"T's view; and likewise in many other authors — from all their words we learn that kli sheni in any case does not absorb throughout; and also Rif in Hagahot Teshuvot Tashbetz siman 533 wrote that kli sheni neither emits nor absorbs, and wrote that thus saw R"Y; and likewise Maharil in responsum s.s. 174 that we hold like R"Y that kli sheni does not absorb and does not emit; and even Hagahot Asheri perek Kirah that wrote kli rishon absorbs even in kli sheni — Peri Yosef Mahariach — one may say this means kedei kelipah; and likewise Or Zarua general rule 34 that kli sheni does not absorb in kli rishon nor in other things even kedei kelipah; and likewise at end of general rule 17; and from where do we permit food in a bowl into which a sheretz fell even though yad soledes bo is in it, and we do not require kelipah for a bowl of milk that one pours hot meat into it — for boiling irui certainly absorbs kedei kelipah, but kli sheni does not; and in responsum of the Geonim they permit for this reason in name of Rabbenu Yoel b'dieved a chicken plucked in kli sheni in water of issur — end; and likewise further in general rule 27; and Maharshal himself wrote in responsum siman 62 that kli sheni absorbs only kedei kelipah; and what he wrote in his book forbidden foods siman 71 is astonishing, and I expanded on this even though it is a simple matter in my humble opinion to remove from the heart of this Mechaber who followed Maharshal's words in his book — he did not notice that his words are astonishing and contradict all poskim and contradict one another. Also what he wrote to resolve Tur's words in what he challenged Rashba — the spirit of the wise is not pleased with his explanation.
