And some dispute and say that in salting there is no distinction, etc. The reason is that in salting there is no power in what is bland to nullify the power of salting; and likewise salting has no power to make the bland salty, for such is the way of salt — it cannot be nullified except through rinsing — likewise Semak; and in Tur it is written in this plain meaning, and these are his words: it appears he holds that heat through salting forbids all of it, etc.; and Beit Yosef challenged: behold Rashba himself, in what deals specifically with fatty substance, etc. — and every examiner must search with all his power to justify our rabbi Tur's words from this wondrous difficulty, that he copied the beginning of the words and not the end; and I saw emendations on this that do not please me at all; and what appears to my mind in this: Tur inferred from Rashba's words, who distinguished between issur salted above or below — from which the reason of the matter that all is forbidden in fatty issur is because the permitted itself became entirely hot, and through this it receives taste of issur that is fatty; but Tur does not explain thus, rather even though the permitted is cold — nevertheless the fatty substance that comes from salted issur goes and spreads in the permitted, even though it is cold, because spreading itself is hot; and the difference between these two reasons: if according to Rashba's reason, that it is hot — it implies that in one piece that absorbs d'oraisa issur through salting, the entire piece becomes forbidden, since salting of fatty issur makes the permitted piece hot — likewise in lean in one piece, for they did not require fatty substance except from piece to piece, which does not absorb in lean; but in one piece it certainly emits what it absorbed in all its parts, since it is hot; and on this he says: it appears "heat through salting" — for at first glance it is superfluous language, this "heat" — he should have said that through salting it forbids all of it; rather he intended what I wrote, that it implies from Rashba's words that the heat of salting causes the entire piece to boil and automatically the entire piece is forbidden, even in lean; and Rosh could not have written thus, only in lean there is issur only as much as a peel, even in one piece; and therefore Tur afterward wrote properly; and also what he distinguished, etc. — meaning: and further, why did he distinguish between above and below — from another side thus appears to my mind in this explanation; and even if you find somewhere in Rashba's words that it does not seem to him as our rabbi Tur explains his words here — nevertheless our rabbi is saved from the great difficulty we mentioned, that he did not examine Rashba, only the beginning of his words and not the end, which contradict one another.
