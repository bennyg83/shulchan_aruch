(seif 5) Fell to a known place. Peri Chadash wrote: specifically to a known place; but if we do not know how it fell, we are not stringent to require netilat makom for the whole piece since there is sixty in it, and netilah is only a stringency; but in lean issur where netilah is by law, one must remove the thickness of a finger throughout the piece — see there; and it appears to me that even in lean issur, if it is known that it touched only one place and we do not know the place, the netilah is batel in the majority of other removals, for there is no concern lest they cook them, for in cooking there will be sixty in the whole piece against the milk:
