Or basar b'chalav. Rashal challenged in chapter 3 siman 45: behold we hold absorbed issur does not pass without gravy, even in basar b'chalav; and proof from a drop of milk that fell on meat in siman 92, that when that piece was forbidden without stirring and covering, it does not forbid others that touched hot one another — only Tur was not precise here, unless we explain what is written here in basar b'chalav deals with hot meat that touched cheese — that is considered issur from itself — end of his words; meaning the meat should be forbidden because of this also in Terumat HaDeshen HaArukh house 4 gate 4 likewise explicitly on meat forbidden from milk, that it does not return and forbid other meat without gravy, even if both are hot; and likewise further on daf 99 side 2, that milk or blood do not spread.
Even if they were roasted together. The reason in Tur is that the issur absorbed in the piece does not emerge from it to absorb in another without gravy — end of his explanation: it does not forbid at all, even as much as a peel; and likewise explicitly Rashal in Piskei HaGedolah siman 45, that a peel is not required.
But if it absorbed from spreading things, etc. — it implies milk does not spread, for if not, why do we say in siman 92 seif 2 that that piece alone was forbidden and not the others — and as I wrote nearby also in the name of Terumat HaDeshen HaArukh and Rashal; and above siman 87 seif 10 proves we say milk is called issur that spreads, as written there; and see what I wrote in seif 9, what is difficult on Shulchan Aruch's ruling here.
But a vessel that absorbed, etc. — the reason for this is that specifically food that has something to emit from its body — then absorption does not pass without gravy; but a vessel that has no emission from its body — then what it absorbed emerges properly; and according to this it is simple that a vessel is not forbidden from food with absorbed issur that does not spread, such as liver or meat salted in an unperforated vessel — as will be explained in siman 108 seif 1, that this does not spread.
