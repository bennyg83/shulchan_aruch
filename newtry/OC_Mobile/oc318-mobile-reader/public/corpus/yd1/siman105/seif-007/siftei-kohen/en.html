That which says a forbidden piece, etc., or basar b'chalav, etc. It implies this is a meat piece in which milk was absorbed and the whole piece is considered the body of the prohibition — as the poskim said in siman 92 (whom I brought there s.k. 10); and Taz wrote above siman 101: it has no status of a piece fit to be honored unless its prohibition is from itself such as nevelah and basar b'chalav, etc.; and he also explained this is meat in which milk was absorbed; and so Maharai (in Hagahot Shulchan David siman 91) and Bedek Malkhut and Derishah section 23 and other Acharonim understood Tur's words which are like the Mechaber's — that a meat piece with absorbed milk forbids its fellow by contact without gravy. But Rashba wrote in Torat HaBayit (house 4 gate 1) on these matters that even in basar b'chalav the milk does not spread — and even though they made it like a nevelah piece they did not make it like nevelah itself for this matter — end of his words. In Maharai's writings siman 249 he wrote in his teacher's name: I am also uncertain about meat that received taste from milk when there was not sixty to nullify it and it became forbidden and again touched while hot other meat — Mordechai (chapter 105) in R' Yehudah's name and Semak that it does not forbid without gravy from the sugya of a drop of milk; and they proved this reasoning from that which was shaken and covered; and it is difficult, for Semak wrote from that which is written in Semak that basar b'chalav is different from other prohibitions — behold chalav is not nullified — specifically when it is the root of the prohibition and not when forbidden through absorption of prohibition; and regarding meat forbidden from milk we do not say thus, and it is not nullified; and even though it is a complete mishnah in Avodah Zarah that chalav of basar b'chalav is not nullified — until here his words. Also in his gates he wondered at Semak and Mordechai in R' Yehudah's name and supported them on this; and he wrote: it appears to answer somewhat — certainly in basar b'chalav as long as it is mixed it is called the root and body of the prohibition; and even the meat without milk is like kilayim in cloth: when flax thread is mixed in a wool garment it is forbidden to cover with the whole garment even on the side where there is no flax thread; and if cut from the garment that side without flax — that side is permitted; so too it is established for the rabbis that no taste goes from this piece except meat taste alone and no milk unless through gravy — and it is as if Elijah came and told us a small piece was cut from this meat with no milk taste and it is permitted, like kilayim — requires study — end of his words; and so Maharshal in Orach Shemesh and in his book chapter 95 siman 45; and so it is compelled initially from the rabbi siman 92 who wrote if a drop of milk fell on a piece in the pot and he did not shake or cover at all — only that piece is forbidden and the rest of the pot is permitted; and if he held that in basar b'chalav once it is itself nevelah and considered body of prohibition it forbids its fellows without gravy — then what is in the pot would be forbidden for us who hold hot with hot without gravy forbids all (as Raavad challenged in Ma'akhalot Assurot chapter 9 and Ra'ah in Bedek HaBayit daf 100); and if so necessarily it must be explained here that hot meat touched hot cheese — as Maharshal explained in Orach Shemesh and his book and Bach brings; and this is not primary — rather it appears the rabbi holds that although we hold hot with hot without gravy forbids all — this is because we are not expert between lean and fatty; nevertheless what is not body of prohibition only through absorption is not so stringent; and we establish it on the law that forbidden absorption does not spread without gravy; and milk does not spread — as Rashba said and as in siman 92 s.k. 3; and so I wrote there in Ran's name; also I proved there that this is the poskim's and Taz's view that lean milk prohibition is called and does not spread — see there; and proof: Turei Zahav general rule 28 wrote generally that if one absorbed non-spreading prohibition it does not forbid by contact even though we are not expert between lean and fatty — only we are certainly not so stringent; also Maharshal himself wrote in Orach Shemesh there that he strains to explain Tur's words thus; and Bach in siman 92 also understood Tur like Maharai — except he challenged there from that which the Talmud says in chapter 105 absorbs absorbs emits does not emit — and he answered it is only so the other pieces are not entirely forbidden but removal is certainly required — end of his words; and it may be so, for the Talmud did not say thus there but before we established chadichah na'aseh nevelah as is explained there explicitly; but after the conclusion one may squeeze it — forbidden; and chadichah na'aseh nevelah — therefore the meat is considered body of prohibition and forbids; and so Ran end of chapter 25 and the rabbi's Maggid in Ma'akhalot Assurot chapter 9 in Raavad's name and Ra'ah in Bedek HaBayit there; and therefore what Maharshal wrote in his book and what Maharai and the stringent wrote in all this — investigate — I see no place for this stringency, etc., that absorbed prohibition does not spread without gravy even in basar b'chalav, etc., as R' Yehudah proved clearly from the sugya of a drop of milk, etc.; and Tosefet Yom Tov was not precise, etc. — end of his words — he was not precise, for there is no clear proof here, as is said; and it may be that R' Yehudah in Mordechai and Semak and other poskim also hold thus and only brought proof for other prohibitions from that sugya of a drop of milk that says in the Talmud absorbs absorbs emits does not emit — before it was thought chadichah na'aseh nevelah and body of prohibition; and even though Maharai in his writings and Hagahot Shulchan David did not understand their words thus — nevertheless it is not compelled — see there; and proof for my words in Semag daf 91 beginning side 3 he also wrote R' Yehudah's words and Mahara Shtain explained his words that absorbed basar b'chalav forbids like nevelah — see there; Again I found in Orach Shemesh Maharshal printed in Lublin daf 33 a hagahah from Maharshal who brought conversely proof from chapter 105 that regarding basar b'chalav a piece forbids its fellow without gravy; and he wrote there so ruled his teacher the Gaon — see there:

And so the rabbi ruled in Turei Zahav general rule 28 section 1; and further he ruled there in Orach Chayyim's name: that which says a piece forbids its fellow by contact — specifically when its prohibition is from itself; but a piece forbidden from prohibition through absorption does not forbid by contact even if roasted or salted together — and this is when it absorbed non-spreading prohibition; but if it absorbed spreading prohibition it forbids by contact — end of his words; and I already wrote in the preceding s.k. that the rabbi's and Orach Chayyim's view is that although we do not distinguish lean from fatty — this is in the body of the matter; but what became forbidden through absorption we are not so stringent; and it is proved there in Turei Zahav that blood is not considered spreading prohibition for this; and so in many Talmud places and poskim that blood does not spread; and although we hold everywhere also for blood to estimate in sixty because we do not distinguish lean from fatty as the rabbi wrote in section 9 — nevertheless where the piece absorbed blood it does not forbid other pieces through salting and roasting; nevertheless it appears this is specifically when it was salted and rinsed as required and afterward absorbed blood — as in Orach Chayyim end of general rule 12 in such a case it does not go from piece to piece through salting and roasting; but meat not yet salted that still has its own blood — it is simple in the Talmud and all poskim that they forbid in salting and roasting — as explained in section 70; and Maharshal in Orach Shemesh and his book chapter 95 siman 45 wrote specifically with two dry hot items through cooking we hold thus — but in roasting and salting we properly say it spreads from piece to piece — for us even in non-fatty prohibition;
