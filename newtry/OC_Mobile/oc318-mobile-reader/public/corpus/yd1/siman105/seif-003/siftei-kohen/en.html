If both are hot from the heat of kli rishon, etc. Maharshal's view in Orach Shemesh and in his book chapter 105 siman 71: in a solid item such as a piece of meat or fish or rice or millet that one pours hot from the pot — if yad soledes bo applies its law is always like kli rishon; and I cannot decide — rather it appears since Tosafot chapter Kirah wrote the reason to distinguish kli rishon from kli sheni — because kli rishon stood on the fire its walls are hot and hold heat a long time — therefore everything depends on the walls; therefore a solid item too, since it is called dry, walls do not apply; and so Orach Chayyim general rule 37 section 7 that in a dry item there are no cooling walls and it cooks forever as long as yad soledes bo applies — see there; and so it appears initially in end of Shulchan David on flies and in Orach Chayyim general rule 57 section 3 in Mordechai's name: if one poured food with a fly into a bowl and the fly fell first — the bowl is forbidden as much as a peel like hot into cold, for a fly coming from boiling cannot be like kli sheni — end of his words; and as Maharshal explained there — though I found the rabbi's handwritten gloss on Orach Chayyim general rule 27: it appears to me he speaks from the reason of pouring — since the fly mixed with the food it is still called kli rishon like other pouring that forbids as much as a peel; but without this there is no distinction between fly and other pouring — end of his words; the rabbi has his own reason here in hagahah and above siman 94 section 4 follows his reason; and so Turei Zahav end of general rule 61 that the custom is when eating meat in a bowl with a milk knife we do not forbid — but the plain sense of Shulchan David and those poskim implies like Maharshal; and so it appears initially from Beit Yosef siman 107 and as Perishah wrote there explicitly — see there (and see siman 107 and investigate); and the rabbi in Bedek Malkhut wrote in siman 107 we also come from pouring — and he rejected Maharshal's words without compelling proofs — requires study; nevertheless certainly in rice and millet if clear and poured like gravy it appears one may rely on the rabbi's words; and so Orach Chayyim implies there that specifically in dry items kli sheni does not apply; and perhaps Maharshal meant only rice and millet that are not clear — and so his words there imply — see there; and Bach also wrote opinions from his heart that are not compelling — the conclusion I wrote:
And if the one is cold the permitted needs a peel. Meat and cheese that touched each other and one of them was cold — both need a peel; and so Orach Chayyim implies there; and it is simple; and Maharshal wrote in Orach Shemesh and in his book chapter 25 section 70: if the prohibition is hot it appears simple per Semak's view to distinguish between heat of fire and salting that all is forbidden; and I wonder, for Semak (siman 200 daf 96 beginning side 2) wrote at end of his words in chapter Blood of Sin Offering we say piece with piece, thin strip with thin strip — peel its place; one might say what is different from hot with hot that is forbidden, etc. — and perhaps one might say there with hot it deals this on that and there with thin strip it deals side by side — end of his words; and Mordechai brought it in chapter 105 — behold even when both are hot they permit with a peel; and so Orach Chayyim brought there; and Maharshal himself in Orach Shemesh siman 28 on his name — see there:
Forbidden prohibition that he placed in a permitted vessel, etc. This is Orach Chayyim's view; and so Rashba in his novellae (chapter 105 and Torat HaBayit daf 73): according to those who say a bowl in which one salted other meat after rinsing is cold — if one put hot meat in it a peel suffices whether in dry boiling or wet boiling — hot into cold, tattei overpowers; and with a peel it suffices; and so our teachers Tosafot — end of his words; and Maharshal chapter 95 section 45 and chapter 105 section 60 divided Avodah Zarah and wrote: it appears to me this reasoning has no basis at all, since I proved from all the great ones that in a same-day bowl it forbids a hot item without gravy because it has no emission from itself (as the rabbi section 7 said) — therefore one cannot say regarding bowl or spoon upper overpowers or lower overpowers except with two food types whose nature is hot and cold — unlike a vessel whose nature is not so; therefore if next to it it emits and absorbs — therefore whenever it is hot where yad soledes bo applies it forbids; and it is also forbidden from the prohibition itself in the vessel where there is no sixty, for we hold heat of roasting and heat of cooking are all in sixty — end of his words. In the Talmud chapter 25 regarding a drop fell from its gravy it does not initially imply so; and so it is proved explicitly in Tosafot chapter 25 (daf 111 side 2 beginning s.v. the halachah) and Rosh there regarding fish that rose in a meat bowl and one of them was hot — whether for one who says upper overpowers or lower overpowers, a peel is required — end of his words; it implies even in a vessel we say tattei overpowers; and so Rif in Hagahot Teshuvot siman 249; and so Shulchan David at end of the book on flies; and so Beit Yosef sections 94 and 95 and the rabbi and Acharonim; and Rashba in his novellae chapter 105 daf 117 side 3 and Orach Chayyim general rule 36 and the rabbi (and as below section 7) also hold that a vessel forbids a hot item without gravy — or we do not depend on this at all; for although it forbids hot without gravy — this is where both are hot or the lower is hot to forbid as much as removal; and for us the upper hot forbids as much as a peel — unlike if it did not forbid without gravy it would need neither removal nor peel; and Maharshal connected this to that — I do not know what one has to do with the other; I also wonder at him, for he himself ruled in chapter 105 that if one put boiling gravy in a bowl in which meat was salted, sixty against the peel suffices; and if you say tattei does not apply in a vessel we need sixty against the whole thing, for he holds there in siman 71 the whole vessel is forbidden in salting — requires study:
