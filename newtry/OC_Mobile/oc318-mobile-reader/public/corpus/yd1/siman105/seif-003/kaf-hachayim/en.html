(3) And likewise a vessel of forbidden food that is not absorbed from the beginning that permitted food was steeped in it for twenty-four hours — the whole thing is forbidden. Ohr HaChaim general rule 30 law 9. But Bach s.k. 1 challenged this — why should the permitted food be forbidden in a vessel of forbidden food that is not absorbed from the beginning, since the prohibition is from pickling and behold at that moment it is not absorbed from the beginning and the vessel has no power to forbid the permitted food steeped in it, see there; and likewise Shach challenged s.k. 2. And Kelalei HaGahot in Hagahat Turei Zahav section 6 wrote that for practical halachah we rule like Taz and Shach that permitted food steeped in a vessel of forbidden food that is not absorbed from the beginning, or milk in a meat vessel or vice versa — the food is not forbidden because it is nosen taam lifgam and permitted after the fact. Beit Levi section 1, Chokhmat Adam section 3, Chakal Yisrael there. However Peri Chadash section 1 agreed with Ohr HaChaim. And likewise Lechem Pri Chadash section 1, Kereti section 1. It turns out this matter is a twofold dispute and therefore one should not permit after the fact except in great loss; and what Zivchei Tzedek section 2 wrote to permit even in minor loss and he brought support that this is Shulchan Aruch's view that there is no pickling for vessels since he ruled regarding yayin nesech that there is no pickling for vessels — we already wrote above in section 1 that regarding yayin nesech all agree except whether we learn from yayin nesech or not; and if so from Shulchan Aruch's words one cannot decide that he holds that in every case there is no pickling for vessels; and if so we return to our law that this matter is a twofold dispute and one should not permit except in great loss.
