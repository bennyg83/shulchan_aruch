If both are hot from a kli rishon, etc. — see Shach s.k. 8, who brought Maharshal's view of a solid item, and even rice and millet if they are in a kli sheni — nevertheless the law of kli rishon applies to them, since there is no cooling partition; and Shach did not decide; and I did not understand — if so, from where do Tosafot in Zevachim and Shabbos prove from that which teaches "he poured into it boiling" that in every iruy it cooks as much as a peel — perhaps the vessel cools what is hot that fell into it until it immediately cools and has no power to cook even as much as a peel; and that which we say regarding "he poured into it boiling" that requires purging — there it deals with pouring consecrated meat into a vessel, and it is a solid item that always preserves its heat and the vessel is not cold to cool it at all, and it is always a kli rishon — and with this it is certain that it cooks in the vessel at least as much as a peel; but in pouring a thin item that loses its heat through cooling of the vessel — perhaps it immediately loses its heat and does not cook at all — therefore Shmuel, who holds one peels, deals with meat that fell into milk and it is a solid item that does not lose its heat at all and remains always a kli rishon; and on the contrary, with this one may reconcile the precision of the mishnah that teaches: one should not put spices into a kli rishon — implying that it is permitted to pour; and in the conclusion it teaches: but one may put into the bowl — implying iruy is forbidden; and Tosafot already wrote that the inferences contradict; and according to what I wrote it is resolved — for it is not decided how he can say one should not pour on them, implying even a thin item — and this is permitted, for it does not cook; and likewise no reason to say: but one pours with rice and millet and the like, a solid item — forbidden to pour, for this cooks — therefore the tanna stated plainly; and therefore Tosafot must be — requires further study. And the main proof of Maharshal is from what the poskim wrote: if one poured boiling water and there was a fly in the bowl — the fly forbids and is not included in sixty — and from this he learned its law; and Rama rejected because of iruy forbids; and Shach in s.k. 8 appears that he does not hold — why did he cite a fly, every iruy forbids; and according to what I wrote it is resolved: boiling stopped, and were it not for the fly there would be no iruy; but a fly, since it is a solid item, even when boiling stopped — forbids; and if so, his proof goes.
Issur that one placed, etc. — see Shach s.k. 10, who brought Maharshal's view that since the vessel has no emission from its body — if so, tattei and ila'i do not apply, and it always forbids and all becomes forbidden; and likewise Ra'ah; and Shach challenged and argued from the sugya in Pesachim daf 76 in the sugya of dripping from gravy onto earthenware — there it implies that even in a vessel ila'i and tattei apply — see there. Heaven forbid to say the sugya of the Gemara was in its place in concealment of a matter — but one may reconcile that he certainly holds regarding absorption and emission: in a vessel, since it has no emission from its body, tattei gavar does not apply, for it is easy to emit and absorb — this is regarding absorption; but that the body of the vessel should receive heat from the thing that fell into it until its heat is like a kli rishon and it again roasts what falls in its gravy — in this there is no difference between vessel and food: what relevance has this, that it receive heat in its substance, to that which has no emission in its substance — behold we judge the body and substance of the thing whether it receives heat and preserves its heat so much, etc.; and if so, what difference is there between vessel and food — and if so, there in Pesachim the Gemara's question from dripping gravy on the oven — the oven should receive heat and be so hot that it roasts the thing — in this there is no difference between vessel and food, and he properly inferred like Rav, who holds ila'i gavar — requires further study. However, the other proofs Shach brought are clear proofs that nevertheless in an earthenware vessel there is room to be stringent — not because of Maharshal, who has no emission from its body, only as Tosafot wrote in Zevachim daf 95 side 2 s.v. he poured, who brought in the name of the Yerushalmi that he wanted to say iruy does not cook at all and it is like a kli sheni, and he wanted to bring proof from that mishnah "he poured into it" — and he rejected it, for the mishnah teaches "he poured into it boiling," etc., in earthenware, for earthenware absorbs more, even in a kli sheni; and if so, since this reasoning is mentioned in the Yerushalmi and Tosafot, and also Maharshal's view tends to be stringent — if so, from where to reject and be lenient; and from that which the Gemara challenges from dripping gravy on earthenware, etc. — there is no proof to the contrary, for one may say that even though it hurries to absorb, but that it receive heat and again roast the drop — it is obvious it equals other things, and the Gemara challenges properly; but regarding quickness of absorption one may say as I wrote. And in truth Ramban's view, as Rashba wrote in his name in Terumat HaDeshen daf 92, does not imply thus, for he inferred from that dish of salt in which they broke it — even though salting does not cook; and it is difficult: the guilt is in earthenware, and it hurries to absorb even without cooking — if so, what proof is there for other vessels; rather we learn he does not hold thus; and it is possible the question why break it — let it be salted in it a second time, for even though it hurries to absorb, to emit we did not hear — if so, behold it is permitted to salt in it, for it is not hot at all; but this is strained in his intent, and it is simple he does not hold to distinguish, only according to his view that absorption and cooking are two matters; and if so, that which the Yerushalmi answers "earthenware absorbs" — the intent is not that there is a difference between earthenware and other things, only absorption is different; and if purging and rinsing are not required, only hag'alah, as Ramban wrote there — in earthenware it does not exist in reality, purging and rinsing, for he holds like Rashi and Tosafot that earthenware has no hag'alah at all, and therefore he challenges there from copper that has purging — we learn it cooks; but according to Tosafot's explanation it properly implies that in earthenware one should be stringent; and if so, Maharshal and Ra'ah in any case are stringent with a vessel — one may say in earthenware.
Forbidden to pour, etc. — this is when the upper is cold and the lower hot, for steam forbids; but both cold or both hot — even if the lower is hotter — nevertheless permitted, for this law is taken from the mishnah in chapter 5 of Machshirin, as the mishnah teaches: one who pours from cold to cold and from hot to cold is pure; from cold to hot — impure; and we establish it like the first tanna, that specifically cold into hot is forbidden; and likewise Rambam in the laws of food impurity; and he gave the reason that steam rises upward and mixes it; but otherwise permitted, for there is no connecting stream in forbidding — as Maharil wrote in responsum siman 72; and Taz gave the reason because of connecting stream — and if so, even when both are cold or both hot they are forbidden, and it is not only cold into hot.
