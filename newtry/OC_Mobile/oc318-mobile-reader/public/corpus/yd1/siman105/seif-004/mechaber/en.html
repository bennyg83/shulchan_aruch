In what matters does this apply — when it fell into the dish, because the gravy conveys the emission of the issur and mixes it throughout the dish; but issur, whether hot or cold, that fell on roasted food at the fire — it forbids only kedei netilah, which is the thickness of the width of a finger; therefore a thigh roasted with its gid, or a piece of issur roasted with a piece of heter and touching one another — one must remove kedei netilah around the gid, and likewise from the piece at the place it touched a piece of issur; and likewise if issur fell on a piece in the pot that is outside the gravy and one did not stir the pot and did not cover it — it forbids only kedei netilah; but if it is in gravy — for Rashi [all of it, and for R"Y] even part of it, or even all of it outside gravy, and one stirred or covered the pot — the gravy exudes the taste and mixes it and it enters throughout.
