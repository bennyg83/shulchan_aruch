(seif 4) If issur fell on a piece in the pot that is outside the gravy and one did not shake — it forbids only kedei netilah. And in my humble opinion the essence is as Beit Yosef wrote in name of Semak and Hagahot Maimoniyot Laws of Forbidden Foods: if the issur piece touches all the heter pieces — it is absorbed in all and forbids them b'nosen taam; and so appears to me proven explicitly in Tosafot Pesachim (daf 100) and Rosh and Ran and Rashi Pesachim, for they explained Rav deals there when the heter piece is placed entirely outside the gravy and the nevelah piece is placed on it and the nevelah imparted taste in it — see there; therefore it forbids all b'nosen taam and kelipeh does not suffice; and it cannot be said it deals when the nevelah piece is fatty — for then why did they require shaking at the end to forbid other pieces in the pot as Tosafot and Rosh wrote there, and I brought them siman 92; for behold in oily issur it spreads in all pieces as explained in Gemara and all poskim; and Taz this siman seif 8 and 9; rather one must say there it was when the nevelah piece is placed and touches on every heter piece — even in issur that is not fatty it forbids all; but it does not go from piece to fellow without gravy in issur that is not fatty — similar to a drop of milk as is written siman 92; and so is proven explicitly in Gemara Zevachim (daf 97) where it states in Mishnah: a valid piece that touched an invalid piece is forbidden — not all the piece is forbidden; it is forbidden only its place of contact; and in Gemara it teaches: one might think if it touched part of the piece all of it should be forbidden — Scripture says "if touch" — forbidden only its place of contact alone; how so? — one cuts the place it absorbed; proven explicitly that specifically when it touched part of it one cuts the place it absorbed, for it is not absorbed except at the place of contact without gravy in lean; and even if it was a thin cracker it is called lean as Mateh Yehonatan wrote general rule 36 s.k. 11; but when it touched all of it — absorbed in all and all is forbidden; and now what Mordechai challenged in name of R' Yehuda in Pesachim regarding Gemara Zevachim — in Pesachim chapter Keitzad Tzolin (daf 76) it states hot into hot all is forbidden, and in Zevachim we say forbidden only place of contact — he left in requires-study; and one must say he holds there is no distinction between cooking and roasting without gravy in hot into hot; and as some poskim hold thus — brought in Turei Chadash; and even so there is difficulty for me, for in Zevachim it explicitly deals when it only touched part, and in chapter Keitzad Tzolin it deals when it touches all; and again I found in Rashba responsum siman 264 they challenged him Mordechai's challenge above; and the questioner reconciled whether they are one upon the other or one beside the other; and Rashba was not satisfied with this distinction and reconciled: in Zevachim it deals with lean — similar to cracker; and in this Mordechai's challenge to Turei Chadash general rule 23 on Tur siman 92 who wrote: if it cooked with others — sixty is needed to nullify all; and it implies he concludes its cooking remained as it was outside the gravy; and there is practical difference also for us in completely lean as Mateh Yehonatan wrote general rule 37 s.k. 3 and as is written in Orach Chayim siman 467 regarding chametz bread and matzah that suffices; and if it touched all — forbidden.
