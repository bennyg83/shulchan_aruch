He salted it. See above siman 91 seif 5 where the measure of its salting was explained.
Removal of. Shach wrote: and even though we hold even in brine, even in completely lean, to estimate with sixty — nevertheless the fat of the gid is only d'rabbanan and we are lenient regarding it; however that is for the essence of the law; but to be concerned for custom the Rav wrote siman 64 seif 28 that where there is not a little loss one should require sixty; and Taz wrote the Mechaber follows his reasoning as he wrote in seif 65 that lean milk does not spread and sixty is not needed to nullify it.
The gid. Shach wrote: it should have said a greater novelty — even in completely lean as above seif 65 in roasting, etc.; and nevertheless it appears Taz said only when both are salted; but not when the issur was salted and the heter bland, for since it is bland it has no power to season the issur.
Itself. Taz explained: the milk touched each one of them and there is not sixty in each against the milk — forbidden, for perhaps it absorbed much and has no partnership to nullify; and from this is also derived a leniency: if clearly the milk only touched one of them — then the others are permitted; but wonder at Shulchan Aruch that above seif 7 it ruled if it absorbed from spreading things in nature such as fat — it forbids its fellow even without gravy; and here it ruled absorption of milk does not go out without gravy; and one may answer: there it deals with roasting and here in salting, for we conclude in Gemara salting is like boiling — roasting and Turei Chadash and Shulchan Aruch conclusion is so — it is like roasting — end of his words [and see Peri Chadash what he reconciled] {Rama: (and see in Nekudot HaKesef he reconciles this difficulty.)}
To be honored. The reason: since the issur is not from itself; and Shach wrote: therefore it deals when there is in all three to nullify the milk itself; for otherwise it is not nullified in majority, for perhaps they will cook them and impart taste; and see above siman 109; and behold Shach left the Mechaber's words in requires-study according to his view that we do not say chanan in other issurim — I abbreviated here because there is no practical outcome for us who hold chanan in all issurim.
Salting. Meaning: even in actual milk; and behold it is fitting in any case not to be stringent only in fat and not in lean, but because we are not expert, etc.
Leniency. Taz and Shach wrote: meaning when the issur is stuck to the piece — then all combine to nullify the milk even though it does not touch all of them — like in cooking; and also regarding if there is milk in that piece — according to those who require sixty, that means sixty is required in that piece itself against the milk in it and other pieces do not combine to nullify milk in that piece; and according to custom we combine also other pieces; and unlike Levush who explained the leniency is regarding not needing kelipeh — he was not precise in this, for truly even according to custom kelipeh is needed where it touched; and proven in Turei Chadash that even if the pieces do not touch the milk but touch the piece that touches the milk — all combine if the issur is not davuk as in cooking.
Brine. Taz wrote: blood is also in this category even though blood does not spread — nevertheless sometimes there is a mixture of brine and fat and therefore we hold in siman 70 salting forbids blood with sixty.
On Pesach. Shach wrote: however in Orach Chayim siman 467 seif 14 the Rav wrote one must forbid that entire piece on which a wheat was found on Pesach and permit the others through kelipeh; and Taz wrote Maharshal's conclusion: no distinction between salted, roasted, and baked — their law is like cooking to forbid with sixty; and chametz on Pesach in any amount; and nevertheless we do not say in these three that it is like cooking except stringently — meaning sixty is required against the milk; and if it is davuk — sixty against the entire piece; but not to be lenient — meaning not to require only kedei netilah even if there is sixty; but nevertheless kedei netilah is required; and therefore ruled regarding matzah that folded slightly in the oven before Pesach — sixty is required in that matzah against the issur and kedei netilah; and not like Rama in Orach Chayim who did not require sixty in the matzah; Maharshal concludes further we do not hold as Tur wrote that also in salting if the heter is fatty and issur lean — all is forbidden; for also in roasting it is a novel matter and no author wrote so; nevertheless in salting certainly we are not so stringent except we say wherever the issur cannot go and spread — even the fatty piece is permitted — end of his words.
