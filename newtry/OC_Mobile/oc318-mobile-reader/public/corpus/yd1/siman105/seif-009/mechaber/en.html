Something salted that is not eaten because of its salt — its law is like boiling and it emits to forbid kedei kelipah; and if it is gid fat and its tendons and membranes, one should be stringent and require netilat makom; and if it is actual milk, sixty is needed to nullify it; and if the meat salted with it is fatty, even in gid fat and its tendons sixty is needed to nullify it and to remove from the place it touched kedei netilat makom, and at least kelipah; and all this regarding the piece itself to which milk was clinging; and regarding other pieces salted together — if there is not in each one by itself sixty to nullify the milk, they are forbidden; and they do not combine together to nullify the milk, for milk does not exude from piece to piece without gravy; and if it is not known whether it touched all of them, all are forbidden; and if it is known that it touched only one and it is not known which — all are permitted, for one in two is nullified, even if they are pieces fit to honor guests: {Rama: And some say all salting forbids only kedei kelipah (Tur and Mordechai in name of Ra'aviah and R' Efraim and poskim and Ran and Rashba in Mishnah and Ra'ah in Beit HaBayit daf 92 in name of Ramban, and requires study on what Beit Yosef wrote siman 91 and Tosafot beginning general rule 38 — see there, Ramban for the opposite); and because we are not expert between fatty and lean meat, the practice is to estimate in every salting with sixty as in cooking (Mahari"v and Arukh and Hagahot ShaDa and Mordechai in name of Or Zarua and all later ones) — that if there are sixty among all together against all pieces to which milk clings, then all is permitted except that piece to which issur clings; however that piece the issur touched requires a little kelipah (Rashba); and if he does not know which touched, all is permitted (Mordechai perek 3 and ShaDa); and if there are not sixty, all is forbidden, and one should not change the practice; and even though there is some leniency here if the issur was fatty and exuding — nevertheless one may rely in such a case on the words of the lenient ones, and it appears correct to them that salting does not forbid except kedei kelipah, so we need not estimate between fatty and lean issur, for we are not expert; and all this is in issur to which oiliness applies, such as milk or brine and the like; but in issur that has no oiliness at all, such as chametz on Pesach — for all, salting forbids only kedei kelipah (the Rav's reasoning from the meaning of the aforementioned poskim); and see in Orach Chaim laws of chametz how they practice; and see above siman 64 if membranes or threads of milk were salted with meat — some say in great loss one may permit through salting via kelipah even though they already practiced estimating every salting with sixty even in lean issur; and one may rely on this in lean issur that is only practice to estimate with sixty (according to Rambam's view and those who follow him and Maharam Padua in responsum to the Rav siman 90); but not in fatty issur that by law requires sixty:}
