And if it is the fat of the gid. According to his reason, as he wrote in seif 5 that lean milk does not spread and does not require sixty to nullify it.
And if the meat that was salted, etc. That it adheres to the milk, as he concludes afterward — and all this, etc.
If there is not in each one of them, etc. Meaning: if milk touched each one of them and there is not in each one sixty against the milk — forbidden, lest it absorbed much and has no partnership to nullify.
That milk does not spread from piece to piece. From this is also inferred a leniency — namely, if it is clear milk touched only one of them, then the others are permitted; and I wonder at Shulchan Aruch, whose rulings contradict one another: above seif 7 he ruled that if it absorbed from spreading things, such as fatty substance, it forbids its fellow even without gravy — and that is Rashba's view whom Tur brought; and here he ruled that milk absorption does not emerge without gravy — and that is Maharam's view in Mordechai chapter 3; and Beit Yosef brings it and says: here it deals with lean milk that does not spread, for in Mordechai it is written explicitly that this law is on the milk on the stomach, which is the main milk; and further, if the milk were lean, why would each need sixty by itself — therefore one must say Maharam disputes Rashba and holds that even in fatty issur it does not emerge without gravy, whatever is absorbed in it — end; wonder on Shulchan Aruch that he did not notice this dispute; also in Levush it is written like Shulchan Aruch here — and it is very wondrous; and one should not say that above it deals with roasting and here with salting — for we conclude in the Gemara salted is like roasted — and Beit Yosef and Shulchan Aruch's conclusion is thus, that it is like roasted.
Even if they are pieces fit to be served to guests. The reason is that the milk in them is absorbed issur, and absorbed issur cannot make a piece fit to be served to guests, etc. — as written siman 101.
And some say that all salting does not forbid, etc. Meaning: even fatty substance — and it is Ravyah's view in Beit Yosef, because the issur does not pass through all but remains in its place; and Rama says that although it is fitting for us to be stringent and require sixty to satisfy those who hold that even in salting the issur spreads through all of it — that is in a fatty issur; and to be lenient in what is not fatty, but we are not expert, etc.
And even though there is in this some leniency if the issur is fatty. Explanation: regarding whether there is milk in that piece — for according to those who hold sixty is required, meaning sixty is required in that piece itself against the milk in it, and other pieces do not combine) to nullify milk in that piece; and according to custom we combine also other pieces; and further consider if the milk is not attached to any piece, only rests beside one of them — we should have said they do not combine, and in each one it touched, sixty is required by itself; nevertheless we hold all combine; and in Levush the explanation of the above leniency is regarding that we do not require a peel — for according to those who say salting forbids as much as a peel, the peel nevertheless becomes forbidden, etc.; and he was not precise here, for in truth even according to custom a peel is required where it touched — whereas where it did not touch, certainly nothing is needed according to all, for it forbids only the peel that touched it.
Such as milk or brine. One must say blood is also included in this, even though blood does not spread, as is found in many places — nevertheless sometimes there are mixtures of brine and fatty substance; therefore we find in siman 70 that salting forbids in blood in sixty. And I will write Rashal's conclusion in the chapter Gid HaNasheh siman 28: that which we find a distinction between cooking and salting and roasting and baking — that is specifically for R' Eliezer of Metz and those who follow him, who hold in these three one removes only the place of spreading and according to estimation; but we hold like R' Avraham of Sens in the name of R' Yehuda HaLavan and Maharai after them, that the law of these three is like cooking and requires sixty like other issurim; and automatically chametz on Pesach in any amount; and nevertheless we do not say in these three they are like cooking, only stringently — meaning sixty is required against the milk, and if it is attached, sixty against the entire piece; but not to be lenient — meaning not to require only as much as removal even when there is sixty — nevertheless as much as removal is required; therefore he ruled regarding matzah that folded somewhat in the oven before Pesach, sixty is required in that matzah against the issur and as much as removal, as Maharai wrote in Terumat HaDeshen — and not like Rama in Orach Chayyim, who did not require sixty in matzah. Further Rashal concludes in his Orach Chayyim and in his book that we do not hold as Tur wrote, that also in salting, and permitted fatty and issur lean — all is forbidden; for also in roasting this is a novelty, that we say permitted imparts taste to issur and makes it fatty, and a colleague does not do so — nevertheless in salting we certainly are not so stringent, only we say: wherever the issur cannot go and spread, even the fatty piece is permitted.
