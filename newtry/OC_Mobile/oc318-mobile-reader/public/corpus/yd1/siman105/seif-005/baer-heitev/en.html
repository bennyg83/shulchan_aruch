Its fat. Taz wrote, and these are his words: Beit Yosef wrote this is learned from siman 108 regarding smell — that even the heter fat is forbidden; and the reason: the heter goes and seasons the issur and returns and gives smell to the heter; and it is difficult for me, for we hold the forbidden cannot forbid except where the issur itself can go there; and I saw they reconcile that here it deals when the issur is forbidden from itself; but below it deals from absorbed issur — and this is not correct at all to distinguish, for even in issur forbidden from itself and it is lean — it has no power to forbid more than kedei netilah; rather certainly this matter has no basis; and the essence of the rule; and Maharshal wrote: a piece that has blood of itself is also called forbidden from absorption and not issur forbidden from itself.
Place. In that place the issur is considered more as if it were apparent; and see in siman 94 seif 7; and Shach wrote: Maharshal ruled that in lean one should require kelipeh in salting and removal in roasting even if there is sixty; but in fat, Maharai wrote: one who is stringent is a fool walking in darkness; and the view of the early famous poskim is as the Mechaber — that even if there is sixty in it, netilah mamash is required even in fat.
Applies. And Darkei Moshe wrote: brine of nevelah or the like also spreads — similar to milk; and Shach wrote: it is explained in several places that blood does not apply regarding oiliness; however in siman 69 it will be explained that the practice is to estimate in salting with sixty even in completely lean; if so likewise in roasting, for roasting and salting are equivalent; and that which the Rav stated here anonymously that a completely lean issur forbids only kedei netilah — that is regarding d'rabbanan issur.
