That the milk of an animal is lean, etc. — and for this Rosh in the chapter Kol Shehu and Tur in Orach Chayyim siman 467 wrote that milk that fell into a pan does not forbid the entire pan — their intent is with lean milk — requires further study on the words of Sefer HaTerumah that Mordechai brought in chapter 3 in his name, who wrote that therefore Rashi explained on that olive-sized piece of fat that fell into a pot, which is a ladle — that if it were without gravy, even that piece on which it fell would not be forbidden, only as much as a peel — requires further study, for behold milk spreads, and in any case that piece is entirely forbidden; and Sefer HaTerumah is not available to examine, and requires further study.
And permitted that was roasted with it fatty, etc. — see Shach s.k. 19 on the difficulty in Tur's words, for he ruled that what became forbidden cannot forbid. And Shach answered to distinguish between the body of the issur — for it imparts taste from the permitted — but regarding what is absorbed we do not say it imparts taste; and Rama distinguishes in Darchei Moshe between lean milk — in this we say it imparts taste — and not in other matters. And Taz wondered on him in s.k. 10, for we derive this law of imparting taste from reicha miltha, that we say permitted imparts taste to issur, and there it does not deal with milk, only meat — and in truth, like his words; and that which he wrote to reject Shach's words — Shach already answered in Nekudot HaKesef; but on Shach's words one should analyze from what Tosafot wrote, for the fat of the gid spreads — and one may answer the fat of the gid is lean; and further, that fat does not forbid more than the gid; and since the gid does not forbid, its fat is not forbidden — and this is Rabbenu Yerucham's view, etc.; and likewise Rosh there; and it is difficult: what of it if the fat were entirely permitted and they did not treat it as issur at all — nevertheless there is fat here and it imparts taste to the gid to spread through the entire thigh. And for this answer we do not say the fat of the gid is lean; and likewise Rosh wrote in the chapter Kol Shehu regarding a grain found in a chicken on Pesach, that it forbids the entire chicken because of rolling — the wheel carries taste through all of it; but if it did not roll, it forbids only as much as removal; and he does not distinguish whether the chicken was fatty or this proves he does not hold it imparts taste even to the body of the issur; and likewise Magen Avraham inferred in Orach Chayyim siman 447; therefore one must say the answer in truth is to distinguish between something that by nature is sometimes fatty or lean, such as meat — in this we say it imparts taste, like that case of scent; but the gid in its substance is lean and fatty substance cannot apply to it; and likewise chametz on Pesach, which is lean by nature — in this we do not say the permitted goes, etc.; and all this is in the body of the issur; but in absorbed issur we do not say thus regarding fatty substance — and Shach answered; and therefore Tur wrote two pieces of meat in which issur was absorbed and permitted roasted together, that if the issur is lean, even if the permitted is fatty — nevertheless it does not forbid more than as much as a peel, since the issur is absorbed, the fatty substance does not impart taste to carry, etc. — and this is Shach's answer; and even though meat sometimes has fatty substance — both answers are necessary; and this law we learn: if so, what lacks a peel should forbid again — and so always. And according to the authors who hold what became forbidden cannot forbid, etc. — still difficult: meat that has fatty substance forbidden by a peel should forbid one another; and if so, still difficult: a thigh that has a gid, since there is fat of the gid — according to this view that fatty meat forbidden as much as a peel from the gid should forbid from here onward; and likewise a chicken from a grain; and therefore one must say Shach's answer as well — both are necessary — and this is clear; and otherwise Rashba's view, as Peri Chadash wrote explicitly, that we do not say at all the permitted imparts taste to issur — therefore one may be lenient — and both answers as I wrote are true. That which Maggid Mishneh wrote in the laws of Pesach and Shulchan Aruch copied in Orach Chayyim siman 447 seif 1: a loaf of chametz that touched a loaf of matzah and there is nothing there that spreads — it forbids only as much as a peel — implies there is fatty substance that spreads to forbid all of it, even though chametz in itself is lean — nevertheless permitted imparts taste to it; and likewise Magen Avraham inferred there — possibly he nevertheless disagrees; and Shulchan Aruch who copied it followed his view, for here he omitted this law of what became forbidden cannot forbid, etc., and was concerned to be stringent. But in truth his view is void against Tosafot, Rosh, Tur, Rashba that I wrote; and especially one may say in what Maggid Mishneh wrote "and there is nothing there that spreads" — his intent is not on fatty substance, only the explanation that they are in a pot — for even though there is no gravy, then steam in the pot spreads taste through all of it, for he holds possibly like Ran, and he cited a decided matter; and therefore for practical law the main thing is as I wrote.
