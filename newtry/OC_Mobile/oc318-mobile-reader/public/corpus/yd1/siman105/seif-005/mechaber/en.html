In what matters — that roasted food forbids only kedei netilah: thigh with its gid and the like, a lean thing that lacks power to exude throughout the piece; but a fat kid roasted in its milk — if there are not in the whole kid sixty against all the milk in it, it is forbidden to eat even from the top of its ear, for since it is fatty it exudes throughout; but if it is lean, even though there is not in it sixty against all the milk in it, it forbids only kedei netilah, for milk of an animal is lean — milk is lean by nature and does not exude; and even a lean piece of issur and a piece of heter that was roasted with it — if the heter is fatty, the issur exudes throughout it; and anything that has exuding that fell to a known place on the piece in roasting — even though there is in the piece sixty to nullify the issur, netilat makom is required {Rama: (Mordechai in name of several rabbis and Semak and Hagahot Ashiri and Or Zarua general rule 14 and Agur in name of Maharil): Rama: And all cooked without gravy or baked — its law is like roasting in all matters (Tur and Beit Yosef); and some say we are not expert which is called lean or fatty, and one should forbid in every case until there are sixty (Hagahot ShaDa and Hagahot Rambam forbidden foods and Or Zarua and Semak and Agur in name of Maharil); and even if there are sixty, netilat makom is required (Arukh) — and such is practiced; and specifically in issur of milk or other issur to which oiliness applies; but in issur to which oiliness does not apply and it is certainly lean, it forbids only kedei netilah (see seif 9) (so it appears in Beit Yosef):}
