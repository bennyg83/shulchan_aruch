And even if the issur piece is lean, etc. Beit Yosef wrote that this is learned from what we find thus in siman 108 regarding reicha miltha — that even permitted fatty substance is forbidden for one who holds reicha miltha — and the reason is that permitted goes and imparts taste to the issur and returns and gives scent to the permitted. And I wondered: behold Maharai wrote in Hagahat ShaDaL on the law of reicha miltha, and these are his words: and one must distinguish between this and that which we say that what became forbidden cannot forbid, except where the issur itself can go there — for this too, the lean nevelah has no imparting of scent, for scent is different, because imparting carries the scent and the lean taste with it — end of his words; learn from this that the essence of this stringency is because of scent — for there we do not care about that view that what became forbidden cannot forbid; and since we hold reicha miltha in siman 108, b'dieved it is permitted — why should it be forbidden here even b'dieved, for there is here only scent issur — for this view that what became forbidden cannot forbid was said even when they touch one another — and nevertheless Maharai wrote we do not say thus except for reicha miltha specifically; and this is simple, that Tur did not intend here to forbid because of scent, for likewise in salting it is so, and scent does not apply; and Beit Yosef further challenged at the end of this siman from what Tur wrote there that what became forbidden cannot forbid, etc., and even if the permitted is fatty, etc., it is permitted; and Darchei Moshe wrote that here it deals with lean milk in the issur piece, that he calls it lean since it does not spread; and specifically in this we say the fatty substance spreads, since in any case there is milk on it; and it is difficult: behold we derive reicha miltha from it, and there it deals with lean meat, not lean milk; and likewise challenged in Drisha on it. And further difficult: behold milk was not mentioned here, only an issur piece. And I saw they reconcile: here, since the issur is forbidden from itself, we say even if the issur is lean and the permitted is fatty that carries the issur with it — since necessarily it emits nevelah gravy — therefore the fatty substance of the permitted mixes with nevelah gravy; but below, where it deals with absorbed issur, we say even though that piece that received the issur is fatty, the fatty substance in it does not carry the absorption of issur with it — and these words are entirely incorrect, for both have a boundary and limit: when the issur is from itself and it is lean, it has power to forbid a piece beside it without gravy up to as much as removal, and afterward its power ceases; and in absorbed issur it has power to go to the end of that piece itself where the issur fell, and from there onward it has no power at all without gravy, even though it rests directly on it — if so, a fortiori: what for absorbed issur, where in the very place of the issur there is fatty substance — nevertheless it does not help to give power to the absorbed issur to go outside its boundary — all the more for issur from itself, whose boundary to forbid is up to as much as a peel in the permitted piece, and no more — that the fatty substance of the permitted piece should not help take it outside its boundary; thus is it clear to me outside the boundary of another — the peel in issur from itself, as outside the boundary in the entire piece beside a piece forbidden through absorption of issur. And further clear proof from the source of this law, learned from what Tosafot and Rosh wrote in the chapter Gid HaNasheh, whom Beit Yosef brought in this siman — namely from what they wrote, that if a piece is found half in brine and half outside the brine, that which is outside the brine is not forbidden from what is below, and even if there is fatty substance in the brine we do not say it became nevelah and spreads upward, since the brine itself does not spread — and likewise Tur siman 69 and here at the end of the siman; and behold before you that brine is forbidden from itself, being blood that emerges from the meat — and even so fatty substance does not harm it to carry it where it goes; and what Beit Yosef wrote to reconcile, that blood is slick — is wondrous: just as there fatty substance does not cause blood, what is not its nature — so too it does not cause to absorb what is not in its nature to absorb anyway. Rather certainly this matter has no foundation, and the essence of the general rule — they said thus without examination; and Tur's words are wondrous, as Beit Yosef wrote; and were I not afraid, I would emend Tur's words in this language — except: even an issur piece that is fatty and a permitted piece that is lean, etc. — and this is what he says: not only in one piece that has milk in it — namely like that kid they roasted in its milk mentioned above — rather even if there are two pieces, one issur and one permitted, and that of issur is fatty and spreads the issur through all the permitted — and this is proven: if according to the text before us it is difficult — he should have said: not only in fatty issur and lean permitted, rather even in fatty permitted; also the wording "spreads through all of it" written in Tur is not resolved according to the text before us — he should have said that it spreads in the issur and returns and spreads in the permitted; and according to what I wrote, everything is resolved; and see from this what I will write in this siman seif 9 — Rashal's conclusion on this for practical law; and Rashal wrote in the chapter Gid HaNasheh siman 45: a piece that has blood of itself is also called a piece forbidden through absorption, and not issur from itself.
