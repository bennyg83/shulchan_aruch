And some are lenient regarding what is outside the brine. From the rabbi's words in Turei Zahav general rule 22 section 4 it appears this is primary. And so Rosh and Rashba in chapter 105 regarding meat salted in an unperforated vessel; and even a permitted piece inside the brine that is fatty; and so Taz siman 69 section 18 and other poskim. And we forbid there meat salted in an unperforated vessel even what is outside the brine — but this was explained there s.k. 88: the reason is because when salted in an unperforated vessel the blood spread from place to place — see there. And perhaps for this the rabbi hinted in Bedek Malkhut; but certainly if the prohibition is fatty, even what is outside the brine is forbidden, as the poskim imply there; and so Tur at the end of this section; and Orach Chayyim general rule 30 section 10; and general rule 10. Therefore it would appear to forbid for us what is outside the pickling, since we do not distinguish between lean and fatty as below in section 5 and section 9 in hagahah — unless one might say the rabbi should not write a leniency here since for us all forbid; and all the more he should not rely on Turei Zahav's lenient reasoning as primary; and furthermore in section 70 he wrote that meat which fell into brine — what is outside is permitted; and Orach Chayyim also holds we do not distinguish lean from fatty, yet in pickling he permits what is outside; therefore it appears that even though we do not distinguish, this applies only in salting and roasting and similar cases, but we are not so stringent in all respects; and so I wrote in s.k. 17 — see there:
And if it is kavush inside brine or vinegar, etc. — the same as cooked. The Mechaber's view is like Rosh to distinguish between brine and other things: in brine we do not require me'eit l'eit because the boiling of the salt suffices when it rests the measure that one would put it on the fire and it begins to boil; and he equated vinegar to brine; and he follows Beit Yosef who wrote: kavush in brine — Rosh wrote at end of chapter 25 that its measure is as if placed on the fire until it boils and begins to cook; if kavush inside brine in this measure, everything inside the brine is forbidden; in less than this measure only as much as a peel, the law of salting — behold it is like boiling — end of his words. One might wonder: behold Rosh himself wrote in his commentary at end of Avodah Zarah that even in vinegar we require a full day; and Beit Yosef brought it in this siman — but certainly one should distinguish vinegar from brine: brine that we forbid because of the power of salt in it suffices in the measure that one puts it on the fire and it boils, because salted is like boiling as is explained in Rosh — unlike vinegar; and the Mechaber himself wrote in siman 104 therefore if it fell into beer or vinegar in cold and he removed it whole — if it did not rest me'eit l'eit it is permitted — requires study. (Again I saw in Sefer Shareit Yehudah he brought Beit Yosef's responsum where he apologized for his words in Beit Yosef that he agrees in vinegar one needs me'eit l'eit and his words in Shulchan Aruch here are strained to reconcile — see there.) Also Bach ruled that in vinegar it is kavush immediately like brine; and he wrote Rosh agrees to this — and it is not so as I explained; also in Derishah he directed Rosh's view to another view that is incorrect because he wanted to reconcile what Hagahot Maimoniyot wrote in the name of Rosh (I did not find this in the new Hagahot before me) that when we say kavush kemevushal — this is in vinegar and brine as Rashi explained — end of his words; and this needs no answer, for certainly Hagahot Maimoniyot did not mean this Rosh but another such as Rosh of Palermo or Rosh of Lunel, as is Hagahot Maimoniyot and Mordechai's way to bring them; rather it appears like Rosh and Tur and Beit Yosef that vinegar equals other liquids. And so Mordechai end of chapter 2 of Beitzah; and so Maharshal in Orach Shemesh and in his book chapter 95 seif 49 and chapter 105 siman 91; and so Orach Chayyim general rule 30 law 8 that in vinegar it is not kavush except me'eit l'eit — see there. And so in Sefer Alef-Resh daf 37 side 2 and daf 54 side 2: Orach Chayyim wrote general rule 30 section 9: thin milk that rested me'eit l'eit in a rinsed meat bowl of the same day — they are forbidden even b'dieved; and the same for other permitted steeped in a prohibition vessel of the same day — end of his words; and he copied in Sefer Alef-Resh daf 54 side 2. One might say: since it only became kavush after me'eit l'eit — if so, after me'eit l'eit it is nosen taam lifgam; and also in general rule 33 end section 3 he wrote if it rested even lukewarm where yad soledes bo does not apply — in this it never becomes same-day at all, for the cold food in it does not make the taste of absorption in the vessel walls for the better, even though regarding prohibition we forbid in such a case if it rested me'eit l'eit in a same-day prohibition vessel as is said above in the responsum kavush — nevertheless the taste is impaired — end of his words; and his words are not understood, for if it rested me'eit l'eit the taste is also impaired.
