Pickled. Or Zarua wrote: if the meat stood long in its salting, me'eit l'eis, even the vessel is forbidden b'dieved and requires sixty against the entire vessel if it is of wood or earthenware, for we hold kavush k'mevushal even in vessels; and one should not infer from a barrel of yayin nesekh that is permitted to brew beer in it l'chatchila, for regarding yayin nesekh they were lenient; however in metal vessels we do not require sixty against it b'dieved unless it became forbidden through boiling; and likewise permitted matter that stood in a vessel of issur — ben yomo — and likewise issur that stood in a vessel of heter me'eit l'eis — the vessel is forbidden — end of his words. And Taz wrote on this, and these are his words: I cannot understand why the heter should be forbidden in a vessel of issur ben yomo, for behold the issur is from pickling; after the measure of pickling which is me'eit l'eis it becomes cooked, and behold at that moment it is not ben yomo, etc. {Rama: (And the author of Magen Avraham heard in name of R' Yaakov from Lublin: granted if the vessel stands empty the taste is impaired; but here where the heter stands in it constantly — if so it already began to receive taste to its benefit, and after me'al le'al it truly becomes as cooked and therefore is forbidden. And likewise in Hagahot Issur VeHeter siman 69 and siman 105 there, unlike Acharonim who wrote to be lenient in this; and see Peri Chadash who also disagrees with Taz.)} And it appears we should not forbid b'dieved what stood heter in a vessel of issur me'eit l'eis {Rama: (and in Taz there is an error here, see there)} except l'chatchila as the law of nitelat perah; also what Or Zarua wrote to distinguish between wood and earthenware vessels and metal vessels — we do not find this distinction anywhere; rather the essence is that in all vessels we do not forbid them b'dieved as stated. And Shach wrote he found in a manuscript of the Rav in a gloss on Or Zarua that he sensed this difficulty and wrote that it deals when the issur is somewhat apparent; and the language of ben yomo does not imply so; and moreover behold in general rule 40 he wrote a rinsed bowl — requires study.
They are lenient. Shach wrote: from the Rav's words in Turei Chadash it appears this is the essence regarding a lean issur; and even though we do not distinguish between lean and fatty — that is in salting and roasting and the like; but to this extent we were not stringent.
And doubt. Meaning: we see it was pickled only there is doubt whether it was pickled me'eit l'eis; and Taz left this law in requires-study and his view is to permit in doubt, see there [and Peri Chadash rejects his proof and the essence of the law is as Rama] {Rama: (and in Nekudot HaKesef he attacked him and rejected his proofs, see there; and see in siman 104 in Taz s.k. 3 it implies he contradicts his words here, see there, and it is simple.)}
Vinegar. Shach wrote: requires study at the beginning of siman 104 the Mechaber ruled that in vinegar one must also soak me'eit l'eis, see there; and therefore he brought that truly vinegar is equivalent to other liquids that are not forbidden until it stood me'al le'al {Rama: (and see in Orach Chayim siman 467 in Magen Avraham s.k. 28 who distinguishes between beer vinegar or wine vinegar, see there; and Peri Chadash wrote vinegar requires me'al le'al, and likewise Kesef Gadol, and as Shach's view.)}
For brine. And see in siman 69 seif 18; and Shach wrote: nevertheless in any case salting is like boiling immediately to forbid all of it; and practical difference: where issur fell into brine of heter — d'rabbanan issur and heter are truly lean, for in salting even for Rav Bechor Shor it forbids only kelipeh; but in pickling at the measure that they place it on fire and it boils — it forbids all of it.
