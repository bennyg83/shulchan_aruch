(1) [Seif 1] Forbidden food that fell with permitted food for twenty-four hours in cold is called pickled and behold it is as cooked, etc. Regarding vessels — whether we say pickled is like cooked, i.e. if forbidden food was steeped in a permitted vessel for the measure of pickling which is twenty-four hours, whether the vessel becomes forbidden — there is a dispute. Ohr HaChaim in his compendium general rule 37 end law 1 wrote that pickling does not forbid except sixty against the peel; and in his compendium general rule 30 law 9 wrote that also in vessels there is pickling and if forbidden food was steeped in it for twenty-four hours the entire vessel becomes forbidden; and likewise Tosafot Chadashim end of general rule 5 wrote this, and it is not as Ohr HaChaim wrote in general rule 37 that pickling does not forbid in a vessel except peel. Kenesset HaGedolah in Hagahat Turei Zahav section 6, Lechem HaPanim section 1, Mishnat Zahav section 1, Arukh HaShulchan section 1, Chokhmat Adam general rule 57 section 11. And regarding what we say later siman 135 seif 13 regarding yayin nesech that even when putting it away for storage it does not absorb more than a peel — Ohr HaChaim already answered this there and wrote that for yayin nesech they were lenient, see there. And likewise Mishnat Zahav there in the name of Shach that one should not learn from yayin nesech, see there. And as is clear from the words of the other Acharonim mentioned that one should not learn from yayin nesech who hold like Ohr HaChaim. However Taz s.k. 1 wrote to challenge Ohr HaChaim's words mentioned and concluded that after the fact one should not forbid them through forbidden food that fell for twenty-four hours; and likewise Mishnat Zahav there end of section 1 that he found a manuscript for Taz that pickled does not absorb in a vessel except peel and there is sixty in the food against the peel — end of his words — and it appears he learned this from the law of yayin nesech. And likewise is Shach's view at end of s.k. 2 who wrote that also otherwise there is an opinion that it is not called pickled except in three days etc. — it appears he does not hold like Ohr HaChaim in this. And likewise Lechem Pri Chadash in seif 1 that according to Bach and Shach one does not forbid after the fact. However from Shach's words later siman 135 s.k. 33 and siman 137 s.k. 9 it appears explicitly that he holds there is pickling in vessels, see there. And if so what is written here is not precise. It turns out this law is a twofold dispute whether we learn from yayin nesech and peel suffices or not. And see further Rabbi Chakal Yisrael Chayei David part 1 siman 87 and Orach Chayim what he expanded at length on this matter. He brought the views of those who forbid and those who permit, see there; and therefore it appears that in great loss one may rely on those who permit — that is if forbidden food was steeped in a vessel for twenty-four hours and before he has his own twenty-four hours of steeping in it a permitted food, one may be lenient only in great loss but not in minor loss since many forbade.
