(1) [Seif 1] Forbidden food that fell with permitted food for twenty-four hours in cold is called pickled and behold it is as cooked, etc. Regarding vessels — whether we say pickled is like cooked, i.e. if forbidden food was steeped in a permitted vessel for the measure of pickling which is twenty-four hours, whether the vessel becomes forbidden — there is a dispute. Ohr HaChaim in his compendium general rule 37 end law 1 wrote that pickling does not forbid except sixty against the peel; and in his compendium general rule 30 law 9 wrote that also in vessels there is pickling and if forbidden food was steeped in it for twenty-four hours the entire vessel becomes forbidden; and likewise Tosafot Chadashim end of general rule 5 wrote this, and it is not as Ohr HaChaim wrote in general rule 37 that pickling does not forbid in a vessel except peel. Kenesset HaGedolah in Hagahat Turei Zahav section 6, Lechem HaPanim section 1, Mishnat Zahav section 1, Arukh HaShulchan section 1, Chokhmat Adam general rule 57 section 11. And regarding what we say later siman 135 seif 13 regarding yayin nesech that even when putting it away for storage it does not absorb more than a peel — Ohr HaChaim already answered this there and wrote that for yayin nesech they were lenient, see there. And likewise Mishnat Zahav there in the name of Shach that one should not learn from yayin nesech, see there. And as is clear from the words of the other Acharonim mentioned that one should not learn from yayin nesech who hold like Ohr HaChaim. However Taz s.k. 1 wrote to challenge Ohr HaChaim's words mentioned and concluded that after the fact one should not forbid them through forbidden food that fell for twenty-four hours; and likewise Mishnat Zahav there end of section 1 that he found a manuscript for Taz that pickled does not absorb in a vessel except peel and there is sixty in the food against the peel — end of his words — and it appears he learned this from the law of yayin nesech. And likewise is Shach's view at end of s.k. 2 who wrote that also otherwise there is an opinion that it is not called pickled except in three days etc. — it appears he does not hold like Ohr HaChaim in this. And likewise Lechem Pri Chadash in seif 1 that according to Bach and Shach one does not forbid after the fact. However from Shach's words later siman 135 s.k. 33 and siman 137 s.k. 9 it appears explicitly that he holds there is pickling in vessels, see there. And if so what is written here is not precise. It turns out this law is a twofold dispute whether we learn from yayin nesech and peel suffices or not. And see further Rabbi Chakal Yisrael Chayei David part 1 siman 87 and Orach Chayim what he expanded at length on this matter. He brought the views of those who forbid and those who permit, see there; and therefore it appears that in great loss one may rely on those who permit — that is if forbidden food was steeped in a vessel for twenty-four hours and before he has his own twenty-four hours of steeping in it a permitted food, one may be lenient only in great loss but not in minor loss since many forbade.
(2) And what is the case — in a wooden vessel or earthenware vessel one should forbid, but in a metal vessel one should not forbid if forbidden food fell for twenty-four hours. Ohr HaChaim there. However Minchat Yosef on Tosafot Chadashim there section 12 wrote that there is no distinction between wooden, earthenware, or metal vessels regarding pickling. And likewise Beit Levi section 1; but Peleti end of section 1 brought support for Ohr HaChaim's words and likewise Arukh HaShulchan there that the main view is as Ohr HaChaim's words. And in a glass vessel one who is lenient in great loss did not lose. Mishnat Zahav there end of the words "and if". And this is according to his view that he forbids pickling of vessels in every case; but according to what was written in the previous section that in great loss one may be lenient there is no difference, and all the more so in metal vessels and glass vessels where there is a dispute.
(3) And likewise a vessel of forbidden food that is not absorbed from the beginning that permitted food was steeped in it for twenty-four hours — the whole thing is forbidden. Ohr HaChaim general rule 30 law 9. But Bach s.k. 1 challenged this — why should the permitted food be forbidden in a vessel of forbidden food that is not absorbed from the beginning, since the prohibition is from pickling and behold at that moment it is not absorbed from the beginning and the vessel has no power to forbid the permitted food steeped in it, see there; and likewise Shach challenged s.k. 2. And Kelalei HaGahot in Hagahat Turei Zahav section 6 wrote that for practical halachah we rule like Taz and Shach that permitted food steeped in a vessel of forbidden food that is not absorbed from the beginning, or milk in a meat vessel or vice versa — the food is not forbidden because it is nosen taam lifgam and permitted after the fact. Beit Levi section 1, Chokhmat Adam section 3, Chakal Yisrael there. However Peri Chadash section 1 agreed with Ohr HaChaim. And likewise Lechem Pri Chadash section 1, Kereti section 1. It turns out this matter is a twofold dispute and therefore one should not permit after the fact except in great loss; and what Zivchei Tzedek section 2 wrote to permit even in minor loss and he brought support that this is Shulchan Aruch's view that there is no pickling for vessels since he ruled regarding yayin nesech that there is no pickling for vessels — we already wrote above in section 1 that regarding yayin nesech all agree except whether we learn from yayin nesech or not; and if so from Shulchan Aruch's words one cannot decide that he holds that in every case there is no pickling for vessels; and if so we return to our law that this matter is a twofold dispute and one should not permit except in great loss. And see above siman 69 section 33.
(6) There. Forbidden food that fell with permitted food for twenty-four hours in cold is called pickled, etc. And if the pickling was in stages — such as forbidden food fell with permitted food for twelve hours and they removed the permitted food outside and after some hours returned and placed it into the forbidden food for one hour — whether it is considered pickled in such a case where there was an interruption in between or not, see above siman 70 section 242 where we brought the dispute in this and wrote there that the consensus of most poskim is to permit after the fact if it already happened, and even one who wants to be stringent nevertheless in great loss it is permitted, see there.
(7) And pickling applies only to a solid thing in a liquid thing — one emits and absorbs from this to that, not in two moist things that would immediately mix and forbid each other, and not in two solid things even if they are somewhat moist — pickling does not apply to them even if they stand many days, thus. And even fat and forbidden food placed inside it — as long as it is hard it is not called pickled; only if it is soft as stated above siman 104 in hagahah; but if it is soft that the forbidden food placed inside it moves from place to place it is called pickled. And forbidden or permitted food pickled in cold water and the like — Minchat Yosef on Tosafot Chadashim in responsum at end of the book siman 14 permitted and said that in cold pickling does not apply and likewise it is reasonable that it does not move and all the more so becomes like a dry stone inside it and does not emit; but Beit Levi in responsum at end of the book siman 29 and here section 1 wrote in the name of Bach that it is pickled and I did not understand — for whatever is hard pickling does not apply and such is the halachah at least in great loss. And dry with dry that are somewhat moist — even sealing does not require after the fact and nevertheless initially one should take like the thickness of a finger. Mishnat Zahav section 1 in the name of Beit Yosef Chayei Adam part 1 section 1 and in Ein Mishpat there sections 3 and 4, Arukh HaShulchan section 8; and likewise Chokhmat Adam wrote this, general rule 58 section 1.
(8) There hagahah. And wherever we say pickled is like cooked — even what is outside the pickling is forbidden, etc. And there are those who are lenient regarding what is outside. And likewise in Tosafot Chadashim general rule 22 law 6. Taz s.k. 2. Shach s.k. 1. Kenesset HaGedolah in Hagahat Turei Zahav section 4, Peri Chadash section 2, Peri Toar section 2, Lechem Pri Chadash section 2, Beit Levi section 2, Chokhmat Adam section 4, Shulchan Petach David section 1, Chokhmat Adam there. And even in forbidden oil — in salting what is outside the brine is permitted in pickling. Kenesset HaGedolah there. Minchat Yosef on Tosafot Chadashim there or Taz. Beit Levi there. But Shach there wrote that this is when the forbidden food is lean — but if the forbidden food is oil even what is outside the brine is forbidden. And likewise Peri Chadash there that if the forbidden piece is oil even what is outside the brine is forbidden for all — because the fat spreads. Kereti section 2, Chokhmat Adam there. Beit Yosef there section 4. However Arukh HaShulchan end of section 14 wrote that from Rama's wording it does not appear so but certainly in pickling for twenty-four hours also in oil it does not spread upward. And Shulchan Petach David there wrote that when there is no great loss one should forbid in oil like Shach in pickling for twenty-four hours. And such is the practical law since there is a dispute one should not be lenient except in great loss. And this is if there is not sixty in the pickling against the forbidden food — the permitted food that is outside is permitted in great loss; but if there is sixty in the permitted food inside the pickling against the forbidden food, otherwise everything is permitted even what is inside the pickling without great loss and all the more so what is outside.
(9) However from piece to piece that is placed one on the other it appears that even Shach would agree that in oil outside the pickling it does not emit from this to that. Shulchan Petach David there end of section 1 — meaning even for Shach who forbids in oil what is outside the pickling — if there is a piece on that piece from which emission goes outside the pickling, that piece is permitted and this is even without great loss.
(10) And in pickling in brine and in vinegar it is not inferior to salting — we are stringent in every case. Arukh HaShulchan there. Meaning even for those who permit in oil what is outside the pickling — if it was pickled in brine or vinegar one should forbid what is outside the pickling because they are sharp things and spread upward.
(11) And all this is in pickled that is cold; but in cooked — such as if a forbidden piece fell into a permitted dish and part of the piece is outside the gravy — we require sixty against the entire piece even what is outside the gravy because through the gravy that is boiling it emits and absorbs in all of it. Ohr HaChaim general rule 28 end of section 3, Chokhmat Adam in its explanation section 70; and likewise above siman 94 section 2, see there.
(12) And if the gravy is forbidden and a permitted piece fell into it and part of it is outside the gravy — we require sixty in the permitted piece that is inside the gravy and that part that is outside the gravy does not combine for sixty to nullify the forbidden food — we go to stringency as stated above siman 92 section 5, see there.
(13) There in hagahah. And doubt of pickled is forbidden. Because pickled is forbidden from the Torah. Ohr HaChaim general rule 30 end law 1, Kenesset HaGedolah in Hagahat Turei Zahav section 3 and challenged Bach s.k. 3 who disagrees and likewise Shach in Nekudat HaKesef challenged him. Peri Chadash section 3, Peri Toar section 3, Lechem Pri Chadash section 3, Beit Levi section 3, Kereti section 3, Mishnat Zahav section 1 in the words "and pickling", Arukh HaShulchan section 4, Chokhmat Adam general rule 58 section 4, Beit Yosef Chayei Adam part 1 section 2.
(14) There in hagahah. And doubt of pickled is forbidden. Meaning we see it was pickled only that there is doubt whether it was pickled a full day or not. Taz s.k. 3. Lechem Pri Chadash there. Beit Levi there. Mishnat Zahav section 3.
