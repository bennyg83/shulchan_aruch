Issur that fell, etc. Taz wondered on Orach Chayyim, who wrote that a vessel forbidden through kavush may not have permitted matter soaked in it from me'eit l'eit — for when is it kavush? At the end of me'eit l'eit, and already noten ta'am li'fegam; and likewise Shach above siman 69; and Peri Chadash wrote that completion of kavush is at the end of me'eit l'eit, but noten ta'am li'fegam is not until after me'eit l'eit finishes; and Magen Avraham in Orach Chayyim siman 447 preceded him — see there. But all this does not suffice, for if so it must be that the issur emerges and the permitted enters the vessel without resting a single moment — for if one waits, still impairment and kavush come together, and this is impossible to narrow so it does not delay even one moment; therefore a remedy for this question has not yet arisen. And what appeared to me — that it is far to perceive by sense a cold thing that was kavush twenty-four hours, that all twenty-four hours until the last moment it should do no action at all to absorb or emit anything, and in the last moment of twenty-four hours it should do its action to absorb and emit — this thing reason does not compel, only it emits slowly and absorbs slowly, only taste is not sensed until completion of twenty-four hours, then the action of emission and absorption of complete taste finishes; and if so, there is no longer noten ta'am li'fegam, for most taste already emitted before and taste was also absorbed before, and it awakens to absorb so it should not be impaired, since it absorbed in it anew — see there and requires further study. And after I wrote this I found likewise in responsum Chut HaMeshulash daf 100, who also wrote this reasoning — see there and in the omissions; and the matter is close to natural reality. However I do not know from where Orach Chayyim learned this, that when one says this from himself without proof from the early ones, may their light shine. And in truth one may understand in Tosafot Chullin daf 8 side 2 s.v. one who slaughters, and likewise in Avodah Zarah, and likewise in Rosh, who all inferred from gentile oil that R' Yehuda the Prince permitted — for he holds noten ta'am li'fegam is permitted — from which plain gentile vessels are not ben yomo — see there. And it is difficult: from where do we know — for the oil R' Yehuda the Prince permitted was not shelek, for below daf 38 side 2 R' Shimon and R' Shimon bar Rabbi dispute whether shelek oil is forbidden — from which the oil R' Yehuda the Prince illuminated was plain oil without shelek; and if so, from where did it absorb — and therefore it must have been kavush in it from me'eit l'eit; and the above question is difficult for him: if gentile vessels are ben yomo, nevertheless before kavush finished, time of impairment already passed, like Taz's question — and in this Peri Chadash's answer certainly does not suffice, from where we prove gentile vessels are not ben yomo — for so much we are not concerned everything happened in one moment, and this is almost not in reality, and all the more not common. However from the Gemara and Rosh it is not so much proof, for it appears they brought the view of Rashi and Rabbeinu Tam that night spoils — only to be stringent they said me'eit l'eit, as Tosafot wrote, and the stringent practice. And likewise it appears from Rosh's words, who wrote: and all the strained explanations — if we heed our teachers; but if we say me'eit l'eit, one need not all the strained explanations — and that is to be lenient he did not rely; but to be stringent he certainly holds one should not distinguish from Rashi and Rabbeinu Tam's words; and according to their view, if one removed the issur from the vessel in the middle of the night and two hours afterward they soaked permitted matter and it fell until the second night before daybreak — behold it fell from me'eit l'eit and is kemevushal, and impairment did not occur, for the entire night did not pass in completeness — and this is certain that the entire night is required, as written above siman 103 at length; and six hours from this night and six hours from the second night do not combine to carry the night to impairment — and if so, certainly forbidden, since their intent is not to be lenient against Rashi and Rabbeinu Tam, and especially Tosafot concluded like Rabbeinu Tam, only they wrote the stringent practice. However I did not see in Orach Chayyim that Taz brought so much necessity from what he wrote in general rule 2 law 8, that a vessel forbidden in kavush is permitted l'chatchila to soak in it — for one does not soak the second meat me'eit l'eit — there is no proof to say if they soaked, b'dieved it would be forbidden; one may say he speaks l'chatchila, that noten ta'am li'fegam is forbidden l'chatchila, and this even l'chatchila is permitted, since there is no way to soak it so much time, and he was not stringent even initially. And what he wrote in general rule for one who holds permitted soaked in an issur vessel ben yomo is forbidden, and soaking less than me'eit l'eit is not permitted — if so, Beit Yosef's question: how do you find it — one may say thus: his entire intent is to say the vessel forbids what fell into it from me'eit l'eit; and we say even in a kavush vessel, even though the outer side is not surrounded by water as above in salting laws; and also according to Mahariv's view, that in a copper vessel we require fire passing underneath — he teaches that kavush helps; and it exists in reality if they soaked milk in a vessel me'eit l'eit, and after milk already soaked in it twelve hours they also placed meat in the water, and at the end of twelve hours the milk was taken but the meat remained twenty-four hours — and the soaking water is not forbidden until they forbid the meat — it deals after soaking milk me'eit l'eit one added more water — and examine. And so very much; and if so, because of kavush of meat with milk it did not become forbidden, for they were not kavush together me'eit l'eit; but because of the vessel it became forbidden, kavush in it me'eit l'eit, and all of it is not noten ta'am li'fegam, for from the time of removing milk from the vessel there were only twelve hours and it was kavush in it from me'eit l'eit — see there; and Orach Chayyim did not come here to teach us noten ta'am li'fegam and nosen ta'am for Shabbos — that is known — only his entire intent is to teach us that kavush helps in a vessel as in foods — examine. And what Orach Chayyim wrote to say metal vessels are not forbidden in kavush — Taz wondered from the Tur to distinguish — it appears he holds like Mahariv, who rules like the Yerushalmi that copper does not absorb unless fire passes underneath; and if so, it is explained that through kavush it does not forbid; and see what I wrote below on this.
And doubtful kavush forbidden, etc. — for it is a matter of Torah from what we say: it is a novelty that if you feed him, etc.; and from this they learned the law of kavush from me'eit l'eit — from which it is d'oraisa; and Knesset HaGedolah brought in the name of an authority that is not in my hands to examine, for it is locked away — from where do we know it is d'oraisa; and I did not understand what he would say in the proof mentioned; and perhaps he would say in kavush in vinegar and brine that is only enough to place on fire — and this is possible that it is not d'oraisa. However Rambam wrote this law of kavush kemevushal plainly and did not mention at all in his book at the end of chapter 15 of the laws of forbidden foods that a tame fish kavush with a pure fish, etc., requires two hundred — this taught us the law of kavush; from this it appears he holds kavush kemevushal like Rashi in vinegar and brine — for if he held also in water and me'eit l'eit he would have written; and likewise in chapter 7 of Shevi'it he explains on that mishnah of new sumac kavush in old oil that sumac taste is absorbed more in oil — and specifically old sumac, but not new — from which in general we do not say regarding oil kavush, only sumac is different. And likewise the baraita: carobs in wine forbid, for it gives taste in it — implying other things kavush in wine do not forbid; and likewise in the laws of terumah chapter 15 halachah 9 he ruled explicitly that all terumah vegetables kavush with non-sacred vegetables are permitted except with garlic, onions, and lettuce that are sharp, as Kesef Mishneh wrote — from which in general we do not say kavush kemevushal. However in chapter 22 of the laws of Shabbos halachah 10 he wrote: one who pickles on Shabbos is forbidden, for it is like cooking — and it implies there from the Torah; therefore there is room to say Rambam distinguishes between pickling and pickling, between brine and water, against those poskim who hold in every case kavush from the Torah — that mishnah of terumot is difficult, for it teaches: all that are kavush with one another are permitted except lettuce — why do they absorb from terumah, since kavush kemevushal; and what it says sharp versus not sharp. Again I saw Tosefot Yom Tov on the mishnah challenged thus on Shmuel, who said salted is like roasted, etc., and also wrote Rambam copied "all that are kavush" — and in truth requires further study as I wrote, that he holds like Rashi and distinguishes between kavush; but against those who disagree with Rashi — it is difficult and strained to say he holds like the Yerushalmi that teaches kavush is like shelek, and he holds shelek is weaker than cooking, even though according to our Gemara shelek is more than cooked, as Tosefot Yom Tov also wrote accordingly — and one must say he holds since terumah is d'rabbanan and terumah of vegetables is also d'rabbanan, the Sages were lenient and did not forbid it except sharp things like onion and lettuce, etc. However on Mordechai it is difficult, for he challenged Rashi who does not say kavush except in brine and vinegar, from sumac of Shevi'it kavush in oil, etc. — for if so Rabbah and R' Yochanan in chapter 25 daf 111 side 2 should be difficult, that kavush is not kemevushal — that mishnah as Tosefot Yom Tov wrote in truth; and one must say as Rambam wrote, that sumac absorbs immediately from oil; and see Tosafot there s.v. salted, etc., who also distinguish for R' Yochanan in fish whose scales softened — and likewise in sumac; and if so, even for Rashi requires further study that in a place where they softened, all agree we say kavush everywhere, even in water and oil — and strained to reconcile — requires further study. Summary: kavush in vinegar and brine one may lean to be lenient in its doubt, since a complete mishnah: all that are kavush, etc., are permitted.
Kavush — meaning it was kavush inside and we do not know whether it was kavush a full day — forbidden. The rabbi Taz in s.k. 3 wondered at what was written from a mikveh that was presumed that its waters diminish, etc.; and on this Nekudot HaKesef and Peri Chadash replied: here the defect is before you, for the issur is inside it, unlike there where there is no defect — and they answered his claim well; but what will they say to what was written in the preceding siman, that we establish all tereifot even if the wound healed and the defect of tereifot is before you — nevertheless on presumption of kashrus, and we say "now that they deteriorated"; and Orach Chayyim himself rules thus on cheeses and wrote proof from a mikveh that was measured and found lacking — defect before us; and nevertheless, if there were not the reasoning of chaser v'ata chaser v'ata, we would not render impure retroactively; and if so, here where chaser v'ata does not apply — why establish retroactively? Orach Chayyim. All this is according to his view that all impurity is as at the time of finding, as above, even for non-sacred; but according to what I wrote above, that is all for kodshim, but for non-sacred — presumption of kashrus, and we say "now that it deteriorated" in such a case one may declare fit. And any doubtful kavush they said is forbidden — such as when there are two vessels, one had water twenty-four hours and one did not, and we are uncertain in which vessel it was — and many such cases that do not apply now that it fell; but in a doubt like this we establish on presumption of kashrus and we say "now it fell"; and in any case this law is a dispute of the poskim and it is clear that it depends on that which is above siman 81 — requires further study.
Or in vinegar, etc. The rabbi in Shach s.k. 2 elaborated to challenge that in vinegar we require me'eit l'eit, and he proved this law from Rosh regarding a mouse in vinegar, who wrote that we require kavush me'eit l'eit; and behold I wonder how he decided the law to be lenient — even if Rosh's view is so, behold Rashi does not hold this pickling in cold at all; and if so, even kavush of me'eit l'eit he does not hold — for all the time of me'eit l'eit we derive a day from that which if you feed him all day; and this is for Rashi proof that therefore the explanation is not from kavush; and if so, certainly kavush in brine — the measure as Rosh wrote, enough to place on fire and boil; and if so, likewise for kavush in vinegar — for in every place Rashi equates brine and vinegar regarding kavush; and Rashi who explained in the Gemara kavush in vinegar and spices — and as kavush in spices is immediate, for that is the matter of brine — so in vinegar, for one should not distinguish in Rashi; and in Pesachim daf 39 Rashi explained: not pickled in vinegar; and in daf 76 pickling in vinegar is kemevushal, and if they were pickled — behold forbidden — behold he did not blend time at all, and the plain meaning is immediately enough to boil — forbidden in vinegar immediately, even though Mordechai in Pesachim wrote Rashi does not compare in chapter 2 of Avodah Zarah vinegar to brine — that is regarding forbidding in two hundred, because its taste is not so strong — and this is proven from Rashi's wording on vinegar that fell into groats, that for pickling in it Rashi holds they are equal — and the matter is clear. And likewise clear in Rambam, who holds lettuce is a sharp item and carries pickling, as I wrote in explaining the mishnah — and all the more vinegar, for every person recognizes there is more sharpness in vinegar than in garlic and onions included in the name lettuce, as Rambam wrote; and Lechem Mishneh wrote on Rambam regarding a plain mouse that fell into beer or vinegar that forbids, and he did not write it needs to remain in it time — because he holds beer and vinegar from their sharpness absorb more — end of his words; behold even in beer he holds it is sharp; and all the more in vinegar, which is sharp — one need not me'eit l'eit to pickle it; and behold Rashi and Rambam are among the stringent. And it appears it is the Gemara in Pesachim daf 74 side 2: omtza that became thick — Tosafot explained there s.v. became thick, etc., in the name of Rivba, and likewise Rosh in chapter 3, that it deals with raw meat placed in vinegar so the blood should congeal in it — if it remains in it until it becomes thick, the blood is uprooted from it and emitted into it, and it and its limbs are forbidden, for vinegar returns and absorbs inside the meat; and Ravina holds that even if it did not become thick, its limbs are forbidden, for it is impossible there should not be traces of blood — end of his words; and if there is no power in vinegar to absorb except kavush of me'eit l'eit — from where would the meat return and become forbidden; and even though one may say vinegar has power to extract blood by nature of vinegar — from where would its power absorb in the piece until they said: if vinegar — forbidden, it too is forbidden, even without becoming thick — were it not a minority matter, the piece would also be forbidden, as Tosafot wrote; and if vinegar is not sharp, how would it absorb in the piece — and strained to distinguish between blood and other things — from where do we distinguish and be lenient; and also that Rosh wrote regarding brine that it should be placed on fire — likewise in blood; and nevertheless we derive from it for other issurim: from this it appears that even inferior wine vinegar also carries kavush — not like Magen Avraham in Orach Chayyim siman 447, who wants to distinguish between inferior wine vinegar and fine wine — for in the Gemara we say even in inferior vinegar they congeal that it came to the scabs of the fruit; and plainly we say vinegar — if it became thick, that the piece is also forbidden — from which wherever it says "came to scabs" it absorbs — and the matter is clear. And hence even if we say like Shach's view, that Rosh who wrote on that mouse that fell in vinegar requires kavush me'eit l'eit — disagrees; nevertheless to be stringent we heed other poskim; and one may also say in Rosh he distinguished between vinegar of beer and vinegar; and in truth there is some proof vinegar is sharp, for Ravina wanted to measure it in a kav, and said it is not less than terumah; and it is difficult: if you want to be stringent to rule on it like terumah that it should absorb like terumah in a kav — it suffices to be like terumah; and behold we established: all that are kavush with one another are permitted; and here too kavush would be permitted as terumah does not forbid — and therefore one must say garlic and onions even in terumah forbid, and vinegar is likewise sharp, certainly like garlic and onions — see there; and the matter is necessary. And therefore it is more plausible in Rosh that he holds like Rashi, who explained that which he asks in vinegar — perhaps the strength of vinegar prevents giving taste li'fegam, and Rosh holds two things do not work: the sharpness of vinegar to prevent impairment and to nullify impairment, and to hurry pickling so one should not need me'eit l'eit so much — we do not say; and therefore Rosh explained it was me'eit l'eit — thus it appears to me in Rosh's words; but however Rosh's words may be, in any case the clear proof mentioned decides that vinegar is like brine regarding kavush; and if so, one may say the author cited to be stringent; but above in any case there is a doubt, perhaps it does not enhance at all — if so, so much he did not want to be stringent in vinegar, for it is a double doubt, whether it enhances and whether it is kavush — so much we are not stringent; but here the plain meaning is to be stringent, we heed.
