And some say that he is not, etc. The Beit Yosef writes that l'chatchila we rule that whenever the lender can first demand payment from the guarantor, the arrangement is forbidden unless the lender accepts upon himself not to demand payment from the guarantor until he first demands payment from the borrower and cannot collect from him, or the borrower is violent and the rabbinical court cannot extract payment from him, or he does not appear before the court, such as where he fled, and the like. Whenever the guarantor functions like an ordinary guarantor, it is permitted. B'dieved, however, we rely on the dissenting view, and where the money presently stands, we leave it in the possessor's hands in accordance with the rule in monetary doubts that the burden of proof rests upon the claimant. End of the Beit Yosef's words. It is clear from his words that with an unconditional guarantor who entirely replaces the borrower, even if he has already paid the interest, the money is extracted from the lender. This is unlike the view of the Rama in Darkei Moshe. The Bach is even more lenient and rules like R' Yosef Karkosa that the arrangement is forbidden only l'chatchila, but b'dieved the guarantor bears the entire loss since it resulted because of him, and there is no prohibition of interest even with such an unconditional guarantor. The view of all the other poskim is not so. It appears in my humble opinion that R' Yosef Karkosa himself was lenient only regarding an arev kablan, but not regarding a guarantor who entirely replaces the borrower. See there. This also resolves the difficulty left by Responsa Masas Binyamin at the end of siman 67 against R' Yosef Karkosa and the Rama, that the Gemara implies that where a Jew serves as guarantor the interest is forbidden by Torah law. One can say that the Gemara discusses an unconditional guarantor who entirely replaces the borrower. Even without this, there is no difficulty, because they may hold like the Baal HaItur, cited by the Beit Yosef, that the Gemara specifically discusses a non-Jew who borrowed from a Jew, etc. See there.
