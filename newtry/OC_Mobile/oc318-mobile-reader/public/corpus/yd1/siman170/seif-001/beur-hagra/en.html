But it is not necessary, etc. Tur — not like R' Yitzchak Karkuza, who said it is forbidden only ab initio; and Tur expressed wonder at him: how could one say to him to give interest? And if it was already [given], etc. So wrote in Sefer HaTerumah folio 66, column 3; and a guarantor — even if there was a concern in the matter, it is only dust of interest, which does not pass from lender to lender; but it requires study, for if so, what is the [Gemara's] objection: 'and since,' etc.? And it seems that this is the reason of Rashba, who wrote: specifically in [the case of] a 'peel-off guarantor' — for otherwise it would not be fixed interest, since it does not pass from lender to lender; and a 'peel-off guarantor' is truly a borrower; and it seems there is a scribal error in Sefer HaTerumah, and the word 'and a guarantor' is superfluous.
