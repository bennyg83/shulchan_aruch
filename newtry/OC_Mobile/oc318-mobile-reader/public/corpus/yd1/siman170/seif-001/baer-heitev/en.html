To push him off. The Shach writes that it is explained in Beit Yosef that with an arev sheluf dotz, even if he already paid the ribit, we extract it from him, unlike the Rav's view. The Bach is still more lenient and rules that it is forbidden only lechatchilah, but bedieved he bears the entire loss and there is no ribit even with an arev sheluf dotz. The view of all the poskim is not so. It appears to me that R. Yosef Korkos himself was lenient only with an arev kablan, but not with an arev sheluf dotz. This resolves what responsa Masat Binyamin at the end of siman 67 left difficult against that R. Yosef and the Rav, and the Taz also left their words requiring study, that the Shas implies that where a Jew is guarantor it is Torah-level ribit. One can say that the Shas speaks of sheluf dotz. In any event, there is no difficulty, as the Baal HaItur wrote, that the Shas speaks specifically where a non-Jew borrowed from a Jew, etc.; see there. Thus the Shach. To give. The Taz writes: from here it appears to me that if one made an agreement with another that if he does not pay his debt by the set time he will pay all his damages, and the lender borrowed money elsewhere through a heter with profit, the borrower need not pay him the profits that the lender gives others. For here too the borrower is liable for the guarantor's damages, and nevertheless he need not give him ribit, even though the guarantor gives it permissibly to the non-Jew. Even if he explicitly stipulated that he would take money on profit through a heter and the borrower would pay it, this contains a prohibition of ribit, and even if he gave it, we extract it from him. What the Taz wrote, that we extract it, follows his view that this is fixed ribit; according to the Rama, who holds it is avak ribit, we do not extract it. I further saw fit to mention a matter concerning two partners: if one of them takes money from a non-Jew with interest for the partnership, pays ribit from the partnership, and they divide the profit, this is fixed ribit, for the non-Jew recognizes only the one who borrowed from him. It is not like communal trustees, where it is permitted because it is for a mitzvah need, unlike here. Rather, both must borrow from the non-Jew, meaning both must sign the document. But if one borrowed from a Jew through iska and gave it to the partnership, he may properly give the profit from the partnership, for in the way he received it through iska he gives it to the partnership through iska. Regarding the case of taking from a non-Jew that I mentioned, it seems one should distinguish: if the partner who works in the partnership takes money from a non-Jew with interest and places it into the partnership business, he may first fill the repayment with what he must give as ribit to the non-Jew, and they divide the remainder, for the ribit he gives the non-Jew is included in the loss or reduces the profit share. But if one partner merely places money with his fellow, and the fellow alone conducts the partnership business, and the one who placed the money took money from a non-Jew with interest for the partnership, he may not take from the partnership to repay the ribit, since he is not the one conducting the business; it is as if he placed that sum from his own pocket for the partnership. Thus the Taz. See above siman 169 s.k. 27 in the name of Sefer HaZichronot. Already. I wrote above in s.k. 2 that the Taz disagrees with the Rama and rules that even if the ribit was already paid, we extract it, but this is not the view of the Shach, and in Nekudot HaKesef he strengthened his words against the Taz. The Taz further writes that he was asked about Reuven, who paid Shimon to be an arev kablan for him before Levi so that Levi would lend him money, whether there is any concern for ribit, since perhaps Shimon will have to pay the sum Levi lent Reuven, and Reuven will then be Shimon's borrower, making the payment ribit. He answered that this is completely permitted, for this payment is not connected to a loan; at the outset the guarantor never expected he would have to pay. But certainly, one who pays the guarantor to issue a document solely upon himself to the lender, that is forbidden, for immediately he becomes the guarantor's borrower and not the lender's. In Nekudot HaKesef he writes: I am hesitant about this, for nevertheless the payment is not payment for a loan, but payment for obligating himself on behalf of his fellow, and it seems the practice is to permit this. Where one pays another to issue a document upon himself for someone who wishes to buy merchandise from another, and the seller does not wish to trust the buyer, so the buyer asks this person to issue a document to the seller and pays him for it, there is a remedy: the guarantor should say to the seller, "I give you a document for the money, and I am the purchaser," and then afterward he gives it to the buyer as a new sale from him. This is simple and completely permitted. See Chavot Yair siman 190.
