And one may not price with a householder. It is explained in the Talmud as follows: granted, a laborer — if he does not have, he can borrow from a fellow laborer; but a householder — if he does not have, it would be beneath his dignity to borrow from a laborer. And alternatively: whoever gives money to a householder for produce gives it in the best quality. And it seems to me there is a practical difference between these two answers regarding whether the householder explicitly said "I will give you the produce of a laborer" — according to the first answer it is forbidden, since it is beneath his dignity to have dealings with the laborer and give the buyer his own produce in place of it; and according to the second answer it is permitted in this case. And the Tur wrote that even from a laborer's produce one may not price with a householder — which follows the first answer, applying the stringency; and likewise one should understand the Shulchan Arukh here: that one may not price at all with a householder for a laborer's produce; but a householder for his own produce is certainly permitted.
