That he not profit. See responsum of Chavot Yair there regarding Torah scholar who gave money for wine at rate of one rich man who makes much wine yearly — he will make another measure for him and scholar takes wine measure for measure; he wrote: apparently there is prohibition concern, for Sages were strict that buyer not profit at all; must deduct usual deficiencies; clearly regarding wine too must deduct deficiencies stated regarding deposit; and per nature of wines in that place — all the more labor, handling, and deficiency from measure for measure; and further in those countries hard to rely on market rate of wine, for there are superior vineyards, mountains and valleys — one measure praiseworthy, one blameworthy in price. And even though this rich man has no benefit from advancing money due to great wealth, only does favor for scholar free — nevertheless this giver profits by advancing money, as stated One Who Borrows folio 65: what did you see that you went after your hand, etc. (and as above siman 173 seif 1); and further who will say this rich man is like Rav Pappa's wealth — if business came to him, etc.; but he leaned to be lenient after this rich man is known to enjoy scholar from his property and was not strict with scholar on all matters warranting strictness whether labor or deficiency; known he does not act at all for his own benefit in receiving money; and possibly would also have agreed if given at end, only scholar did not bear face to ask; and automatically what scholar enjoys taking wine as needed is not because of advancing money; unlike Trashei of Rav Pappa where lender loses; similar to what is stated siman 165 seif 17 regarding scholars who lent each other small amount because they knew ribbit prohibition and gave as gift; here clearer and more known, for scholar importuned rich man; also not manner of loan — seems permitted for law; but what spread in some communities where exceedingly rich think it mitzvah to take money from scholar and give fruit of field per hundred because they have no need for money, only act for lender's benefit — this scholar — complete prohibition unless done in best possible manner — see there in omissions.
