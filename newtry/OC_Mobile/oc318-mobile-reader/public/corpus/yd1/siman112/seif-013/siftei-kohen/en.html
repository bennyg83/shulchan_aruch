One who is not careful, etc. And so the Mechaber in Orach Chaim, siman 168, seif 5; and so the Rav in Turei Chayim there, law 5; and the Mechaber wrote there at the beginning that one careful about pat akum who has clean pat akum and unclean pat Yisrael before him removes pat akum from the table until after ha-Motzi; the reason at the beginning is that no other Jew reclines with him; but at the end and here, since another Jew reclines with him, if he does not bless on clean bread there is enmity, as below, seif 15 in the gloss; and even though it appears there that there is enmity concern only when one careful eats with others who are not careful, but one who eats only with one who is not careful — there is no enmity, for what comparison is there between this and that? — nevertheless, since regarding ha-Motzi one must bless on the finest, if he does not bless there is great enmity; alternatively, specifically when both their breads are equal is there no enmity when there is only one who is not careful; but when the bread of one not careful is cleaner and finer and nevertheless he does not eat it, there is certainly enmity — so it appears to me; and with this it is reconciled that the Bach's challenge on Terumat HaDeshen from the Mordechai, chapter Keitzad Mevarchin, regarding what he brought in the name of the minister of Kutzi and challenged Torat HaBayit and the Mechaber on this law they wrote here, and explained the Mordechai's words in the name of Ravyah in chapter 1 of Avodah Zarah in a strange, far-fetched way — all his words are not plausible, as I challenged him at length in my books — see there; and I proved with many proofs that the explanation of the Mordechai's words is as Torat HaBayit, the Rav, and other later authorities understood; and so is explained explicitly in Agudah — see there.
