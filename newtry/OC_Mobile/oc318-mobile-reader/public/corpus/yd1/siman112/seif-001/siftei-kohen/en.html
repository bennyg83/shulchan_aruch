Bread of nations, etc. It appears the view of the Mechaber and the Rav throughout this siman that there is no prohibition in pat akum except when a gentile baked it; and as Beit Yosef wrote according to the Rambam and Rashba that even though a gentile kneaded and shaped, whatever a Jew did in the baking is permitted; and therefore the Mechaber did not write the distinctions of kneading and shaping that Beit Yosef wrote, because all that Beit Yosef wrote is specifically according to the Ran and Tur, whereas not according to the Rambam and Rashba; investigate; and so the Rav in Turei Chayim, general rule 71, law 2, and he brought there many later authorities who hold thus:
Idolaters. See above, siman 123, and siman 124, s.k. 12:
Because of marriage concern. But because of gentile revulsion there is no concern, for ordinarily their vessels are not ben yomo, as below, siman 122:
And even in a place where there is no marriage concern. Such as bread of heretics who have no children is forbidden — not because of the seller's or giver's daughter did they say, but because of gentiles' daughters: if this one has none, that one has. Rashba there:
And also it is not forbidden, etc. The Bach challenged this and wrote that there is no doubt legume, rice, and millet bread does not go upon a king's table, as appears from the Tur's words; rather the Rosh deals with a cooked dish made from rice and millet, where there is room to distinguish whether it goes upon a king's table or not — end of his words; and so R' Yerucham, Netiv 17, chapter 7, that legume bread, etc., has no concern of bishulei akum nor pat akum since it does not go upon a king's table — so the commentators — end of his words; and Beit Yosef brings it in Bedek HaBayit and writes there that such is the Tur's view; and see Turei Chayim there, law 11, in the name of Issur VeHeter:
