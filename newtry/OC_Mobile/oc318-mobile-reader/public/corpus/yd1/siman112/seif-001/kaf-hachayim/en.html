1) [Seif 1] The Sages forbade eating pat akum because of marriage concern. So in the Gemara, Avodah Zarah, daf 35, side 2; and we say there in the Gemara, daf 36, side 2: they decreed on their bread and oil because of their wine, and on their wine because of their daughters, and on their daughters because of something else — meaning, if you permit him to eat with you at one gathering, afterward he will come to drink his wine and marry his daughter and worship idolatry. Shulchan Gavoah, section 1.
2) There. The Sages forbade eating pat akum, etc. — it appears the view of the Mechaber and the Rav throughout this siman that there is no prohibition in pat akum except when a gentile baked it; and as Beit Yosef wrote according to the Rambam and Rashba that even though a gentile kneaded and shaped, whatever a Jew did in the baking is permitted; and so the Rav in Turei Chayim, general rule 71, law 2, and he brought there many later authorities who hold thus. Shach, s.k. 1.
4) There. The Sages forbade eating pat akum, etc. — and even a Samaritan who is not an idolater — his bread is forbidden. Parshat Torah, end of section 3, section 84, section 2, and he wrote it is plain.
5) And regarding bread of Karaites one should be stringent and forbid their bread like gentiles, for they too do not eat our bread. Peri Chadash, section 2. Lechem HaPanim, section 2. Binyamin Zeev, section 1. Section 84 there. However, what they wrote that they do not eat from our bread is no proof, only because they regard them as gentiles and there is marriage concern among them. Shulchan Gavoah, section 2.
9) There, gloss. And even in a place where there is no marriage concern it is forbidden — such as bread of heretics who have no children; and likewise bread of youths, young men, and eunuchs — even though they have no sons and daughters it is forbidden. Rashba in his responsum there, siman 248. Taz, s.k. 1. Shach, s.k. 4. Knesset HaGedolah in Hagahot Taz, section 2. Peri Chadash, section 4. Lechem HaPanim, section 4. Binyamin Zeev, section 2. Erech HaShulchan, section 2. Chacham Adam, general rule 65, section 1.
12) Gentile bread through cooking is forbidden — not like one who wished to permit it: where did its prohibition go? Maharil in his responsum, siman 195. Knesset HaGedolah in Hagahot Taz, section 8. Hilchot Ketanot, volume 2, siman 83. Lechem HaPanim, section 9. Binyamin Zeev, section 12. Minchat Yonatan in Solut leMinchah at the end of Turei Chayim, general rule 71, law 1. Birkei Yosef on Shiyurei Berachah, section 20. And in his book Yosef Ometz, siman 79. Zivchei Tzedek, section 7. Ben Avraham, parashat Chukat, section 4.
13) There. And they only forbade bread of the five species of grain, etc. — for this is an important matter and leads to familiarity. Taz, s.k. 2.
16) There, gloss. And also it is not forbidden because of bishulei akum if it does not go upon a king's table. It appears he understood the condition he set — "and if it does not go upon a king's table" — applies to bread too; but this is not so, as proven from the Tur's words that regarding bread there is no doubt that plainly no rice or millet bread goes upon a king's table, but in a cooked dish it is possible it goes upon a king's table. Bach. And so Rabbeinu Yerucham, Netiv 17, chapter 7, that legume bread, etc., has no concern of bishulei akum nor of pat akum since it does not go upon a king's table; and Beit Yosef brought it in Bedek HaBayit and wrote that such is the Tur's view. And so the Levush. Taz, s.k. 3. Shach, s.k. 5. Knesset HaGedolah in Hagahot Beit Yosef, section 8. Binyamin Zeev, section 3. Minchat Yonatan on Turei Chayim, general rule 71, section 18. Mishbetzot Zahav, section 3. Chacham Adam, general rule 65, section 1 — and he means that presumably we say it does not go upon a king's table and one need not be concerned, and it is permitted to eat baal habayit's bread.
