And we are not concerned about bread, etc. And one should not forbid because of gentile milk, for impure milk does not stand; and if we say there is a slight admixture of milk in it, nevertheless it is nullified in its minority since it is mixed in, and it is not comparable to butter in siman 115 which is not mixed in — end of Rashal's words; and in Derishah and Maharach, of blessed memory, they brought it; and to me, the younger, it seems astonishing how he said milk admixture is nullified in its minority, for we say at the beginning of chapter Elu Overin: Babylonian kutach dulls the heart because of milk admixture in it — and if it were nullified it would not dull; and furthermore it is stated explicitly in chapter Ein Ma'amidin (Avodah Zarah, daf 35) that one who curdled kemakha — which is kutach — the milk is forbidden; and so in Mordechai it is written explicitly in chapter Ein Ma'amidin in the name of Rabbenu Tam that milk that a gentile milked — for eating and for kutach I forbid — end of his words; and so in Issur VeHeter, general rule 44, law 5. And it appears to my mind that kutach of a gentile is certainly forbidden; rather, here it deals with when it was made with supervision that a Jew sees it, only that the non-Jew puts his bread there, for there is no prohibition of pat akum in that — so it appears to my clear mind.
