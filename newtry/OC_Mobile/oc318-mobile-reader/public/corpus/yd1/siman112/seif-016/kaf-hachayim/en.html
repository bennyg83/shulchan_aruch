16) There, gloss. And also it is not forbidden because of bishulei akum if it does not go upon a king's table. It appears he understood the condition he set — "and if it does not go upon a king's table" — applies to bread too; but this is not so, as proven from the Tur's words that regarding bread there is no doubt that plainly no rice or millet bread goes upon a king's table, but in a cooked dish it is possible it goes upon a king's table. Bach. And so Rabbeinu Yerucham, Netiv 17, chapter 7, that legume bread, etc., has no concern of bishulei akum nor of pat akum since it does not go upon a king's table; and Beit Yosef brought it in Bedek HaBayit and wrote that such is the Tur's view. And so the Levush. Taz, s.k. 3. Shach, s.k. 5. Knesset HaGedolah in Hagahot Beit Yosef, section 8. Binyamin Zeev, section 3. Minchat Yonatan on Turei Chayim, general rule 71, section 18. Mishbetzot Zahav, section 3. Chacham Adam, general rule 65, section 1 — and he means that presumably we say it does not go upon a king's table and one need not be concerned, and it is permitted to eat baal habayit's bread.
