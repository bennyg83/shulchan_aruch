Avodat HaTaharah; and see in the responsum of Devar Yosef, siman 28: in places where they practiced leniency regarding pat akum we have no power to forbid buying a roll from the gentile baker who also sells cakes kneaded with water and forbidden fat, for the baker has special vessels for each craft as is the way of artisans; and all the more so that he himself showed them that he has vessels, a mold, and a pan dedicated whether for kneading, shaping, or baking, and he certainly would not lie to ruin his craft. Also there is a double doubt: perhaps they used today a vessel that absorbed forbidden taste, or perhaps the rolls were baked — perhaps they did not use it; and if you say they used it, perhaps in something that spoils the taste, etc.; and ordinarily a shopkeeper wipes his vessels, etc.; and one need not be concerned lest he take with soiled hands or lest some mixture occur unintentionally — regarding this the Raavad did not say we follow the majority of gentiles, etc. — see there. But it is good to distance oneself from ugliness; and in the responsa of Mayim Rabim, Yoreh De'ah part, many responsa forbid and permit, and he writes that those who forbid were counted and outnumbered those who permit — see there at length; and so too Be'er Heitev, siman 253.
