There are places that are lenient in the matter and buy bread from the baker, the non-Jew, in a place where there is no Jewish baker because it is a time of distress {Rama: (and some say that even in a place where pat Yisrael is available it is permitted) (Beit Yosef according to the Mordechai, Semak, Hagahot Maimoniyot, Maharai, and Issur VeHeter, beginning of general rule 44)} but pat baal habayit — there is no one who rules leniently regarding it, for the essence of the decree is because of marriage concern, and if one eats pat baal habayit he will come to dine with them: {Rama: Pat baal habayit is only so called if he made it for the members of his household; but if he made it to sell it is called palter even if that is not his usual practice; and likewise a palter who made it for himself is called baal habayit (so it appears in Beit Yosef):}
