10) And likewise bread made in the royal house and in ministers' houses is forbidden, even though certainly the king does not intermarry with us — for the Sages did not differentiate. Knesset HaGedolah there. Binyamin Zeev there. Erech HaShulchan there. Mishbetzot Zahav, section 1.
11) And the same applies to an apostate to idolatry — there is no marriage prohibition in his daughters, since he is like a full gentile and is in their decree, and his bread is forbidden. Erech HaShulchan there. And what Parshat Torah, section 1, wrote to permit appears because he did not see Erech HaShulchan's words cited above, who brought proof from the Ran — see there; and see below, siman 113, section 1.
14) And if one mixed other species with the five species of grain, we follow the majority — even with rice, regarding pat akum one may be lenient. Mishbetzot Zahav, section 2.
15) If one found bread on the road, we follow the majority of passersby. Eruvin, 64, side 2. And if they are half and half, it is a d'rabbanan doubt and we rule leniently. Erech HaShulchan, section 4; and he wrote, and not like Peri Chadash, section 13, who was stringent. Section 84, section 13. Sdei Chemed, section 2. Chiddushei Ra'ah Kook.
