(There, in Taz, s.k. 9) And to me, the younger, it seems astonishing, etc. There is no wonder at them, for as their words are explained explicitly in the responsum of Maharam of Rothenburg, siman 154, and these are his words: we recite in the Beit Yosef that kutach of a gentile is permitted — if because of bread that is in it, there is no reason to forbid it in itself; but it is astonishing, for it is forbidden because of milk that a gentile milked and no Jew sees it. And one may say that perhaps impure milk does not stand, and therefore kutach is not visible — end of his words. And if so, perforce one must say that kutach was different in the time of the Gemara from this kutach; or, as the Bach wrote, and these are his words: and to us, kutach and butter are both not in the category of the decree on milk and cheese; and regarding butter they practiced prohibition in some places because milk remained in the crevices; but in kutach, where there are no crevices, they did not practice prohibition anywhere — and this too is within Rashal's words — end of his words. And if so, to my mind, Babylonian kutach is certainly different, for it was not thick and there is much admixture in it; and that which we say in curdling kemakha — and so R' Chananel wrote that curdling for kutach is forbidden — that is when a gentile curdles for himself; forbidden in curdling for kutach since it is milk before us and is in the category of the decree on milk that a gentile milked; and furthermore it is possible he chooses kutach that is not thick, but kutach that gentiles sell to others — since it is not milk before us and it stands — is permitted. In Beit Yosef, daf 102, side 4; and even though before this he answered two Talmuds, etc., I did not understand his intent in this, for according to what he answered, it is like one who mixes with his hands — all the more so one should not say it is permitted here with gentile leaven because they did not decree on mixtures of pat akum since he mixes the leaven with his hands; and so too in Hagahot ShaDa, siman 75, that l'chatchila one should not put gentile bread in a bowl — plainly forbidden, for it is like mixing with hands — end of his words; and requires study.
