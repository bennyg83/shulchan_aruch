For it is not customary to sell produce, etc. Gourds, however, are customarily purchased while small. Thus, when he purchases them now, they are sold in the market in that condition, and that is their present value; when they are left, they grow on their own. So wrote the Beit Yosef in the name of the Maggid Mishneh. Tosafos write that the orchard case is more stringent than a mortgage of Sura because there he acquires the land immediately for its produce, whereas with an orchard he acquires nothing, and the money remains a loan with the owner until harvest. They give a further answer as well. End quote.
