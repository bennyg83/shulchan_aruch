Before it is fully finished, etc. Rashi, s.v. 'the vineyard,' etc., and Rosh there, contrary to Tosafot's statement in the cited gloss that according to another version it is also permitted, etc. Rabbeinu Yeruham answers Tosafot's question by explaining that the squash is considered fully formed and fit to be picked then, though it will still grow larger, unlike grapes in a vineyard. He writes that Rif omitted the vineyard ruling because we follow the alternative version, and that most authorities agree it is permitted even when the produce is not yet fit to be picked. According to the cited explanation of Tosafot it is likewise permitted. Our editions of Rif do include the vineyard ruling, as do Rosh. Maggid Mishneh distinguishes the cases: a vineyard differs because further labor is required, so the seller incurs expense, and also because it is not customary to purchase, etc., as stated in the gloss, 'and it is not comparable,' etc. See Nimukei Yosef and Beit Yosef.
