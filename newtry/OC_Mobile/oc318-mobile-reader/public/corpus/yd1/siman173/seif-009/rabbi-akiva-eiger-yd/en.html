(Shach, seif katan 24) At the present price or at the low price. I am astonished, for below, siman 175, seif 3, it is explained that one may stipulate according to the higher future price. This is an explicit Mishnah. Thus, the seller bears the risk of a fall in price and the buyer receives the benefit of a rise, yet it is permitted. I later found that Tiferes LeMoshe raised this question. The essential distinction between this case and an ordinary advance purchase is explained by Tosafos, cited by Taz here, and by the early authorities cited in Shitah Mekubetzes. In an ordinary advance purchase, the seller can deliver the produce immediately, and the buyer must accept it, after which all responsibility rests upon the buyer. Here, however, they stipulated that the seller may not deliver it until a fixed time. See there.
