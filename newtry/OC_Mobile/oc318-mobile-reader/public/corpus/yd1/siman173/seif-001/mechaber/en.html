Many detailed laws of interest; it contains 19 se’ifim. If he sold his fellow an item worth 10 zehuvim for 12 because he is waiting for him, it is forbidden — even if the seller is rich and does not need the money, and the merchandise would not spoil with him. To what does this apply? To something that has a known market rate, or something whose appraisal is known, like pepper or wax. But a cloak and similar, which has no known market rate and its appraisal is not known, it is permitted to sell it at a higher price to be paid at a certain time, provided that he does not say to him explicitly, “If you give me immediately, it is yours for 10 zehuvim, and if for such-and-such time, for 12.” And there is one who says that even if he does not explain explicitly, there is no permission except when he raises it a little; but if he raises it a lot, until it is recognizable to all that he raises it because of the delay of the money, it is as if he explained [explicitly], and it is forbidden.
{Rama: An item that sells for 10, and sometimes, when ministers come to the city, sells for 12 — if it is common that most times the ministers come to the city, it is permitted to sell it for 12 (Hagahos Mordechai, perek Eizehu Neshech).}
