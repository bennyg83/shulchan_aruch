He returns his money to him. For it is like a document containing interest, from which the principal may be collected.
Take upon yourself my royal service. The Ramban explains that he now gives him 200 gold coins to enter in his place into the king's service that is imposed upon him and is worth 300 gold coins. This is forbidden, for because he advances the money, the other pays more on his behalf.
Perhaps he will appease him for less. For this is like one who gives money to another to save him from coercion. There is no debt upon him, but only coercion by the king. This is unlike the first case, where an actual debt rests upon him and the other accepts responsibility to pay it, while receiving from the borrower less than that debt. That constitutes interest, even though it is possible that he could also discharge the debt to the creditor for less. Nevertheless, the principal debt presently rests upon him. This is explained there in Responsa Rashba, siman 686. See the gloss at the end of this siman and siman 177, seif 10.
