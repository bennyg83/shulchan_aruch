One who has a promissory note against his fellow, or a loan by mouth: it is permitted to sell it to another for less, even if the time of repayment has not arrived, and there is no concern of interest in it (Ba’al HaTerumos) — provided that responsibility for the document and the loan is on the buyer; for example, if the borrower becomes poor such that he has nothing with which to repay, he will not go back on the seller. But responsibility that comes because of the seller — for example, it was found paid, or an earlier creditor seized it — need not be on the buyer. And if the seller has collateral from the borrower in his hand, it is permitted to sell it to the buyer; and likewise, it is permitted that the borrower guarantee it for him with guarantorship or with collateral. And if the seller transgressed and accepted upon himself responsibility, his condition stands; and if he cannot collect his debt, he returns his money to him, and if there is profit it belongs to the seller. And just as he can sell it to another for less, so too he can sell it to the borrower himself. {Rama: It is forbidden for a person to say to his fellow, “Here are 10 dinarim, and exempt me from what I owe 12 dinarim to so-and-so,” even though it is possible that he can exempt himself with 10 dinarim; and this is specifically when he owes 12 dinarim. But if he does not owe, only that the king will impose upon him an occupation with the other residents of his province in exchange for 12 dinarim: if he says, “Accept upon yourself the occupation for me,” it is forbidden; but if he says, “Save me from the occupation,” it is permitted, for perhaps he will appease him with less (a responsum of Rashba, siman 726).}
