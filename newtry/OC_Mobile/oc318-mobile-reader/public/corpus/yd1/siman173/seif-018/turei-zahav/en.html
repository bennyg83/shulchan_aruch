Under responsibility for the ship. This means that the lender accepts responsibility for the money throughout the ship's journey. Even so, it is forbidden, for he gives money and receives money, and the same coins do not return to him. It is like renting money, discussed at the beginning of siman 176; the arrangement never leaves the category of a loan and constitutes the dust of interest. If the lender does not accept responsibility, it is fixed interest. So wrote the Rivash, siman 308. The Rama likewise distinguishes at the beginning of siman 176, regarding renting money, between acceptance and nonacceptance of responsibility with respect to the dust of interest. The Levush wrote here that even when the lender accepts responsibility it is fixed interest. This is an error, for he did not examine the source of the law or even his own words at the beginning of siman 176.
And some permit lending, etc. I see awkwardness in the wording here, for it implies that these authorities disagree with the Rivash above. This does not appear correct. The Maggid Mishneh cites this from Responsa Binyamin Ze'ev, siman 364: In that kingdom it was customary to give 12 dinars at the Cologne fair and receive 13 dinars in Worms or Mainz upon returning. Rabbeinu asked Rashi, who answered that it is permitted only if the lender acquires the merchandise that the borrower purchases with that money, and the lender or his agent transports it under the lender's responsibility until the place where he receives the money and gives him 13 dinars. Without this, it is forbidden. End quote. It appears obvious that the Rivash agrees, for here there is no loan at all, but a complete sale. The lender acquires the merchandise there, and upon returning home from the fair he sells it back to the borrower. In the Rivash's preceding case, however, all agree that it is forbidden because there is an actual loan. One might have thought to permit it because of the lender's responsibility, and the Rivash rules that responsibility does not permit it. This appears clear and obvious to me.
