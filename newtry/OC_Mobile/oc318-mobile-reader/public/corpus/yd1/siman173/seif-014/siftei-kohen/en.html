A barrel etc. The entire seif is Rambam’s language; and Beit Yosef and the Acharonim struggled to reconcile his words. The Derishab wrote that in the first law he speaks of a case where the recipient holds the money until summer and profits from it and it bears the name of a loan on him — therefore we require the seller to accept responsibility; and in the second law he speaks of a case where there is no money of a loan in his hand at all etc. Bach wrote that in the first clause he accepts responsibility only until the appointed time, but after the time arrives it is in the buyer’s possession for all purposes and he can return them to their owner — for that too is included in accident-responsibility; and in the second law he speaks of a case where they set no time at all, rather always if he does not sell it as he wishes he should return it — see there. And all this does not seem right to me, and my heart tells me that a scribal error fell in the books of Rambam: and this phrase "if he did not find to sell it and profit from it etc." should appear after the end of the second law — "it is permitted even though if it were lost, stolen, or turned sour it would be in the buyer’s possession, since if he did not find to sell it and profit from it he must return it to its owners"; but in the first clause it is permitted only because loss or breakage is in the seller’s responsibility, but he could not return it to the seller when he did not sell it as he wished — so it seems to me.
