It is permitted to advance payment, etc. The Gemara relates that Rava of Barnish said to Rava: 'See, Master, the rabbis receive interest. They give money for wine in Tishrei and choose good wine in Teves.' Rashi explains that they take good wine; had they taken it in Tishrei, it might have soured. Thus, because they advanced the money in Tishrei before the market price was established, the seller accepted responsibility. This is unlike one who possesses wheat, for wine commonly sours and perhaps the wine assigned to them would have soured. Rava answered: 'They gave money for wine, not for vinegar. From the outset, wine that becomes wine was wine, and wine that becomes vinegar was vinegar.' Its defect was already within it, though not visible, and they never purchased defective wine. Tosafos in Bava Metzia 64b imply that those rabbis accepted responsibility for every defect other than souring. This is unlike fixing a price for produce that the seller possesses, where the buyer cannot lose under any circumstances, because the seller may supply other wheat and consume the wheat he owned when they made the agreement. In the wine case, however, they relied specifically upon the homeowner's wine and could not purchase wine from the market and supply it. The Tur's words should be understood similarly. When he writes that the seller is to supply good wine in Nissan and that the seller's acceptance of 'the responsibility' is not interest, he means only the responsibility that the wine be good, which is the immediately preceding matter. The buyer accepts the other risks, such as a fall in price or a defect affecting all of the seller's wines. The Derishah writes that the seller accepts every possible responsibility and it is nevertheless permitted, because it is no worse than fixing a price for produce in siman 175, where the seller may accept all responsibility. He overlooked these words of Tosafos. The Tur certainly does not disagree, for he did not mention their view at all, and the Gemara itself mentions only that they did not pay for vinegar, implying that they were not particular about other defects.
Good wine in Nissan. So reads the Tur, whereas the Gemara above says Teves. The Beit Yosef writes either that the Tur's text of the Gemara read Nissan, or that the Tur holds Teves was not precise and the same law applies even until Nissan. Tosafos in Bava Metzia 64a, s.v. 'If it sours, it is in your domain,' write that the law applies throughout the year. This second explanation of the Beit Yosef appears primary, for the Rambam, Rif, and Rosh all mention Teves, and otherwise the Tur should at least have cited them and noted the variant readings. The Beit Yosef asks from Tosafos' question concerning the Gemara's ruling that one may say, 'Here are 4 zuz for a known barrel of wine; if it sours, it remains in your domain.' This is the final law of this seif. Why is an explicit condition necessary, when even without it souring remains the seller's responsibility, as in the case where the rabbis paid in Tishrei and chose in Teves, which Tosafos understand as applying all year? Tosafos therefore explain that the condition in the latter case refers not to actual souring, which requires no condition, but to a lesser deterioration. The Beit Yosef was troubled because the Tur writes in the latter case, 'If it sours,' implying that he rejects Tosafos' answer. He therefore concluded that the Tur disagrees with Tosafos and allows selection only until Nissan. I am astonished why he created an unnecessary dispute. Tosafos did not challenge the underlying law, but only asked why the condition was needed. The Tur preserved the condition stated in the Gemara because the ruling is certainly true. Tosafos' question can be answered otherwise: In the latter case he sold a specific barrel, and without an express condition, souring would indeed be the buyer's responsibility once that barrel was selected. In the first case he sold good wine without specifying a barrel, which is as though he expressly stipulated that it not sour. The Beit Yosef himself ruled practically that Nissan is not precise and the law applies throughout the year.
