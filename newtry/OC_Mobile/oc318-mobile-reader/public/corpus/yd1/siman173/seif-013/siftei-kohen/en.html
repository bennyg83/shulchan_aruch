It is permitted to prepay etc. Rashi explained this is where the market rate has not yet been established and he has the wine; and it is not comparable to below siman 175 where he has wheat, because wine tends to turn sour and perhaps all of it turned sour — yet it is permitted here for the reason that if the wine goes bad he did not take from him; and this one that turned sour — its spoilage was inside it from the beginning, only it was not apparent.
On condition that he give him good wine in Nisan. We do not consider as ribbit what the seller accepts responsibility for in exchange for the advance of money — Tur; meaning responsibility for souring — that the wine will be good; but if the seller also accepts responsibility for price fluctuation — it is forbidden; and likewise proved in Beit Yosef. The Derishab reversed the matter and elaborated, but I did not reach the bottom of his view in all his words. Also what he wrote that it is no worse than fixing produce-credit below siman 175 where the seller can fix even with responsibility for price fluctuation on the seller etc. — that is not correct; for below siman 175 only fixing at the current rate or the lower rate is permitted; but when the seller accepts responsibility for price fluctuation — it is certainly forbidden (and likewise Bach in the final treatise that it is forbidden); also the proof he brought from Nimukei Yosef is nothing — for certainly both the questioner and the respondent require that the buyer accept responsibility for price fluctuation; only the questioner posited that with a known barrel even if the buyer accepts responsibility for the lower price — it is forbidden to say "if it turned sour in your possession," and because of responsibility for the lower price it is considered like responsibility for souring — see there and examine carefully.
In Nisan. For the clarification of wine is until Nisan, and from the outset its spoilage is within it, only it is not apparent; but after that it is possible that originally it was wine and now it is vinegar — and it is forbidden; and some poskim say until Tevet; and it appears that everything depends on the place and time, which is the time of wine clarification; and Tosafot wrote that Tevet is not specifically but the same applies to all days of the year; and per Beit Yosef, since it is rabbinic, it seems we rely on Tosafot's words; and Darkei Moshe brings it; nevertheless it is proper to be stringent.
And likewise it is permitted etc. This teaches us that even during other times of the year it is permitted; alternatively it teaches us that even a known barrel is permitted.
And if it turns sour etc. See CM siman 232.
