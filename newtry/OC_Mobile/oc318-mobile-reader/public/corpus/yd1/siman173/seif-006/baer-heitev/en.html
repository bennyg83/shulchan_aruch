To a moneychanger. Tur limits the permission to a moneychanger, whose business ordinarily involves such exchanges. For another person it is forbidden unless he expressly states that the transaction is a sale. Shach.
That he has. Unminted money is treated like produce, and ownership permits the transaction. Beit Yosef in the name of Ramban.
The correct view. Maharshal, Levush, Bach, and Taz disagree with Rama. This is unlike a seah-for-seah loan, because the recipient receives more than the present value, and permission exists only as a sale. Therefore he must own the full amount of the currency at home. Taz condemns the custom of creating a false sale by having the borrower admit that he owns silver he does not actually possess; a false admission cannot remove a Torah prohibition. Later authorities defend the established Polish heter only when structured according to Maharam's enactment as a genuine investment arrangement.
With money. This means unminted money of a different kind. Money for money of the same kind, even both unminted, and produce for produce, are treated as loans. If he explicitly says, 'Lend me,' even two different species are forbidden, as in siman 162. Shach.
