To a money-changer etc. Tur wrote in the name of the Rama: specifically a money-changer whose practice is such; but with another person it is forbidden unless he says to him explicitly by way of purchase and sale; unlike what Beit Yosef wrote — that Tosafot and the Rosh do not hold so.
A minted dinar. That is legal tender; and see the responsum of Maharam Elshaker siman 50.
That are not minted, etc. This implies specifically unminted pieces, which are treated like produce. But minted coins, even of another denomination, have the law of a loan and an increase is forbidden. This appears to be the view of Tosafos and the Rosh, and the Bach likewise implies it, unlike the Prisha.
And it appears to me, etc. The Levush challenges the Rama: once currency is treated like produce for this purpose, the rules of sale apply, and one may discount only against merchandise presently in his possession unless a market price has already been established. The Prisha, Bach, and Maharshal agree. The Bach's astonishment at the Rama is nevertheless excessive, because the Rama notes that some manuscripts of the Tur contain the opposite reading, which he regards as correct.
Only a dinar in exchange for money. This means unminted money of a different kind. Money for money of the same kind, even if both are unminted, and likewise produce for the same produce, is a loan and an increase is forbidden; only an equal se'ah for a se'ah is permitted under its rules. This is when the transaction is unspecified. If he expressly says 'lend me,' making it a loan, even two different types of produce are forbidden, as explained at the end of siman 162.
