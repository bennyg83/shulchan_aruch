To a money-changer etc. Tur wrote in the name of the Rama: specifically a money-changer whose practice is such; but with another person it is forbidden unless he says to him explicitly by way of purchase and sale; unlike what Beit Yosef wrote — that Tosafot and the Rosh do not hold so.
A minted dinar. That is legal tender; and see the responsum of Maharam Elshaker siman 50.
That are not minted, etc. This implies specifically when they are not minted, for then they are like produce, as Beit Yosef wrote in the name of the students of Rashba in the name of Ramban; so wrote Nimukei Yosef and Ateret Zekenim. But minted coins, even if they are another type, are like a loan and forbidden. So it appears to me to be the view of Tosafot and Rosh, and so it appears in Bach seif 5, unlike Perishah seif 6 who wrote that Tosafot and Rosh disagree with the Tur and Mechaber; see there. And to me it appears, etc. Ateret Zekenim challenged the Rav on this: since coin is like produce by way of purchase and sale, he cannot discount for him except what he has in the house, unless the market price has been established, in which case it is permitted even if he has nothing, as below in siman 175 and above in siman 163. So wrote Perishah, Bach, and Maharshal; see responsum Mabit part 2, page 2, siman 7. But what Bach wrote against the Rav, expressing great wonder how he ruled leniently in something explicit as forbidden in the Tur, etc., I greatly wonder at his wonder, for the Rav wrote that in some copies of the Tur the reading is the opposite, and that this is correct. Only a dinar for coins. Meaning coins that are not minted, which are another type. But coins for coins, even if both are not minted, since they are one type, and likewise produce for produce, are forbidden, for it is a loan; only seah for seah is permitted, but not an excess. So it implies in the poskim. It implies this is without specification, but if he said explicitly "lend me," which is by way of loan, even produce for produce of two species is forbidden. This is what we said above at the end of siman 162, that it is forbidden to borrow a seah of wheat for a seah of millet even if he has millet; see there.
