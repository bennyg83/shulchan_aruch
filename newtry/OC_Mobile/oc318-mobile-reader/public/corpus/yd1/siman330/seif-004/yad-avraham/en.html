(Siman 330 seif 4 in Shulchan Aruch) If it is uncertain, he is obligated. See the Shach, who left requiring study why he stated it without qualification and did not explain that nowadays, when challah is rabbinic, one is exempt in a doubt. According to what the Mishneh LaMelekh writes in chapter 7 of Hilkhot Terumot, that we are stringent in a doubt of terumah nowadays even where there is a presumption of permissibility, this works well. A doubt of terumah nowadays differs from other rabbinic prohibitions, and the same applies to challah, whose law is like terumah. Therefore they were stringent also regarding the doubt of a convert who had dough and it was not known when he rolled it, even though it had a presumption of permissibility, as Rashi writes in Chullin 134. See there for the reason that we are stringent in Torah-level terumah because it carries death; here too they were stringent even nowadays. In my novellae to the Rambam, Hilkhot Terumot chapter 1 and Hilkhot Bikkurim chapter 6, I proved that this is also the Rambam’s view. One may give a reason for this based on what the Mishneh LaMelekh writes in chapter 8 of Hilkhot Malveh regarding shemitah nowadays, which is rabbinic, that a condition that it not cancel the debt is ineffective, because its essence is Torah law, and the only distinction between rabbinic and Torah law is time; they strengthened it like Torah law. This reasoning also applies to terumah and challah nowadays, for the Sages were stringent regarding their doubts as with Torah law. Furthermore, the Temple may speedily be rebuilt and people would then also be lenient as now, as stated in the first chapter of Beitzah and in other places. With this the question of Sefer Makom Shmuel in Niddah 33 is removed. The Mishneh LaMelekh wrote similarly in Hilkhot Bekhorot regarding the presumption that an agent performs his agency. This is unlike Sefer Machaneh Ephraim, Hilkhot Sheluchin siman 21, who wrote that according to the view that terumah nowadays is rabbinic, we say regarding it the presumption that an agent, etc., and wondered why the Shulchan Aruch did not write so. According to my words, there is no wonder.
