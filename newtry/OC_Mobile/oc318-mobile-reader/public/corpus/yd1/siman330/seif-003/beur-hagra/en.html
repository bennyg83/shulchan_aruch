In Chullin the discussion is not about partnership with a non-Jew regarding challah, but about terumah; and Sefer HaTerumah and the other authorities already raised the question from terumah. The Rosh's answer cannot apply there, for any amount of terumah renders the produce obligated. Rather, the statement there follows Rabbi Ila'i; according to the Sages, in the second version, a partnership with a non-Jew is as explained above, siman 331 seif 11, and this is the conclusion of Sefer HaTerumah. The case of arnonah in Pesachim remains difficult, and one must say that even the Israelite's portion does not contain the requisite measure without the portion taken for the tax. In practice, however, no difference follows from this. The main difficulty is the claim that one can separate from within it because there is bilah. This is incorrect, for we rule in Rosh Hashanah 13b that, according to all, there is no bilah, as Tosafot wrote. Rashi's language concerning liquid led him to this, but flour is not considered liquid for this matter. Although Menachot calls flour "liquid" with respect to a dry item mixed with a liquid one, that is only regarding nullification, not here. And the Yerushalmi in Terumot, where Bar Pedaya says to grind it and permit it, also concerns nullification. It appears to me, however, that if they bought grain or flour in partnership from the outset, he may separate from within it, for every particle belongs to both of them jointly, and challah has no fixed minimum measure, as above in siman 331 seif 11. But if separate flours were later mixed together, there is no repair unless he takes enough that it certainly covers his portion, as appears from the manuscript text of the Beit Yosef and from Zevachim and the other places. Thus the distinction is between produce acquired jointly from the beginning and separate portions that were mixed afterward; in the latter case he must separate from elsewhere, or take enough to cover the obligation beyond doubt.
