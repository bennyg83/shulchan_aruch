She is obligated. The reason is concern for appearances. Although concern for appearances would seemingly not apply here, since who would observe her and know whether she separated challah at the time of kneading, at the time of baking, or afterward, nevertheless we rule that wherever the Sages prohibited something because of appearances, it is forbidden even in the innermost chambers. However, this requires study, for perhaps here too we should say, as above in seif 4, Be’er Heitev, seif katan 6, that this applies specifically to challah in Eretz Yisrael, whose obligation is by Torah law, but not to challah outside Eretz Yisrael. Tosafot in Ketubot 60a, s.v. “mema’achen,” and similarly Piskei Tosafot, Chapter 8 of Avodah Zarah, siman 14, write, as I cited above in siman 87, that the rule forbidding something even in the innermost chambers was stated only where there is suspicion that one is violating a Torah prohibition, but not regarding something whose primary prohibition is rabbinic. I subsequently found that Responsa Pri Tevuah, siman 66, raises this issue and writes that, as a matter of practical halachah, we do not rule in accordance with the aforementioned Tosafot in Ketubot.
