(Likkut) And specifically when it is recognizable, etc. Yerushalmi there: What is a dogs' dough? Reish Lakish says: any [dough] into which he mixed bran. The Mishnah stated likewise: when the shepherds eat from it. [But] sometimes the shepherds do not eat from it. R. Yochanan said: any [dough] that he made in the shape of thick loaves (ke'avin). And a Baraita likewise: if he made them as thick loaves — obligated; if he made them as thin student's loaves — exempt. R. Ba in the name of Shmuel, R. Ami in the name of R. Chiyya Rabbah: even if he made them as round rolls (kloskalin). But we learned: "if the shepherds do not eat from it" — resolve it as: he made it from the outset so that the shepherds should not eat from it. The Rosh, §2, and the Tur ruled in the name of the Ramban like R. Yochanan. But the Rambam wrote only the language of the Mishnah and ruled like Shmuel and R. Chiyya Rabbah, holding that they dispute R. Yochanan — and so it appears, since he said "kloskalin," unlike the Beit Yosef. And Rashbam ruled like Reish Lakish. See the Orach Chaim, siman 454, §2, where the Rambam wrote that this is not the case, etc. — following his approach like R. Ba, who [holds] he made it from the outset, etc. Nonetheless, even according to R. Ba one may say the reason of the commentators applies, as written in the gloss there. The reason of Rashbam who ruled like Reish Lakish: the sugya of our Gemara in chapter 2 of Yom Tov accords with him — as we say there, 21a: "R. Chana raises an objection, etc. — a dogs' dough is different, etc." — implying that even though it was made for dogs, since he can eat from it, it may be baked on Yom Tov. One must say in the latter clause specifically where he cannot eat from it. And all those Amoraim in the Yerushalmi cited above hold that we do not apply the principle of "since" (ho'il) — but we hold that we do apply "since," and therefore must follow Reish Lakish. (End of quote.)
