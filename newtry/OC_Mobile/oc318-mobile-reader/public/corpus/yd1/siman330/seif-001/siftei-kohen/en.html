The dough of a non-Jew is exempt. For it is written "your doughs" — and not that of a non-Jew. The Derisha wrote: in places where women buy dough from a non-Jewish baker and take challah from it — it appears this is forbidden, etc. — unless one relies on it since biblically money effects acquisition and they give money before the kneading. Or alternatively: since this is their regular practice, it is possible [the transaction was made] even without giving money beforehand — it is therefore permitted, since the non-Jew knows the Jewish custom, and with this understanding he kneaded it from the outset, and it is treated as if he gave it to the Israelite as a gift before the kneading. End quote. But in the responsum of Masa'at Binyamin, end of §1, he wrote that this is not acting properly and the custom should be abolished — see there.
