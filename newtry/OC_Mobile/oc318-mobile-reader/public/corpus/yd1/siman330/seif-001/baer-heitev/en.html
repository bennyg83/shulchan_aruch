Kneaded. We do not follow the one who rolls the dough, neither for obligation nor exemption, but the one to whom the dough belonged at the time of rolling. The Derishah wrote: in some places women buy dough from a non-Jewish baker and take challah from it. It appears forbidden unless we say one may rely on the fact that by Torah law money effects acquisition and they give the money before rolling. Additionally, since this is their practice, perhaps even without giving the money beforehand it is permitted, because the non-Jew knows the Jewish custom and kneads from the beginning with that in mind; its law is as though he gave it to the Jew as a gift before rolling. In Responsa Mas'et Binyamin he wrote that they do not act properly and the custom should be abolished. End quote from Shach. See above siman 326 s.k. 3, where I wrote about this.
