A bekhor, etc. This is the Rambam's language. He holds that the dispute in the Mishnah and the baraita is one dispute: what the baraita says, “and the sages say even,” etc., provided that he is careful not to make a blemish, while Rabbi Shimon permits even if he will make one, if he does not intend it. The baraita adds that if he made a blemish it may be slaughtered on that basis, etc. Although the sages and Rabbi Shimon hold that imposing a blemish on an already blemished animal is permitted by Torah law, nevertheless it is rabbinically forbidden; but since by Torah law it is permitted, it is permitted even in a place, etc. The halakhah in both matters follows Rabbi Shimon, as stated there in the Gemara. Tosafot there s.v. “if we say,” etc., have another approach, that they disagree in two matters, etc. So wrote the Rosh and Tur, except that the Tur wondered why he ruled like Rabbi Shimon and explains that Shmuel ruled like the sages. His words are forced and astonishing: do we not find throughout the Gemara that the halakhah is ruled like an individual? On the contrary, when it rules like the sages we ask, “is this not obvious?”
