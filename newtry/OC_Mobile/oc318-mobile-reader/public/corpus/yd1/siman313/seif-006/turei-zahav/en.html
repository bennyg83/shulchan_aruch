A blemish through this bloodletting. This follows Rabbi Shimon. But the Rosh and Tosafot ruled like the Sages, who hold that if the bloodletting saves it from death, then even before the bloodletting it is considered blemished, namely with a passing blemish, since it has a cure. Nevertheless, this passing blemish is effective such that he may let blood and even impose a permanent blemish upon it, even rabbinically, because of loss. After the blemish produced by the bloodletting, although it is permanent, one may not slaughter on its basis, as a decree lest at times it could be healed by bloodletting in a place where no blemish is made. Therefore another permanent blemish is needed here in order to slaughter on its basis. Rabbi Shimon does not hold of this decree.
