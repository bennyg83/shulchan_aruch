It is permitted to inflict, etc. The Gemara explains that even after part of a limb has emerged, so long as most of the fetus or its head has not emerged in a manner that gives it the status of being born, as explained above in siman 14 and below in siman 319, one may inflict a blemish. For example, if a kid has long ears visible outside before its entire head emerges, it is not yet considered born, and one may nick its ear before most of its head emerges. The same applies in comparable cases. Bach at the end of siman 319 writes that according to Rambam, who holds that its sanctity applies retroactively, one may inflict a blemish only while the fetus remains entirely inside and has not emerged into the air of the world at all. This does not appear from Rambam or from the Mechaber below in siman 319. Rashi in Chullin 70a likewise indicates that even according to the opinion that sanctity applies retroactively, it is permitted to inflict a blemish before most of it emerges. Rashba explicitly writes in his novellae there, page 73, that until most of it emerges it is not sanctified and therefore one who blemishes it is not regarded as blemishing consecrated property.
