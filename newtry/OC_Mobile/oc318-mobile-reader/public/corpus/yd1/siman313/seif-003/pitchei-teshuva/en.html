The watchman inflicted a blemish in it. See responsum Chavat Yair simanim 34 and 35; and from his words it is clear that this is specifically if he inflicted a blemish that they are not accustomed to make in their animals. But if he castrated the bechor, for it is known to all that in the vast majority they castrate males near birth, he transgresses an issur by leaving the pregnant cow with him, for presumably he will make this blemish in it, since it is l'shevach; for the law of a watchman is to do with others' property as with his own, as in Choshen Mishpat siman 291, and if so this is actual gerama and is considered intentional; see there.
Even though he knows. [See Baer Heitev until the end of the dibbur, "that bechor is completely permitted, etc."; and see Sefer Tiferet Moshe, who wrote that it is a scribal error and one should read "it is a complete issur"; see there.]
