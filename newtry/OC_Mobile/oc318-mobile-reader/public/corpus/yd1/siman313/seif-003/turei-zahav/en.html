We do not say that he intended to permit it. This implies that even a kohen is permitted in this case, which requires examination — why should he be believed here? He is after all suspected of inflicting a blemish on it himself; all the more so he might tell a non-Jew to inflict a blemish on it. And in the Piskei Maharai, this law is written regarding an Israelite who has a doubtful firstborn — and it still requires examination regarding a kohen in this case.
