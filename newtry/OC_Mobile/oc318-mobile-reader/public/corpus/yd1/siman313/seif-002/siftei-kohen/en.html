[Even if he asked. And the same law applies even without the question of the non-Jew — if they themselves speak casually and say, “This will never be slaughtered except by means of a blemish,” without intending thereby that he should inflict a blemish on it — this is what the Talmud and Rashi say:
“And if a non-Jew speaks casually.” Even though above in siman 16, seif 11, we say that a non-Jew speaking casually (mesilah le-fi tumo) is not believed for testimony even for a prohibition, one cannot say that this case is different because it is a rabbinic prohibition — that for leniency one may distinguish between a Torah prohibition and a rabbinic one, as above in siman 98, se’if katan 2 — but for stringency the opposite is more logical, that all the more so he is not believed in a rabbinic matter to be stringent. One may answer that this case is different, since we follow the assessment of his intention (umdena), and where we see that the non-Jew’s intent was to permit [the animal], we conclude that when a non-Jew speaks casually he is telling the truth — for if not, why would he have inflicted a blemish on it?
