If he commanded him, it is forbidden. See Be'er Heitev in the name of the Shach. See Chatam Sofer responsa siman 112, who wrote that what the Shach says here, that where there is an assessment he is believed when speaking casually, means as follows: since from the outset the Sages enacted that a bekhor is forbidden on the basis of mere assessment, as he wrote immediately before this regarding one who places a blemish through others, we follow assessment for prohibition; so too here we follow a non-Jew's casual statement as an assessment. This was the rabbinic enactment regarding placing a blemish in a bekhor, to follow assessment. But regarding other prohibitions, even where there is an assessment, we would not believe them even when speaking casually, as the Rama wrote in siman 130 seif 8, second gloss. From the language of Minchat Yaakov, kelal 59 s.k. 7, it seems that he did not quite understand it this way; see there. See what I wrote above, siman 57 seif 13, s.v. ba-tzavar.
