To inflict a blemish on a firstborn. See Responsa Chatam Sofer, siman 306, who discusses at length whether nowadays this is a Torah or only rabbinic prohibition. Rambam, Hilchot Issurei Mizbeach chapter 1, holds it remains biblical; Rashi, Tosafot, and Rosh hold it is only rabbinic. The questioner suggested a practical consequence that one could intentionally blemish a doubtful firstborn because a rabbinic doubt is treated leniently. Chatam Sofer rejects this. Even according to Rashi, Tosafot, and Rosh, the rabbinic prohibition exists because the act looks like blemishing consecrated property. A doubtful firstborn is itself treated as a firstborn for the Torah doubts of shearing and work. Intentionally blemishing it therefore looks no less like blemishing sacred property. Thus even through a gentile it should be prohibited. See also the end of siman 318. Chatam Sofer further discusses there and in siman 236 whether it is presently possible in principle to offer communal korbanot.
