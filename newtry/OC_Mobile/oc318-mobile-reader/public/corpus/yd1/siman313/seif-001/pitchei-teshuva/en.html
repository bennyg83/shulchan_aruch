To inflict a blemish on a firstborn. See Responsa Chatam Sofer, siman 306, who discusses at length whether nowadays this prohibition is by Torah law or only rabbinic. The Rambam in Hilchot Issurei Mizbeach, chapter 1, rules that even nowadays it is a Torah prohibition, whereas Rashi, Tosafot, and the Rosh hold that it is only rabbinic. The questioner suggested that according to the latter view, one practical difference would be that one could deliberately cause a blemish in a doubtful firstborn, applying the principle that a doubt in rabbinic law is treated leniently. The Chatam Sofer rejects this. Even according to Rashi, Tosafot, and the Rosh, the rabbinic prohibition exists because the act appears to be deliberate blemishing of consecrated property. In a doubtful firstborn, since for shearing and work we treat the animal as a firstborn because of a Torah-level doubt, deliberately inflicting a blemish is the clearest possible appearance of blemishing consecrated property. Therefore even having a non-Jew do so should be prohibited. See also the end of siman 318, and siman 236 regarding whether any offerings, at least communal offerings, can in practice be brought nowadays.
It may not be slaughtered on the basis of that blemish. See Baer Heitev of Maharit. Its citation of Shevut Yaakov concerning a firstborn given to a shepherd is not accurate. There the ruling applies even where the animal was only a doubtful firstborn. See there.
