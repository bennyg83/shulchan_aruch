And it is permitted to place lime. See Shevut Yaakov II siman 97, who was asked during a plague, may the Merciful One protect us, where the authorities ordered that the dead not be buried in their ancestral graves in the city unless lime was poured upon the body to consume it; otherwise they would bury him in one of the forests, in a place without human settlement. Which is preferable? He discusses the words of the Rashba's responsum on this at length and concludes that it is preferable to pour lime on him and have him buried near other graves, rather than be buried in the forests like a donkey burial without any guarding, where dogs might drag him away and the like; see there. See Har Avel, branch 11, where he was asked by one sage whether he had acted properly in instructing his children after him to place lime in his grave so that he would decompose quickly. He answered that although this is permitted, as written in Yoreh De'ah siman 363, nevertheless one should not devise strategies against the acts of the Holy One, blessed be He, etc. However, the custom of seeking earth from Eretz Yisrael to scatter on the body of the dead is because it says, "and His land will atone for His people," and this has a basis in the Yerushalmi, Kilayim chapter 12, end of halachah 3; see there. So too in the introduction to Noda BiYehudah Tinyana it is written that the Gaon z"l instructed that the holy soil he had prepared be scattered upon his body; see there. In Noda BiYehudah Tinyana, Yoreh De'ah siman 210, he was asked about one who became ill with stone disease, may the Merciful One protect us, and the physicians cut him as they do, but it did not help and he died. Is a Jew permitted to cut into the body of the dead to see the root of the wound, so that saving lives may result from it? He answered that if another patient with this illness is before us, it is permitted; if not, it is forbidden. See there. Chatam Sofer responsa siman 336 also wrote that if a patient with a similar wound is before us and they want to dissect this dead person for that patient's cure, it is close to certain that it is permitted. He further wrote regarding one who wants to sell himself during his lifetime to physicians so they will dissect him after his death in order to learn medical practices from him: this does not enter the category of pikuach nefesh at all. Since there is no pikuach nefesh here, there is both a prohibition of benefit and a prohibition of disgrace to the dead. If he is not concerned for his own disgrace, anyone who is not concerned for the honor of his Creator, etc.; see there.
