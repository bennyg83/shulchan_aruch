And although, etc. This is the Rambam's view, as he also writes in his Commentary to the Mishnah, chapter four of Avodah Zarah. All later authorities disagreed with him, since many incantations are recorded in the Gemara. He was drawn after philosophy, and therefore wrote that sorcery, Divine Names, incantations, demons, and amulets are all false. They have already struck his position upon its crown, for many actual incidents are found in the Gemara. Regarding Divine Names and sorcery, she uttered a formula and bound the boat, and they responded, etc. (Shabbat 81b; Chullin 105b). There are also the incidents at the end of chapter four of Sanhedrin and in the Yerushalmi there involving Rabbi Eliezer, Rabbi Yehoshua, and Ben Beteira; Rabbi Chanina and Rabbi Oshaya, who created a third-grown calf; Rabbi Yehoshua, who uttered a formula and suspended him between heaven and earth (Bekhorot 8b); and Avishai ben Tzeruyah (Sanhedrin 95a), among many similar accounts. The Sages also said, at the end of chapter four of Sanhedrin and in Chullin 7b, 'Why are they called keshafim,' etc. The Torah itself testifies, 'and they became serpents.' See the Zohar there. Amulets are likewise mentioned in many places, and the incantations are too numerous to count. Philosophy misled him, through its persuasive influence, to interpret the entire Gemara allegorically and uproot its statements from their plain meaning. Heaven forbid that I believe either the philosophers or their multitude. Rather, all these matters are according to their plain meaning, though they also possess an inner dimension, not the so-called inner meaning of the philosophers, which is itself external, but the inner meaning taught by the masters of truth.
