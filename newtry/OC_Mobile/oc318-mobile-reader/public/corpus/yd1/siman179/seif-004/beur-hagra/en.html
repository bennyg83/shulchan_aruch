Likewise, it is permitted, etc. This follows the Rambam's and Tur's explanation of the statement there that consulting a child is of this type, as the Gemara states there that Rabbi Yochanan would test matters by asking a child to recite a verse, etc.
Some say, etc., and some prohibit. Their reasoning is based on the statement there, 'Any divination that is not,' etc., which implies that in circumstances like theirs it is divination and is prohibited. See Tosafot there, s.v. 'Like Eliezer,' etc., and s.v. 'And like Yonatan,' etc. Regarding the statement, 'A house, a child, and a woman,' etc., the Rambam writes that it is permitted because the person did not direct his conduct by it and did not refrain from acting. Rather, he made a sign for himself from something that had already occurred. This differs from Rashi's explanation. Semag and the other authorities challenged the Rambam, since Rabbi Yochanan tested matters through a child and refrained from descending to Babylonia because of it. The same appears at the beginning of the chapter 'One Who Was Seized,' and from the account that Rav tested matters by a ferry, etc. Rather, the statement, 'A house, a child,' etc., means that one may rely on them, as Rashi explains. The statement, 'Any divination,' etc., was not said concerning the prohibition itself, but concerning whether one may rely on it or be apprehensive because of it. Only where one initially established it as a sign, as Yonatan and Eliezer did, may he rely on it and be apprehensive. If he did not initially establish it as a sign and it occurred on its own, he need not be concerned. Concerning this they said, 'A house, a child, and a woman, although,' etc. Meaning, although he did not initially establish them as a sign, they may nevertheless constitute a sign that warrants concern, provided the pattern has become established, etc. This differs from Yonatan's divination, which was significant even the first time. See Radak to I Samuel 14. This is also Raavad's view, and there is consequently no need for Tosafot's strained explanation cited above.
And one who walks, etc. As stated at the end of chapter three of Nedarim: 'Whoever conducts himself with wholehearted trust,' etc.; 'whoever practices divination,' etc.
