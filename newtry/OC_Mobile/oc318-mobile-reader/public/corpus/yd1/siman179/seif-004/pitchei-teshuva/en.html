Rather, he says it generally. Chatam Sofer discusses customs based on the testament attributed to Rabbi Yehudah HeChasid concerning building a stone house or building where no house previously stood. He rejects slaughtering a rooster and hen there as a supposed substitute for the first inhabitants and advises disregarding practices not grounded in the Gemara or poskim. Where construction is needed to house Jews in exile, it is a mitzvah and worthy of protection; inaugurating the home with Torah and prayer is preferable.
