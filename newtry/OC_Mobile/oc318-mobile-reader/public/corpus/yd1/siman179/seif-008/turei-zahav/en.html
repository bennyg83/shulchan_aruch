He spits and afterward recites, etc. The reason is that since he mentions the Name of Heaven over the spittle, this constitutes a disgrace, God forbid. Therefore, one should not challenge this from the practice of spitting before reciting "But we bow," for there everyone knows that the spitting is intended to disgrace the idols of the non-Jews, and mentioning the Name of Heaven afterward is an honor to Heaven. So it appears to me.
And afterward he recites, etc. So too it appears in Rashi, that specifically whispering after spitting is prohibited. However, the Tur writes, "and he mentions the Name of Heaven and spits," which implies that the prohibition applies even when he spits afterward.
Nevertheless, there is a prohibition. Meaning, to whisper a verse over a wound, even without mentioning the Name of Heaven. The same applies to another illness that is not a wound. So wrote the Beit Yosef.
