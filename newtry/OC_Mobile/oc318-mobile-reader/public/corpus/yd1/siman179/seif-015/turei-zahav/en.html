Holding the eyes is forbidden. But there is no lashes for this, because he did not do an act; and when the Rambam wrote the law of one who practices divination, he wrote: one who holds the eyes and makes it appear before viewers that he does a wondrous act when he did not do it — this is included in divination and he receives lashes; and afterward in the law of sorcery he wrote: a sorcerer is liable to stoning, and that is one who did an act of sorcery; but one who holds the eyes and shows that he did when he did not — he receives rabbinic lashes, because this negative commandment stated regarding a sorcerer "there shall not be found among you" includes him, and it is a negative commandment that does not entail court-imposed death, and one is not lashed for it — end of his words. And many challenged these words of the Rambam against each other; and see in Beit Yosef what he wrote on this; and also in Kesef Mishneh he elaborated on this; and Maharshal also wrote from this — investigate on him; and in my humble opinion it appears the Rambam holds there are lashes for this negative commandment even though there is no act, since in the eyes of viewers it is considered that he does an act even though in truth he does not. And an example for the matter we find in the chapter HaPo'alim regarding silencing with voice, that he is lashed even though there is no act — nevertheless since through his voice it results that the animal goes and treads without eating, as Tosafot wrote there — behold since on one side it is an act, even though in truth there is no act in the person himself; and likewise according to the Rambam, since in the eyes of people it is an act, he is lashed for it; and this is specifically here that in the eyes of people it is considered that he does a wondrous act — meaning the act itself is wondrous that it is impossible to do, and this one appears to do it; and therefore he wrote regarding a sorcerer that one who holds the eyes and makes it appear he did an act — it would also be fitting to lash him for the reason we said, only he is exempt since he is included in sorcerer and was given as a warning for court death; and the difference between them: holding the eyes means he makes it appear he does a wondrous act — he is included in the negative commandment of divination; but holding the eyes of a sorcerer means he makes it appear that through this the act is done, such as that the cucumbers in the field were uprooted that is mentioned in the Gemara regarding this, and it does not appear to them that he himself uproots them — this is not included in divination but in sorcery, and for this there are no lashes; and the Rambam learned this from that which is found in the Gemara in the prohibition of divination that it is holding the eyes, and in the prohibition of sorcery they also said holding the eyes — therefore there is a difference between them; and it is possible this difference was a tradition in his hand to distinguish between divination and sorcery in this matter — so it seems in my humble opinion.
