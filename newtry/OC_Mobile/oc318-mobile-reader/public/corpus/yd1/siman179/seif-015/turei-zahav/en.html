Sleight of hand is prohibited. However, there are no lashes for this, because he performed no action. When the Rambam wrote the law of an onein, he stated: "One who performs sleight of hand and makes it appear before the observers that he performs an astonishing act, although he did not actually do so, is included among the oneinim and receives lashes." Afterward, in the law of a sorcerer, he wrote: "A sorcerer is liable to stoning, provided that he performed an act of sorcery. However, one who performs sleight of hand, meaning that he makes it appear that he performed an act although he did not, receives lashes for rebellious conduct. This is because the negative commandment stated concerning a sorcerer, 'There shall not be found among you,' includes him, and it is a negative commandment given as a warning for a beis din imposed death penalty, for which ordinary lashes are not administered"; end quote. Many have raised a contradiction between these words of the Rambam. See what the Beit Yosef wrote concerning this; the Kesef Mishnah also discussed it at length, and my father-in-law, of blessed memory, likewise wrote about it; see there. In my humble opinion, it appears that the Rambam holds that ordinary lashes apply for this negative commandment even though it involves no physical action, because in the eyes of the observers he is considered to be performing an action, although in truth he does not. We find a parallel in the chapter HaPoalim regarding muzzling an animal through one's voice, for which one receives lashes even though it involves no physical action. Nevertheless, since his voice causes the animal to proceed and thresh without eating, as Tosafos wrote there, it follows that because it is an action from one perspective, ordinary lashes apply, even though the person himself performed no physical action. The same applies according to the Rambam here. Since in the eyes of people he is performing an action, he receives lashes for it. This applies specifically when, in the eyes of people, he is considered to be performing an astonishing act, meaning that the act itself is wondrous and impossible to perform, yet he appears to be the one performing it. Therefore, regarding a sorcerer the Rambam wrote that one who performs sleight of hand and makes it appear that he performed an act would likewise deserve lashes for the reason stated above, except that he is exempt because he is included in the prohibition of sorcery, which was given as a warning for a beis din imposed death penalty. The distinction between them is as follows. Sleight of hand in the category of an onein means that he makes it appear that he himself is performing an astonishing act; this is included in the negative commandment against an onein. Sleight of hand in the category of a sorcerer means that he makes it appear that through this person an act occurred, such as that the cucumbers in the field were uprooted, which is mentioned in the Gemara regarding this matter, but it does not appear to the observers that he himself uprooted them. This is not included in the prohibition of an onein, but in the prohibition of sorcery, and ordinary lashes do not apply. The Rambam derived this from the Gemara, which states regarding the prohibition of an onein that it includes one who performs sleight of hand, and also states regarding the prohibition of sorcery that it includes one who performs sleight of hand. Necessarily, there is a distinction between them. It is possible that the Rambam possessed a tradition defining this distinction between an onein and a sorcerer. So it appears in my humble opinion.
