An act, etc. This follows the statement there: 'Through their secret arts,' these are acts of demons; 'through their sorceries,' these are acts of witchcraft, etc. Evidently, an act involving demons is included within witchcraft. This is Ramah's view. Rosh proves the opposite from Abaye's explanation there, 'Which are acts of demons and which are,' etc. This implies that acts involving demons are permitted, for otherwise what practical difference would there be? This proof is not conclusive, since the Gemara similarly explains in Pesachim 111b, 'of the house of Paracha,' etc., and asks what practical difference follows, answering that it concerns an amulet. The same may apply here. On the contrary, since the Gemara says that an act performed through Sefer Yetzirah is permitted initially, it should have stated the greater novelty that an act involving demons is permitted. Rosh's words are therefore strained. However, the passage in chapter Chelek (Sanhedrin 101a), 'One may not consult demons on Shabbat; Rabbi Yose says,' etc., and, 'Even Rabbi Yose said only,' etc., clearly implies that consulting them is permitted during the week. Rosh rejects this proof by suggesting that perhaps only asking about a theft is permitted, while performing an act is prohibited. This is the meaning of, 'and there are,' etc. See Orach Chaim, siman 307, seif 18.
(Likkut) An act, etc., and there are, etc. As stated there (101a), consulting the princes of oil, etc., is permitted, except that they give false answers. Rif's version, though it is absent from our text of Rif and appears only in Rosh, reads, 'because they give false answers.' Meaning, one will not be drawn after them and come to trust them. This implies that other acts involving demons are prohibited. This requires study, for the baraita there states, according to Rif's and Rosh's version, 'One may not,' etc., and, 'Even Rabbi Yose said only,' etc. Perhaps this too refers specifically to the princes of oil, etc., but that is strained and requires study. End of Likkut.
And through adjuration, etc. This resembles use of Sefer Yetzirah, which is permitted. As stated regarding Ashmedai (Gittin 68a), 'Perhaps your master's Name is upon you,' meaning that he had adjured him. Many adjurations are recorded, including the incidents involving Rabbi Chanina and Rabbi Oshaya (Shabbat 81b; Chullin 105b), Rabbi Yehoshua (Yerushalmi, end of chapter four of Sanhedrin), and Rabbi Chanina bar Papi (Kiddushin 39b), as well as many similar incidents in the Gemara and Midrashim in which the Sages used them. See Ramban's responsum, cited by Beit Yosef.
Nevertheless, most, etc. As stated there, 'Even Rabbi Yose said only,' etc., 'in a case such as this,' etc. The incident involving Shlomo (Gittin 68b) proves this, yet even he experienced terror, etc. See the end of Sefer Chassidim, and above, siman 246, seif 21, at the end of the gloss.
