And there is no, etc. See Tikkunei Zohar 104b.
Therefore they practiced, etc., because, etc. As it is stated, kings are anointed beside a spring (Horayot 12a; Keritot 5b); water is drawn through pipes before a groom and bride, etc. (Berakhot 50b); a person should accustom himself to eat at the beginning of the year, etc.; they should be raised beside water, etc. (Horayot there; Keritot 6a). At the end of chapter eighteen of Shabbat: one who lets blood on Sunday, etc.; on Tuesday, what is the reason, etc.; four, which is four, etc. A virgin marries on Wednesday because the blessing to the fish was stated then, and a widow, etc., because the blessing to man was stated then (Ketubot 5a). There are many similar examples. This is the meaning of the ruling cited above in Shulchan Aruch, 'They practiced,' etc.
Regarding that which a person, etc. As stated at the end of Shabbat concerning Rabbi Akiva's daughter and Rav Nachman bar Yitzchak, who were apprehensive of the astrologers' predictions. This is not the prohibition against divination through the stars. They also said there, 'One who is born under the moon,' etc., and, 'The constellation of the day does not determine,' etc. Likewise, as stated above, 'On Tuesday, what is the reason,' etc. The statement that one may not ask means that one may not initiate an inquiry. This is the meaning of, 'but one should not investigate,' etc. The same applies to omens from birds, as in the incident of Rav Ilish (Gittin 45a), when Ilish fled. See Taz 123:1, Beit Yosef, and Responsa Rashba, siman 413, where he discusses this at length.
