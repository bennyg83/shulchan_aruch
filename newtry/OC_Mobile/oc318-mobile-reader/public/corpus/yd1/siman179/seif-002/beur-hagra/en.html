They customarily, etc. See Zohar, Parashat Pinchas, 234a.
And there is no, etc. See Tikkunei Zohar, 104b. And therefore the custom is, etc. because, etc. As written [that] kings are anointed by a spring (Horayot 12a; Keritot 5b); and one draws water in a conduit before a groom and bride, etc. (Berakhot 50b); "a person should accustom himself to eat at the beginning of the year, etc. — gourds upon water, etc." (Horayot ibid.; Keritot 6a); and at the end of chapter 18 of Shabbat: "a portion [is eaten] on the first [day of] the week, etc. — on the third, why? etc. — the fourth, because it is the fourth, etc."; "a virgin is wed on the fourth day, since [on it] was said the blessing of fish; and a widow, etc. — since [on it] was said the blessing of man" (Ketubot 5a); and many similar [teachings] — and that is the meaning of what the Shulchan Aruch writes: "they customarily, etc." In whatever a person, etc. As written at the end of Shabbat, in the baraita of R. Akiva; and in the story of Rabi Nachman bar Yitzchak there — that they were apprehensive about the words of the Chaldeans — and this is not [the same as] those who divine through stars. And it is stated there: "one who was born under the moon, etc." — "it is not the sign of the day that causes, etc." — and as above, "on the third of Shabbat — why not, etc." — and what is stated: "one may not consult [omens] ab initio" — and that is the meaning of "but one should not inquire, etc." — and likewise with birds, as with the incident of Rav Ilish (Gittin 45a): "Ilish fled"; and see Avodah Zarah 123a; and see Beit Yosef; and in Rashba, siman 413, who elaborated on this.
