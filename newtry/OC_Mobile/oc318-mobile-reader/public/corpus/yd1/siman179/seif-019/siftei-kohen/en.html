One who burns incense, etc. Rabbi Yerucham wrote: one who burns incense for a demon is an idolater, and even if he does not burn for the sake of idolatry but to bind — he is liable to stoning; and Ramah wrote that we learn from that which one burns incense for a demon to bind him and compel him to do his will — he is liable because of a medium — end of his words; and Beit Yosef wrote: I do not know what relevance a medium has to this, and it appears one should erase "because of a medium" and he is liable because of idolatry he says — end of his words; and according to his words here in the composition it is astonishing, for in chapter 4 of deaths (Sanhedrin 65a) we say: rather Ulla said (medium that is taught in Keritot) regarding one who burns incense for a demon — Rava said to him: one who burns incense for a demon is an idolater; rather Rava said: one who burns incense to bind (Rashi: he does not burn for divine worship but through incense the sorceries are done to bind demons here) — behold one who burns incense for a demon is liable because of a medium; and it appears to settle that Beit Yosef holds the Talmud implies that burning to bind means he burns generically and through incense demons connect and he is liable because of a medium, as Rashi wrote; but when he burns for a demon even though he did so to bind the demons — he is liable because of idolatry, for otherwise what does Rava challenge Ulla, that perhaps he speaks of burning for a demon in order to bind the demons; and we do not find in any matter one who burns incense for a demon who is not liable because of idolatry; and Ramah explicitly mentions burning incense for a demon like Ulla's language — if so, even in order to bind him to do his will he is liable because of idolatry; and furthermore necessarily he speaks because of idolatry, for otherwise what does he say "we learn from this" — meaning this one; and all the more so he says thus: we learn from this that since he does not establish Ulla's view as burning for a demon in order to bind him — behold burning for a demon in every matter is liable because of idolatry; and furthermore there are many proofs in the Talmud and Rashi as Beit Yosef, and the Gaon Avraham Abele z"l explained them in a responsum — see there; and it emerges that if he is liable because of idolatry he is an apostate regarding the entire Torah, as above siman 3 and siman 119; unlike if he is liable because of a medium, that he is only an apostate regarding one matter — and let it be clear.
One who learns from the heretic, etc. And in my humble opinion it appears the main thing in the Talmud in Shabbat (chapter Kol HaGadol 75a) that it is permitted to learn from a heretic sorcerer words of Torah and matters of sorcery to understand and to rule; and likewise is proven in Rashi and Tosafot there; and it appears to me that Shmuel also admits to this, unlike Beit Yosef; and specifically from a sect idolater who learns from him even words of Torah — liable to death; and what Rabbi Yerucham wrote on this — either it is a scribal error, or it deals with when he learns matters of sorcery to do. And Bach in siman 182 strained and wrote that a generic sorcerer is a sect member and it is forbidden to learn from him to understand and to rule, unless it is known he is not a sect member, etc.; and according to my humble opinion it appears as I wrote.
