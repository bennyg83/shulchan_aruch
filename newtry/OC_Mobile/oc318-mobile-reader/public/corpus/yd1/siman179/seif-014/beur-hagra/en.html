To adjure, etc. Hagahot Maimoniyot cites this in the name of Rabbi Eliezer of Metz, based on the account at the beginning of chapter three of Berakhot involving the pious man, and on the accounts of Shmuel and Ze'iri, and at the end of Moed Katan, 'Let him appear to me,' etc. Beit Yosef hesitated whether this is also permitted after death, and wrote that the proofs from Berakhot can be rejected. His rejection is itself untenable. See Darkei Moshe. This is the meaning of the gloss, 'and there are,' etc. See Shach.
