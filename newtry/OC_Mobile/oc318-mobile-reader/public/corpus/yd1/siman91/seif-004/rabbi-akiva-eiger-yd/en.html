(Avodat HaTaharah) Even matzah in hot use. And I have difficulty — if so how did Terumat HaChacham write to resolve that Orach Chaim distinguishes between a vessel used hot and one used cold, namely that Orach Chaim deals with a gentile vessel whose use is cold — if so why did he write that it is permitted to place spices that do not extract a hard dry thing in dry — for even hot that extracts is permitted to use; investigate.
(seif 4) And if they fell into each other while cold, one rinses the meat; and b'dieved if one did not rinse the meat and cooked it, 60 is required against all the meat; for specifically dry with dry does not require rinsing only because of concern for contact and adhesion on the surface — in that case it is permitted b'dieved; and likewise hot meat that fell requires peeling; nevertheless after peeling, thorough rinsing is also required — Noda BiYehudah (general rules 22 and 23 section 11; and see Peri Megadim siman 105 in Mishbetzot Zahav s.k. 2).
(Taz s.k. 5) From piece to piece without gravy. It is astonishing — for here there is gravy since they are below and the milk prevails, which is called gravy.
(Shach s.k. 8) And it appears there is no contradiction. And in Magen Avraham (siman 467 s.k. 133) he distinguishes that here specifically when the lower prevails, the peel is only a general stringency because it absorbed a little — in that case we are lenient b'dieved; but not in general — see there; and specifically when the stream was interrupted; but milk that fell on meat without interrupting the stream — perhaps even 60 does not help, for everything the stream touches is constantly forbidden, each drop and drop — and likewise below siman 104 regarding a stream of hot fat on a mouse; and see Peri Megadim in Mishbetzot Zahav (siman 105 s.k. 6); however according to Kapos Temarim (siman 104), that which we say there regarding fat, that with great loss we rule even in moist there is no noten ta'am ben noten ta'am because fat congeals immediately and is like dry — see there; if so with milk streaming on meat, 60 helps, for in moist there is no piece-not-piece; nevertheless Rama ruled below (siman 92) that in basar b'chalav even in moist we say noten ta'am ben noten ta'am, and it implies even in basar b'chalav which is rabbinic; if so here all the milk is forbidden, and especially for those who hold that when the stream is not interrupted it cooks the measure of peeling — then it is basar b'chalav d'oraisa.
