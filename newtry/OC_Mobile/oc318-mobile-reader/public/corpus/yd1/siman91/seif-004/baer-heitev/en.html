Prevails. Shach wrote that for us even hot roasted meat outside the pot is also forbidden; and Taz wrote all this is specifically regarding prohibition because of itself, but if it is absorbed prohibition it does not pass from piece to piece without gravy — see siman 105 s.k. 7 (and in Shenei Luchot in the name of Maharshal it appears to him when the prohibition is hot and the permitted cold placed next to each other — all are forbidden; and if the prohibition is cold and the permitted hot — one should be stringent per Rashba to require peeling — end of his words).
One peels. Because the lower is cold it cools the upper, except that it absorbed a little; therefore peeling is required; and above siman 105 s.k. 3 it is explained that even if hot milk fell on cold meat through pouring without interrupting the stream, peeling suffices; and likewise even if the stream was interrupted, peeling is required — see there.
B'dieved. [Riva, Maharshal, Maharai, and Taz agreed with them that 60 is required in the milk against the peel forbidden from it] and Shach wonders at this and brings several proofs to refute this law; and Rama himself wrote in siman 98 s.k. 4 that a vessel forbidden through salting the measure of peeling requires afterward sixty; and therefore it must deal here where the peel is not recognizable, namely something blended; but if recognizable and peeling applies, if they did not peel, sixty is required against the peel — see there at length.
And permitted. Orach Chaim wrote in the name of Mordechai and Semag that specifically in basar b'chalav the law is so, but in other prohibitions even if both are cold, peeling is required; and Shach challenged this — it requires study, for this is not found in Mordechai and Semag, and on the contrary from the conclusion of their words it implies the opposite — see there; and from Taz's words it implies one should be stringent also in basar b'chalav if one did not peel to require sixty against the peel; and he wrote that so appears to him for practical law.
