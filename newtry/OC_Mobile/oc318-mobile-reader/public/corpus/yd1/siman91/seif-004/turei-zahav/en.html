And all the milk is permitted. Tur brought Riva's view that there must be 60 in the milk against the peel forbidden from it; and Maharai wrote in Hagahot Shach that although elsewhere a peel does not forbid its fellow even if the peels are fatty, etc., because there is no power for what is forbidden where the prohibition cannot go — likewise there is no meat taste in all the milk; nevertheless 60 is required since it is a moist thing and mixes together; and it appears practice is stringent, not like Rosh — end of his words; and Shulchan Aruch ruled like Rosh, namely R' Yitzchak and Rambam who do not require 60 against the peel; and Orach Chaim general rule 59 rules leniently like Rosh in the name of Maharam, that wherever the Sages required peeling and it was cooked thus without peeling, even without 60 against the peel it is permitted even l'chatchila, for the Sages required peeling only as an initial stringency — end of his words; and in general rule 36 he wrote the opposite, that a bowl forbidden the measure of peeling forbids up to 60 against the peel; and Terumat HaDeshen wrote his words in general rule 36 are per Riva's view, and it is a great strain that Riva's view was not mentioned there, and on the contrary his words are to rule plainly; and I wonder at Maharai in the name of Rosh to be lenient, for Rosh brought two views in chapter 25 and did not decide, and on the contrary Maharshal wrote therefore he brought Riva's view last, for so is his conclusion; and in my humble opinion it can be proven from Rosh's words in chapter 7 of Chullin the opposite, and Tur brought it in siman 10 regarding slaughtering with a gentile knife — peel the place of slaughter; and Rosh wrote one should not slaughter l'chatchila relying that he will peel afterward — it turns out that whatever is not peeled he eats prohibition even b'dieved, for whatever is permitted b'dieved we are not concerned initially lest one forget — and this is proven in siman 1 in Tur regarding "however l'chatchila they may give him to slaughter relying that he will inspect," and siman 2 that we are concerned lest he forget and not inspect, etc.; and so Beit Yosef there explicitly; however this can be rejected — perhaps permitted b'dieved without peeling, nevertheless one may not rely l'chatchila since there is clear prohibition with a remedy, as the beginning of this siman and Maharshal chapter 25 siman 60 ruled stringently like Riva that 60 is required against the peel even b'dieved; and he further wrote that even if there is 60 in the meat against the milk, nevertheless the meat requires peeling, for it did not absorb throughout; also in investigation he brought Maharshal's view to be stringent; and Rama ruled here and in Terumat HaChacham that milk does not require 60 against the peel — thus he does not hold like Riva; and so Terumat HaChacham general rule 23 — if one poured permitted food into a forbidden bowl it is permitted, for by law only peeling is required; and it is astonishing that in siman 69 seif 16 he ruled that for a moist hot thing 60 is required against the peel — they are Rashba's words there in Beit Yosef, and so Terumat HaChacham general rule 12 — namely like Riva; thus his words contradict each other; and since Rashba also holds so, we should rule like Riva to be stringent, and everywhere 60 is required against the peel; and so in Mordechai chapter Kol HaBasar in an incident before Rabbenu Yehudah — they kneaded dough, etc., he ruled there 60 is required against the peel of the bowl; and so implied at the end of seif 5 in hagahah regarding gravy falling on a vessel — hag'alah and earthenware breaking are required, and for wooden vessels peeling suffices — thus b'dieved we require nullification of what was forbidden the measure of peeling; and further proof from what Rosh brought, Beit Yosef beginning siman 96 — a slice of cress cut with a forbidden knife is all forbidden though it only forbids the measure of peeling, because the slices are small and one cannot know where it was cut, therefore all was forbidden — end of his words, from which we learn that where peeling is impossible, all is forbidden; and further proof from Orach Chaim siman 95 seif 3 — if one poured from a kli rishon of meat onto a milk vessel it forbids all of it, etc. — end of his words; and although pouring does not forbid more than the measure of peeling, nevertheless it forbids all where peeling is impossible; and further proof from Rama siman 98 seif 4 — but if through hot salting, etc., one estimates only the measure of peeling, etc. — end of his words; and further proof from Orach Chaim general rule 36 din 6 — if placed in a boiling pot, 60 is required against its peel, etc.; and I saw in hagahah at the end of Orach Chaim in the name of Maharai that practice is like Riva to require sixty, and so is practical law.
