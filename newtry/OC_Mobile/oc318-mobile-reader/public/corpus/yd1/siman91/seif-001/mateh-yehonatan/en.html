(siman 91 seif 5) Salting, etc., and it waited the measure of salting for a pot. See the Acharonim who challenged from that which is written above siman 69 that even beforehand it is also like hot; and one may answer that there is a difference between salted meat and gravy — gravy is hot beforehand and salted meat is not hot until it waits; and Pri Te'uar explains the reason — because meat cools the salt since it lies on it, unlike gravy which is collected in a vessel by itself and nothing cools it — it is immediately hot; or one may say as we say regarding Shabbat that salt is needed in a pot like ox meat — it implies that salt does not cook ox meat, and nevertheless it is forbidden to put hot meat in it — because even though the meat is not cooked, the moisture in it cooks, as Rosh wrote in Shabbat there; if so here too — the moisture which is gravy is immediately called hot, unlike meat which is not called hot until it waits the measure of salting. But one may ask — how does the Mechaber intend to permit before it waited, for once meat was salted with cheese there is certainly gravy here; and Shach who wrote to distinguish between gravy and salt — this is according to his view that after rinsing the openings of exudation closed and it will never expel anything, even gravy; and here it deals with meat already salted and rinsed and there is no gravy; however for poskim who hold that even after rinsing it still has exudation of gravy — it must deal with after all exudations of gravy finished as above; and one may say that gravy exuded through salting is not forbidden because of basar b'chalav, for in all prohibitions if not for the verse "the impure to forbid their gravy" we would say it is truly permitted; if so regarding meat which is permitted one may say regarding gravy there is no Torah taste at all, and there is some proof — if not, why is the verse of the impure needed; if so in gravy there is no Torah taste — if so it is not forbidden at all in basar b'chalav because of gravy — investigate.
