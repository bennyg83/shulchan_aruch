To rinse. Avodat HaTaharah in the name of Bach; however a sharp thing even if dry is like cold moist and requires rinsing, as written in seif 5; and if they are dry, etc. — likewise Peri Megadim and Chavat Da'at; and this rinsing means plain rinsing without rubbing; but permitted that touched forbidden grease requires rubbing until one knows by estimate that greasiness left him — so appears Peri Megadim in Shach (and see s.k. 3); but Chayei Adam wrote specifically knife pressure and greasiness require rubbing, such as cutting meat with a knife that cut cheese; but pressure without greasiness or greasiness without pressure suffices with plain rinsing; and see responsum Beit Ephraim Yoreh De'ah siman 29 that his view too is that greasiness without pressure suffices with plain rinsing; but he wrote specifically milk that was not dissolved in water, but milk dissolved in water — Rashba wrote it adheres more and requires great rubbing; therefore above siman 64 he forbids rinsing in a vessel used to rinse cheeses though there is no pressure; and he further wrote that thick milk or butter that fell on cold meat and congealed there requires great rubbing — see there; and a soft thing like cucumbers siman 96 seif 5 and the like requires scraping; and a thing that cannot be rinsed at all, such as water — see s.k. 21 afterward what its law is; and if salted or roasted without rinsing and something the measure of a peel was absorbed on the surface, nevertheless permitted without peeling; and likewise for other prohibitions a slight amount is permitted even in a moist thing that cannot be rinsed at all — end of his words.
