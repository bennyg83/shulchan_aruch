And specifically, etc. — the Acharonim Shach and Peri Chadash elaborated greatly, and both intended the same regarding earthenware — one should be stringent even if customary to rinse, as Shach wrote s.k. 3: "or rather the main point is that earthenware is different," etc.; and in my humble opinion Rama's law is inferred from this without distinguishing, for Peri Chadash himself wrote therefore the distinction between earthenware and a rinsed vessel — there one can kasher and use cold, and we are not concerned lest it stay and come to stumbling through hot use, for one can kasher it; but earthenware has no remedy and is forbidden even cold lest one leave it and come to stumble; and this is the reason for concern with an absorbed-forbidden vessel; but in this siman it deals with placing meat in a milk vessel or the reverse, and the forbidden vessel mentioned is not precise — only regarding meat it is called forbidden here, and all agree it is permitted, for when one leaves meat or milk vessels and leaves them, why forbid placing cold — certainly cold is permitted like a rinsed vessel; and therefore Rama forbade cold lest one leave it — this applies to meat or milk vessels, and all is judged like a rinsed vessel and permitted; and this is Rama's reason, therefore he omitted this law distinguishing earthenware from other vessels.
