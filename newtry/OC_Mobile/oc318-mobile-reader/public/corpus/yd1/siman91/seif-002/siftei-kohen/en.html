To place meat, etc. And the same applies to place forbidden cold in a permitted cold vessel that is not customary to rinse — forbidden l'chatchila, because Rashba wrote in Tur HaShalem long and short 112b, and in the short version; and see siman 10 s.k. 20 and siman 69 s.k. 83.
And specifically, etc. The Mechaber's words require explanation, for his words in Terumat HaChacham and Darkei Moshe too require study, for in Terumat HaChacham general rule 17 din 2 he brought Orach Chaim's words (that R' Chananel wrote, brought by Beit Yosef s.k. 105) that gentile vessels rinsed well are permitted to place spices or onion or other sharp things that do not extract a hard dry thing in dry — end of his words; and afterward he wrote in din 4: however Tur Yoreh De'ah siman 91 in Ba'al HaItur's name wrote that everything requiring rinsing, such as cold meat in a forbidden cold bowl, is forbidden l'chatchila lest one eat without rinsing; and specifically cooked meat that is not customary to rinse, but what is customary to rinse such as raw meat and the like is permitted l'chatchila — end of his words; and it implies spices and the like that are not customary to rinse are forbidden to place in a forbidden vessel; yet one may distinguish that a forbidden vessel used hot is forbidden to use with permitted cold, and so Tur Yoreh De'ah explicitly in siman 121; however if forbidden cold use was used, permitted cold use is permitted even l'chatchila; therefore Rashba permitted salting meat in a yayin nesekh vessel even l'chatchila; and so with chametz vessels one may use matzah, dealing in such a case as in the Gemara, brought by Tur Orach Chaim siman 451; and so appears Yoreh De'ah siman 69; or distinguish whether the vessel was washed well initially — if washed well initially it is certainly permitted as Yoreh De'ah siman 122; and similarly Darkei Moshe who first challenged Ba'al HaItur from matzah and distinguished between moist permitted and dry, then wrote it further appears to distinguish between something that absorbed prohibition through hot or pickling and salting, etc., and this resolution appears primary to him; and from this understand his view that although the vessel is forbidden because of absorption of prohibition, nevertheless since prohibition was not absorbed through hot only through pickling or salting, permitted cold use is permitted (and Ba'er Hetev's words on this are confused — see there); this is the Mechaber's path, but all his words from beginning to end are unclear to me: at the outset what he wrote 'but one may distinguish,' etc., and so Taz in siman 121, etc. — I did not grasp his intent, for it is not found there as he says, and on the contrary it appears explicitly the opposite, as Tur wrote there: vessels used hot such as pots and kettles require hag'alah and immersion and are permitted; and Rabbenu Shemuel wrote specifically hag'alah then immersion, but if immersed first it is like immersing with a sheretz in hand; but R' Yitzchak wrote one may immerse first and if he only wants cold use he may immerse and use cold, and when he wants hot use he hag'alas and the prior immersion suffices — end of his words; and so Rosh end of Avodah Zarah; and what he wrote 'therefore Rashba permitted,' etc. — is irrelevant, for Rashba would permit anyway since raw meat is customary to rinse, and if because of salting it is nothing as Rashba wrote there for several reasons brought by Beit Yosef end of siman 105 — see there; and what he wrote 'and so with chametz,' etc. — is not agreed by all, but Rif in chapter Kol Shelah, Rambam, Ramban, and other poskim as explained Beit Yosef Orach Chaim, and Rashba in his novellae brought proof from Rif and Ramban; however Ra'ash chapter Kol Shelah and Tur there hold that with beit se'or and charoset one may use matzah cold (but they too infer from the Gemara only that except beit se'or and charoset one may use matzah hot, but cold they hold even beit se'or and charoset are permitted by reasoning — see there); and it implies for them too if chametz was absorbed hot, one may use matzah cold — what difference absorption by cold or hot; and on the contrary Ra'ash there brought the dispute whether for beit se'or and charoset even hag'alah does not help though for other vessels used hot hag'alah certainly helps for all; and in Avodah Zarah 34a they distinguish use hot and cold — for using permitted hot or pickled in them; but using permitted cold is obviously no distinction, as Ran chapter Kol Shelah, and so Semak, Shach, and Semag prohibition 141 (Noda BiYehudah 72b); and what he wrote 'and so appears Yoreh De'ah siman 69' — truly from Tur's words there it implies if one salted meat in an unperforated bowl, one may eat afterward cold without rinsing bowl or food; but the first view he brought is Rashba and supporters whom Beit Yosef brought that even if a forbidden boiling vessel was used, the law is so; if so the question returns, for Rashba ruled in Tur HaShalem long and short like Ba'al HaItur, and Beit Yosef brings him; and what he wrote 'and so appears Yoreh De'ah siman 122' — requires study; and he did not fulfill explaining distinguishing moist permitted from dry; for Rashba there explicitly permits eating cold moist in it — see there; and what Darkei Moshe distinguished between moist permitted and dry fails to explain, for Rashba explicitly permits cold moist there — see there; but in truth it appears to me what Orach Chaim wrote — gentile vessels rinsed well — means we know certainly they were rinsed well, such as a Jew rinsed now; and it does not mean rinsing specifically is required, for poskim who learned this hold thorough wiping is like rinsing everywhere; and Ba'al HaItur deals without wiping; or the main point is earthenware is different, forbidden even cold, and Taz follow their view that even earthenware permitted cold is permitted; and what Orach Chaim wrote that it does not extract hard dry in dry excludes sharp moist spices; but here, not extracting hard, even moist; however all occasional use, but regular use even cold forbidden lest use hot, as Rama hagahah siman 121 s.k. 5 — end of his words.
