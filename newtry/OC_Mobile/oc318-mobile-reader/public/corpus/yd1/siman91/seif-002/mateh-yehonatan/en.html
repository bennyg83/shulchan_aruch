(there) And some say that after it waited in its salt the measure of salting, etc. See Shach s.k. 11 who wrote that the Mechaber followed Terumat HaDeshen siman 159 — see there; and I heard from my master and teacher that from Rosh's second version in resolving the incident of Rashi it proves that salt after waiting the measure of salting always stands in its heat — thus Rosh in chapter 25: "and he said we do not say salted is like hot except at the time of its exuding; if so at the time of completing exuding it is not considered hot, for regarding the dove that fell into brine we say salted is like hot, etc., and one may answer, etc.; and in section 38 he wrote: 'and meat that was salted and waited, etc., or the strength of the salt ceased, etc., and because of that salt it is no longer considered hot after completing exuding, etc., and it is not comparable to the dove, etc., that was considered hot if the brine was not eaten because of its salt, for there the strength of the salt did not cease' — and one may analyze his words — if so why did he challenge above that the vessel should be forbidden because of the salt dissolved, lest after completing exuding it is no longer called hot; and one cannot challenge from the dove, for there the strength of the salt did not cease as he wrote here; and it must be that Rosh holds that even though we say the strength of the salt ceased — meaning it is no longer considered strongly salted since its strength ceased in extracting blood from the meat — nevertheless gravy always stands in heat, for there is no language to say the strength of the salt ceased; if so Rosh's challenge is sound that meat should be forbidden because of blood-salt dissolved in gravy — as in brine, through the salt in it it is always considered hot, so too gravy that exuded before waiting the measure of waiting — through the salt in it it always stands in heat; therefore one should wonder at Terumat HaDeshen who permitted in that incident — for presumably it was not yet rinsed and presumably there was still gravy and it is always hot and treifah gravy was absorbed in kosher meat and one should forbid even with great loss; and one may say Rama brought his view here in basar b'chalav where otherwise there is reason to say gravy does not forbid in basar b'chalav as above — and although Rama holds below that gravy is like meat, here too it is on this reason alone he does not wish to rely; what is not so here — one may say he relied on Terumat HaDeshen because of this reason that gravy does not forbid in basar b'chalav; and this is somewhat clear from Rama bringing Terumat HaDeshen's law here regarding basar b'chalav where its place is not here — he should have brought it below regarding mixture of other prohibitions siman 105 — but certainly there he does not wish to rely on Terumat HaDeshen — as he wrote — investigate.
