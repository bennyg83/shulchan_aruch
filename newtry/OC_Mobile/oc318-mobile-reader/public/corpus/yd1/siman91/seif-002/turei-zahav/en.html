Everything that requires rinsing, etc. Tur wrote in this regard "everything," etc. — one may challenge: what does "everything" mean, for explicitly in the Mishnah we say "provided they do not touch each other"; and one may answer that specifically meat and cheese themselves, but regarding their vessels one need not be careful — it teaches that everything requiring rinsing, even in vessels, are equal; and one may challenge further: Beit Yosef wrote in siman 2 that wherever b'dieved is permitted we are not concerned initially lest one forget, as will be cited in seif 4; if so why are we concerned here lest one eat without rinsing, since if one eats without rinsing there is no prohibition, for even where peeling is required some hold it is permitted b'dieved without peeling? And one may answer that where there is no clear prohibition before us but only doubt requiring investigation afterward, since b'dieved it is permitted one may rely l'chatchila on the investigation afterward; unlike here where there is clear prohibition in placing kosher meat in a forbidden vessel, only that it has remedy through rinsing afterward — we do not rely on rinsing afterward lest one forget.
Forbidden l'chatchila, etc. Maharshal chapter 25 siman 44 wrote, and therefore travelers who eat salted herring on gentile vessels do not act well, for rinsing is required after placing on them; and all the more those who buy salted cucumbers called ogrekes with brine and eat in their bowl, for even the cucumbers require rinsing if placed in their bowl; if so the brine, to which rinsing does not apply, is all forbidden — end of his words; and Terumat HaChacham general rule 16 wrote that in time of pressure, such as lodging in a gentile house, it is considered b'dieved — end of his words.
And specifically in a thing that has moisture, etc. Darkei Moshe proved this from the law of a vessel used for chametz in cold use that one may use matzah, as in Orach Chaim siman 451; and further he wrote there and Terumat HaChacham general rule 17 on what Tur wrote here that anything not customary to rinse is forbidden in a forbidden cold bowl — he challenged this from Orach Chaim that gentile vessels rinsed well are permitted to place dry spices, etc.; and he answered — see there; and from all this it is explained that a vessel used for forbidden cold use is permitted to place dry hot food, as we noted regarding chametz vessels; if so a fortiori permitted cold moist use is permitted, more than dry hot is less than cold moist, as Orach Chaim siman 451 and the Rashba case of salting in a yayin nesekh vessel; and in this hagahah it implies that even for dry use we require that initial use was cold, and then cold use is permitted specifically; and this is very astonishing, for it was already explained that it is not so except per the language of this hagahah — it is astonishing that he wrote it is permitted to place dry food, etc. — this is superfluous language already mentioned initially; and it appears there is a scribal error and it should read: but a completely dry thing, or if the vessel did not absorb only through cold use, it is permitted to place moist food without rinsing; and the explanation is that he refers to Shulchan Aruch's forbidden cold bowl where hot use was used, and on this he said dry use is permitted without rinsing, and if forbidden use was not used initially only cold, afterward permitted without rinsing even for moist food — as appears to me, and Levush also mentioned only dry alone, per my view.
