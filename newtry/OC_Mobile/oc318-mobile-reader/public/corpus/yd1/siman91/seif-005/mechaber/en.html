Salted meat that cannot be eaten because of its salt — that is, like the salting they salt for a pot — and it waited the time of salting for a pot, as long as it was not rinsed, it is not considered "not eaten because of its salt": {Rama: And some say that after it waited in its salt the measure of salting, it is no longer considered hot (Tosafot and Rosh chapter 25 and Semag for one version); and for great need, such as great loss and it is for the sake of a mitzvah meal, one may rely on those who are lenient, but otherwise one must not be lenient at all (Terumat HaDeshen siman 159); even if the meat was not salted on both sides but only on one side — all that was salted with salting that is not eaten because of its salt is considered hot (Beit Yosef in the name of responsum of Rashba siman 265); but anything not salted so much is considered cold even if salted on both sides; and some say we are not expert in the matter and we must consider even salting of roasted meat as hot (Arukh 30), and it is good to be stringent where there is no great loss:} and salting like the salting needed for roasting — even after it was rinsed — is still not eaten because of its salt, and as long as they were not soaked in water its law is like hot to forbid the measure of peeling, and there is no difference between upper salted and lower unsalted and lower salted and upper unsalted — always the salted absorbs in the unsalted and does not absorb from it; therefore salted meat and cheese that touched each other require peeling both at the place of contact, and if one is salted and the other unsalted, the salted is permitted with rinsing and the unsalted requires peeling: {Rama: And some say in every salting we estimate at 60 (most early and late authorities), and see below siman 105 how we practice; and that pure salted and impure unsalted is permitted — this is when the impure is dry; but if it is a clear substance, the pure is forbidden since it is not eaten because of its salt — it absorbs the prohibition next to it, and both are forbidden (so it appears in Mordechai and so wrote Hagahot Maimoniyot, Shach, Semag, and Hagahot Mordechai), and specifically when the pure is a little moist; but if it is completely dry, it does not absorb from a moist thing next to it.} And if they are dry — even if they are salted — or moist not because of salting, rinsing suffices: {Rama: All gravy from meat that was salted — even if it was not salted only for roasting — is considered hot (Hagahot Maimoniyot chapter 9 in the name of Machzor Vitry, Shach, Hagahot Ashiri from Avodah Zarah, and Agudah chapter 25); therefore if gravy fell on cheese or on a vessel it forbids (Arukh), even in a place where the meat does not forbid because it is not considered hot — nevertheless the gravy is considered "not eaten because of its salt." And gravy of meat that forbids that fell on a vessel requires hag'alah, and if it is an earthenware vessel it requires breaking (Shach there and Orach Chaim); and if it fell only on one place in a wooden vessel and the like — one peels its place and that suffices (Orach Chaim and see above seif 4 hagahah 1):}
