That is, etc. The Mechaber holds like Ran whom Beit Yosef ruled, namely that after waiting the measure of salting for a pot it is hot, and not beforehand; and that which is explained in his words siman 69 s.k. 20 that even beforehand it is hot, and somewhat implies there that after waiting the measure of salting it is not hot — it must be different there, for it deals with blood prohibition, and therefore blood and salt are hot at salting (and as Mechaber wrote in hagahah that even where meat is not considered hot, its gravy is considered hot — see there), but not when it waited the measure of salting when the strength of salt ceased because it expelled all blood; but here it deals with meat and cheese prohibition — as long as not salted properly the meat is not hot, but when it waited the measure of salting it is considered hot, for the strength of salt did not cease since it expelled nothing; certainly it deals that meat was already salted and rinsed and now salted again, for otherwise cheese would be forbidden because of blood; and the Mechaber in hagahah compared meat and cheese to blood prohibition — and because the Mechaber followed Terumat HaDeshen siman 159, I must respond to his words as I will explain in s.k. 12; therefore necessity compels bringing Terumat HaDeshen briefly and responding per my humble opinion: in Terumat HaDeshen they asked regarding a brit milah meal where much meat was salted in one vessel and geese in another, after waiting the measure of salting and more they took and mixed all meat with geese, turned everything top to bottom until all touched, and afterward one goose was found treifah and there is no 60 even against one goose; he answered to permit with great loss and mitzvah meal, for Tosafot and Rosh wrote in chapter 25 for one version that after the measure of salting the strength of salt ceased since it expelled all blood; and so Semag (prohibition 137 beginning 41) — reasonable reason; but in Semak, Shach, and Hagahot Ashiri from Avodah Zarah wrote that salted meat and cheese touching each other are forbidden even after the measure of salting; and one may strain to distinguish only the salt itself weakened but the piece itself remains hot forever — if so what is Rosh and Semag's challenge from the dove in brine that if brine is properly salted the dove is forbidden though brine was salted days ago; and one may answer it is different there because salt did not cease and expelled nothing; nevertheless without challenge, those geonim clearly disagree; except Semag itself also wrote like Semak and supporters that below it is forbidden; therefore it appears not to be lenient except with mitzvah meal and great loss; and in short Maharshal chapter 25 siman 68 challenged and resolved his challenge, following him Bach siman 69 — their words are strained as the examiner will see; and in any case no reason to distinguish if salt is not hot why the piece is hot; but per my humble opinion from poskim' implication we do not say the strength of salt ceased except where it expelled blood, for salt's way is to expel blood, not other things; and as Rosh and Tur siman 90 wrote — thus only in that case after waiting the measure of salting the strength of salt ceased since it expelled blood; unlike other things, and therefore Semak and supporters dealing with meat and cheese from basar b'chalav, not blood, and the strength of salt did not cease since it expelled nothing — obviously after the measure of salting it is considered hot; and so Orach Chaim general rule 58 din 32 — kosher meat the salt always in its strength, for it did not expel anything — see there; and this is what Rosh and Semag wrote that in brine the strength of salt did not cease since it expelled nothing — this appears clearly to me; if so the geonim do not disagree, Semag's words are resolved, and Tur and Mechaber's words agreeing; and I proposed this to the Gaon our master and teacher and other great teachers and they discussed and agreed; and therefore it appeared to permit even without great loss and mitzvah meal; but since from other poskim regarding Rashi's incident it implies even regarding blood we do not say the strength of salt ceased after the measure of salting — therefore do not add — not to permit except with great loss and mitzvah meal; and therefore Mechaber wrote in Semak hagahah that Riva says gravy exuded from meat after the measure of salting has no law of hot blood to forbid cheeses where it fell (brought by Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe siman 69) — meaning even after the measure of salting it is considered hot, only it has no law of blood since it waited the measure and all its blood left; and so in Hagahot Shach section 14; and therefore do not add — permit only with great loss and mitzvah meal; and nevertheless within the measure of salting always forbidden even with great loss and mitzvah meal, for all poskim hold within the measure it is hot, and Ran is singular against them; and Rashba in Mishmeret HaBayit (73) refuted this twice — see there.
