And it waited. And that which in siman 69 s.k. 20 it is explained that even beforehand it is hot, and somewhat implies there that after waiting the measure of salting it is not hot — it must be that it is different there, for it deals with blood prohibition, and therefore blood and salt are hot at the time of salting, but not when it waited the measure of salting, when the strength of the salt ceased because it expelled all the blood; but here it deals with meat and cheese prohibition — as long as it was not salted properly the meat is not hot, but when it waited the measure of salting it is considered hot, for the strength of the salt did not cease since it expelled nothing; certainly it deals that the meat was already salted and rinsed as required and now was salted again, for otherwise the cheese would be forbidden because of blood — end of his words.
And some say. Behold Shach challenged this hagahah and his view is that the Mechaber was not precise at all in comparing meat and cheese to blood prohibition and followed Terumat HaDeshen siman 159 — there the case was salting blood prohibition in geese where one was found treifah, etc., and he permitted with great loss and a mitzvah meal; but that case is not comparable here, for there it appeared to permit even without great loss because regarding blood prohibition we say that after waiting the measure of salting the strength of the salt already ceased; but since from other poskim's words regarding Rashi's incident it implies that even regarding blood we do not say the strength of the salt ceased after waiting the measure of salting — if so do not add — not to permit except with great loss and mitzvah meal; unlike here regarding meat and cheese where the salt expelled nothing — it is obvious that even after the measure of salting it is considered hot; and he wrote that he proposed this to the Gaon our master and teacher and other great teachers and they agreed to his words. However within the measure of salting it is forbidden forever (even regarding blood prohibition) and even with great loss and mitzvah meal, for Ran's view that before the measure of salting it is not like hot is singular against all poskim — end of his words (and Peri Chadash wrote one must not be lenient at all even with great loss and mitzvah meal, since Ra'avad, Ramban, Rashba, Ra'ah, R' Yitzchak, and Ran hold we never say the strength of the salt ceased even if it stayed in its salt a full day or more — who will be lenient against them even with great loss — see there).
Upper. Shach wrote — and although in siman 70 s.k. 4 and siman 105 s.k. 11 he ruled to distinguish in salted meat between upper and lower — it must be specifically to forbid all of it, namely in fatty meat there is a difference, but to forbid the measure of peeling such as in lean meat there is no difference, for although the lower prevails, nevertheless peeling is required; however in Beit Yosef this siman it does not appear so — investigate.
To peel. (*) These circled words are not in the old edition. Shach wrote it is not relevant here to say since it is busy expelling it does not absorb, or as in other places that specifically regarding blood we say this and not regarding other prohibitions.
And both. Terumat HaChacham wrote — this is if only the meat was salted, but if only the milk was salted the milk is permitted; and Shach on this wrote it requires study for practical law, since Mordechai and Semag forbid — who will tell us to be lenient; also Maharshal wrote, etc., and it requires study, for in Semag it is forbidden; and in Hagahot Shach that Beit Yosef brought in siman 70 it implies soft milk that is not congealed is not called a clear substance, and so Maharshal.
Completely. Explanation: even if salted, all is permitted (for it is as if they were not salted since they already dried from their salting — Terumat HaChacham and Shach; and Peri Chadash wrote per what is written above that we do not say the strength of the salt ceased — if so when it becomes moist again it revives and absorbs from the salt next to it and one should forbid even if completely dry — and so is primary).
One. Shach explained that if it fell on the entire vessel peeling does not apply, for the vessel would be ruined, or great effort is required; and therefore if there is no loss and one wishes to exert himself it is permitted (for salted gravy is not absorbed more than the measure of peeling); and although in siman 121 s.k. 2 and Orach Chaim siman 451 s.k. 16 Rama wrote in hagahah that wherever hag'alah is required, if one peeled the vessel it does not help — there it deals with something absorbed through hot use and the like that is absorbed in the entire vessel; but salted gravy is not absorbed except the measure of peeling, therefore peeling suffices.
