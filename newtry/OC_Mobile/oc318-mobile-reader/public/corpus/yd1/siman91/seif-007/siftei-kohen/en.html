But hot roast, etc. Know there are three views: Sefer HaTerumah, Sha'arei Dura, Rosh, Tur, Maharil, and other poskim hold that hot roast, even cold, falling into milk, even if eaten because of its salt, requires peeling; and if the meat has cracks or is seasoned with spices, even if live, all is forbidden; and Ra'avad, Rashba, and Ran hold if roast is cold, only rinsing is never required except peeling if hot; and if cracks or seasoned and hot, all forbidden; and from Rashba's words there it appears so is Rashi's view, and so truly appears Rashi's view in chapter Keitzad Tzolin (Pesachim 76) end of 76a — see there; and Maharshal chapter 25 siman 69 did not look except Rashi chapter 25, therefore wrote there is no implication from Rashi; and Avodah Zarah, Hagahot Mordechai in Maharaf's name, Semak section 205 (96b), appear roast cold requires peeling, and if seasoned or cracks and cold roast, all forbidden, but not live; and so Semag — see there prohibitions 137 page 47 side 4 and 138 page 49 side 3; and so Rosh apparently, and Maharam of Rottenburg responsum 218, and so all in siman 106 (beginning page 123); and so appears Orach Chaim general rule 40; and so Maharai, and Maharshal there; and Mechaber in Terumat HaChacham general rule 22 only here in hagahah is lenient with great loss — and it requires study whether to be lenient since many poskim hold so even for live; and furthermore in the Gemara it appears to deal with cold roast, as Tosafot wrote chapter Keitzad Tzolin 75b s.v. 'and if roast,' etc., and Pesachim 96b s.v. 'until it reaches,' etc. — see there; and so Hagahot Maimoniyot chapter 15 from Rambam; and Ba'er Hetev ruled plainly like Mechaber and it is not clear at all.
Even if eaten because of its salt, etc. It is imprecise — he should have taught us even if not salted at all, since it is hot like any hot into cold requiring peeling; and it should read: because since it is eaten because of its salt and was not salted at all, one law for both — he did not go into fine distinction.
Requires peeling. Like any hot into cold requiring peeling; and do not challenge how peeling suffices though milk is soft and the roast that fell seems cooked in it; and one may answer since it fell into milk it is hot into cold, and although roast sinks into milk and milk floats on roast, it is not cold into hot — whatever is in its place prevails and cools the roast that fell; therefore peeling suffices — end of Rashba's words; and he wrote in section 38 page 234a: and per what we rule below siman 105 s.k. 11 that in salting we do not say upper/lower distinction, initially it is not a question; and it is astonishing — per his words the question stands more, for it is as if cooked and one cannot answer it is hot into cold; and certainly Rashba did not come from salting, for it was eaten because of its salt, but from hot roast — as his words make plain.
And likewise baked and cooked. Meaning baked and cooked are considered like roast — one says hot, one says cold.
And some say even if they are cold the law is thus. Because since roasted, baked, or cooked, although cold it is soft and absorbs; and from poskim' words it implies no difference between slightly salted eaten because of salt and never salted — forever in cold roast the law is thus; and so Maharshal concludes in his book there. Written in Hagahot Maimoniyot there: nevertheless milk is permitted and we do not say roast, baked, or cooked expels; and he brings it in Orach Chaim and Terumat HaChacham there; and the law if milk was salted — see s.k. 15.
And some say even if cold, etc. From this language it implies the Mechaber agrees here too regarding hot roast the law is thus — if not seasoned and no cracks, peeling suffices — and so is primary, since Ra'avad, Rashi, Rashba, and Ran hold so regarding hot roast, though Hagahot Maimoniyot and Orach Chaim there hold hot roast does not suffice with peeling — see there; however all this specifically when hot roast fell into milk; but if milk or other prohibition fell on hot roast — its law in siman 92 s.k. 3 and siman 105 — see there.
