Its salt. Shach wrote that even if it was not salted at all, peeling is also required, like the law of hot into cold that requires peeling; and although the roast sinks into the milk and the milk floats on the roast, it is not like cold into hot, for whatever is in its place prevails and cools; therefore peeling suffices.
Baked. Explanation: baked and cooked are considered like roasted — one says hot, one says cold.
Thus. And the reason is that since it was roasted or baked or cooked, although it is cold it is soft and absorbs, and there is no difference between slightly salted that is eaten because of its salt and not salted at all — forever in cold roast the law is thus; nevertheless the milk is permitted and we do not say the roast or baked or cooked expels.
Great loss. Shach wrote it requires study whether to be lenient with great loss, since many poskim forbid even live meat; and Taz wrote that from Rama's words here it implies that with great loss one may be lenient per the first view that is lenient regarding eaten because of its salt — all that is not hot roast; and above in seif 5 Rama wrote we are not expert and should forbid even salting of roast without great loss; and it must be that we do not combine two leniencies for the sake of great loss — namely that salting of roast is not called not eaten because of its salt, and also to rely on the first view here that we do not forbid live meat with cracks except in hot roast; we do not do so thus; however in hot roast that is not seasoned and has no cracks, peeling suffices — and so is primary; however all this when hot roast fell into milk, but if milk or other prohibition fell on hot roast, its law will be explained in siman 92 and siman 105.
