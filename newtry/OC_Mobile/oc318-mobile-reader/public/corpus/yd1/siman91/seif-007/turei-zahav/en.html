And some say even if they are cold. This is Rosh's view, who explained so the incident of the dove that fell into milk in the Gemara; and Maharai wrote in Hagahot Shach, etc. — in Hagahot Ashiri from Avodah Zarah he wrote that live meat has no cracks; and so is our practice that before salting the meat we soak it and there is much blood mixed with water and we are not precise whether there are cracks in the meat that would forbid from blood and water together; and sometimes it soaks a long time and one may distinguish between blood and other prohibitions; and Maharshal chapter 25 siman 69 wrote on this — what appeared to him from Maharai's words regarding roast that even if roast and milk were not salted at all one should forbid, for he did not bring proof to permit live meat except from soaking meat, etc. — it does not appear to me, for cold does not absorb from cold even if seasoned, and the Sages only forbade in the dove that fell into brine that was slightly salted — then forbidden if it has cracks; but if not salted at all, neither meat nor brine — no prohibition at all in cold, even roast; but I fear to disagree with Semak, Semag, Hagahot Maimoniyot, etc., who wrote plainly that the law is so even if neither meat nor milk was salted, etc. — I fear to disagree with them l'chatchila and to permit what they forbid — end of his words; it implies that in live meat it is obviously permitted if neither was salted; and in roast, if one was salted even if eaten because of its salt, it is forbidden even per Maharshal, for at least one was slightly salted; and Rama who wrote that with great loss one may be lenient per the first view lenient regarding eaten because of its salt — all that is not hot roast; and above in seif 5 he wrote we are not expert and should forbid even salting roast without great loss; and it appears to me we do not combine two leniencies for great loss; however in hot roast without seasoning and without cracks, peeling suffices — and so appears to me; however all this when hot roast fell into milk, but if milk or other prohibition fell on hot roast — its law in siman 92 and 105.
