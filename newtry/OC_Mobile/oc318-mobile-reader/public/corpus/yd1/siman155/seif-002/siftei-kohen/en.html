But etc. See in the Derushah which concludes: when it is evident from his words that he intends toward an idol it is forbidden, even if he does not mention the idol's name and even if he does not say there is no remedy except from these — but when there are other waters and trees and he says “take from these which are in such-and-such a place,” he is certainly particular — see there. And the Bach in a later pamphlet elaborated and wrote: even if the gentile does not say explicitly, but we learn so from the implication of his words — there are two ways: (1) when those waters or trees are more available and the gentile says “take from these” — it is forbidden even if he did not say explicitly “take from those of the idol”; (2) when those are not available and he said “take from those of the idol” — also forbidden; and there is no permit except when they are not more available and he also did not say “take from these” — unlike the Shulchan Aruch and unlike the Derushah — end. In my humble opinion the Derushah's view is also so, and so appears the Mechaber who seems to agree.
And there are those who forbid etc. And the consensus of the poskim is like the first view; and so ruled the Or Zarua there.
