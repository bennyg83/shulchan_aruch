Danger. The Mechaber appears to hold that where there is no danger, treatment by a non-Jew is always permitted. The Rosh and Tur rule that where there is neither illness nor danger, one should not be treated by him. Since no professional expertise is involved, if he kills the patient people will not attribute it to lack of skill; they may say the patient's time had arrived or that the healer hated and killed him. He therefore does not fear damaging his professional reputation. Shach.
To rely upon. He fears that the patient may consult others and expose a false prescription. One should not purchase the medicine from him, however, because he may lie in order to sell it or mix poison into it. Beit Yosef.
For payment. Maharshal writes that this distinction is novel because the Gemara speaks without qualification, and intense hatred may cause him to disregard payment where his professional reputation is not at risk. Bach follows Maharshal. Shach disagrees and concludes that Rama's ruling is primary.
A heretic. Meaning a non-Jew, even one publicly recognized as an expert. Rosh.
