Whether it is permitted to receive medical treatment from a non-Jew; it contains 3 se’ifim.
Any injury or illness that involves danger — for which one desecrates Shabbos — one may not receive treatment from a non-Jew who is not an expert for the public (and all who perform bloodletting are considered experts for bloodletting) (Tur and Beit Yosef in the name of Tosafos and Rosh, and in Hagahot Maimoni, perek 9, and R' Yerucham, and Agudah, and Semag), for we are concerned about murder. And even if it is a safek whether he will live or die, one does not receive treatment from him; but if he is certainly going to die, one receives treatment from him, for we are not concerned here for chayei sha’ah. And if he said, “This drug is effective,” or “it is harmful,” one may rely on him — provided that he does not take that drug from him. (And some say that all this is forbidden only when the non-Jew treats for free, but if he treats for payment, in every case it is permitted, for he fears the loss of his fee) (Hagahot Ashiri, perek Ein Ma’amidin, from Or Zarua) (and the practice is to be lenient). But if he comes to heal him with an incantation, it is permitted, provided that he does not know that he mentions the name of elilim; but if he knows that he mentions the name of elilim, it is forbidden even if he knows that he will certainly die. And if he is an apikores, even an unspecified incantation is forbidden, for certainly he mentions the name of elilim. (And it is likewise forbidden to learn an incantation from him) (Hagahot Mordechai, perek Ein Ma’amidin):
