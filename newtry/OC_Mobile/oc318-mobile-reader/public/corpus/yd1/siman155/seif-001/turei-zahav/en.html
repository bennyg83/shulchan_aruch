And all those who let blood, etc. Regarding the Tur's statement, “for example, when a Jew let blood for him,” Beit Yosef writes that it concerns one who is not skilled in this. Darkei Moshe writes that one must say the Tur described the ordinary case. If a skilled practitioner let blood for him, the place of the bloodletting would not normally hurt. The case must therefore concern an unskilled person. Such treatment by a non-Jew is forbidden, and the Tur therefore specified a Jew; this concludes his words.
For we are not concerned for a short extension of life. Tosafos write: Regarding the statement in Yoma that on Shabbos we clear rubble from a person because of even a short extension of life, in both cases we act for his benefit. There, if we do not show concern, he will die. Here, if we do show concern and he is not healed by this treatment, he will certainly die. We therefore abandon the certainty of death and act upon the possibility of recovery.
