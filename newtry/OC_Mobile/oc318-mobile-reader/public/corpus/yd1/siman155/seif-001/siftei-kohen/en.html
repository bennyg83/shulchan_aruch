But, etc., for wages in every matter it is permitted. Maharshal wrote: this is a novelty in Avodah Zarah — the Gemara states plainly, and also "great is hatred," etc. — that he is not concerned for his wages where there is no concern to ruin his reputation; so too is the view of most authors — essence; and after him Beit Chadash followed and wrote: since the Rosh, Ran, and other authors wrote regarding a sick person without danger that one may be healed from him — perhaps he will die — they will attribute the matter to lack of his knowledge and diminish his livelihood; behold it deals with wages, and nevertheless in illness where there is danger or in tzamarurin one may not be healed from him — end. I did not stand on the end of their minds in what they wrote that the Gemara states plainly — on the contrary in the Gemara (26a) it says: what is healing money and what is healing lives? If we say healing money for wages, healing lives for free — Rashi: he does not kill because he is concerned for loss of his wages; behold the Gemara holds that for wages it is permitted; and although the Gemara concludes healing money is his animal, healing lives is his body — because it challenges "let it teach one may be healed from them for wages," meaning the language of healing money and lives does not imply so; nevertheless the law is true that for wages it is permitted — otherwise it should have asked what is different for free and what is different for wages. And what is written "and also great is hatred," etc. — is difficult on the Gemara that it should have divided thus and we do not say "great is hatred"; and more — from where does he say "great is hatred," and are we dealing with a gentile who hates him? Behold it deals with a plain gentile — rather because they are suspected of sorcery it is forbidden; and whoever for wages does not lose his wages — and what they wrote that the authors "will diminish his livelihood" — behold it deals with wages — it does not exist; for then they will attribute the matter to lack of his knowledge and say he is not expert and diminish his livelihood relative to others who will not be healed from him because of lack of his knowledge — know that they wrote regarding tzamarurin and similar where they will not attribute the matter to lack of his knowledge but will say he was his enemy; and if he diminishes his livelihood — that is his wage he takes from this sick person — if so when they say he was his enemy there is no doubt he diminishes his livelihood but certainly as explained; and on the contrary from their words it implies the opposite — immediately it is permitted because of the reason he does not diminish his livelihood relative to others, all the more where he loses his wage relative to this one it is permitted. And all this is clear — and the Rav's words stand properly.
