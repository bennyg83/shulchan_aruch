But for payment it is permitted in every case. Maharshal calls this a novelty and argues that the Gemara speaks without qualification; the Bach follows him and infers from the Rosh and Ran that even a paid non-Jewish doctor is forbidden in dangerous illness or bloodletting. I do not understand their position. The Gemara itself initially explains 'medical treatment of money' as treatment for payment and 'treatment of life' as free treatment, and Rashi explains that for payment the physician will not kill the patient because he fears losing his fee. Although the Gemara ultimately explains the terms differently, that is only because the wording 'money' and 'life' does not fit the proposed distinction; it never rejects the substantive rule that payment supplies a reason for trust. The claim that the non-Jew's hatred is too great is likewise unsupported, for the discussion concerns an ordinary non-Jew, not one known personally to hate the patient. Since non-Jews were suspected of bloodshed, unpaid treatment is dangerous; payment and professional reputation can remove the concern. Accordingly, the Mechaber's ruling has a sound basis.
