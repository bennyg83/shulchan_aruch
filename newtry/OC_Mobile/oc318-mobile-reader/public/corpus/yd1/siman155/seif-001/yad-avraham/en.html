(Siman 155 in Shulchan Aruch seif 3 in the gloss) Permitted to burn a sheretz or other forbidden thing and eat it for healing. Minchat Yaakov klal 46 wrote: he does not know from where that it is forbidden not for healing — for otherwise it is lost for human food, and the words of the Rosh in chapter 2 of Pesachim were hidden from him — chametz that was charred before its time is forbidden for eating although the name of prohibition was not yet upon it, because we say "since he ate it he esteemed it." If so here too regarding a burned sheretz — if not for healing certainly one should not permit it for eating even though it is lost from human food, from that reason that we say "since he ate it he esteemed it." Only when he ate it for healing it is permitted — then it is not relevant to say "since he ate it he esteemed it," for the illness proves on him that he does not eat it for its importance but because of healing; and so is the implication of the Rambam's words in chapter 4 of Hilchot Chametz — bread that molded, etc., he is not obligated to burn — and that is when it molded before its prohibition time as Hagahot Maimoniyot writes. And nevertheless he wrote only that he is not obligated to burn; but for eating it implies forbidden — because we say "since he ate it he esteemed it"; and although for chametz some permit also for eating as the Rosh writes in Pesachim — nevertheless for other prohibitions certainly one must forbid rabbinically; and so in chapter 14 halacha 11 the Rambam ruled: if one ate forbidden food until it spoiled and stank and was lost from human food — he is exempt; it also implies there is nevertheless prohibition; and so the Peri Chadash examined in Orach Chaim siman 442 — except the reason we say "since he ate it he esteemed it." And what Minchat Yaakov challenged from what we rule that things burned are permitted — one can say permitted for benefit specifically; also what he challenged from that we rule a chick of a nevelah is permitted because "from the time it could be accepted, when it spoils" — to Kesef Mishneh: there it changed to a permitted thing and a forbidden thing became mixed.
