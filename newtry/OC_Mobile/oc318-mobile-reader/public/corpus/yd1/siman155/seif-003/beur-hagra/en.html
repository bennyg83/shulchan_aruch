Some say that all, etc. Like the prohibition of Torah not in its usual manner of benefit ; forbidden rabbinically as written above "one is not lashed," etc. ; it implies but rabbinically it is forbidden. Turei Zahav and the Ran question this.
And even stam yeinam, etc. In the Ran chapter "Kol sha'ah" it implies equal to other rabbinic prohibitions; but the Rivash siman 255 wrote: perhaps because of his pressing need he mentioned it; but stam yeinam is in the category of idolatry like asherah wood ; although there is only a plain negative commandment it is forbidden, and so sorcery; and even in rabbinic prohibitions as written in Sanhedrin 75a, and so stam yeinam which they forbade for benefit and made it like actual yayin nesech, as Tosafot 29b s.v. yayin menalan write; and so we say there 62b its prohibition is stringent like yayin nesech, and we resolve its rental is forbidden like yayin nesech; and we say there "perhaps they fertilize with them" ; and although all things burned their ashes are permitted, it is in the category of idolatry whose ashes are forbidden, and even its rental wheat all the more so stam yeinam itself; see there ; except for the first reasoning that holds even for idolatry it is permitted in such a case, whatever he did not say specifically from idolatry and stam yeinam, and all the more so in our time when otherwise they permit for benefit as written in siman 123 in the gloss.
And to make from it, etc. The Rashba forbids as written in siman 123 seif 2 ; we say anointing is like drinking, and all the more bathing; and we do not say it is permitted in rabbinic prohibitions of benefit except benefit but not drinking. And although anointing like drinking is only rabbinic and a mere asmachta in what Tosafot and the Rosh write in the last chapter of Yoma ; otherwise how did they permit anointing on Yom Kippur for the ill in his head, and bathing there for several matters and immersion ; nevertheless bathing is forbidden, for we find they were stringent even in rabbinic prohibition like terumat chutz la'aretz as we say in Niddah 32a, and all the more so in idolatry and stam yeinam prohibition where even rabbinically one should be killed and not transgress as above ; except for the first reasoning in seif 2 one need not deliver as above. There in Rashba ; but the Rav permitted for the aforementioned reason per his view that stam yeinam in our time is permitted for benefit otherwise, and bathing nevertheless is not actual drinking; so he wrote "provided," etc., as above.
(Likkut) Provided that he does not eat, etc. Meaning, this applies to all rabbinic prohibitions. The Ran in chapter 2 of Avodah Zarah writes: Nevertheless, regarding eating we have no permission, for perhaps the Sages treated them like Torah prohibitions and forbade using them for healing when there is no danger (end).
(Likkut) Since there is no danger. Where there is danger it is permitted, not only according to the first view in seif 2, but even according to the latter view. Since ordinary gentile wine is permitted for benefit because contemporary non-Jews are not idolaters, and it is forbidden only because of intermarriage, it is like other prohibitions, which are permitted in danger, and like all rabbinic prohibitions, since it contains no element of idolatry, as above. Therefore Rabbeinu Yitzchak ruled that ordinary gentile wine nowadays is nullified in sixty, unlike prohibitions that are forbidden in any amount because of an idolatry concern, as stated in Avodah Zarah 73a. Although the Rashba disagrees with this, he nevertheless rules that any wine forbidden only for drinking forbids a mixture only in sixty, as stated above, siman 134, seif 2 (end).
All the more so it is permitted, etc. As stated there, 66b. Tosafot there, s.v. “Abaye,” however, write that sprinkling wine for fragrance is certainly forbidden, for it constitutes full benefit. See siman 108, seif 8, in the gloss, which attempts to distinguish between actual idolatrous libation wine and ordinary gentile wine. This does not appear correct, and the Shach already challenges it there, seif katan 25.
Permitted, etc. For all things burned their ashes, etc.; and he wrote except, etc., and even for eating it is permitted; and we also rule that all prohibitions one is not lashed, etc. ; all the more eating live fat, etc.; if so for a sick person where there is danger it is permitted as above; so too the Mordechai in Pesachim there.
(Likkut) Except for idolatry wood. Not like Mordechai chapter 2 of Pesachim who wrote that also in kilaei hakerem forbidden because he holds ashes are permitted because it is not in the usual manner of benefit ; therefore he also wrote that all prohibitions not in the usual manner of benefit are permitted ab initio (and see above siman 142 s.k. 28 what is written there, and it appears the Mordechai learned from this) ; but Tosafot in Avodah Zarah 12b and the Rosh in Pesachim there wrote there is prohibition, and so the Ramban learns from there that in rabbinic prohibitions even in the usual manner of benefit it is permitted for healing ; for Torah prohibitions not in the usual manner of benefit there is nevertheless prohibition; so too the Ran and Rivash. And in Tosafot in Avodah Zarah there they were uncertain regarding idolatry whether one is lashed not in the usual manner of benefit ; behold it does not depend on ashes; however even if you say for idolatry one is only lashed in the usual manner of benefit, nevertheless idolatry for healing is forbidden here as the Ran and Rivash siman 255 write ; in idolatry even rabbinic prohibition is forbidden for healing even in danger, as written in the chapter "Ben sorer u'moreh" they forbade there in danger even with an unmarried woman [therefore he wrote except, etc., and did not also write all buried things whose ashes are forbidden because not in the usual manner of benefit is permitted for healing] ; and in other prohibitions both are permitted in one matter: ashes are permitted, and moreover because not in the usual manner of benefit; and according to the first reason even for a healthy person it is permitted, and so it implies permitted ab initio; and one might say specifically for benefit and not for eating ; what is different that both are from the Torah (end).
(Likkut) Permitted to burn a sheretz or, etc. Not precise ; likewise with meat and milk that is forbidden, which is among buried things whose ashes are forbidden as written at the end of Temurah; granted other buried things nevertheless one is not lashed for them not in the usual manner of benefit and it is permitted for healing as written in chapter 2 of Pesachim and above in Shulchan Aruch ; not so with meat and milk as above in Shulchan Aruch. Except for idolatry wood. Not because one is lashed for it not in its usual manner of benefit ; in Tosafot in Avodah Zarah they were uncertain, and see above siman 142 seif 10 dispute on this ; rather because of the seed of idolatry prohibition, even something rabbinic is forbidden (end).
Except for idolatry wood. This is not because one is lashed for benefiting from it in an unusual manner, for Tosafos in Avodah Zarah are uncertain about that, and see above, siman 142, seif 10, where there is a dispute on this point. Rather, because anything connected to idolatry is forbidden even where the prohibition is only d'rabbanan. End.
And every sick person, etc. As stated in the final chapter of Yoma, in the Mishnah on 82a, according to medical experts; likewise, where the treatment is established, as stated there on 83a and 84a regarding one bitten by a rabid dog, etc. This proves the point.
And we do not permit, etc. For we rule Shabbat is overridden for pikuach nefesh and was not permitted as the Rambam writes in chapter 2 of Shabbat and as written in Menachot 64a: Rava asked ; a sick person, etc. ; obvious, etc.; and therefore there is no difficulty in Yoma 84b "no doubt this Shabbat," etc. ; one who says, etc. ; because it is dangerous in delaying it; so he wrote: since there is no danger in the matter ; meaning in delay, although he is a sick person where there is danger; so too in every prohibition as written chapter 83a "we feed him the lighter," etc.; and there R. said: where possible with non-sacred, etc. ; possible with non-sacred, obvious, no need, etc. [And at the end of chapter 2 of Pesachim: a person should not chew wheat, etc. ; although it is not in its usual manner of benefit and is permitted as the Rambam writes in chapter 5 of Hilchot Yesodei HaTorah in the second version, etc. ; but not in the usual manner of benefit such as making a poultice or a sip from vinegar, etc. ; except the mishnah is because he can soften the wheat in fruit juice and similar; rather chewing because it is closer, not like Magen Avraham siman 466.]
