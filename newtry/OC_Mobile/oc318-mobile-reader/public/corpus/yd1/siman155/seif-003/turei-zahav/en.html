Or from a particular tree, etc. The Tur writes, “Specifically when he says that there is no cure for him,” etc. His words appear contradictory, because at the end he forbids the treatment whenever the person merely mentions the name of an idol. It appears that merely mentioning the name of an idol certainly creates no prohibition, because he uses it only as a landmark to identify where the water can be found. The prohibition applies only when this is combined with his statement that there is no cure for the patient, etc. At the end, the Tur teaches that one might have thought that since the water is found only at the idolatrous site and the healer says, “You have no cure,” etc., the treatment is forbidden as though he had explicitly mentioned the idol, because the water exists only there. He therefore teaches that this is not so. This appears correct in my humble opinion. I have seen explanations that force an elephant through the eye of a needle to explain the Tur's words, but what I have written appears correct.
