And specifically. See Beer HaGolah and Semag. This rule is not universally accepted, because most poskim do not rule like R. Eliezer ben Yaakov, who is an individual opinion in this area, as is clear in many places. Here, however, even the first Tanna agrees, because the act remains within his power. Kiddushin records R. Eliezer asking R. Yochanan about a similar declaration, and R. Yochanan answers broadly. Tosafot, Nazir 12a, likewise distinguish a case where the matter is in one's power. Rabbenu Tam explains that a person can appoint an agent for something he can presently bring about, though that reason alone is not always enough. Here he can already separate from fully kneaded dough, whereas a yevamah cannot be divorced without first undergoing yibbum. Although Tosafot's language could imply that one may separate only from another already kneaded dough and not from flour itself, that is not correct. The Gemara's case of produce in a garden uses the language of the particular example, but the same principle applies to other attached produce or to the produce itself. See seif 2. (Likkut) And specifically. This follows R. Eliezer ben Yaakov in Kiddushin and Beer HaGolah, according to Rambam and some commentators who rule like him. See Even HaEzer at the end of siman 40. But this authority is not actually needed, because in this kind of case even the Sages agree, as the Gemara itself states. It is possible those authorities understood the opening clause also to follow R. Eliezer ben Yaakov and therefore ruled like him because R. Yochanan agrees, even though elsewhere R. Yochanan holds one cannot acquire something not yet in existence. In this case, as Rabbah, Rav Yosef, and R. Chanina explain, it is not treated as entirely nonexistent because the matter remains within one's power.
