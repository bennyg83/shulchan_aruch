Rather, even the Sages (Rabbanan) concede in this case, as stated in Kiddushin there: "R. Elazar asked R. Yochanan, he said, etc. He replied: all, etc." — as R. Yochanan holds, etc. And the same applies here — since it is within his power to knead, and likewise it is in Tosafot to Nazir 12a s.v. "be'isha": "and it answers: since it is within her power, etc., as we say, etc." — and in any case with this reasoning alone, etc. The meaning is: the reasoning of "within his power to knead" — that he can appoint an agent — is not the only reasoning. Rather, the reasoning is also: since he can now separate from a kneaded dough. This is unlike the case of a yevamah [levirate widow], where it is impossible to release her without intercourse by any remedy. Even though from Tosafot's words it is implied that specifically from a kneaded dough may she separate from her own or another's, but not from the grain itself — as stated there: "produce of a garden bed, etc." — this is not so, for it also says there "likewise the produce of this garden bed, still attached, etc." — and it is not specifically stated "on behalf of this detached garden bed," for the same applies for another attached [garden] or for the grain itself, from the fact that the Gemara raises an objection: "one who says, etc." — "he raises an objection, etc." — "but rather, etc." — it simply adopted the language of the Mishnah, and similarly Tosafot adopted the language of the Gemara.
