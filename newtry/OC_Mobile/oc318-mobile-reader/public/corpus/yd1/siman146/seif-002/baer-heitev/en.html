Nullification. Taz writes that Beit Yosef, in the name of Semag, explains the reason as a decree lest one confuse it with idolatry belonging to a Jew. Since this is only a decree, Taz understands that it applies specifically when the object came into the Jew's possession after acquisition. If it never entered his possession, but he purchased it and paid the money, although payment legally acquires from a non-Jew, the decree concerning nullification was not imposed. Bach disagrees and writes that even where the Jew did not acquire it, such as finding it before the owner's despair or stealing it, it rabbinically has no nullification, as a decree because of idolatry belonging to a Jew.
