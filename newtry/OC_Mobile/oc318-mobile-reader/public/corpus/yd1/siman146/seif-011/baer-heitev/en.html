Permit. Bach writes that this applies only to sale. A pledge never permits, even when given to a Jewish goldsmith. Taz writes that a pledge can permit where it was given as collateral, the due date arrived, and the non-Jew did not redeem it, so it effectively became a sale. Rama's reference to a pledge means specifically such a case. An ordinary pledge does not nullify. Shach agrees and notes that Rama should have clarified which type of pledge permits.
