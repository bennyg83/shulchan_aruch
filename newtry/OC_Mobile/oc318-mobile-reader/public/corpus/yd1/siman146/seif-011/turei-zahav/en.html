Since he nullified one limb, etc. Beit Yosef explained the Rambam's intent — Marya dehai pesika — who challenged him from what we hold in the dispute of Rav and Shmuel at the beginning of chapter All Images, whom Beit Yosef brings regarding an idol that broke by itself — that an ordinary person cannot restore it, and it is nullified for all; and above chapter 25 Rav and R. Yochanan dispute and we hold like R. Yochanan stringently; therefore the Rambam resolved: Rav and Shmuel do not dispute where an ordinary person cannot restore it and it is permitted — they do not speak without nullification; rather in any case nullification of one of them is required, namely we consider them as if still in their completeness since it broke by itself; and if a gentile nullified even one of its limbs — so too it helps now; but where an ordinary person can restore it — each piece is by itself an idol; therefore each piece must be nullified by itself; but in the Tur he wrote here that if an ordinary person cannot restore it — it is nullified; and on this it is certainly difficult — behold we hold like R. Yochanan that what broke by itself is not nullified; and it appears the Tur also holds like the resolution Beit Yosef brought in the name of Ramban and Ran that there is a distinction whether the idol's essence remains or not; therefore above in the dispute of Rav and R. Yochanan it deals where the essence remains, only a little broke from it for nullification if the gentile did so — we hold it is not nullified since it broke by itself; and in this it is not relevant to say at all whether an ordinary person can restore it or not, for the gentile does not seek to restore it since its essence remains and he worships it as at first; unlike in the dispute of Rav and Shmuel it deals where all of it broke — and this is explained in what they said "an ordinary person can restore it" — evidently its worship is void as long as it is not restored; in this the Tur properly ruled where it cannot be restored — it is nullified even if it broke by itself, for certainly the gentile nullified it after he saw all of it broke; and one can say: why did the Tur not write the law where its essence remains — one can say we learn from what the Tur wrote where an ordinary person cannot restore it — it is nullified when it broke by itself; automatically we know where restoration is not needed, namely where its essence remains — it is forbidden when it broke by itself, as above; the Tur's view is correct:
