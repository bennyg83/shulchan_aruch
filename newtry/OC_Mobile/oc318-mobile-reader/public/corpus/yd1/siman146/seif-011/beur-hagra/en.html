Idolatry, etc. As stated there on daf 49b, according to everyone, etc., and in accordance with R' Yochanan on daf 41b.
Therefore, etc. Unlike Shmuel there; and as we said siman 141 62:
And if it was, etc. This follows Rav there, 49b, according to the latter answer. There are several variant readings in that passage; see the Ran and the various explanations given so that it not conflict with our ruling like Rabbi Yochanan that broken pieces are forbidden. According to the Rambam's reading, however, they disagree here regarding other fragments, meaning whether nullifying one fragment nullifies all of them.
(Likkut) And if it was, etc. This follows the Rambam's reading. According to Rashi's reading, however, the distinction concerns fragments of fragments. Ordinary fragments are always forbidden, while fragments of fragments are permitted when an ordinary craftsman cannot restore them. The Raavad rules this way in his glosses, and the Ran likewise writes so. The Ran records another reading: alternatively, all agree that fragments are not worshipped, and here, etc. Although we rule that idolatry which broke by itself remains forbidden, there, since the principal idol remains intact, its fragments are not worshipped. The distinction regarding fragments is whether an ordinary craftsman can restore them, while the principal idol remains intact. See the Ran at the beginning of chapter 3 at length (end).
