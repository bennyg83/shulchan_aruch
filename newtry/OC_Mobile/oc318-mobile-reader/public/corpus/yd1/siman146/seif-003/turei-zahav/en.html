One who buys scrap metal, etc. There is a place in the Tur that needs explanation, and we will explain it with Heaven's help. In a baraita it is stated: if before he gave money he pulled — he returns; and if after he gave money he pulled — he takes it to the Dead Sea; and he wants to prove from this in the Gemara that pulling with a gentile does not acquire; and Rava rejected it — in truth it is a mistaken transaction; rather the first part where he gave money it appears as an idol of Israel; the latter part where he did not give money — not so. And the Tur wrote: if he pulled before he gave money he returns to him, for it was not yet acquired; and if after he gave money he pulled — it was already acquired for him and it is an idol of Israel and has no nullification and must be taken to the Dead Sea; and so the Rambam, etc. And it is wondrous — for we conclude in the Gemara that in every case there is no acquisition from mistaken transaction, only the distinction is because it appears acquired or not. And Mahari Chalav wrote that what the Tur wrote "it was already acquired for him" is according to the viewers' opinion who will think he acquired — for everyone holds it was acquired for him; and it does not settle at all that in truth there is acquisition for a gentile, whether by pulling according to R. Tam or by money according to Rashi, as the Tur wrote below siman 322 — only to satisfy both he wrote there that one should do both pulling and money regarding firstborn; and if so it is impossible to say thus in the Tur's words, which are according to the tanna of the latter baraita who required both here; for if so the baraita is not like Rashi nor like R. Tam; and since the law is that one suffices — why concern ourselves with viewers only when there are both and not with one of them, that they too will say it was acquired for Israel since the Tur tied the prohibition to a place where there is acquisition; besides that the language does not imply so in the Tur; and in Perishah he wrote the Tur truly intended as written in siman 322 and therefore required both — and already rejected because of what we wrote that the Tur's words are the baraita's words; and further he was not precise at all, for necessarily he required both only to be stringent, such as to exempt from firstborn; but not to be lenient; and if so here it would be a leniency from this that he could return if there is not acquisition of both — and this is obviously a void matter; and on the contrary the Tur is stringent when there is one of them, namely in siman 132 — see there because of the reason that pulling acquires regarding measuring for a gentile's vessel and money acquires regarding giving a dinar to a shopkeeper; and how can he say here to be lenient until there are both — rather it is simple this is not so. And what appears to me in the Tur's words: after I was precise further — why did he need to write here "and it is an idol of Israel and has no nullification and must be taken to the Dead Sea" — do we not yet know the law of an idol of Israel until here? In the end it appears to me: since we saw according to the conclusion in the Gemara that the matter depends on money — it is difficult apparently why I need pulling in the baraita, since pulling does nothing, it being a mistake only because of viewers; if so let us also be concerned with money alone, for according to Rashi money acquires in general, and let us be concerned here too because of viewers who will not know of his mistake; and therefore it was possible to say that in truth the essence depends on money; and that which the tanna took "if after he gave him money he pulled" — this pulling is not literal, but rather meaning he pulled and found the idol for this one it was guarded; and he wrote it is not so, but the essence is that whenever if he had known there was an idol in the scrap metal he would have had acquisition by law — then we decree when there is a mistake because of viewers who will think he knew of them; but if by law he knew — we do not decree because of viewers when he did not know; and therefore if he pulled alone — then even if he knew there is no acquisition on the idol, for certainly when he pulled all the scrap metal he intended only that when they are in his hand he will clarify for himself what is permitted and return what is forbidden — permitted he wants to acquire, forbidden he does not want to acquire, as we say in chapter HaSho'el; but when he already gave money for all the weight of the scrap metal — it is impossible to say so, for certainly if he had known there was an idol inside he would certainly have acquired it; and nevertheless when he erred at the time of purchase they were concerned for marit ayin and were stringent in this specifically when there are both. And one can say: why do I need pulling — behold with money alone it also acquires in a known case; and if so let us be concerned in error because of viewers. One can say: even though it is known and he acquired the idol through his money — nevertheless since it did not come to his hand he need not take it to the Dead Sea, but nullification of the gentile suffices, as we proved nearby that specifically "it came to his hand" we require; therefore the Tur needed to write here that it must be taken to the Dead Sea to teach what I wrote; and the measure of his words: one who buys scrap metal and found an idol in error — if before he gave money he pulled he returns and need not be concerned for viewers since the purchase was not yet acquired even if he had known there was an idol there; but if he did both he does not return, since if he had known it was already acquired; and if you say: why do I need pulling for this — answer: it is an idol of Israel and must be taken, etc., since it came to his hand — therefore they were concerned also in error; and one can say: why did Rava need to establish the baraita truly that there is mistaken transaction and because of viewers, and not establish it when he knew and requires both, as we wrote — for the baraita says "and found an idol," implying at the time of purchase he did not know; and therefore it should have been permitted in every case since he did not know of the idol — why is it forbidden when there are both — rather necessarily because of viewers and according to the distinction we mentioned. And according to our words it is also settled what the Tur and Rambam wrote; and Beit Yosef challenged: behold the baraita is arranged; and according to what I wrote it is settled — it teaches us we require both and money alone does not suffice, as I wrote above; and everything is settled with Heaven's help:
