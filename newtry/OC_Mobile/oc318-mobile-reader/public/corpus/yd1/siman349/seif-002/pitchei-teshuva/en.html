When they are tied. See Baer Heitev and Responsa Panim Meirot III, siman 33, by my ancestor. A man died with an artificial tooth in his mouth made from the bone of a non-kosher animal, and the question arose whether it should be removed or buried with him. One scholar wished to rule that it should be removed and burned like an ornament of the deceased, and not buried with him, for several reasons. Panim Meirot rejects his arguments and writes that it need not be burned. It should simply be left as it is and buried with him; see there.
Therefore it is permitted to remove rings. See Bechor Shor, novellae to Avodah Zarah 29b, who infers from this that if an ornament of gold, pearls, or the like is tied to the deceased, benefit from it is prohibited, and according to Bach, cited in Baer Heitev seif katan 1, even if it is braided into the hair. At first glance even rings should be prohibited if fastened tightly, for Rosh at the beginning of Chullin writes that the "binding" stated regarding tefillin means securing them firmly upon the arm. Yet the entire ruling is very difficult. At the end of the first chapter of Arachin we find prohibition of adornments of the deceased only for items resembling part of the body, such as a wig or similar item, namely an artificial tooth. No source prohibits other ornaments. Furthermore, gold and silver jewelry cannot be more stringent than the deceased's clothing, which plainly does not become prohibited even if attached to him at death, and no one has ever been heard to prohibit it. He discusses the matter at length and concludes that since Rambam and Semag permit even the deceased's own hair, and Tosafot Bava Kamma 10a, s.v. shehashor, appears to agree, while Rashba and Ramban prohibit only the deceased's own hair and not a wig, one may at least rely upon the lenient authorities regarding jewelry of silver, gold, pearls, and the like, which are not analogous to part of the body. Even if they were tied to the deceased or braided into the hair at death, they may be permitted, so that Jewish property not be needlessly destroyed; see there.
