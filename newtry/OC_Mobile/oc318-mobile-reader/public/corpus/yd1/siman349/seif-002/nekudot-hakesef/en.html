(There seif 2) But his actual hair, etc. The Rambam and the Semag ruled that his hair is permitted for benefit. Although the Beit Yosef and Kesef Mishneh challenged them, and Responsa Rashba siman 330 elaborates in rejecting the Rambam’s words, nevertheless one who examines carefully the sugya at the end of the first chapter of Arakhin will see that the law is with the Rambam. What is written in Rashi’s commentary there, “and the halachah is that anything attached to her at the time of the final judgment is forbidden with her, etc.,” is a scribal error; it should read: “therefore anything attached to it and standing with it,” and it refers specifically to an animal. See there and examine carefully.
