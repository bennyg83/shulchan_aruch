(Siman 349 in Shulchan Aruch seif 1) Whether a gentile. So he wrote in Bedek HaBayit in the name of Responsa Rashba, and it is in siman 365. See Tosafot to Bava Kamma 10a, end of s.v. shehashor, which implies that a dead gentile is permitted for benefit. So too it is implied from what the Maggid Mishneh writes in chapter 2 of Hilkhot Maachalot Assurot in the name of the Ramban, that the flesh of a Jew who died is forbidden for benefit; end quote. This implies that a dead gentile is permitted for benefit; see there.
