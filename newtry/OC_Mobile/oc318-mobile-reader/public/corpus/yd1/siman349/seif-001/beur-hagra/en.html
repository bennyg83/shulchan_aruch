(Likkut) Between a gentile, etc. But Rashba in Bava Kamma wrote definitively that a deceased gentile is permitted for benefit — and so too Tosafot there 10a s.v. shehashor, and Rashba writes there that this refers to an uncircumcised slave; see there. And so too in the chapter of HaParah (54a) and he refutes the first answer of Tosafot. One can say: "and the dead animal shall be," etc. — is indeed entirely derived from "and the dead animal" — as written there 54a. But rather the verse comes to exclude a gentile and uncircumcised [slave], since the prohibition of benefit from the dead we derive from Miriam, and only Israelites like her. End quote there.

See Mishneh LaMelekh end of Laws of Mourning, who elaborated and wrote in the name of the Yerushalmi that it is permitted.
