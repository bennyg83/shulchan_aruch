(Likkut) Whether a gentile. Rashba in Bava Kamma states plainly that the corpse of a gentile is permitted in benefit, and Tosafot there likewise write this. Rashba explains that the case discussed elsewhere is an uncircumcised slave. In chapter HaParah he rejects the first Tosafot answer that would derive a broad prohibition from "the dead shall be his." The verse can be explained differently, while the prohibition of benefit from a corpse is derived from Miriam and applies specifically to Jews like her. (Likkut) Whether a gentile. Bedek HaBayit cites Rashba otherwise, but the Yerushalmi in Shabbos explicitly says it is permitted. The Yerushalmi discusses carrying out a corpse on a bed and distinguishes a gentile corpse without offensive odor that is being taken out for a dog. This shows the corpse itself is permitted in benefit. Rashba and Ran on Shabbos 94b likewise explain that according to R. Shimon one can have a melachah needed for its own purpose when removing a gentile corpse for a dog. Ramban in Torat HaAdam writes the same. Tosafot, Ramban, Rashba, and Ran all therefore permit benefit from a gentile corpse, and the Yerushalmi supports them. This also appears from Sanhedrin 19b. See Kesef Mishneh, Hilchot Arachin chapter 5, halachah 17. (Likkut) Whether a gentile. Bedek HaBayit cites Responsa Rashba, siman 365, but it is not clear from that responsum that Rashba considered it prohibited. In his chiddushim he writes explicitly in two places that it is permitted. Mishneh LaMelech at the end of Hilchot Avel discusses the issue at length and cites the Yerushalmi as proof for permission. (Likkut) Whether a gentile. Tosafot and Rashba do not prohibit it. Mishneh LaMelech and later authorities also bring proof from David's use of Philistine foreskins for kiddushin, demonstrating they were not prohibited in benefit, and from Sanhedrin 19b, which says they were fit for dogs and cats.
