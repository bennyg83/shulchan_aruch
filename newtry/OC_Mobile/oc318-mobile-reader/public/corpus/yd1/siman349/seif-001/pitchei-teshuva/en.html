Whether a gentile. See Baer Heitev of Maharit and Responsa Even Shoham, siman 30. He concludes that the corpse of a Jew is prohibited in benefit by Torah law, while the corpse of a gentile is prohibited only rabbinically. Therefore, where a person has a non-life-threatening illness and physicians prescribe a bone from a corpse or material from burial shrouds for treatment, one may use a bone from a gentile corpse because the prohibition is rabbinic. See also Tzemach Tzedek, siman 13 and its gloss; Yad Eliyahu, siman 66; Pri Tevuah, siman 25; Birkei Yosef; She'ilat Yaavetz, part 1, siman 41; Teshuvah MeAhavah, part 1, siman 47; and the responsum of R. Baruch, author of Ta'am HaMelech.
In benefit. See Baer Heitev of Maharit regarding the statement that one may nevertheless consume mummia. See Mishneh LaMelech, Hilchot Ma'achalot Assurot chapter 8, halachah 18; Hilchot Avel chapter 3, halachah 1; and chapter 14, halachah 21. Regarding Bach's statement concerning a garment of the deceased, see Responsa Yad Eliyahu, siman 50.
