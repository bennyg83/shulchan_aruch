Whether a non-Jew. See Baer Heitev of Rabbi Maharit. Responsa Even Shoham, siman 30, concludes that deriving benefit from the corpse of a Jew is prohibited by Torah law, while benefit from the corpse of a non-Jew is prohibited rabbinically. For this reason, regarding a person with a non-dangerous illness whose physicians said that he needed a bone from a corpse, or material from a corpse's shrouds, for a certain treatment, he permits taking a bone from a non-Jewish corpse, since the prohibition is only rabbinic. See also Responsa Tzemach Tzedek, siman 13 and the gloss there; Yad Eliyahu, siman 66; Pri Tevuah, siman 25; Birkei Yosef of the Chida; She'elat Yaavetz I, siman 41; Responsa Teshuvah MeAhavah I, siman 47; and the responsum of Rabbi Baruch, author of Taam HaMelech.
For benefit. See Baer Heitev of Rabbi Maharit. Regarding its statement that nevertheless it is permitted to consume mummy medicine, see Mishneh LaMelech, Hilchot Maachalot Assurot 8:18; Hilchot Avel 3:1 and 14:21. Regarding what it cites from Bach about a garment of the deceased, see Responsa Yad Eliyahu, siman 50.
