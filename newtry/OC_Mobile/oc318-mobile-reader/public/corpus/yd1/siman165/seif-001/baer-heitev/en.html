The currency. Meaning he lent in a manner obligating repayment in the currency currently circulating.
He deducts. Where deduction is required but was not made, some authorities classify the excess as fixed interest and others as avak ribbit.
The increase. Even if produce became cheaper because abundant crops entered the market, he does not deduct the increase. Gemara.
One fifth. If melted into bullion, one fifth is lost through smelting and the goldsmith's fee. Taz explains that this leaves no real gain. Levush offers another explanation based upon heavier old coins or presumed intent.
All. Deducting only up to one fifth does not suffice. Once deduction is required, the entire excess is deducted. Taz.
To the lender. This applies whether the obligation arose through a loan or commerce. There is no distinction between a change in weight and a change in metal composition. If purchasing power for produce and other goods did not change, no deduction is made up to one fifth.
Craftsmen. Two craftsmen are asked separately, each speaking innocently, as is done regarding Persian documents. See Choshen Mishpat 68:2.
In Choshen Mishpat. Although Rama there cites dissenting views, all agree that currency is governed by dina de-malkhuta, like taxes and levies, because nothing is more directly a royal matter. See Choshen Mishpat 369.
