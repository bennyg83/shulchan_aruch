The lender etc. on the coin. Meaning he lends him in such a manner that he is obligated to give him the current coin, in the manner explained in Choshen Mishpat end of siman 74 — see there; and Be’er Heitev; and see in responsum R. Moshe Alshekar siman 115 at the end.
He deducts for him etc. And in every case where he must deduct for him and did not deduct — Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Be'er Heitev that some say it is fixed interest and some say it is dust of interest.
And if they did not decrease on account of the addition. Even if they decreased on account of the market price — such as when most crops came to the world and they decreased — he does not deduct for him — Gemara.
Up to its fifth. For if he were to return it as bullion, the fifth would be deducted for the loss in smelting and the goldsmith's wages; or alternatively: even in the original coin there are coins that weigh more by up to a fifth; or alternatively: presumably the borrower's intent also was that if the coin changes he will not deduct at all up to this measure.
And the same applies to lending on coin etc. Meaning the lender in a matter where he is obligated to pay him the current coin and the coins have decreased — then the borrower must pay him in the manner explained.
On account of the new coin. On account of its value he gives him the new coin.

And if he decreed, etc. This is the Rav's wording in Choshen Mishpat end of siman 74: if the king enacted how debtors must pay, dina de-malkhuta dina, and according to what he enacted he shall pay; and there are those who disagree, see Choshen Mishpat siman 369 regarding dina de-malkhuta dina, end quote. In Sema there, the view of those who disagree is marked as responsa Rosh kelal 103 simanim 1 and 2, and Rivash siman 196, and so it is in Darkei Moshe there. This requires study, for it is not found so in responsa Rosh there, and in Rivash there the opposite is explicit: although if the king had not commanded explicitly about repayment of debts, by law one would pay according to the law as explained, nevertheless since the king explicitly commanded regarding repayment of debts that the new coin be accepted at the earlier value, and he did not say this only about one private person but that it should apply in all lands of his kingdom, this is dina de-malkhuta, end quote. Perhaps for this reason the Rav did not bring here the dissenting view, or because he wrote in Choshen Mishpat siman 369 seif 8 that the primary view is like those who say dina de-malkhuta applies in every matter; if so, regarding currency we hold dina de-malkhuta dina, and so is primary in law. Furthermore, it is clear to me that even those who hold dina de-malkhuta applies only to taxes and levies dependent on land concede that currency matters have the law of taxes and levies. So it is explicit in Baal HaTerumot, sha'ar 46 part 1, after he explained these laws in Tur and Mechaber of this siman: if the king decreed on his kingdom that every lender to his fellow shall be repaid from the second coin that he now made, even though they reduced its silver, or that he shall give from the second even though they added to it greatly, in this certainly there is one who says dina de-malkhuta dina. I received from my teachers, who received from the French sages, that dina de-malkhuta dina was said only in matters that are the king's business, such as roads, customs, and taxes, etc. I asked Ramban z"l about this, and this is what he wrote me: from your words I learned that you rely on some sages of the previous generation who ruled that dina de-malkhuta dina applies only to the king's affairs for his needs, such as bridges and land subject to tax. In truth this reasoning is transmitted to us from some of our teachers, but even so I do not agree with your words regarding currency matters, for there is no greater royal matter than this: they are masters of the coin, they make it and issue it in their land; by the elevation of currency their loss is maintained, for all their profit and all their right is currency. This law is entirely like the law of land that pays tax, where the king's decree applies to it. But we have already considered this reasoning and seen that it breaks and is confused in the Talmud, etc., end quote. He further wrote there at the end of the sha'ar that the law of the kingdom in this is law, and its hefker is hefker. All this is clear. I needed to elaborate only because I saw Maharshal, Perek HaGozel Etzim, end of siman 13, write that Mordechai wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Tam and Rashbam how they judged dina de-malkhuta regarding invalidated currency; since this was not mentioned in Tosafot or any author, it shows those matters were nullified, for it was only a fixed custom in their days, but nowadays there is only law, end quote. Bach cites him in Choshen Mishpat end of siman 74 and writes that the Rav in the Shulchan Aruch gloss also wrote that some disagree, and that in a dispute of authorities the burden of proof is on the claimant. I wonder at them, for the law Mordechai wrote is agreed to by Ramban, Baal HaTerumot, and Rivash, and so Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Hagahot Maimoniyot, chapter 4 of Hilchot Malveh; so too responsum Mahari"l siman 112, Rabbeinu Yerucham, Teshuvot Maimoniyot, and Nimukei Yosef write. Beit Yosef also brings below and in Choshen Mishpat the poskim who hold dina de-malkhuta applies in all matters. The other poskim did not write this specific law because once they wrote in many places that we hold dina de-malkhuta dina in all matters, it is obvious that the same applies here; and also because they hold currency is like land, as Baal HaTerumot wrote. What Bach wrote about the Rav's view has already been explained not to be the Rav's view in halachah.
