The lender etc. on the coin. Meaning he lends him in such a manner that he is obligated to give him the current coin, in the manner explained in Choshen Mishpat end of siman 74 — see there; and Be’er Heitev; and see in responsum R. Moshe Alshekar siman 115 at the end.
He deducts for him etc. And in every case where he must deduct for him and did not deduct — Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Be'er Heitev that some say it is fixed interest and some say it is dust of interest.
And if they did not decrease on account of the addition. Even if they decreased on account of the market price — such as when most crops came to the world and they decreased — he does not deduct for him — Gemara.
Up to its fifth. For if he were to return it as bullion, the fifth would be deducted for the loss in smelting and the goldsmith's wages; or alternatively: even in the original coin there are coins that weigh more by up to a fifth; or alternatively: presumably the borrower's intent also was that if the coin changes he will not deduct at all up to this measure.
And the same applies to lending on coin etc. Meaning the lender in a matter where he is obligated to pay him the current coin and the coins have decreased — then the borrower must pay him in the manner explained.
On account of the new coin. On account of its value he gives him the new coin.
