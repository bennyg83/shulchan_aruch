He is permitted in date honey, etc. The Bach wrote: one must examine why it did not teach that if he said “these dates” or “that I taste dates,” he is forbidden in what emerges from them, as it taught regarding olives and grapes, and as above at the end of siman 216. Likewise regarding vegetables, above seif 10, it should have taught that he is forbidden even in field vegetables and gourds, just as it taught that if he said “that I taste pickled foods,” he is forbidden in all pickled foods; and regarding roasted, boiled, and salted, it taught that when he says “that I taste,” he is forbidden in all roasted, boiled, and salted items, as in the Tur; see there. Why here did it not teach so? End quote. It appears that there is no difficulty at all, for is the Tanna a peddler, to count and go on? But in truth, in all of these cases the law is so.
And furthermore, that what is called date honey, etc. But with whey, when they call it “whey of milk,” they call it so because the character of milk is still upon it, and not to distinguish this from another item that is not called whey except this. There.
