He is permitted date honey, etc. The Bach writes that this requires consideration. Why did the Mishnah not teach that if he said, “These dates,” or “Dates that I taste,” he is forbidden what emerges from them, just as it teaches regarding olives and grapes and as stated above at the end of siman 216? Likewise, regarding vegetables above in seif 10, it should have taught that he is forbidden even field vegetables and squash, just as it teaches that one who says, “Pickled food that I taste,” is forbidden all pickled foods. Similarly, regarding roasted, boiled, and salted foods, it teaches that when he says, “that I taste,” he is forbidden every roasted, boiled, or salted food, as stated in the Tur. Why was this not taught here? End of his words. It appears that there is no difficulty, for the Tanna was not required to enumerate every case like a merchant listing his wares. In truth, however, the same law applies to all of them.
