Whey. Rashi, the Rosh, and the Ran explain that this means the liquid separated from milk. See above, siman 81, seif 5, and siman 87, seif 8.
And some say if they call, etc. at first glance it implies: if they call whey "whey of milk," whether he vowed from milk or from whey he is forbidden in both, as Ran wrote; and so noted in Shulchan Aruch Beis Yosef in the name of Ran. But it is difficult: since Beis Yosef rejected Ran's words with good reason, how did the Rav also rule thus; and also the view of Tur and Rashi in the Mishnah and the other poskim appears explicitly not like Ran; and Bach ruled likewise; and so Perishah wrote. Therefore it appears this refers to one who vowed from milk: if they call whey "whey of milk" he is forbidden in whey, as Tur and the poskim wrote; but one who vowed from whey ; even though they call it "whey of milk" ; is permitted in milk; and so Ateres Zahav wrote explicitly.
