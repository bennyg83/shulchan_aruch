And parboiled food. This means food that is partly cooked but not fully cooked. Food cooked excessively is included in the term “cooked” in every context. Tosafos and the Ran wrote this, and the Bach ruled accordingly. This is the primary ruling.
Rabbeinu Yerucham writes: Regarding vows, we follow ordinary speech. Where there is no applicable ordinary usage, such as one who vows in the Holy Tongue in a place where people do not speak the Holy Tongue, we follow the language of the Torah. This is clear from the chapter HaShutafin. End quote. The Beis Yosef cites this at the end of the siman.

Rambam chapter 9: after writing all the laws written in this siman — "in all these matters and similar ones, the law of one who vows and one who swears is the same" — and Beit Yosef brings it; and it is obvious. And see above siman 239, where it was explained that the law of an oath is equal to vows in every respect except for several matters that were explained there.
Forbidden to lend, etc. — from what it says in chapter Shevu'at HaDayanim: "one who lends his fellow on a pledge," etc., concluding "lest he take out this deposit" — end of Likutei Maharil there. And I do not understand what evidence this is — certainly a deposit is included in a pledge, for a pledge itself is a deposit; but a pledge is not included in "deposit." And if he vowed not to accept a deposit, that would be from what the Sages said "distance yourself from deposits" — and this does not include "not to lend on a pledge." And it appears we follow the vower's intent, as the Rav wrote. In the responsum of Maharam Prague printing siman 965: a scribe of Reuven gave a handshake to Shimon that when he finishes Reuven's work he will not write for anyone except Shimon; afterward the city burned and one of Reuven's pamphlets that the scribe had written was burned; now the scribe asks whether he may rewrite that pamphlet when he finishes his work before writing for Shimon. And when the pamphlet burned he had not yet finished the book for Reuven — he can certainly write, for Reuven's work is not finished. But certainly if the pamphlet burned after he had completed the entire book then his work would have been finished and he could not write. Evidence from tractate Kelim chapter 27: a garment in which he wove three by three, etc. Moshe Azriel son of Rabbi Eliezer Darshan — end. And the same applies to all similar work. And so we see from the evidence he brought from tractate Kelim.
