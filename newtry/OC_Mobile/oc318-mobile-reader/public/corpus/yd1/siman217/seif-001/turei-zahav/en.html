Even roasted or boiled. Tur mentions only roasted meat and concludes that he is prohibited in it and in boiled meat. This is puzzling. Why did Tur not mention boiled meat initially as well? One cannot say the word "and boiled" was accidentally omitted at the beginning, because then Tur should have said, "he is prohibited in them," in the plural, just as he later says, "he is permitted in them." Derishah therefore explains that whenever ordinary language calls roasted meat "cooked," boiled meat is certainly included. Thus it is correct to say that if people call roasted meat "cooked," he is also prohibited in boiled meat. I find support from the Gemara, where R. Yoshiyah prohibits based on the verse, "and they cooked the Pesach." R. Yoshiyah's position also includes boiled meat, as appears from the sugya, yet the verse he cites refers only to roasted meat. We must therefore say the two are linked: once roasted food is called cooked, all the more so boiled food is called cooked. Here the term "boiled" means partially cooked rather than fully cooked, because that is how people use the word, even though elsewhere "boiled" can mean cooked more intensely than ordinary cooking.
