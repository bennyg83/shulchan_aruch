Beneath the covering — meaning: membrane spread on the kereis, and they call it tarpeh in foreign tongue, or naatz; and beneath that membrane is chelev adhering to the kereis itself; and Rabbenu Efraim forbade and Rabbenu Yoel permitted, and he elaborated in Mordechai with their proofs; and we rule like Rabbenu Efraim:
Like other chelev — to nullify it in sixty. Nevertheless it appears to me we do not say regarding it that the piece itself becomes nevelah to require sixty against the whole piece to which this chelev adheres if it does not have sixty in itself, since most poskim hold we do not say the piece itself becomes nevelah except in meat and milk, like Rabbenu Efraim below siman 92; and so I found Maharshal, chapter 8, siman 54, because whatever does not impart taste has no Torah prohibition, only d'rabbanan they decreed here that the piece itself becomes nevelah; and since others eat this chelev like Rabbenu Yoel in similar cases, they did not decree; and nevertheless he wrote there that regarding what we rule pure chelev seals a puncture, as stated siman 46 — this chelev too is not pure to seal a puncture, since for Rabbenu Efraim it is Torah chelev and punishable with karet:
But one does not forbid vessels, etc. — in Agudah it is written: however, these do not refrain from the vessels of those or their cooking, only they do not eat the kereis; and Maharshal wrote on this: since he wrote they are careful only from the kereis itself, behold the dish cooked with it they eat — and this is what is meant that they are not careful from their cooking; and even those who forbid and hold it as full chelev — nevertheless, the slight chelev on the kereis is nullified in sixty against the kereis, broth, and turnip with it; therefore:
