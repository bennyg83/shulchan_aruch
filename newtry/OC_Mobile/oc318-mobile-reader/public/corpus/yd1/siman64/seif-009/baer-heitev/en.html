In part of it — but for everyone one must remove the membrane under the cover, and they disagreed only regarding chelev under that membrane — Beit Yosef in the name of Mordechai:
In sixty — Taz wrote: nevertheless we do not say regarding it that the piece itself becomes nevelah to require sixty against the whole piece to which this chelev adheres (since Rabbenu Efraim ruled we do not say the piece becomes nevelah except in meat and milk); but regarding sealing a puncture it is not called pure chelev and does not seal (since for R. Efraim it is full chelev — see above siman 48):
Vessels — for presumably the slight chelev on the kereis is nullified in sixty against the kereis, broth, and turnip with it (and Agudah wrote these do not refrain from the vessels of those or their cooking, only they do not eat the kereis; and Maharshal wrote on this: since he wrote they are careful only from the kereis itself, behold the dish cooked with it they eat — and this is what is meant that they are not careful from their cooking; and even those who forbid and hold it as full chelev — nevertheless presumably the chelev on the kereis is nullified in sixty against the kereis, broth, and turnip with it; and Shach wrote even if there is a mixture of chelev adhering to the kereis in a dish and it is not nullified in the dish, they do not refrain from eating, see there; and Peri Chadash wrote for us who rule taste is like the essence d'oraisa, one may not be lenient in this; and in responsa of Maharalbach daf 145 he wrote: but if there is a custom, let them act according to their custom, such as in the land of Rhineland there are some places that practice prohibition on chelev on the abomasum, and when they come to places that practice leniency they eat from their cooking from the broth or meat that was cooked with the prohibition, and all the more they use their vessels, which are Beit Yosef; and specifically that the custom be in eating, such as those places that practice prohibition live in that country where some practice leniency and some prohibition — one may say the places that practice prohibition did not accept the prohibition entirely to forbid its emission, broth, and what was cooked with it; but if the custom is only in feeding, meaning the feeder is from the country where some practice prohibition and some leniency and the feeder practices leniency and the eater is from a country where all practice prohibition, such as from Poland when he comes to places that practice leniency, even though they are accustomed to feed nearby places that practice prohibition — it is forbidden for him to eat from their cooking; therefore it is not proper what people of Poland do when they come to Ashkenaz and eat from their cooking; however, that which it is forbidden to eat a forbidden thing because of ancestral custom and countrymen's custom applies specifically when he does not see leniency in the matter; but when leniency appears in the matter and he comes from a place that practiced prohibition to a place that practiced leniency, he may eat — Shach siman 119 s.k. 20, see there):
