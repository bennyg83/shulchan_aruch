Tosevet, membrane. In the Gemara it is taught: "all chelev that covers the innards" — just as chelev covering the innards is membrane and peeled, so all membrane and peeled; Rashi: a thin membrane is on it and the membrane is peeled from above the chelev that is not so adherent; and he includes chelev on the abomasum, and all the more so on the intestines; R. Akiva says: just as chelev covering the innards is tosevet, membrane, and peeled, so all, etc.; Rashi: tosevet is a word — when it is a word spread on the innards, and likewise on the intestines at their head; but chelev of the abomasum is not tosevet but pieces by pieces and adherent; and on the intestines from the head onward — that is from the hadra dekanta when they separate it, chelev remains with them and it is not tosevet but adherent to them. And in Tur is written tosevet, membrane, and peeled — meaning spread upon the membrane such that when the membrane is peeled it peels with it; and he gave another sign that flesh should not cover it — and this is also explicit in the Gemara. And some challenge Tur: why did he explain what they said, membrane and peeled, on the membrane under the chelev — behold Rashi explained on the membrane above the chelev; and it appears there is no difficulty, for Rashi explained so in R. Yishmael's words, but in R. Akiva's words it is not so; for if you say R. Akiva's membrane and peeled is also like R. Yishmael, only he adds tosevet — R. Akiva should have said tosevet and membrane, etc.; rather, it must be one matter: tosevet sits entirely on the membrane beneath it — and this is Rashi's intent when he wrote spread on the innards, namely that the membrane with the chelev on it is spread on the innards; and Maharshal also wrote Tur's words thus, that R.
