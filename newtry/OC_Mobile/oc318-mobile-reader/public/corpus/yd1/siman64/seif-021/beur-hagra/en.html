A butcher, etc. — there 96a: bar Peloni, etc. Nevertheless.
(Collection) But Rashba wrote: since it says "the halacha is," we learn that the one who says so does not come to dispute; and the Gemara ruled not like the one who says so, but specifically a kezayit in one place (end of his words).
And the practice of kosher, etc. — for this is what is stated there: a butcher after whom was found, etc.; and do not challenge the unspecified case of "picks," etc., but regarding a butcher upon whom it is incumbent to pick the meat — even if he sold unspecified and did not sell on the presumption that it was picked, they flog him and remove him; not so another. And this is what Rashi stated there s.v. "from the force of the manner," etc. There.
