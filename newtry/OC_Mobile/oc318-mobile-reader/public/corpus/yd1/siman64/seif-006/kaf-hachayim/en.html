(16) [Seif 6] Chelev of the flanks, etc. — in the Gemara Chullin 93a: this tarba of kolovesata is forbidden and punishable with karet — and this is chelev on the flanks. And Rashi: tarba of kolovesata is a small bone placed on the bone called naka, connected to the spine of the alyah from above, and upon it is chelev beneath the head of the loins called lombil. And Rambam wrote chapter 7 of Forbidden Foods: chelev at the roots of the thighs inside — liable for karet; and this is chelev on the flanks. And Rif explained tarba of kolovesata where it leans on the waist — and this is chelev of the flanks. And Ran wrote that which they said "and this is chelev on the flanks" is not to say this alone is essential chelev on the flanks, but to say this too is of chelev on the flanks. Beit Yosef. And certainly certainly one measure: at the end of the third bone called kulit there is another bone like sockets in males, connecting thigh to body — and they came to explain chelev of the flanks explicit in the Torah in parashat Vayikra "and the two kidneys and the chelev on them, on the flanks," etc., spreading until there. Shulchan Gavoah ot 15.
