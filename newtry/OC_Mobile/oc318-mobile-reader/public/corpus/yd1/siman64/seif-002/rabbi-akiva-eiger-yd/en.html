In Or Yekarot, parashat Devarim: for a dangerously ill person they feed light food first; therefore if there is chelev of pure animal and chelev of impure animal — if he practiced impure chelev, he must feed pure chelev, for in impure there are two prohibitions; but if he does not practice, feed impure, for it is lighter, no karet; in pure there is karet — see there; and similarly above (siman 62) this practical difference applies: if apostate Jew has kezayit flesh, giddim, bones, and apostate of impure — because he does not practice impure, regarding impure prohibition he has only "do not draw close"; give him impure; and in my humble opinion there is another practical difference: one ate chelev of impure animal and afterward slaughtered pure and said "this is for my sin-offering" — if chelev prohibition applies and he is liable for a sin-offering, the animal is forbidden as slaughtering kodashim outside — as Shulchan Aruch above (siman 5); therefore he informed us there is no chelev law and he is not liable for sin-offering, and the animal is permitted:
