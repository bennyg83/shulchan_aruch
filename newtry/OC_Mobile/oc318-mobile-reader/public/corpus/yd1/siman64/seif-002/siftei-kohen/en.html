Meir, who said chelev of a stillborn fetus is forbidden, it is only a rabbinic stringency, not like the view of the Rambam (and the Mechaber), who hold one is liable for karet, end of his words — I did not reach the end of his view, for if so Rabbenu Meir would be against the Gemara, Chullin 92b, where it is proven explicitly that chelev of a stillborn fetus is forbidden d'oraisa according to R. Meir, and so it is stated in several places in the Gemara; rather, in his haste he did not examine, for Rabbenu Meir there explicitly refers to chelev of the gid, regarding which we say in the Gemara that for R. Meir it is d'rabbanan, and the Rambam ruled chapter 8 of Forbidden Foods and the Tur below siman 68, and all the poskim — and this is clear:
And chelev of a nevelah, etc. — thus it appears from the words of the Rav the Maggid: even though one is not liable for karet on its chelev, nevertheless there is prohibition because of chelev; and nevertheless the Rav did not need to write this, since in any case it is forbidden because of nevelah, and there is also lashes because of nevelah, as the Rambam and Tur wrote — and it is simple:
