(Siman 64 seif 1) Chelev of ox, etc. forbidden; and of other species permitted. See Peri Chadash on chelev of nevelah and treifah: liable for chelev and nevelah from inclusive prohibition; not like Maggid Mishna on Rambam chapter 7 Forbidden Foods who wrote additional prohibition applies only when prohibition is added to a piece that was permitted at first; here no addition to chelev itself, only inclusive like gid hanasheh of nevelah and treifah. Lechem Mishneh also wondered on Maggid Mishna. And in my humble opinion, nevelah and treifah prohibition was indeed added in the chelev itself relative to when it was permitted; as Rambam wrote below seif 14; and though in chapter 7 he explained because of inclusive prohibition added in its flesh, nevertheless in truth it was added in chelev itself relative to when permitted; and unlike gid of nevelah and treifah, where Rambam wrote chapter 8 explicitly it is inclusive, for there it was forbidden from the outset relative to when permitted, as Rambam wrote chapter 6 of Laws of Offerings, even for non-sacred animals. And this is explicit from Tosafot Zevachim 70 and chapter Gid HaNasheh 101, even for non-sacred animals; and so in Ta'am HaMelech on Forbidden Foods. And what Pri Megadim challenged from chelev of spleen forbidden when fit for offering — in my humble opinion, it is fit for olah; examine. [And so I omitted in Sefer HaMakneh, Kiddushin 56b — see there]: And what Pri Megadim challenged: nevelah and treifah fit at first for offering, but blemished animals why added — already anticipated in Sefer Machaneh Reuven. And in my humble opinion, not difficult: was fit for bechor on bamah, only lacking limb — difficult for Maharam of Lublin; but per Rambam even lacking limb permitted for bechor, as Sha'ar HaMelech on Shechitah; still treifah permitted for bechor per Maharam in end of Zevachim, and then no addition. And so for stillborn, chelev forbidden when born — no addition; perhaps say since nevelah chelev prohibition is found in complete animals, it applies even where no addition. And from Kiddushin 77: zanah prohibition applies to challalah prohibition even without addition, since prostitution disqualifies an Israelite — similarly here. But one may reject: kohanim have extra commandments in Scripture — perhaps not generally. And Tosafot Yevamot 33 imply some nevelah has no addition, only inclusive; and so Tosafot Shevuot 24; and this where it was blemished from creation, not treifah, and stillborn — no addition; perhaps only where presumed complete from creation, as above siman 41. And what Pri Megadim challenged on impure chelev: when formed, impurity applies, and after months all agree no chelev prohibition when no air and no months — not so, proven Pesachim 100b: impure animal is not called "in animal you may eat" like water; if so, liable for chelev per R.
