On a bird — not specifically a bird; the same applies to a limb from an animal per the Gemara's law; only the Tanna used "bird" because even though its blood is slight, it still requires conditions.
In every case one should forbid — even a bird, and even though a bird exudes and what difference does it make regarding a bird in coating as implied from Rashi — nevertheless it is forbidden because we are concerned the flour will clump if it is not so thin and will prevent exudation from the bird; and for us who hold chanan, if so the coating is chanan and forbids the bird, and ein hana'asur does not apply here — perhaps it absorbs something.
And afterward rinsed, etc. — this is obvious; what concern is there that it will exude its blood? Rambam is stringent because he holds all meat requires scalding in boiling water; but for us who do not require scalding, this is superfluous language.
And pashtida, etc. — and Peri Chadash disagrees; one should not depart from what Rama said in a rabbinic matter.
