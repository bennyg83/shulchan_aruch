Even though they fulfill them he is called wicked. Explanation — since he does not seek to permit it; and this that he is called wicked is the words of Shmuel in Nedarim (page 22), and this that he is called a sinner is a baraita (page 77). And the Tur brought both, and it is difficult why the baraita returned that he is a sinner — behold even a wicked one he is; and even though he derives it from a verse "and if you cease vowing there shall be no sin in you" — it is difficult on the verse itself thus, for also Shmuel derives from a verse that he is wicked — all the more so it is difficult on the Shulchan Aruch that brought in this language "he is called wicked and he is called a sinner" — why did he mention sinner at all since he made him wicked? And it seems to me there is a distinction in the matter — if he has an opening to permit and does not permit, he is called wicked; and if he did not find an opening, nevertheless as we find on page 23 that the Sages were very distressed on account of Rabbi Shimon bar Rabbi because they did not find an opening for him — on this he said that nevertheless he is called a sinner as long as his vow is upon him — as above, correct.
