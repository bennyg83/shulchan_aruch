Until she tells him, ‘I immersed.’ This is not comparable to the ruling above in siman 184, seif 11, where he need not ask her. There, there is a compound doubt: perhaps she did not see, and even if she did see, perhaps she immersed. Here, however, she certainly saw, and a doubtful immersion cannot remove a definite status. This is stated in the Gemara in Perek Kol HaYad. Yet this is difficult in light of what I wrote above in klal 59, seif katan 8, and in Kuntres HaSefeikot, that even where there is a compound doubt we do not permit the matter when it can be clarified by asking. Why, then, do we permit it above without asking? Perhaps that case is different because there is an additional presumption that she was previously pure, and ordinarily a woman immerses at the proper time. Nevertheless, where she has a definite status of impurity, we do not rely upon this, because a doubt cannot remove a definite status.
Once enough days have passed for her to count and immerse, she is believed. If the husband is uncertain whether enough days have passed, she is believed. Shach. The Taz writes that even if she ordinarily begins wearing white garments on the sixth day after seeing, and in this instance that would have been impossible unless she had begun on the fifth day, she is nevertheless believed when she says that she immersed.
