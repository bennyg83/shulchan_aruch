If he is, etc. See Even HaEzer, siman 47, seif 4.
And if she gave, etc. Ibid.
For example, etc. The Yerushalmi, cited by Tosafot there, s.v. 'and even,' etc.
Nevertheless, etc. The Gemara there: 'And even so,' etc.
But if, etc. See the explanation of the Gra. Presumably she provides a plausible explanation for her statement so that she will not forfeit her kesubah. Tosafot cited above, s.v. 'and if she did not,' implies that here even a plausible explanation is ineffective.
She wears, etc. See Rashi there, s.v. 'she established herself,' etc. The Rashba writes that she certainly did not perform an overt act. See the Shach.
She said, 'So-and-so,' etc. The Gemara there. Although the Rosh cites the Yerushalmi as requiring witnesses, and the Rashba rules likewise, this requirement concerns forfeiture of the kesubah. Beit Yosef explains that this is also clear from the Rosh, who wrote regarding forfeiture of the kesubah: 'It is reasonable, for how can money be taken away on the testimony of one witness?' The Ra'ah and Ritva, however, write that the Ramban disagrees with the Rashba and accepts the sage's word entirely. They write that this is implied by the Gemara's statement, 'He went and asked him,' which indicates that we rely upon her statement alone. The Yerushalmi's requirement of witnesses means that her claim that she told the sage and that the sage ruled to her in that manner must be verified. Regarding the sage's actual ruling, however, since she made the matter depend upon him, he is believed. Likewise, where she served him, although this did not occur before witnesses, there are grounds supporting the claim. See the Ritva and Even HaEzer, siman 17, seif 8.
