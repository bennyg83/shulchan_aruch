She is impure. See Be'er Heitev in the name of Turei Zahav, who states that if she made the declaration in public, an explanation does not help. Torat HaShelamim disagrees and writes that even if she made the declaration in public, an explanation helps. Kreti U'Pleti likewise rules this way. See Turei Zahav, who writes that if a woman gave her explanation before establishing her status by wearing the garments of a niddah, it is effective because she disclosed the explanation beforehand. See Siftei Kohen, who writes that if, because of the circumstances underlying her explanation, she had no option other than to wear the garments of a niddah, her explanation is effective. Even if her husband did not know of those circumstances, she is permitted, since no other course was possible. See there. According to this, if she gave the explanation cited by Siftei Kohen in subsection 3, "I thought I was a niddah, but I have now examined myself and found that the blood came from a wound," it should always be effective. One cannot argue that she should not have worn the garments of a niddah, since she genuinely erred, thought that she was a niddah, and properly wore those garments. I have, however, seen that Kreti U'Pleti writes that even this explanation is ineffective. Torat HaShelamim likewise writes this in the name of Maharik, siman 87, and the matter requires examination. See also Responsa Shemen Rokeach, volume 1, siman 43, and Responsa Chatam Sofer, siman 15, concerning this matter.
