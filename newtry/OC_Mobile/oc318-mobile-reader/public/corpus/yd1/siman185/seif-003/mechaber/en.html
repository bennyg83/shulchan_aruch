If she said to her husband, “I am tamei,” and afterward said, “I am tehorah,” she is not believed (if it is after “kedei dibbur”) (Beit Yosef in the name of Rabbeinu Yerucham). But if she gave an “amtlah” for her words — for example, she says that she did not say so to him at first except because she did not have the strength to endure tashmish, or another claim like this — she is believed. {Rama: Nevertheless, one who wants to be stringent upon himself not to believe her — it is a measure of piety (Beit Yosef). But by the law she is believed, even with silence afterward, only that she comes and lies by her husband and he knows and recognizes that what she said at first, “I am tamei,” she did because of a quarrel that she had with him (Mahari Weil, siman 22), and similar.} But if they saw her wearing garments designated for the days of her niddah and afterward she said, “I am tehorah,” even though she gave an amtlah for her words, she is not believed. (If she said, “So-and-so chacham purified a stain for me,” and the chacham says that she is lying — the chacham is believed and she is tamei) (Ran in the name of Ramban, and Rabbeinu Yerucham).
