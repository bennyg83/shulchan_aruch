Sage is believed and she is tamei. Taz s.k. 3 distinguishes between prohibition and monetary matters; and Mahari Brandeis established mnemonic; and so should be in settling author's words in Even HaEzer reciprocally; but Rosh's words are not settled at all — certainly if Ramban says his law that one who speaks from witness and witness contradicts him — witness is believed in general — fine to distinguish monetary from prohibition; but here Ramban as Rosh said in Shevuot learns his law from Ketubot where she said so-and-so purified this stain for me and it was found a lie — proves sage is believed since she spoke from him; and if for monetary he is not believed — still difficult from Ketubot for there regarding monetary she leaves without ketubah and likewise with witnesses as Rosh in name of Yerushalmi in Ketubot — still his proof is nothing. Can say even for prohibition she is believed since his proof from Ketubot does not exist. Therefore seems if we analyze Ramban's words he brought proof from Ketubot she said so-and-so purified stain and did not bring from earlier taught so-and-so fixed my dough and they asked and found lie — in this also Ramban's proof as proven; and conversely in Rosh chapter HaMedir he wrote entire Yerushalmi with witnesses and afterward Rosh explains appears fixer not believed to say "I did not fix for her" if she contradicts him — reasonable matter how remove money with one witness; needs witnesses such as she said so-and-so fixed for me at that hour and witnesses testified at that hour he was not in city, etc.; and afterward Rosh brought "he had relations with her as niddah" such as she said so-and-so sage purified stain and they asked and found lie (b). Must understand why Rosh cited language only matter of fixing dough and omitted so-and-so purified stain — Rosh should cite both languages dough and stain; granted challah is dough and it is same; but why omit stain and explain only dough; and why not explain this matter after citing stain. Therefore seems to distinguish dough from stain — in dough if she alone said she fixed it — it is credibility and when she said so-and-so fixed for me she has migo and can properly contradict him; and Rama in responsum siman 66 in place of migo she is believed to contradict; and further she said so-and-so fixed for me is extra language — suffices she said it is fixed without her statement — then like words not intended and unaware; therefore although she said so-and-so fixed for me her words are strong for behold in her hand to fix and needs no so-and-so — therefore he is believed to contradict and needs two witnesses; but in stain can she rule to purify and defile? Must need sage (c) — therefore no migo and no strong credibility for nothing in her hand — all from sage we live; therefore when she contradicts the sage is believed not she; therefore in Ketubot when Rosh explained with witnesses he did not mention stain only dough in her hand — with this her words strong therefore needs witnesses; but in Shevuot when Ramban brought proof he did not bring dough proof at all for must be with witnesses — only brought stain proof and here established his law woman not believed to contradict witness in stain. Not difficult either way when no proof from dough and challah and must be with witnesses — from where for Ramban perhaps even regarding stain credibility and also with witnesses as explained in Yerushalmi. Not difficult: one, on stain we say in Gemara two explanations — either she said so-and-so sage purified stain or presumed niddah among neighbors — must say why first explanation at all — second is correct and true presumed among neighbors lashes and she is full niddah — therefore first needs witnesses for presumed among neighbors unlike latter does not need witnesses; and Gemara seeks to find establishment in mishnah in all forms with and without witnesses — therefore not like Yerushalmi no witnesses in stain — otherwise if with witnesses why this trouble depending on sage; can say Ran brought in name of Ramban no need witnesses for we say "and they asked him" implies not depending on witnesses only on his inquiry; therefore regarding stain depends on inquiry not witnesses — Talmudic style cites "and they asked" in others because of stain although in others no inquiry needed; but if also in stain needs witnesses — "and they asked" cited in Shulchan Aruch is superfluous — on whom do we cast? Can say although deals with witnesses — "and they asked" means needs two witnesses besides one and that so-and-so who fixed dough or sage combines with them — behold two witnesses; therefore only need his inquiry — but this itself difficult why need this and what does it teach — plain witness combines; should state generally with witnesses — why Gemara repeats to inform us; but in dough fine to say she said so-and-so kohen fixed this dough — then unspecified kohen even familiar kohanim — perhaps kohen interested if says did not fix will fix again dough and work reaches him and great terumah; teaches nevertheless believed; and teaches much in testimony we hold whoever is interested even remotely does not testify; but in stain fine why "and they asked" — must say deals she alone contradicts and Ramban's words prove as stated; per my humble opinion matters are clear in Rosh and Ramban — authors labored in vain; and regarding challah also deals with kohen although in our Gemara "so-and-so kneader" in Rif reads only so-and-so — referred above so-and-so kohen. Rama wrote in responsum siman 66 since dispute exists — can say rabbinic prohibition woman is believed (d); must say what rabbi wrote here stain means blood on cloth attached also called stain as we say there so-and-so purified this blood — and Rosh and all other poskim brought stain meaning blood on cloth sometimes called stain — plain. If this reasoning were solid proof to distinguish Torah from rabbinic could settle Ramban and Rosh's words plainly in feeding not tithed and challah not taken — all rabbinic prohibition for we hold terumah and challah nowadays rabbinic — see Laws of Terumah — therefore needs witnesses unlike niddah which is Torah does not need witnesses — therefore Ramban established on niddah; not difficult perhaps also with witnesses as written above several resolutions; and this was place to settle Yerushalmi and Bavli together — Yerushalmi says all with witnesses for Yerushalmi says so-and-so saw stain — rabbinic unlike Bavli says saw niddah blood — Scriptural therefore "and they asked him" no witnesses needed sage alone believed; but my mind not at ease to distinguish in Ramban's words themselves between Scriptural and rabbinic — Rama's distinction to decide between disputants for some hold like Ramban and some dispute — his intent to rely rabbinically on disputants; but per Ramban's view itself to distinguish Torah from rabbinic my mind not at ease — requires study.
