I am pure. And “jokingly I told you at first” — she is not believed — Rambam; and likewise Sefer HaTerumah.
And similarly. The Rambam's language: such as her husband came to her and his sister or his mother was with her in the courtyard, and she said “I am impure,” and afterward returned and said “I am pure, and I only told you I am impure because of your sister and your mother so they would not see us” — she is believed; and likewise all similar cases — end of his words; and Sefer HaTerumah and Mordechai in the name of Or Zarua wrote that if she said “I thought I was becoming a niddah but now I checked myself and found that the blood came to me due to a wound or bruise” — and similar — she is believed.
Except that she came and lay down etc. Nevertheless it appears the husband is obligated to ask her why she came to him — for she had said to him “I am impure” — for he must draw the explanation from her mouth, etc.; and furthermore Mahariv only permitted when she said to him at the outset “I feel a pain in my bowels and I suspect a change of veset,” etc.; but not when she said to him explicitly “I am impure” — Bach; (and likewise in Maadanei Melech p. 303, that Mahariv only permits when she said “I feel,” etc., but the Rav holds the same applies when she said “I am impure” — see there); and it is implied from his words that he does not disagree with the Rav; and so it appears that certainly the Rav admits that whatever can be clarified — we clarify; he came only to say that we should not think that her lying next to him is nothing and that he need not separate from her — but he must separate from her until she explicitly states the explanation — and it is easily understood.
She is not believed. The reason, written in the name of Rashba: due to embarrassment or coincidental coercion she may have said “impure,” but to take action to that extent — to wear niddah garments — she would not wear them; and likewise Bach who concludes: for it would have sufficed when she said to him “I am impure” and she did not need to wear niddah garments; and not comparable to above siman 1 seif 13 regarding a butcher who made a sign on the head of the lamb, etc. — for there he had no other option; but here she could have said “I am impure” and did not need to wear niddah garments — end of his words; and likewise Derishab; from their words it appears that where she had no other option — she is believed; and likewise the Rav wrote in responsum siman 2 regarding a woman who gave birth three times in the eighth month and was concerned about the evil eye and wanted to hide her pregnancy from her household and neighbors and said in front of them “I am impure,” etc. — that her husband is permitted to come to her; for even though it states there in the question that her husband agreed with her to say so — nevertheless it appears from the Rav’s responsum that even if her husband did not know about it, she is believed — see there where he elaborated; and he concludes: and furthermore it appears that even Ramban admits that an explanation helps her — even though he wrote that it does not help, that is specifically an explanation of the type that helps regarding “I am impure to you,” meaning where she says she had no strength or other such explanations; and for the reason Beit Yosef wrote that she would not be so foolish as to maintain she is a niddah among her neighbors; but in such a case where the main explanation depends on others and she could not do it another way — certainly we say such an explanation helps; and as Tur wrote that it is no worse than when she said explicitly “I am impure to you,” etc. — see there.
