The sage is believed. It appears to me that this applies specifically to the sage himself, since she relies upon him and is thereby found to have spoken falsely. However, if the sage is not present and a single witness testifies that the sage ruled the matter forbidden for her, he is not believed. For we say in the second chapter of Kesubos, 22a, that wherever the Torah believed one witness, he is regarded as two witnesses, etc. Accordingly, here as well, that witness is not believed to contradict her, since she is regarded as two witnesses. Furthermore, even concerning adultery, one witness is not believed to forbid her to her husband, and the same applies here, in my opinion. The credibility granted to the sage here applies specifically regarding the prohibition. However, concerning monetary matters, such as causing her to forfeit her kesubah, the sage is no more credible than any other single witness. This is clear from the words of the Rosh in the chapter HaMadir.
