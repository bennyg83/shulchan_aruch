He should fast for forty days. See Orach Chaim siman 568, where it is stated that two or three consecutive days and nights are reckoned as forty days. See there. See also responsa of Maharam of Lublin, siman 2.
On the night associated with the fast, meat and wine are forbidden. At first glance this appears to mean the night preceding the fast day, for it is called the night of the fast since the day follows the night. However, Maharshal writes in Perek Oto VeEt Beno, siman 11, regarding the practice of some women who refrain from meat on Monday and Thursday when they fast: some refrain also on the following night, meaning Tuesday night, and this resembles sectarian practice, since they act contrary to the Sages of the Talmud, who rule in this context that the night follows the day. Maharshal labors to explain their custom, but his words are strained and surprising and contrary to the sugya. In my opinion, the reason is simple. The Gemara in Chullin 83a states that with sacrificial matters the night follows the day. Since this practice substitutes for a fast, as Maharshal writes there, and a fast stands in place of a korban, the reason is readily understood. Others refrain on the preceding night. Maharshal discusses the reasons at length and concludes that when one fasts, it is proper, where possible, to refrain from meat and wine both before and after the fast. This, he writes, is the meaning of the Gemara's statement that his teeth were blackened because of fasting, for his teeth would not have blackened unless he refrained from meat and wine before and after the fast. See Orach Chaim siman 558 in the gloss.
