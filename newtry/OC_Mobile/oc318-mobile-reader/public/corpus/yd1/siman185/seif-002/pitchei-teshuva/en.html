Turei Zahav writes that even if she generally begins wearing white garments on the sixth day after she menstruates, and in the present case that would have been impossible because, had she immersed, she would have needed to begin wearing white garments on the fifth day, she is nevertheless believed when she says that she immersed, since doing so was possible. See there. If a woman suspected of violating the prohibition of niddah committed adultery while she was a niddah, she is not thereby considered suspect in relation to her husband, and she is believed even at the time of her veset. If she caused her husband to violate the prohibition of niddah, however, she is no longer believed to say at the time of her veset, "I am pure," until she repents. At a time other than her veset, and likewise when she says that she immersed, she is believed, except during the winter, when immersion is burdensome. Thus writes Chavat Da'at. It is possible that in a place where payment is required for immersion, she is not believed, as Siftei Kohen wrote above in siman 2, subsection 14. See also Noda BiYehudah, second edition, Even HaEzer, beginning of siman 26.
