An incident involving a woman who gave birth three times in the eighth month and feared that the evil eye was causing this; she wished to conceal her pregnancy from her household and neighbors. Her husband agreed that she should declare herself impure at the beginning of her pregnancy in order to conceal it — and Rema was asked whether her husband may come to her in private, and he responded permissively, elaborating on the giving of an explanation in a case of one who has been established as a niddah among her neighbors. And in my humble view it seems that this does not fall under the category of "established as a niddah" at all — since she disclosed this before she had established herself as tamei; for the principal stringency in "established as a niddah" according to Ramban is that she performed no act for the sake of the explanation, as Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Rashba — whereas here she nullified this proof from the outset. And this matter is comparable to one who registers a protest against a bill of divorce or a gift — even though he afterward performs a major act, since he disclosed his intention from the outset that no substance shall be in whatever act he performs afterward, we do not follow the act; so too here. And there is no difference between whether she disclosed this in her husband's presence or before witnesses — since her husband knows the truth, the Torah has not forbidden her to him at all. And even according to the reasoning I wrote in the name of Maharal of Prague — that if it becomes known to many people, the situation is worse — nevertheless here it is permitted, for that reasoning only applies to establish her as tamei against her husband when he cannot rely on the leniency; but in this case where her husband knows she is truly pure before she was established as such against others — it is certainly permitted. And this also appears correct from the last chapter of Kiddushin: "we stone and burn based on presumptions; and an incident of a woman who came to Jerusalem with a child riding on her shoulders — she raised him, and he had relations with her and they were stoned — not because he was certainly her son, but because he was attached to her." There too: if from the outset, upon entering Jerusalem, she had stated before two witnesses that this is not her son but that she would raise him and establish him as her son before the many — they certainly would not have stoned her afterward, since there is no presumption at all when she undermined it from the outset; and even if she later explicitly said this was her son — since she nullified from the outset what she would say afterward — all this seems obvious. And I am astonished at Rema in his responsum siman 2 who did not address this matter, but instead compared it to giving an explanation after the establishment of the presumption — that even though this does not help in "established as a niddah" according to Ramban, here it is a good explanation and helps — see there. But in my view there is no presumption here at all, for there was a cure before the affliction: she disclosed it first to her husband when she was still believed about herself to be pure. So it seems to me obviously. And further I saw in that same responsum that Rema raises the difficulty: why do they say "she has been established as a niddah, her husband receives lashes" — and not "she receives lashes"? And he answered what he answered. And in my view: it teaches us a novelty — that even he, for whom there is a side of merit in that she did not tell him she was a niddah as wives normally inform their husbands — so he might say "I relied on this and attributed the change of clothing to something else" — it teaches us that this amounts to nothing and he receives lashes. So it seems to me.
