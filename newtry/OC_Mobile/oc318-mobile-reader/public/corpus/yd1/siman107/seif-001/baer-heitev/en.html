To the bowl. Issur VeHeter wrote that if he did not pour them into the bowl but they remained in the pot and he also poured the boiling ones from them — it is forbidden, lest the forbidden egg was at the bottom of the pot and the eggs near it became forbidden; for until now, when the water was conveying taste to all the eggs, they all assisted in nullifying it, and now that they are not there the other eggs do not assist in nullifying; and since through the moisture of water on them they forbid in any case also the others that afterward touch that which touched the forbidden eggs — therefore they are all forbidden, even if there are a hundred — end of his words; and see in Shach, who elaborates greatly on this law:
Main point. Conclusion of Taz that one should be stringent — in eggs forbidden, and in fish permitted as long as it is possible that there is sixty against the little water that remained in the pot; but in eggs, which are not accustomed to be cooked so much, this does not help; and even according to those who permit one should be stringent if he cooked many eggs in a pot and among them was one that is forbidden and he removed each one with one spoon — there is concern lest he removed the forbidden one first and the spoon became forbidden and consequently the others afterward; for that which we say we do not establish an issur, etc. — that is because we say the issur is in the majority, etc., see there; however all this is in a Torah prohibition, for one who holds that egg blood is also forbidden from the Torah; but in a rabbinic prohibition one may be lenient, similar to nishpakh in siman 98, see there; and in Issur VeHeter wrote that even for one who holds egg blood is rabbinic there is to forbid after the issur became known, and Taz disagrees, see there; and if an impure fish with a pure fish, and likewise an egg with an embryo with other eggs — is it called min b'mino or not — see Shach siman 101:
