And it forbids again everything in the bowl. So one must say, and Issur VeHeter wrote general rule 27 that if he did not pour them to the bowl but they remained in the pot and he also poured the boiling ones from them — forbidden, lest the forbidden egg was at the bottom of the pot and the eggs near it became forbidden; for until now when the water was conveying taste to all the eggs they all assisted in nullifying, and now that they are not there the other eggs do not assist in nullifying; and since through the moisture of water on them they forbid in any case also the others that afterward touch that which touched the forbidden eggs — therefore they are all forbidden, even if there are a hundred — end of his words:
And he did not pour them all at once into the bowl, etc. And if there is doubt in the matter whether he poured at once — it seems from Tur to be stringent, and so Issur VeHeter wrote:
And some permit, etc. The reason Rama wrote here we do not establish issur, etc. is the reason of Rabbeinu Shimshon whom he brings in Semag; and accordingly even in eggs they are permitted. But there another reason for permission is stated, and so in Tur — that is, he permits in fish because those few fish that became forbidden in the pot are nullified afterward in majority mixture and do not return and forbid the rest; and according to this one should forbid in eggs, since it is not customary to cook eggs so much together that twenty or thirty eggs that became forbidden are nullified in majority mixture of heter; and so explicitly in Semag; accordingly there is also a practical difference in the fish themselves — for the second reason Hagahot Shach siman 51 wrote specifically when he poured the water before he poured the last fish remaining in the pan; but if water or gravy remained with a little fish at the end and he poured them to the bowl — all forbidden, for pouring is like a first vessel — end of his words; and according to the first reason there is no distinction in this, and Rashal wrote chapter 3 siman 44 that since Tur mentioned the second reason we establish like him, and one should be stringent in eggs; and accordingly one should be stringent also in the second practical difference I mentioned in the fish themselves — except Rashal wrote there that the water in the bowl does not forbid all the fish through the mixture, since they are in a second vessel; and that little water that remained in the pot also has no power to emit and absorb in the fish in the bowl; and also some say that little water itself that became forbidden at the end of the pouring will forbid the fish in the bowl because it was nullified in the bowl in sixty — end of his words; accordingly if there is concern there is not sixty against that water that remained in the pot, certainly one should forbid all the fish afterward; but opinion of Rama is to rule like the first reason, which is opinion of R"SH that we do not establish issur at all to say the issur was at the end and imparted taste; and Turei Chayim wrote and furthermore since there is to be uncertain in the matter we follow the lenient view, for it is no worse than if we were uncertain in nishpakh the gravy that wherever there is sixty to nullify it in its kind in majority it would only be rabbinic doubt and we follow the lenient view — end of his words; and this is not correct, for since the doubt is whether there is Torah issur here we say it is as certainly Torah issur and as if the issur were clearly at the bottom in the pot — it turns out you have no advantage except in what was nullified one in two, and still there is rabbinic issur and sixty is required, unlike nishpakh the gravy where there are two advantages — one nullification in majority which is from the Torah, and the second nullification in sixty rabbinic; and since there was doubt in nullification which is rabbinic we follow the lenient view in it; and this matter is a general principle in many places that doubt of Torah issur is not considered permitted on account that you have nullification in majority, as we wrote in siman 66 seif 4; and furthermore it is difficult for me here — an egg with embryo or blood is min she'eino mino, for the issur is not its kind with the eggs; and in Hagahot Shach wrote in name of Semag we establish the pan on its presumption and we do not establish issur to say the issur remained at the end, and wrote on this and it seems to me that that which we do not establish on presumption regarding doubt whether he salted slaughtered meat with nevelah or doubt whether there was sixty against the issur not in its kind — that is like legs to the matter — end of his words — evidently also in eggs here there is doubt of Torah issur in any case; and furthermore Turei Chayim brought proofs that everything should join to nullify the issur — I did not know what this applies here, for in eggs we say it is not customary to cook so many eggs that there is majority among everything against those forbidden, and so it seems from words of those who permit in Tur that wrote they are nullified in majority of heter — it seems specifically majority we require; therefore it seems to be stringent like opinion of Rashal that in eggs forbidden and in fish permitted as long as it is possible there is sixty against the little water that remained in the pot. In that matter of fish mentioned in Tur — Beit Yosef's and Rama's explanation is he poured the fish from the pan to the bowl and we are concerned lest the impure one remained there, as written above, and so seems the explanation in Hagahot Shaarei Dura that I brought above, and so in words of Rashal chapter 3 siman 44; and it is wondrous according to this what Tur wrote "the rest in the pan" — he should have said in the bowl; and one must force that Tur deals where the fish remained in the pan only the gravy was poured, and there is concern lest the impure one remained at the bottom of the pan, as written above in name of Issur VeHeter; and regarding practical halachah it is one matter, and Maharach challenged Beit Yosef's explanation that one should forbid what is in the bowl through the pouring that came from the pan, and I already wrote in name of Rashal shortly that there is no difficulty. {Nekudot HaKesef: see there.}
That we do not establish issur, etc. I already wrote opinion of Rashal that we do not establish so, and so is primary to be stringent in eggs; and even according to this opinion one should be stringent if he cooked many eggs in a pot and among them was one that is forbidden and he removed each one with one spoon — concern lest he removed the forbidden one first and the spoon became forbidden and consequently the others afterward; for that which we say we do not establish issur, etc. — that is because we say the issur is in the majority, and so Turei Chayim quoted in name of R"SH who is master of this opinion, unlike in this law. And likewise Issur VeHeter general rule 51, and wrote there further in this law that even though there is no embryo in the forbidden egg only blood, and egg blood is rabbinic and rabbinic doubt for leniency — that is specifically before the issur became known with certainty in the egg; but after the issur became known with certainty once, one should not distinguish between rabbinic doubt and Torah issur doubt, for whatever the Sages enacted like Torah enactment is enactment — end of his words. {Nekudot HaKesef: see there.} And this matter is not clear, for at the beginning of Beitzah we challenge one who explains the reason of issur of an egg laid on Yom Tov from a decree rabbinic from that which is taught and a doubt is forbidden, for it is a rabbinic doubt and for leniency; and if like words of Issur VeHeter he did not challenge anything, for perhaps he says thus that certainly we have a forbidden egg, only there is doubt whether it was exchanged with another or not — and in this we do not follow the lenient view according to words of Issur VeHeter, but rather that also in such a case it was rabbinic doubt and for leniency; and regarding the main law that Issur VeHeter wrote we establish egg blood is rabbinic — it was already explained in siman 66 seif 4, ruling of Rama that wherever one in two is nullified we rely on one who says egg blood is rabbinic; but without this we are stringent like one who says egg blood is Torah law — therefore here that one in two is nullified, for we recognize the forbidden egg, and therefore even in an egg forbidden on account of blood one should forbid all of them if he took them with a spoon each by itself from the pot — for it is Torah doubt lest the spoon became forbidden first, as seems to me for practical halachah:
