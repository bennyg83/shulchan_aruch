And all the more so a community, etc. This is the view of the Rav who learned from the words of the Rashbatz thus. And not like Terumat HaDeshen siman 281. And in my humble opinion the words of the Rashbatz do not imply against the words of Terumat HaDeshen, for there Terumat HaDeshen concludes that if the community enacted thus in cherem it is obvious it is from the Torah like an oath and forbidden; only he is in doubt if they enacted without specification in general enactments whether it is considered from the Torah or rabbinic — see there explained thus (and Beit Yosef copied it briefly). If so, the Rashbatz explicitly deals with an oath which is from the Torah. And so Maharshal distinguishes in the first chapter of Chullin siman 36 explicitly. And furthermore one could say: specifically an individual who swore — Rashbatz says he is forbidden because we follow them according to the language of people, and according to the language of people it is forbidden. Unlike a community that made an enactment — and so Maharshal distinguishes there explicitly. And this is what he wrote: Rabbi Yirmiyah asked — meat of the nose that Israel brought with them to Eretz Yisrael, what is its law? And it stands unresolved. And Rosh wrote (and likewise Rabbenu Yerucham netiv 14 end of part 6): it appears to me there is a practical difference from this question to a person who forbade himself from one of the species from a known time onward, and when the time arrived he had of that species that he was eating and going until the time arrived — whether it is permitted to eat what remains in his hand. Or such as when the court wanted to forbid one matter, as they forbade cheeses of gentiles and cooked foods and the like — whether what he has of that species in a Jew's hand is forbidden — end quote. And I say that according to his words it appears regarding one who forbids himself some matter who says "behold this matter is forbidden to me" or who mentioned in his vow a korban or konam — it is a complete vow from the Torah; or by mentioning the Name — which is an oath. If so, it is forbidden to eat from that species that remained in his hand, for a doubt in Torah law is stringent, since the question was not resolved. But in our unspecified vows where he does not attach with any matter and does not forbid the species upon him but says "behold I vow that I will not eat or drink from this species" — which is a rabbinic vow — it is permitted to eat from that species that remained in his hand, for a doubt in rabbinic law is lenient. But I do not concede to his words in this, for one may not compare vows that we follow according to the language of people to other prohibitions where according to the language of people it is forbidden to eat from that species when the time arrived — end quote. It appears the latter explanation is primary, for there is practical difference where the court wanted to forbid some matter such as cheeses, etc. And since the question was not resolved — if so, in such a case it is permitted, for a doubt in rabbinic law in a rabbinic prohibition such as this is lenient. Nevertheless, specifically when the court wanted to forbid; but if the court decreed in their court place in cherem and curse not to eat this matter — if so, it is from the Torah, for the court has power to penalize even not according to law if their intention is to reinforce a matter. If so, in such a case it is a doubt in Torah law to be stringent — end quote. And I copied all his words because they contain several legal distinctions. Nevertheless, what he challenged against Rosh is nothing, for Rosh only said there is a practical difference regarding a vow for a time; but he does not deal with the matter of following the language of people in vows — for this is straightforward everywhere that we follow vows according to the language of people. Rather he deals where in the place or time the language of people is not thus, for in vows we follow the language of people according to the place and according to the time, as was explained in the previous siman.
