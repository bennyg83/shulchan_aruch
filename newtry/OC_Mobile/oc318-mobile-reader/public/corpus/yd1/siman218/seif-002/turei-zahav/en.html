We follow his intention — these are Rashbatz's words that Beit Yosef brought; and although this law opens with great ones and closes with great ones, nevertheless it is Torah and I must learn, for not only was it difficult for him from Talmudic words but from his own words as we will explain: that which he wrote that we follow intention is astounding — for we hold like Shmuel that mouth and heart must agree, as written above siman 210; and what do we care about his intention since he explicitly said he will not make — implying what is already made is not included; and explicitly regarding "you shall not make for yourself a graven image" — I have only "you shall not make"; from where not to maintain what is already made? Scripture teaches "it shall not be for you" — behold what is already made is not included; and in Teshuvot HaRashba siman 277 he wrote we judge according to intention even if against his utterance — clearly meaning we do not interpret the language according to its plain meaning, as above regarding wool-wearing prohibition per Rosh; but nevertheless it can be interpreted according to intention, unlike when it completely contradicts as here; and what Rashbatz brought as proof from one who vows wool shall not go upon him in seif 1 that we follow intention — does not apply, for there the language is not contradicted at all, unlike here; and moreover he contradicts himself, for at the end of siman 208 Beit Yosef in his book brings Rashbatz: one who says he will not marry a woman within three years — if he said language implying he will not perform marriage with her and he transgressed and married, it is like one who swore on a loaf and ate it — he is asked and they permit him; but if he said language implying she will not be his wife within three years they do not permit — and they ruled in Shulchan Aruch there; behold before us that we follow the language's meaning and do not say "what he said — he will not perform marriage" means she will not be his wife; so too here one who said he will not make wine does not mean he will not have wine — it is one matter; and moreover it is a fortiori: there, where it is close that he did not insist on the marriage time — for what does he care — but only that he not have a collar hanging on his neck and be busy with his wife's needs, and this matter he included in performing marriage since marriage is the beginning and he took the beginning — nevertheless we say we follow the language as plain; all the more here where he did not intend to lose what he already has for fear of danger but only not to enter anew into making wine — i.e., preventing profit — therefore he said specifically "will not make"; all the more we follow the language and only what left his mouth is forbidden; and moreover if two kinds were dangerous to sell to a gentile and he swore on one — would we follow intention that he fears danger and forbid the second? This is certainly not so; here too are two kinds — preventing profit and saving from loss — as we find this distinction regarding lost objects in Choshen Mishpat and mourning siman 380 seif 6 that one is not like the other; here too likewise.
And all the more a community, etc. — the Rav learned this from Rashbatz's ruling that Shulchan Aruch already mentioned; and I say even if no place were given to that ruling as we recalled, nevertheless this law is true and explicit in Rosh's words first chapter of Chullin on the question the Gemara asks regarding nostrils of cattle that Israelites brought with them to Eretz Yisrael — what is the law; Rosh explained it emerges regarding one who forbids himself one species from a known time onward, and when the time arrived he had of that species he ate until the time — whether he may eat what remained; or if a court wanted to forbid something — whether what he has of that species in an Israelite's hand is forbidden — end; and since it remained in his pouch we are stringent biblically; yet we can say on the contrary from there is proof to permit here, since this remained in his pouch and every rabbinic doubt is lenient, and here too is rabbinic, for there is no prohibition except from the community's enactment; and so I saw in Teshuvot HaRashba siman 281 who brought in the name of one of the great ones who ruled regarding a community enactment not to sell meat above the price they set — and one had slaughtered meat to sell dear since he had it before the enactment — he brought proof from this question and wrote in the name of Baal Teshuvot HaRashba that since it was merely an enactment and not an oath; but whenever they made a ban it is biblical and we are stringent; and he wrote afterward: although I have some hesitation on that ruling of Semag who wrote one who swears not to enter the community enactment is considered a vain oath — apparently community enactment is also biblical — he concludes that in vows that are only rabbinic, such as on something without substance, etc., it is a rabbinic doubt for leniency; therefore according to this there is no place for the "all the more" Rama wrote here regarding community enactment — for first Shulchan Aruch wrote regarding one who swore and an oath is biblical — how does he derive from this regarding enactment that is not from Torah; and although Teshuvot HaRashba wrote he has some hesitation on enactment, nevertheless it is clear there his intent is not to dispute and be stringent on enactment and nullify the practical ruling of that great one; and indeed I already wrote in siman 228 seif 33 to resolve Teshuvot HaRashba's hesitation well — specifically where it is to fence a matter of sin it is biblical, unlike where it is to save money or other reasons not related to sin. I again saw in Rashal first chapter of Chullin siman 36 who brought this Rosh explanation on the question we mentioned — Rosh's first answer that it emerges regarding one who forbids himself, etc. — follows that it is a rabbinic doubt, for whenever a person forbids himself in vow language saying he will not eat a certain thing without attaching a forbidden matter it is only rabbinic; and he disputes Rosh on this, for in vows one follows people's language and according to people's language it is forbidden to eat from this species when the time arrives; therefore the second answer of Rosh applies regarding when a court wanted to forbid, etc. — rabbinic doubt for leniency; nevertheless specifically when a court wanted to forbid, but if they decreed with ban and curse not to eat this thing it is biblical, for a court has power to ban and excommunicate even beyond the law if their intent is to fence; if so it is a biblical doubt and stringent; and according to this what was done in days when sages of Russia gathered the majority of the people not to drink ordinary Israelite wine unless notable witnesses testify to its kashrut and they limited time for their decree — all that already remained in their hands is also forbidden, for it is a biblical doubt — end Rashal; behold in enactment without ban he clearly permits in doubt; and since in Rama's case it was rabbinic doubt we are lenient and there is no prohibition unless the enactment was with community ban; and even here there is a practical difference if that remaining thing became mixed in some mixture where there is doubt — it is a double doubt since the prohibition itself is not clear but only from what remained in his pouch in the Gemara; and when a doubtful prohibition became mixed — when is it double doubt — was explained in siman 110 above end of seif 9 that two mixtures are required; unlike if the source were Rashbatz's words and the prohibition were certain — it would not be called double doubt except with three mixtures as written in siman 110 there; and truth will teach his way that this is only doubtful prohibition as Rosh explicitly explained — so appears clear to me.
