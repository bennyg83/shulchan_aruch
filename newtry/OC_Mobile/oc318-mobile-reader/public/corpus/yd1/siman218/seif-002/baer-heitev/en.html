By clear presumption. Turei Zahav explained: if the language indicates another matter, even though from the side of heart-intention one could understand that he had some matter in mind through which the meaning of the language would be nullified — we do not say thus. For since it is for the benefit of his fellow, his fellow gains from the side of the giver's language — for in this we do not require that his mouth and heart be equal unless it is clear presumption. Unlike one who vows between himself and himself — we nullify the language according to its meaning and interpret it on another matter, like that case of one who was laden, etc. But in truth, if he gave a gift to his fellow and said language that has two meanings — certainly the choice is in the hand of the giver to interpret it in a matter that is good for him. And the burden of proof is upon the one who extracts from his fellow (and in this manner understand this gloss) — end quote:
