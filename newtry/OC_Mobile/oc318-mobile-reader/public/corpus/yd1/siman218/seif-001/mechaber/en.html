How we follow the intention of the one who vows; it contains six se’ifim. Regarding anyone who vows or swears, we look at the matters on account of which he swore or vowed, and we learn from them to what he intended, and we follow the subject matter and not the literal meaning of the speech. How so? If he was carrying a load of wool or flax, sweated, and its odor was foul, and he swore or vowed that wool or flax would never come upon him — behold, he is permitted to wear wool or flax garments and to cover himself with them; he is forbidden only to drape them over his back. If he was wearing wool garments, suffered distress in wearing them, and swore or vowed that wool would never come upon him — he is forbidden to wear wool, but permitted to carry a load on it, and permitted to cover himself with wool shorn from the living, for he intended only regarding wool garments. And likewise all similar cases.
{Rama: And all this is specifically when one vows between himself and himself; but when one vows to his fellow, wherever the language of the vow includes, we follow it, unless there is clear presumptive evidence (so it appears in Rivash and in Ramban).}
