(Siman 218, Taz, seif katan 3) He asks, etc. He challenges the Tashbetz and the Mechaber, but there is no difficulty. We do not follow Shmuel's requirement that one's mouth and heart agree except where one resolved in his heart to say 'wheat bread' and actually said 'barley bread.' Here he expressly said that he would not make it for sale, demonstrating that even his spoken words focus on selling. We therefore follow his intent, and the word 'make' is not literal. This also rejects the proof from 'You shall not make for yourself a graven image,' because here he said, 'he shall not make for sale.' His question from one who says he will not marry a woman within three years is likewise no difficulty. The phrase, 'if he used language whose meaning is that he will not make a marriage,' means language that can only be interpreted as referring to marriage, such as where we know that his intent concerned marriage alone. His comparison between preventing profit and avoiding loss as two distinct kinds is also entirely inapposite. Later I found that Responsa Maharit, siman 7, likewise rejects the Tashbetz's view based on Shmuel's rule. In my humble opinion the explanation is as I have written. I also found in Responsa Maharashdam, siman 228, that the Terumas HaDeshen disagrees with the Tashbetz. In my humble opinion this is incorrect, as I wrote in Sifsei Kohen, seif katan 2. He also cites there that my teachers Mahari Mintz and Mahardach disagree. See there; the matter requires study.
