Mabit writes that a man who vowed after a dangerous pleasure voyage never again to enter a ship with his wife was understood to forbid only such leisure travel, not necessary relocation. He similarly interprets a vow not to sit, eat, or drink together according to the dispute that prompted it, permitting later social eating once the parties had separated their households and expenses.
