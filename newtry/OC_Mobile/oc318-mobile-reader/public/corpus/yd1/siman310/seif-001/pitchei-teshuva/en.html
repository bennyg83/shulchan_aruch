It is forbidden. See Responsa Brit Avraham, Yoreh Deah, siman 14, letter 8, who writes that the prohibition after the fact applies only where the firstborn was slaughtered without authorization of an expert. But where an individual expert examined his own animal, and the reason he may not do so is merely suspicion, we do not find that the meat is prohibited after the fact.
The same applies nowadays. See Responsa Meir Netivim, siman 42, that utensils used with this meat require kashering like utensils used with any prohibited food.
