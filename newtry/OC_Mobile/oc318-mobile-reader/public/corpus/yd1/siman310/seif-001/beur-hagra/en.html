One who slaughters, etc. Like Rabbi Meir, for the halakhah follows Rabbi Meir in his decrees, as stated at the beginning of Ketubot chapter 5, 57a. Although this applies specifically to his decrees and not his fines, as stated at the beginning of Yevamot chapter 4 from the case of a pregnant woman, etc., and as Tosafot wrote there, here when it says Rabbi Meir imposed a penalty, it is not literally a fine but a decree, as stated there that Rabbi Meir holds we decree, etc. A proof is from the Mishnah's wording, “since it was slaughtered”; if otherwise, it should have said, “and he slaughtered it,” unlike Rashi in Yom Tov chapter 3, 27a s.v. “lest,” etc., and s.v. “Mishnah,” etc. Ramban. The Rosh likewise proved from the phrase “because of those who change,” indicating that it is because of a decree. His proof is not understandable, for is it stronger than what the Gemara says explicitly, “a decree,” etc., and nevertheless Tosafot and Rashi explain that it is a fine? But Tosafot there s.v. “Rabbi Meir,” etc., ruled like Rabbi Yehudah and hold that it is a fine, following Rashi's view. The sugya also follows Rabbi Yehudah, for the question “they asked” is according to Rabbi Yehudah; see Rashi s.v. “false,” etc., and there, “shall we say we learned,” etc. To me, however, from there is a proof for the ruling of the geonim that the halakhah follows Rabbi Meir, from the Gemara's statement “perhaps,” and this is not difficult, etc., according to their approach.
