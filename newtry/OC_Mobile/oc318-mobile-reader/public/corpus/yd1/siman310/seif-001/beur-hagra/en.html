One who slaughters. This follows R. Meir because the halachah follows R. Meir in his preventive decrees, as stated at the beginning of chapter 5 of Ketubot 57a and elsewhere. Although this principle applies specifically to his decrees and not his penalties, as stated at the beginning of chapter 4 of Yevamot from 37a, where Tosafot explain the distinction, the Gemara's use here of the expression "R. Meir penalized him" is not literal. It is a preventive decree, as the Gemara itself explains that R. Meir holds we make a decree. Proof also comes from the Mishnah's wording "since it was slaughtered"; if this were a penalty for the person's act, it should have said "since he slaughtered it." This is unlike Rashi in chapter 3 of Yom Tov 27a. Ramban likewise understands it as a decree. Rosh argues from the phrase concerning animals likely to be exchanged, showing a decree. That proof alone is not decisive because the Gemara explicitly calls it a decree and nevertheless Rashi and Tosafot can understand its legal form as a penalty. Tosafot there rule like R. Yehudah and treat the matter as a penalty, following Rashi. The flow of the Gemara also follows R. Yehudah, since the unresolved question is framed according to his position. In my view, however, that very discussion supports the Geonim's ruling that the halachah follows R. Meir, because the Gemara ultimately explains that the cases need not conflict, in accordance with their approach.
