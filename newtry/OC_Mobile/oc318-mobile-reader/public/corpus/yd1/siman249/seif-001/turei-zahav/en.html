Certainly he may not use tithe money to pay taxes, even if taxes taken by force can count as charity, because this would be paying a personal debt from charity funds. Supporting one's minor children is also called charity, yet one may not use tithe funds for that ordinary obligation. One may use tithe money to purchase synagogue honors if he intended this at the time of purchase, because the money goes to charity and the owner's incidental benefit is permitted. If he first incurred the obligation without that intention and later seeks to pay from tithe, it is payment of a debt and forbidden. Derishah cites a responsum permitting tithe funds for serving as sandak, bringing a groom to the canopy, or buying books to study and lend to others, where he could not otherwise perform the mitzvah. Books bought this way should be marked as purchased from tithe so heirs do not treat them as private property. [Citation 40.]
