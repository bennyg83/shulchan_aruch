And this fifth that they mentioned, etc. It appears that the same rule applies to the ordinary person's tithe. In the first year he gives one tenth of the principal, and afterward one tenth of the profit. This is also implied by the Perishah, seif 3.
One should not use his tithe money, etc. Maharshal and the Derishah write in the name of a responsum of Maharam that any mitzvah opportunity that comes to him, such as serving as the sandak, assisting a bridegroom and bride in entering the chuppah, and the like, or purchasing sefarim from which to study and lending them to others for study, may be funded from tithe money if he otherwise could not afford and would not perform that mitzvah. In the responsa of Maharam of Rothenburg, Prague edition, siman 75, he writes that one may distribute his tithes to his adult children whom he is not obligated to support. One may even give to his father if he is poor, because of the mitzvah of honoring one's father, and all the more so to his children. This applies where the city has no ordinance requiring payment into the communal charity fund. It implies that this is permitted even if he can support them from another source, for this itself constitutes charity, as stated below in siman 251, seif 3.
