That he profited. And if he had profit in one business and loss in one business — Beit HaTov of the Rav Mahari"t z"l wrote in name of Shaar Ephraim siman 84: if at the time he had the loss he had an accounting that he had a loss — if so what he profited afterward is a different matter and does not fill the loss with the profit; but if he had no accounting until year's end and calculated all accounts — profit and loss of the whole year — then the loss is filled from the profit — see there; and similarly wrote in the responsum of Chavat Yair in the omissions to page 213 — see there; and see in the responsum of Noda BiYehudah tinyana siman 194 who did not rule so except if profit and loss were in one year — always deduct one from the other; but in two years do not deduct — see there in the gloss from the author's son; and see further in responsum Chut HaShani end siman 90 from this. And what Beit HaTov wrote in name of Chavat Yair: if he travels to market, etc. — also expense is counted as principal; and see in She'elat Yaavetz part 1 siman 6 who doubted this, and his view is not to deduct expense in any matter. And he also wrote regarding dowry money that fathers practice dividing maaser according to what is straight in their eyes — they do well, and the sons emerge with this yotzei yedei chovah, and they profit thereby that it is not fixed as obligation to separate maaser from here on; for if they did so themselves it becomes a vow; however this is only when the fathers commit with tithed money; but if they put in plainly such-and-such money for the couple and afterward at wedding time they take maaser — one may say the fathers have no right in maaser unless with the couple's knowledge, for it is their mitzvah; and perhaps since it is practiced with intent that thus they committed from the outset to be for the fathers tovat hanaah in them — see there. And see in Shelah page 262 side 1 who wrote that also from inheritance he inherited from his father he must give maaser, even though his father was careful all his days with tithes — nevertheless he must give anew — see there. And see in responsum Noda BiYehudah kamma part 14 siman 73: one was asked who practiced maaser kesafim all his days and had a special ledger written with all his income how much maaser is due, and likewise registered distribution of charity from maaser money; and sometimes he precedes giving to the poor and afterward when he profited deducts from maaser. After his death the accounting was found that maaser owes him a sum — whether the heirs can deduct this from their maaser; and he answered: even while his father was alive in his lifetime not so clear he could deduct what he already preceded to the poor; and the Rema's words below siman 257:5 are regarding lender and poor person by way of loan, not by way of gift. However since they practiced so, one may say stam like his explanation that he gives him in a manner that he will repay from his maaser — and all this while alive; but not his sons. Indeed if it is time of distress for them there is room to be lenient also in this; but at least the heir must separate maaser and afterward withhold for himself — see there; and see in Sefer Mishnat Chachamim end Laws of Foundations of the Torah what is written on this matter. And see further in She'elat Yaavetz there who was uncertain regarding a woman who brings her husband money she has herself that she inherited and the like in a manner that her father's permission does not control it — whether the husband is obligated to tithe the nitzav money and melog property; and he ruled leniently that she does something of mitzvah with them; but he is not obligated to give maaser to the poor — nevertheless one who is stringent — good for him. And he also wrote: if the woman died and the husband inherits her ketubah — requires tithing inherited property from her; however if he inherits only Torah ketubah — not obligated to tithe; and there in omissions he wrote: perhaps there is room to be stringent in what was worth selling in tovat hanaah — per this appraisal he tithes — see there. And see in responsum Arba'ah Turim Even siman 6: one was asked who held his maaser money with him until it happened for suitable poor or his relatives to give them; and meanwhile he took out the money for his needs, and afterward one of his relatives wrote him to send him a sum to assist his daughter's marriage; and he had no cash and sent merchandise by a trustworthy man and the merchandise was stolen on the way — whether the sender is liable or not; and he answered: if this man had not used the money for his needs, only those coins he separated for maaser — he would send to the poor person and it was lost on the way — certainly he is not liable; and even if he bought merchandise with those coins to send to the poor in order to profit — he would also be exempt, and we do not say he was negligent in buying the merchandise; but since he used the money for his needs, and here the accident came through negligence — for if he sent money near Lod certainly it would not be stolen, for he could hide it — if so the sender is liable at least from laws of Heaven to give other money to this poor person — see there.
