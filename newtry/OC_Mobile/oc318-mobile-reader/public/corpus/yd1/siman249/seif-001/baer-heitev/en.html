Mitzvah. Maharshal and the Derishah wrote in name of the responsum of Maharam: every mitzvah that comes to his hand, such as being a baal berit or bringing bride and groom to the chuppah and the like; and likewise to buy books to learn in them and lend them to others to learn in them — if he did not have means and was not doing that mitzvah, he may buy from maaser; and likewise to distribute his tithes to his adult sons whom he is not obligated to support — permitted, for even for his father it is permitted to give if he is poor because of honor of his father {Rama: and all the more for his sons, for permitted where there is no communal regulation to give to the charity box, and even if he has means to support them from elsewhere, for this is charity}. And Taz wrote it is forbidden to pay taxes with them; and regarding buying mitzvot in the synagogue with maaser money — it appears to me if at the time of buying the mitzvot his intent was on this it is permitted, for the money goes to charity; and even though he benefits — behold in every maaser there is tovat hanaah for the owners, unlike if at the time of buying the mitzvot he did not have intent and afterward wants to pay from maaser — this is like paying his debt from maaser; and also when buying a book with maaser money he must write on it that this is from maaser so his sons know this and not seize it for themselves — end quote.
