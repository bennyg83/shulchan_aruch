Mitzvah. Maharshal and the Derishah write in the name of a responsum of Maharam that any mitzvah that comes to his hand, such as serving as baal brit, bringing a groom and bride to the chupah, and the like, or buying books to learn from and lend to others to learn from, if he would not have had the means and would not have performed that mitzvah, he may buy it from maaser. It is also permitted to distribute his maaser to his adult sons, whom he is not obligated to support; even to his father it is permitted to give if he is poor, because of honoring his father. All the more so his sons, where there is no city enactment requiring donation to the charity fund, and even if he has the means to support them from elsewhere, for this is tzedakah. The Taz writes that it is forbidden to pay taxes from it. Regarding buying mitzvot in the synagogue with maaser money, it appears to me that if at the time of buying the mitzvah he had this in mind, it is permitted, for the money goes to tzedakah. Although he benefits, every maaser has tovat hanaah for the owner. But if at the time of buying the mitzvah he did not have this in mind, and afterward wants to pay from maaser, it is like paying his debt from maaser. Also, when buying a book with maaser, he must write on it that it is from maaser, so that his children will know this and not take possession of it as theirs. Thus his language.
