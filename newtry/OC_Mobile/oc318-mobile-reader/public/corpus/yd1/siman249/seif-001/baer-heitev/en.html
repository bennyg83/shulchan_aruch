A mitzvah. Maharshal and Derishah write in the name of a responsum of Maharam that any mitzvah that comes to one's hand, such as serving as a baal bris, bringing a bride and groom to the chuppah, and similar mitzvos, and likewise buying sefarim to study from and to lend to others for study, may be paid from maaser funds if he otherwise lacks the means and would not perform that mitzvah. He may likewise distribute his maaser to adult children whom he is not obligated to support. Indeed, he may even give it to his father if the father is poor, because of honoring his father; all the more so he may give it to his children where the city has no regulation requiring the money to be placed in a communal charity fund, even if he could support them from other funds, because this support is tzedakah. Taz writes that one may not use maaser to pay taxes. Regarding purchasing honors or mitzvos in the synagogue with maaser money, it appears to me that if at the time he buys the mitzvah he has in mind to pay with maaser, it is permitted, because the money goes to tzedakah. Although he benefits from the purchase, the owner always retains the benefit of choosing the recipient of maaser. If, however, when purchasing the mitzvah he had no such intention and only afterward wishes to reimburse himself from maaser, this is like paying an existing debt from maaser and is forbidden. Likewise, when one buys a sefer with maaser funds he should write in it that it was purchased from maaser, so that his children know this and do not treat it as their private inheritance.
