Mitzvah. Beit HaTov (and apparently it seems Maharil and Maharam dispute each other; but per what is written in Be'er HaGolah ot 5: specifically a mitzvah he is obligated in anyway, etc. — one may say they do not dispute at all; and see in responsum Chatam Sofer siman 231 who wrote Be'er HaGolah erred here, for in Maharil Laws of Rosh Hashanah and responsum siman 56 he wrote explicitly the reason because maaser belongs to the poor, etc.; but nevertheless there is no dispute here, for Maharil and Rema deal where he practiced also to separate maaser from his profit and give to the poor, and again whatever he separates is automatically for the poor, and it is not such a thing to buy from it a lamp for the synagogue even if he is not obligated in them; but if from the beginning of practicing maaser kesafim he stipulated he can do from it a mitzvah matter — Maharil agrees to Maharam) (see there in the next siman he did not write so, only that Maharil holds maaser kesafim is literally d'oraisa, etc.). And he wrote there in the matter of the question: one who married his son to a Torah scholar's daughter and obligated to give the Torah scholar every week three gold coins that the couple would be sustained at his table six years so the son would learn by his father-in-law the aforementioned Torah scholar — and now it is heavy for him and he wants to give this from maaser money. He raised: if immediately at time of marriage his intent was to pay from maaser money — permitted; and nevertheless from being good he may divide maaser and give half to poor people generally and half to his son — example for that which we learned chapter 8 of Peah 46 "he would save," etc.; but if at time of his obligation he did not have this intent — a person does not pay his debts from maaser (and this accords with Taz's words) — see there; and further in Shach there is a typo: what he wrote "even for his father," etc., because of honor of his father — should read "if not because of," etc. And there in siman 232 they discussed one who died and left books written on them they are from maaser money from his relatives, and he would lend them to others all his life; and the guardians over his poor orphans lacking bread want to sell them to sustain the orphans and claim he wrote only all his life and after death they can sell them; and he elaborated on this and raised the law is with the guardians — see there. And what he wrote "and likewise to buy books," etc. — see in Sefer Mishnat Chachamim Laws of Foundations of the Torah page 19 side 1 who wrote in name of his father the Gaon z"l: if he bought books with them — forbidden to sell them even to learn Torah and marry a woman; and even though they permitted a Torah scroll to sell to learn and marry a woman — that is a Torah scroll of an individual as is clear in Orach Chayyim siman 153; and these books many acquired rights in them and it is a mitzvah to lend them to others — if so he has no permission to sell them; and he z"l wrote on this: if he stipulated with the buyer that he will lend them to others and he does not sell him except tovat hanaah he has in them — uncertain if permitted to sell in this manner — see there. And see in responsum Shevut Yaakov part 2 siman 85 who gave a reason for the custom at bride and groom that they pay from maaser money the musicians and servers of the wedding feast. And he wrote: nevertheless forbidden to take from him wage of server and maidservant, although enough for him per his work — with a small maidservant whose wage is small, he only wants a large maidservant who supervises more in matters of prohibition and permission — nevertheless forbidden — see there. And see in responsum Chavat Yair there in omissions at the end of the book who wrote one asked him to instruct him: he practiced separating maaser from profit; and his hands were negligent, for it became known to him clearly he gives to charity and sustenance to his sons and daughters which counts as charity more than a fifth of profit; and he answered: he does not act well and does not emerge yotzei yedei maaser thus, per the statement of Chazal "do not tithe by estimation excessively"; and Tosefet Yom Tov wrote: not specifically "do not tithe by estimation excessively" — likewise even once it is forbidden — end quote; and see in Sefer Mishnat Chachamim there who disputes him: even if we say maaser kesafim has law of maaser ani — nevertheless regarding separating by estimation it is not like maaser tevuah, and he can add if he wishes, and he acts well if he added — see there.
