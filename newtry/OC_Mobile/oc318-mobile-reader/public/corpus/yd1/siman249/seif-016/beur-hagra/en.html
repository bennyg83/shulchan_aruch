There is an authority who says, etc. This is based on the Yerushalmi at the end of Peah and at the end of the fifth chapter of Shekalim. Rabbi Chama bar Chanina and Rabbi Hoshaya the Great were walking among the synagogues of Lod. Rabbi Chama bar Chanina said to Rabbi Hoshaya the Great, "How much money my ancestors invested here!" He replied, "How many lives your ancestors lost here! Were there no people who could have labored in Torah?" Rabbi Avun built a gate for the great study hall. Rabbi Mani came down to them, and Rabbi Avun said, "See what I built." Rabbi Mani replied with the verse, "Israel forgot its Maker and built palaces." Were there no people who could have labored in Torah? The Tashbetz had the reading, "or sick people lying upon the refuse heap." Maharik inferred from this that for other charitable needs a synagogue takes precedence, but this inference is not compelling, especially according to our reading. See Tosafot, Bava Batra 9a, s.v. Shene'emar, etc.; all the more so regarding a synagogue.
The practice, etc. The Sifrei at the end of Parashat Shoftim expounds: "Atone for Your people" refers to the living; "whom You redeemed" refers to the dead. This teaches that the dead require atonement. See also the end of the first chapter of Horayot. Charity atones, as stated in Bava Batra 9a: "When the Beit HaMikdash stood, etc." It also saves from Gehinnom, as stated there, 10a, and Scripture says, "And He will strengthen your bones." See there, 9b.
