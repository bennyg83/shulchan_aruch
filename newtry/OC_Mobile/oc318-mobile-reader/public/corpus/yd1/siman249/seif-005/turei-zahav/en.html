From the wage of the giver. So in the Gemara: great is the act more than the doer; and it does not mean his wage is more than the doer's by coercion, for this doer by coercion lost his right as written in seif 3; rather it means more than other doers of charity even willingly; and in the Tur it is written "like the wage of the doer" — plainly it is a typo; and so I saw in old manuscripts; and in the Perishah wants to reconcile: in the Gemara it says more than the doer by coercion — and this is puzzling from what we proved.
