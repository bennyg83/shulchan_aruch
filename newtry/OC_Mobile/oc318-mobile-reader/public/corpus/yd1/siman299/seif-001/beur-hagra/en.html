And not, etc. This is stated in the Tosefta there and cited by the Rosh MiShantz there: when he brought, etc., but one who makes, etc. In the Gemara: a garment in which kilayim was lost, etc. This is the meaning of the Rama's gloss, that even if it was lost, etc., and of the Shulchan Aruch's statement, even, etc. This matter, however, is not because they are threads, for even beforehand it is forbidden, as stated in the Jerusalem Talmud cited above: wool and linen that he mixed are forbidden; how so, etc. There, Abba said in the name of R' Yirmiyah: wool and linen that he mixed are nullified. Rav said they are forbidden in their mixture. What is the dispute? Rav speaks where he made them into a garment by themselves, whereas R' Yirmiyah speaks where he wishes to mix them with others. See Tosafos, Niddah, daf 61b, s.v. 'a garment,' etc., and the Rosh there. The words of the Shulchan Aruch are difficult. Likewise, the words of Rambam and the Shulchan Aruch cited above, who wrote 'because they are not,' etc., are difficult. The Ra'avad already challenged this and wrote: This is an error. Is wool in linen nullified in a majority? He should have said because it is not shua, spun, and woven, etc. In truth, it is not shua, spun, and woven at all. The words of Kesef Mishnah there are even more difficult, and he erred greatly there.
