Public needs. Meaning, even for a discretionary purpose, in accordance with Rabbeinu Tam there, and not in accordance with Ri Migash, who wrote that this applies only to a mitzvah purpose. The Rosh brought proof for Rabbeinu Tam from the first chapter of Arakhin 6a, where it is stated that once the donor's identity has been forgotten, the funds may be redirected even to a discretionary purpose. See siman 259, seif 3, and the Yerushalmi at the end of the second chapter of Shekalim: 'One does not protest against the administrators'; see there. There is no proof from there, however, because that case too concerns another mitzvah purpose. Nor is there proof from the passage in Arakhin, as Ri Migash wrote, because that case is different: anyone who donates to a synagogue donates subject to the community's discretion, for they may even sell the synagogue itself in order to drink beer with the proceeds, as stated at the beginning of the fourth chapter of Megillah.
Even though they did not, etc. As stated there: Rav Ashi said, 'I stipulated, etc.' This is certainly true of the community itself.
The same applies to a charity administrator, etc. His words are difficult, for a charity administrator is certainly forbidden to redirect the funds, as stated in Bava Batra there: 'At first the master would do, etc.,' and in Arakhin there: 'Once it has come into the hand of the charity administrator, etc.' Rather, the case must be one in which the community granted him authority, as stated there: 'Once he heard what Shmuel had said to him, etc.'
(Supplement) The same applies to a charity administrator, etc. As stated in the Yerushalmi, third chapter of Megillah, halakhah 1: R. Chiyya bar Abba went abroad, and they gave him coins to distribute to orphans and widows. He went and distributed them to Torah scholars. Must he set aside other funds in their place? R. Bava bar Mamal said that he must set aside replacements; R. Zeira said that he must set aside replacements; R. Ila said that he need not set aside replacements; and R. Yasa said in the name of R. Elazar that he need not set aside replacements, etc. A Gaon likewise explained the statement in the first chapter of Bava Batra 8b, 'and to redirect it, etc.,' to mean that afterward he must provide other funds in their place for the original purpose. See Responsa of the Rosh, rule 13, section 8. Nevertheless, the conclusion of the Yerushalmi is that he need not set aside replacements. (End of supplement.)
The same applies to an individual, etc. As stated in the Tosefta, second chapter of Megillah: one who pledges charity, before the intended recipients have acquired it, etc., may alter it only with their consent. This is also stated in siman 251, seif 5; see there.
Likewise, if he specified, etc. This follows from the statement at the end of the eighth chapter of Bava Kamma concerning the poor of Pumbedita, etc. The Rambam wrote that the funds had already been acquired by them, etc., indicating that they may not be redirected. See there, and the Tosefta cited by the Rashbam in Bava Batra 43b and by the Rosh there, section 56: 'One who says, Give, etc.'
And if it is impossible for him, etc. As stated in Bava Kamma 111a: 'With what case are we dealing? Where they departed, etc.,' and the Gemara explains what the ruling teaches, etc. Here too, since they departed without instructing him otherwise, they waived their claim in his favor, allowing him to act as he wishes. See there.
