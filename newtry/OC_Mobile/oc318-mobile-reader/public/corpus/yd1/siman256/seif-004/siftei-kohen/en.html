The difficulty is that the Rosh explicitly wrote and we learn in Yerushalmi "the poor may give to the poor", etc., and "they do not protest the hands of the administrators" — meaning if they see there is need in the matter and they come to change, they do not protest their hands — end quote. And that of "the poor may give to the poor" the Rosh explicitly establishes there in the case that occurred that they needed to collect for the need of the poor, etc. Rather certainly Yerushalmi speaks when the administrators see there is need in the matter — and that is what the Rosh wrote: if they see there is need in the matter, etc. — meaning even though they cannot change in a case that occurred by chance, etc., even for a mitzvah matter, nevertheless when the administrators see there is need in the matter they may change for a mitzvah matter. On this Beit Yosef wrote: it seems his view is that the Yerushalmi of Shekalim when they come to change for a matter that is the need of the poor specifically — therefore the administrators may change when they see there is need in the matter; but for optional matters not — and this is clear.
