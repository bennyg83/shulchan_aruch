And specifically charity that they were not accustomed to give, etc. The Mordekhai in the name of Or Zaru'a wrote: specifically when they decreed on them a fast because of drought or because of a plague of wild animals or because of distress, and they decreed on themselves charity and also on the townspeople who come to them to give charity to annul the decree through repentance, prayer and charity — then they must give to the gabbaim of the city because of suspicion, and must also give to the shamash of the city where there is a shamash. But charity they are accustomed to give always and is fixed in every place — even if those townspeople in their town were not prevented from giving it, even though now they come here they need neither gabbaim nor shamash but bring them to their homes. Therefore those townspeople who come to communities for Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur and mention souls and vow charities — there is no suspicion at all, for they will certainly give; also they do not give them to the shamash of the city, for this charity does not come from the power of that city but from the force of statute and custom — therefore they bring their charity to their settlements and do with it as they wish — end quote Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe. However it seems if they took etrogim or lulavim in this city or similar they are obligated to give to this city and cannot return to take them to their city, for they acquired the mitzvah in this synagogue; also otherwise there would be loss to this charity that the people of that city would buy this mitzvah — and so is the custom. I again found thus in responsum of Mahari"t part 1 siman 60 page 129 — see there. Kolbo wrote siman 116 that there are ancient enactments of village people who have no minyan who come where there are communities and bring wax candles that are lit on Yom Kippur — the remainder is left in that synagogue where they were lit on Yom Kippur; and if they did so two years, one leaves them where lit and the other lights in his city where he prays individually — end quote.
One who has other money, etc. — the wording of R. Toviah in the Gemara: you are not sworn on it after you have no share in the profit; and even if the enactment of your place preceded the enactment of his place I would tell you to send it to him and he will bring it to the treasury — if they did not specify in their enactment that they separate tithe from all they profit whether it comes to their hand or to another's hand. And so it seems to me regarding one who lends money for half the profit — he must pay half the tithe to the owner of the money and he is obligated to bring it to the treasury since half the profit is his — end quote. Beit Yosef brings it in siman 258 and the rabbi wrote his words according to his understanding, and after him the Taz followed; but in truth it is not so and the matter is simple that what he wrote "and if they did not specify in their enactment", etc. — he meant even if they did not specify in the enactment of the place of the lender; and so explained Maharam of Tikotin in the margin of Mordekhai chapter HaGozel batra, and similarly in Sefer Gedulat Mordekhai there that in the second version he explicitly wrote even if they did not specify, etc. Therefore what emerges from Mordekhai and Gemara is: one who engaged with another's money — if all the profit is for the lender and he has no share in the profit, he sends everything to the lender's hand and he gives the tithe there, even if the enactment of the place of the engaged preceded the enactment of the place of the lender, and even if they did not specify in the place of the lender that they separate from all others profit from their money; and if lent for half, he pays half the profit to the lender and half the profit the engaged gives in his city — understand well.
