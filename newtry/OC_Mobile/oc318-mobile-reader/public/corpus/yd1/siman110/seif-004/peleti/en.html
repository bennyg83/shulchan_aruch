"And it became mixed with others, etc." — It is nullified in the majority, etc. All the acharonim wondered: behold there is safek in itself and safek in mixtures and it is not double safek according to Rashba and the Mechaber, as Rama wrote; and in truth one can force to explain that it deals in a manner where it is not recognizable — only in a minority of stores it is found; by way of parable: a treifah lung but the animal is not known — if so by Torah law one in two is nullified, only d'rabbanan it is not nullified and it is only kevua d'rabbanan; and in this safek in itself, etc. does not apply — since the first safek is not d'oraisa — and it is forced in the language of Shulchan Aruch; and Shach answered that kevua is a novelty, and you have in it only its novelty; and see Pri Chadash who challenged him from the reasoning of what he wrote kevua and what he wrote other treifot — he spoke well at first glance that we have no right to invent this law from our hearts without proof; however in my view for practical halachah this law we learn from the Gemara that we say there in the incident of that butcher — in the latter version that they said: because of this fool that he intended to cause pain, all the slaughterhouses are forbidden; and we investigate the reason: to cause pain — if not, all slaughterhouses are forbidden; R' Avraham: slaughterhouses and Jewish butchers — meat found in a gentile's hand is permitted, etc.; and Rashba wrote that it deals with those who bought from the slaughterhouses where treifot were already known in the slaughterhouses; and Rashba challenged: if so it is kevua and obviously forbidden — why did he challenge from slaughterhouses and Jewish butchers that it is permitted? There there is no fixedness unlike in that incident; and Rashba resolved: who knows that this butcher sold to the other slaughterhouses — perhaps only to those slaughterhouses he said he sold — see there; and it is difficult: behold the butchers are kevua as Tosafot and Maharsha wrote — see there — and it is obvious; therefore the slaughterhouses that bought from them are also called kevua, for they bought from a place of fixedness; and among those butchers one is the wicked butcher who sells treifot; if so the slaughterhouses that buy from the butchers have safek whether they bought treifah from the butcher or not — and this is the mishnah: butchers who sell kosher meat and one of them is treifah, and they bought and do not know from which they bought — whether from this butcher or not — its safek is forbidden, for it is kevua; and you have no clearer law than this explicitly in the mishnah that the slaughterhouses bought and do not know whether they bought from the treifah seller or not; if so obviously it is kevua; and one must say: did the slaughterhouses that bought — even if we say they bought from this butcher — did they buy only from him and from that animal presumed treifah alone? Behold they also bought animals and from other butchers who sell kosher meat and they are not in the category of safek kevua — they also bought; if so behold there is also safek in mixtures and it is double safek: safek whether this piece is from that butcher or from other butchers; and if you say from that butcher — perhaps from the kosher he bought and we say kosher. And this is the law stated in Shulchan Aruch: he bought and it became mixed with another — it is double safek; and this double safek is preferable to the double safek above — for there one cannot reverse and say safek kosher safek treifah; and if you say from treifah he bought — perhaps it was not taken from this butcher — for in the end it is treifah; unlike for Shulchan Aruch it reverses well: perhaps this is from the piece of meat that was not taken; and if you will say: this is what was taken in the slaughterhouses — perhaps it was taken from the slaughterhouses that sell kosher (and see below Kontras HaSfeikot); if so this law is forced and the poskim did not invent from their hearts to say it is a novelty — only from the Gemara's words; investigate well, for it is sharp.
