"It is nullified." The Shach wrote; however, they are forbidden to eat at once, for then there is only one safek; and it is plain — except that it requires study, for the Mechaber wrote in seif 8 that it is permitted only with three mixtures; and one must say that since a fixed case is a novelty, you have in it only its novelty and it is permitted if it became mixed afterward — and this is forced; end of his words. And the Taz wrote that in truth one should be stringent here to require three mixtures if it is not a d'rabbanan issur — that is, such as nine shops and one separated not before us, which is permitted by Torah law since it separated not before us, except that the Sages forbade it because of meat hidden from the eye; in this specifically it helps if it became mixed afterward, and it is permitted even if it is a piece worthy of honor. And one may say that in this one does not need two safeks, for we establish a d'rabbanan safek to be lenient; one may say that since here there is d'oraisa issur but it was permitted by force of nullification in the majority and the Sages forbade it and considered the minority as if it were half since it is fit to be honored — until here — two safeks are required to permit.
"In such a case." The Shach wrote that even if it separated before us, or a non-Jew took it before us, nevertheless since the issur is in its place it is like half-and-half, and since, etc. — however, in this it is possible to permit if it became mixed in a place of great loss. And in such a case, since there are poskim who hold that separation before us is only d'rabbanan — however, that is specifically when he does not eat them as one, for otherwise there is no double safek. And what the Rama wrote, "since the first safek is from the Torah" — is not specifically [that]; rather, even if it would have been forbidden because of mixtures, it would not be permitted if it became mixed afterward in mixtures, as below, seif 8; rather, it comes to exclude if the safek of issur were forbidden d'rabbanan — it would be permitted in a mixture; or alternatively, it teaches us that there is no need, for it is less than mixtures, since a fixed case is a novelty — it teaches us that since the first safek is forbidden by Torah law we do not say thus; and all this is according to the Rama. But the Shach disagrees on this, as he wrote in seif 8, see there.
