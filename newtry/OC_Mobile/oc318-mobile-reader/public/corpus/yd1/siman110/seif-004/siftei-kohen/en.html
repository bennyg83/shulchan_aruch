And it was mixed with others, etc. The Bach challenged the Mechaber, for the Rashba spoke only of separated in our presence that its prohibition is only d'rabbanan — but not regarding one who bought, for the first safek is forbidden from the Torah and is like safek treifah that was mixed, that the mixture is forbidden, as below, seif 9, end of his words. And so in the hagahot at the end of Issur VeHeter, general rule 25, law 12, he wrote that the Rashba (and the Tur brings him) permits only when separated in our presence, that they consider it d'rabbanan; and in truth so it appears apparently from his language in the short Torat HaBayit that the Tur brought, who wrote: and it appears to me that they forbade only when it is by itself, etc. — it appears it refers adjacent to separated in our presence, and this is the language of 'they forbade' — it appears the Sages forbade. However, in the long Torat HaBayit, daf 120, end of 2b, he wrote, and these are his words: however, specifically when this piece that was taken from the kevua was not mixed among other pieces of permitted meat, etc. However, one may say it refers to a piece worthy of honor, for it deals there with it, that he calls it there kevua; and so in the language of the poskim, mixture of a piece worthy of honor is called kevua — and so too the first safek is d'rabbanan, and he says that if one took a piece worthy of honor from the kevua and it was mixed with others, it is nullified in the majority because of double safek. However, the Rav HaMagid, chapter 8 of Magen Avraham, law 11, also wrote in the name of the Rashba that one who takes from the kevua they forbade only when it is by itself, etc. — and he did not mention there anything of a piece worthy of honor, only kevua of slaughterhouses; and it is forced to say when a non-Jew takes from the kevua, for he speaks unconditionally; and furthermore, if it were that it refers to a non-Jew, why need he say at all 'one who takes from the kevua,' for he already mentioned a non-Jew explicitly before. And if we say this is not forced and nevertheless it deals with a non-Jew — if so, also regarding the Mechaber it requires study, for we can say that he too deals with a non-Jew (but in the Taz one cannot say so, for he wrote: even if he took from them by hand, etc. — from his words it appears explicitly that it refers to an Israelite, see there). But it appears more to say according to the plain meaning that the Rashba and the Rav HaMagid deal even when an Israelite bought from the slaughterhouses; and also in the short Torat HaBayit he did not mention at all that one who took when a non-Jew was in our presence is only d'rabbanan — and on the contrary he wrote unconditionally several times that it is as if Israelites took — it appears it is entirely as if Israelites took and forbidden also d'oraisa; and so Issur VeHeter and the Rav in Torat Chatat who wrote in the name of Tosafot and Mordechai that a non-Jew took or separated in our presence is forbidden d'oraisa — necessarily so they inferred, for Tosafot and Mordechai did not write thus; but certainly it appears reasonable to them, since they wrote unconditionally that it is as if Israelites took — it appears forbidden also d'oraisa. If so, also in the Rashba's words it appears thus; and there is no difficulty from what the Rashba and Mechaber hold in safek treifah that was mixed in the majority that it is not nullified — for here the law of kevua is a novelty, as I wrote in s.k. 14 in the name of the Ran, because everywhere we follow the majority and you have in it only its novelty — meaning, whatever is by itself; but not when mixed afterward. This appears clear in the Rashba's view — and the same applies to the Mechaber:
Nullified in the majority, etc. It requires study — lest it should only be a mixture; behold, the Mechaber holds in s.k. 98 that it is permitted only with three mixtures, and the Rashba follows his own reasoning, for he holds it is permitted with two mixtures, as below, s.k. 49. And one must say that the Mechaber holds: since kevua is a novelty, it is worse than mixtures, and whatever was mixed afterward we follow the majority and it is permitted. And furthermore he holds: since otherwise many poskim permit with two mixtures, as will be explained in s.k. 49 — if so, with kevua, which is a novelty, you have in it only its novelty, and it is permitted when mixed afterward. And this is forced:
Because double safek. A doubt at the time one eats the piece: lest this is not the one that came from the kevua; and if you say it is this one, lest it is from the permitted portion. According to this it is forbidden to eat all of them at once, for there is only one safek — and this is plain:
And there are those who forbid in such a case. And so is the view of the Bach, and not as the Taz, who copied only the Mechaber's words and nothing more — he did not apply it and did not take notice of everything I wrote above:
In such a case. In this law of kevua — even if separated in our presence it is forbidden by Torah law as half-and-half, and forbidden afterward when mixed — so too in Torat Chatat, general rule 43, end of law 8, in the name of Issur VeHeter, and he ruled thus in the simanim. And this is that he expanded here in the hagahah and wrote: because wherever the prohibition is in its place, etc.; and he did not write briefly because since the first doubt is from the Torah, etc.; rather he comes to say that even though separated in our presence, or a non-Jew took it in our presence, nevertheless since the prohibition is in its place it is like half-and-half, and since, etc. However, in this it is possible to permit if it was mixed in a place of great loss — and in such a case, since Maharshal, Piskei HaGahot, siman 94, ruled also like the Rashba, and so is the view of the Ran, and there are poskim who hold that separated in our presence is only d'rabbanan; however, this is specifically when one does not eat them all at once, for otherwise there is no double safek, as below, s.k. 52:
And since the first safek is from the Torah, etc. If so, if it were forbidden because of mixtures, it would not be permitted when mixed afterward in mixtures, as below, s.k. 8 — rather it comes to exclude if it were safek of prohibition that is forbidden d'rabbanan, it would be permitted in mixtures as below, s.k. 53; or it teaches us from the negative — that it is worse than mixtures, since kevua is a novelty, it teaches us: since the first safek is forbidden from the Torah, we do not say thus. And all this the Rav needed in his hagahah, for he holds below, s.k. 8, that the second mixture is forbidden to eat even though one does not eat it all at once; but in Issur VeHeter itself it comes out well, as I wrote below, s.k. 54, in his name, that with two mixtures the second mixture is permitted when one does not eat them all at once — if so, it informs us here that it is worse than mixtures and is like safek treifah that was mixed, as below, seif 9, and the mixture is forbidden even though one does not eat it all at once — and this is clear:
