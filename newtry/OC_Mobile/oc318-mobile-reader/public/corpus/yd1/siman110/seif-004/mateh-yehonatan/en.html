(There, seif 3 in the hagahah) And that which we say, etc. At first glance this contradicts what he wrote in Orach Chayyim siman 339 s.v. "nine bundles of matzah and one chametz" — a mouse came and took one, he must return and check, for every fixed case, etc.; and if the mouse separated from the place where it was fixed and a mouse came and took it from there — he need not return and check, for "whatever separates, the majority separates," etc.; and it is implied that if the mouse took from the place of fixing, even though it did not take it before us, nevertheless we say it is called fixed like every fixing, etc. But here regarding a non-Jew we say: if it is found in his hand — it is considered separated, even though the non-Jew took it from the place where it was fixed. And one may say: granted, there it is different — for as soon as the mouse took it, the obligation of removal applies to him; and even though it did not take it before us, nevertheless now when the safek becomes known to him — if so, the main question is about the time of its taking, and then it was fixed; therefore the mouse would have to take it after it separated. But here regarding a non-Jew — when they took from the slaughterhouses they took for themselves; if so, at that time no safek applied to a Jew at all, for behold the non-Jew took for himself and no safek arose for the Jew; rather only now, when one wants to buy from the non-Jew, then the safek applies, and then it is separated, and we say "whatever separates from the majority separates."
