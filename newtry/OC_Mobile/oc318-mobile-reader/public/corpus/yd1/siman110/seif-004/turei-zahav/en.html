Most stores, etc. — There is wonder here on Shulchan Aruch, for Rashba did not write thus, that mixtures help, except when one bought from butcher shops where there is treifah meat mixed among the permitted pieces — that two kinds of kevua are before us: one is Torah-level kevua, namely nine permitted stores and one treifah, regarding which we say from the Torah "every fixed item is half-and-half," etc., since the issur is recognizable in its place, and in this there is no distinction between piece worthy of honor or not; and rabbinic kevua is in one butcher shop where treifah was found, mixed among the permitted pieces, and he does not know from which he bought — for from the Torah all meat in the butcher shop would be permitted, nullified in the majority; only the Sages decreed that if there is a piece worthy of honor there, it should not be nullified, and they gave this butcher shop the law of kevua, as they called it in the Gemara in Zevachim (73a) regarding a mixture of living creatures that are important, that it had kevua; and so Tosafos wrote in Pesachim (9), in the passage beginning "this is nine stores"; and so in Semak at the end of siman 214 they also called it rabbinic kevua. Rashba wrote regarding that rabbinic kevua that if afterward one bought from this fixed set and it became mixed with others, it is permitted because of double safek; but in Torah-level kevua that we mentioned, certainly afterward mixtures do not help, as Rashba himself wrote — Tur brought at the end of this siman that the first safek must be permitted from the Torah specifically, and the issur is only d'rabbanan; and likewise the law of a non-Jew's cup that became mixed, which Rashba brings proof from — it would be thus, that from the Torah even in an important vessel it is nullified, and the issur is only d'rabbanan, as they wrote in Zevachim (73a), in the passage beginning "rather Rava said," and so it is explained in Terumat HaDeshen HaArukh — see there — that they wrote this law on this law that I mentioned; and so Shulchan Aruch should have written: from butcher shops where treifah became mixed and one bought from there and does not know from which of them he bought, and it became mixed with others, etc.; and so is the view of Issur VeHeter klal 25 din 12, that specifically regarding rabbinic issur — such as nine stores — and one of them separated not before us, it is permitted from the Torah, since it separated not before us, only the Sages forbade it because of meat hidden from the eye, as Shulchan Aruch wrote here at the end of seif 3 — only then does what became mixed afterward help, and it is permitted even if piece worthy of honor; and one should not challenge: behold we establish rabbinic safek to leniency — why do we need two safeks? Issur VeHeter already resolved there: since here there is Torah issur that was permitted through nullification in the majority and the Sages forbade it and considered the minority as if it were half, since it is piece worthy of honor — therefore two safeks are needed to permit. It follows that Rashba and Issur VeHeter are entirely equal — Rashba deals with rabbinic kevua, and even if one bought before us from there and afterward it became mixed with others, it is permitted, since even at the place of fixedness there is only rabbinic issur; but in Torah-level kevua like the case of nine stores, double safek does not help except after it separated not before us, whose issur is rabbinic. And behold I saw the author of Hagahot Issur VeHeter at the end of the book, who wrote on this that Issur VeHeter disagrees with Rashba, that here he does not distinguish whether the safek arose at the time of fixedness or not, and he was not precise at all — and although I am not worthy to disagree, nevertheless the clear truth is that the authors of Shulchan Aruch, the Rav Beit Yosef and afterward Rama, who brought here Issur VeHeter's dispute on this law in the name of "some forbid," etc., did not investigate this sufficiently — for in truth all agree in this law, as I wrote; so it appears to plain understanding. Maharach too explained Rashba's words as I wrote, not like Shulchan Aruch — and see what I will write in this siman seif 8 on Shulchan Aruch's words here; further, we do not establish like this seif except that we require three mixtures specifically.
