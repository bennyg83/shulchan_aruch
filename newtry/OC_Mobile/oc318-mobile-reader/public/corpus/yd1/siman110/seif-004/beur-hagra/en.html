Most shops, etc. — through the end of the siman. First I will explain all the sugyot relevant to this in Zevachim (74a) — R' Tavya said "A ring," etc., through "two" — and all the poskim ruled like Rav and R' Shimon, that the halachah is like Rav regarding prohibitions. And the Rambam wrote in chapter 16 of Hilchot Maachalot Assurot that we require three mixtures; but in chapter 7 of Hilchot Avodah Zarah he wrote that with two mixtures it is permitted. And he holds that pomegranates in the granary are different, since they are considered important — the first mixture is certainly [forbidden], for the Rabbanan made it certainly [forbidden]; therefore they are not nullified, as written (Zevachim 73a) — important things are counted and not nullified, and it is as if it exists in actuality. And the second mixture is the first safek — perhaps it is not there. And the third mixture is double safek. Not so with avodah zarah, which is forbidden only because safek avodah zarah is forbidden — not like Tosafot, who wrote that the mishnah in Avodah Zarah (74a) and these [cases] are all because of davar sheb'minyan. And even avodah zarah, as written there and in Yevamos chapter 1, 2, and Zevachim (72, 1) — except that basar b'chalav and the like are because of davar sheb'minyan. And according to his view who explains not like R' Meir — and a ring is not among the seven things. And that which is not counted in chapter 3 of Orlah and in the Yerushalmi there — because only important things are counted there (and see s.k. 1 in the collected notes). Therefore the second mixture is permitted. And according to common understanding, and the Rambam's version in the Gemara — "if there is safek," etc., "one of them for the majority is permitted" — it is a Tanna [who taught]: R' Yitzchak says, etc. And this is the correct version, for with our version it is difficult — at first he says safek, etc., "How?" — it appears the second mixture is permitted, and at the end he says specifically the third mixture. And furthermore it is difficult — it should challenge Rav too, who says "unless they separated," etc., and even though Rashi explained it regarding mixture, and so the Rambam explained there — and this is what he says "they do not forbid," etc., that it implies the mixtures. And with this the Ra'avad's question is resolved — why did he rule like Rav; R' Oshaya disputes Rav and holds that we do not say "prohibition is in the majority." It is as written there in "two." And the Rambam himself ruled in the beginning of chapter 15 of Hilchot Terumot like R' Oshaya — except that there it is regarding the first mixture. Nevertheless it is difficult, for in the baraita it only permits the third mixture, and so the conclusion of the Tanna. But according to his version it works out, and the conclusion of the Tanna is that there the second mixture is the first safek — thus double safek is permitted. And likewise from what Shmuel said "Leave avodah zarah," etc., it appears Rav deals with double safek, and he said "they separated sixty" — for then it is as if all were mixed and the first mixture remained. Not so with forty, which is double safek. And the Rashba's view — that even with pomegranates in the granary it is permitted in the second mixture. And his version in the Gemara — "if there is safek, avodah zarah is forbidden and safek safeka is permitted" — the conclusion of the Tanna is that the baraita [teaches] pomegranates in the granary, in any quantity — "How? One of them fell into a myriad and from a myriad to a myriad they are forbidden; R' Shimon says: to a myriad is forbidden, to three and from three to another place is permitted," as written in Terumat HaDeshen. And this version is more correct, for one need not distinguish between avodah zarah and other prohibitions — for there it appears avodah zarah is stricter than other prohibitions. And furthermore, with our version it does not appear they disagree — the Tanna Kamma deals with two mixtures and R' Shimon with three. And such is Tosafot's view even with our version — they only said three mixtures regarding eating them all together, for with two there is one safek since one certainly eats one from the first mixture. And with this all the aforementioned difficulties are also resolved. And the Tanna Kamma mentions two mixtures — that in all cases they are forbidden, even one by itself; if so, all the more so with more. And R' Shimon mentions three — that in all cases they are permitted. And the Beit Yosef wrote that even according to the Rashba the second mixture is forbidden to eat them together, for the aforementioned reason that there is only one safek here, as written in the Rashba in siman 109, seif 1. And another view for Rashi — if one separated one from the second mixture to another place, the second mixtures are permitted. And one must say according to his view that Rav too — when he separated from the second mixture — and what he wrote "unless they separated forty," etc., refers to what he wrote above "they separated forty to one place," etc. — "one of the forty," etc. And he explains here that it is only permitted when they were mixed beforehand in two mixtures and afterward separated one of them — then the remainder are permitted. And sixty too in such a case — that we require double safek, and afterward separated one to permit the remainder (and this is Tosafot's intent there, s.v. separated. And in the Commentary he did not explain thus — not like the Shach in s.k. 53 who left it as requires study). And according to his view who wrote in Avodah Zarah (74a) that a mixture nullified in the majority requires that one throw away one of them — and from here the Tosafot rejected the explanation, as the Rosh and Ra'ash challenged from the mishnah in chapter 2 of Orlah — one need not remove, and they only said this regarding terumah, as noted in siman 109, seif 1. And what he wrote there "but a fig" — even though it is not davar sheb'minyan — there regarding what was mixed in less than a hundred, for it deals with terumah; therefore R' Tam explained the mishnah in chapter 3 of Orlah and chapter 3 of Avodah Zarah "others in others," and Tosafot Pesachim (27, 1), Yevamos (39, 2), Yevamos chapter 1, 1, Sanhedrin chapter 1, Avodah Zarah (49, 1), Zevachim (72, 1), and the Rosh chapter 3 of Orlah beginning of mishnah 6, see there. And he wrote that R' Yitzchak bar Baruch establishes that all or most were mixed with others, as written in Zevachim there "sixty to one place," etc. — except that in the beginning of Yevamos there they said "and its safek is forbidden, and if it was mixed," etc. — even though it is double safek. And R' Tam explained there and in Zevachim there that "and if it was mixed" refers to certainty. And so too the Rosh there — but R' Yitzchak explains "from its safek." And some say for double safek, since there is one safek in itself and one safek in mixture. And the Rashba explained that we require either both in mixture — as that passage in Zevachim "or both in itself" — as that passage in the first chapter of Kesubos (9, 1). And the Semag, Semak, Semak, Maggid Mishneh chapter 16 of Hilchot Maachalot Assurot, and Hagahot Asheri siman 58, siman 33, s.v. Horah R' Yaakov and responsa — explained the reason: since safek is forbidden from the Torah, one may not permit in a mixture that constitutes certainly that it was mixed — for even in such a mixture it is forbidden, as written in Zevachim there "What of three, that there is majority," etc. — for from the Torah it is nullified. Not so one — and that is when it is safek d'oraisa, forbidden in the second mixture. And the Rashba brought in siman 401 the dispute of R' Tam and R' Yitzchak, and agreed with R' Tam. And he brought from the dough of a widow in Kesubos (14, 1) which is called double safek, and see there Tosafot s.v. almanat, etc. And this is according to his view. But according to the aforementioned poskim there is no proof that it is like the first safek in majority that is permitted from the Torah — and likewise here (requires study), as written in Kiddushin (73, 1) — R' Acha said: "By Torah law," etc. "a definite mamzer," etc. — thus it is like majority. And so too the Maggid Mishneh in chapter 1 of Hilchot Yevamos — the halachah wrote that the Rashba agreed with R' Tam — but all the poskim ruled according to R' Yitzchak's reasoning and their view. And from Kesubos there appears proof according to R' Yitzchak's explanation — for there when he wrote "those d'oraisa," etc. — thus in d'oraisa double safek does not help. And even though it does not stand in the conclusion thus — because their safek is permitted from the Torah, as noted — and Rashi explained there s.v. d'oraisa, etc., and s.v. chalal, etc. And in Zevachim there — the Ran [quoting] R' bar Avuh, R' Tavya said, etc. — "I say two things." And the Rambam explained that he means two fell. And he does not explain the matter of R' Eliezer "two, two, twice." And likewise one, one, twice. And likewise he does not explain there Avodah Zarah 2 — R' Yirmiyah raises an objection, and the Sages say, etc. — "Rather R' Eliezer: If one offered," etc. And answers "one pair." And this is what he says "I," etc. — he means one pair. But regarding a barrel there in "two" he only wrote one. And one must say that he distinguishes between a barrel whose fall is discernible [or specifically avodah zarah and kodshim require two, as Tosafot distinguished (Chullin 95a) regarding separating according to R' Tam], as wrote the Kesef Mishneh (and see s.k. 26). And even though the Gemara says "and it is necessary," etc., it appears they are equivalent — and according to common understanding. Except that it is difficult why he ruled like Rav — for as R' Eliezer said, and he ruled himself in the mishnah there like the Sages "even if all the heads were offered," etc. And likewise the mishnah in the first chapter "all shall die." And so all the poskim ruled like Rav and R' Levi — except that one can say they hold the words of Rav and R' Levi apply even according to the Rabbanan, and it is different there because important things are deferred, and all the more so slaughtered animals — not like Chananiah the Egyptian, as written there (73b). And the Gemara challenges Rav, who holds in chapter 6 of Yoma that important things are not deferred — but we hold like R' Yochanan. And even if they rule like R' Eliezer there is no difficulty, since Rav and R' Levi hold like him. And likewise the sugya in Yoma (34, 1) is like him. But for the Rambam, who ruled not like R' Eliezer and also ruled like Rav that important things are not deferred — because the mishnah in Yoma there is like him — also in this his words are not plausible, for the sugya in the Gemara in many places is like R' Yochanan. And one may say Rav specifically said it fell by itself, and did not infer from the mishnah, but because it appears even if one died by itself it does not help. And Rashi explains "I say two things" — to eat them two by two. And so too all the poskim. And Tosafot there s.v. venafel — he does not challenge from what he wrote "and they separated forty," etc., "and from a myriad," etc. — specifically it fell into the Great Sea. And so too Rashi there. And another view for the Ra'avad, who ruled not like Rav and R' Levi — for they spoke as an individual, and R' Yochanan disputes R' Levi in the Yerushalmi (see s.k. 21). And likewise he ruled not like Rav "if they separated forty" — R' Oshaya disputes him that we do not say prohibition is in the majority. And furthermore he wrote that even for avodah zarah we require three mixtures like our version, as in the conclusion there. And see chapter 3 of Chullin (95, 1) — Tosafot s.v. hen he challenged this from what we say there "separated is permitted." And in Zevachim there we say "decree because of kavu'a." And he said to him specifically when intentional, but by itself it is permitted — for if so why did they say "they shall graze until," etc., since they separated. And likewise rings, etc. — that one separated one of sixty, etc., and likewise pomegranates in the granary, etc. — separating does not help at all. And they explained that we only decree in mixture, but when known we do not decree lest one take from the prohibition. And She'iltot holds like the first reasoning that there is no distinction between mixed or not — only that it is forbidden only when intentional. And so too the Rashba in the name of R' Tam. And what he wrote regarding rings and pomegranates in the granary "they separated," etc. — that is, he explained "before us" that it constitutes like from the fixed [prohibition], as noted seif 3. And he holds that even in mixture where its prohibition is not because of kavu'a — for behold it is nullified in the majority from the Torah — rather its prohibition is because of its importance d'rabbanan, as Tosafot wrote in chapter 3 of Gittin s.v. safeko and in Zevachim there s.v. rather, etc. — only that its prohibition is also specifically when kavu'a; nevertheless it is forbidden when separated before us like when kavu'a. And that which we say in Zevachim "and let us pen them," etc. — that is regarding where it was not seen, as Tosafot wrote in chapter 3 of Gittin there and in Zevachim — not like what is written in Pesachim there that even before us it is permitted in mixture. And the Ran resolved that "before us" its prohibition is because of kavu'a — and that is the explanation of Rava. And that which they knew, etc. — one may say since the beginning of the grazing is intentional it is forbidden, as the Rashba wrote. And what is written below. And Tosafot in Sanhedrin (79b) wrote in the name of R' Tam that even lechatchilah it is permitted — "let us pen them" means they wander from this that R' Levi said there, who said "But here regarding an ox that did not," etc. And that passage in Zevachim regarding kodshim is different — for we decree, but generally not. And so too the Mordechai chapter 3 of Gittin in his name. And also according to the explanation one must say that which regarding rings (and Tosafot in Sanhedrin there chapter 1 and Zevachim 71, 1 distinguished that important things are different since it is possible, etc.) — in "separated before us" that it constitutes like kavu'a, as the Mordechai wrote in the name of R' Tam. And the Ran wrote that whoever separated before the safek was born — even if they separated intentionally and took from the fixed — is permitted, since the safek was born after he separated, there is no room to decree. And so too the Rosh in responsa. And this is what he says in Zevachim there — Rav who said like R' Eliezer, even though Rav said it fell by itself specifically, but if he threw it down, even b'shogeg, not — as Tosafot wrote s.v. the aforementioned. And R' Eliezer said "If the head was offered," etc. — and one must say that it was offered before the safek was born. And with this what the Rosh wrote is resolved, and in the hagahah above siman 107, seif 1 — we say "prohibition is in the majority" — R' Levi: even though fish is a beriah, and likewise an egg that has an embryo for the Rambam — since he found the fish afterward, and at the time when the thirty or twenty remained in the pot the safek had not yet been born — then those that were separated in the pot are permitted if it was not discernible, and we depend on "prohibition is in the majority." And these are his words — the Rosh in chapter 3 of Gittin in the name of She'iltot: "And if the majority of them were lost to one place and the minority to another place, we say prohibition is in those where the majority are, and those where the minority are are permitted." And the same applies to R' Yitzchak's view before the safek was born — and as he explained in the second mixture "forty to one place" and "sixty," etc. And Rashi explained there (73b) s.v. amar, etc., and s.v. umeshaneh. And now I will explain one by one. "Most shops," etc. — so too nevertheless in chapter 8 of Magen Avraham in the name of the Rashba, see there, halachah 11. And as written in Zevachim there — "forty to another place do not forbid" — and like R' Shimon there; except that some say for that reason that there the first safek is in the mixture and permitted from the Torah. Not so when the first safek is forbidden from the Torah — for every kavu'a, etc., whether for leniency or stringency, as written in the first chapter of Kesubos — except that the Rashba holds like R' Tam that even one safek in itself and one safek in mixture is permitted. And even for R' Yitzchak according to his explanation — since they are not one subject where the first is in itself, not so here where the first is also not in itself. And so too in Terumat HaDeshen — one must be concerned for R' Yitzchak's words, as written in seif 9. But all this is according to his view that with two mixtures suffices. But according to the Rambam's view that we require three mixtures — that the first mixture is certainly — if so with safek we require two mixtures. And in seif 8 he ruled like the Rambam's words.
And some forbid, etc. According to his view who wrote in seif 9 like the Semag and responsa in the name of R' Yitzchak as noted, and R' Levi — even according to the Rashba's view and Tosafot who permit with two mixtures — and in seif 8.
