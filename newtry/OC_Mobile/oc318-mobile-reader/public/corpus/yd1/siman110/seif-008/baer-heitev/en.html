"**From the mixtures.**" That is, that it separated before us, or that half of them separated — for if it separated by itself, even without mixtures it is permitted according to the Mechaber in seif 6. And the Shach wrote that it appears that in great loss and in such a case, certainly the second mixtures are permitted when he does not eat it all at once. (Also the Taz wavered on this and concluded at the end that initially one should be stringent until there are three mixtures.)
"**A certain issur.**" d'oraisa; but a certain d'rabbanan issur is like a doubtful d'oraisa issur. And the Taz and Shach rule that the second mixtures are permitted when he does not eat it all as one, even for a certain d'oraisa issur; and the third mixtures are permitted even for a certain issur to eat it all as one. And likewise a doubtful issur that became mixed is permitted in the second mixtures even to eat it all as one — for a doubtful issur with two mixtures is like three mixtures of a certain issur.
"**Matirin.**" The Shach wrote that even with three mixtures one must forbid something that has matirin (and all the more so that one may not permit something that has matirin when one of them fell to nullification). However, it is difficult — for in Orach Chayyim, siman 497, seif 4, stam the Rama ruled that doubtful prepared food on the second day of Yom Tov is permitted because it is double safek, even though it is something that has matirin. And one may say that case is different — for there, since we observe the second day of Yom Tov only because of the custom of our ancestors, we are not more stringent than they; or alternatively, and this is the main point: that which double safek is forbidden in something that has matirin — that is specifically in mixtures, for what difference does it make to permit through nullification? Without that, it has permission. But double safek that comes from elsewhere — even in something that has matirin it is permitted. And now it is reconciled that which the Rama wrote in siman 493 that stam grain is permitted for the reason of double safek; however, without that, one may say that there it is certainly for need.
