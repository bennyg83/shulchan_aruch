(There in the Shach, se'if katan 59) A doubt of a d'rabbanan issur regarding something that has a permitted aspect is forbidden. And know that the Peri Chadash wrote in Hilchot Yom Tov, and the Peri Megadim in Orach Chayyim, that that is specifically a doubt of an act, like a doubt whether it was laid on Yom Tov regarding an egg and a doubt of prepared [food]; but regarding a doubt of a dispute of the authorities and a doubt of law, we are lenient in d'rabbanan even regarding something that has a permitted aspect; and so it is implied in Shevut Yaakov siman 96; and one can prove so from the sugya of Menachot (daf 68b). And with this the sugya at the end of Shabbos is well: that R' Yochanan said the halacha is like R' Shimon who does not hold of muktzeh; and it challenges: yet R' Yochanan said the halacha is like an anonymous Mishnah, and we learned, etc., we do not water and slaughter the wilderness [animals]; and R' Yochanan answered he found another anonymous [Mishnah]; and it concludes that Beit Hillel hold like R' Shimon; and Rashi explained: Beit Hillel is considered like an anonymous [Mishnah]. And it is difficult: still, what preference has Beit Hillel over an anonymous [Mishnah] according to R' Yochanan? But according to what the Peri Chadash distinguished between a doubt of an occurrence and a doubt of law or a dispute of the authorities, where we are lenient — that of Shabbos is well: regarding the d'rabbanan issur of muktzeh we do not need the preference of the anonymous [Mishnah] of Beit Hillel who do not hold of muktzeh, for even when equal we rule that the halacha is like the lenient opinion in the words of the Sofeirim, as R' Yochanan himself ruled in the first chapter of Avodah Zarah (daf 7). And so the opinion of the Rashba and the Ran in Nedarim (daf 47b) is proven, who wrote that it is reasonable that we take the inquiry of Rami bar Chama to be lenient; and if one said "konam these fruits upon so-and-so," he is permitted in their exchanges, for since regarding a mere prohibition of benefit the exchanges are not like growths except d'rabbanan, it is a d'rabbanan doubt and to be treated leniently — end of their words. And it is difficult: for nedarim are something that has a permitted aspect through [release by] a sage, as is stated there (daf 59); and regarding something that has a permitted aspect they were stringent in its doubt, as Rav Ashi said at the beginning of Beitza (daf 4) regarding an egg laid on Yom Tov, that its doubt is forbidden because it is something that has a permitted aspect, and anything that has a permitted aspect, even d'rabbanan, is not nullified; and Rashi explained: just as they were stringent in its nullification, so too for that reason they were stringent in a doubt. Rather it is proven from this that the Rashba and the Ran hold that specifically regarding a doubt of an occurrence, like that of a doubt whether it was laid on Yom Tov or on a weekday, did Rav Ashi say they were also stringent in a doubt; which is not so regarding a doubt of law in a dispute of the authorities or in an unresolved inquiry like that of Nedarim — we are lenient in d'rabbanan even regarding something that has a permitted aspect. And I wrote further on this, with Heaven's help, in my novellae on Chullin.
(Laws of double doubt in the Shach, letter 2) He wrote in the name of a responsum of the Rashba that regarding a d'rabbanan issur that has a chazakah of issur, we are stringent. And in the book Even HaEzer, chapter 2 of Shabbos, he disputes this: that we do not rule like R' Yehudah regarding doubtful tevel, as the Rambam wrote, for the anonymous Mishnayot are not like him. And so Maggid Mishneh wrote in chapter 4 of Bechorot; and so is the opinion of the Ran in Sukkah (46b); and see the book Ezer Yisrael and the book Chavat Da'at. However, the anonymous [Mishnah] of Tohorot — one can say that there it deals with a doubt of tumah that did not have a chazakah of issur, and not with doubtful tevel, as Tosafot Yom Tov wrote. Rather our master's reason is that we do not rule like R' Yehudah against his colleagues; and so it is implied in Minchat Yaakov. And what the book Beit Efraim wrote, that this depends on the two answers Tosafot wrote in Pesachim (daf 9), and wondered that the later authorities did not mention [them] — already Sha'ar HaMelech preceded him in Hilchot Mikvaot, klal 7, and in Tziyun LeNefesh Chayah on Berachot; and also his words there are not compelling according to the opinion of the Rambam, as is written in Magen Avraham. And see in the book Sidrei Tohorah. And what the book Ezer Yisrael wrote as a proof for issur from that we are stringent regarding a doubt of prepared [food] — it is not so, for there it is something that has a permitted aspect. And what the book Beit Efraim wrote as a proof for issur from Eruvin (daf 32) — the words of Maggid Mishneh in Hilchot Bechorot escaped him; see there. And what Chavat Da'at wrote as a proof to forbid from that of a doubt of avot ha-tumah d'rabbanan — the Peri Megadim also so wrote; but it is not a proof, for there even though the issur of tamei was not established, and the reason is they made it like that of the Torah to make a person tamei so as to burn terumah and kodashim upon it — they also made it like that of the Torah to make a doubt tamei. Rather this requires further study regarding what the Taz wrote in siman 201, se'if katan 86, who explained there the words of the Mechaber who forbids like R' Yosei; and that is also the language of the Rambam in Hilchot Mikvaot. And what the book Kereti UFeleti wrote to bring a proof to permit from Menachot (68b) regarding a doubt of the day of the waving, where an issur was established — the book Ezer Yisrael and the book Chavat Da'at rejected it, for there it is better than other doubts, for we are experts in the fixed [calendar of] the moon. And there is a proof for their words from what the Rambam wrote at the beginning of chapter 10 of Hilchot Ma'achalot Asurot, that a doubt is forbidden from the words of the Sofeirim; and if this were like every doubt it would be forbidden from the Torah, since an issur was established in a doubt of a d'oraisa issur. And regarding a double doubt from one name — he wrote in responsa Tur HaEven siman 41 that that is like that of the name of ones regarding seduction of a minor girl, that of itself it is ones, and if it were not ones, presumably she would not have been a minor; but if in one doubt there is a dispute and in one doubt according to all it is permitted, it is not one name, for it is like one who permits more than his fellow. He wrote in the book Levushei Serad that the opinion of poskim who were not brought in the Shulchan Aruch we do not join even for a double doubt [and similarly I saw that Urim VeTumim wrote in the laws of seizing that one may not say "I hold like" an opinion not brought in the Shulchan Aruch]. And may his Master forgive him for this — for shall we, according to the decision of Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe, set the opinions of the great poskim as nothing? And already the gaon wrote so in Noda B'Yehuda, Even HaEzer volume, siman 47.
Question and answer for siman 110 Question: They slaughtered seven animals in one village a parasang's journey from our city, and one man of our city's people took one innard called tzaftzucha and brought it to his house, and there he found in it a needle, and it was brought before me; and its head was protruding from the outer side and most of it was stuck inward diagonally until the other side into the cavity of the tzaftzucha; and there was no clot of blood there at all — only from outside near the needle there was a red appearance, but no illness or dissolution at all was recognizable. I also cut in the place where it was red and there was in it no moisture of pus and blood. Also it had not rusted — only in the middle it was black. And I saw in the cavity of the tzaftzucha that there was still dung in it, and I thought it had not yet been rinsed until I asked and they said it had been rinsed; and before finding the needle all seven animals were cut into large pieces as is customary, all together, and they salted them in three and four places and rinsed them; and afterwards they found the aforementioned needle of one animal of the seven animals and it was not known from which. And there was in the matter a great loss and also a time of pressure. Answer: It appears to permit the mixture as the law of dry in dry in siman 109, since it became known before cooking; for there is no reason to forbid here neither because of the salting nor because of a piece worthy of honor, as I will explain. And at first: there is here only a doubt of tereifah, for all the issur here is because the tzaftzucha was rinsed before it was brought before me — for if it had not been rinsed beforehand it would be plain to permit according to what the Shach wrote in siman 48 se'if katan 14 and se'if katan 30: that both regarding the omasum and regarding the beit hakosot it is kosher in great financial loss even if the needle is stuck from side to side if there is no clot of blood, for we say after shechitah it was stuck, for there is something for it to catch on; and if it were that from life there was a clot of blood there was; and so the plain sense of the sugya of "Eilu Tereifot" (daf 51) implies: that wherever there is something for it to catch on we say so — and what difference to me omasum or beit hakosot; and if so the same law applies regarding a gizzard; and so Minchat Yaakov wrote (and before me they testified that this innard is the beit hakosot, and according to my words there is no practical difference in this). And if because of "there are who say" that he brought in the gloss of the aforementioned siman that we are not experts in checking for a clot of blood — already the Shach wrote there that several later authorities did not bring this, for they do not hold this stringency, and that so it is proper to rule; and so Minchat Yaakov wrote. And if because it was red from outside — already it is explained in the book Peri Megadim in the name of responsa Noda B'Yehuda at the end of siman 46 and in siman 48 that redness of the flesh alone does not harm even with a needle, for a mere change of appearance does not disqualify in organs, as is written there and at the end of siman 50 and in siman 43; and so Noda B'Yehuda wrote in siman 13 — unless there is an affliction from one side and the appearance from side to side. And if because of rust that was in the middle of the needle — it does not harm according to the opinion of Rashal; and even though the Bach disputes him, nevertheless in great financial loss certainly one may rely on Rashal, for also the Rav in the book Kereti UFeleti agrees with him and explained the reason: since the rust is not on the head of the needle with which it punctures, we say that after shechitah it pressed and punctured with its head; and so Minchat Yaakov klal 94 brought only Maharshal's opinion. And especially since rust is not mentioned in the Gemara and is also not such a proof as blood to say it was from life, for it is possible that it swallowed thus a rusty needle; and from the Taz the Peri Chadash and other later authorities are lenient regarding rust when the needle is found in the cavity of the innard — which is not so regarding blood; and likewise when stuck from one side and one did not check if there is rust upon it, Knesset HaGedolah permits and Levushei Serad brought him even without great financial loss — which is not so when it was lost without checking for a clot of blood. And it emerges that since all the deficiency here is because it was rinsed before bringing the needle and the checking went away — if so it is only a doubtful tereifah; and so the Rashba wrote in a responsum from which is the source of this law of rinsed, that we declare it tereifah from doubt; and Beit Yosef and Be'er HaGolah bring him. And if so in any case it avails regarding a double doubt as will be explained. And even though Maharam of Lublin in a responsum, brought in the Shach siman 50, wrote regarding a needle that is common that it is more for issur than to permit — that is regarding permitting it entirely not for the reason of "once slaughtered it is permitted"; but nevertheless it is well considered a doubt regarding a double doubt, as is written explicitly in the book Peri Megadim in his introduction to Hilchot Tereifot and in Mishbetzot Zahav siman 33 se'if katan 6 — because it has a chazakah for heter, for when it was born there was no needle in it; and this reasoning he also wrote in the book Pri Chadash at the end of the chapter "HaMadir"; and if the doubt were entirely equal we would have to permit because of a chazakah, but because it is more common for issur we do not permit; but because it has a chazakah it avails regarding a double doubt. And consider yourself: if the Ravyah is in doubt regarding the law of rinsed and it entered his mind to permit entirely for the reason of "once slaughtered it is permitted," as is written in Haggahot Asheri "Eilu Tereifot" — shall we not at least count it as a doubt? And further, is it inferior to a doubtful derusah of which Tosafot wrote that it is common for issur and even so it avails regarding a double doubt in siman 57? And likewise when a wing is found broken and it is not known whether it was done from life or after shechitah — we permit for the reason of a double doubt in siman 53 and end of siman 110; and there too it is more common that it break from life, for it shakes and goes here and there, as is written in responsa Beit Yaakov siman 84: that even though it is common for issur it is a doubt regarding a double doubt because it has a chazakah for heter. And further one may say here also to attribute for heter: that it is possible that through the shaking of its transport a parasang's journey it pressed and passed from side to side, and beforehand it was not from side to side, and for this reason the needle was not seen in its innard before they transported it; and so they told me that the innard was lying by the wall where they leave needles there; and in such a case one can also attribute for heter, for several later authorities permit entirely as explained in siman 50 and in responsa Beit Yaakov the aforementioned siman — for attribution is better than a doubt. And since we have merited to rule that in any case it is only a doubtful tereifah — if so if it were recognizable on its own, certainly because of the salting alone we would not forbid the rest; for Shevut Yaakov permitted a doubtful tereifah that was salted with kosher meat because it is a double doubt: perhaps it was not a tereifah, and perhaps the law is like the Ravyah who holds that salting forbids only enough for peeling; and Peri Megadim brings him in siman 105 and in several places, unlike Levushei Serad who raised a difficulty against Minchat Yaakov that an individual against many is not called a double doubt — for therefore in great financial loss we rely on an individual against many as is stated in Choshen Mishpat siman 25 and in the Shach Yoreh De'ah siman 242; know that Minchat Yaakov himself so wrote in the laws of double doubt regarding an individual, etc. Also what Levushei Serad wrote to forbid because of the peel that was forbidden in salting — he himself did not so write in siman 91. And see in Noda B'Yehuda siman 20 what he wrote that one should not forbid in cooking because of a peel that was forbidden in salting in its kind, for perhaps we do not say in salting chaticha na'aseh neveilah, and afterwards the issur was squeezed in cooking — it is like a doubt of poured regarding d'rabbanan. And so it is implied from the Bach's words siman 74, brought by Minchat Yaakov klal 31: that regarding a doubtful issur we do not forbid in salting more than enough for peeling. And see there in Peri Megadim. And further there is [room] to permit: for here there is a doubt whether they salted the issur with the heter at all so much that afterwards there was not in the rest of the mixtures a majority; and also it is possible that the issur was salted only on its own and afterwards mixed; and if so it is exactly like a doubt whether unclean fish were salted with clean ones, which we permit in siman 83 seif 5 for the reason of a double doubt: perhaps they were not salted together at all, and even if you say they were salted together perhaps there was no fat in them and then they are forbidden only d'rabbanan. And so too here: perhaps the tereifah was not salted with them at all, and even if you say it was salted perhaps it was lean and then sixty is needed only d'rabbanan, as is written in the book Peri Megadim in his introduction to Hilchot Taarovot. And see in the later authorities siman 69 seif 11: that wherever one can attribute that the issur was not salted at all with the heter, we attribute to be lenient in its kind even regarding milk and fat. And that which in siman 70 it is implied that a doubt whether tereifah meat was salted is forbidden even though it is in its kind — one may say that there he says to forbid enough for peeling; or also when there is no majority; but when there is a majority and a doubt whether it was salted it is permitted; and in responsa Even HaEzer he wrote that such a case is like a doubtful mixture which is permitted in buying and selling when there is a majority as explained in siman 111. And since because of giving taste of salting there is no reason to forbid — if so there is here only because the issur was mixed and is not recognizable on its own; and if so it has the law of dry in dry, regarding which we rule that one in two is nullified, for the entire mixture is transformed and becomes heter. And if because it is a piece worthy of honor regarding which we rule in siman 101 that it is not nullified — one should rule here that it is well nullified, for there is here a complete double doubt of one matter: perhaps the law is like Rabbeinu Tam and his supporters that Turei Zahav brings in siman 110, that a doubtful tereifah that was mixed and the doubtful issur was not known beforehand is permitted even regarding important things that are not nullified, for they hold that this is counted as a double doubt even though one doubt is in the body of the issur and the second doubt is through mixture. And even if you say that the law is not like Rabbeinu Tam to permit in such a case regarding unusual things — perhaps the law is like the Rashba and his supporters brought in Yoreh De'ah siman 101 who hold that a large piece is not worthy of honor unless it is worthy of honor as it is; and so the Mechaber brought there this opinion anonymously; and even though the gloss there wrote that we practice like "there is one who disputes" that he brought there, that in every manner it is worthy of honor — already it is explained in the book Peri Megadim there and at the end of siman 110 that such a case is counted as a doubt — see there. And if so it is a complete double doubt that is entirely in the mixture: perhaps like Rabbeinu Tam that even regarding an important thing it is nullified in such a case; and perhaps like the Rashba that it is not an important thing at all and certainly it is nullified in the majority. And again I found that such a case is stated explicitly in responsa Mabit siman 37, and Levushei Serad in the siman brought him: that if a piece worthy of honor regarding which we practice to be stringent was mixed with others, and there is a double doubt even one doubt regarding a bird, etc. — all the more so it is permitted; and that is as I wrote. And further that even in each one of these doubts there are several later authorities who are lenient; for the Shach in the abridgment of the laws of double doubt siman 1 ruled like Rabbeinu Tam; and even though he was stringent in this to eat two by two, nevertheless other later authorities are entirely lenient when it was not known, as Minchat Yaakov wrote, brought in the book Peri Megadim, and he agreed to this. And likewise regarding a piece worthy of honor the later authorities brought in the name of the book Magen Giborim who wrote that it is proper to rely on the Rashba that a large piece is not worthy of honor. And especially that according to the Rif a piece worthy of honor is nullified so long as it is not a prohibition of benefit, as the Rashba and the Rosh wrote, brought by Beit Yosef siman 101. And so I found that Rabbeinu Yeruham wrote so in the name of Rashi chapter 2 of Avodah Zarah. And since so, we should not be so stringent regarding the law of a piece worthy of honor as in our case where there is also a double doubt to permit. And like the gaon in Noda B'Yehuda siman 33. And one should not forbid because of what the Shach wrote in Yoreh De'ah siman 110 seif 5: that if a tereifah was found in the butcher shops it is forbidden to take from then on even a piece not worthy of honor, for not everyone is expert in this and they will err between worthy of honor and not worthy. For that is specifically in butcher shops where many buy and the matter is near to err — they decreed to forbid even a piece not worthy of honor. And Nekudot HaKesef explained that here the many are not in one gathering and for this reason he did not say they remind one another, and with this he resolves the Taz's difficulty here from the chapter "Chavit" — see there. [And know that with this is also rejected what the Taz wrote in Hilchot Avel siman 381 — see there. And it appears there is a proof for this distinction from that which is said in the Gemara at the beginning of Eruvin (daf 3) regarding a mavoi part of which is within twenty and part above twenty: that the many rely on one another and do not remind — for there too the many are not all in one gathering.] And what the book Peri Megadim raised as a difficulty on the Rashba and the Mechaber who wrote this law of butcher shops — yet they hold that raw [meat] is nullified and there is no [room] to decree on account of a piece worthy of honor — that is not so, for the Rashba wrote explicitly in the book Mishmeret HaBayit (daf 120b) that a raw piece is well worthy of honor so long as it is worthy of honor as it is; and the Mechaber too wrote so according to "there is one who disputes" that he brought there in siman 101 that a raw or large piece is considered worthy of honor, and it is well applicable to decree that which is not worthy on account of that which is worthy; and all this is in butcher shops where many are common; and there too it is only a mere stringency as the Tur wrote in the name of the Rashba, and it is in Torat HaBayit HaKatzar; and the Rashba himself in Torat HaBayit HaAroch daf 120 wrote regarding butcher shops that specifically a medium piece worthy of honor is forbidden, but a piece not worthy of honor is permitted. And so all the later authorities wrote that this law of butcher shops is only a mere stringency, and let us not add upon it; and for this reason for an individual in his house one should not be stringent at all to forbid a piece not worthy of honor. And that which the later authorities brought there in seif 5 the case of Beit Hillel regarding a butcher who slaughtered seven animals in his house, etc., and a needle was found in the beit hakosot that was a tereifah according to law without hesitation, and he declared tereifah all those that were in the house, etc. — there too it was regarding a butcher by whom many are common; and even though it was in his house, nevertheless wherever many are common by him they decreed that which is not worthy of honor on account of that which is worthy of honor. And so it is implied from the Shach's language there who wrote "and for an individual in his house one should not be stringent" — it is implied that for a butcher in his house one should be stringent. And further, for there it was a tereifah without hesitation — which is not so here where it is only a doubtful tereifah; and for an individual in his house certainly one should not be stringent for this reason that I wrote. Know that the Rav Peri Megadim and other later authorities brought the aforementioned case of Beit Hillel and even so permitted in such a case as I will write; and therefore it is for this reason that we said. And greater than this I saw the Rav Peri Megadim in Siftei Da'at siman 110 se'if katan 37 who permitted regarding a doubtful tereifah in a shop that was mixed with a kosher shop: perhaps the law is like the Ran who holds that a mixture of shops is not kavu'a and is nullified, and perhaps it is not a tereifah. And if regarding a mixture of shops where there is one who says it is d'oraisa, and further they were mixed one in one, he permitted because of the aforementioned double doubt — all the more so one should permit regarding a piece worthy of honor which is d'rabbanan that was mixed in a majority, for it is a complete double doubt like a mixture as I wrote. Further there is in our case a supporting reason to permit according to what is written in responsa Maharival regarding a doubtful tereifah to permit the vessels to cook in them after a night has passed for the reason of a d'rabbanan doubt to be treated leniently; and the later authorities bring him in siman 1 regarding a slaughterer who converted or was found not to know. And so the author of Peri Megadim brought at the beginning of the laws of double doubt and leans there to be lenient in great financial loss in such a case regarding a doubt of a Torah issur that through rolling became d'rabbanan when it was not known beforehand until it became d'rabbanan — for example a doubt of a Torah issur that fell into its kind in liquid to a majority and was not known — see there; and so he wrote in siman 81. And if so so too in our case one may lean that it became a d'rabbanan doubt since it was not known until after it was mixed in its kind in a majority; and even if it were not dry it would avail; and all the more so here where there are poskim who permit and hold that it is nullified without that, as above; and see in the book Levushei Serad. But according to what Sha'ar HaMelech wrote in Hilchot Mikvaot klal 6 from the sugya of Yoma (daf 55b) it is implied that it does not avail that it was not known — only when the two doubts come together does it avail. And it appears to me there is a proof from Rashi in Shabbos (daf 34a); and with this one may say what Beit Yosef wrote in siman 69 — and examine carefully there. And see in the responsum of the Rif that Beit Yosef brought in siman 101 that it is implied even when the two doubts did not come together — see there. And see in the Ran chapter "Kol Sha'ah" who brought a proof from the sugya of the chapter "Eilu Ovrin" (daf 44) regarding that which he said: and if you say an olive's bulk in the time of eating a half-loaf is d'oraisa, why do we say "I say" — it is implied that if we say it is d'rabbanan it is fine that we attribute to be lenient; and even though the essence of the issur is d'oraisa, since its measure is d'rabbanan we follow to be lenient; and so it is implied from what the Rambam wrote in chapter 8 of Hilchot Mikvaot. However according to what the Shach wrote in the laws of double doubt siman 19 there is no proof from there, for we attribute that there was no issur here at all; or also that there they came as one. And so the Rashba's words in a responsum siman 488 are resolved with this — see there. But there is a proof from that of Yoma (daf 64) regarding a mixture of disqualifications of ba'alei mumim: that they are nullified from the Torah, only d'rabbanan they are not nullified, and we attribute to be lenient if one of them was offered, etc., even though certainly an issur was mixed into it. But according to what Sha'ar HaMelech wrote in Hilchot Mikvaot there is also no proof from there. And see Gittin (daf 53b); and so it is implied in the Rashba's responsum there that a doubt of a d'oraisa issur that was mixed in a majority is a d'rabbanan doubt. And see the Rosh chapter 3 of Sukkah regarding the inquiry of an etrog, and in chapters 2 and 3 of Pesachim regarding a doubt of a bit and a doubt of checking that were removed and afterwards rolled and became d'rabbanan — that we follow to be lenient in a d'rabbanan doubt even though the essence of its obligation is d'oraisa. And so he brought anonymously in the Shulchan Aruch Orach Chayyim siman 648 seif 4. What emerges from all that I have written is that our case is only dry in dry; and according to the opinion of the Rif and Rashi and Rabbeinu Tam and his supporters and the Shach and Magen Giborim and Minchat Yaakov and Peri Megadim and Maharival the mixture is permitted; and if so we should not be stringent regarding a d'rabbanan issur, for the Torah is concerned for the money of Israel as is said in the chapter "Eilu Tereifot" (daf 49); and all these poskim are worthy to rely upon in such a case.
