And if one of the mixtures fell, etc. — in this seif there is room to wonder on Shulchan Aruch and on Rama; first let us clarify the matters. It is stated in the first chapter of Beitza on daf 3 regarding an egg born on Yom Tov, that a safek is forbidden, and if it became mixed in a thousand all are forbidden; and Rabbeinu Tam challenged — for behold it is double safek when it became mixed; and therefore Rabbeinu Tam explained that "if it became mixed" refers to when it was certainly born on Yom Tov, but if the safek became mixed, it is double safek and permitted; and even though it is stated there afterward regarding kilayim of a vineyard — if they became mixed in others and others in others, all shall be burned — it appears double safek does not help there according to Rabbeinu Tam, and "others in others"; and R. Yochanan disagrees with him in Yevamos chapter HaAreil and holds that in such a case it is not double safek, for the reason mentioned in the seif after this — rather three mixtures are required; and as it is stated in Zevachim (daf 74) regarding issur of orlah that fell into myriads of issurim and from the myriads into three and from the three into another place it is permitted — learn that three mixtures are required; and Rashi explained: when there are three mixtures, then even the middle ones are permitted; and Tosafot there in Zevachim wrote that in the second mixture it is forbidden to benefit from all at once, but from the third mixture it is permitted to benefit from all at once — and not as the Kuntres explained, end of his words. And behold according to Rabbeinu Tam, R. Levi there in Zevachim — only two mixtures — Rashba holds like Rabbeinu Tam, who wrote: if the minority separated from the majority they are permitted, even without mixtures afterward, by double safek — and that is both regarding living creatures, which he recalled in Terumat HaDeshen in the fourth house gate 1 daf 103, and regarding a cup of a non-Jew on daf 114; and he himself explained in the book Mishmeret HaBayit that the reason is that he deals with safek tereifah that became mixed — therefore if they separated it is permitted by double safek, etc., see there; and this is unlike Maharach z"l, who wrote here to distinguish between living creatures and other important items; and in Tur too he wrote likewise: if the minority separated they are permitted; and according to him it is impossible to say that he deals with safek tereifah, for he does not deal except with certainty that became mixed; and in order not to leave a difficulty — behold he himself wrote above from this that even if it separated not in our presence it is forbidden — one must say that "they are permitted" that he says is through mixtures, as he wrote afterward: "and if all the majority became mixed," etc. — this is the explanation on the beginning; and Beit Yosef wrote likewise on Rashba's words — and that is briefly, that he wrote in Tur's language on this; but not at length, that it is written that he deals with safek tereifah that became mixed — therefore the minority that separated is permitted even if it did not fall to another place; and Tur's view is like Rabbeinu Tam and Rashba, that two mixtures suffice; and Rambam holds like R. Yochanan that we recalled — and therefore the Mechaber copied his language here that we require three mixtures, and holds that the second mixture remains in its issur — and not as I recalled in the name of Rashi, and also not like R. Yochanan. And there is wonder on Shulchan Aruch — for above in this siman seif 4 he ruled like Rashba that two mixtures suffice, and here he ruled like Rambam. And on Rama there is room to wonder in what he wrote on Shulchan Aruch — and nevertheless one person may not eat all of them — and this is according to Beit Yosef's words, who wrote thus since the permission is by double safek; and Rama holds that also regarding three mixtures Beit Yosef wrote thus — for he wrote stam this regarding something permitted by double safek. And behold explicitly Tosafot that I recalled wrote that all are permitted in the third mixture, even at once; and likewise the implication of Terumat HaDeshen's words — that what is permitted by double safek is fully permitted, and he did not say one must not benefit at once, as he recalled afterward when one of them fell to the sea; and so too explicitly in Issur VeHeter klal 26 — that safek issur that became mixed and it is an important item is permitted in the second mixture to benefit from all at once; and certain issur is forbidden in the second mixture to benefit at once, but in the third mixture it is permitted to benefit at once — and it follows there is great wonder on Rama, who copied Issur VeHeter's words here as he wrote "but safek issur," etc.; and at first he wrote that one person may not eat all of them — it follows there is a contradiction: why is the second mixture in safek issur different from the third mixture in certain issur — requires study to resolve his words; for according to what appears, the third mixture at once in certain issur is permitted — and Beit Yosef who wrote that one person may not eat all of them, according to Rama's understanding that it applies also to three mixtures — even though he did not see Issur VeHeter — nevertheless from Tosafot's words there is room to wonder on him, as we recalled; behold before you is a refutation of Rama. However I found support for Shulchan Aruch to forbid eating all of them or casting one of them — from the words of Hagahot ShaDa, and these are his words: once R. Yitzchak slaughtered many poultry for one wedding and checked the knife between each one, and after he slaughtered many he found the knife damaged and did not mention in which poultry; and he asked Rabbeinu Tam, and he answered: cast one and then all are permitted — for it is nullified, since it is not a piece worthy of honor, since feathers are still on it and it lacks great preparation; and even though each one stands in double safek — safek whether this is it, and if you say this is it, perhaps the vertebra bone was damaged — nevertheless, as soon as the knife was damaged it was forbidden, and one may not permit without casting one, end of his words. And at first glance this hagahah is puzzling — for according to Rabbeinu Tam double safek helps even though only one is equal in the substance of the issur and the second through mixtures; if so, why did he answer nevertheless, as soon as it was damaged, etc.; and further, why did he permit specifically because there are feathers there — behold even a piece worthy of honor would be permitted by double safek; and it appears that in truth there is permission also by double safek — but Rabbeinu Tam took even certain issur first, and afterward wrote: even though each one stands in double safek — he did not come to forbid everything, but only regarding casting one he speaks — for even though there is double safek in each one, and if so there are two reasons for leniency and one need not cast one — on this he resolved: since nevertheless, as soon as the knife was damaged, on one of them there was certainly issur — therefore one must be stringent and one may not permit without casting one; and therefore he concluded again: and one may not permit without casting one, even though they already said — but as I explained, this is the conclusion of the answer — so it appears to me the correct explanation of Hagahot Shaarei Dura, and not as I saw they force its explanation in another matter; and therefore Shulchan Aruch ruled well that where there is permission by double safek one must cast one — and this is from the side of stringency, since at any rate on one of them there was clear issur at first; and if so, also when safek issur became mixed, whose law is that only two mixtures are required — likewise one must forbid at least one of them — for what difference is there; and Rama should not have written stam that it is permitted, for it implies everything — rather Rama did not intend this; and specifically "why" that is adjacent to the matter of one person eating all of them — rather it refers to what Shulchan Aruch wrote that we require three mixtures — on this he said: specifically certain issur; Some say regarding davar sheyesh lo matirin, etc. — Tur wrote in the name of some say that for all important items that are not nullified, double safek does not help in them, and he disagreed with this; and Beit Yosef holds that those "some say" are the view of R. Yochanan, who does not permit by double safek unless it is in one substance, as will be explained afterward; and he was not precise, for certainly in this Tur agrees as well, as he wrote nearby explicitly — rather he deals with a complete double safek; and those "some say" are in Semak at the end of siman 214. Nevertheless what Rama wrote here regarding davar sheyesh lo matirin — some say double safek does not help, and he concludes to be lenient in a case of need — this is puzzling, for Turei Chayim at the end of klal 94 wrote, and these are his words: Ran wrote at the beginning of the chapter Ein Tzedadin that double safek is permitted in davar sheyesh lo matirin, such as a safek egg on the second day of Yom Tov, end of his words; and Arukh klal 25 wrote that the custom is to forbid even double safek in davar sheyesh lo matirin, end of his words, Turei Chayim. And behold in Ran there it is explained that specifically regarding rabbinic issur it is permitted by double safek in davar sheyesh lo matirin — that is, safek egg on the second day of Yom Tov, since muktzeh is forbidden only d'rabbanan, it is double safek in d'rabbanan; and perhaps Turei Chayim also intended this in what he wrote "safek egg," etc., to indicate that he deals specifically with d'rabbanan. But it is difficult what he concluded that Arukh disagrees with this — and perhaps he too concedes this and was stringent only regarding Torah issur; and so it appears explicitly from his words in klal 25 of this law that he wrote, and these are his words: apart from davar sheyesh lo matirin, that even if there is safek in something, etc., all of them if there is double safek in them are permitted without any nullification at all, end of his words.
