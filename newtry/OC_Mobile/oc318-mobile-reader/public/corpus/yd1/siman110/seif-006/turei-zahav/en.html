That he separated intentionally. For then specifically we decree lest one take from the fixed item — that is Rashba's view; but Tur in the name of Rosh concludes that even if it separated automatically it is forbidden, lest one take from the fixed item; and Maharshal ruled in chapter Gid HaNashe siman 24 like him; and so Turei Chayim klal 43 wrote that it is good to be stringent.
That if one separated intentionally, etc. — This distinction was written to resolve what we rule here, that even if it separated there is issur lest one take from the fixed item — and that is R. Yochanan's view, which Beit Yosef brought in siman 57 regarding a gored animal, and there ruled like R. Tam that it helps if we confine them and we say "everything that separates from the majority separates" — therefore he distinguishes with this distinction; and Darkei Moshe and Turei Chayim wrote that Maharam Schina answered him accordingly. I wondered: behold we say explicitly in Zevachim (73a) that this does not help — there it challenged regarding living creatures that became mixed, that all are forbidden for sacrifice — why offer one of them and say "everything that separates from the majority separates"? And it answered: it would be kevua, and every fixed item, etc.; and it challenged: let us confine them so they move, and say "everything that separates from the majority separates" — meaning then there would no longer be fixedness; and Rava answered: a decree lest one take from the fixed item. If so, behold explicitly that even uprooting all of them does not help. And Turei Chayim brings proof for this distinction from Orach Chayyim siman 329 regarding whether one may remove the heap on Shabbos in safek of a Jew — that if all fixedness is uprooted it helps — see there; and it is not relevant here, for there that decree does not apply as here. Further, if uprooting all helps, why do we say there in Zevachim that all graze until they die — and behold certainly all will be uprooted in the end? I saw many forced resolutions on this; what appears to me is correct: after all, R. Yochanan does not disagree with R. Tam, who distinguishes between consecrated animals and hullin, as Beit Yosef copied Tosafos' and Rosh's words — rather regarding the law itself, namely what is written in chapter HaTaarovot, that we do not permit if individuals separated from there — for R. Tam holds that in hullin it is permitted even in such a case, even if they separated before us, only that one should not scatter them by hand; but in hullin too it is forbidden — why is this different? Since they placed upon this the name kevua; nevertheless, if all are moved here, it is permitted by law, only because of the decree lest one take from the fixed item, as it says there in the Gemara — in this R. Yochanan concedes that in hullin they did not decree so much, for in consecrated animals they were more stringent; and what Tur wrote here: "but living creatures and other important items, even if they separated," etc. — we decree lest one take from the fixed item — that is if one does not scatter all of them, for then there is no permission except if they separated not before us; in this there is good reason to decree even in hullin lest one take from the fixed item, for the observer will not notice any distinguishing mark — unlike if we confine them so they move, and if we permit through this there is a great distinguishing mark in the matter and one will not come to err and take from the fixed item; but in consecrated animals, since they were stringent, they decreed even in this. And that Tur did not write here the remedy to confine until they move — because here it includes other important items too, to which this does not apply, as Tosafos wrote in chapter HaNisrafim (85) in the name of R. Tam, that this applies only to living creatures — therefore Tur did not write here from this, and relied on what he wrote above in siman 57 that there is this remedy for living creatures. So it appears to me, correct.
