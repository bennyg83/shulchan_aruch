"By itself." The Shach wrote: even according to the one who forbids separated-by-itself — and the reason that separated-by-itself is forbidden is lest one take by hand from the fixed quantity, and we do not find such a decree except where they already stand in presumption of issur; but before the doubt arose it is not proper to decree anything, for all stand in presumption of heter. What do you say — lest one take after the doubt arose? We do not find such a decree in the Gemara. And furthermore, here there is no concern to err, for if he took while it was still in presumption of heter, for that reason he will not come to take after they were forbidden. And all this is when it separated not before us, or when all of them scattered — even before us it is considered as if it separated not before us. Specifically if the minority separated — then we say "whatever separated, etc.," and the issur remained within the majority; but if half of them separated at once, they are forbidden. However, most poskim disagree and hold that even if they separated by itself not before us, one must forbid; and the conclusion of the Taz and Shach is that one should be stringent (and some say regarding nine shops — there the issur is recognizable in its place, unlike here where everything is mixed together, we are concerned). And there is no distinction between live animals and other things — not like the Bach. And the Shach wrote: if they separated, even though they returned and came together in one place — as long as all of them scattered first and were uprooted from the original fixation, it is no longer considered a fixed case, for the law of fixed case is a novelty and you have in it only its novelty — that is, that it remained in its first place; and the Peri Chadash disagrees.
"An egg." For we say "whatever separated from the majority is considered separated"; and even according to the one who holds that an egg is considered davar sheb'minyan — it is permitted, for in nine shops it is permitted in separated-by-itself, even a piece worthy of honor; for this reason we do not decree regarding an egg lest the owner take from the fixed quantity; end of his words, Shach.
