Important things, etc. The Rashba — and according to his view and She'iltot — as noted.
And that which is forbidden, etc. As written in Yoma chapter 4, 2 — Rav asks there regarding "separated," etc. And according to the Rosh's explanation there "That which separated all of them," etc. — since all were uprooted there is no fixed Israelite here, and "whatever separates from the majority separates" — he means we compare there to separated before us. And if fixed ones remained, "separated before us" does not help — not so when all were uprooted that there is no fixed here. And so too the Rashba — that if all scattered, even before us it is permitted. And the Shach s.k. 35 — and he explains "separated all" that all scattered. And the Rosh is more lenient — that even if they did not scatter, only all were uprooted, and one separated from them it is permitted. And according to his explanation "separated all" it refers to the first one who separated to another courtyard and separated that one separated from all of them. And the Rashba's explanation is more precise. And he wrote here according to the Rosh's explanation that the same applies to separating lechatchilah at the time of their binding it is permitted, for there is no room to decree because of kavu'a, for there is no kavu'a here. And he wrote this to resolve the Tur's words at the end of seif 57 regarding a trampled mixture who explained "let us pen them so they wander," etc. And the rabbi explained that it deals with such a case — and it is not correct, for nevertheless "let us pen them" lechatchilah — for otherwise the question in Zevachim there "and let us pen them for all of them." And so too the Rashba in Terumat HaDeshen the short version — "Whoever all scattered are all permitted," etc. — in another matter when they scattered by themselves; but in our case if they scattered they are forbidden — a decree lest one take from their place of gathering. And likewise forbidden to gather them in a narrow place so they are shaken and scatter — for whatever causes their movement one must decree lest one take from the fixed. But the Tur's words there are according to Tosafot's explanation in Sanhedrin there in the name of R' Tam, as noted. And likewise one must say the words of Halakhot Gedolot that the Rosh brought in the chapter "or mixed with" that "or mixed were let us pen them," etc., and as written above in the Shulchan Aruch at the end of seif 16. And the later authorities agreed with R' Yitzchak's explanation. And likewise the Rosh agreed in chapter 3 of Gittin and responsa. And nevertheless the rabbi's law is true when all separated by themselves — that afterward even intentionally it is permitted. And this is what he says "but if they separated" — he means by themselves — and there is no difficulty from that in Zevachim there "and they separated forty to a place," etc. — "one separated one of sixty forbids." What he wrote there "returns to rest and fixation," as Tosafot wrote in Yoma there s.v. hen, etc., and as written in Nazir (12, 1) — there "returns to rest," and Rashi explained there. And this is what he says "and at the time of their uprooting," etc.
They separated some, etc. — he means even intentionally, and it refers above to what he said "And that," etc.
Only, etc. — for we do not need "whatever separates from the majority separates," and as written in Zevachim there what he wrote "From three," etc. "two" — and he [means one of two].
And if it was mixed, etc. — for he separated by itself and it is permitted even according to R' Yitzchak's explanation — that it is not applicable to decree lest one take from the fixed, for there is no mixture here in an egg, and it is not applicable to decree because of the roosters. And he means by this even for Semak — that an egg is davar sheb'minyan — nevertheless with separation it is permitted since there is no mixture in it, as noted. And he said "was mixed" to include — all the more so without it being mixed, that separation is permitted, as written in seif 3.
