"Live animals, etc." — To be: for this approach of "let us suppress them since they move," etc. — Tosafot expanded in Zevachim and Sanhedrin; and we already wrote above siman 57 in Tur that he cited the incident of R' Tam who permitted a gored animal because whatever separates, etc.; and Tur in this siman ruled like Rosh: wherever the issur is recognizable it is forbidden and we do not say whatever separates, etc. — a decree lest he take from kevua; and above in siman 57 in the incident of R' Tam regarding safek gored that became mixed — Semag's view is to permit by force of double safek; and Tur wrote there is permission even for certain gored that "let us suppress them since they move," etc.; and they wondered on Tur that he contradicts his words; and Beit Yosef wrote Tur wrote thus in Semak's approach by way of "if you wish" — if you wish to be lenient like R' Tam who has double safek, go and be lenient like R' Tam who distinguishes between hullin and kodshim and holds whatever separates, etc. — until here is his language; and I already wrote above siman 57 in Peleti that this too is astonishing — who connected these laws to each other until we would say if R' Tam's law is true and just, Beit Yosef is also true and just — it would be astonishing if Semag regarding two safeks holds like R' Tam and regarding "let us suppress them," etc. does not rule like R' Tam; and as I wrote in Peleti; and it appears further that I already wrote above there is to wonder on R' Tam who permits in the explanation and we do not decree lest he take, etc. — because he distinguishes between hullin and kodshim — for the difficulty is from Tosafot Zevachim daf 71 s.v. "even" who challenged R' Tam from that ring of avodah zarah that separated forty to one place and sixty to forty-one and one fell from the forty to the others — Rav and Shmuel disagree and in one of sixty all forbid — and why should we say the majority separated; and Tosafot resolved: one must distinguish regarding important items since it is possible through the remedy of "let us suppress them since they move," etc. — see there; and it is difficult: if so nine stores that also cannot be remedied — why permitted; and one must say there the issur is recognizable and we are not concerned he will take from kevua; and according to this we are forced to say also this reasoning — and whence to distinguish between hullin and kodshim since from this in "found" we follow majority — requires study. And in my novellae I said to sharpen students and the Holy One blessed be He rejoices in pilpul; and it appears from that baraita in Sanhedrin daf 76 that teaches: a goring animal — its offspring is forbidden, its offspring became mixed with others and others with others — all gather them into a pen, etc.; and one can say: why did it count this specifically on offspring of the goring animal and not on the goring animal itself if it became mixed with others, etc. — and what is its nature that it chose its offspring? Therefore it appears R' Tam holds to distinguish between hullin and kodshim lest ten priests come at once in the Temple and take from kevua; and one must understand in hullin too lest they come at once and slaughter and eat and return to fixedness; and one must say: since d'oraisa was already nullified one in two, only the Sages forbade because of their importance — if so even if ten come, etc. — nevertheless there is no concern of Torah issur, for it was already nullified and they decreed only in the stringency of kodshim. And accordingly according to what Maharil wrote above siman 100 from Tosafot beginning of Bava Metzia regarding "jumped," etc. — it appears their words: d'oraisa is not nullified; and as I wrote: at least one must say they made it like Torah law without being nullified — see there; and this offspring of the goring animal forbidden from the beginning of its creation — if so it is beriah and is not nullified; if so the law is equal to kodshim and we do not say "let us suppress them," etc.; therefore the baraita that teaches became mixed with others, etc. — and it is difficult to suppress since they move; therefore it teaches offspring of the goring animal that is beriah and suppression does not apply to it — a decree lest he take, etc. — and requires study; and R' Tam's words are forced. But still one can say: therefore it counted offspring of the goring animal because it is difficult as Tosafot wrote in "became mixed with others and others with others" — behold there is double safek in mixtures; but offspring of the goring animal is beriah and beriah by Torah law is not nullified — if so the first safek is forbidden d'oraisa and the second in mixtures we do not say; therefore it teaches offspring of the goring animal; if so Tur's words "if you wish" on Semag who rules regarding gored double safek like these — even though the first safek is d'oraisa nevertheless it is called double safek; if so it is difficult why it counted offspring of the goring and not the goring itself — for what difference if the first safek is d'oraisa; and all the more so another reason besides goring applies that suppression, etc. helps; and accordingly also in Semak's incident this remedy in its place to suppress, etc.; investigate. In any case there is here room: whence did R' Tam derive his law to distinguish between hullin and kodshim. However that above difficulty to R' Tam who distinguishes between hullin and kodshim — if so that which teaches in Avodah Zarah these forbid in any amount — necessarily it deals where one cannot suppress them since they die and the like, as Tosafot wrote; but regarding all sacrifices even though one can suppress them, nevertheless forbidden lest he take from kevua; if so that which the Gemara challenged there regarding all sacrifices that commoner too — behold he taught it these are forbidden and forbid in any amount; and the Gemara answered: if from there one would say specifically for commoner but for the Most High one might say do not lose all of them, etc.; and it is difficult: what need for the derivation — behold it teaches much that suppression does not help for the Most High unlike commoner where it helps; if so this one's law is not like that one's — and obviously it needed to teach and it teaches much; and one must say: otherwise it is difficult why the Gemara challenged "let us suppress them" — behold as long as we do not suppress them and they explained all forbidden to the altar — if so it is rejected from the altar and there is rejection regarding kodshim — and what helps that we suppress them. And strained to say: since it is in his power to suppress it is not called rejection — for at least initially it was not fit; and in Hadas even though it is in his power to gather grapes however small it is called rejection — see there in Sukkah. And further difficult: why Tosafot challenged in Chullin and Zevachim why should they graze — behold since it separated automatically it is permitted — behold there is rejection regarding kodshim that when it is in his power the animal will graze and separate; and one must say the Gemara's difficulty is according to one who holds important items are not rejected — if so it should be difficult "let us suppress them," etc.; but according to one who holds important items are rejected the Gemara does not challenge anything; if so one can say the Gemara's difficulty is from that Avodah Zarah and the derivation it makes for one who holds important items are rejected — if so "let us suppress them," etc. does not apply and he challenged well. And further one can say: certainly that which the Gemara challenged "we learned" refers to dead animals or stoned ox — but other disqualified offerings of the altar that are not prohibitions of benefit it did not challenge — for this does not follow from that Avodah Zarah where prohibitions of benefit are counted that are not nullified; but treifah and other disqualified offerings of the altar do not follow at all from there; and until here the Gemara's difficulty: let us teach only the other first ones — it should have omitted; and on this the Gemara makes the derivation; and if so why need what it teaches that we do not say "let us suppress them" — behold that treifah and offspring of wall it should have taught to suppress them, etc.; and one must say in kodshim they were stringent; if so all the more so the first clause; investigate.
"But if separated of itself it is permitted." — This is Rashba's view. And he challenged from this that we say: forty to forty-one do not forbid and sixty forbid — and we do not say the majority separated, for they separated of themselves; and Rashba answered in Terumat HaDeshen that it deals that they separated before us; and according to Tosafot that separated before us is kevua d'oraisa — if so if they separated before us it is full kevua and kevua is a novelty and it is like half-and-half; if so how do we say to distinguish between forty and sixty that the issur is in the majority — behold kevua like half-and-half and both are taken from kevua — and what do we care whether much or little — the Torah's decree that it should be like half-and-half; and this is the reason Tosafot challenged from that ring — did they not notice one can say separated before us, as written in Pesachim; rather they hold it is kevua d'oraisa; and Rashba holds it is not kevua d'oraisa; and Pri Chadash's view in s.k. 19 is that Rashba holds it is kevua d'oraisa — and it is not as I wrote.
"But if all uprooted, etc." — This is R' Shachna's view to resolve Tur; and Shach in s.k. 37 sought to bring proof from Rashba's words — and there only in "they all separated of themselves" is mentioned, but they were before us — there Rashba wrote: since no fixedness remained it is like not before us; but regarding lest he take from kevua — we do find in Rashba a difference whether all were uprooted or some remained; and it was renewed from the mouth of R' Shachna, may his memory be blessed, and he compared it to what is written in Orach Chayyim Laws of Shabbat: if in one courtyard there is majority gentiles and one Jew and one gentile separated and the avalanche fell on him — we desecrate if all separated and at the time of their separation one separated and the avalanche fell on him — we do not desecrate; and Shach in s.k. 37 wrote the case under discussion is not similar to the proof — and so it appears at first glance they are not similar at all; but one can say: in truth R' Shimshon wrote thus to resolve Tur's and Rosh's words who ruled above siman 66 regarding oto v'et beno that suppression helps and he holds to distinguish because we decree lest he take from kevua — that is, for otherwise it is not clear to us that in that case there is no issur; and I already wrote above in the name of Rosh whatever separates, etc. — nothing emerges from safek, only the Torah permitted; and with this the Sages saw to decree lest he take from kevua — for otherwise in presumption of safek; but if it is clear to us that certainly permitted — permitted and we do not decree lest he take from kevua; therefore when all were uprooted where there is no place of fixedness at all — if so there is no d'oraisa safek — we do not decree lest he take from kevua; and so there in Shabbat in the explanation of some of them the matter is safek as Rosh wrote — only the Torah permitted and in saving life we go to leniency — unlike when uprooted, since certainly the majority separated and certainly he is a gentile and there is no safek at all and we do not desecrate Shabbat; and learn Maharash's law of mixtures from five of Shabbat — and requires study; investigate. Only there is some wonder on Rama who copied R' Shachna's words that Taz answered in Tur so there would not be contradiction between what he wrote in siman 110 and siman 116 — and in Tur well one can say R' Shachna's law is forced, for from that ring it is proven we decree lest he take from kevua; and in Sanhedrin R' Elazar said: an ox that gored became mixed in oxen whose judgment was finished, etc. — and regarding valid oxen it does not teach because there is a remedy of "let us suppress them," etc. — see there Tosafot and Maharsha and Rashal — learn there is a remedy in suppression and we do not decree lest he take; therefore Tur holds to distinguish when all were uprooted we do not decree lest he take, etc.; and there regarding an ox mixed in valid oxen there is a remedy of uprooting all — and requires study; and this is R' Shimshon's answer; and if the difficulty: even in Zevachim "let us suppress them" and they will uproot all and lest he take from kevua does not apply — one must distinguish between kodshim and hullin; and if the difficulty: if so whence do we derive this law at all to distinguish between all uprooted and some — behold regarding stoned ox in Sanhedrin they dealt in hullin and in them we do not decree lest he take from kevua — one cannot say thus, for the ring of hullin is forbidden in the explanation — until here lest he take from kevua; and the difficulty of stoned ox — and one must distinguish between all uprooted and some; and that of Zevachim one must distinguish between kodshim and hullin — and Tur's words are resolved. But Rama who did not emend anything on the Mechaber's law to distinguish between separated of itself and intentional — if so the above difficulty stands in its place: if he holds to distinguish between hullin and kodshim — whence do we derive at all that in hullin some uprooted does not help — and that it does not help in kodshim — one must distinguish; and from the ring of avodah zarah it is not difficult for him — for otherwise it is difficult: behold it is separated of itself that is permitted — and one must say like Rashba in Terumat HaDeshen that there it is separated before us; and if he does not hold to distinguish — Taz's difficulty stands in its place: let all uprooted be in kodshim — for one cannot distinguish — requires study; certainly it is impossible as I wrote — for that which the Gemara challenged "let us suppress them" and we do not say there is rejection regarding kodshim because it is in his power to suppress and move them and there is no rejection at all — if so this is in his power to move them; but to make all uprooted as R' Shachna wrote — it is not clearly in his power, sometimes it will happen and sometimes not — if so one cannot make in this a manner that important items are rejected; investigate — and Taz's difficulty is not difficult. And it would appear to say there is proof for R' Shimshon's law from the Gemara that challenged "let us suppress them," etc. — and it answered lest ten priests come — Rashi explained and they return to their fixedness; and it is difficult: who forces him to take the entire mixture of forbidden ox that became mixed in a thousand and take from it only nine hundred and suppress since they move and whatever separates the majority separates and there is no forbidden ox in it — still there is no concern they will take ten and return to fixedness — for even if it returns safek whether issur is in it — behold not all mixtures are together; and one must say: certainly this law was known to them that lest he take from kevua is forbidden — only this applies when some were uprooted; but when all were uprooted it is permitted; if so that which it challenged "let us suppress them," etc. — it knew lest he take from kevua — only the difficulty is "let us suppress them" that all should be uprooted; and on this the Gemara answers to uproot all lest ten come, etc. — for they return to fixedness — for it dealt with all uprooted; and the Gemara challenged: is that so, etc.; and the Gemara answered: in truth even when all uprooted in kodshim we are concerned lest he take from kevua — for in kodshim they were stringent — and requires study; and R' Shachna's law is forced in the Gemara; investigate — and Taz's difficulty is resolved.
"The last two forbidden." — It appears the reason is because it is nullifying issur — if the entire mixture will be permitted through his action, behold he nullifies issur; but when he leaves two forbidden there is no nullifying issur here, for at least two are forbidden. And with this one can understand Tosafot's words in Zevachim above that challenged R' Tam who distinguishes between kodshim and hullin — if so if it became mixed in sin offering of dead or stoned ox why should they die — nevertheless all should graze until they become thin and become separated and suppress them, etc. — then it is called hullin and suppression helps and they become fit, for whatever separates the majority separates, etc.; and it is difficult to challenge itself in the Gemara that it makes a derivation: if it taught in Zevachim and not in Avodah Zarah one would say it applies only to kodshim but not commoner, etc.; and it is difficult: how is it possible to err why should they die — they should graze until they become thin and redeem and become commoner and nullified and permitted — for in majority they are nullified. And one can say: at first glance one can answer Tosafot's difficulty: when can we suppress them — if before redemption behold it is still kodshim for they are not yet redeemed and we do not permit suppression for R' Tam; and if after redemption that already went out to hullin — it is difficult: behold even though one can suppress them, nevertheless two must be forbidden because of nullifying issur as I wrote; if so he redeems before suppression and does not know who will be last — until here one must redeem all and in any case there are among them two that remain in issurim — if so how can he redeem — behold one does not redeem kodshim to feed them to dogs; and with this he redeems two that will certainly die and this is forbidden and forbidden to redeem them and there is no place for Tosafot's difficulty; but this is if the reason they distinguish between hullin and kodshim is because of kodshim sanctity — if so even though blemished as long as not redeemed kodshim sanctity is upon them; but in R' Tam it is explained in Tosafot that which he distinguishes between kodshim is because in kodshim we are concerned lest ten priests come — see there; if so when already blemished and only lacking redemption this reason does not apply; if so well he can suppress — lest they come, etc. does not apply — for it is blemished; if so well they challenged that he can suppress and redeem and the last two he will not redeem and there is no "one does not redeem," etc. — and well they challenged. But in the derivation in the Gemara it distinguishes explicitly between kodhesh and commoner because of stringency of kodshim — if so one can say that which we say commoner no — the explanation is not that it should be nullified explicitly, only the intent is "let us suppress them since they move." And so one must say: how did it enter your mind in commoner it is nullified — behold the mishnah is a piece of nevelah, etc. — it is not nullified, for it is piece worthy of honor; if so how did it enter your mind it will be nullified through suppression — if so there is no difficulty why should they die — it should graze and afterward it will be nullified — for if before redemption it will not be nullified at all, and if after redemption behold one does not redeem, etc. — and requires study; investigate.
"The last ones, etc." — This is all to resolve that there would not be contradiction in Tur; but Shulchan Aruch who did not make this distinction — if so it is difficult: for here he forbids in the explanation intentionally and in siman 16 regarding oto v'et beno that became mixed he ruled "let us suppress them since they move"; and with this Maharshal and Pri Chadash resolved: since oto v'et beno is davar sheyesh lo matirin — lest he take from kevua does not apply, for tomorrow everything is permitted and from the Torah one does so for one day — what tomorrow is entirely permitted; and one should examine what is written in Orach Chayyim siman 497 regarding eggs that a gentile brings to sell on Yom Tov — even though majority of eggs are from Yom Tov nevertheless forbidden, for we do not follow davar sheyesh lo matirin after majority — so Ran; and it is difficult: nevertheless gentiles who bring to sell in the market from their house and in their house majority of eggs laid on Yom Tov — let us say whatever separates the majority separates; and if it is kevua behold it is not recognizable; and one must say we decree lest he take from kevua; and it is difficult: behold for davar sheyesh lo matirin we are not concerned he will take from kevua — if so this contradicts his reasoning; and one can force to say: regarding an egg in any case there is presumption now that it laid — therefore we are not lenient to rely on separation, etc. since presumption contradicts. And there is proof for Maharshal's reasoning from this that we say in Beitzah: one set aside two and found three — forbidden; and the Gemara says "if you wish": if they are others they are forbidden, and if not behold there is one, etc.; and one can say: why did the Gemara count at all this concern "if they are others" — for this depends on the dispute of Rabbi and rabbanan: he left nine and found ten — R' Eliezer says: stacked coin, scattered coin, all of them; and rabbanan say: hullin — and behold there is clear issur that one not ready became mixed in it (and see Ran and Yesh Seder who wrote in the mishnah only this reason of mixed in); and one must say: if there is only this one mixed in it one issur — it is difficult how it teaches forbidden to "let us suppress them since they move" and whatever separates the majority separates; therefore the Gemara says another reason: they are all others. Only it is difficult: how is it possible to suppress them, etc. — behold there is a decree lest he take from kevua; and until here one must say regarding davar sheyesh lo matirin we do not decree thus — and the matter is forced.
"The egg permitted, etc." — Shach cited in the name of Taz that an egg is separated and we do not decree lest he take from kevua — for from an egg regarding a chicken we do not decree, end of his words; and it is unnecessary, for Taz wrote this according to the poskim's approach that even separated of itself we decree lest he take from kevua; but according to the Mechaber's and Rama's ruling who did not emend regarding separated of itself — we are not concerned he will take from kevua in any case; regarding an egg there is no concern, for behold it is separated of itself.
