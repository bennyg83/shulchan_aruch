And other important items. Such as a piece worthy of honor, and likewise davar sheb'minyan and a whole creature, and the like:
After the mixture became known it is forbidden. But before the mixture became known it is permitted — even according to the one who forbids separated automatically; so too the Rosh and Tur, and Maharshal agreed in chapter 3, siman 21, and Darkei Moshe and the later authorities — and not as the Rashba and Beit Yosef. And the reason: the Rosh wrote in a responsum that the reason separated automatically is forbidden is lest one take by hand from the kevua; and we do not find such a decree except where they already stand in a presumption of prohibition. But before the doubt arose it is not applicable to decree anything, since they all stand in a presumption of permissibility. What can you say — lest one take after the doubt arose? We do not find such a decree in the Gemara. And furthermore, in this there is no room to err, for if one took while it still stood in a presumption of permissibility, for that reason he will not come to take after they became forbidden — necessarily:
But if separated, etc. For all the minorities that separated to one side — we say of them that whatever separates from the majority is presumed to have separated from the majority, and the forbidden item remains within the majority, end of his words — Rashba in Mishmeret HaBayit, daf 103, 1a; and so he wrote in the short Torat HaBayit, house 4, gate 1, daf 39, 2b. And according to this, if they separated half of them at once they are forbidden; and so it appears explicitly in the short Torat HaBayit, house 4, gate 2, daf 42, 2b — and not as the Perishah, seif 28:
If separated automatically. Not in our presence, or they all scattered — even in our presence it is permitted, since the kevua has already been nullified. So wrote the Rashba in the long Torat HaBayit, daf 104, 2b, and the Beit Yosef brings it; and so too in Mishmeret HaBayit, daf 103, 1a: if they all scattered in our presence it is considered as if they separated not in our presence, see there:
Permitted. The Rosh and many poskim ruled that separated automatically is forbidden — according to the law of nine slaughterhouses, for there the forbidden item is recognizable by itself and we are not concerned lest one take from the kevua, for we do not deal with a majority; unlike here, where everything is mixed together. And so too Mahariv in Hagahot Shaarei Dura, siman 44 — so is the practice, and Ran brings it in his explanation; and so too Maharshal, Piskei HaGahot, siman 23; and so Issur VeHeter, general rule 26, law 11. And the Rav in Torat Chatat itself, beginning of general rule 43, wrote that it is good to be stringent, and in the simanim there he wrote unconditionally that it is forbidden; and so in Eifah Rabbati, beginning, daf 51, that it appears to be stringent. And the Bach, seif 11, challenged the Mechaber, and his view is to distinguish between living creatures and other important items: for with living creatures, since it is possible by penning them that they move, it is permitted when separated automatically; but with other important items it is forbidden — and he wished to derive this from the Rashba's words and expanded at length. I did not reach the bottom of his view in all his words; but there is no distinction, as appears from the words of all the poskim:
And that which is forbidden, etc. The Bach wondered at this ruling and wrote that it is not true here in Shulchan Aruch — and I did not reach the bottom of his view; for certainly here the Rav's words are true, for the Rashba wrote explicitly that if they all separated in our presence it is considered as not in our presence. And even though the Rashba spoke only that if they scattered after the fact — but it is forbidden to scatter them at first, as it is forbidden to take from there — nevertheless, the Rav also did not mention here that it is permitted to scatter them at first, and spoke only that if he separated them after the fact, even if he separated them intentionally not knowing there is prohibition in the matter — even though they separated in our presence, since he separated intentionally it is permitted since they all scattered, it is considered as separated not in our presence. However, in Torat Chatat, general rule 43, law 2, he wrote to resolve the Tur's words, for he holds separated not in our presence is forbidden; and above, siman 16, regarding oto va'beno, and at the end of s.k. 57, regarding derusah that were mixed, he ruled that we pen them that they move, and whatever separates from the majority is presumed to have separated from the majority, and the last two are forbidden — for in siman 16 and 57 it deals with when the entire mixture separated from its place until no fixing remained in its place. And this distinction is not necessary, for whence do we know that separated all is preferable to separated automatically not in our presence? And the proof he brought from Torat Chatat requires study, for in my humble opinion it is not a proof at all — see there. And furthermore the Bach challenged: if so, why did the Tur write the last two are forbidden — were they not all uprooted and all scattered, etc., end of his words. And according to my humble opinion, the Rav's view is that even though they returned and came together in one place — all that scattered first and were uprooted from the first fixing — again it is not considered kevua, for the law of kevua is a novelty, as I wrote in small seif 14 in the name of the Ran, and you have in it only its novelty — meaning, that it remained in its first place; and so it appears explicitly from his language in Darkei Moshe. And this is his language: the truth is what the gaon, my teacher and relative, Maharash Shachna, may his light shine, resolved for me — that what the Tur wrote here is while the important items remained in their place, only that one or two separated; and therefore we do not say whatever separates from the majority is presumed to have separated from the majority, since they still lie in their place it is still kevua. But if they separated and all moved from their place so that no kevua remained there — if they all separated, it is permitted — necessarily:
The egg is permitted. For we say: whatever separates from the majority is presumed to have separated from the majority, and we do not decree regarding an egg lest the roosters take from the kevua — Torat Chatat there in the name of Issur VeHeter. And according to this, even according to the one who holds an egg is considered davar sheb'minyan, it is permitted; for with nine slaughterhouses it is permitted when separated automatically, even a piece worthy of honor — and this is plain.
