And that they were slaughtered, etc. Specifically, they must have been slaughtered after they were mixed; we require that they were slaughtered b'shogeg. But if they were slaughtered before they were mixed, even intentionally they are nullified; and investigate:
B'shogeg. Even after the mixture became known, as explained in siman 101, seif 6, see there; and see siman 99, s.k. 15:
And specifically small animals, etc. The Bach wrote: it is astonishing, for in siman 101, seif 3, he ruled that even a chicken in its feathers is nullified, and all the more so a lamb in its skin and wool — and it appears his words contradict one another, end of his words. And it requires study, for he wrote in Torat Chatat, general rule 42, end of law 3, that a lamb in its skin and wool is nullified specifically because of a piece worthy of honor (meaning, because it requires great preparation and lacks great processing before it becomes worthy of honor); but nevertheless it is not nullified for the reason of davar sheb'minyan (since it is important, for regarding davar sheb'minyan we do not care about what lacks processing), as below, general rule 42 (and there is a practical difference if it is in a place where they do not sell it by count — it is nullified), end of his words. And so too at the end of general rule 42: specifically small animals — after they were slaughtered their importance is nullified and they no longer become a piece worthy of honor or davar sheb'minyan, etc. For this he wrote here: and specifically small animals that are not fit to be honored — meaning, they are not important — and if so it is not davar sheb'minyan; and above, siman 101, he wrote that it is nullified only for the reason of a piece worthy of honor, and this is clear.
