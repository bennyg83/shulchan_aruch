"That is not worthy of honor." — Bach challenged: behold a lamb in its skin and a chicken in its feathers is not called piece worthy of honor; and Shach and Pri Chadash answered that it is borrowed language so there should not be davar sheb'minyan; and the matter is strained in Rama's language. To me it appears: even though we do not follow recognition here, one should be stringent and follow recognition — for those treifot that became mixed — it deals thus, as written in chapter HaTaarovot regarding treifah: you find a mixture only in a gored or fallen animal — for after slaughter there is inspection to check whether kosher or treifah; if so the treifot could have been clarified; and anything that can be clarified — nullification does not apply; and when does nullification take effect? When they already removed the innards and it is not known from which are the innards in which were the treifot — then nullification takes effect and then it is piece worthy of honor, for it was already flayed — and requires study. Also one can say: if they are large, then the heart and liver are not nullified, for they are piece worthy of honor; and even though the body of the chicken is not piece worthy of honor — its feathers disqualify it — nevertheless there is no practical difference for internal organs: one can leave the chicken in its feathers and remove the internal organs from it, as is done every day; if so they are not nullified and lamb and chicken do not mix; therefore he requires small important items, for internal organs too because of their smallness are not piece worthy of honor and are discarded.
