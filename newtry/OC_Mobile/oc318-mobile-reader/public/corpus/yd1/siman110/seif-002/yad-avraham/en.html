(There seif 3) But meat found in the market, etc. — and even if found among the stores it is considered separated. So too Tosafos in the first chapter of Kesubos — specifically where one store is recognizable; but where it is known there is a treifah in one store and it is not known which, if it separated it is forbidden according to Rosh who forbids separated automatically, lest one take from the fixed item since the issur is not known. It appears that if there is a Torah-level safek in one store and it is not known which, and it separated automatically, all permit — as proven in the Gemara in Nazir regarding one who tells his agent, "Go and betroth a wife for me," and he died — he is forbidden to all women; and it challenged: we find in the market and he betrothed — let us say everything that separates from the majority separates. Know that Magen Yeshuah permitted in great loss if it separated automatically regarding important items. And regarding whether we follow leniency in a double safek — Ramban wrote, cited in Chiddushei HaRan on Megillah chapter 1, that it is like two doubts; but Taz wrote we follow stringency in its safek, and R. Matityahu Ber forbids in his Beit She'arim. There is a question from Megillah: "You were a child" — one does not bring proof from a child; and if double safek is like two doubts, behold in rabbinic matters an adult is believed to testify what he saw as a child. But from Kesef Mishneh chapter 6 of Laws of Forbidden Foods regarding tumah there Rambam is lenient and there it is double safek. See Tosafos Sanhedrin (12). And at first glance there is a question according to Rambam who holds we follow leniency from the Torah from Pesachim (3b) and Chullin (91) regarding "they were identified and afterward became mixed" — for if they were forbidden from the Torah, burning would not be required; nevertheless we follow stringency on every side; and so too it is proven that one in one was not established as issur — for otherwise it would be forbidden from the Torah, and why require burning? One can say: just as it was established for eating, it was also established for burning. And what he wrote in Atzei Levona as proof for Rambam from Tosafos' difficulty in Shabbos (130) — why was a verse needed to forbid safek circumcision on Shabbos? One can say Shabbos is different, for there is a Torah prohibition — requires study. Further there s.k. 14 he left in doubt Rashi's explanation in Zevachim that he challenges R. Eliezer and R. Nissim from the mishnah of a stoned ox that became mixed — they all die; why not say the issur died and the rest are offered? What is the difficulty? After all, one must offer two at once — and how can one person slaughter two sacrifices at once? We say "slaughter it" — that one person should not slaughter two sacrifices. One can say: since it is impossible otherwise that they not die and consecrated animals not be lost, it is considered bedi'eved.
