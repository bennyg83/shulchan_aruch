And behold in siman 57 regarding safek derusah that became mixed, that we do not permit by double safek in the name of R. Yochanan — Beit Yosef wrote: I found written — does R. Yochanan disagree with the Talmud, that we say double safek in d'oraisa? And he challenged — behold even a certain derusah that became mixed is permitted from the Torah by majority, only d'rabbanan it is forbidden on account of davar sheb'minyan; if so, all absence in d'rabbanan safek and we should be lenient. And he answered on this: R. Yochanan deals where after the first safek arose the safek became known and it is full issur, and the name of certain issur applies to it — thus according to what I wrote there is no challenge at all, for here is different from other double safek as I mentioned; and also the second challenge is not difficult at all, for there is no Torah permission in the substance of the issur itself, only through nullification in the majority, and the Sages said wherever there is an important item nullification in the majority does not help. I found again so in Darkei Moshe — on account of this he wrote to be lenient, etc. — see there.
It is issur d'oraisa, etc. — see what I wrote in siman 66 seif 4 in the hagahah, that from this it appears to me Rama decided there that egg blood is d'rabbanan when mixtures were made. And I saw in a newly printed book of Maharar Binyamin, who wrote in responsum siman 37, to bring proof that according to R. Yochanan there is no issur in double safek that is not in one substance — rather one safek in its substance and the second through mixtures — except specifically when known at intervals — from what Rashba wrote that Beit Yosef brought in this siman, and these are his words: how can you say perhaps this is not it, and if you say this is it, perhaps that safek was permitted — behold you already forbade it from safek that fell in its substance, and after you forbid it how can you return and permit it, end of his words; and he means: since it was already known and forbidden before it became mixed — behold if it was not known and forbidden before it became mixed, we permit by double safek; and it is not clear to my humble opinion, for this was caused by the citation that Beit Yosef copied from Terumat HaDeshen the long version — and in truth Beit Yosef abbreviated in his citation, for there daf 115 he wrote: and in my humble opinion it is difficult against R. Yochanan that he only permitted double safek of two mixtures from the first chapter of Kesubos — safek under her she committed adultery, safek not under her, and if you say under her — perhaps by oness, and it is double safek; and behold the safeks are not on account of mixtures, and nevertheless we say double safek to be lenient; and one may say there it is different, for it cannot come before you except through two safeks, and you cannot be stringent and say how can you permit when you already forbade, as you say regarding safek egg laid on Yom Tov that immediately it stood in prohibition for us and afterward became mixed — see there and investigate.
But if there were two safeks, etc., and both safeks became known, etc. — the Rav copied this from Hagahot Issur VeHeter at the end of the book, who wrote, and these are his words: and the same applies if the two safeks were complete double safek — that is, in one matter and in one substance — nevertheless it is not permitted thereby unless they come at one time; but if the first safek became known first, count that time as certain issur, and when the second safek comes, even without mixtures, there is only one safek, end of his words; and I did not understand these words at all — for since he said "unless they come at one time," this implies that sometimes it is possible they do not come at one time — and how can you find that it becomes known between the first safek and the second safek in double safek when they are in one substance and one matter, such as in the safek "under him she committed adultery" and safek oness mentioned above — for one cannot say the first safek became known before the second became known; and if you wish to say that at first they noticed the first safek and did not notice the second safek, and when they notice it the matter is revealed retroactively that it was never forbidden — behold no new matter was born for us, only knowledge was lacking — and in Rama's words there is somewhat room to force and explain that he says thus: whenever both safeks became known specifically together, this is a sign of double safek that is proper — for in double safek in two substances it is possible to become known at intervals. And so I saw in a responsum of Maharar Binyamin z"l, who wrote that one should not heed this hagahah, for it is plain that in double safek through two mixtures it is permitted even when known at intervals, and he brought proper proof for this — automatically the same applies in double safek if there is issur here. The general rule emerging in double safek is thus: whenever the first safek is forbidden because it is important and is not nullified in the majority — even though the essence of the issur is rabbinic, such as safek egg born on Yom Tov that afterward became mixed — double safek does not help in it, since the safeks are not in one matter; rather we require that both safeks be through mixtures; and "known at intervals" neither adds nor subtracts anything from double safek. And likewise if there are two safeks whether there is issur here entirely — then it helps in all cases; only there is a distinction in double safek through mixtures — that if it is certain issur and it is important, we require three mixtures, as I wrote in seif 8; but if safek issur became mixed, we require only two mixtures and there is double safek to be lenient:
There is room to permit by double safek, etc. — I saw in this a dispute of Baalei Karnayim — that is Issur VeHeter, and on him the author of Darkei Moshe and Turei Chayim klal 43 seif 7, and on him in responsum Masat Binyamin siman 38 — and in all their words I wonder, as I will explain: Issur VeHeter klal 26 din 2 wrote: one who slaughters with a checked knife and after shechitah the knife is found damaged and afterward became mixed among other animals — one may not permit by double safek, perhaps it is not this one, and if you find to say that it is this one, perhaps it was slaughtered properly — for we conclude in the Gemara: an animal while alive stands in presumption of issur — therefore there is only one safek, perhaps it is not this one — for if you say it is this one, perhaps it was slaughtered properly — for whatever is safek we forbid it because of presumption it was certainly issur and not safek — and therefore one may not permit by double safek except where there is no presumption of issur — when you say "if you say" you cannot establish it on presumption of issur, like this open door that I found at the beginning of Kesubos — "if you say" "under him" there is no establishing presumption of issur beforehand, etc., end of his words. And he wrote on this in Darkei Moshe and Turei Chayim that this is not correct — for Rashba wrote in responsum siman 401 that double safek helps even where there is presumption of issur; and so it appears from the laws of safek derusah that became mixed — for if not for the reason that the first safek was in its substance, there would be double safek — and we would permit even though it had presumption of issur of limb from a living creature, end of his words. And so too here in Shulchan Aruch — and Maharar Binyamin mentioned disagrees with him on Rama, and wrote that there is no proof from safek derusah — for there presumption of issur of limb from a living creature helps only regarding safek that occurred because of shechitah, but not regarding issur that does not depend on shechitah — and he elaborated to explain this matter. And according to him safek derusah where the safek was born from life will be permitted — for it is like the case where a wolf took, etc. — for there is no presumption of issur, and we say in it "it was slaughtered, it is permitted" — and Tosafot's words in chapter 4 of Achin (Yevamos daf 30) escaped him, who wrote, and these are his words: and in the first chapter of Chullin we say: an animal while alive stands in presumption of issur, etc. was slaughtered, it is permitted etc. it appears specifically for a defect born after shechitah it was in presumption of heter, such as a wolf came and took its innards, etc. — but if the safek was born from life, such as safek derusah — not because at the hour the safek was born it was still in presumption of issur end of his words — behold before you that presumption of issur of limb from a living creature helps for safek derusah — and if so Rama properly brings proof from safek derusah regarding double safek, that we see in it that it helps if it is possible to say so; but we do not care in this what was at first in presumption of issur of the living limb — even though it helped to forbid in one safek — rather I see that regarding the halachah all were correct, as I will explain — it is stated in responsum of Rashba siman 401, and these are his words: I see wonder in the matters you asked regarding double safek where issur was presumed, like Kesubos — how is issur presumed? For there is only safek of one corpse that was lost — and if you call it "issur was presumed," every mixed item that we presumed in issur because of its mixture — behold your question is explained from a ring of gentiles, etc., end of his words — and he brings on this many proofs; and afterward wrote: and do not answer me from Rav Huna in the first chapter of Chullin — he checked the knife and slaughtered, and it was found spoiled — behold this is forbidden, lest it was spoiled in the skin; and an incident occurred with thirteen animals and they forbade even the first — learn from this that double safek is forbidden, for he follows his own view that an animal while alive stands in presumption of issur, etc.; and know further that double safek is preferable to majority, and it is possible its permission is stronger than majority — for in lineage one is disqualified by one majority and qualified by double safek; and just as we follow majority to permit in all Torah prohibitions, even where presumption of issur was established, such as a piece of issur that became mixed in two of heter — the same applies to double safek that it is permitted, even where presumption of issur was established, end of his words. And it appears difficult what he wrote in that case of Rav Huna that he mentioned first — that we follow presumption of issur and double safek does not help — and what is different from that which he wrote afterward that we follow double safek even where presumption of issur was established; and if you investigate you will find that Rashba distinguishes in that presumption of issur of Rav Huna — that an animal while alive stands in presumption of issur until you know it was slaughtered properly; and end — since the doubt afterward is in shechitah itself whether it was done properly, and to remove it from presumption that it was not slaughtered, double safek does not help — since in that issur itself from which it had not certainly departed; but if the presumption of issur is because of double safek — that is, it had presumption of limb from a living animal and was slaughtered properly, only there is doubt because of double safek, such as it became mixed first and afterward became mixed in other mixtures — certainly double safek helps; and so all other issur, such as a piece of chelev that became mixed in two mixtures — for Rashba explicitly compared double safek to majority in this way; and it follows from our words that Rama did not challenge Issur VeHeter at all — for Issur VeHeter forbade only in safek shechitah, and that which Rashba mentioned, that case of Rav Huna — there certainly double safek does not help for the reason I mentioned; and also Maharar Binyamin — it appears to me — disagreed with Rama as we mentioned, and said double safek does not help where presumption of issur was established; he ruled well on his question that deals also with safek shechitah — only it appears he wants to rule thus in every double safek where presumption of issur was established, even in a safek not regarding shechitah — for he wrote against Rama that he has no proof from Rashba's words siman 401 — for there in siman 401 he does not speak of presumption of issur, as Rashba wrote explicitly in responsum that where there was full presumption, double safek does not help; rather it deals with presumption of safek issur, such as safek challal in Kesubos; and according to the questioner's view they called presumption of issur as explained there, and on this he wrote to permit; but with full presumption of issur, like this case of Rav Huna, double safek does not help — until here is his language; and may he rest in honor — for he did not investigate Rashba's words well, because he did not see the end of the matter — for Rashba wrote explicitly at the end "and know further," etc., as I mentioned; it is clear that even with full presumption of issur it helps; and from the outset he wrote plainly that that presumption of issur that the questioner called presumption of issur — that is, from the side of mixtures — obviously it helps; and he concluded that even with full presumption of issur, such as a piece of chelev, double safek helps in it like majority, and we do not say it does not help except in that case of Rav Huna where the doubt is in shechitah, as I mentioned; and the Rav Maharar Binyamin understood in Rashba that every presumption of issur is like that of Rav Huna — and it is not so. What emerges from our words for the halachah: even in every issur certainly double safek helps to permit if it does not contradict the presumption itself — like safek in shechitah, that if double safek helps in it, it would contradict the presumption that it was not slaughtered but limb from a living animal — in this we say it does not help; and similar to this is a woman who is in presumption of married woman and afterward safeks arose regarding her husband's death or divorce — even if there were several double safeks they would not help, since you come to remove her from the category of married woman that she was first, and you come to contradict it directly; but in other presumption of issur where safeks for permission arose from another side, such as from mixtures — certainly double safeks help to remove from issur, for from the outset there is nothing here that contradicts one another; and therefore in safek derusah, if there was full double safek, it well helps to nullify presumption of issur of limb from a living animal that the innards call thus — it appears to me clear and correct in this:
