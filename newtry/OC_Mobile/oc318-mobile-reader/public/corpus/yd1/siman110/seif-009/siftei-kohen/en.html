Since the first safek was in the substance itself, etc. — meaning there is no law of two mixtures that the second mixture is permitted through double safek — and as I wrote in s.k. 49, that there the first safek was not in the substance itself but in mixtures; unlike here, where the first safek was in the substance itself; and so Tur, Rashba, and other poskim. But it is difficult: Tur, Rashba, and the poskim did not need this distinction except according to their view that they hold the second mixture is permitted; but the Mechaber, who holds in s.k. 8 that the second mixture is forbidden — if so, even if the first safek was not in the substance itself, double safek that became mixed would be only two mixtures; if so, why did the Mechaber need the reason that since the first safek was in the substance itself, etc.; and also why need he write this entire law of safek treifah that became mixed with others — whence shall we say it is better than two mixtures, for all poskim hold that at least it is not better than two mixtures; and likewise it is difficult on the Rav: why need he write at all the reason that the first safek is forbidden d'oraisa; and one cannot say the Mechaber and Rav came to teach us that even safek treifah that became mixed in two mixtures — the second mixture is forbidden — there is no law of three mixtures that the third mixture is permitted, for here the first safek is in the substance itself and d'oraisa the second mixture is certainly forbidden — here it would be permitted, for it is proper double safek where the first safek is not in the substance itself and is not forbidden from the Torah, as will be explained below, s.k. 63, end of law 4; and so the Rav in s.k. 8 that with safek issur the second mixture is permitted; and requires study:
And some say the reason, etc. — and the practical difference is: according to the Mechaber's reason, even if the first safek was d'rabbanan, since it was in the substance itself it is not nullified in mixtures; and according to the Rav it is nullified; and it follows from this: if cheese was switched and it is not known whether it is Jewish cheese or gentile cheese — in such a case it is safek d'rabbanan and for stringency — and as below, s.k. 63, law 18: for the Mechaber it is not nullified in mixtures even though the first safek is d'rabbanan, since it is in the substance itself; and for the Rav in the hagahah it is nullified, as below, s.k. 63, in law 17, since the first safek is not from the Torah — if so, it is double safek. And one can say that even the Mechaber speaks only where the first safek that was in the substance itself is forbidden from the Torah; and the Rav in the hagahah is stringent even if the first safek was not in the substance itself, since it is from the Torah it would not be double safek; or it is possible one said one thing and one said one thing and they do not disagree; and the Rav came only to add that through double safek, if there is any issur here at all, it is permitted even in the substance of the issur — and see in responsum Noda BiYehudah, vol. 3, siman 100:
Laws of double safek. (a) It is not called double safek. Even if the first safek was not known until after the mixture — such as when several eggs became mixed together and afterward it became known there was a safek of tereifah in the hen, and it became known that one egg is from her that she laid after the safek of tereifah arose — and so in all similar cases; even though no safek of issur was known until after the mixture, and thus both safeks became known together as one — nevertheless, since the first safek is forbidden from the Torah, it is not double safek. So Darkei Moshe raised in siman 57 and Turei Chayim klal 43 din 7; and Beit Yosef challenged in siman 57 in the name of Masat Binyamin that one permits even when the first safek was not known before the mixture, on account of double safek — and that is what he concludes here in the hagahah: "but if there were two safeks whether there was issur here at all and both safeks became known together," etc. — thus at the beginning, even if both safeks became known together, it is not double safek; and so is implied from the poskim, unlike responsum Masat Binyamin siman 37 that challenged the Rav on this; and the foundation of his challenge is that from where the Rav brought this law from Issur VeHeter klal 26 it does not appear so — rather it is explained in Hagahot Issur VeHeter thus; and we should rely more on the Rav, author of Issur VeHeter, for he was a great established rabbi, than on the words of the hagahah — end of his words; and this is not so, for in Issur VeHeter itself it is implied many times that even if the issur was not known it is forbidden, for he wrote in his conclusion that the two safeks are not one matter, for the first safek was in the substance itself and is forbidden from the Torah — see there and investigate.
And even if it had presumption of issur, etc. Thus the Rav in Darkei Moshe siman 57 and Turei Chayim klal 43 end of din 7: Issur VeHeter wrote that anything that has presumption of issur — one does not permit any double safek, and his words are not correct — for Rashba wrote in responsum siman 401 that double safek helps even for something that had presumption of issur — end of his words; and at first glance it is implied that he challenges Issur VeHeter in what he wrote there (klal 26 din 2) regarding a hen that had a safek at shechitah and became mixed — that it is not nullified by double safek since the first safek is from the Torah and also because it had presumption of issur, for a live animal stands in presumption of issur; and on this Masat Binyamin siman 38 challenged the Rav and wrote that on the contrary, from Rashba's responsum there it is implied that presumption of issur in such a case does not prevent double safek; and in truth his words are not necessary here, for even though from Rashba's responsum there regarding what he wrote "do not answer me from Rav Huna who said a live animal stands in presumption of issur" — it is implied thus at first glance — nevertheless without that there is an error in the wording and one may emend to understand as the Rav wrote — see there; and also afterward Rashba wrote in conclusion: "and know further that double safek is preferable to majority," etc. — see there and investigate.

However Rashba in his chiddushim Pesachim daf 53a wrote that even where there is double safek, inspection is required where one can clarify by inspection; and so in my humble opinion to prove from the Gemara — for Maharai himself in his rulings there brought this law from what Rabbenu Tam holds, that safek derusah that became mixed among others is double safek even though one can clarify by inspection; and R' Yochanan too would permit by double safek if not that the first safek is d'oraisa — end of his words.
