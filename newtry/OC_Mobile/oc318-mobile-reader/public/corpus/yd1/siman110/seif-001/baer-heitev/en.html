"That it." The Shach wrote that even in several thousand it is not nullified; and know that it says in the Gemara that important items forbidden in benefit, if they became mixed even in a thousand, all are forbidden in benefit. However, the Tosafot wrote that it is permitted to sell them to a non-Jew except for the monetary value of the issur in them, if it is a matter where there is no concern that the non-Jew will afterward sell them to a Jew. However, what the Ra'avad wrote — that the enactment of diverting benefit to the Dead Sea also suffices for all issurim — is not the view of the poskim; rather, specifically for avodah zarah, where one takes its monetary value, diverting benefit to the Dead Sea by redemption suffices, and not for other issurim. Not like the Levush in Orach Chayyim siman 445 who wrote also regarding bread baked on coals of chametz that diverting benefit suffices, etc., for that is not so and we do not rule thus; and see in Orach Chayyim se'if 347 and below siman 142. And it requires study regarding the Rama who did not emend here that an important item, even not in its species, forbids in any amount, as written in siman 101, s.k. 66; end of his words; and see siman 109, seif 1.
"Badan." The Shach wrote that Rashi explained that Badan are places, and I heard regarding nuts whose shells are soft and break off in the hand; end of his words. And sealed barrels — specifically large ones; but small ones are not important, and one in two is nullified. And if they are open, even yein nesekh [barrels] are nullified, for they are not considered important. Shach.
"The house." To exclude those of a baker, which are not considered important — specifically in their time; but in places where even the baker's are considered important, it is obvious that they are not nullified. And live animals, even though they are not forbidden from the beginning of their creation — such as an ox condemned to stoning and one that was gored, and the like — are not nullified on account of their importance. Shach.
"To count them." The Shach wrote that even though their practice is to always count them and they are not sold at all by estimate, they are judged by their measure since they are not important.
"Always." [And he disagrees with the Mechaber in this regarding what is his practice to count it always] the Taz wrote; but a thing that is sometimes also sold by estimate — such as eggs, for sometimes a full basket of eggs is sold [by estimate] — is nullified. And the Maharshal ruled that a thing that is sometimes sold by estimate and sometimes by count is not nullified; and the Issur VeHeter and Bach wrote that this is the practice; and the Maharshal concluded that all the more so nowadays, when there is not found at all in our regions to sell a full basket of eggs by estimate without count, it is obvious that an egg is not nullified; and see siman 86.
"In its safek." Meaning, if there is in it a safek whether it is an important item or not; but other safeks we find that we go to stringency, as will be explained in this siman in several places. Shach. And the Maharshal wrote that karkashta, even though they do not sell it nowadays except by count, is nullified since they eat it only by filling; end of his words.
