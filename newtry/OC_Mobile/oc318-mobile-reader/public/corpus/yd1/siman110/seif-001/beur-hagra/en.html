Important thing, etc. All of this is the language of the Rambam, and it appears to mean specifically in species in its kind, not as he wrote above in siman 101, seif 6, in the hagahah. And even though one can push back, because one does not find a dry mixture of a species not in its kind that is not discernible, nevertheless it is superfluous language — only here does he say specifically. And behold, ostensibly there is proof for the words of the aforementioned hagahah from what is written at the beginning of Yevamot (54), and in chapter 8 of Zevachim — the Tanna of the case of a liter of dried figs, etc.; and behold, for R' Yehudah without blilah, species in its kind is not nullified. And likewise what he says there at the end, "she rolled it in a circle," etc., s.v. ya'alu. And likewise in the chapter Nezikin (54b), "they fell and split," etc. — the words of R' Yehudah; only here it deals with not in its kind. But already Tosafot explained in Zevachim 73, s.v. Rabbi Yehudah — R' Yitzchak asks that species in its kind is not nullified except wet with wet. And with this it is also resolved the chapter Notel (141b) — R' Yitzchak says even we raise, etc. And behold, here it is species in its kind, that not in its kind is nullified in sixty, as written in chapter 2 of Orlah to be lenient and to be stringent — species not in its kind, etc. And likewise in Yerushalmi chapter 6 of Nedarim: "and anything that has no permitted form, such as terumah," etc., and not in its kind nullified in sixty. And that passage in chapter 5 of Yevamot (38b) that says wheat and wheat are not nullified according to R' Yitzchak. And likewise that which is said in Menachot 23 regarding the mishnah "two meal offerings," etc. — it is according to R' Yitzchak in flour that is wet with wet. And so too the Tosafot there (22), s.v. veR' Yitzchak, etc. — see there; and there, and in Yevamot chapter 1, 1, s.v. R' Yehudah, etc. Nevertheless, the words of Tosafot are not necessarily that he holds like the Rambam, but because one does not find a mixture of not in its kind in dry, as noted above. (Collected notes) "Important thing," etc., and likewise beriah, etc. See Rambam chapter 16 of Hilchot Maachalot Assurot, who wrote "important thing," etc., seven things, etc.; and likewise a piece of nevelah, etc. — for behold it is a piece worthy of honor; and the same applies to a piece of basar b'chalav or of hullin that was slaughtered in the Azarah, etc.; and likewise gid hanasheh, etc.; and likewise every beriah — therefore the stoned ox, the heifer whose neck was broken, the bird of the metzora, and the firstborn donkey, etc. But the rest of the things — even though their way is to count them — behold, these are elevated in their measure, and that is the mishnah in chapter 5 of Avodah Zarah (74a): [And what he wrote nevelah and gid hanasheh in chapter gid hanasheh, and not like the sugya of Avodah Zarah that says there to exclude nevelah, as Tosafot wrote] And that which is not counted in the mishnah of Orlah except those seven things — Kesef Mishneh wrote that only what grows from the ground is counted there, similar to bundles of clover over which they disagree there. And the Rambam at the end of Avodah Zarah wrote that only Orlah and kilayei hakerem are counted there, as it says "what is fit for Orlah is Orlah; what is fit for kilayei hakerem is kilayei hakerem," etc. Even though in the commentary it appears the mishnah there is R' Meir and his view is better — that specifically also prohibitions of benefit apply; but some say that even according to the Rabbanan it is derived from what is asked in Avodah Zarah there: "and let it teach nut shells," etc. [And it is written that Rav Ashi in the chapter of mixtures (73a) — that beriot are counted, etc., and according to the Rabbanan] And that which the Rambam did not count the first three — yein nesekh, avodah zarah, and Orlah skins — he holds, as written in the Yerushalmi, because of "it shall not cleave to your hand," etc., and not because of importance; but it requires study nevertheless. And they, etc. — he explains according to the Rabbanan relative to R' Meir; and in the dispute of the Sages and R' Akiva he explains according to R' Akiva, because of the dispute; and afterward it is anonymous, as he wrote there "How," etc.; and as written in the Yerushalmi there "the loaves were spread" — the mishnah of R' Akiva of R' Eleazar ben Azariah, even regarding loaves on a festival; and the halachah is like the anonymous mishnah, as written in the chapter HaChalitzah (42b).
Some say, etc. — according to R' Meir, because R' Yitzchak and R' Levi argue according to his view, and the sugya in the Gemara at the beginning of Yevamot and in chapter 8 of Zevachim, and in chapter gid hanasheh, and in chapter HaAreil is like him — and like R' Yochanan. And the Semak ruled like R' Levi, like the Tanna of a liter of dried figs in Zevachim and the beginning of Yevamot there — that R' Yitzchak and R' Levi only disagree regarding R' Meir: R' Yochanan holds "whatever its way is to count" like R' Meir according to R' Yehoshua in a liter of dried figs that is nullified; and R' Levi holds that terumah b'zman hazeh is different because it is d'rabbanan, as written in chapter HaAreil (Yevamos chapter 1, 1). And R' Yochanan according to his own reasoning holds that terumah b'zman hazeh is d'oraisa — and this is what he says there, "I say it is different — a circle," etc.; and this is the aforementioned baraita of a liter of dried figs that was rolled on the surface of a circle, etc.; and there he says to him "But behold," etc., "Do you think," etc.; and with this Tosafot's question there, s.v. she'ani, is resolved, "and if you would ask," etc. (And Tosafot did not wish to explain thus according to their view, as they wrote there, s.v. amar, etc., "even though," etc., "and now one must say," etc. And our rabbi was careful about this in the collected notes below, as written, for ostensibly it is difficult since, etc.) But regarding R' Yehuda according to R' Yehoshua they do not disagree, and the halachah is like R' Yehuda against R' Meir. And Rav Pappa establishes the mishnah in Zevachim there according to him, and likewise the baraita at the beginning of Yevamot; and the Semag who ruled like R' Yochanan holds that since Rav Ashi establishes it in another manner, it appears he does not hold like him — but it is not necessarily that he wishes to establish it even according to the Rabbanan. And in chapter gid hanasheh (97, 2) it can be said that he gives the reason, etc. — and does not establish it regarding "was mixed, it itself" (the Ramban and Rashba there, and R' David requires study) — it appears it is not nullified; and so in the Shulchan Aruch, siman 106, seif 3: (Collected notes) From what he wrote there — what the Semak wrote to rule "whatever its way is to count," even regarding d'rabbanan, in the sugya in chapter HaAreil — what he wrote "I say it is different — a circle," etc. Meaning: mishnah chapter 4 of Terumot of R' Eliezer and R' Yehoshua who disagree regarding a circle on the surface of the circles of an olah; and that mishnah comes according to R' Meir, as written at the beginning of Yevamot — and therefore one must say because terumah b'zman hazeh is d'rabbanan, he says to him "But behold," etc., "Do you think," etc.; therefore there it goes according to R' Meir and R' Yehuda who disagrees there at the beginning of Yevamot according to R' Yehoshua, and holds it does not go up because he holds "whatever its way is to count" — and so too is their dispute regarding a piece; and as Tosafot wrote (in Yevamos) there, 2, s.v. R' Yehuda, etc.; and this is what he says "But I differentiate," etc.; and there therefore it is not needed because of d'rabbanan, but because of "at v'chol" (bread and all), and the same applies to circles; and he ruled like R' Yehuda because R' Meir and R' Yitzchak — the halachah is like R' Yitzchak; but this is not sufficient — for in a place where the anonymous is like R' Meir, the halachah is like R' Meir, that anonymous in mishnah, etc. (as written in Yevamos 42b), and as the Rif wrote in chapter Kol HaKeilim. And it is possible he explains according to R' Levi because the sugya at the beginning of Yevamot (and in chapter 8 of Zevachim) according to R' Levi who says "this Tanna," etc., that even regarding d'rabbanan, etc.; and he is according to R' Levi who holds the reason of R' Meir is because it is d'rabbanan; but R' Yochanan holds it is d'oraisa, and according to the Rambam's explanation that all fruits is d'oraisa except vegetables — not like Rashi and Tosafot. Nevertheless the difficulty stands in its place — why did he rule like R' Yehuda that even regarding d'rabbanan it is not nullified; and furthermore, at the conclusion in Yevamos there where he says "This is according to the Rabbanan and I who say like R' Yosi" — it applies even to R' Yochanan, which ostensibly is difficult since R' Yitzchak holds like the Rabbanan — what forced him to establish that case of the circle according to R' Yosi? Rather that at the conclusion certainly it is like the Rabbanan. And what he wrote "I who say," etc., he speaks regarding the mishnah in Yevamos that we explicitly taught R' Yosi in it; and what he wrote there "But I differentiate" — in the words of R' Levi who says that from this mishnah there is no proof that their dispute is regarding "at v'chol" like in a piece; but in truth whether regarding d'rabbanan or d'oraisa their dispute depends on "at v'chol"; and this is what he says at the beginning of Yevamot (and in chapter 8 of Zevachim) "this Tanna" that even regarding d'rabbanan — for in truth the anonymous mishnah is like the Rabbanan that it is d'rabbanan, and nevertheless it is not nullified according to R' Yehuda (end of passage): (That which "important thing," etc. — see above siman 101, s.k. 7.)
