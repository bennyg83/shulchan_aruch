(there) "But we find the opposite in the Mishnah in chapter Chavit," etc. One must say that this case is different here, for not everyone is expert in the law of a piece worthy of honor, and it is likely they will err to say that just as pieces that are not worthy of honor are permitted — meaning, the same law applies — meaning; and my father-in-law the Gaon Mahar Gershon, may he rest in Eden, challenged him for another reason — some say to that case of ten people, for there the ten people are in one place — until here we say they remind one another, and it is like that case of lowering the Pesach offering in the first chapter of Shabbos daf 19, as the Gemara explains there — the reason is that members of a fellowship are vigilant — whereas here one must be concerned that they will come to take them one after another — until here are his words, and he aimed well:
(there) He should have challenged from what the Tur himself wrote above in this siman, etc. This does not come close to that, for there it is permitted because importance is nullified and a decree does not apply there, whereas in kavu'a of stores it does — and investigate:
(there) "For behold from one body we have before us prohibition and permission, and this is impossible," etc. There is nothing in this — for a piece worthy of honor that is not nullified is rabbinic, and they said to permit in such a case; and without this too one must say, for behold with one piece separated automatically it is permitted, and with one separated before us it is forbidden, even though it is from one body — but certainly this law is separate and that law is separate, and the same applies here; and we find many such cases:
(there) And it appears that Beit Yosef emended thus, etc. This is certainly not so, for in the old responsa of the Rosh printed in Venice in the year 512 and in Constantinople in the year 482 it is stated explicitly thus, and they were before Beit Yosef; and in the new responsa of the Rosh printed in the year 567, which was long after Beit Yosef's passing, this is missing — and this is omission by the printers beyond doubt:
