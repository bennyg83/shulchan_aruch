Some say that every davar sheb'minyan, etc. — that is like R. Yochanan in the Gemara according to R. Meir, who holds "we taught: that which is customarily counted" — meaning they always sell only by count, unlike an egg, which sometimes they sell a basket full of eggs; that is nullified — unlike R. Levi, who holds "we taught: everything customarily counted," and a forbidden egg is not nullified; that is Tur's view, which he brought, and so Semag in the name of Ritzba; but in Shulchan Aruch siman 442 and in the hagahah there in the name of most geonim they ruled like R. Levi, and so Maharshal in his Or Zarua, and Shulchan Aruch who ruled here at the beginning of the siman that specifically an important item, etc., is not nullified, and davar sheb'minyan is nullified — that is like R. Akiva in the Gemara, who argues with R. Meir. It is surprising on him that in siman 87 seif 3 he ruled like R. Levi, for here an egg is not nullified — and nowadays an egg is something always sold by count, and even R. Yochanan would concede; so too Maharshal wrote. And regarding the halachah, since it is a rabbinic matter, it is fitting to rely on those who permit in a case of loss — as Rama wrote, that it is not called davar sheb'minyan unless they always sell only by count, like R. Yochanan; and Maharshal wrote that karkashta, even though nowadays they sell it only by count, is nullified, since they eat it only by filling — and that is from Mordechai in the second chapter of Beitzah.
