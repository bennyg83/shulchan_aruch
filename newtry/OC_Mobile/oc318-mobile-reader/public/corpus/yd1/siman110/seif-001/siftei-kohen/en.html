Species in its kind, etc. The entire seif is the language of the Rambam beginning of chapter 16 of Magen Avraham, and likewise Orach HaShalom rule 25 law 17 that specifically species in its kind — but the Rab in Torat Chayim rule 40 law 6 challenged the Orach HaShalom on this and ruled that even not in its kind forbids in any amount, and likewise in the hagahah above siman 101 end of seif 6 — and it requires study why he did not emend here at all to the Mechaber's words — and it appears he holds that the Rambam and Mechaber did not mention species in its kind except because one does not find it not in its kind, for the prohibition would be recognizable — and he follows after Tur siman 109 who wrote that a dry item not in its kind is such as when it crumbles into fine pieces — if so an important item that does not crumble into fine pieces one does not find it not in its kind — and in Torat Chayim and the hagahah there who wrote that even not in its kind is not nullified — he did not mention it except because of beriah, that it deals there with one that was mixed in sauce and dish of beriah — even though it is small it is not nullified — this is how it appears to me to be the Rab's view — and nevertheless even not in its kind forbids an important item in any amount and one finds well that it was mixed not in its kind as written in siman 109 s.k. 8, see there:
In any amount. Meaning: even in thousands it is not nullified. And know that it is stated in the Gemara last chapter of Avodah Zarah (74a) that important items forbidden in benefit if mixed even in a thousand all are forbidden in benefit — and so agreed by all poskim — however Tosafot wrote in Yevamot chapter HaAreil (Yevamot 81 top of 2b) and in Zevachim (73b) in the name of the Yerushalmi that it is permitted to sell them to a non-Jew except for the value of prohibition in them (as below siman 134 regarding yein nesekh) if it is a case where there is no concern that the non-Jew will sell it afterward to a Jew — and likewise Rabbeinu Shimshon and the Bertenura at the end of Orlah, and so in the Agudah chapter of mixtures, and in the Nimukei Yosef chapter HaAreil, and likewise the Ran end of chapter Kol HaTzlamim, and so wrote in the last chapter of Avodah Zarah in the name of the Ra'avad and he agreed thus — and likewise the Ra'avad in the hasagot chapter 16 of Magen Avraham law 7, and in Kesef Mishneh there he wrote that the Rambam also holds thus — and not like the author of Megdol Oz who wrote there mixed items without taste — however what the Ra'avad wrote in the hasagot there that likewise the enactment of R' Eliezer — that is, diverting benefit to the Dead Sea — is effective for all prohibitions is according to the Rashbam whom Tosafot and the Ran brought there — but this is not the view of Tosafot and the aforementioned the view of the Rosh end of chapter Kol HaTzlamim, not like the Levush in Orach Chayyim siman 445 seif 2 who wrote also regarding bread baked on chametz coals that it appears redemption by diverting benefit to the Dead Sea suffices and it is permitted in benefit — end of his words — this is not so, for we do not hold thus except regarding idolatry that captures its value — and see in Orach Sealed. But open ones — even of yein nesekh — are nullified, for they are not so important — so Tosafot end of Avodah Zarah 74a s.v. ha tanan leih etc., and in Yevamot there s.v. And loaves of the householder. To exclude those of the baker that are not considered so — this is plain in the Gemara and poskim — and one must say that this is specifically in their time — but in places where even those of the baker are important items it is obvious they are not nullified — and it is a simple matter:
Living creatures. Even though they are not forbidden from the beginning of their creation — such as the stoned ox and the gored animal and the like — they are not nullified by virtue of their importance — so it is plain in the Gemara and poskim:
Even though their way is to count them. Forever — and they are never sold at all by estimate — they are elevated in their measure since they are not important items:
Whose way is always to count it, etc. But an item that is sometimes also sold by estimate — such as eggs that are sometimes sold as a basket full of eggs as is stated in the Gemara beginning of tractate Beitza — is nullified — and likewise in Torat Chayim rule 40 law 1 and rule 63 law 3 — and Maharshal in Orach HaShalom wrote that we practice stringently like the Semak (and R' Yehuda and R' Yitzchak and other poskim) that an item that is sometimes sold by estimate and sometimes by count is not nullified — and likewise in his book Pnei Yehoshua siman 50 and he elaborated on this, see there — and likewise the Bach that so we practice — and so wrote Orach HaShalom rule 25 that so is the practice. And he further wrote there Maharshal: and all the more so nowadays when there is nothing found at all in our borders to sell a basket full of eggs by estimate without count — it is obvious that an egg is not nullified — end of his words — and likewise the Bach — and see above siman 86 s.k. 10:
