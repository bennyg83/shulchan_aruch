"In its kind." — and the Rama and the later authorities hold that even not in its kind they are not nullified; and that which it mentions "in its kind" is because not-in-its-kind is uncommon, so it would not be recognizable and the mixture would become known; but if it happens, even not in its kind they are not nullified; and see what I wrote, siman 101, see there.
"In any amount." — nevertheless, they have a remedy that he sell to a non-Jew except for the monetary value of the issur in them; but to throw benefit-value to the Dead Sea — most poskim forbid, except for avodah zarah where one takes its monetary value. Shach and Pri Chadash — see Peleti; and see what I wrote that it appears one needs that the non-Jew not know the purchase price but only relies on the Jew who tells him such is the price and reduces the purchase price regarding a stoned ox and the like; and if so, there is no presumption of benefit.
"Sealed barrels." — large ones, both in orlah and in yein nesekh; and so too the Rama below — this regarding whole ones but not cut ones, for then there is no importance.
"Of the master of the house." — of terumah and kilaei hakerem; and regarding chametz on Pesach it depends on the view of Rashba — according to his approach, since chametz in any amount, even a slice is not nullified, and all the more so of a baker; and the Rosh disagrees that regarding dry in dry, according to all we require an important item; and on the surface, after midday, Rashba concedes that we require loaves of a master of the house — and that is according to the view that he holds chametz is not called davar she'yesh lo matirin. See Peleti.
"And live animals." — even though they are forbidden from the beginning of their creation and if so are not called beriah, nevertheless they are forbidden because of importance; and if slaughtered, nullified; but that which is forbidden from the beginning of its creation is called beriah, and even in its death as long as it is whole.
"To count them always." — not so an egg, for sometimes they sell a full basket of eggs by estimate; and Rashal wrote that now it is not found to sell thus, and even an egg is not nullified; and karkashta — Mordekhai wrote that even though sold by count, it is nullified.
"And we follow leniently." — see Peleti that I raised by way of example yein nesekh that became mixed in other wines and there are in it sealed barrels and it is not known if it is of libation wine or kosher wine — behold these are stringent, for perhaps it is yein nesekh and forbids and is not nullified, being an important item.
