(SA siman 110 seif 3) Nine stores, etc. — this is forbidden, for every fixed item is treated as half-and-half. Shach wrote that there is another Torah-level fixed case: when one store has a treifah and it is not known which store, and one bought from one of them and knows from which — that is a Torah-level fixed case. This is learned from Darkei Moshe, who explained Tur's words in Rashba accordingly. But then Rashba's words here would contradict his chiddushim in chapter HaTekabel; likewise Tur's words here would contradict his words in EH siman 35, per Maggid Mishneh ch. 9 of Laws of Marriage, who holds that such a case is not called fixed — and as Tosafos in Nazir (12b) s.v. assur, and in the first chapter of Bava Metzia (6a): "Fixed applies only where forbidden and permitted are sold and known, and he did not know from which he bought — such as nine stores." Chochmat Adam cites Rashi in Gittin (54b), that one might say it is not because of fixed status but because the issur is distinct and not fit to mix — as Rambam ch. 9 of Terumos, Turei Zahav there, and Rosh ch. 3 of Shevi'is. So too Magen Yeshuah klal 73 in the name of Kerisos: it is not called fixed unless the issur is recognizable and known in its place. This is explicit in Eliyah Rabbah OC 698 and in responsa Motzal Me'esh siman 50. There is a question from Berachos regarding medalgim on aronot of a tefach — most have a tefach opening and a minority do not; there the aronot are fixed in place, yet we do not say every fixed item is half-and-half — rather we require the issur to be recognizable and known. However Beit Avraham cites Rokeach that there it is because there is safek whether an aron without a tefach opening exists, and safek is not called fixed. See Avodah Zarah (53b), which implies asherah trees were not nullified in the majority even when unrecognized; but idolatry is different, for it is never nullified even when not fixed. Shaarei Teshuvah on Megillah cites Leshon Limudim: we do not say fixed is half-and-half except where one took or separated before us from the fixed set; but where it did not leave the fixed category — such as towns that exist on their own — we follow the majority, as Ran there regarding reading the Megillah. But from Kerisos it appears such a case is considered fixed — e.g., when one field is certainly forbidden for plowing and sowing and safek arose in other fields; he challenged from chapter Kol HaTzlamim that if one place certainly worships and in other places there is safek, they would not all be forbidden.
