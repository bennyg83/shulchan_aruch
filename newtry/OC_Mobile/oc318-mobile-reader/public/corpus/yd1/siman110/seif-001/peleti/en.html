"He is, etc." — See Shach and the acharonim that one may sell everything to a non-Jew except the monetary value of the issur in it; and Rashba wrote that the Tur brought that one need not tell the non-Jew, "I sell to you except for the value of libation wine in it," but one may sell it outright, end of his words; and it appears one must say that the non-Jew does not know the market price at which the Jew reduces the purchase price — for if he knows, then the non-Jew holds a benefit for him and this is full benefit, and behold he benefits from prohibitions of benefit; and what he wrote that one gives to a non-Jew as a gift or reduces the purchase price for him — it is true that because of concern for "do not show them favor, do not give them a free gift" one need not be concerned, for his intent is not for that; even though Rashba gave a reason that it is forbidden to free one's slave because of a free gift, even though he intends that he have a synagogue to pray — nevertheless other authors did not explain like Rashba; therefore it appears clear that one must say the non-Jew does not know the market price at all and relies on the Jew for whatever he says, whether much or little; if so, even though he absorbs the monetary value of the issur, nevertheless the buyer does not perceive this and there is no presumption of benefit here; investigate. And behold in what Shach wrote that many disagree with Raavad and hold that casting money into the Dead Sea does not help except for avodah zarah that seizes its monetary value — and in my view Raavad's view is likewise, for Rambam there in Laws of Forbidden Foods chapter 16 halachah 16 wrote important items and they are not nullified, such as a stoned ox, etc.; and a firstborn donkey that became mixed in a thousand donkeys — all are forbidden; and Raavad wrote regarding firstborn donkey: we maintain like R' Eliezer and Rashbag that one takes the benefit to the Dead Sea. One must investigate why Raavad established his words on firstborn donkey and not on all important items mentioned at the opening of Rambam's words — important items, etc., such as stoned ox, etc.; rather it appears to Raavad that wherever its monetary value is seized in issur, one may take the benefit to the Dead Sea, for the issur is weighty in it; but wherever its monetary value is permitted, his taking it to the sea does not help. However Rambam ruled in Laws of Firstborn that if one sold a firstborn donkey its monetary value is forbidden in benefit — see there in Raavad that he wondered: does a firstborn donkey seize its monetary value? See there in Maggid Mishneh that wrote one must say Raavad's critique is not on Rambam but on the Gemara — the Gemara is arranged: if the law is true that monetary value of firstborn donkey is forbidden and issur of firstborn donkey is upon it, and it is an arranged Gemara — therefore Raavad holds his taking it to the sea helps and he established his critique on firstborn donkey; but in other cases he concedes, since there is no seizure of monetary value here; investigate.
"And sealed barrels." — This is a great matter, even regarding libation wine that forbids in any amount; and so too Rama below in siman 134 seif 2 in the hagahah; and this is unlike Rashba's words in Terumat HaDeshen daf 107, that wrote: this difference between wet and dry applies only to something that is davar sheb'minyan; but in orlah and terumah, just as in wet it forbids more than sixty, so in dry it forbids more — for what is wet and what is dry does not depend on davar sheb'minyan at all; therefore in orlah and the like, wet is not forbidden in any amount — even in dry they are nullified unless it is an important item that is not nullified; but chametz on Pesach and the like, which forbids in wet in any amount — in dry too it forbids in any amount, and one does not need an important item; if so, regarding libation wine one does not need important sealed barrels, for in wet it forbids in any amount; and so too in chametz: in wet in any amount, even in dry we do not say one in two is nullified. Pri Chadash challenged in Orach Chayyim: it appears from Rama's words that stam the first view in Shulchan Aruch siman 447 that chametz in dry in dry we do not say one in two is nullified — whereas here regarding libation wine he concludes unlike Rashba that one specifically needs a sealed important barrel, and if not they are nullified. It appears the root of these matters stems from the Gemara in Avodah Zarah daf 74: these are forbidden and forbid in any amount — libation wine, avodah zarah, and hides of stillborn calves, etc.; and the Gemara challenged: what did the tanna count? If davar sheb'minyan — let him teach pieces of nevelah; if prohibitions of benefit — let him teach chametz on Pesach; and it answered: the tanna has two — prohibitions of benefit and davar sheb'minyan. Rosh wrote: no — this disagrees with R' Chaim Levi that it is not nullified; rather this tanna does not count anything that does not have both; and in the end Rosh proved that chametz on Pesach in dry — if it forbade in any amount, we would say the tanna has two — prohibitions of benefit and davar sheb'minyan — even though chametz forbids in benefit, since there is no davar sheb'minyan it is nullified in the majority, etc., end of his words. One must understand his words — for he already explained: even though we say it requires two — davar sheb'minyan and prohibitions of benefit — davar sheb'minyan alone forbids; only the tanna does not count except what has both; if so, conversely we could say chametz forbids because it has prohibitions of benefit — only the tanna does not count except something that has both; but for the law, chametz that has prohibitions of benefit forbids; and one must say: granted regarding davar sheb'minyan one can say as I wrote that therefore he does not count it except in combination with prohibitions of benefit — and the difficulty itself is difficult: why does the tanna teach only what has both, and why does he not teach even something that has both apart from that? Besides that one can say there is no room to err, since it relies on the mishnah in Chullin that a piece of nevelah is not nullified because it is a piece worthy of honor — it itself teaches that one should not say that where an important item is not nullified, that is eating prohibition, but prohibition of benefit is nullified — as some authors hold; it teaches in the mishnah two — important item and prohibitions of benefit — that it is not nullified even the prohibition of benefit in it; but here there is no need, for he counts only two; but in truth prohibitions of benefit alone are not nullified — this itself is difficult: why does he not count prohibitions of benefit alone, which we do not find in any mishnah; and from this we learn that prohibitions of benefit are not nullified even regarding prohibition of benefit they are not nullified — one must say he does not hold it. And for Rashba who holds chametz forbids even without an important item, the above difficulty stands in its place: why did the tanna not teach it? One must say plainly: the tanna teaches what in wet is nullified — it teaches us, since an important item is not nullified, as he wrote; but where in wet it is not nullified in any amount, it is obvious that in dry too it is not nullified — for what is wet from dry; and as Rashba wrote, therefore he does not teach chametz, since in wet too it forbids in any amount; and all those that he teaches are not forbidden in wet and are forbidden in dry — unlike chametz. But it is difficult: behold libation wine in wet in any amount — if so, in dry too in open barrels it is not nullified, and behold he taught it (and see in Terumat HaDeshen to Rashba regarding what he wrote where in wet in any amount, in dry in any amount — and he wrote a practical difference for chametz and did not write a practical difference for libation wine); therefore it appears regarding libation wine what he wrote wet and what he wrote dry does not apply, that there is reason to distinguish between wet and dry — for Tosafot wrote several times that which they said specifically liquid in its kind is not nullified is because they relied on R' Yochanan who holds species in its species is not nullified; accordingly for R' Yochanan it must be specifically wet; and see what Tosafot wrote that even rabbanan who hold they ascend do not require wet in wet; if so, wherever they established in its kind it is not nullified they relied on R' Yochanan or on rabbanan regarding ascending; if so, wet is specifically required, and Rashba's claim applies only to Rashba speaking for himself regarding chametz — for the reason is not because of R' Yochanan, for even not its kind forbids, and the reason is because of the stringency of chametz; and he challenged well what he wrote wet in dry; if so, regarding libation wine one needs sealed barrels, but for chametz one does not need an important item — and requires study; and there is no contradiction from chametz to libation wine; investigate.
"Homeowner's loaves." — This depends on the dispute of Rosh and Rashba — for Rashba, since in wet in any amount, in dry too in any amount; if so, homeowner's loaves and likewise of a baker — if so, the difficulty is what the Gemara challenged in Avodah Zarah daf 74: let it teach homeowner's loaves regarding chametz on Pesach — for for Pesach one does not need homeowner's loaves, and even a slice is not nullified, and behold the tanna did not count except what has both, as it answers the difficulty; and let it teach chametz on Pesach — requires study at first glance. And behold also for Rambam and Ran who hold chametz is called davar sheyesh lo matirin — the Gemara's challenge will be difficult: let it teach homeowner's loaves because of chametz — for chametz in any case is forbidden in any amount even baker's loaves, for it does not depend on importance at all, only because of davar sheyesh lo matirin. And perhaps one can say they explain the mishnah on firstborn donkey as Rashi and Raavad explain — that it deals while alive and forbidden in benefit until redeemed, and they do not establish it after death because of piece worthy of honor — for one can hold that a donkey even if nullified is not fit to honor guests, or it is difficult further why I need it; therefore they explain from life; if so it deals from life — then it is R' Yehudah who holds firstborn donkey from life is forbidden in benefit, and all the more so for R' Yochanan who holds in Bekhorot daf 10 side 2 that even in death firstborn donkey is not permitted in benefit according to R' Shimon — then it is R' Yochanan; and since the mishnah follows R' Yochanan, and R' Yochanan holds chametz after Pesach is forbidden in benefit by Torah law and is not called davar sheyesh lo matirin, as Ran wrote explicitly — see there; and he challenged well: let it teach loaves, etc., regarding chametz; investigate. (And in truth this itself is difficult: why did they give a reason in firstborn donkey because of importance and not the reason because of davar sheyesh lo matirin that has remedy and redemption and commandment of redemption beforehand; and so too it is a creature that forbids from the beginning of its creation — and perhaps since one can redeem it, it is not called forbidden from the beginning of its creation; but this is certainly difficult: what does firstborn donkey have to do with davar sheb'minyan — it has davar sheyesh lo matirin; then it deals after death — requires study; investigate.) And what appeared to me to resolve this difficulty we wrote — behold chametz that in wet wet in any amount, in dry one does not need an important item — one must investigate in the Gemara that answers "whose view have you heard? R' Akiva" — behold he taught him there R' Akiva adds even homeowner's loaves. One must understand what the Gemara sought here: "whose view have you heard, R' Akiva" — behold he taught it, etc. — he should have answered briefly: behold he taught it there, etc. — what do I care about R' Akiva or another? Therefore it appears according to Rashba the difficulty "let it teach" refers to on the surface from midday until night that it is nullified in sixty as explained in Orach Chayyim siman 447 — if so, that in wet in sixty, in dry one in two is nullified except an important item; and he challenged well: let it teach regarding chametz — that is on the surface after midday, that because of the importance of loaves it is not nullified even though in wet sixty suffices. And behold Rosh in chapter 2 of Pesachim brought the view of authors who hold from midday until night it is permitted in benefit — for this too is included in "you shall destroy it," and that is cessation — see there; and in Razah and in Milchamot — however R' Akiva who holds in the first chapter of Pesachim only on the first day on Yom Tov, as it is written "you shall destroy it" and destruction is only burning, and burning is a primary labor and forbidden on Yom Tov, etc. — learn that he holds "you shall destroy it" means burning; and so Rosh wrote at the beginning of chapter 1 of Pesachim explicitly for R' Akiva "you shall destroy it" means burning; if so it is obvious it is forbidden by Torah law in benefit; and this is the Gemara's words above — for certainly the questioner who challenged "let it teach loaves" was not unaware that he already taught it there — did he not know the end of the mishnah? The beginning of the mishnah he knew that we already answered him "behold he taught it there" — and the end he did not know; rather since the tanna has two, he counts prohibitions of benefit and important item — one can say this applies to prohibitions of benefit d'oraisa, but d'rabbanan he does not count (for regarding nullification of important item there is a difference between d'oraisa and d'rabbanan, as we say in Yevamot: I repeat circle in circles ascends — that is d'rabbanan); therefore he challenged: let it teach homeowner's loaves regarding chametz on Pesach — and that is on the surface after midday, for then we require an important item since we require sixty and we do not hear even prohibitions of benefit d'rabbanan; and the Gemara answers: who is the tanna of loaves, etc. — R' Akiva; and according to him behold he taught it there; and one cannot say he should have taught to inform us even regarding prohibitions of benefit d'rabbanan — for for R' Akiva on the surface after midday it is forbidden by Torah law; if so behold he taught it there — and requires study; investigate.
"And some say." — That every davar sheb'minyan — and the author of Terumat HaDeshen ruled: everything that is customarily counted, like the tanna of a liter of dried figs; and I brought proof that the halachah follows "everything that is customarily counted," etc. — Tosafot chapter 2 of Chullin challenged: this that we say in Nazir that a father vows his son a nazir, and R' Elazar said in order to train him in mitzvot — and the Gemara challenged: if so the offerings are hullin and he brings hullin into the Temple courtyard; and it answered: hullin that were slaughtered in the Temple courtyard is not d'oraisa; and the Gemara challenged: behold one eats nevelah of melikah; and it answered: he holds there is no shechitah for fowl from the Torah; and Tosafot challenged: if so Rabbi who also holds in order to train him in mitzvot — then he must hold there is no shechitah for fowl from the Torah; and Rabbi holds explicitly like "as I commanded you," etc., that there is shechitah for fowl from the Torah; and Tosafot resolved: strained — and the difficulty at first glance is enormous; and it appears at first glance: why did the Gemara challenge that one eats melikah and nevelah — what is the difficulty in the Temple? Certainly this fowl alone was not with the priests — thousands were each day from zavim and zavot, childbearers and the like; if so when the priest would perform melikah on this fowl he would mix it with other fowl and one in two would be nullified and the issur would revert to heter and there is no issur at all. To resolve this one must say: it is true that d'rabbanan issurim they were not concerned — for they said and they said in order to train them, etc.; but to permit two d'rabbanan issurim in order to train him we do not say; and here there are two issurim — first they nullify issur initially and second: behold even though it became mixed, nevertheless it is not nullified — only by Torah law it is nullified; only behold in chapter HaTaarovot the Gemara challenged: let it be nullified in the majority even d'rabbanan — see Tosafot that piece worthy of honor does not apply, especially in small birds; and the Gemara did not find an answer or tanna of a liter of dried figs who holds everything customarily counted; and R' Eliezer resolved: important items are important and are not nullified; if so here too in Nazir that we say d'rabbanan is not nullified — the Gemara's difficulty: even though d'rabbanan was nullified — and one could answer important items are important does not apply here, for we say after melikah they will mix and nullify and the importance of davar sheb'minyan is nullified; and we have only if we say like the tanna of a liter of dried figs: everything customarily counted is not nullified, and birds are everything customarily counted; if so the Gemara's difficulty fits for R' Elazar who holds everything customarily counted — if so he has two d'rabbanan issurim and one cannot say he mixed it in a majority of permitted birds — and one must say there is no shechitah for fowl, etc.; but for Rabbi — requires study — for the house of Rabbi holds davar sheb'minyan is nullified; if so it is possible he nullified it among the permitted birds — requires study; and we hear from this that Rabbi — the halachah is like him — holds "everything that is customarily counted."
"And we follow safek to be lenient, etc." — Know that Rashba in Terumat HaDeshen daf 115 challenged R' Yochanan who holds double safek — one safek in itself and one safek in mixtures we do not say — from this that we learned in tractate Orlah: one who dyes a full sit of orlah peel and wove it into a garment — R' Meir says burn the garment, etc.; and if it became mixed with others he did not teach — learn: if it became mixed with others, according to all it is permitted because of double safek; and one can say that case is when a full sit was cut from the garment and one does not know whether a full sit of issur was cut or not — that is two safeks regarding two mixtures, end of his words. To understand his words one must know that this full sit we establish in the Gemara as tzipporet — which is an important item and is not nullified; and Rashba explains in the mishnah that it deals that he wove into a garment tzipporet and is uncertain whether from that wool dyed from orlah he wove it or from other wool; if so when it returned and the garment became mixed in another garment — behold here is double safek: safek this tzipporet was not from wool forbidden in orlah — if so automatically nullified; and if you say it was and forbids the garment — perhaps it is not this garment and it is double safek; and Rashba challenged this to R' Yochanan — behold there is one safek in itself and one safek in mixtures; and on this Rashba answered: certainly he wove from wool of the issur tzipporet, but there is in the garment more wool of heter and he recognizes only one bird — he cut and threw it; if so when this garment became mixed with another there is here double safek: safek there is no issur at all — he threw the forbidden tzipporet; and if you say he did not throw and it is still in the garment — perhaps this is not the garment at all — end of his words. One must understand his difficulty — for he holds the first safek: perhaps this tzipporet was not spun from that wool — it is an important item; if so it is a rabbinic safek and for leniency, and one cannot say as written in the name of Issur VeHeter that if safek is called piece worthy of honor it is forbidden — for there the safek is because of lack of knowledge; but here there is no safek in tzipporet that is an important item; and one must say this too is called rabbinic safek, since an important item is before us it is like presumption of issur; investigate. For a great law is heard from this by way of parable: if barrels of libation wine became mixed among many permitted barrels and one of them is a large sealed barrel and it is not known whether that barrel is of libation wine or of permitted — it is forbidden, for we say: perhaps libation wine, and sealed barrels are not nullified — like tzipporet that we establish as of orlah.
