1) [Seif 1] "An important item forbids in its kind in any amount." That which it specifies "in its kind" is not precise, for the same applies — if it was mixed not in its kind, it is not nullified. Shach s.k. 1. Peri Chadash note 1; Lechem HaPanim note 1; Beit Lechem Yehudah note 1; Kereti note 1; Chokhmat Adam note 1; Shulchan Panim note 1, who wrote "and unlike Minchat Kohen, daf 97, side 4." Chayei Adam, klal 53 note 15. Beit Yitzchak vol. 1 note 1; Zivchei Tzedek note 1. And see above siman 101, end of seif 6 in the hagahah, and in our words there, b'siyata diShmaya.
3) There. "An important item forbids in its kind in any amount" — meaning: even in several thousands it is not nullified. Shach s.k. 2. Peri Chadash note 2; Lechem HaPanim note 2; Beit Lechem Yehudah note 2; Shulchan Panim note 2; Chayei Adam ibid. Beit Yitzchak ibid, in Amudei Zahav note 3; Zivchei Tzedek note 2.
4) And the same applies: certainly a rabbinic prohibition that is customarily counted is not nullified even in a thousand. Knesset HaGedolah in Hagahot HaTur, note 5; Shulchan Panim ibid; Erekh HaShulchan note 5 — and even if it is a rabbinic prohibition and is not a prohibition of benefit but only a prohibition of eating alone, it is not nullified, and such is the halachah, not as Peri Toledot, note 3, and Shulchan Panim ibid. Erekh HaShulchan ibid. And the same applies above in the hagahah, siman 101, seif 1, regarding a piece worthy of honor — see there.
5) And important items that are forbidden in benefit — if they became mixed, even in a thousand, all are forbidden in benefit. Avodah Zarah daf 74, side 1; and thus agreed by all the poskim, Shach ibid. Knesset HaGedolah ibid, note 1; Peri Chadash ibid; Lechem HaPanim ibid; Beit Lechem Yehudah ibid; Chokhmat Adam note 2; Shulchan Panim ibid.
6) However, it is permitted to sell them to a non-Jew, except for the monetary value of the forbidden portion within them, if it is a situation where there is no concern that the non-Jew will afterward sell them to a Jew. Shach ibid. Knesset HaGedolah ibid. Peri Chadash ibid. Lechem HaPanim ibid. Beit Lechem Yehudah ibid. Kereti note 2; Chokhmat Adam ibid. Shulchan Panim ibid.
7) However, the enactment that one may sell the whole thing to a non-Jew except for the monetary value of the forbidden portion within it is stated regarding all Torah prohibitions, even when the prohibition was not nullified by Torah law — provided there is no concern that the non-Jew will return to sell it to a Jew. And such as bread baked on asherah wood and the like — one may break it into pieces and sell it thus to a non-Jew, since it is not the way of a Jew to buy it thus from a gentile. Peri Chadash ibid. Lechem HaPanim ibid. Shulchan Panim ibid, who wrote that even when it is not nullified one in one — for if a non-Jew wove a garment or baked bread, it is effective to sell it except for the monetary value of the forbidden portion within it. Zivchei Tzedek note 6.
