Permitted. Avodat HaTaharah of the Maharit — and these are his words — that brought here a case of a butcher who slaughtered seven animals in his house and they were all kosher, and he took from his house to the butchers (called "yatkes" in Yiddish) two animals. And furthermore he took from a third animal half, and the other half he left in his house with the four animals that remained in his house. And the intestines of all seven animals became mixed, and a needle was found in the lung — which is tereifah according to law — and he does not know which animal was tereifah. And behold according to law: the four that are in his house are certainly tereifot, and the two that separated — meaning that they were taken from the house to the butchers — are certainly permitted because the majority separated, and it is called automatic separation. But the doubt is on the half that is in the butcher shop — to say the part outside is kosher because the majority separated; if so, also the inner part that is in his house would be drawn outside and would also be kosher; or vice versa — the part in the house is certainly tereifah and the part outside is drawn inside and is also tereifah. Behold the Chalkat Mechokek and Maharash M'Kraka permitted, and Rav Shmuel Kaidenover and Beit Yosef ruled forbidden on the half outside that was drawn inside. See Beit Yosef; and see Pri Chadash 101 that wrote the half outside is kosher and the half inside tereifah — end of his words. And see in BeKli Sheni in his chiddushim to Chullin 42 that disagrees with Pri Chadash and wrote that the gaonim's view is compelled by the Gemara in many places that one should not rule something so wondrous — see there. And see in She'eilat Ya'avetz end of 60 that wrote Pri Chadash's view is correct — see there. [And see in Sefer Shev Shmattata siman 4 and in responsa Chemdat Shneimah siman 6 what he wrote on this. And see in responsa Ch"H siman 99 regarding a woman who had five slaughtered geese — after she sold from them to one woman one goose and half a goose, she sold to her friend two geese; and at first she lifted one goose to buy it, and again the second, and went and left one and a half at home. Again after this tereifah was found in one of the gizzards — and behold one and a half that separated first there is no doubt that they are permitted; but regarding the last two the students were in doubt: one — perhaps what she lifted, one, and did not yet take out from the house is not called separation until they exit from the house — if so both of them on the same day separated and left only one and a half and there is no majority; or one might say that the one from when she lifted to buy and left two and a half on the table — it is separation from majority and that goose was permitted; and again when she lifted the second and left one and a half — it would also be separation from majority of heter according to Pri Chadash siman 109 that we do not require one in two but mere majority; or concern according to Minchat Yitzchak (see siman 109 s.k. 1) — and if so the last at least is forbidden; and he answered at length and concluded that regarding separation we do not require specifically that it exit from the house, but any that separated from its place in a manner of separation — we say majority separated — to exclude if the woman owner of the geese moved a goose from her geese to another place without intent of separation, it is not called separation from its place;
