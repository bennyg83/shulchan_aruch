But to buy from here onward is forbidden. Explanation: even if it separated not before us — whereby it would be permitted in Torah-level kevua of nine stores — nevertheless here it is forbidden as a decree lest one buy from the fixed item itself; unlike Torah-level kevua, where the issur is recognizable in its place, we are not dealing with wicked people who would buy intentionally from the clear issur. For this reason too, whatever one bought before the safek arose in rabbinic kevua is permitted, even if it separated before us — for behold it is still entirely permitted, and we do not decree on one who bought at a time of permissibility lest he buy after the issur.
That not everyone is expert. So Rashba wrote — Tur brought him — and wrote on this: I do not understand what he wrote to permit pieces not piece worthy of honor, but he was stringent to forbid them, for it appears they are forbidden by law as "every half," etc. Beit Yosef and other great authorities wondered on Tur, for "every fixed item is half-and-half" applies only to nine stores where the issur is recognizable in its place — unlike here, where the issur is not recognizable, and they decreed only on piece worthy of honor; and on a piece not piece worthy of honor it is plainly nullified in the majority, and its law is like a mixture — and Beit Yosef, Darkei Moshe, and Perishah resolved that Tur understood Rashba's words as dealing with a case where there is issur in one butcher shop and it is not known in which shop. The explanation is surprising — how could Tur write on this? And Avraham Abarbanel wrote that whatever they bought before the safek, all is permitted; in such a case, which is Torah-level safek, certainly even before the safek arose it would be forbidden — and Rosh permitted only rabbinic kevua. I saw many explanations and they wearied themselves to find an opening to explain our master's words; and I wondered further why Beit Yosef did not also challenge Rashba — why he was stringent here to forbid even pieces not worthy, lest they err more than what is found in the house. And if you say that in butcher shops where many frequent we decree more stringently — I found so in Maharshal, chapter Gid HaNashe siman 24; but we find the opposite in the mishnah in chapter Chavis (Shabbos 147a): "but ten people," etc., and Rashi: since they are numerous they remind one another — and on an individual they decreed more; rather, this reasoning does not apply. What appears to me is that our master Tur's words are correct; and when Beit Yosef challenged from himself, he should have challenged from what Tur himself wrote above in this siman — and likewise a piece cut after it became mixed is nullified, as I explained above; if so, it would be unseemly for our master to contradict his own words within the time of speech — therefore it appears to me he intended well: after all, we do not permit above at the end of siman 101 by cutting one of the mixtures except through migu — that if the issur was cut, behold all are permitted, etc.; and this applies when there is only one forbidden piece here, and then we can say the issur was cut and the rest is entirely permitted — unlike here, where one animal is found treifah in the butcher shops and it is not known which it is: even when you would permit a piece not piece worthy of honor through nullification in the majority, nevertheless the worthy pieces are still forbidden — even if you say this unworthy piece is from the issur, for the issur is not in one piece but a whole animal is found treifah; if so, you cannot say that if this piece is the issur, all are permitted — and behold certainly there are still other worthy pieces that are forbidden; therefore we cannot permit all the pieces, even those not worthy, for from one body we have issur and heter before us, and this is something impossible except that certainly the pieces not worthy are also forbidden like the worthy, since both are from one body — therefore our master Tur challenged well that all are forbidden by law because of rabbinic kevua, as they decreed upon it to be like half, as he wrote above; and until here our master did not permit above in this siman regarding pieces that were cut, except if all the issur is only one piece — and then there is migu: above, if the issur was cut automatically, no issur remains here — unlike here; and this is clear and proven — for if not, why was Rashba stringent here more than what is found inside a person's house, as he wrote above? Therefore he challenged him with a conclusive answer. Again I saw in what Beit Yosef copied in this siman from Rosh's responsum siman 500: and whatever was cut from the lambs into thin pieces, none of which is piece worthy of honor, etc. — all is permitted, end of his words; and according to this there is a difficulty on Tur, that Rosh's words would contradict each other. And behold in the Rosh responsum in my possession the words "and whatever was cut from the lambs" are missing — and it appears Beit Yosef emended thus; and in my view it must be there: "and all the lambs that were cut into thin pieces," etc. — but we do not permit one lamb that was cut from it while the rest would be forbidden, since both are one body.
