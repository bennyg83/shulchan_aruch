Who, etc. Nevertheless there in the name of the Rashba: (Collected notes) Who, etc. The Tur in the name of the Rashba — and this is from the words of Terumat HaDeshen the short version — and he did not see the words of Terumat HaDeshen the long version, who wrote there (120, 2) that according to R' Yitzchak chapter 3 of Chullin (95, 1) (in Tosafot) s.v. hen, etc. — forbidden. And so too R' Shimon, end of his words. But he wrote that it appears to him like the words of R' Tam and She'iltot, and therefore it is permitted in such a case. And there is a difficulty on the Tur who ruled like the words of R' Yitzchak, and here he wrote the words of the Rashba [and likewise the Beit Yosef challenged] — and so too in the responsa that certainly if he had seen the words of the Rashba he would not have written thus in his name. But he wrote that nevertheless the Rosh does not hold that this depends on that — for behold he too ruled like the words of R' Yitzchak, and even so he holds that in such a case it is permitted, as the Tur wrote in his name. And nevertheless one should not be lenient, since in the Rashba it appears that according to R' Yitzchak it is forbidden. And he wrote that so too R' Shimon — and the Mechaber's reason is because he ruled in seif 6 like the words of R' Tam and the Rashba; therefore he wrote here too like the words of the Rashba. But the later authorities hold like the words of R' Yitzchak (end of passage).
Everything, etc. As noted — that everything one separated before the safek was born — s.v. permitted — in all cases.
But to take, etc. — he means it goes without saying regarding piece worthy of honor that it is forbidden, as written in Zevachim there — and it follows that it has the law of kavu'a, etc., even though it is not literally kavu'a, only their importance remains. And even though there regarding rings one fell into the sea they are permitted — and the Tur wrote in the name of the Rosh that once the safek was born, even if he threw it down it is permitted, for there is no room to decree against intentional action, as noted — and as Rav said like R' Eliezer, and R' Eliezer "If it was offered" he says, as noted. Nevertheless, behold Tosafot wrote there specifically regarding the Great Sea since they are not there; however regarding eating it is permitted, as written in seif 7. And even the later authorities who disagree — before the safek was known they concede.
