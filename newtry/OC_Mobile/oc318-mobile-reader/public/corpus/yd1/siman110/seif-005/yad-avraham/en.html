(There in Shach s.k. 59) A rabbinic safek issur in something that has a permitted aspect is forbidden. Know that Peri Chadash in Laws of Yom Tov and Peri Megadim in OC wrote that this applies specifically to a factual safek — such as safek whether it was born on Yom Tov regarding an egg and prepared food — but in safek of a dispute among authorities or safek of the halachah we follow leniency in rabbinic matters even regarding something that has a permitted aspect. So too in She'eilat Shalom siman 96; one can prove this from the sugya in Menachos (68b). With this one can answer the sugya at the end of Shabbos: R. Yochanan ruled like R. Shimon who has no muktzeh; it challenged — but R. Yochanan ruled like an anonymous mishnah, "We do not give water or slaughter desert animals"; R. Yochanan answered he found another anonymous source; Rashi: Beit Hillel is considered like an anonymous mishnah. The difficulty remains: what relevance does Beit Hillel's anonymous mishnah have for R. Yochanan? But per Peri Chadash's distinction between factual safek and safek of halachah or dispute of authorities where we are lenient — one can answer this Shabbos case: regarding muktzeh, a rabbinic issur, we need not infer from Beit Hillel's anonymous mishnah that they have no muktzeh, for in any case we hold the halachah follows the lenient view in rabbinic matters — as R. Yochanan himself ruled in the first chapter of Avodah Zarah (7). So too Rashba and Ran in Nedarim (47b) wrote it is reasonable that we pose a question that leans to leniency: if one said "These fruits are konam upon so-and-so," substitutes are permitted — since regarding issur hanaah itself substitutes are only rabbinic for adults, it is a rabbinic safek and we are lenient. The difficulty: nedarim has something that has a permitted aspect in the question (59b), and regarding something that has a permitted aspect we are stringent in its safek — as Rav Ashi said at the beginning of Beitzah (4a) regarding an egg born on Yom Tov, its safek is forbidden because it is something that has a permitted aspect, and anything that has a permitted aspect is not nullified even d'rabbanan; Rashi: just as we are stringent in its nullification, so too we are stringent in its safek. Rather it is proven that Rashba and Ran hold specifically regarding factual safek — as in egg born on Yom Tov or on a weekday — that Rav Ashi said we are also stringent in safek; but in safek of halachah in a dispute of authorities or in an unresolved question like nedarim, we are lenient d'rabbanan even regarding something that has a permitted aspect. I wrote further on this in seif 4 in chiddushim on Chullin.
