And the treifah pieces were not known, etc. That is, treifah pieces were mixed among the permitted ones in one slaughterhouse and he does not know from which piece he took; but if the pieces stand by themselves and there is safek in the slaughterhouse, its law was already explained in s.k. 14:
But to take from here onward. The Bach wrote at the end of s.k. 6: this is specifically with pieces that were mixed one into another; but if the slaughterhouse that has the prohibition is not known as kevua — such as when a treifah lung was found and it is not known from which slaughterhouse — we go according to the Rosh and our master (meaning, according to his explanation that it is the words of the Rosh and Tur, see there, but it is not necessary as written in s.k. 14) that by law all the pieces in the slaughterhouses are forbidden, even if they are not a piece worthy of honor; and it is possible that the Rashba also does not disagree on this, for he said nothing about this and does not deal with it, end of his words (and so in Darkei Moshe and Torat Chatat and Derishah they explained that the Tur holds this is kevua, see there):
Forbidden. It appears even if they already took it, it is forbidden (and in another version it says: but to take from here onward is forbidden — and if so it implies nothing). And so it appears more in the simanim of Torat Chatat, end of general rule 43, see there, and inside in Torat Chatat there:
Since not everyone is expert, etc. The Bach wrote at the beginning of s.k. 6: this is the reason — since the mixture is in slaughterhouses, where many buy from there, the matter is close to error, which is not so when mixed thus for an individual in his house, where one should not be stringent (meaning, even though there are also pieces fit to be honored there that were not mixed), but only to rule on the law of a piece not fit to be honored, that it is nullified in the majority — until here is his language.
