"And the mixture is unknown." — As I wrote in the name of Ra'ah; and behold the difficulty: what the Gemara challenged on Rav who said meat that disappeared is forbidden — from this that we learned: nine stores, etc.; and in "found" we follow majority — and he needed to force regarding gentile's hand: why did it not answer plainly in "found" in a Jew's hand and the Jew bought it before treifot were known in the slaughterhouses — and it is separated; and this is in "found" that was found in his hand before the safek arose, and we follow majority as written here in Shulchan Aruch; and meat that disappeared from sight — there is no Jew guarding it; and if we say as some hold in Rashba that those two versions of Rabbi — if he said outright "because of this fool" or "to cause pain" they disagree in that law whether separated before the safek arose — whether its law is like explicit or like buying from kevua — it is resolved; for accordingly the Gemara cannot establish the mishnah thus — for if so the difficulty would stand from this version to the other version — and requires study. And with this one can understand Rambam's words that we expanded in siman 63: how he wrote in "found" in a gentile's hand forbidden because of disappearance from sight — and it is difficult from the Gemara's words "found in a gentile's hand is different"; and Maggid Mishneh wrote that thus is explained in Yerushalmi — nevertheless it is difficult as above: how did he leave Talmud Bavli words and rely on Yerushalmi words — is this not the measure? And see what I wrote above siman 63; and accordingly it is resolved that Rambam certainly holds Yerushalmi words are correct in the reasoning that a gentile does not pay attention and does not guard at all — only the mishnah is strained for him to the Gemara that teaches explicitly "found — we follow majority" and he has no answer except in "found" in a gentile's hand; and why need "found" in a Jew's hand before the safek arose — for if so this version would be difficult as above. But according to what we hold like that first version that before the safek it is permitted — if so one can say in a Jew's hand and before the safek arose, and we return to Yerushalmi's reasoning that "found" in a gentile's hand is forbidden; and so Rambam ruled. However from Rashba's words in his novellae and Terumat HaDeshen daf 102 it does not appear thus — for he holds the two versions disagree in that law whether before the safek arose is called separated or not — see there; if so the above difficulty stands in its place; and according to what I wrote above siman 63 that therefore it teaches in "found" we follow majority and does not teach permitted — for the majority is before us: nine sellers of slaughtered meat — only we hold majority of the world if city gates are not locked combine; and as I wrote; and accordingly granted in "found" in the market or in a gentile's hand there is well room for safek that perhaps it came from majority of the world through dealers and captives — unlike if found in a Jew's hand who bought from stores — only since he bought before the safek arose it is called "found" — here there is no safek in majority of the world, for the Jew bought them clearly from these stores; if so the language "we follow majority" is not possible; investigate. And behold one must understand on Rif, may his memory be blessed, that he omitted that incident said of that butcher — both the first version and the latter version — without any mention; and did the Gemara bring it for nothing? And in truth if the matters are as I wrote, one can say those two versions disagree in that law regarding the explanation before the safek. I said in my novellae to resolve: behold Rashba challenged in the explanation after the safek not before us — if it is in a place where the issur is not recognizable it is forbidden — a decree lest he take from kevua; if so even in the explanation before the safek arose we decree lest he take from kevua after knowledge of the safek, since the issur is not recognizable. And the difficulty is clear as the Mechaberim wrote — only as they said: in a place where the issur is recognizable we do not apply lest he take from kevua — for when he eats issur; but if the issur is not recognizable we are concerned he will take from kevua and think the issur was nullified; if so one can say this applies where by Torah law it is truly nullified only d'rabbanan it is not nullified — there is concern he will be lenient; but where by Torah law it is not nullified — if so the heter reverted to full issur — we do not decree he will take from kevua, for behold the d'oraisa issur was not nullified and the issur stands in place. And clear proof for this reasoning according to one answer in Tosafot Zevachim — and see above siman 109 that even dry in dry R' Yochanan holds species in its species is not nullified; and it is difficult: what the Gemara challenged in chapter HaTaarovot: all sacrifices that became mixed, etc. — why should it be nullified in the majority, and the Gemara toiled to establish according to tanna of a liter of dried figs and the like; and it is difficult: why all the toil — say it is R' Yochanan who holds species in its species is not nullified; and all the more so: if so it will be difficult what it challenged afterward "let us suppress them since they move" — and let us say whatever separates, the majority separates; and it answered: a decree lest he take from kevua; if so why need R' Yochanan who holds d'oraisa is not nullified — if so he would have recognizable issur and lest he take from kevua does not apply as I wrote; if so Rif holds that which Rabbi permitted in the slaughterhouses — since they bought before the safek arose and he was not concerned lest he take from kevua — Rabbi according to his reasoning who holds a drop of milk like R' Yochanan that species in its species is not nullified — if so lest he take from kevua does not apply as above; but according to what we hold not like Rabbi and species in its species is nullified — if so even though they bought before the safek, nevertheless forbidden — a decree lest he take from kevua; therefore Rif omitted that incident that we are concerned he will take from kevua; investigate. And with this one can say in another manner on Rambam who ruled like Yerushalmi in "found" in a gentile's hand forbidden — and it is against our Gemara — one can say it is difficult for Rambam from what Ran wrote: how is "found" in a gentile's hand permitted — from majority he separated? Behold he certainly bought nevelah meat that is reduced for him as Ran wrote regarding a place where they do not announce; and one must say the mishnah of nine stores deals when treifah was found in one of the slaughterhouses and it is not known in whose it was the treifah — if so well we follow majority, for there is no concern for reduced price, for the seller did not know it became treifah in the store — and requires study. "Except after separation." — And if they sold all the animals one by one until everything was sold and afterward the treifah became known — it occurred to Ba'al Darkei Noam: the last ones who bought the meat are forbidden, for the first ones who bought everything bought from majority and separated; if so the treifah remained until the end; if so in the portion of the last ones is the treifah and it is forbidden. And there is proof from this that Rashba argued: how do we decree in the explanation lest he take from kevua — even in the explanation before the safek arose we also decree lest he take from kevua; and Rosh answered in responsum: between before treifot were known and after treifot were known it is recognizable and we do not decree, end of his words; and afterward Shach continued and ruled: before it was known we do not decree on after it was known; and it is still difficult: we should decree even for one who bought before it was known lest he take from kevua — and not from after it was known, only from those who bought last before it became known — for we establish in them treifah and we decree on all lest he take from them, and they are all before the mixture — and Rosh's answer does not apply; and all the more so that those who bought last are permitted — for Rashba wrote: if we suppress them since they move, the last two are forbidden; but if all were uprooted, everything is permitted; if so those who bought before the safek arose and did not leave all of them — it is as if all were uprooted and permitted entirely — for at the time treifot became known, all treifot already dispersed from the slaughterhouses; and it is obvious.
"But to take from here, etc." — Bach's view to distinguish regarding kevua: if ten animals are slaughtered before us and one treifah lung is found and it is not known from which animal — it is not called kevua and d'oraisa is nullified; but if here are ten designated stores and in each store was one animal and it became clear that one of the animals is treifah and it is not known from which store — it is kevua and like half-and-half; and Pri Chadash challenged that d'oraisa is not called kevua unless the issur is recognizable — if so according to Bach one must say d'rabbanan is called kevua; only it is difficult: is there a place in Gemara and early poskim that they made kevua and forbade it d'rabbanan? And behold in the substance of the law that wherever the issur is not recognizable in its place it is not called kevua — investigation is needed, for Pri Chadash in s.k. 13 brought R' Shimshon's view in Keritot: wherever the issur is not recognizable in its place it is not called kevua, etc. — for if not all fields would be forbidden in planting because of mixture of Eitan brook and there is no nullification in them, for it is kevua; rather since the issur is not known it is not called kevua (and in truth this proof is not so strong — is it clear to us there is mixture of Eitan brook until we call it kevua upon it; and if it cannot be otherwise that over time Israel on their land presumably brought heifer of atonement — nevertheless perhaps since it is forbidden in planting they built on Eitan brook houses and towers and the like — since it was not clarified there is here a field forbidden in planting, obviously it is not called kevua); and Pri Chadash also brought proof from Tosafot Nazir that wrote explicitly: wherever the issur is not recognizable it is not called kevua; therefore one who says to his agent "Go and betroth a wife for me" outright and the agent died — as we hold he is forbidden to all women in the world — it is a mere penalty that we follow majority and majority of women are unbetrothed; and if this one accepted betrothal — it would be kevua — it is not kevua since the issur is not recognizable. But accordingly according to what the poskim wrote — and see Rashba's novellae in Gittin who holds for Ramban that by law it is forbidden and not a penalty — learn he holds it is full kevua even though it is not recognizable; if so the matter is a disputed tannaitic statement; and we say in the Gemara Yevamot daf 16 side 2: a gentile who betrothed in this time — we are concerned for his betrothal lest he is of the tribes; and the Gemara challenged: behold whatever separates, the majority separates; and it answered: in a place where they are fixed; and Rashi explained it is kevua and like half-and-half — and behold there the fixedness is not recognizable — do we know who they are? Nevertheless it is kevua. And truly Tosafot explained: in their place they are the majority — but not for this reason, only lest they go to him — see there; and automatically we hear such a case is kevua; and so in Sanhedrin daf 80 side 2 that Tosafot challenged: stoned among burned, etc. — let us follow majority; and Tosafot resolved: it is kevua; and they challenged "let us suppress them since they move"; and they resolved: lest he take from kevua, end of his words — from this it is explained that even though the issur is not recognizable, nevertheless it is called kevua; and truly were it not for Tosafot's words in Nazir I would say in every place one in two is nullified and kevua does not apply — for it was nullified immediately and the issur returned to permit; and you find only recognizable issur where nullification does not apply; and so they wrote in Tosafot Chullin and Zevachim — but person to person nullification does not apply (therefore Tosafot challenged in Sanhedrin let us follow majority and did not challenge to nullify in majority); if so even though it is not recognizable kevua applies since it was not nullified; and all the reason that we require recognizable issur — for if not behold there is nullification — unlike person to person where there is no nullification — and requires study. Only this Nazir is difficult for him — for it also deals with person; and one can resolve with difficulty; and Tosafot chapter 2 of Bava Batra in the sugya of "fallen" that challenged R' Chanina who said majority and proximity — follow majority: what does it teach us — it is a mishnah: nine stores, etc., in "found" we follow majority even though it was found near the store selling treifah; and it is difficult: what is the difficulty — one can say the mishnah deals where it became known that one store sells nevelot but it is not known which — if so how is proximity applicable — behold the treifah store is not known at all (and see what I wrote above that with this Ra'ah's difficulty is resolved — see there); and if the difficulty is a decree lest he take from kevua since the issur is not recognizable — behold this "lest he take," etc. is a Gemara statement and Rava is who innovated for us this answer in chapter HaTaarovot lest he take from kevua; if so R' Chanina cannot rely on this that its law would be proven from the mishnah — perhaps we establish the mishnah as I wrote and we do not say Rava's answer; and until here one must say he holds additionally: if the issur is not recognizable we do not say kevua; if so how is "he bought," etc. established as kevua like half-and-half — learn the issur is recognizable; and well one can say let us follow proximity; investigate. However Rama below siman 114 seif 10 wrote: if it is known that some gentiles certainly put wine in it and some do not put — we follow majority in it, but it is forbidden to buy for his home, for it is kevua — see there — learn even where the issur is not recognizable it is kevua; however one can say: necessarily a Jew saw the gentile put wine in it — for if not whence do we know the gentile certainly put wine — behold he does not see; and one must say a Jew saw the act and spoke and went — if so even for us the issur is not distinguishable — nevertheless since there is a Jew who knows only he is not before us — nevertheless regarding kevua we call the issur recognizable, for at least a Jew knows; investigate; if so most authors hold regarding an animal certainly we require recognizable issur.
"That is not, etc." — And Tur wrote in kitchens: there are pieces worthy of honor and there are those not worthy of honor — all forbidden, for not everyone is expert between piece worthy of honor and what is not — meaning; and so Rashba; and Tur wrote that by law it is forbidden, for it is kevua; and the acharonim toiled to explain his words: what kevua is here since the issur is not recognizable; and Bach wrote that Tur holds even though the issur is not recognizable it is kevua — and we already wrote on this. However the rabbi Taz cited a piece that was cut into two — one worthy of honor and one not worthy of honor; if so if we judge like Rashba that piece worthy of honor is forbidden and the rest permitted — then the piece would be half permitted and half forbidden; if so the law's way is to forbid everything and not say half the piece is kosher and half treifah; and for this Tur intended in kitchens: there are pieces not worthy of honor — it was not cut from a piece worthy of honor, and as this is forbidden so this is forbidden; and Pri Chadash challenged there is no substance in these matters — for what if half is permitted and half forbidden, such is the law: this was nullified and this was not nullified — see there. And behold at first glance there is proof for Taz's law from this that we challenged at the beginning of chapter HaTaarovot: let it be nullified in the majority; and Tosafot challenged: is a large ox less, etc. — behold it is piece worthy of honor; and what is the difficulty — perhaps the Gemara's difficulty: why should they die — for in Zevachim we hold if the meat was lost and burned and only fat and emurim remain, he throws some blood — if so even though it became mixed in Temple issurim and one cannot eat the meat since it was not nullified, nevertheless the fat and emurim are nullified, for fat is not called piece worthy of honor and the crop, gizzard, and kidneys — all are not worthy of honor and are nullified and fit to be offered; if so they should be offered and even the blood thrown and the meat burned; if so the Gemara's difficulty why should they die is well — and strained to say Tosafot hold specifically if after slaughter and before throwing the loss happened to the meat he throws the blood, but before slaughter whatever is lacking even ordinary meat he should not slaughter l'chatchila — whence for Tosafot this and to challenge thus; rather Tosafot hold an established reasoning that it is impossible in one piece and one body that half will be nullified and half not — if so since the animal's body was piece worthy of honor it was not nullified, even the fat and innards will not be nullified; investigate. So I argued for Taz; but for the correct law as Pri Chadash's view. And behold Beit HaLevi cited an incident where one rib was found half of which was taken from it before treifot were known and half after it was known; and the author of Choshen Mishpat and the Gaon R' Eishel, may his memory be blessed, permitted both; and the Gaon Maharshak author of Beit HaZahav and he is the rabbi Beit HaLevi ruled to forbid both; and Pri Chadash permits what was taken before and forbids what was taken after treifot were known. And behold in truth at first glance the Gaons' words R' Eishel and the author of Choshen Mishpat are reasonable: if we permit half a rib of what they took before the safek arose because whatever separates, the majority separates — if so we establish that rib that the majority separated and it came from a kosher animal, for such is the tradition "the majority separates"; and it is as if witnesses testify this is from the kosher — if so how can we say afterward to forbid the second half that was already clarified according to Torah presumption that a rib is from a kosher animal? But my colleague the pious Gaon R' Avraham Pasilburg, may his memory be blessed, argued: behold in that law to permit what they took before the safek arose — Tosafot argued lest he come to take from kevua; and Rosh resolved: between what was taken before and what was taken after — no difference; and this is all in what was taken before the safek arose; but if we come to permit eating half a rib that is after the safek arose — here there is well concern he will take from kevua; and Rosh resolved: it does not apply, for it is truly after the safek arose — will the whole world discern that the second half of the rib is separated from before, etc.; and with this certainly we decree; investigate. And I toiled and found there is nothing in what is written in Beit Meir in Sefer Asifat Zekenim to Bava Metzia: he wrote regarding one of the counted ones who jumped into it — exempt from tithe; and Tosafot challenged "let us suppress them since they move" — and let us say whatever separates, the majority separates; and it is mentioned in that book an answer in the name of Rosh: that which whatever separates, the majority separates is not certain and nothing emerges from safek — for perhaps he did not separate, only the Torah permitted this safek without forbidding; if so since it is safek it does not apply to tithe that we say the majority separated and it is liable for tithe — for tithe the Merciful One said certainly and not safek tithe, end of his words. And according to this that which whatever separates, the majority separates does not make certainty, only safek — requires study from what I wrote that the rib taken before the safek arose is permitted because the majority separated; and not that this is certainly from the kosher ones, only safek — however the Torah did not forbid this safek above — unlike what remained until after the safek, behold it too is in safek as before, and in that safek the Torah did not permit at all; if so there is no difficulty at all. Therefore the correct view is Pri Chadash's words: the first half rib is permitted and the second half forbidden.
