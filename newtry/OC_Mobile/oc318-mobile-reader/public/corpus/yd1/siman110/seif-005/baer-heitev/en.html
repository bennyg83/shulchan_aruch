"**Pieces.**" That is, tereifah pieces became mixed among the kosher ones in one bundle and he does not know from which piece he took; but if the pieces stand by themselves and there is a doubt in a shop, its law was already explained above, seif 3.
"**And from then on.**" The Shach wrote that it appears even that what was taken is forbidden; but in some texts it reads "but to take from here and onward is forbidden" — and if so, nothing is implied at all; end of his words.
"And they will err." The Maharshal and Bach wrote: that is the reason — since the mixtures in the butcher shops are such that many buy from there, it is close to err; unlike if it became mixed thus for an individual in his house, where one need not be stringent even though there are also pieces worthy of honor that were not mixed. And the Taz disagrees on this, for the reason that we find in the Gemara that among many one may be more lenient because they remind one another, and for an individual they decreed more [and Nachalat Binyamin challenged on this that it is not proper to say "many remind one another" except when they are in one gathering; but here there is concern that they will take one after the other, see there].
