(There, in Shulchan Aruch s.k. 7) And specifically when eating the remainder two by two. The Rambam explained that which says "R' Eliezer only permitted with two" — that is, it refers to falling, meaning two must fall; and afterward even if one remains, it is permitted. And he challenges him: according to his view he should not challenge from that which the Sages say, etc.; according to my view one may well say that if one remains it is permitted, and R' Eliezer only spoke regarding the fallen ones. And it appears to me that from here one may say: why did the Gemara not challenge from R' Eliezer himself, who said in explanation "if he offered the first one, all are permitted"? And one must say that the Gemara was in doubt about the explanation of Bava Batra — whether it refers to falling or to eating — and it did not wish to challenge from R' Eliezer himself; one may say that for falling one suffices, but when eating from the remainder one must eat two by two; therefore the Gemara challenges from both sides. And the Sages say: even if they offered all of them and not one remained among them — that one is forbidden; it is implied that according to R' Eliezer if one remained, it is permitted to offer; and this is difficult for R' Eliezer. And if so, if we say that which says "R' Eliezer only permitted with two" refers to falling — the beginning of the mishnah is difficult, that which says in explanation "if he offered the first one, all are permitted" — that is falling; and the Gemara answers: what is "one"? One pair — that is, for falling there must be one pair; but regarding eating one may eat each one independently. And one should not challenge — from where does the Rambam know to explain thus? One may say the Rambam proved that necessarily "one pair" refers to falling — for behold Tosafos challenge: how do we derive from R' Eliezer that if one fell into the sea it is permitted? Behold R' Eliezer derives from a verse that is written "no blemish shall be accepted in it" — it is implied specifically when standing independently; but through mixtures it is permitted; and how do we derive from him other forbidden things — perhaps it is a gezeirah ha-katuv in a korban? And one may say: necessarily the verse refers regarding one in one — for if one in two, otherwise by Torah law it is nullified in the majority; if so we derive well, as we see that the Torah permitted if a blemished animal became mixed — even one relative to one is permitted for offering; if so, the same applies to other forbidden things where one became mixed one in two, for by Torah law it is nullified in the majority — only the Sages decreed; but where it fell properly we say the forbidden item fell — until here from this the Rambam proved that necessarily "one pair" refers to falling; for Tosafos' difficulty is difficult for him; and one must say as he wrote, and it is difficult for him: how do we say that one must eat them two by two? If so, how do we find that one may offer a blemished animal? For behold if one became mixed with one, even offering two at once does not help — for behold the entire reason is that one must eat both at once because we say "since this one was permitted, this one too"; but here there is no need for this, for both are forbidden — or it refers to falling; and we find well that if two became mixed with two and two fell into the sea, we say these two fell — investigate. See Shach, who brought that for something whose falling is recognizable one needs only one; and at first glance there is proof for his words from that which he challenges R' Nachman, who said "a ring," etc. — behold R' Eliezer only permitted with two; and he answered: I too say two; and why did he not also challenge R' Levi, who permits with barrels where one of them fell? Or that in this all agree that one needs only one because its falling is recognizable; but it is difficult from the Gemara's explanation — why two? And derive one from its fellow — behold they are not equal, for with barrels one needs only one, and with rings two must fall — requires study.
