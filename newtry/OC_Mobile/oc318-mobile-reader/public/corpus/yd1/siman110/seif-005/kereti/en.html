Rather, after it separated. And if he sold every animal before the treifah became known — the view of the Rav, author of Darkei Noam, is that those people who bought last have tereifah meat. See Peleti where I wrote that everything is permitted; and so too is the common practice.
"For not everyone is expert" — this implies that strictly speaking it is permitted, for a fixed case does not apply: even though a prohibition was presumed in the slaughterhouses, since the prohibition is not recognizable, a fixed case does not apply. The Bach's view follows the Tur that nevertheless a fixed case applies; and if so, strictly speaking it is forbidden. See Peleti. And regarding an animal, it appears certainly that it is not called a fixed case; but person-to-person, where nullification does not apply, it appears that it is a fixed case even if it is not recognizable. Nevertheless, the view of the Rama appears that in one of the animals it follows the Bach; and see Peleti, that one should force the interpretation that the Rama also holds thus, that the prohibition must be recognizable.
Everyone — the view of the Taz: one rib that was cut into two parts, one large and a piece worthy of honor and one small; and he does not mean that both of them are not nullified. The Pri Chadash disagrees, that the small one is nullified. And see what I wrote in Peleti — proof for the Taz. In any case, for the practical halachah the correct view follows the Pri Chadash. There was also a practical case with a rib that was divided into two, and half was taken before the doubt arose and half after the doubt arose; and I agreed with the Pri Chadash that the first is kosher and the second is treifah.
"Expert" — meaning in slaughterhouses; but in one's home one need not forbid a piece that is not a piece worthy of honor — Pri Chadash.
