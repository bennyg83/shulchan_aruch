If one ate one of them unintentionally, etc. — these words are puzzling; it appears to me here that the reason for this permission Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Hagahot ShaDa and Terumat HaDeshen: even though R. Yochanan wrote that specifically when it fell by itself to the sea we permit — and as Shulchan Aruch wrote afterward — but if he threw it, even unintentionally, we penalize unintentional like intentional; that is specifically regarding falling to the sea; but eating unintentionally does not apply to penalize lest he eat it intentionally, for according to his view the issur is the food — for in his mind the issur will go away, etc. And this contradicts what Rosh wrote in the responsum Beit Yosef brought nearby: if one ate one of the mixtures, even to a Jew — as explained there in responsum, klal 20, law 17, that specifically before the safek arose it is permitted, but if eaten after the safek arose it is forbidden because of a decree of intentional action; and it appears to me the reason is we are concerned lest he feed it intentionally to a dog or a non-Jew — for if we do not decree regarding eating to a Jew, why was there need there to seek permission because it was before the safek arose — behold even after it arose there is permission; rather certainly there is no permission; and further Rosh deals there with eating and brings this — and even though R. Yochanan wrote regarding throwing unintentionally that it is forbidden, etc., and Tur likewise wrote thus — learn there is no distinction between eating and throwing at all; and this contradicts what Terumat HaDeshen wrote above; and without doubt the author of Terumat HaDeshen did not see that responsum — and had he seen it he would not have disagreed; and in particular the reason is reasonable — certainly there is concern as I wrote, lest he feed it to a dog — as we are concerned if he actually gives it to a dog, so too we are concerned if he eats it unintentionally; until here it appears to me to be stringent here regarding eating one of them in every manner, and there is no permission except if it is eaten by itself to a dog; and the matter is clear:
And even to eat them two at a time, etc. — so it is in Tur; and Beit Yosef challenged him from Tosafot in Zevachim, who wrote: "to benefit from all at once — it is obvious it is forbidden, because in this eating one certainly eats one issur" — meaning that to eat two, even for one person, is permitted — for if not, let them teach us the novelty that for one person it is forbidden even two after two, and all the more so at once; and one may say that our master expounds that "to benefit from all at once" is forbidden even when two people benefit from them, since at one time they benefit from all, etc., end of his words Beit Yosef; and on the basis of these words Rama ruled in both cases stringently — whether one person eats all of them, even two by two, or two people at one time. And I say: their honor rests in its place — Beit Yosef's difficulty is nothing, for Tosafot's words in the chapter Gid HaNasheh (Chullin daf 100) at the top of the page escaped him, where they wrote in this language: "but for one man, perhaps it was forbidden," end of his words — in the law of an item not considered important that is nullified in the majority, it follows all the more so here — for even Rosh who is lenient there as Tur wrote in siman 109 that it is permitted to eat them at once, concedes here since there it is permitted by the scriptural decree "after the majority to incline," which does not apply here when one was lost among them and it is permitted by double safek, as Beit Yosef wrote in siman 109 in the name of Rosh, chapter Gid HaNasheh, end of his words — forbidden for one person to eat all of them; it follows there is no difficulty at all from what Tosafot wrote in Zevachim that to benefit from all at once is obviously forbidden — for certainly so it is, that for one person to eat all of them not at once is not obvious to them as forbidden but perhaps forbidden; but to benefit from all at once is plainly forbidden — their words are precise and one cannot say novelty here, but our master here regarding one that was lost goes stringently and forbids also what Tosafot were uncertain about regarding nullification in the majority; rather regarding nullification in the majority in siman 109 he holds like his father Rosh that it is permitted even for one person by the scriptural decree "after the majority to incline," that he did not write they disagree between them; but here both Tur and Rosh are uncertain regarding one person — automatically we follow stringency — it follows our master wrote well here. Again I saw in Derishah and Perishah that he emends Tur's words to "or that one person ate all of them together" — and it follows from this that not together there is no issur — but this is not so, for also in siman 120 below it is forbidden regarding a non-Jew for one person; and there in Derishah he noticed this and wrote that Tur distinguishes between this and the issur of a non-Jew — but this is certainly not so, for many times we derive many laws from the laws of non-Jews, such as a cup of a non-Jew that fell among cups above, as Beit Yosef brings; and he brought in Derishah proof for his words from Terumat HaDeshen siman 171, who wrote in the name of the Yad, and these are his words: investigate — only for others to eat two by two together, or that two people eat them in such a manner, is specifically permitted — behold in truth it appears from this that it is permitted for one person to eat all of them two by two; and one must say that he had a different text in Tur's words — but to eat them one by one, that one person eats all of them, is forbidden — and he did not have the text "or"; or if according to Derishah's text it is difficult — for if so, why did he write at the end "one person," for at the outset too he deals with this, except that the issur there is that he did not eat them two by two — rather he certainly had the text as I recalled; but nevertheless the truth guides his way — the text before us in Tur is true, since also in siman 120 he wrote thus, that for one person it is forbidden in every case, and there is full issur in one person eating all of them, even if he ate them two by two — even according to Tur who is lenient regarding nullification in the majority in siman 109 that it is permitted to benefit from all of them, here he concedes as I wrote nearby; and all the more so according to Tosafot that I recalled, that even in nullification in the majority the law would be so for issur. And since the foundation of Beit Yosef was hidden in this — in that he challenged Tur — automatically his building that he built on this fell, that it would be forbidden for two people to benefit at one time from them — for we have no power to invent an issur ourselves; and further, if Tur had expounded thus he would have written this issur explicitly, for it is a great novelty and is mentioned explicitly in Tosafot and in Rosh according to Beit Yosef's explanation — and how did they omit it; and further, below siman 120 regarding a cup of a non-Jew that became mixed, these laws too are written, and there it is not applicable that two people should not benefit at once — for it is impossible that they can be careful at every meal that each one not use his cup at the time of his meal when his fellow also eats, and likewise the ring that he mentioned there — rather they certainly did not say any issur on this. And further proof from two inspected houses on the eve of Pesach that Tur brings in Orach Chayyim siman 439 — when they come to ask about this one after the other, both of them do not require inspection — and behold at one time on Pesach each one benefits from his house; and you might say that here we compare it to when they come to ask at once, where both require inspection — if so, let us say to you that it would be forbidden here for two people to benefit from all of them, even not at one time, as there with houses that we consider them as one when they come to ask at once; and explicitly Tosafot wrote in chapter 2 of Kesubos (daf 26) that if you permit all the priestesses it is like one house — rather, until here, here they did not decree on two people at all in any manner — so it appears to my humble opinion for the halachah; but I have no power to be lenient in this, since the poskim have already ruled to forbid:
