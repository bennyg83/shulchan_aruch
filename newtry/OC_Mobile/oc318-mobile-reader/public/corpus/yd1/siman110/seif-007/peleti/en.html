"And one of them ate, etc." — Terumat HaDeshen's view is that if one ate it b'shogeg, it is not applicable to decree shogeg on account of meizid, for how will he eat issur. And Shach and Taz challenged that we decree lest he feed it to a non-Jew and to a dog — and this is not so great a difficulty, for the explanation of shogeg on account of meizid is that he will do the matter b'meizid and say "I am shogeg" — throw into the sea b'meizid and say "I am shogeg"; if so, here regarding eating how can we say he will eat intentionally and say shogeg — are we suspected that he will eat issur b'meizid? And if he feeds a non-Jew b'meizid and says "I am shogeg" — this is truly permitted, for it is nevelah; but regarding the food that he eats b'shogeg there is no concern that he will be meizid and say shogeg — end of his words. But this that the mishnah teaches "one who cooks on Shabbos" — dispute of R' Meir and R' Yehudah whether we decree shogeg on account of meizid — I did not understand according to Terumat HaDeshen's words; behold he does not come to cook on Shabbos b'meizid; and just as we are not concerned he will eat issur, how is it possible to be concerned he will do a primary labor on Shabbos to cook by hand. And the entire sugya we heard in Gittin revolves around this. And one must say that certainly if the normal law is to be concerned and to penalize shogeg on account of meizid in every manner — we are concerned even that he will perform a transgression by hand, whether light or severe; and the Gemara's sugya follows correctly. But we rule regarding cooking issur that we do not decree shogeg on account of meizid — only Beit Yosef wrote regarding falling to the sea we are more concerned and decree shogeg on account of meizid; and on this is Terumat HaDeshen's view: since the normal law is not to decree at all, only we are stringent where we are stringent when there is concern that nevertheless he will keep the Torah's statute; but here if he transgresses b'meizid he will eat issur so much — we do not act to decree shogeg on account of meizid; and nevertheless there is reason to be concerned for the words of the acharonim to forbid, for this is a mere stringency — requires study in any way.
"One of them, etc." — It appears that if one cooked in a vessel and it was eaten and the vessels still exist, it is forbidden — for behold we come to judge the nature of the vessels, whether they require hagalah in a rinsing vessel and earthenware breaking that the issur was cooked in it; if so, behold we judge upon it and the matter returned to safek and everything is included in the safek — and this is obvious. And if it is difficult — this that the Gemara challenged in chapter HaTaarovot on Rav who said: a ring of avodah zarah that became mixed in a hundred rings and one fell to the sea — all are permitted; and the Gemara challenged: all sacrifices that became mixed in a sin offering of those to die and a stoned ox — all die; why should all die, etc.; and the difficulty is: what is the difficulty? "All are permitted, etc." — Here is a place that requires study, and therefore I was compelled to expand somewhat; and we already wrote in chapter HaTaarovot: Rav said — a ring of avodah zarah that became mixed in a hundred rings and one of them fell to the sea — all are permitted; and the Gemara challenged: all sacrifices that became mixed, etc. — all die; why should all die? Since one of them died all are permitted. The Gemara answers: this is because he says like R' Eliezer who says: if the head of one of them was offered, all the heads are offered; and the mishnah is like the Sages who forbid even if only one remained — it goes out to the house of burning; and the commentators challenged, and likewise Raavad on Rambam who explains like Rav, and likewise Shulchan Aruch that one of them fell to the sea — all are permitted; and he ruled stam mishnah "all sacrifices," etc. — all die; and there is a great difficulty on Rambam and Shulchan Aruch how they ruled explicitly against stam mishnah; and likewise acharonim below who ruled unlike R' Eliezer that it is a lone teaching — requires study. And behold I expanded in my Chiddushim and Alkut briefly, and I will write that Maggid Mishneh wrote in the name of R' Yehudah Corcos: specifically fell — but if one threw it by hand it is forbidden; and here it says "they die" — that is, by hand; therefore all die; and one should not rely on this one that died — and accordingly there is no place for the Gemara's difficulty. And the gaon Maharash Kodnover challenged this difficulty, and it appears that in Sanhedrin chapter HaNechnakim on the mishnah "A murderer," etc. — R' Elazar establishes the mishnah regarding an ox whose judgment was not completed that became mixed with other oxen whose judgments were completed — all are exempt, for they do not complete the judgment of the ox except in the presence of the ox; and Rava establishes regarding an ox that killed that became mixed with ordinary oxen — they stone all. What he wrote — and accordingly for R' Elazar, even if it became mixed with whole oxen — nevertheless it is exempt, for they complete its judgment not in court; if so, all the more so one ox standing for stoning that became mixed with other whole oxen — they do not wait for it; if so, one must say this that the mishnah teaches "the stoned ox — they die" — that is, they wait until it dies, for in court it is impossible, for they do not complete, etc. But for Rava who says an ox that killed that became mixed with whole oxen — they kill it — all the more so kodshim that became mixed — they stone it — and it is obvious. And behold Rav's statement regarding the ring of avodah zarah, etc., and R' Elazar's statement in Zevachim regarding barrels of terumah that became mixed and one fell to the sea — all are permitted; if so, just as the difficulty for Rav regarding the ring, so the difficulty for R' Elazar regarding the barrels — and obvious; and if so there is no room to amend Rashi's version that "they die" is by hand — for the difficulty is for R' Elazar too; and according to him it is impossible to say so, for R' Elazar holds unlike Rav — only "they die" means they wait until they die as explained in what I wrote; if so, even though for Rav it fits, the difficulty is for R' Elazar who also said Rav's law — but according to Rav, in the mishnah the explanation of "they die" is in court; therefore Rambam wrote that he ruled in Laws of Nizkhei Mamon: an ox whose judgment was completed that became mixed with whole oxen — they stone it; if so, that it appears by hand — requires study, as Rashi wrote — and there is no difficulty here. However it appears that Maharshak followed Rashi in Sanhedrin that they stone all because they are forbidden in benefit — and what difference does it make that they stone to fulfill "and you shall burn the evil from your midst" — and accordingly if like the Gemara's difficulty for Rav, certainly one should not kill them by hand, for there is benefit in this: if they do not die by hand and one dies by itself all are permitted — and you have no greater benefit than this; if so the Gemara's difficulty fits well: why should they die by hand — with this all die; they should not die, only leave them to die by themselves and then all are permitted — end of his words. And behold at first glance there is a difficulty on this that the Gemara answers this difficulty of Rav who says like R' Eliezer that it is a lone teaching — let us say the mishnah is R' Yehudah who holds there in the sugya regarding the ring that even double safek is forbidden; if so this law of Rav regarding falling does not exist, for the entire reason as Tosafot wrote is because of double safek — perhaps the issur fell, and if you say the heter — perhaps this is not the issur; and accordingly for R' Yochanan who forbids double safek, this too is forbidden — if so establish the mishnah as R' Yochanan; and further that according to Beit HaZahav's view that they die automatically and this is in his hand, and before one of them died all are forbidden — let us say the mishnah is like a tanna who holds important items are set aside; if so before one died all were forbidden as it was set aside and does not return; and it appears one must say that Rava who says here this resolved is R' Eliezer, and Rava establishes the mishnah in chapter These Are Strangled — it is deficient and lacking, and thus it teaches: an ox whose judgment was completed among other whole oxen — they stone it; R' Yehudah says they gather into a booth; if so also this difficulty that the Gemara challenges on the mishnah in Zevachim is difficult too on the mishnah in Sanhedrin — why should they stone all since one of them was stoned — behold all are permitted; and why need there be setting aside regarding kodshim — for it deals with hullin there; and what setting aside there — and why R' Yochanan who is stringent on double safek — behold it teaches R' Yehudah says they gather into a booth; and since the end is R' Yehudah, the beginning is not R' Yochanan; therefore Rava resolved it as R' Eliezer and rabbanan disagree, and every difficulty is resolved in one thrust. Therefore it appears Rabbeinu Tam holds that there is a basis there in those oxen — see there; and according to him there is no difficulty on that in Sanhedrin from one falling to the sea; if so the difficulty is in its place; therefore Rabbeinu Tam explains that even for R' Yochanan double safek is permitted — only regarding pomegranates in the granary they were stringent, and there is no difficulty at all — end of his words. In another way one can say: Tosafot infer "limbs of unblemished in blemished," etc., and Tosafot ask why they are not nullified; and if ascending is not nullified — behold it does not ascend; and also just as for R' Yochanan who says species in its species is not nullified — specifically wet in wet — likewise according to rabbanan. And further one can say in a plain way — that according to what Tosafot and authors bring regarding one of them falling to the sea that all are permitted — nevertheless one may not eat them individually, for there remains only one safek; and at first glance it is difficult: what did the Gemara challenge Rav from the mishnah that teaches "they die" — behold in that difficulty "let us suppress them since they move" — let us say whatever separates from the majority separates; the Gemara answered: lest ten kohanim come and take it individually; and the Gemara challenged in Megisshei De'asa, etc., and Rashi explained: since they were already nullified how will they return to forbidden status; and accordingly here where one of them died upon whom the issur was suspended — in this there is room to say that kohanim will offer individually as explained in the Gemara; and is Megisshei De'asa not applicable — what of it that it is permitted, nevertheless to eat individually is forbidden — and behold here it will be offered on the altar individually and it is obvious it is forbidden — and there is no place for the Gemara's difficulty. "Two by two." — In the mishnah we say: limbs of blemished in limbs of unblemished — if the head of one of them was offered, all the heads are offered; and the Sages say: even if all were offered except one — it goes out to the house of burning; and we say in the Gemara: R' Eliezer permitted only two by two; and the Gemara challenged: the Sages say even if all were not offered except one, etc.; and the Gemara answers: what is "one"? One pair — end of his words; and Rashi explains: it is obvious that one must offer or eat the remainder two by two, for in one there is certainly heter, but one is forbidden; the Gemara challenged well: what are the Sages saying — even one? Behold with one R' Eliezer also forbids; and it answers: one pair — R' Eliezer permits; and Rambam explains: R' Eliezer permitted only two that must be offered; and likewise falling to the sea — two, for their falling is recognizable; and they challenged him: if so what did the Gemara challenge from the Sages — even if only one remained, etc. — for it appears according to R' Eliezer it is permitted; it is obvious it is permitted — R' Eliezer requires only that the falling be two to recognize their falling, but in the remainder there is no difference even for one; and one could answer: Ra'am — the Gemara's difficulty: "one" stated in R' Eliezer's words and "one" stated in the Sages' words — each has one meaning; if so if you explain this that R' Eliezer says "if the head of one was offered" — meaning one pair — if so even "one" stated by the Sages, even if only one remained — likewise its meaning is one pair; and the difficulty is: why — behold even if the remainder is only one literally it goes out to burning according to the Sages; and Ra'ah explained well the Gemara's difficulty; but in the Gemara's answer that it answers "one pair" — it is not resolved, for it itself is difficult: why need a remainder of a pair — even one, all the more so a pair.
