"Of itself." Even after the mixture became known, or if it fell unintentionally before the mixtures became known — we suspend, etc., because by Torah law it is already nullified in the majority; and specifically if it is in a matter that is lost from the world; but if it fell to a place where it can be found, the rest were not permitted. And if it was cut or crushed, its law was explained in siman 101, seif 7, see there. And regarding that which the Mechaber permitted if eaten unintentionally — the Shach and Taz disagree and hold that we penalize the unintentional like the intentional, and there is permission only specifically if eaten of itself by a dog. And the Shach wrote further: and it appears specifically if it was eaten or one of them fell before it separated from them; but if it separated from them before us, or separated by itself — for the one who forbids separated-by-itself — since it became forbidden, also the mixtures, if it fell afterward to nullification, remain in their issur.
"One." And we say: since from one association he is also not of forbidden substance — and from this it appears that if two became mixed, one must eat four; and likewise three — one must eat six, six; investigate; end of his words, Shach.
"**One person.**" And even though in a piece not worthy of honor there are those who permit one person to eat it — above, end of siman 101 — that case is different, for there, since it is nullified by Torah law and also d'rabbanan in the majority without double safek; unlike here, where it is permitted only for the reason of double safek, for we say: perhaps the issur fell; and if you say the issur did not fall — perhaps he will not eat now — the issur, Shach.
"**Two.**" And the Shach and Taz contradicted the Rav's words here with several proofs; and the Taz concluded, and these are his words: but necessarily here they did not decree on two people at all in any manner — so it appears correct in my humble opinion for the halachah; but I have no authority to be lenient here, since the authors of the Shulchan Aruch already ruled to forbid; end of his words.
