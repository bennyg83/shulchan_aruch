7) However, the enactment that one may sell the whole thing to a non-Jew except for the monetary value of the forbidden portion within it is stated regarding all Torah prohibitions, even when the prohibition was not nullified by Torah law — provided there is no concern that the non-Jew will return to sell it to a Jew. And such as bread baked on asherah wood and the like — one may break it into pieces and sell it thus to a non-Jew, since it is not the way of a Jew to buy it thus from a gentile. Peri Chadash ibid. Lechem HaPanim ibid. Shulchan Panim ibid, who wrote that even when it is not nullified one in one — for if a non-Jew wove a garment or baked bread, it is effective to sell it except for the monetary value of the forbidden portion within it.
