"An item," etc. From what R' Levi [says] in Zevachim there — and even for eating it is permitted, from what is written there (a barrel of terumah, etc.) — but figs, etc. The Ran ruled, and the Rashba ruled like Rav Yosef — not like the Rambam (who ruled like Rabba even though in the case of the ring he ruled like R' Nachman. He holds that specifically regarding eating we require that its falling be discernible. See in the Ramban and the Ran, chapter 5 of Avodah Zarah, and in Terumat HaDeshen), because the Yerushalmi in chapter 4 of Terumot [follows] Rav Yosef, as written: "There were before him twenty figs, and one fell and one of them was lost" — Resh Lakish says, their doubt is nullified in the majority, and R' Levi [rules] as Tosafot wrote in Zevachim 73b, s.v. rather — since by Torah law it is nullified in the majority, in a case of doubt they establish it by Torah law. Therefore he did not distinguish in the Shulchan Aruch between whether its falling is discernible or not. And even though R' Yochanan argues with R' Levi in the Yerushalmi there, since the Gemara brought only the words of R' Levi, it appears that such is the halachah.
And a davar she'yesh lo matirin. The Rashba, and in seif 8 in the hagahah that there are those who disagree.
And if it was eaten, etc. So too the Rashba and the Rosh — [when] it fell into the sea, some say [it is permitted] only when it is no longer in the world. And ostensibly it appears to mean specifically when it was eaten on its own, similar to [when] it fell, as written below. But in Turei Even and Hagahot Devarim they wrote that even if it was eaten unintentionally — whether by him or by another Jew — it is permitted, for there is no reason to decree [against] one who acts unintentionally out of concern for one who acts intentionally, since in his mind the prohibition is that it be eaten, and how would he feed it to a fellow Jew? And as Tosafot wrote, that for a prohibition that is discernible in a fixed item we do not decree regarding separating — as noted. And this is what he means by "unintentionally." And he wrote in the hagahah "whether," etc., and in Turei Zahav and Shach.
That it fell on its own. Tosafot there — but if he threw it down, even unintentionally it is forbidden, for we penalize one who acts unintentionally out of concern for one who acts intentionally — and this requires study, for we rule like R' Yehudah in chapter Nezikin (53) [and] 54, that by Torah law we penalize, as in one who cooks on Shabbos, as written in Orach Chaim at the beginning of siman 318, and for rabbinic [matters] we do not penalize, as written, and like the unattributed mishnah there "one who imparts impurity," etc., and so wrote the Rambam; and there "they fell and split," etc., and we rule like R' Yosi and R' Shimon, as written above in seif 2 and in siman 101, seif 6, and as written in the Yerushalmi of Orlah chapter 3, and Tosafot brought it in Gittin there. Nevertheless, R' Yosi holds like R' Abahu in the name of R' Yochanan: all prohibitions whose violation renders one liable to bring a sin offering — unintentionally [it is] permitted, intentionally forbidden — and we rule like R' Yochanan, as written in siman 99, seif 5. But Tosafot hold that the halachah follows R' Meir there, that by Torah law we do not penalize and for rabbinic [matters] we do penalize, from what is written in Shabbos 38a, "the next day he went out and taught them, 'One who cooks,'" etc., and he did not distinguish — and we rule like Rabbenu Yonah there, and Tosafot ruled for this reason (and also because Rav Arai agrees with him in Chullin there) like R' Meir in Chullin 15a, s.v. mori, etc., and likewise the passage in Sanhedrin 5a, that he must pay even without handing over — and likewise for R' Sheshet in chapter 4 there one must say that we penalize unintentional [acts] out of concern for intentional [acts], according to Tosafot's explanation in Gittin 53a, s.v. shelo, etc. (but according to Tosafot in Bava Kamma 100a, s.v. time'a, and in Sanhedrin 33b there is no proof from this) — and all the more so in that case of "they fell and split," where even R' Yehudah in his own dispute concedes to him that it comes to circumvent [the law], and likewise here. But we, who rule like R' Shimon and R' Yosi — the same applies here. And that which it says "it fell" — some say that here too it is speaking of "they fell," etc., whether unintentionally, etc., and likewise at the end of chapter 5 of Terumot, and afterward "they fell" there in Chullin, "if unintentionally," etc., and so wrote the Rashba — that likewise throwing [it] down unintentionally is like any one who nullifies a prohibition, etc. (and see in Orach Chaim siman 318).
Regarding [separating to] a known place. From what is written there, "they separated forty," etc., "one of them separated to a myriad," etc., and therefore it says "the Great Sea," as noted.
And specifically [when two fell]. According to Rashi there, and in siman 110 he also wrote the words of the Rambam that two fell — and he did not write here that the Rambam too wrote only regarding idolatry. And according to the first explanation of the Kesef Mishneh, that specifically for idolatry and consecrated items we require two — for according to the second explanation, because its falling is not discernible, the Rambam had no need to decide that a fig is not [such a case] at all; rather, we require two. And likewise in the Shulchan Aruch here he should have distinguished — and the approach of the Gemara does not appear thus.
For there is no alternative, etc. Rashi there.
And even to eat them all at once, etc. And so too in the Shulchan Aruch, siman 110 — and these are the words of the Tur here and there, and these are the words of the Rosh in chapter gid hanasheh, siman 34, and Peri Yehoshua that it is forbidden, etc. And Tosafot in Zevachim 74b, s.v. veAbba, etc., "and to derive benefit," etc. And the Beit Yosef challenged — but from their words it appears to mean specifically [when eating] all at once it is forbidden. And one can explain that the Tur explains that they are dealing with two people, and this is what he means by "and even two," etc. And all the later authorities already challenged [this], for the language of Tosafot and the Rosh does not appear thus — for Tosafot there wrote, as I explained above, and that is in s.v. "he separated," etc., "if it helps," etc., which shows that it deals with one person; and behold the Rashba, who holds regarding dry with dry that the prohibition remains in existence and it is certainly there, and nevertheless permits one person to eat them; and even Tosafot, chapter gid hanasheh 100a, s.v. beriah, was uncertain in the matter, and nevertheless concedes here. And siman 109. And proof from what is written there in Zevachim — that he says like R' Eliezer, and behold eating from the altar is like one person; rather, specifically for one person to eat them as one it is forbidden. And likewise that which is written in seif 8 in the hagahah, and what is written there, etc., is also all at once. And as Tosafot wrote there, "if it helps," etc., and likewise one can explain the words of the Tur and Shach.
