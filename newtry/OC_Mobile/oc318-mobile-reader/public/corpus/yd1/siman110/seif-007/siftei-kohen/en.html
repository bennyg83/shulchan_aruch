And something that has a permitted aspect. See below, s.k. 56 and 57:
And it was eaten, etc. — language of the Tur; and R' Yehuda explained: specifically when it fell by itself; but if one dropped it, even b'shogeg, it is forbidden lest intentionally (and R' Yehuda's words are in Tosafot, Zevachim, beginning of daf 74, and above, siman 99, s.k. 15). And our master the Rosh, may his memory be blessed, wrote that if they dropped it before the mixture became known it is permitted, for it is not applicable to decree, since the mixture was not yet known. And the Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Mahariv in Hagahot Shaarei Dura and Terumat HaDeshen (siman 175) that even according to R' Yehuda, if they ate or fed b'shogeg to a member of the covenant, the rest are permitted and we do not penalize in such a case lest intentionally — meaning, lest one feed one of them intentionally in order to permit the others and hang the matter that the forbidden item will be eaten. If so, according to his view, he would eat the forbidden item certainly; but if he fed b'shogeg to a dog or a non-Jew we penalize. And according to this the Mechaber wrote 'and it was eaten b'shogeg'; and regarding 'dropped' he was precise to write 'and it fell by itself.' But in my humble opinion Mahariv's words are not necessary, for it appears that although there is no decree lest one feed intentionally, nevertheless if it was eaten it is certainly permitted — all the more so if it fell to the sea. If so, we are concerned that since we permit when eaten b'shogeg, they will drop intentionally to the sea. And furthermore, since it is permitted for him to eat, we are concerned lest he feed a non-Jew or a dog to permit the others. And also in the responsum of the Rosh, general rule 20, siman 17, it appears explicitly that even if eaten we penalize b'shogeg lest intentionally, for he wrote there regarding an incident where the community took sheep and afterward it became known that one sheep there was treifah — that if one of the sheep was eaten, all were permitted; and even though R' Yehuda explained specifically when it fell, but if dropped b'shogeg we decree lest intentionally — since it was eaten before the doubt became known, it is not applicable to penalize, end of his words. Unless you say it deals with when eaten by a non-Jew — and this is not implied in the responsum there, see there:
