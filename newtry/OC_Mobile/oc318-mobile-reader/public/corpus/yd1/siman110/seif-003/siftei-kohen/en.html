Nine slaughterhouses, etc. The Raah wrote in Bedek HaBayit, daf 120, end of 1a, that the same applies if there were ten slaughterhouses in the city, all presumed to sell slaughtered meat, and one bought from one of them and does not know from which — and afterward it became clear to us that one of these slaughterhouses sells nevelah meat, which is forbidden. It is not comparable to separated automatically, for there are no offspring of safek from itself, but from the clarification of the slaughterhouses that became clear now that one of them is forbidden — and from that a safek arose at that hour when it was separated automatically in the buyer's hand from them. If so, this safek is not new but merely revelation of a matter: at first it was hidden from him that he thought they were all permitted, and when it became clear that it is forbidden, the matter was revealed retroactively that at the hour of purchase of this piece, when it separated from them at that hour, there was its safek — and this is clear, end of his words. And the Rashba too in Mishmeret HaBayit there agrees with him in this, except that he disagrees there and explained his words in Torat HaBayit that the Mechaber copied in siman 105: specifically when safek treifah arose in slaughterhouses — such as when a treifah was mixed among the permitted in a piece worthy of honor, or even certainly a treifah that was not known until after he bought, and there is safek whether the certainly treifah was in this slaughterhouse from which he bought — but nevertheless he knows from which slaughterhouse he took; it is found the safek is not from clarification of slaughterhouses but that he does not know if he took from the permitted or from the treifah. If so, since he took before the treifah was found, we do not follow that hour but the present, when it is separated automatically, and it is permitted — so is the view of the Rashba in Mishmeret HaBayit there, see there, and investigate. But the Ran wrote in Piskei HaGahot on kevua of nine slaughterhouses, and these are his words: Tosafot wrote that it is not called kevua except when the prohibition was known before he bought; but if the prohibition was not known at the hour he bought, even though after he bought the prohibition was revealed and it became kevua, we do not say it should be kevua retroactively. And there is a practical difference for one who bought meat from the slaughterhouses and afterward it became known that there was treifah among them — it is permitted; for since at the hour he bought it was not kevua, for the law of kevua that it should be like half-and-half is a novelty, and you have in it only from its hour of novelty and onward — meaning, from when it became kevua, but not retroactively, end of his words. And so according to what the Bach and the later authorities explained the Tur's words — for he holds: if it is known that there is treifah in one slaughterhouse and it is not known in which, and he bought from one of them and it is known from which he bought — it is kevua, since there is no mixture in the pieces but each piece stands by itself and the mixture is in the slaughterhouse — if so, it is proven that the Tur holds like the Ran, for he wrote on this; and so our master the Rosh, may his memory be blessed, that all that they took before safek, all is permitted, and after the doubt arose all is forbidden, end of his words. But in truth it does not appear so in the responsum of the Rosh, general rule 30, siman 17, for it deals there explicitly with when the pieces were mixed one into another — that even though they are a piece worthy of honor, it is permitted when they took before safek, from the reason that we do not decree lest one take from the kevua, since then they all stood in a presumption of permissibility, and separated automatically is preferable — but not in safek of slaughterhouses; and also the Tur's words apparently do not appear so; and the Beit Yosef understood them that it deals with mixtures of pieces — and so in Derishah, siman 19, in the name of the Beit Yosef, see there:
That every kevua is like half-and-half, etc. So we derive in Bava Kamma (daf 44b) and in the first chapter of Kesubos (daf 15a) from the verse \"and he rose\" — excluding one who throws a stone at a group of people where nine Israelites and one non-Jew stood: he is exempt because of a doubt of life-and-death, to be lenient; for even though the majority are Israelites, nevertheless it is kevua and is considered like half-and-half. And know that what we rule — that every kevua is like half-and-half — applies both to be lenient and to be stringent; such as nine slaughterhouses selling nevelah meat and one selling slaughtered meat, and one bought and does not know from which he bought — it is like half-and-half and is forbidden only because of safek, and we do not say it is like the majority.
