"For all that is kevua, etc." — They proved from this mishnah that majority and proximity — we follow the majority; for if not, in "found" why do we follow majority? Let us see if it is close to the store selling treifah or to the others — rather learn we follow majority. One must investigate regarding one who bought from the fixed: it is forbidden if he stands with its meat in his hand near the store selling slaughtered meat — if we do not permit — for proximity is d'oraisa and majority is d'oraisa, only majority is preferable to proximity; and in kevua where there is no majority — if so we return to proximity, for proximity is d'oraisa, and he bought from the close one; and from the mishnah there is no proof — for it teaches "its safek we are stringent" — it deals where there is no proximity and it was found near the place close to all three. However from the Gemara in Nazir chapter 2 there is some proof — for it teaches there: one who says to his agent, "Go and betroth a wife for me" — he is forbidden to all women in the world; and we say the reason is it is kevua, for it returns to fixedness — see there; and it is difficult: that city where the agent dwells — should the women be forbidden and the other permitted? Behold they are closer to the agent than other women in other towns — and we follow proximity, for proximity is d'oraisa; and all the more so since the Torah taught us that kevua overrides majority and majority is preferable to proximity — even proximity it overrides and we do not follow proximity at all, and nothing emerges from safek; investigate.
"If found in a gentile's hand." — One must understand according to what most authors wrote the reason: in a place where they do not announce, it is forbidden to buy from the gentile and we do not say let us follow majority — for certainly they were kosher; if so if treifot happen to a Jew he will not be able to sell to a gentile lest the gentile sell it to a Jew; therefore it is a regulation not to buy from a gentile; and correspondingly a Jew may sell to him — see there. And the difficulty: this "found in a gentile's hand in nine stores," etc. — how does it deal? If in a place where they do not announce — how does it teach those after majority and permitted? For in a place where they do not announce we do not follow majority — and all the more so a place where they announce and they announced that we do not follow majority for the reason the commentators wrote; and how does it teach permitted? And some say: they did not announce — are we dealing with wicked people? For in truth one sells nevelah meat and there are treifot in the slaughterhouses — and how is it possible they did not announce? And if they announced — behold it is forbidden to buy from a gentile; and so as Ran wrote: therefore in a place where they do not announce it is forbidden to buy from a gentile and we do not follow majority, for we are concerned the gentile buys from treifah meat that they reduce the price for him from kosher meat; and it is difficult: if so nine stores and found in a gentile's hand — why do we permit? Let us be concerned he bought from the store selling treifah meat that is reduced for him, as Ran wrote; and truly Ra'ah's view in Bedek HaBayit (see Pri Chadash s.k. 13): if he bought and does not know from which store he bought — only at the time of purchase he did not know that any store was presumed treifah, and after purchase it became clear in one store treifah — behold this is kevua and forbidden even though he bought before the safek became known; and at first glance his words have no proof — whence to distinguish in his thin reasoning without proof and to answer Rashba; but according to the above it is resolved: he challenged all the above and resolved that found in a gentile's hand — the gentile bought it before he knew there are treifot in the slaughterhouses at all; if so it is permitted to buy from him, for we follow majority and presumably he bought kosher meat; and why then should it be forbidden for a Jew to sell to a gentile as above, or as reduced for him as Ran wrote — for at the time of sale he did not know there are treifot here at all; therefore permitted — and requires study. (And with this is also resolved what I established in Kereti letter 11: and is a store selling treifah permitted to sell treifah to a Jew without telling him explicitly "this is treifah," etc. — for accordingly in the three sales he did not know and erred and it was hidden.) Nevertheless it teaches at the beginning: he bought, etc. — safek forbidden — behold Ra'ah's law is forced that in such a case he explains: even though it was not known then the safek. And according to Rashba one must say in a settlement where that store sells treifah — it all deals with gentiles — that is, nine gentiles sell kosher and the tenth store too is a gentile and they do not announce at all — nevertheless meat found in a gentile's hand is permitted, for we say he buys from the majority; and here that concern does not apply — for accordingly a Jew would be permitted to sell then at reduced price as above — and requires study.
