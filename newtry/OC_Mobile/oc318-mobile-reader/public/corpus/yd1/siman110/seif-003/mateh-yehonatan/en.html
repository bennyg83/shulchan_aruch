(There, seif 2) And specifically small live animals, etc. In the Shach, s.k. 13; and one may say in the Beit Yosef, siman 101, s.k. 26 — and it would appear according to Shach's view that since from the outset it is a live animal and is never nullified, if so afterward it will be worthy of honor even though it is not now worthy of honor because it lacks action, nevertheless it is never nullified. And one may say a proof for this view from that which is in chapter HaTaarovot, daf 73 — he challenged: "and we suppress them, since they move, lest 'whatever separates, the majority separates'" — and he answered: a decree lest ten priests come at once and offer. One said to him: this is d'rabbanan; "if so, from the body itself it should be forbidden" — except: lest ten priests come at once and take, etc. Behold, at first glance what does he mean at the outset, "lest they come," etc. — while it is still alive there is no concern at all according to what we now hold, and there is concern only after death; and after death, why should it not be nullified? Until here one must say: since it was already a live animal and after flaying and butchering it becomes a piece worthy of honor, never nullified — as I wrote.
