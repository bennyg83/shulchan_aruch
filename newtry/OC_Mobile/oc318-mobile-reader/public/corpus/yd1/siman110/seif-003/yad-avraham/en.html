(There in Taz s.k. 5) "You might say that in butcher shops where many frequent we decree more stringently — but we learned the opposite in chapter Chavis (147a): but ten people, etc." Rashi: since they are numerous they remind one another. See Nekudot HaKesef, who challenged that there is different — there the ten people are in one gathering, therefore we say they remind one another; but here we fear they will come to buy afterward. It appears this distinction is compelled from Eruvin (3a) regarding part of a beam within twenty and part above twenty, etc.: Rabah said an alleyway is invalid; Rava of Parzinka explained — an alleyway belongs to the public, they lean on one another and do not remind. This challenges the mishnah in Chavis that with many they remind one another. However, per Nekudot HaKesef's distinction whether the many are in one gathering or not — one can also answer from Eruvin: there too sometimes there are not many in the alleyway and an individual comes to use it at that hour and there is no reminder; nevertheless there is no difficulty on Taz from there. This distinction applies only to the latter wording; but in the first wording there Rabah said "the public reminds" — in every case we say the public reminds one another. So too is the main point in his chiddushim on Eruvin (18a), and there is support for Taz's view.
