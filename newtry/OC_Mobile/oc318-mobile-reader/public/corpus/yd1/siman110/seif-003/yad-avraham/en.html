(Siman 110 in the Shulchan Aruch, seif 3) Nine shops, etc. — behold this is forbidden, for every kavu'a is like half and half. The Shach wrote that there is another d'oraisa kavu'a: for example where in one shop there is a tereifah and he does not know in which shop, and he took from one of them and knows from which — it is a d'oraisa kavu'a. And this is learned from Darkei Moshe, who so explained the words of the Tur in the words of the Rashba. But accordingly, the Rashba's words here would contradict his words in his novellae on the chapter "HaMitkabel," and also the Tur's words here would contradict his words in Even HaEzer siman 35, according to what Maggid Mishneh wrote in chapter 9 of Hilchot Ishut, that he holds that such a case is not called kavu'a, as Tosafot wrote in Nazir (daf 12) s.v. "asur," and in the first chapter of Bava Metzia (daf 6), and this is their language: and kavu'a is applicable only regarding something where issur and heter are sold and known, and he does not know from which he took — for example nine shops. And what is written in the book Chavat Da'at from Rashi's explanation in Gittin (54b) — one can say that it is not for the reason of kavu'a, but because the issur is separate and not fit to be mixed, as the Rambam wrote in chapter 9 of Terumot, and Tosafot Yom Tov there, and in the Rosh, chapter 3 of Shevi'it. And so Minchat Yaakov wrote in klal 73 in the name of the book Keritut, that it is not called kavu'a unless the issur is recognizable and known in its place. And so it is clearly implied in the book Eliyah Rabbah in Orach Chayyim siman 698, and in responsa Mutzal Me'Esh siman 50. And there is a proof from chapter 3 of Berachot regarding skipping — that is, over coffins — it is proven, etc., that most have an opening of a tefach and a minority do not have an opening of a tefach; and there the coffins are fixed in their place, and we do not say every kavu'a is like half and half; rather it is proven that we require that the issur be recognizable and known. However, in the book Beit Efraim he wrote in the name of the Rokeach that there it is because there is a doubt whether there is a coffin that has no opening of a tefach, and from doubt we do not say kavu'a. And see in Avodah Zarah (53b) that it is implied that asheirah trees were not nullified in the majority, even those that are not recognizable; however, idolatry is different, for it has no nullification forever, even when not kavu'a. In the book Sha'ar HaMelech, Hilchot Megillah, he wrote in the name of the book Lashon Limudim that we do not say kavu'a is like half and half except where he took or separated before us from the kavu'a; but where it did not leave the kavu'a — for example towns that stand on their own — we follow after the majority, as the Ran wrote there regarding reading the Megillah. But from the words of the book Keritut it is implied that such a case is considered kavu'a — for example when there is a field that is definitely forbidden for plowing and sowing, and a doubt arose regarding other fields; and he raised a difficulty against him from the chapter "Kol HaTzelamim": that if there is one place where they definitely worship, and in other places there is a doubt, they are not forbidden.
(There, seif 3) But meat found in the market, etc., and even if it was found among the shops, it is [treated as] having separated. So Tosafot wrote in the first chapter of Ketubot; and specifically when one shop is recognizable; but where it is known that there is a tereifah in one shop and it is not known in which, if it separated it is forbidden according to the Rosh, who forbids separating of its own accord, for one can decree lest one take from the kavu'a, since the issur is not known. And it appears that if there is a d'oraisa sixty in one shop and it is not known in which, and it separated of its own accord, it is permitted according to all, as is proven in the Gemara of Nazir regarding one who says to his agent, "Go and betroth a woman for me," and he died, that he is forbidden with all women; and it challenges: if we found [him] in the market and he betrothed, let us say whatever separates [is treated as] from the majority it separated. And know that Minchat Yaakov permitted in a case of great financial loss if it separated of its own accord regarding important things. And regarding whether we are lenient in a doubt of a gezeirat hakatuv — the Ramban wrote, brought in Chiddushei HaRan in the first chapter of Megillah, that it is like a doubt of Sofeirim. But in Turei Even he wrote that we are stringent in its doubt, and there is a proof from Tum'at Beit HaMenuga that his twilight is forbidden. There is a proof from that which is said in Megillah: "you were a minor, and we do not bring a proof from a minor"; and if a gezeirat hakatuv is like a doubt of Sofeirim — yet regarding d'rabbanan an adult is believed to testify what he saw in his minority. But from the words of Kesef Mishneh, chapter 6 of Hilchot Avot HaTumah, regarding tumah of idolatry, that the Rambam explained to be lenient, and there it is a gezeirat hakatuv. And see Tosafot Sanhedrin (12). And ostensibly there is a proof for the opinion of the Rambam that a doubt of a gezeirat hakatuv from the Torah is to be treated leniently, from Pesachim (83b) and Chullin (91) regarding [items that] were recognized and afterwards mixed: for if they were forbidden from the Torah they would not require burning; however, we follow in every direction to be stringent; and so it is proven that one in one — the issur was not established; for if not, it would be forbidden from the Torah, and why does it require burning; however, one may say that just as the issur was established regarding the issur of eating, it was also established regarding burning. And what the book Atzei Levonah wrote as a proof for the Rambam from Tosafot's difficulty in Shabbos (daf 130) — why did the verse need to forbid a doubtful milah on Shabbos — one can say that Shabbos is different, for there is in it a Torah prohibition — and examine carefully. Further there in se'if katan 14 he left in further study Rashi's explanation in Zevachim, who raises a difficulty according to R' Eliezer and according to R' Nachman from the Mishnah of a shor ha-niskal that was mixed, etc., they should die — why? Let us say the issur dies and the rest be offered. What is the difficulty — for one must offer two at once, and how can one slaughter two zevachim at once — for we say "you shall slaughter it," that one not slaughter two zevachim. And one may say: since it is impossible otherwise that they not die and kodashim not come to loss, it is like after the fact.
