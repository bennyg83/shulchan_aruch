Except that nowadays the custom is to prohibit bathing throughout all thirty days. Or Zarua explains that the custom arose from concern that one who bathes may comb his hair, which is forbidden throughout the thirty days. According to our practice, however, which permits combing as stated in siman 390, bathing should also be permitted. The Maharshal explains instead that bathing was prohibited because one entering a bathhouse commonly cuts the hair of his head. One should not ask why bathing is not likewise prohibited on Chol HaMoed, when haircutting is forbidden. On Chol HaMoed the prohibition applies to everyone, and a multitude reminds one another. Mourning restrictions apply to an individual, who may forget his mourning and cut his hair like everyone else. This distinction appears in Shabbat 146, where the mishnah permits ten people to act together, and Rashi explains that because they are numerous they remind one another.
