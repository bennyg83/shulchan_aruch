Except that nowadays the custom is to prohibit washing for all thirty days. Or Zarua explains the custom as a concern that one may come to comb the hair, which is forbidden during thirty days. According to our practice, where combing is permitted as explained in siman 390, that reason would no longer apply. Maharshal gives another reason: people who enter the bathhouse commonly cut their hair, so washing is prohibited because it may lead to haircutting. One might ask that during Chol HaMoed haircutting is also prohibited, yet bathing is permitted. The distinction is that on Chol HaMoed the prohibition applies to everyone, so people remind one another and the risk of forgetfulness is small. Mourning restrictions apply to the individual mourner, so there is greater concern he will forget and act like everyone else. Shabbos 146 provides a similar principle that where many people are involved they remind one another.
