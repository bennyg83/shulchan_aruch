To prohibit all washing. See Baer Heitev in Shach's name. Dagul MeRevavah writes that the leniency applies only to face, hands, and feet, but washing the entire body, even in cold water, remains prohibited throughout thirty days according to our custom. Teshuat Chen, siman 31, discusses this at length and concludes that a God-fearing person should personally refrain from full-body cold washing, but others may be instructed leniently when there is some mitzvah need, particularly on Erev Shabbos. Recreational bathing in rivers should remain prohibited. Baer Heitev further cites Maharshal that washing was prohibited because of its association with haircutting. Taz answers the apparent question from Chol HaMoed, when haircutting is prohibited but washing is not, by saying that on Chol HaMoed the haircut prohibition applies to everyone and therefore does not create a distinctive mourning concern. According to this reasoning, if Pesach or Sukkot interrupts shivah but does not cancel the thirty-day restrictions, perhaps bathing during Chol HaMoed should be permitted because everyone is already prohibited from haircutting. However, Maharshal's reason itself is difficult: if washing is prohibited because of haircutting, why is washing not likewise prohibited for a father or mother after thirty days so long as haircutting remains prohibited until friends rebuke him?
