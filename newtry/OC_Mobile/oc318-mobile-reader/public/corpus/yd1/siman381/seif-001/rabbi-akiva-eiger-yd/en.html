(Siman 381, Shach seif katan 1) There is no prohibition at all. It appears his intent is as follows. According to the reading "all washing for thirty days," Rama would seem to add two stringencies: first, in the type of washing, because Mechaber permits washing one's face, hands, and feet with cold water, while the custom prohibits all washing, even face, hands, and feet with cold water; second, in duration, extending the prohibition for thirty days. Shach therefore emends the text to read "washing for thirty days," meaning that whatever Mechaber prohibits by law for seven days is prohibited by custom for thirty days, namely the whole body in cold water or face, hands, and feet in hot water. But face, hands, and feet in cold water retain their ordinary law and are permitted even within seven days. Thus when Shach writes that there is certainly no prohibition of washing in cold water, he means face, hands, and feet in cold water. Proof comes from Shach below, siman 399, seif katan 7. Why did he not simply answer that Rama there speaks about washing the entire body in cold water? Evidently even that remains prohibited for thirty days. Nor can that passage be explained as face, hands, and feet, because those are permitted even without a festival during the seven days. This also resolves Rama in Orach Chayyim siman 606, seif 4, who permits washing and immersion on Erev Yom Kippur even though the custom prohibits washing for thirty days. If whole-body cold washing were otherwise permitted, one could fulfill the mitzvah immersion without any special dispensation. Therefore, the entire body in cold water is prohibited for thirty days. I later saw Eliyah Rabbah there struggling because of Shach's words here, understanding Shach to permit even whole-body cold washing. In my opinion, Shach means as I have explained.
