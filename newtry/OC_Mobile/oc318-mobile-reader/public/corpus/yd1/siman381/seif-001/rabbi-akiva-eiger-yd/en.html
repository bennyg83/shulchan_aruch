(Siman 381, Shach, seif katan 1) There is no prohibition at all. In my view, the Shach means that according to the reading “all bathing for thirty days,” the Rama would appear to add two stringencies: first, in the type of bathing, since the Mechaber permits washing one’s face, hands, and feet in cold water while custom forbids every form of bathing, even these in cold water; second, in duration, extending the prohibition to thirty days. The Shach therefore emends the text to “bathing for all thirty days,” meaning that whatever the Mechaber forbids by law during the seven days is forbidden by custom for thirty days, namely the entire body in cold water or the face, hands, and feet in hot water. Washing the face, hands, and feet in cold water remains permitted even during the seven days. The Shach’s statement that cold bathing is certainly not forbidden refers to the face, hands, and feet in cold water. His words below, siman 399, seif katan 7, prove this, for otherwise he could simply have explained that the Rama there refers to bathing the entire body in cold water. Evidently even that is forbidden for thirty days. The Rama in Orach Chaim 606:4 is thereby understood: one may bathe and immerse on the eve of Yom Kippur despite the custom against bathing for thirty days. If full-body immersion in cold water were otherwise permitted, this dispensation would be unnecessary. Eliyah Rabbah understood the Shach differently and was forced to explain that the Rama speaks where only hot water was available. In my opinion, the Shach’s true intent is as stated here.
