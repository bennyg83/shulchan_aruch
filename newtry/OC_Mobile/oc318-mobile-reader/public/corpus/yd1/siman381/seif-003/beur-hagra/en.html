(Compilation) A woman who has given birth, etc. See Be’er HaGolah and the Yerushalmi at the end of Chapter 2 of Berachot, and Chapter 3 of Moed Katan: “A mourner is forbidden to bathe throughout all seven days.” This applies only to bathing for pleasure, but bathing that is not for pleasure is permitted. This is illustrated by the incident in which Shmuel developed sores. He came and asked Rabbi Assi, “May I bathe?” He answered him, “He may bathe.” What case are we discussing? If it involves danger, then even on Tishah B’Av and even on Yom Kippur it is permitted. Rather, it is a case that does not involve danger. This is the text cited by the Rosh there, siman 31. This is also the meaning of the words, “And similarly, one who,” etc. The Rosh writes there that this is unlike Rabbeinu Chananel’s explanation that an istenis means one whose health is endangered. Rather, the Ramban explains Rabbeinu Chananel’s words as referring to one who suffers greatly, as written below, “and if he does not bathe,” etc.:
And not every, etc. The Rosh likewise writes this there in the name of Rabbeinu Chananel. See Be’er HaGolah. Thus far:
