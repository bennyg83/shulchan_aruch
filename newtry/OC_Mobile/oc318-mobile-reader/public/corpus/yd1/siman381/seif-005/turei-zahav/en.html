And certainly she may not bathe to put on white garments, etc. In the responsa of Maharil, siman 16, he wrote as follows: she is forbidden in marital relations, and it is forbidden to immerse for a mitzvah immersion on Yom Kippur and Tisha B'Av — for our House of God is fitting for such [abstinence], etc.; and so too during the seven days of mourning. But within thirty days one is permitted to immerse and to have relations, and even to bathe to whiten [garments]. The Or Zaru'a wrote that this is permitted, only that she should change the bathing slightly. But a white shift she certainly may put on, and a white sheet she certainly may spread — so that she not come to uncertainty — end quote. My brother the Gaon, Moharar Yitzhak HaLevi z"l, wrote that the statement "but a white shift, etc." applies also within the seven days; and so too it is explained in Beit Yosef, which wrote that within the seven days she is permitted to bathe, only that she should change the bathing slightly, etc. The Rema here understood that Maharil's statement "but a white shift, etc." applies only within thirty days — implying that within seven days it is forbidden to bathe for the whitening of garments. This is astonishing, for is it not a bath of pleasure, so why should it be forbidden? And the prohibition against bathing for immersion is because it is without need, since she is forbidden in marital relations; but bathing for whitening — why should it be forbidden? In any case it seems to me that even the Rema did not intend here to defer the putting on of white garments entirely until after seven days — that is obviously not so, for she can put on white garments without bathing altogether, as written in Yoreh De'ah, siman 196:3, in the gloss, that even if she bathed only the lower part of her face it is sufficient, and in a pressing situation even without bathing at all is sufficient, as long as the shift is clean, etc. Why then should we defer the putting on of white garments here for nothing? Rather, certainly the Rema only intended to forbid the bathing alone, not the putting on of white garments:
And again I saw in the responsa of Masa Binyamin, siman 5, that he permits even bathing within the seven days in order to put on white garments, and he disagrees with the Rema here, and his words are correct. But nevertheless in practice it seems to me that she should not bathe for the putting on of white garments; rather, she should wash the lower part of her face, put on a clean shift — not actually white — and count seven clean days. This seems clear and correct to me; and one who is stringent and defers the putting on of white garments entirely is among those who cause astonishment:
