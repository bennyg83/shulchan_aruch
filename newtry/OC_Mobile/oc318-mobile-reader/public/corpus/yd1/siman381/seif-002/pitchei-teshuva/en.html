To forbid all washing. See Be'er Heitev in the name of the Shach. See Dagul MeRevavah, who wrote that this refers specifically to his face, hands, and feet, but washing his entire body even in cold water is forbidden for us throughout the thirty days; see there. See Teshuat Chen responsa siman 31, who discusses this at length and concludes that a God-fearing person should be concerned to forbid himself even cold-water washing of the whole body, but for others one should rule leniently, specifically on Erev Shabbat, where there is some mitzvah need. But pleasurable bathing in rivers should be forbidden; see there. Regarding what Be'er Heitev further wrote in the name of Maharshal, that they forbade it because of haircutting, etc., see the Taz, who wrote about this: one should not ask, if so, on chol ha-moed, when haircutting is forbidden, washing should also be forbidden for this reason. One can answer that on chol ha-moed the prohibition applies to everyone, etc.; see there. According to this, perhaps if Pesach or Sukkot occurs within the seven days, where the decree of thirty is not canceled from him, as below siman 399, he may wash on chol ha-moed, for the decree due to haircutting does not apply, since haircutting is already forbidden because of chol ha-moed, and people will not stumble, since the prohibition applies to everyone. In truth, however, this reason of Maharshal is difficult to me: if so, why did they not forbid washing for one's father and mother even after thirty days, as long as he is forbidden to cut his hair, namely until his friends rebuke him, as below siman 390 seif 4?
