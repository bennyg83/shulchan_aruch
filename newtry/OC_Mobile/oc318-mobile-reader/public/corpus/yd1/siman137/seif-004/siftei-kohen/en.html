And likewise permitted, etc. In Rashba he wrote several reasons and Eitan HaTzvi and Bach bring them; Bach rejected his words and wrote we do not rule like all these reasons; and therefore this law Rashba and Mechaber wrote does not exist — only ab initio it is forbidden to salt in a forbidden vessel as above siman 105 seif 12; and also after the fact it is forbidden if it pickled a full day within it — so he wrote; and his words are not decisive; and furthermore since we rule it is permitted to put beer and other beverages — whatever the taste may be — all the more permitted to salt in them; and even though we do not rule like Rashba's reasons, nonetheless certainly the law is true (and Rashba who wrote all these reasons — meaning because he held beer is also permitted from those reasons and compares salting to beverages); and if we say the reason is as Tur wrote because the wine spoils them — and likewise Rosh: the reason that wine emitted into it is spoiled from the outset; and it is possible also Rashi who wrote the reason that beer nullifies the taste of wine, etc. — his intent is so (and not like Bach in another explanation); and as appears from Bach's words that this reason is primary — if so, all the more salt that nullifies the taste of wine; and on the contrary beer and beverages do not help to kasher the vessel, and salting with sharp brine helps as above siman 135 seif 12. (And from Rashba it also appears that even if it stood a long time it is permitted, for he wrote the reason that since they absorbed only to the peel amount the small prohibition in them is nullified in beverages and salting — see there; and even though we do not rule like Rashba that a vessel that absorbed a small prohibition is permitted to put heter abundantly ab initio as above siman 122 s.k. 2 — nonetheless regarding after the fact we certainly rule like Rashba that presumptively there is sixty against emission of the vessel that absorbed only to the peel amount, as appears from all poskim — and this is clear; and even though Rosh wrote and even though in all we estimate and there is no sixty in water and beer to nullify wine — nonetheless permitted because the taste of wine emitted into them is spoiled from the outset — so he wrote — it must be he does not disagree with poskim who hold presumptively there is sixty, for Tur also holds so — rather he comes to say that even when there is no sixty, such as when he put small beverages — permitted; know that Rosh wrote in a responsum and Beit Yosef brings it in this section that if he put wine in a gentile's vessel it is permitted because there is sixty against the peel — see there.) And furthermore it appears from Taz's words that even if he puts beer and beverages a long time — even though afterwards it becomes pickled — in pickling there is no distinction between salting and other beverages; rather certainly all beverages nullify the taste of wine absorbed in the vessel walls; and see above siman 105 that there vessels of other prohibitions emit nosen ta'am leshvach in all beverages and in salting — unlike wine whose taste clings in other beverages — all the more in salting; and such is the Rav's view in Tosafot klal 17 — see there; and in markers in one law he wrote that cheese molds of gentiles need initially hagalah to salt cheeses in them; and in law 3 he wrote it is permitted to salt meat in a gentile's vessel into which they put wine for permanence; and such is his view from what he ruled here like the Mechaber (and likewise Eitan HaTzvi).
