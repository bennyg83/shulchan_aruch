Permitted to put, etc. Because they nullify the taste of wine — so wrote the poskim.
Whether other beverages. And we do not decree beverages because of wine since it is merely incidental — Gemara; and accordingly it is forbidden to put beverages in them on a permanent basis; and so we hold above siman 122 seif 4 in Hagah regarding other forbidden vessels — see there.
And likewise it is permitted, etc. Rashba gives several reasons for this ruling, which are cited by the Levush. The Bach rejects his reasons and writes that we do not rule in accordance with them; therefore, he says, the Rashba's and Mechaber's ruling does not stand, and l'chatchila it is forbidden to salt in a forbidden vessel, as above in siman 105, seif 12, while after the fact it is forbidden if the food remained pickled there for an entire day. His words are not compelling. Since we rule that it is permitted to place beer and other beverages in these wine vessels, whatever the reason may be, all the more so salting should be permitted. Even if we do not accept all of Rashba's rationales, the ruling itself is certainly correct. If the reason is, as the Tur writes, that the wine impairs those beverages, and as the Rosh writes, that the wine released into them is already pagum, and perhaps this is also Rashi's intent when he writes that beer nullifies the taste of wine, then salt certainly weakens the wine taste at least as effectively. Indeed, beer and other beverages do not kasher the vessel, whereas strong salt water does, as above in siman 135, seif 12. Rashba also implies that even if it remained for a long time it is permitted, because the vessel absorbed only enough to affect a peel, so the small amount of prohibition is nullified in the beverage or salted food. Although we do not follow Rashba in permitting l'chatchila use of a vessel that absorbed only a small amount of prohibition, as above in siman 122, seif katan 2, after the fact we certainly follow him, because ordinarily there is sixty against what is released from a vessel that absorbed only to the depth of a peel. This is implied by all the poskim. Although the Rosh writes that we measure against the entire vessel and there may not be sixty in the water or beer, yet it remains permitted because the wine taste released into them was pagum from the outset, he need not disagree with those who say there is ordinarily sixty. Rather, he teaches that even where there is not sixty, such as where only a small amount of beverage was put in, it is permitted. Proof is that the Rosh writes in responsa, cited by the Beit Yosef at the beginning of this siman, that if wine was placed in a non-Jew's vessel it is permitted because there is sixty against the absorbed peel. Moreover, the Tur and Shulchan Aruch imply that beer and other beverages may remain in such vessels for a long time, even beyond twenty-four hours, although then they have the status of pickling, and with pickling there is no distinction between salted food and other liquids. Necessarily all beverages weaken the wine taste absorbed in the walls, unlike the case in siman 105, which concerns vessels of other prohibitions that impart beneficial taste to liquids and salted foods. This also appears to be the Rama's view in Toras Chatas, kelal 17, and in his glosses.
