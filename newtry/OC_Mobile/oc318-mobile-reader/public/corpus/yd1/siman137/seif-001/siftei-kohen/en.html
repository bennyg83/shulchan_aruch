All vessels, etc. It implies likewise vessels not used for permanent storage, which are kashered by rinsing — as above siman 135; if he did not rinse them and put wine in them, it is forbidden for drinking if it remained 24 hours; but Rabbeinu Yerucham wrote that if it dried and afterward a Jew used it without rinsing, it is permitted — though l’chatchilah rinsing is required; and Beit Yosef brings him; and wrote: and I already wrote so in siman 126 in the name of some interpreters; however in siman 127 he brought Ran’s words that if he did not rinse, it is forbidden for drinking; and likewise Rashba in Torat HaBayit in the name of Raavad and Ramban; and it appears there that that is also his own view; and Beit Yosef brings it in siman 134.
Forbidden immediately. For just as the vessel was forbidden by a gentile even if he put wine in it only briefly — so too the Jew's wine was forbidden in it even briefly — Tur. And he wrote in Torat HaBayit siman 201 that there is no distinction between a gentile's vessel and a Jew's vessel that absorbed yayin nesech.
For drinking. Nevertheless, under Talmudic law the wine placed in the barrels is prohibited in any amount. Beit Yosef writes so, and this is explicit in Rashba and the other poskim. Although wine that is itself prohibited only for drinking is normally nullified in sixty, as above in siman 134 and below in seif 4, this case is different: before the wine was absorbed into the vessel it was prohibited even for benefit. When its absorbed taste later mixes into the kosher wine, the original prohibition was one of benefit; it merely no longer forbids benefit because the prohibited substance is not present in tangible form and only the vessel's absorbed taste emerges. The Raah, however, implies that wine absorbed in vessels is nullified in sixty because the resulting prohibition is only for drinking. According to him, the wine is prohibited only where sixty is lacking. The Mechaber's rulings here and in the following seifim, however, support the first explanation: wine itself forbids in any amount, while grape skins and seeds, being a different kind, are nullified in sixty.
And it is dry. But if it was moist enough to moisten, what mixes with it is forbidden — as above siman 126 — Beit Yosef.
24 hours. Because cold in cold expels and absorbs within 24 hours — Rosh.
And if there was in the wine, etc. This is where he did not intend to nullify the prohibition. If he intended to nullify it, the mixture is forbidden, for we rule that one may not intentionally nullify a prohibition, and if he did so the nullification is ineffective, as explained above in siman 99. This applies where the wine had already become prohibited. It is, however, permitted l'chatchilah to mix water into wine so that if some prohibited wine later becomes mixed into it, the water will nullify it. Mordechai and other poskim write that Rabbeinu Tam instructed the people of his town to place a pitcher of water in every barrel for this purpose. Beit Yosef and the later authorities in siman 134 likewise permit this.
Six times. Rashba writes in a responsum that even wine drawn through the barrel's tap is permitted, because the absorbed taste expelled from the tap is nullified by the water mixed into the wine: whenever wine exits, water mixed with it exits as well and nullifies the prohibition absorbed in the tap-hole. Beit Yosef cites this. It is nevertheless possible that l'chatchilah one should not draw the wine through the tap-hole, because this resembles intentional nullification. The Bach disagrees and argues that it is not intentional nullification, since the person's purpose is merely to draw wine and not to nullify the prohibition in the tap. The same principle appears above in siman 84, seif katan 35, and siman 115, seif katan 28.
Against the thickness of a peel, etc. We measure against the entire peel-thickness and not merely against the amount presumed actually to emerge from the peel. Beit Yosef writes this in the name of Rashba, meaning that we do not know how much taste emerged, as explained above in siman 98 and in many places.
The peel-thickness of the barrel. The wine is absorbed only to the depth of the thin layer that can be shaved from the wood with a plane, as explained above in siman 135, seif 13. Rashba, cited by Beit Yosef, and Rivash, siman 133, write this. It appears that this applies even to a vessel used for permanent storage, and this is likewise implied by the poskim at the end of Avodah Zarah and by the Bach. Their words further imply that even if the wine is known to have remained there for twenty-four hours, ordinarily we do not assume absorption beyond a peel-thickness. The Bach, however, writes that according to Rabbeinu Tam and the Rosh a vessel used for permanent storage absorbs more deeply and requires sixty against the entire vessel. That inference is not compelled. Still, where it is definitely known that yayin nesech remained in the vessel for twenty-four hours, it appears that all agree with Rabbeinu Tam that the vessel is treated as kavush, which is like cooked, and absorption extends throughout the vessel. Accordingly, if kosher wine is later placed in such a vessel without water mixed into it, it is forbidden, because sixty against the entire vessel is required and the vessel's contents ordinarily do not equal sixty times the vessel itself. When the Rama writes that sixty suffices, he discusses the ordinary case where it is not known that the prohibited wine remained for twenty-four hours; there, even a vessel used for permanent storage is treated as having absorbed only to a peel-thickness, and the contents can contain sixty against that amount. See also siman 135, seif katan 33.
Ben yomo. For even if they are not ben yomo they forbid — for nosen ta'am lefegam applies only in a dish but not in wine vessels; on the contrary, the older they are the more they impart a beneficial taste — so wrote the poskim (and Rivash siman 433 and Torat HaBayit siman 201).
