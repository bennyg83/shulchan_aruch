All vessels, etc. It implies likewise vessels not used for permanent storage, which are kashered by rinsing — as above siman 135; if he did not rinse them and put wine in them, it is forbidden for drinking if it remained 24 hours; but Rabbeinu Yerucham wrote that if it dried and afterward a Jew used it without rinsing, it is permitted — though l’chatchilah rinsing is required; and Beit Yosef brings him; and wrote: and I already wrote so in siman 126 in the name of some interpreters; however in siman 127 he brought Ran’s words that if he did not rinse, it is forbidden for drinking; and likewise Rashba in Torat HaBayit in the name of Raavad and Ramban; and it appears there that that is also his own view; and Beit Yosef brings it in siman 134.
Forbidden immediately. For just as the vessel was forbidden by a gentile even if he put wine in it only briefly — so too the Jew's wine was forbidden in it even briefly — Tur. And he wrote in Torat HaBayit siman 201 that there is no distinction between a gentile's vessel and a Jew's vessel that absorbed yayin nesech.
And it is dry. But if it was moist enough to moisten, what mixes with it is forbidden — as above siman 126 — Beit Yosef.
24 hours. Because cold in cold expels and absorbs within 24 hours — Rosh.
Ben yomo. For even if they are not ben yomo they forbid — for nosen ta'am lefegam applies only in a dish but not in wine vessels; on the contrary, the older they are the more they impart a beneficial taste — so wrote the poskim (and Rivash siman 433 and Torat HaBayit siman 201).
