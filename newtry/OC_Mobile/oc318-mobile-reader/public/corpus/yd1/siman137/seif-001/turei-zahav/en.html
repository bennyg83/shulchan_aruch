If it is a vessel in which wine is kept for storage, etc. The Tur writes this in the name of the Rosh. I find the Rosh's statements difficult to reconcile, for in responsa, kelal 19, siman 6, he writes that the teaching at the end of Avodah Zarah, 'all of them, if one used them before immersing, scalding, or heating them, the food is forbidden,' does not mention a case where mere rinsing was required. That omission is obvious: since the vessels are dry, if one used them without rinsing, the food is permitted, as cups, pitchers, and flasks are not vessels in which wine is stored. This implies complete permission even if the contents remained there for twenty-four hours. Although the Beit Yosef cites this responsum in siman 126 and explains that the Rosh permitted it only because it had not remained there for twenty-four hours, with due respect, that is not precise. First, if remaining for twenty-four hours creates a prohibition in a vessel requiring rinsing, the original question returns: why did the Baraisa not teach 'before rinsing,' since there would be a forbidden case when the contents remained twenty-four hours? Second, the Baraisa's case of using the vessel before scalding or heating necessarily concerns hot use, for otherwise why would use before scalding or heating be forbidden? Yet even there, if one used a vessel requiring only rinsing without first rinsing it, the food is permitted. Here, however, the Rosh writes that even a vessel not used for storage can prohibit dry food placed in it without rinsing if it remains there twenty-four hours. It therefore appears that these words of the Rosh concern only a vessel requiring the three-day water treatment mentioned at the beginning of siman 135, even though it is not ordinarily used for storage. But where only rinsing is required, there is no prohibition at all even after twenty-four hours. One should not ask why the Baraisa did not mention use before the three-day treatment, because it lists only the procedures stated in its first clause, namely immersion, rinsing, scalding, and heating; the three-day treatment is not discussed there. The Ran likewise cites others who permit use where the vessel was not rinsed, as brought by the Beit Yosef in siman 126. Ramban limits that leniency to a funnel and similar utensils in which wine does not linger, apparently because he holds ordinary non-Jewish wine is forbidden in the smallest quantity. According to us, however, who hold that it is nullified in sixty, there is no prohibition after the fact. In this siman the Beit Yosef also cites Rabbeinu Yerucham as permitting dry use without rinsing even where it remained twenty-four hours. Although the Rama in siman 135, seif 11, appears to prohibit after the fact, perhaps he rules like Ramban. Nevertheless, in my view one who relies on the lenient authorities in this rabbinic matter loses nothing, especially regarding ordinary non-Jewish wine, concerning which the Rosh writes in responsa, kelal 19, siman 1, that we are very lenient whenever there is even a slight ground for permission. This is certainly so with absorbed taste in vessels, which is more lenient, since at the beginning of this siman it was explained that it forbids only drinking.
