And if it is a vessel inserted for permanence, etc. In Tur it is written so in the name of Rosh — and I find Rosh's words contradictory, for in responsum klal 19 siman 6 he wrote regarding what we say end Avodah Zarah "and all that were used before immersion, before hagalah, before whitening" — one tannaitic version forbids and does not teach "before rinsing" — meaning where rinsing is needed; in this matter it is obvious since they are dry if used without rinsing it is permitted, for cups, pitchers, and flasks are not inserted for permanence — so it appears he says entirely permitted even if it stood overnight; even though Beit Yosef brings this responsum in siman 126 and wrote there regarding Rosh's reason for leniency that since it did not stand overnight — I say his great honor rests in its place but he was not precise in these matters: one, if there is prohibition in standing overnight in something needing rinsing and he did not rinse, the difficulty returns to its place — why not teach "until he rinsed," for there is prohibition when it stood overnight; and further that which is taught there "and all that were used" means in heat — for if not, when used before hagalah or whitening why forbidden — behold it is cold; rather necessarily in heat; and nonetheless permitted if used before rinsing in something needing rinsing; and here Rosh wrote there is prohibition in what is not inserted for permanence even dry if used without rinsing and stood overnight — so it seems these words of Rosh are said only regarding requiring irui three days that he wrote above beginning siman 135 in what is not inserted for permanence; but in something needing only rinsing there is no prohibition at all even if it stood overnight; and one should not challenge why not teach "if used until irui" — for it taught only what it taught at the beginning — immersion, rinsing, hagalah, whitening; but irui is not dealt with there — so he wrote regarding this "and all that were used" etc. — unlike rinsing mentioned there at the beginning that should also be mentioned at the end according to Beit Yosef; and Ran also wrote so in the name of others to permit if he did not rinse — Beit Yosef brought in siman 126; except Ramban holds we are not lenient in this except in a funnel and the like where there is no wine delay, not in other vessels; and it appears Ramban holds stam yeinam is forbidden be'mashehu — therefore he was strict; but we who hold batel in sixty there is no prohibition after the fact; and in this siman Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Rabbi Yerucham likewise to be lenient if they used a dry matter without rinsing even if it stood overnight — examine his words; and even though from Rama's words in siman 135 seif 11 it appears forbidden after the fact as I wrote there — it is possible he rules like Ramban; but it appears to me that one who relies on lenient authorities in a rabbinic matter such as here does not lose — especially regarding stam yeinam prohibition where we are very lenient where there is even a bit of heter as Rosh wrote responsum klal 19 siman 1; and all the more regarding vessel absorption which is more lenient since it forbids only in drinking as written beginning this siman — so it appears to me.
