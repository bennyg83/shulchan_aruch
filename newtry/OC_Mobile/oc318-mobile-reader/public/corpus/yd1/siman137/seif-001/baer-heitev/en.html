For permanent storage. Terumat HaDeshen writes that there is no distinction between a non-Jew's vessel and a Jewish vessel that absorbed yayin nesech. The prohibition for drinking appears to apply only where there is not sixty. From the Mechaber, however, it appears that under Talmudic law it is not nullified even with sixty. Shach.
Remained. Regarding vessels not used for permanent storage, which require only rinsing, Shach implies that if they were not rinsed and wine was placed in them, the wine is forbidden for drinking if it remained twenty-four hours. Taz rules that nowadays, since ordinary non-Jewish wine is nullified in sixty, the wine is permitted after the fact. Although Rama in siman 135:11 appears stringent, one who relies upon the lenient authorities in a rabbinic matter loses nothing.
Times. Rashba writes that even wine exiting through the barrel's spigot is permitted, because whenever wine exits, the water mixed within exits with it. Beit Yosef suggests that initially it may be forbidden to draw through the spigot because this resembles intentional nullification. Bach disagrees: the intent is merely to remove wine, not to nullify the absorbed prohibition, as in siman 84:13. Taz distinguishes that there it was impossible otherwise, whereas here one can make a new opening or pour from the mouth. It is also permitted initially to add water to wine so that a later prohibition will be nullified by the water, as stated by Beit Yosef and the later authorities in siman 134.
Of the day. Detrimental taste applies to cooked food, not to wine vessels. On the contrary, the longer they age, the more favorable the taste. If wine is known to have remained in the vessel for twenty-four hours, Rashba and Beit Yosef imply that only a surface layer is absorbed. Shach disagrees: prolonged soaking is like cooking and forbids the entire vessel. Accordingly, if kosher wine is later placed in such a vessel without water, it is forbidden because sixty against the entire vessel is required, and the contents cannot contain sixty against the vessel. Rama's ruling that sixty suffices refers to the ordinary case where it is unknown that wine remained for twenty-four hours, so only a surface layer is assumed forbidden.
