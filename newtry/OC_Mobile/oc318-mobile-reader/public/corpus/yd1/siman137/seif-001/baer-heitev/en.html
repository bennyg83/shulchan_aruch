And regarding vessels that are not inserted for permanence that are kashered by rinsing as above siman 135 — it appears from Shach's words that if he did not rinse them and put wine in them, it is forbidden in drinking for whatever time it stood there overnight; but Taz ruled that for us who hold that stam yeinam is nullified in sixty, there is no prohibition after the fact, and even though from Rama's words in siman 135 seif 11 it appears it is forbidden after the fact — nonetheless one who relies on the lenient authorities in a rabbinic matter does not lose. So he wrote.
Times. Rashba wrote in a responsum that even what comes out through the spigot hole of the barrel is permitted, for any wine that comes out — the water mixed with it comes out with it; and Beit Yosef. But it is possible that initially it is forbidden to remove via the spigot hole because it is like nullifying a prohibition ab initio; Bach challenged him that this is not nullifying a prohibition ab initio since his intent is only to remove wine and not to nullify the prohibition in the hole — and so we say above siman 84 seif 13. So Shach; and Taz disagrees with Bach — disagreeing with siman 84 that it is different there because it is impossible otherwise, unlike here where one can make a new hole or remove through the mouth, etc. — see there. It is also permitted initially to put water in wine so that if any prohibition becomes mixed in it afterwards it will be nullified in the water — so Beit Yosef and the Acharonim in siman 134.
