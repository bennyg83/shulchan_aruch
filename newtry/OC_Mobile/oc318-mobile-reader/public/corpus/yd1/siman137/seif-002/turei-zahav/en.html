But forbidden to remove wine, etc. In Beit Yosef it is written this is specifically if the heter comes from pits and skins — therefore the wine through the hole is forbidden and must make a new hole or remove wine from above — so Beit Yosef in the name of Tevu'ot Shor; but if the heter comes from water mixed in wine as written seif 1 and he removed through the spigot — permitted, for any wine that comes out — the water mixed with it comes out with it and nullifies the prohibition absorbed in the spigot hole; and likewise Rashba in a responsum that emission from the spigot is a small matter and nullifies within the water; but it is possible initially forbidden to remove through the spigot hole because it is like nullifying ab initio — so he wrote; it appears from his words that in heter through pits even after the fact forbidden if removed through spigot; and his reason appears he goes to his view that stam yeinam is not nullified in sixty as written siman 134 — and this that became forbidden due to the spigot hole is like stam yeinam; but why Rama wrote there batel in sixty — here too batel when he removed wine through spigot; and even though at time of removal there is not sixty against the peel of the hole — nonetheless afterwards when wine was poured heter joins heter and nullifies peel of hole in sixty of wine — and this is what Rama concluded here all wine is permitted, etc. And in Beit Yosef's words regarding heter through water that initially forbidden through hole — Maharshal disagreed that there is no nullifying ab initio since intent is only to remove wine and not nullify prohibition in hole — therefore permitted even initially; and to me Beit Yosef's words are correct, for even though we find this reasoning that intent is not to nullify prohibition in siman 84 seif 13 regarding ants in honey permitted to strain and likewise worms in wheat — not nullifying ab initio — it is different there impossible otherwise unlike here possible to make new hole or remove through mouth above; whatever removal in a way with concern of prohibition is called ab initio and forbidden; and so proven from Rashba's responsum mentioned siman 134 seif 5 if he put kosher wine, etc. — he wrote there it is nullifying ab initio — meaning also possible otherwise.
