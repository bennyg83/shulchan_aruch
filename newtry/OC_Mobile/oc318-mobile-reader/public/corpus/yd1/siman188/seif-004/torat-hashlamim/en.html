Whether moist or dry. See the Sema in siman 72 s.k. 8, that in most cases the latter “whether” is the more obvious one. Nevertheless, here one must say that the first “whether” is the more obvious one, for the essential question is regarding dry blood, whereas moist blood is obvious that it conveys tumah.
And they stood in the house. This refers to water drawn from a spring, for river water is warm even without standing in the house; so wrote the Beit Yosef, and this is the implication of the early poskim. The Bach and the Perishah also wrote so. Although in Orach Chaim, Hilkhot Pesach siman 455, it is explained that river water is cold, and therefore it requires no standing or overnight rest at all and one may knead with it immediately, whereas spring water is warm and therefore must stand twelve hours to cool it, that case is different, for it refers to water drawn in the month of Nisan, which is during the rainy season, when the springs are hot. In the summer days, by contrast, it is the opposite, as explained in the Talmud in Pesachim 94b. See the Beit Yosef there in Orach Chaim.
