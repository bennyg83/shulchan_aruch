Whether moist or dry. See Sema in siman 72, seif katan 8, who writes that generally the second case introduced by the word 'whether' is the more obvious one. Here, however, one must say that the first case is more obvious, because the central question concerns a dry piece, whereas a moist piece obviously conveys impurity.
And they stood in the house. This refers to water drawn from a spring, for river water is warm even without standing in the house. Beit Yosef writes this, and it is implied by the early poskim; Bach and Perishah likewise write so. Although Orach Chayim, Hilchot Pesach, siman 455, explains that river water is cold and therefore requires neither standing nor remaining overnight and may be used for kneading immediately, whereas spring water is warm and must stand for twelve hours in order to cool, the cases differ. There the discussion concerns water drawn in the month of Nisan, during the rainy season, when springs are warm, whereas in the summer the opposite is true. This is explained in Pesachim 94b. See Beit Yosef in Orach Chayim there.
