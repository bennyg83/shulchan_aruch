Any blood that comes out of a woman, whether moist or dry, renders her tamei. Moreover, even if a formed creature came out of her — like shells, or like hairs, or like tiny red gnats — she is tamei, provided that they dissolve within 24 hours by soaking them in lukewarm water, and the water remains lukewarm the entire 24 hours that they are in it; and the measure of the warmth is like water that was drawn in the summer from the river or from the spring and stood in a house whose heat of the house warms it; and like the warming of these, so is the measure of “lukewarm” in the days of winter. (And unspecified “lukewarm” is not hotter than the warmth of saliva (Beit Yosef in the name of Ritva, perek HaMapeles).) But if they are so hard that they do not dissolve within 24 hours, she is tehorah, even if they dissolve through crushing, that one crushes them with his fingernails. (And if one crushes them with his fingernails and they did not dissolve, she is tehorah, and there is no need to check by soaking.) (The Maggid Mishneh, perek 5; and so wrote Beit Yosef in the name of Rashba.)
