There, the blood is inside the pieces, though visible through their cracks. Here, by contrast, the bloody moisture lies upon the surface of the pieces. Shach in the name of Maggid Mishneh.
Specifically regarding a woman who has a fixed veset. Shach disputes this ruling, for since she sees only dry pieces, she is pure even at the time of her veset. The same is written in Efei Ravrevei. It is possible that this is also Rama's view. His words, 'specifically regarding a woman,' and 'not at the time of her veset,' may refer back to what he wrote immediately before, that if she examined herself three times and so forth, she no longer needs to examine herself. This exemption applies only not at the time of her veset, but at the time of her veset she requires examination in every circumstance. This is also implied by Levush. If, however, she examined the pieces in lukewarm water and they did not dissolve, Rama likewise agrees that she is pure. See what I wrote above in seif katan 7.
