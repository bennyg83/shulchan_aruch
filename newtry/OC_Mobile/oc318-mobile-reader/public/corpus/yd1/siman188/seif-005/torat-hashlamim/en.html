But if there is any moisture. This is not comparable to above, seif 2, where if she saw blood in a piece, even if it was split, she is tahor. There the blood is inside the pieces, only in the cracks; but here the moisture of blood is on top of the pieces. Shach in the name of the Maggid Mishneh.
And specifically with a woman who has a veset. The Shach challenged this law, for since she sees only dry things, even at the time of her veset she is tahor, and so wrote Sefer Apei Ravrevei. Perhaps the master’s view is also so, and what he wrote, “specifically with a woman, etc.,” and “not at the time of her veset,” refers back to what he wrote just before: if she checked three times, etc., she no longer needs to check. That is specifically not at the time of her veset; but at the time of her veset she requires examination in every case, and so it is implied in the Levush. But if she checked in lukewarm water and it did not dissolve, the master’s view too is that she is tahor. See what I wrote above, s.k. 7.
