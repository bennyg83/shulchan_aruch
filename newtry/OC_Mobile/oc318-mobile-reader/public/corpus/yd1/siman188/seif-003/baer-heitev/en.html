Uprooted. Bach wrote "requires investigation" regarding the practical case whether there is room to be lenient in this — see there.
She saw. Bach wrote — it implies he holds we do not require she examined at the hour the makor was uprooted; and it is not clear except we specifically require at the hour of its fall to the outer chamber she examined and saw no blood at all; but afterward when it was in the outer chamber she did not cease seeing as long as those pieces were in the outer chamber — and R. Shimon purified her, etc. — see there; and see responsum Maharam of Lublin siman 111; and Taz challenged Bach on this and wrote there is no distinction between hour of fall and after fall — what measure can one give when is called hour of fall or after; also he ruled to be lenient here even during her veset time — for Torah forbade only when the makor remains in its place and blood emerges from it; not so here where the makor itself carries the blood with it and this is not way of seeing the Torah forbade — and not like Bach who wrote she is permitted only between veset and veset — see there.
