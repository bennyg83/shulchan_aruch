If she inserted a tube and extracted blood with it, she is tehorah. And likewise if she saw blood on a piece [of tissue], even if it is split and the blood is in the splits in a manner that it touches her flesh, she is tehorah, since it is not the way to see so. And likewise a woman whose makor was torn out, and as it were pieces of flesh fall in her beit hachitzon, is tehorah. {Rama: Even if she saw blood the entire time that the pieces are in her beit hachitzon, she is tehorah, for we attribute the blood to this piece, since we know with certainty that her makor was torn out, and it is because of a wound (so it implies in Tur and Rabbeinu Yerucham and Rosh).} And that is when she cast the pieces into lukewarm water and they did not dissolve, and specifically small pieces, comparable to the tube; but a large piece is tamei even if she did not see anything, for it is impossible for “the opening of the kever” without blood, even in a miscarriage whose form was not completed.
