Even if she saw blood. This refers to the latter clause, where her source became detached, etc. It is taken from the words of the Tur, who wrote that she did not cease seeing as long as those pieces remained in the outer chamber. The Beit Yosef initially explained that she did not cease seeing actual blood, and even so she is tahor, because we attribute the blood to this piece. He found this difficult, because although above we attribute blood to a wound, that applies to a wound located at the sides, whereas here the wound was in the source itself, and perhaps she should be tamei. He therefore reinterpreted the Tur as not referring to blood, but rather that she saw small pieces. The Darkei Moshe challenged the Beit Yosef's stringency concerning a wound located in the source itself, for we say in the chapter HaTinokes that a woman is believed to say, 'I have a wound in the source from which blood emerges,' etc. He therefore explained according to the Beit Yosef's first interpretation. For this reason, whatever she sees, even actual blood, after she senses that the source has fallen into the outer chamber, she attributes the blood to those pieces. This is also the meaning of his words here, where he wrote that it is due to a wound. This is difficult to me. If so, why did the Gemara and the poskim state the reason that it is not her usual manner to see in this fashion? How is this different from the case in siman 187, seif 5, where they were lenient regarding a wound even without this reason? It appears to me that although the Rama wrote correctly regarding the practical law, his reason is not satisfactory. Here they permitted even actual menstrual blood that came from that piece. Namely, the menstrual blood contained in that detached piece of the source was carried together with the piece into the outer chamber. It does not render her forbidden, because the Torah prohibited only when the source remains in its place and the blood emerges from it. Here, however, the source itself carries the blood together with it, and this is not the manner of seeing prohibited by the Torah. This is also clear from the words of the Rosh cited by the Beit Yosef: 'That which we say that this is not menstrual blood means that menstrual blood does not ordinarily emerge in this manner,' etc. This is precisely as I have explained, and there is no relevance here to the blood of a wound. According to this, this ruling is permitted even at the time of her veset, since her seeing is abnormal. This is likewise implied by the Rama's wording in seif 5, where he wrote there, 'specifically not at the time of her veset,' etc., whereas here he wrote no such qualification. My father-in-law, of blessed memory, however, wrote that this ruling applies only between one veset and the next. He further disagreed with the Rama and ruled stringently that she must examine herself at the time the source falls into the outer chamber and establish that she is tahor, and only afterward may she see blood, etc. I see no distinction in this matter between the time of the falling and afterward. Furthermore, what measure can be assigned to determine when it is considered the time of falling and when it is considered afterward? I have written what appears correct in my humble opinion.
