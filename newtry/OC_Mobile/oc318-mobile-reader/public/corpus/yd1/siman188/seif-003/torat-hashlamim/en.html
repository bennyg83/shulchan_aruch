And likewise a woman whose source was uprooted. See Bach, who leaves this ruling requiring study. Shach writes that when pieces fall from her without blood, she is pure according to all opinions. Taz explains that there is no reason to be stringent at all in these laws. This appears to me to be the primary ruling, for one should not depart from the decision of the Shulchan Aruch; the authority of Rash, Rosh, Tur, and Rif is sufficient for us to rely upon them.
And it is caused by a wound. Rama writes this in Darkei Moshe as an explanation of Tur's words, and disputes Beit Yosef, who wrote that we do not attribute the blood to a wound located in the source itself. See Responsa Maharam of Lublin, siman 411, who discusses Beit Yosef's words at length; see there. See also Lechem Chamudot in the chapter HaMapelet, who disputes Rama and writes that Tur's principal reason is that even without a wound there is room to permit her, since blood does not ordinarily emerge in this manner. The same is written by Olat Tamid. Bach writes that this applies specifically not at the time of her veset, but at the time of her veset she is impure. It appears obvious to me that this is also Rama's view. Since he compares this law to a case in which the discharge is caused by a wound, and Rama already wrote above, in siman 187 seif 5, that at the time of her veset, or on the thirtieth day, she may not attribute the blood to her wound, for otherwise she would never become impure, it is explicit that at the time of her veset she may not attribute it to a wound at all. All this is clear, unlike Taz, who argues that because Rama wrote the rule here without qualification, whereas in seif 5 he specified that it applies only not at the time of her veset, it follows that no distinction applies here. I do not understand his reasoning at all. Why would Rama need to repeat here a distinction he had already stated explicitly above? By contrast, in the following seif 5, where she saw no blood at all but only dry pieces, logic would permit her even at the time of her veset, as Shach indeed writes there in seif katan 15. Rama therefore needed to teach that despite this, at the time of her veset she is impure. Consider this carefully.
