We do not rely upon her. Rashba writes that perhaps, just as the matter is not clear to us, it was not truly clear to that sage either, and we cannot now determine it; he may merely have reasoned that the appearance was of a certain kind when in fact it was not. His words are cited by Beit Yosef. According to this reasoning, it would appear that if the woman brings the original blood and says, 'A certain sage declared pure an appearance exactly like this,' she should be believed, for we can see that she is not mistaken in her comparison. Yet the discussion in the Gemara at the end of the chapter Kol HaYad proves that even in such a case she is not believed. Otherwise, why did the Gemara not resolve the incident involving Yalta in this manner? Rashi's language there also implies that whenever the blood itself is before us, we do not rely upon the woman's testimony. This requires study.
