Wax. Or citron or egg yolk — so write the poskim. Shach wrote, and Taz from Beit Yosef is clear, that even if it is not entirely white but like the appearance of a white garment on which dust fell that dulled its whiteness — it is permitted; for such a blackish appearance does not come from a reddish appearance — and so Shach. Bach also found written that a woman from whom a whitish thick discharge came after bathing in a bathhouse for two or three hours — she is impure, for blood's way is to whiten from bathing — and Taz wrote his words are astounding and this is extra stringency that we do not find in the Talmud and poskim — let one who is stringent be stringent for himself and not instruct others so at all. Bach further invented one stringency — when green and white appearances come before the decisor while still moist he should not rule on them until they dry, for sometimes after drying red will be found at the edges; Taz and Shach challenged him and ruled we do not practice so at all — see there. (And see Taz s.k. 1 who wrote that if so what is meant by what they said in siman 186 regarding a woman without veset that she examine herself before relations — what does the examination help since she must wait until it dries and while moist she knows nothing? I did not understand — behold the examination helps if she found nothing; and a few words.)
