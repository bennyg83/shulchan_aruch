Any appearance etc. By Torah law she is impure only in five types of appearances; and the Sages were stringent so that no error be made between one blood and another — they prohibited any appearance inclined toward redness and deemed pure any appearance that cannot be doubted to have any redness at all.
White. And even if not completely white, but like the appearance of a white garment on which dust fell that dulled its whiteness — pure; and unlike Trumat HaDeshen siman 246 who hesitates on this; and all whom I heard and saw were accustomed to permit explicitly without any hesitation at all — Beit Yosef, brought by Darkei Moshe; and see Bach.
Green etc. Bach wrote that this is specifically when it remains green from beginning to end; but when it changed after the stain dried and its edges became red — she is impure, for when it was extracted from the body it underwent change and became green, and when it dried it reverted somewhat to its original red appearance; therefore when a green or white appearance comes before the decisor while still moist — he should not rule on it until it has dried — end quote; and we do not follow this practice; and it appears we do not presume an unfavorable presumption — as we say in chapter Eilu Tereifot regarding the appearance of the intestines, that one need not boil them to see if their appearance changed, for we do not presume an unfavorable presumption; and this was explained above siman 52 s.k. 11; and the same applies here, it seems to me.
Wax. Or an etrog, or an egg yolk — so it is in the poskim; and see above siman 38 s.k. 1 and 2.
