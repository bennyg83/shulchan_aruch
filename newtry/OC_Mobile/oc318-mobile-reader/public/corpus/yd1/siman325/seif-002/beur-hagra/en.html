If there is, etc. This is what the Rosh and the Tur wrote, based on the Rosh there, and likewise on the words of the Rash and Tosafot in Niddah 7a, s.v. u-Makafot, and so too Tosafot in Pesachim 46b, s.v. Mide-Rabbi Eliezer, and the Rosh there, the Mordechai, and Sefer HaTerumah. The matter is divided into three cases. If the doughs are not in a vessel at all, they need not even touch one another, for the house combines them. If they are in open vessels, they require hakafah, meaning that the vessels are brought close and touch one another; this is the rule of min hamukaf, as Rashi explained at the end of the chapter Keshem. But if the vessels are sealed, even touching is of no help. See Maaser Sheni chapter 3, where it says "before the sealing," and the Rambam and the Shulchan Aruch below, siman 331 seif 26. So too in the Tosefta Terumot chapter 3: produce that is scattered within the house, or two sacks of grain in one enclosure, may be separated from one upon the other; but two barrels are not so, as explained in the Yerushalmi, because there is a hollow of a tefach in the barrel. The Yerushalmi there asks: if so, the house should combine them. It answers that this applies where he is particular that they not mix together. This is like the case of tamei dough and tahor dough in which he separates challah from one for the other, but only from the side, and there it explains that the house does not combine them. Sefer HaTerumah emended the text and read "the vessel combines them," and wrote that one must say the reading was corrupt; but there is no need for this. It is exactly as written, and so too in the Yerushalmi in Pesachim there, where they said that he throws it into the air of the house, for the air of the house combines them, but the floor does not combine them, as I wrote above regarding a pit or groove in the floor. And as for what the Yerushalmi says there, that if he is particular about its mixture the house does not combine, see what I wrote above. In such a case, not only is there no combination to reach the measure for challah, but even if each has a measure one may not separate from one for the other unless they touch. And what the Yerushalmi says in Terumot, that the entire winepress-house is one unit and the entire threshing floor is one unit, means only where it is all one continuous holding or one mass. But if there are sacks, they do not interrupt only when no other species lies between them; where another species lies between the upper and lower produce in the basket, one may not separate except when it is min hamukaf. What Sefer HaTerumah wrote, explaining the phrase "the air of the house" as meaning that the dough is literally suspended in the air of the house, is difficult. Rather, it means that it is not placed in a pit or groove in the floor, as I have explained.
