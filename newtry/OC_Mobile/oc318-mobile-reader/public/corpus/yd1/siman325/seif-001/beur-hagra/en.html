Two, etc. Chapter 2 mishnah 4, according to the first tanna, for Rabbi Eliezer does not dispute the first tanna but adds, as it says “even,” etc.
Until they adhere, etc. See the Rash there: one who makes, etc., as taught in Tosefta Taharot, etc.
And if they are not, etc. Like Rabbi Eliezer, for the halakhah is ruled like him in Pesachim chapter 3, 48b, and in Niddah chapter 1. Although in the Yerushalmi there Rabbi Yonah and Rabbi Chiyya in the name of Rabbi Yochanan, and Rabbi Yose son of Rabbi Imi in the name of Rabbi Yochanan, say this is where they bite; some tanna teaches the basket combines but the oven does not, and another teaches the oven combines but the basket does not. Rabbi Yochanan said: if they bite, in both cases it combines; if they do not bite, in both cases it does not combine. The Mishnah is like the Babylonian loaves. Nevertheless, in the Gemara of Pesachim we do not conclude so, as stated there: but it was said about it, etc. Rav Yosef and Abaye hold so, and we follow them. See Tosafot there s.v. “they taught only,” etc., and so wrote the Rash. According to this, even for Rabbi Chanina, they cannot disagree about the text; rather, he holds that a basket combines them without nesikhah. But from the Rambam, Tur, and other poskim, who also rule like the first tanna, it appears they read “even one who removes from the oven,” and he does not disagree with the first tanna. Rabbi Eliezer's nesikhah is regarding a basket, and this is what it says “even,” etc.: according to the first tanna, they must bite while still dough; Rabbi Eliezer says even, etc.; and Rabbi Chanina says no nesikhah is needed at all for this. Nevertheless, touching is required even in a basket according to Rabbi Eliezer, as the Semag wrote, and so the Rosh explicitly wrote there in seif 4; see there. So too the Rash wrote on the Mishnah there. One can also say, etc. Although he rejected this from the phrase “the basket combines them” and not “connects them,” as in Menachot 24a, he already refuted it there, for it is impossible: according to Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi, even nesikhah is required, and it is not difficult for him that it teaches “combines”; and Rabbi Chanina disagrees only that nesikhah is unnecessary. If even touching were unnecessary, what novelty is “even like loaves”? Without touching, even Babylonian loaves do not have nesikhah. This is what it says at the beginning of chapter 4, “and they touched each other,” although touching alone is nothing. If it were only because of the first tanna, it should have said “and they bit,” but it is also because of Rabbi Eliezer. Likewise in chapter 2 there: one who makes, etc., and they touched, etc.; at first glance “and they touched” is extra, for it could have said “and they bit” and we would understand that specifically nesikhah is required. Rather, it is because of Rabbi Eliezer, for Rabbi Eliezer disagrees only with what it says “until they bite.” Regarding an oven, the Rambam rules that they do not combine, unlike Rabbi Yochanan. So it appears, since there and there it says the halakhah follows Rabbi Eliezer, and does not say the halakhah also follows Rabbi Yochanan. Although the Rashba disagrees, see Beit Yosef; the Rambam's words are primary. Even though in the Yerushalmi above Rabbi Yochanan said, if they bite, here and there it combines, he came only to explain their words, that they dispute specifically where they bite. But since we rule only like Rabbi Eliezer, in an oven even if they bit, no.
And even after, etc. As stated: even one who removes, etc.
And if he placed them, etc. An unresolved question. The Rambam wrote that it is doubtful, and if challah is rabbinic he is exempt. It has already been explained in siman 322 seif 2 that nowadays challah is rabbinic; see there.
Therefore, etc. Tur.
That is, etc. So explained the Beit Yosef, and his words are correct. Basket combination is like the biting of doughs; with doughs, even if they bite only slightly it works. So too according to Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi, who says even according to Rabbi Eliezer it must specifically be biting, even a little suffices. The Semag does not hold any of this, and says the concern is only the rim; but anything with an interior, even if all of them are above, has no problem, because the airspace of a vessel combines like the airspace of a house in the Yerushalmi, Pesachim chapter 3.
