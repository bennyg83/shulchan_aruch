And if they are not. This follows R. Eliezer, whose opinion is established as halachah in Pesachim 48b and in the first chapter of Niddah. Although the Yerushalmi there cites R. Yona and R. Chiyya in the name of R. Yochanan, and R. Yose son of R. Imi in the name of R. Yochanan, that this applies only where the loaves adhered, and records one version that a basket combines but an oven does not and another that an oven combines but a basket does not, with R. Yochanan saying that if they adhered then each combines but if not then neither combines, the Bavli in Pesachim does not conclude this way. Rav Yosef and Abaye follow the Bavli's conclusion, and we rule accordingly. See Tosafot there and Rash. According to this, even R. Chanina's view cannot rest on a different textual tradition. Rather, he holds that a basket combines even without actual adhesion. From Rambam, Tur, and the other poskim, who also rule like the first Tanna, it appears that their text reads "even one who removes," so R. Eliezer does not disagree with the first Tanna. His requirement of adhesion refers specifically to loaves in a basket. Thus the first Tanna requires the doughs to adhere while still dough, while R. Eliezer adds that even afterward there can be combination, and R. Chanina says no adhesion is needed in that case. Nevertheless, actual touching is still required even in a basket according to R. Eliezer, as Semag and Rosh state explicitly, and Rash likewise writes. Although Rash questions this from the wording "the basket combines them" rather than "connects them," as in Menachot 24a, he himself rejects that objection. According to R. Yehoshua ben Levi even adhesion is required and the Gemara still uses the term "combines." R. Chanina disagrees only about adhesion. If even touching were unnecessary, what would be the meaning of saying that even ring-shaped loaves combine without touching, when adhesion is inherently impossible there? This also explains why the Mishnah at the beginning of chapter 4 says "and they touched one another." Mere touching alone is otherwise insufficient for the first Tanna, so the phrase is needed also for R. Eliezer. Likewise in chapter 2 the Mishnah's wording concerning doughs that touched is not superfluous; it teaches R. Eliezer's view, since he disputes only the requirement of actual adhesion. Regarding an oven, Rambam rules that it does not combine, unlike R. Yochanan. This also appears from the fact that the Gemara says the halachah follows R. Eliezer but does not separately say it follows R. Yochanan. Although Rashba disagrees, Rambam's view is primary. The Yerushalmi's statement that when they adhered both basket and oven combine merely explains the competing views there. Since we rule only like R. Eliezer, even adhesion in an oven does not create combination.
