(Siman 298, Shach, seif katan 2) The Bach wrote that since it is common. The Bach did not state this as a definitive permission, but as an unresolved legal question. He writes: 'It requires study in our kingdom, where hemp cloth is found in some places, but few people can distinguish linen from hemp, whether one may sew it beneath wool. It appears that everyone must recognize it, as Rosh wrote regarding silk.' He then discusses at length that when it is sewn beneath the garment so it is not visible, and there is no possibility of a Torah prohibition, one may be lenient in a rabbinic prohibition performed in private, as ruled in Orach Chaim 305:11. In my humble opinion, there is room for leniency even regarding a Torah prohibition when the stitching is beneath the garments, based on Tosafos, Shabbos 62a, s.v. vehatanya, who write that when an act remains concealed even in the marketplace, it is permitted.
