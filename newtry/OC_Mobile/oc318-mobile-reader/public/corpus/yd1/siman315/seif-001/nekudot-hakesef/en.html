(Siman 315, Shach seif katan 3) Since the Israelite, and so forth, until "and he must bring proof." Ateret Zahav writes this. However, Tosafot in Bava Metzia 6b and Ketubot 20a, Rosh, Mordechai, Agudah, Semag, Responsa Maimoniyot, and the other poskim appear not to accept this reasoning. They compare a doubtful firstborn to other monetary doubts, and if they held Ateret Zahav's reason there would be no question. Nevertheless, Ateret Zahav's reasoning can perhaps resolve their difficulty. As a matter of practical law, the aforementioned poskim may not actually disagree with him. Tosafot and Agudah imply that according to the view that one does not remove property from the possessor, this applies specifically where the kohen is a recognized regular recipient of that owner's gifts, or else the kohen can claim only the monetary value of the owner's discretionary right to choose a recipient, according to the opinion that such discretionary benefit is property. Accordingly, when those authorities rule that one does remove it, this would apply even if the kohen offers to pay for that discretionary value. Yet Ateret Zahav can still agree, meaning only that the Israelite originally has a chazakah over at least that discretionary benefit before payment, so the kohen remains the claimant throughout. I later discussed this at length in my work Tekfo Kohen and concluded that many poskim agree with Ateret Zahav.
