(Siman 315, in Shach seif katan 3) Since the Jew, etc., through, and he must bring proof. Ateret Zahav writes this. But Tosafot in Bava Metzia 6b and Ketubot 20a, Rosh, Mordechai, Agudah there, Semag positive commandment 95, responsa of Maimonides, Laws of Civil Law siman 39, and the other authorities imply that they did not accept this reason, for they compare a doubtful firstborn to other doubtful cases. Had they accepted this reason, there would have been no question. Nevertheless, Ateret Zahav's reasoning may correctly answer their difficulty. As a matter of law, however, it appears that those authorities do not disagree with Ateret Zahav. Although Tosafot in Bava Metzia and Agudah explicitly imply that according to the view that one does not extract it, this applies only where the kohen is a regular recipient, or that he can claim only the value of the owner's benefit according to the view that such benefit is monetary property, and although the ruling follows the view that one does extract it even when the kohen offers to pay that value, Ateret Zahav may agree. He means that the Jew at least initially possesses the benefit before the kohen pays him, so it remains in his possession and the kohen is always the claimant seeking extraction. Examine carefully. I later discussed this at length in my work Tekfo Kohen and concluded that many authorities agree with Ateret Zahav.
