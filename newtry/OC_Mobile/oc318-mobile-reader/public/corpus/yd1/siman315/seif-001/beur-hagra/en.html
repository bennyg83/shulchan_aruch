Doubt, etc. Mishnah 19b.
(Likkut) And if a kohen seized it, etc. This is the Rambam's view, but most poskim disagree with him. So the gloss rules, that we remove it from his hand. This also appears from the plain sugya in the first chapter of Bava Metzia 6b, for Rabbah holds so, Rav Chananya supports him, and Abaye concludes so there. Tosafot there s.v. “exempts” wrote so, and this is the conclusion, etc.; so ruled the Rosh there and the other poskim. But the Rambam holds like the Rashba wrote in siman 311, that the support there is not precise. Some explain that there it was according to the initial assumption that it speaks of a doubtful bekhor, but according to the Gemara's conclusion it speaks of a doubtful petter chamor, because there it was established in the Israelite's hand: with petter chamor the kohen has not acquired it, and the lamb was already established in the owner's possession, unlike a bekhor and other doubts. Abaye's statement was not meant to refute his rejection, but Rav Chananya's support was thereby rejected. The Rashba wrote a proof for the Rambam from the Gemara's statement there, “one who seeks to extract bears the burden of proof”; if not, why is this absolute, as the Gemara asks in Ketubot 77b? Rabbah's phrase “actually,” etc., was said as a rejection: even if you say we remove it, still there is no proof. So too the Rambam holds in every doubt, whether a doubt caused by claims like that bathhouse case, a doubt arising on its own such as a doubtful bekhor, or a doubt of law, that we say “one who seeks to extract bears the burden of proof,” and in all of them, if one seized, we remove it from him. There is proof from Bava Kamma 35 in the Mishnah, “one who seeks to extract bears the burden of proof,” and there 46, “this is a great rule in law,” etc., and many other places, implying that the one who seized from his fellow must bring proof. So too Ketubot 20a: Rav Nachman said, set two, etc.; see Rashi there s.v. “and establish,” etc.; Bekhorot 49a: Rabbi Akiva says if he gave, etc., and the Gemara says Rabbi Akiva is uncertain, etc.; Bava Metzia 79a: the sages taught, one who rents a ship, etc., if he gave he may not take back, and if not, etc.; there 102b: and Shmuel said, in the case where he comes, etc., what is the reason there? Because he is holding; here too he is holding. Rav Nachman disagrees only because land stands in the possession of its owner. There 103a regarding orchards, “he dug all the orchards he had,” etc.; Tosafot asked from Bava Batra, chapter HaMokher et HaBayit, where “lands” means two lands, “palms” means he gives him two palms, and answered that here the holder has the upper hand. Likewise Bava Batra 105b; there 34b, “this one says,” etc., whoever is stronger prevails; Gittin 60b: now that the halakhah was not stated, etc., whoever is stronger prevails; Ketubot 64a: now that it was not stated, if she seized we do not remove from her, if she did not seize we do not give her; and there 84b: Rabbi Yochanan said, you made it like Torah law. Shall we say they disagree about this, etc.? Alternatively, all agree his fellow, etc.; thus even according to Rabbi Yochanan the halakhah is not clearly like Rabbi Tarfon, while lechatchilah it follows Rabbi Akiva. There it says the relatives of Rabbi Yochanan, etc.; he said, you seized well. So too from the expression “incline” it implies that if one acted like the other view, we do not reverse the act. In other places one can say that if the judge ruled like the view that we do not remove from the holder, we do not reverse, and even here, since they acted by the judge's permission, the judgment is not reversed because it was not an error in an explicit Mishnah, as stated there, “you made it like Torah law.” But here they seized on their own, and he told them, “you seized well.” There 16b: sometimes she seized two hundred and said, etc.; in Bava Batra there, the halakhah is we do not seize, but if one seized we do not remove. All this is proof for the Rambam. Tosafot in Ketubot 20a s.v. “and establish” wrote that it is unlike doubtful bekhor, because the one seizing there is unsure, whereas one who claims with certainty benefits from seizure according to all. They further answered that seizure before the doubt arises helps. What is said there 16, “sometimes she seized two hundred,” Nimukei Yosef at the beginning of Bava Metzia explains that because it is a matter that will be clarified, we do not remove; as stated in Bava Metzia 110a, although we rule like Rav Nachman, etc., here it is a matter likely to be clarified. He further wrote that any seizure with permission helps, like Bava Metzia 102b, Bava Batra 105b, Bava Metzia 79a and 150a, and Bekhorot 49a, as above. At first glance there is proof for them from Bava Batra 32, in the case where one said to his fellow, etc., and the halakhah is like him, etc. The Rosh wrote in Sanhedrin chapter 4 that where two great authorities disagree, etc., as stated in chapter Chezkath HaBatim, etc.; thus, etc. Since it says “and the halakhah,” it implies even if the opposing litigant seized. But this can be rejected. From this emerged the dispute of the Rambam and Shulchan Aruch with the Rosh and Baal HaItur regarding a doubt of law, whether seizure helps or not, each following his approach. The primary law is like the Rambam, as it appears to me.
