And he may eat it. Nevertheless, he is obligated in the priestly gifts in any event. This is specifically with a doubtful firstborn. But where there is uncertainty between two animals as to which one is the firstborn, and he had to give one of them to a kohen, the second is exempt from the gifts, since its substitute is in the kohen's possession; they treated one who has not acquired as though he had acquired, as all this was explained above in siman 61 s.k. 11. We do not extract. For now the Jew is the one seeking to extract, and the burden of proof is upon him. And some say that we extract, etc. Since the Jew at least has an established presumption regarding the benefit of discretion, to give them to whomever he wishes, wherever it is, it remains in his possession, and the kohen is always the one seeking to extract and must bring proof. And so is the halachah. Above in siman 61 seif 23 the Rav did not write that this is the halachah; see there, s.k. 13, what I wrote on this. And even if he gave them, etc. Meaning, not only if the kohen seized them against his will do we extract them from him, but even if he gave them on his own, the kohen must return them to him, for it was a mistaken gift.
