(Siman 131 in Shulchan Aruch seif 1) A Jew who made gentile wine in purity, etc., and a barrel that the wine in it is open to the public domain is permitted, etc. See Shach seif kaf-dalet; and the view of Ran and Rashba in the name of Raavad, that where the gentile does not live in the courtyard it is permitted without a seal — since it is open to the public domain; and it seems to me that everyone concedes this as law — end Shach, see there. And one may say that Tosafot at the end do not hold thus at the end of chapter R. Yishmael 66a s.v. hametaher yeyno shel oved kochavim — they challenge there the explanation of Rashi, who explained that the first part of the Mishnah also deals where he wrote to him "I received it," and without key and seal at all — behold, even Jewish wine is forbidden to leave in his possession without key and seal — see there; therefore he wishes to explain that the first part and latter part both deal with key and seal; and where there is no opening to the public domain, even with key and seal it is forbidden — see there. And if you say he holds that where the gentile does not live there it is permitted — according to me, perhaps the Mishnah deals where the gentile does not live in that house, therefore it is permitted even without a seal; or that Tosafot do not hold thus; but according to me the Mishnah does not deal thus — for if so, the latter part is difficult, which teaches "and if he wrote to him I received it, it is permitted; and if not, forbidden"; and if it dealt in such a case, even without "I received it" it would be permitted; or that it cannot be established in such a case; and always Tosafot hold as Rashba and Ran cited.
