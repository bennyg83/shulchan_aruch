Impedes. And Beit Yosef wrote in the name of a responsum of the Rashba: specifically if he wrote to him "I have received [payment]," or if he extended it as a loan — but otherwise, even if he acquired it from him before witnesses in the manner of non-Jewish transactions where neither party can retract the sale, it is forbidden.
Matter. This applies even to wine of a non-Jew in the domain of a non-Jew who lives there, where the Jew does not live there and the whole city is non-Jewish, because a seal within a seal is like a guard. It appears that a key and seal are considered like a seal within a seal, since they are like a guard. The Bach challenged the Rav, saying he should have written that for us it is permitted even for drinking. This is not a challenge, for in many places the Rav writes similarly according to the law of the Shas, and it is also relevant for us where there is no loss, since it is forbidden in benefit as above siman 124, yet here it is permitted. It further appears that even nowadays the Rav did not wish to be lenient here for drinking, since if it were known that the non-Jew touched it, all would agree that it is forbidden in benefit according to the law of the Shas. Nevertheless, in a case of great loss it is possible to permit it for drinking. Know that throughout this siman, and throughout simanim 129 and 130, where we are concerned that a non-Jew may have touched it, this is specifically where there is reason to suspect that he touched it to drink, exchange, gain some other benefit, or touched it incidentally while handling it. Otherwise, we do not suspect that he touched it for nesech, and it is permitted for drinking in a case of loss, for non-Jews nowadays are not worshippers of avodah zarah, as above siman 128 seif 4 in the gloss. The Rav did not gloss so here because he relied on what he wrote above. Thus far the Shach. The Taz writes that one should not be lenient at all for drinking, but only that one may buy it lechatchilah in order to profit from it.
