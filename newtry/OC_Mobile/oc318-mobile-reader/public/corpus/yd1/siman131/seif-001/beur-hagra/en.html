Whether, etc., whether, etc. Torat HaBayit explains on 146a: 'One who purifies,' etc., means a Jew who purchased grapes, pressed them, and produced his wine in purity, but has not yet paid the non-Jew, so the wine is still regarded as belonging to the non-Jew. It also includes a non-Jew who had his winepress worked by a Jew in order to sell the wine to Jews, and the Jew placed it in that same non-Jew's domain, except that the non-Jew does not live in that very courtyard but in another courtyard, etc. The phrase 'the non-Jew's wine' means that it still belongs to the non-Jew, as Rabbeinu Tam explains in Tosafot there, s.v. There is an authority who says, etc. He explains the Mishnah and baraita as concerning a key or a seal, but with two seals the wine is permitted in every case, for this is equivalent to sitting and guarding. Proof is brought from the discussion on 31a, where Rav said, etc., and Rabbi Yochanan said, etc.; one master holds, etc. If this were not so, the Sages here would prohibit even with two seals and even with wine belonging to a Jew, as stated here: 'The Sages forbid,' etc.
(Likut) There is an authority who says, etc. The Sages here, who refer back to the first clause and say 'until,' etc., are the same Sages as on 31a, as explained above. There the Gemara says according to Rabbi Yochanan that the Sages concede with two seals. Thus, the phrase 'until there is a guard,' etc., is not exact, since two seals also suffice. The same applies to the concluding clause and to the Mishnah, which says 'until he sits and guards'; that too is not exact, since two seals suffice. According to the Rif's approach, where 'a key and a seal' is the case in which the Sages dispute, they nevertheless concede with two seals, and the same applies to the concluding case of one who purifies wine. Tosafot, however, s.v. 'One who purifies,' writes that Rabbi Eliezer makes no distinction between one seal and two seals. Since he forbids in the concluding clause with one seal, he likewise forbids with two. The Rosh writes similarly. As explained above, the baraita necessarily follows Rabbi Eliezer, whether according to the Rif or Tosafot, while only the Sages distinguish. (End of Likut.)
