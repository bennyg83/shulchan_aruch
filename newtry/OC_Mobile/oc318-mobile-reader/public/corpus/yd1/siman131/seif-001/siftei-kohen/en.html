And he did not pay him money. Meaning he did not pay him anything; but if he paid him somewhat such that the Jew has a portion in the wine, it appears from Rashba's responsum that I brought at the end of this siman that it is like a Jew's wine resting in a gentile's possession, which is permitted with a key and seal; and for us, b'di'eved with one seal — see there.
And the Jew lives in the courtyard. For it is considered like a guard, for whom going in and out suffices — so wrote the poskim.
If a Jew lives there. Even another Jew — Beit Yosef and Perishah; but Rashba holds that even if this Jew alone lives in the city, he is not afraid to enter there and falsify — and we specifically require many Jews living there since he fears one of them may see him; and likewise the Ran at the end of the chapter R. Yishmael; and one should be stringent where there is no loss; but for benefit it appears permitted even where there is no loss — since even certain touch by gentiles nowadays is permitted in benefit in a case of loss, as above beginning of siman 123; and further, just as we rely below on sfeik-sfeika and its supporters regarding havla’ah ve-chatumah to permit in benefit — so too here one may rely on the permitting poskim; examine.
And b'di'eved, etc. Meaning: for us it is permitted b'di'eved with one seal, except that l'chatchilah two seals are required; but for the Mechaber, even b'di'eved two seals are required — as above siman 130; and here it deals explicitly even with b'di'eved.
Even with one seal. And with key and seal it is permitted in benefit for us even when not open to the public domain and no Jew lives in the city — as below at the end of the seif in Hagah; and likewise for drinking in a case of great loss.
Even though Jews live in the city. And even many Jews.
And goes out and enters. Even though he stayed a long time — as long as he did not inform him he is sailing, as above beginning of siman 129.
With key and seal. And for us it is permitted with one seal b'di'eved — so proven in the poskim.
Permitted in every manner. Even with gentile wine in a gentile's possession where he lives, and the Jew does not live there, and the entire city is gentile — because havla'ah ve-chatumah is like a guard there; and it is implied from the words of Sefer HaTerumah and his supporters that key and seal is considered havla'ah ve-chatumah, which is like a guard.
To permit it in benefit, etc. Bach challenged the Rav on this: since we hold that every contact of a non-Jew, even intentional, and all stam yeinam, is forbidden only for drinking, as explained at the beginning of siman 123, if so here, where there is doubtful contact, one should rely on Sefer HaTerumah nowadays to permit it even for drinking, end quote. This is no challenge, for in several places the Rav wrote in this manner, and in many places he simply follows the Mechaber, meaning according to the law of the Talmud, where non-Jews were worshippers of avodah zarah; this also has practical effect for us where there is no loss, where it is forbidden in benefit, as above in siman 124 s.k. 71, and nevertheless here it is permitted. It further appears that even nowadays the Rav did not wish to be lenient for drinking by relying on Sefer HaTerumah, but he permits in benefit as Ran wrote in responsum. Although it was explained above in siman 124 s.k. 71 that wherever it is permitted in benefit by the law of the Talmud, for us it is permitted even for drinking, that is where it is clearly permitted by the law of the Talmud, or because most poskim agreed so; unlike here, where it is not permitted in benefit according to everyone, but only because we rely on Sefer HaTerumah, and most poskim do not hold so. Alternatively, and this is primary, it is not comparable to above in siman 124. There we say that wherever it is permitted by law in benefit when he touched it, we descend one level and permit nowadays even for drinking, because non-Jews nowadays are not worshippers of avodah zarah, so their contact is like unintentional contact; and unintentional contact does not forbid drinking even by Talmudic law when that same contact, if intentional, would be permitted in benefit, such as contact of a non-Jew through another object, which is permitted by Talmudic law in benefit. But here, if we knew certainly that he touched it, according to everyone it would be forbidden in benefit by Talmudic law; only we rely on Sefer HaTerumah, or on Raavad who permits doubtful contact in benefit as stated in Ran's responsum, that a seal within a seal is like a guard and he did not touch. If so, what does it help that non-Jews nowadays are not worshippers of avodah zarah? Nevertheless, in a case of great loss perhaps one may permit it for drinking, like Bach's words, for Semag, Hagahot Maimoniyot, Semak, Kol Bo, and other poskim agree with Sefer HaTerumah. Know that throughout this siman, and throughout simanim 129 and 130, where we are concerned that perhaps a non-Jew touched it, this is specifically in a place where there is concern that he touched it to drink, exchange, or for some other benefit, or that he touched it while occupied with it. Otherwise, we are not concerned that he touched for libation, and it is permitted for drinking in a case of loss, since non-Jews nowadays are not worshippers of avodah zarah, as above in siman 128 seif 4 in the gloss; see there in s.k. 4. The Rav did not gloss so because he relied on what he wrote above.
