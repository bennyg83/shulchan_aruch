(There in Hagah) And there are those who say that with a seal within a seal, etc. And this is according to Rashi whom I brought above nearby, who explained the first part of "one who purifies wine" — it deals without key and seal, only that he said "I received it"; but with a key it is permitted even if not open to the public domain, and in a city that is mostly gentiles. And behold, this that requires key and seal that he explained specifically — that is according to Rif and Tosafot, who hold that even with a gentile one needs a seal within a seal — even according to R. Eliezer — as the Mechaber wrote above siman 118 seif 1; unlike what Rama wrote there in Hagah, that with a gentile one needs only one seal — according to Rabeinu Tam — see there. If so, in "one who purifies wine" one does not need a seal within a seal; if so, it is difficult for Rama, who wrote here that one needs a seal within a seal. And one may say: behold, Tosafot wrote according to Rashi — and how would it be permitted to leave in a gentile's house without any seal, etc.; and Rashi must hold like Rabeinu Tam, that generally one seal suffices; and the first part of the Mishnah deals with one seal; therefore if he wrote to him "I received it" with one seal — if open to the public domain and a city where Jews and gentiles live there; but if it is mostly gentiles, or not open to the public domain, it is forbidden — meaning one seal does not help; but a seal within a seal helps; and this is what Rama wrote here regarding one who purifies wine — specifically one needs a seal within a seal — and according to me.
