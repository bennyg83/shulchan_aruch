The Shach wrote: they said this only regarding one who purifies the wine of a non-Jew that grew in his own vineyard, who did not trouble himself much over it, nor incur expenses for it, and does not worry greatly if the Jew should discover him falsifying; but when he conveys wine a great distance with great effort and many expenses, and also purchased it — he is concerned about his effort and expense and will not falsify. Rosh and Tur. And Bach wrote: where the custom is to permit in such a case with one seal, the custom stands.

We are concerned. This is unlike one who purifies the wine of a non-Jew, where a seal within a seal does not permit it, for here it is different: since the Jew has a partnership in it, the non-Jew is afraid and would be caught as a thief. For us, since we hold that one seal suffices, even if the key is not in the Jew's hand it is permitted. The Rav required the key to be in the Jew's hand because in that manner it is permitted even lechatchilah. It seems to me that even if the wine was placed in the house of the non-Jewish partner, it is permitted, for since the Jew has a partnership in it, it is like wine of a Jew placed in a non-Jew's house, which is permitted bedieved for us with one seal. Thus far the Shach. The Taz likewise writes that here it is permitted in a case of great loss with one seal, but he writes that there is a scribal error in the Rama's words and the text should read: a Jew and a non-Jew who have wine in partnership, and the Jew purified it and placed it in the house of another non-Jew. For according to the text before us it is difficult: the seller is not afraid at all, so it should require specifically a guard, and how does the language of purification apply to the non-Jew such that the Rama used the plural "they purified it"? Rather, the text must be corrected as stated.
