They said only regarding one who purifies his gentile's wine that grew in his vineyard who did not trouble himself much and did not expend much, and is not so concerned if the Israelite senses he falsified; but when he transports wine far with great trouble and much expense, and also bought it considering his trouble and expense and did not falsify—Rosh and Tur. Bach wrote: although by law for Taz there is no distinction between one seal and two seals, nevertheless here it is permitted only with two seals as Tosafot imply; and from his words there it implies where the custom is to permit in such a case with one seal, that is the custom. And see responsum of Mahari Bei Rav part 2 siman 208.
The wine of the gentile. And it deals with where the Jew paid his share to the other gentile, or he wrote to him "I received it" such that when the Jew comes he cannot hold it from him — for it is as the Jew's wine; and as I wrote in the next s.k.
And we are not concerned about the non-Jew's partnership. This is not comparable to one who purifies a non-Jew's wine, which is not permitted with only a seal and a key. Here it is different, because since the Jew has a partnership with the non-Jew, the non-Jew is afraid and would be caught as a thief. The Sages spoke only of one who purifies wine that belongs entirely to the non-Jew, where he has no such fear and even if he touches it he would not be caught as a thief. Furthermore, where the Jew rents a house from the non-Jew who lives in the courtyard on condition that the key remain in the Jew's possession, the non-Jewish partner is considered, relative to that house, as being within the Jew's domain. Although the non-Jewish landlord lives in the courtyard, we are not concerned that he will allow his non-Jewish partner to touch the wine, for even where one purifies a non-Jew's wine and places it in the house of another non-Jew who is not the owner of the wine, it is permitted with a seal and key. All the more so here. End of the Rashba's responsum, cited by Darkei Moshe. It appears that the Rashba required the key to be in the Jew's possession only because he required both a seal and a key for deposited wine, in accordance with his view. According to us, however, who rule that one seal suffices, even if the key is not in the Jew's possession it is permitted, because since the Jew has a partnership with the non-Jew, the non-Jewish partner is afraid and will not touch the wine. Moreover, as explained above in seif katan 10, even wine belonging to a non-Jew that was placed in the house of another non-Jew is permitted according to us with one seal, all the more so here. Accordingly, it appears to me that the Rama's requirement that the key be in the Jew's possession is only so that the arrangement is permitted l'chatchila, because l'chatchila a key and seal are required, as above in siman 130. It also appears to me that even if the wine is kept in the house of the non-Jewish partner it is permitted, for the reason stated by the Rashba: since the Jew has a partnership with the non-Jew, the non-Jew is afraid and will not touch it. Olat Tamid likewise mentions only this reason, and it appears that this is why the Rama states the law without specifying where the wine is kept. Accordingly, it is like wine belonging to a Jew that is kept in a non-Jew's house, which according to us is permitted b'dieved with one seal. So it appears to me.
