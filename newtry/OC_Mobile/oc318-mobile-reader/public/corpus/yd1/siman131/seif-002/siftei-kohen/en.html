Permitted. They said only regarding one who purifies his gentile's wine that grew in his vineyard who did not trouble himself much and did not expend much, and is not so concerned if the Israelite senses he falsified; but when he transports wine far with great trouble and much expense, and also bought it considering his trouble and expense and did not falsify—Rosh and Tur. Bach wrote: although by law for Taz there is no distinction between one seal and two seals, nevertheless here it is permitted only with two seals as Tosafot imply; and from his words there it implies where the custom is to permit in such a case with one seal, that is the custom. And see responsum of Mahari Bei Rav part 2 siman 208.
The wine of the gentile. And it deals with where the Jew paid his share to the other gentile, or he wrote to him "I received it" such that when the Jew comes he cannot hold it from him — for it is as the Jew's wine; and as I wrote in the next s.k.
And we are not concerned for the partnership of the non-Jew. This is not comparable to purifying the wine of a non-Jew, where a seal within a seal does not permit. Here is different: since the Jew has a partnership with the non-Jew, the non-Jew is afraid and would be caught as a thief. They said otherwise only regarding purifying wine that belongs entirely to a non-Jew, where he is not afraid, and even if he touched it he would not be caught as a thief. Furthermore, where one rents a house from a non-Jew who lives in the courtyard, on condition that the key be in the Jew's hand, the non-Jewish partner is, as to him, in the Jew's domain; and although the non-Jew landlord lives in the courtyard, we are not concerned that he will let his non-Jewish partner touch it. For if one purifies a non-Jew's wine and places it in the house of another non-Jew who is not the owner of the wine, it is permitted with a seal within a seal; all the more here, end quote from Rashba's responsum, which Darkei Moshe brings. It appears that he required the key to be in the Jew's hand only so that it be a seal within a seal, and he follows his own view that with a deposit one requires a seal within a seal. But for us, who hold that one seal suffices, even if the key is not in the Jew's hand it is permitted, since the Jew has a partnership with the non-Jew and the non-Jewish partner is afraid and does not touch. Furthermore, it was already explained in s.k. 10 that even wine of a non-Jew placed in the house of another non-Jew is permitted for us with one seal; all the more here. According to this, it appears to me that the Rav required the key to be in the Jew's hand because in such a case it is permitted even ab initio, for ab initio we require a key and a seal, as above in siman 130. It also seems to me that even if the wine was placed in the house of his non-Jewish partner, it is permitted for the reason Rashba wrote: since the Jew has a partnership with the non-Jew, he is afraid and does not touch. Ateret Zekenim wrote only this reason, and it appears that for this reason the Rav wrote simply and did not explain where the wine was placed. Therefore it is like a Jew's wine placed in the house of a non-Jew, and after the fact it is permitted for us with one seal, as it appears to me.
