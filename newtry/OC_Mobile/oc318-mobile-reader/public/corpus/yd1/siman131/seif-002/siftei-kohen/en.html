[And we are not concerned for the gentile's partnership. For it is not comparable to one who purifies gentile wine — which is not permitted in havla'ah ve-chatumah; here it is different — since the Jew has partnership, the gentile trembles and is caught as a thief; and they spoke only regarding one who purifies wine that belongs entirely to the gentile, who does not tremble; and even if he touched, he is not caught as a thief; and further, in renting a house from a gentile who lives in the courtyard with intent that the key be in the Jew's hand — behold he is relative to the gentile partner in the Jew's possession; and even though the gentile lessor lives in the courtyard, we are not concerned he will leave his gentile partner to touch it — for one who purifies gentile wine and gave it to another gentile's house who is not the owner of the wine is permitted in havla'ah ve-chatumah; all the more so here — until here the language of Rashba's responsum, and Darkei Moshe brings it; and it seems he did not require the key be in the Jew's hand except because there would be havla'ah ve-chatumah; and according to his reasoning — for he holds that with a deposit we require havla'ah ve-chatumah; but for us, who hold one seal suffices — if so, even if the key is not in the Jew's hand it is permitted; for since the Jew has partnership with the gentile, the gentile partner trembles and did not touch; and further, it was already explained in seif 10 that even gentile wine given in another gentile's house is permitted for us with one seal; and it seems to me that even if the wine rested in the house of the gentile partner it is permitted — for the reason Rashba wrote: since the Jew has partnership with the gentile, he trembles and did not touch (and likewise Atzei Chayim wrote only this reason;
