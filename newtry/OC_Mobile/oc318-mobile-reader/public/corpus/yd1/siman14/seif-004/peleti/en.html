And if it cut—this too is unresolved and there is practical difference between doubt and certainty; and one cut, etc., until only minority remained in the animal, and that minority majority of limb outside—there is to permit from force of double doubt that birth did not apply to minority of limb remaining inside, if we say there was birth for we follow majority, perhaps majority was not born—for majority is in one eye and this is cut and stands. And if so, permitted; and from this reason they wondered on Rif and Rambam who omitted this unresolved question "limb came out and was cut"—though ostensibly unstam like the explanation that even though they did not cite, nevertheless we judge stringently, and from such reasoning leniently seems reasonable, and if they cited also stringently there is no practical difference—and according to what I wrote it is fine. And behold I have a doubt regarding its head that came out cut—whether it is yelud or perhaps specifically its majority as we say "its majority" in one case we require, unlike head. And behold our great rabbi Maharsha at beginning of chapter Behemah HaMekishah that Tosafot wrote on complete offspring whether yelud even if inside was yelud, and in cutting it is the unresolved question below, etc., and Maharsha wrote: in this whole one that came out partially where there is only pressing and nevertheless what is inside is yelud—it must be preferable to cutting—end of his words; and from this it is proved he holds even for head the unresolved question of the Gemara applies, otherwise Maharsha would not challenge anything—and see there he was precise from Tosafot's words. However from Rashi it appears the opposite—Rashi in Bava Kamma daf 11: afterbirth that part came out is forbidden—explained we are concerned perhaps in that part there was majority of offspring; and so Rosh and Rashba in chiddushim to Bava Kamma: perhaps in that part was its head or its majority. And Maharam of Lublin challenged: if so what does the Gemara challenge in Chullin our mishnah "its head too we taught siman and offspring, etc."—what difficulty, there the reason is because of its majority; and he left it requiring study. And in chiddushim I wrote many things. But in plainness of the sugya it must be that there it deals with R' Eliezer and there is an establishment in the Gemara in Niddah chapter HaMapelet that R' Eliezer holds like Shmuel that head does not exempt in miscarriages; and the reason must be because of its majority; and if so Maharsha's challenge in its place—what will its majority help, behold it was dissolved like cutting. But this is not so—for truly for R' Eliezer who holds head does not exempt, etc., the unresolved question applies even in cutting it is yelud. But the unresolved question in Chullin goes to conclusion that we hold head exempts in miscarriages; and if so one may say that of afterbirth that part came out is because of departure of its head, but not because of its majority; and for head there is no practical difference in cutting and in majority we are doubtful; and if so the Gemara's challenge at beginning of their chapter also on this revolves—cutting does not nullify majority of that afterbirth that is not because of its head—what does the mishnah teach us, we taught afterbirth, etc.; and the Gemara's words are correct and clear, and not like Maharsha. However in this I thought to resolve the reason Rambam omitted this unresolved question "cut it, etc."—why is it difficult for Rambam that Tosafot's challenges why does the Gemara challenge at beginning of Behemah HaMekishah "its head we taught afterbirth, etc."—perhaps there because we decree part of it because of all of it. And Shmuel's answer who holds head does not exempt in miscarriages—that afterbirth part came out—how establish it, and to say we are concerned its majority came out—Rambam does not hold like this, as appears Tosafot too do not hold, and the reason because "part came out" we taught even a slight thing where there is no doubt in majority of offspring (and in chiddushim I answered Maharam's challenge on Rashi that it must be part of a slight thing, for otherwise what does it teach us that afterbirth is forbidden—behold that pressing that came out is certainly forbidden and mixed with pressing inside and plainly forbidden)—end of his words—that something very slight came out until mixture is nullified in majority; but according to those who say if it returned the limb it is permitted, one may say it deals with that afterbirth that part came out, it returned it, and there is no concern of mixture, only therefore forbidden lest there be concern for birth. And if so in Bava Kamma for R' Eliezer who holds head does not exempt—we must be concerned for departure of majority; and if so only a little came out, for otherwise anyway forbidden because of mixture; one may say R' Eliezer holds if it returned it is permitted, but at beginning of HaMekishah the challenge is for Rav who holds the limb itself is forbidden—if so not needed thus, and one must say something slight came out and only concern for head—and examine. To resolve this, Rambam holds at end of chapter HaMekishah amoraim disputed this afterbirth that came out, etc.—some say specifically there is no offspring with it but there is—we are not concerned for offspring; and some say, etc.; and it is taught like my view—even if there is, nevertheless forbidden; and Rambam holds: granted if there is no offspring with it, which is certain offspring, fine to say part because of all, for all that came out is certain prohibition; unlike if there is offspring with it, which is only doubt how to decree part because of all—for all that came out its body is only doubt of offspring, and we do not decree so much; and if so fine Shmuel who holds head does not exempt holds in the mishnah the reason is part because of all; and he truly holds as those who say it deals where there is no other offspring with it; but sugya of HaMekishah comes to conclusion as taught that there is other offspring with it; and if so not needed part, etc., as I wrote—and therefore the reason we are concerned its head came out, and fine it challenges—and examine. If so Rambam's words are also correct that he holds the unresolved question in Gemara regarding cutting was resolved; and the challenge from Maharsha from afterbirth part came out does not challenge him—for in afterbirth we decree part because of all that certainly like yelud even if completely dissolved. And this is all according to those who hold there is no other offspring with the afterbirth; but according to what we hold as taught that even if there is offspring nevertheless forbidden—if so not needed part because of all; and if so the reason it is forbidden is we are concerned its head came out; and learn from this that even in cutting it is yelud; and this unresolved question was resolved; and therefore Rambam omitted—and examine. And it is clarified for us from this conclusion that also in Rosh this unresolved question applies like Maharsha, and certainly resolved stringently; and in any case there is no substance in the words of the author of Pri Toar that his view is to be lenient plainly if the whole offspring was cut into limbs and all limbs inside were not forbidden—and this is error, for aside from afterbirth that came out, etc., it is difficult for him as above, also this mishnah "came out cut, etc."—and though Tosafot wrote to distinguish what remained inside only to clarify the doubt of the author of the unresolved question, but heaven forbid to decide to be lenient; and his words are void words to permit prohibition—heaven forbid—and examine.
