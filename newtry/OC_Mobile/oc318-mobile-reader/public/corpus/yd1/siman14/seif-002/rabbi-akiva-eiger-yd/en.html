(Shach s.k. 12) That it should only be a doubt whether there are sixty. But if certainly there are not sixty, one may not permit it by law of rabbinic doubt because of doubt in the unresolved question whether milk is permitted—for since milk from force of outgoing limb is Torah doubt, as Taz and Shach wrote (siman 66 and siman 110); in Pri Megadim he wrote on this: even though Maharival wrote where there is Torah doubt and afterward it rolled that it is rabbinic—we do not say rabbinic doubt leniently—one must say since at beginning of its creation it was in mixture, fine it is rabbinic doubt. And Turei Zahav challenged: also when certainly there are not sixty to permit, it is rabbinic doubt from force of unresolved question—see there in Machatzit HaShekel that he erred in this, for what relevance does this have to Maharival's law, for there certainly there is no measure of rabbinic nullification, only it comes to say since from Torah nullified in majority it is rabbinic doubt lest it not be treifah—in this we say since doubt of treifah in its bones is Torah doubt, it requires nullification like certain Torah prohibition—this is called rolling of rabbinic; but here the doubt whether there are sixty that also rabbinically nullified—this is like nispach that we are lenient lest there were sixty, and it is very plain; and automatically the Turei Zahav's challenge is removed that if certainly there are not sixty, the doubt of unresolved question does not help—we judge it rabbinic doubt, which is truly like Maharival's law. Only Turei Zahav challenged thus from reasoning, since at beginning of its creation it was majority and there was no reality of Torah prohibition—it is fit to say rabbinic doubt leniently.
