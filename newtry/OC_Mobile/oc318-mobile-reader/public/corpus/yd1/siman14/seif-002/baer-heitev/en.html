Forbidden. Because it is said "and flesh in the field, treifah, you shall not eat," and our Sages expounded: once flesh went outside its partition—that is, the womb which is the partition of the fetus—it is forbidden.
The cut. Meaning: the distinction between outer and inner. Beit Yosef wrote: and we practice that we cut until the place where the bone ends. And so with a broken bone where there is no treifah in the body of the behemah or fowl—in any case that limb is forbidden—we cut the entire limb.
Everything. And the Shach explained: specifically if it gave birth before shechitah; but after shechitah—even if born through the birth canal—it is forbidden; and with this what was left in Darkei Moshe and Perishah in Beit Yosef's words is resolved. Beit Yosef wrote: specifically that it returned the limb before its mother's shechitah; but otherwise, even though it gave birth afterward—that limb with the place of cut is forbidden as law of dangling limb (eiver meduldal), since this limb remained dangling when it came out from its mother's womb—it still stands in prohibition of dangling limb and is not permitted through its own shechitah. And Peri Chadash disagrees.
