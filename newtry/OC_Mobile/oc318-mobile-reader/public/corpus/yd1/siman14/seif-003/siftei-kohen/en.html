And if half came out with majority of a limb. For example, most of its hand came out; and when one estimates what came out from the fetus, it is only half; and if one adds the minority of the hand that remained inside after the majority of the hand that came out, it becomes majority—we rule on the minority of the hand inside after the majority of the hand that came out to complete the majority of the fetus, and it is forbidden—end of Tur; and this law is an unresolved question in the Talmud and was not resolved, and the poskim ruled stringently; therefore the Tur wrote in siman 319 regarding firstborn that the one born after it is a doubtful firstborn; and therefore this is clear that its own shechitah does not help it either, lest it not be considered yelud; but what remained inside is permitted through its own shechitah in any case—if yelud, plainly it is permitted; and if not yelud, it is permitted through its mother's shechitah, for it did not come out at all. And if it is nine months dead or eight months alive—we are concerned lest it be yelud and all is forbidden, even what is inside; and if nine months alive, what came outside is forbidden lest it not be yelud and it is flesh that went outside its partition and was forbidden and its own shechitah does not help it either since it was not born before its mother's shechitah; but what remained inside is permitted in any case—even if its mother was treifah, through its own shechitah, as explained in siman 13; and even if it did not return what it brought out, what remained inside is permitted in any case. And what the Tur wrote in seif 4 "and if it cut each limb when it brought it out, even the minority inside is forbidden"—there, since it cut most of its limbs, it cannot be permitted through its own shechitah; or it deals with eight months or nine months dead—and see Perishah who wrote similarly.
Or if most of it came out with minority of a limb, etc. It is plain in the Talmud that it is yelud; and the Mechaber who wrote "in one case it is yelud" does not mean to equate them entirely, and he relied on what he wrote below siman 319: if most came out with minority of limb it is certainly yelud; and with half with majority of limb it is doubtful—and it is astonishing on the Pri Megadim who wrote at the beginning and end that the reason is because we go stringently—whereas certainly this is not so except when most came out with minority of limb, which is plainly yelud; and therefore when it is nine months alive all is permitted through its own shechitah specifically; and when nine months dead or eight months alive—all is forbidden, even what is inside—and see in the s.k. before this.
Most with minority of limb. Thus the Tur: we do not say to add minority of limb that came out after majority of limb inside; rather we add it after majority of fetus that came out, and it is forbidden—end of his words; and so Rashi; and it appears his words are not precise when he says "after majority of fetus," for without this minority of limb outside there is no majority of fetus; and it seems he calls it "majority of fetus" by its end; and the same if inside was more than outside without the minority of limb—we rule on the fetus outside, since with the minority of limb with it there is majority of fetus outside; and so it appears from the Tur below siman 319—and examine.
