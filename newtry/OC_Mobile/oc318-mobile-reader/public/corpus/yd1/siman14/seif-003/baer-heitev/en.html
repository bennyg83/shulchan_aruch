Limb. And the Shach explained: for example, most of its hand came out; and when one estimates what came out from the fetus, it is only half; and if one adds minority of hand inside after majority of hand that came out, it becomes majority—we rule on minority of hand inside after majority of hand that came out to complete majority of fetus, and forbidden; and this law is unresolved in the Talmud and poskim ruled stringently; therefore the Tur wrote in siman 319 regarding firstborn that the one after is doubtful firstborn; and therefore this is clear that its own shechitah does not help, lest it not be yelud; but what remained inside is permitted through its own shechitah in any case—if yelud, plainly permitted; if not yelud, permitted through mother's shechitah, for it did not come out at all.
Yelud. And the Shach wrote: this is plain in the Talmud that it is yelud; and the Mechaber who wrote everything in one case does not mean to equate them fully, but when most came out with minority of limb it is certainly yelud; and with half with majority of limb it is doubtful; and the same if inside was more than outside without minority of limb—we rule on fetus outside.
