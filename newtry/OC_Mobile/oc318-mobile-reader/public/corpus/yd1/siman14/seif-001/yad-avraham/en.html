(Siman 14 seif 4) And if it cut each limb when it brought it out—that too the minority inside is forbidden. This is unresolved in the Talmud at beginning of HaMekishah: do we require its majority at once or not, and it ruled stringently that it is yelud. And it requires study—for what does it need to resolve for him from that which we taught there (daf 69) regarding firstborn: majority came out, behold yelud and exempt from firstborn. And it is proved there explicitly in Talmud, Rashi, Tosafot, Rambam, and Tur siman 319—that it applies whether majority came out at once or limb by limb—see there. And one must say there is nothing to resolve from firstborn—for regarding opening the womb the Merciful One depended, and behold the first womb opened with its majority and exempts what comes after. See Rashi there. But regarding fetus that depends on birth, perhaps nevertheless minority inside is not yelud. And similarly Tosafot wrote there (daf 68b) s.v. "brought out," etc., and at beginning (daf 70)—see there. With this the wonder of Tosafot at beginning of HaMekishah s.v. "the reason, etc." is resolved—why did the Talmud not resolve that departure of head is yelud from what it taught regarding firstborn. And according to what I wrote to reconcile, and therefore he inferred from firstborn to inheritance that it did not depend on opening womb alone but that it be viable offspring, and learn that all of it is yelud; otherwise behold the head alone is not fit to be viable offspring—and see there the sugya and you will understand what I wrote is clear.
