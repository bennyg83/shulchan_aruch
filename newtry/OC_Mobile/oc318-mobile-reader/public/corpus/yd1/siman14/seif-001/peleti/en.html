The animal straining to give birth—the Gemara mentioned "straining" to teach that it deals with a dead fetus, and teaches us that the head is like birth even in miscarriages; therefore it teaches "straining"—not that on its own it brought out the offspring/limb, for it is dead, only strain and pains brought it out in the order of strain without assistance from the animal. And from this is understood what Maharsha wrote there in Tosafot: why does the Gemara challenge—what does it teach us, perhaps it teaches us that even in miscarriages the head exempts; and answer: there is no resolution in the mishnah, lest it deal with its head alive and viable offspring. And this is not so—for from the mishnah it teaches thus, otherwise the Gemara's answer is difficult, why do we not learn behemah from adam regarding head exempts, etc. From this it is clear in explanation that the head is yelud; and it must be that here it teaches even for miscarriages the head exempts; and the mishnah here in any case deals with miscarriages for the reason I wrote, or possibly the mishnah is unstam R' Meir, and for R' Meir viable offspring—shechitah does not permit at all. In any case it is proved not like Maharsha that the mishnah here certainly deals with miscarriage.
