And behold the rabbi author of Mishneh Lamelech in Laws of Tumat Tzaraat argued: if so it is rabbinic doubt—certainly in majority it is nullified; if so why did it rule teiku stringently that forbids and did not rule leniently that milk does not forbid; and therefore he proved the essence is like Mordechai in chapter Zeroa Beshela, etc., who holds where permission is immediately mixed with prohibition like yevamah who spat blood that is immediately mixed with saliva droplets and the like—nullification does not apply; and this too is similar, for immediately when milk prohibition and permission are mixed, nullification does not apply here. And he also brought in name of the comprehensive rabbi R' Chaim Alexandri that his view is the milk stands first separately in each and every limb, and there the milk is squeezed and afterward all descend into the udder—see there; and Mishneh Lamelech argued: if so the difficulty returns to its place—why did Rambam doubt this unresolved question stringently—see there. And behold what he argues that Rambam should have ruled leniently in rabbinic doubt is not a decisive matter to reject him—requires study; see Avnei Nezer siman 337 who brought in name of Hagahot Maimoniyot that a matter that arose in the Talmud as teiku—even for rabbinic prohibition we go stringently; and so later authorities in many places; and here it is teiku and stringently; and also since the prohibition substance is Torah-level only through mixtures it became rabbinic doubt—we do not say in it rabbinic doubt we do not say rabbinic doubt leniently as rabbinic doubt—see below general rules of double doubt in Shach s.k. 19 who wrote so in name of Darkei Moshe. (And possibly Mishneh Lamelech holds if prohibition stands separately and then it is Torah doubt to be stringent only after it mixed, its doubt does not go; but here it is not in reality that it will be separately, and if so immediately it is rabbinic prohibition—this forced distinction on Rambam is strained.) And if so the author of Mishneh Lamelech labored in vain. And truly the words of the rabbi R' Chaim may need us to say nevertheless that not from all limbs they come together into the udder and there milk is made—if so it is zeh v'zeh gorem kosher limb and treifah limb—and see Tosafot s.v. here "three," etc.; and see chiddushim of Maharam Lublin. But according to that rabbi, behold the milk stood separately in the forbidden limb and afterward descended into the udder and was absorbed in the other limb—they called it chalav; behold prohibition and permission mixed—and what relevance does zeh v'zeh gorem have; and this is what Rambam was precise in his clear language "and this is like flesh of treifah mixed, etc."—lest you say zeh v'zeh gorem and permitted; and he says not only mixture of complete milk. And in his words there is further side to permit: when treifah milk descends from forbidden limb into udder and in it milk from other limbs one by one nullified, and in min b'mino we do not say it revives. And behold I have difficulty in the Gemara that challenges and asks what is sucking its milk—even though there is prohibition of limb from the live animal—if you do not say the Merciful One permitted, see there. And it does not say all milk in general comes from milk and blood and the Merciful One permitted—and the same for this prohibition of limb from live animal—and why generally two and here three—for the Gemara's concern there is no milk prohibition in ben pakuah like R' Yehudah that its milk is permitted. And it is a great difficulty for me, for I did not find the commentators sensed it. And I would have said that certainly the whole question of the Gemara is on its milk, certainly nullified in the animal min b'mino in majority nullified, and the other is nispach as Shach wrote, and as I wrote perhaps one by one nullified—only this is when milk already came out and without doubt was nullified; but how was it permitted l'chatchila to leave the forbidden limb from which prohibited milk came out and it be nullified in permitted milk—is this not nullifying prohibition l'chatchila; and what difference between leaving forbidden piece for pot that its emission be nullified, and this case—certainly if impossible to cut the limb from the animal to discard it, plainly permitted for it was nullified and what should he do, and it is like prohibition that fell into permitted pot; but if in his hand to cut the limb and not leave it that milk flow from it—also plainly forbidden, for it is nullifying prohibition; and on this revolves the unresolved question in the Gemara "what is sucking its milk"—if forbidden, forbidden to leave the limb that its milk flow and be nullified, for it is nullifying prohibition; or leave the milk to forbid it; or cut the limb—and we conclude as doubt stringently; and this is what Rambam wrote "and the milk is forbidden to drink, since it comes from all limbs in which there is prohibition; and this is like milk of treifah, etc."—if so if he drinks he nullifies prohibition by hand—for if permitted to leave to nullify piece to which milk adheres without cutting it that it mix in pot—is this not nullifying prohibition; and what difference whether he leaves limb—behold nullifying prohibition; and forbidden to drink the milk even if nullified—and clear. But this if possible to cut and remove; but if impossible, plainly not nullifying prohibition and permitted; therefore the Gemara did not bring from milk and blood—for this is not in category of nullifying prohibition, and when he removes from animal its milk and blood, and if so plainly permitted for afterward nullified in majority, and they did not decree further; but from this limb it is fine to ask—for behold in his hand to cut it, and it is nullifying prohibition like milk adhering to piece.
