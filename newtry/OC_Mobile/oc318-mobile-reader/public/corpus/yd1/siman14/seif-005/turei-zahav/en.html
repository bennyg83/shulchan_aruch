From all the limbs, etc. This is an unresolved question in the Gemara: shall we say that since all milk in general is like eiver min hachai and nevertheless permitted, here too no difference; or perhaps there it has a remedy for its prohibition through shechitah, here it has no remedy through shechitah (and see what I wrote in siman 24 seif 15)—and it was not resolved, and we rule stringently. And it appears clear to me regarding ben pakuah that came on a regular animal, that the offspring has no remedy, as in siman 13 seif 4, that milk of that offspring is also forbidden to drink—for behold all of it is prohibition, since half is slaughtered and it is like anything slaughtered half literally. And that it did not ask regarding offspring of ben pakuah that came on the animal—that law that the offspring has no remedy is from Rav Mesharshia according to the view that we are concerned for the father's seed, and it is a dispute among amoraim in chapter Oto V'et Beno—therefore it is fine to ask regarding issur yotzei, which is according to all; but for practical law certainly one reason applies—that there is no remedy for that offspring's prohibition through shechitah; this seems plain to me, except I saw in my humble opinion he wrote from this challenge that it did not ask regarding ben pakuah we mentioned—that there in truth the milk is permitted, for although the offspring has no remedy through shechitah because of shehiyah, it is only that shechitah does not permit it, but while alive there is no prohibition, for it has no trace of treifah—and he compared it to uprooted tree that Beit Yosef brought below where there is no prohibition but only shechitah is not effective—end of his words. And for practical law this is difficult: for Rashi explicitly in chapter Behemah HaMekishah (Chullin daf 69) regarding ben pakuah that engendered—forbidden is all offspring—behold he calls it forbidden, and how is its milk permitted, for it comes from prohibition; and further proof: we say there in the Gemara what is the law regarding its seed—that issur yotzei—how is it: if it goes to a superior animal, why particularly this that has issur yotzei—even regular ben pakuah too, for Rav Mesharshia said according to those who are concerned for father's seed, ben pakuah that came on a superior animal—that offspring has no remedy, etc.; and if you say this "has no remedy" has no prohibition—how compare it to issur yotzei regarding offspring that comes from it? They are not similar—for in issur yotzei there is prohibition, in ben pakuah there is no prohibition, only no permission of shechitah; rather one must equate them literally, that as in issur yotzei truly forbidden, so in ben pakuah; and as at first he compared them to forbid the offspring, so in conclusion the offspring is permitted only regarding milk one must equate, and what prohibition is in this is in that—as I wrote, clearly the offspring that has no remedy is fully forbidden and its milk forbidden. And what does it challenge "why particularly, etc."—from that of Rav Mesharshia? We said above that it is fine to formulate according to all; one may say: since it is relevant to ask according to all like Rav Mesharshia, therefore it is preferable to ask according to all, unlike here where there is clear prohibition like Rav Mesharshia—one cannot say such a question, since we find clear prohibition in this.
And behold this is like milk of treifah that was mixed, etc. This language is superfluous and indicates apparently that nullification in sixty applies here; and so my humble opinion: if there were sixty in the animal in flesh, bones, and gidim against the forbidden limb, all milk is permitted because of mixtures nullified in sixty. And in my humble opinion one should not be lenient—for it is known that limbs are not equal regarding blood in them, for there is a limb with mostly flesh and minority bone—then there is more blood, and vice versa; and so regarding milk coming from blood in limbs—and one cannot estimate this in sixty; and so I saw in Perishah; and further proof from siman 86 seif 5 that we require sixty-one eggs because not all eggs are equal—and this matter is similar, since one cannot ascertain the calculation; and as written siman 81 seif 2 that there is a distinction in this. The superfluous language here in Shulchan Aruch according to Rambam indicates another matter: for in the Gemara at first it was an unresolved question regarding seed born from that issur yotzei whether forbidden because it comes from prohibition or not, and we conclude that from force we do not say and it is permitted; only it is a question regarding milk to drink it—therefore we hear from Rambam he did not err to say the milk comes from force of limbs, for so the language implies at first "since it comes from all the limbs"—if so it would be like from force of limbs, which is not so, for all force is permitted; rather the milk comes literally in substance from the limbs as if nursing milk before us; and that he wrote "and this is like milk of treifah mixed, etc." means milk literally mixed, not only force like matter of offspring born—as above, correct.
