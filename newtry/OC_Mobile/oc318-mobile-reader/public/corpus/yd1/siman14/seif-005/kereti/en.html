Its milk is forbidden; and the same for what we hold—ben pakuah that came on a regular animal and engendered—that offspring has no remedy; milk of that offspring is forbidden, as milk of treifah—not like the Bach who compared it to uprooted tree that milk is permitted—the Taz and Shach.
Like milk of treifah that was mixed—the Bach's view: if according to visual estimate in the animal there is sixty against the outgoing limb, permitted—for presumably in the milk too there is sixty and it is nullified. Perishah and Taz forbid—for not all limbs are equal in affecting milk, one increases and one decreases. Shach and so Peri Chadash wrote: since in majority certainly they are nullified, only doubt whether there are sixty—like case of nispach below siman 98; and see Peleti what I wrote, that otherwise there is double doubt, as Maharshal wrote.
Treifah, etc.—lest you say it is only force and it is zeh v'zeh gorem and permitted—he teaches us it is not so, but it is like prohibition mixed in permission; and see Peleti.
In kosher milk—see Peleti that I raised: if the outgoing limb is such that one can cut it from the animal and it will not be rendered treifah thereby, then its milk is forbidden—for this is nullifying prohibition l'chatchila; but if impossible, the milk is permitted if one may say there is doubt here, as the Shach wrote.
