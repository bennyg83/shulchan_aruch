"Were crushed." The Torat Chatat wrote that it is implied in the Issur ve'Heter that the ruling that a beriah is nullified if it was crushed means that no significant item remained there; but if a piece worthy of honor remained, even though it was nullified due to the rule of beriah, nevertheless it is still considered significant and is not nullified due to the rule of a piece worthy of honor — and this is a correct reasoning, end of his words. (As is also written [there]: and correspondingly, what commonly falls off slightly through cooking is not considered crushing; but if it separated slightly from its body and from its spine or its head, that is considered crushing. And crushing is effective for any significant item, except for something that has a permitted aspect (davar she'yesh lo matirin) — see there.)
"Of their own kind." The Taz explained: for example, a pot of vegetables in which a beriah was lost — everything is prohibited. And see below, siman 102, seif 1.
