This etc. — Beriah was explained in the preceding siman; and piece worthy of honor (chaticha ha'ra'uya l'hitchabed) is as stated in the aforementioned Tosefta: 'a forbidden piece that became mixed among [other] pieces — even if they number a thousand, they are all forbidden' — and the gravy [is assessed] by noten taam (imparting flavor). If it dissolved, it [is assessed] by noten taam. And in Yevamot chapter 1, daf 2, [the case of] when it dissolved. See Tosafot there, s.v. 'divrei etc.,' and in the Mishnah, chapter 2 of Orlah: 'how [is it if] they were crushed etc.'
Until it is lost etc. — This applies only to piece worthy of honor (chaticha ha'ra'uya l'hitchabed), as stated in the preceding siman.
And even if etc. — Gittin 54b: 'they fell [and were] crushed etc.' See Rashi there, s.v. 'naflu etc.'
And even after etc. — From the fact that it states there [the rule concerning one who acted] deliberately etc. And as noted in siman 99, seif 5, in the Rama's gloss. See there.
And that is when they were crushed etc. — Gittin there.
To crush etc., and likewise etc. — As noted in siman 99, seif 5. See there.
And there is no distinction etc. — The location of this Rama's gloss is at the beginning of this siman. That is to say, a piece worthy of honor (chaticha ha'ra'uya l'hitchabed) is not nullified even among things not of its own species (she'eino mino), contrary to the Or Zarua, who compares beriah and piece worthy of honor to something that has a permitted aspect (davar she'yesh lo matirin). See above, siman 100, seif 3 and seif 4. And as written in the chapter 'Elu Trefot' (daf 67a): 'a person should not skin etc.' And even though presumably there is more than sixty times [the permitted material] relative to it — (according to Rashi there this is not a proof — see the end of siman 100). And see what is written at the beginning of siman 110. See there.
