6) Ibid. "Since if it were to be nullified it would be permitted, etc." — This applies specifically where, if it were nullified, it would be permitted for consumption. But where, even if nullified, it would not be permitted for consumption — such as a piece of basar b'chalav that became mixed with two pieces of neveilah — [the mixture] is permitted in benefit, because the Sages enacted the decree that a piece worthy of honor is not nullified only where, if it were nullified, it would become permitted for consumption. Peri Toar note 2. Chavat Da'at note 1. Siftei Da'at, end of note 1. Erech HaShulchan note 3. Aruch HaShulchan note 5. Zivchei Tzedek note 4, who wrote that this is not in accordance with Kehillat Yaakov who was stringent. And the reasoning is: since even if it were nullified the pieces would remain forbidden for consumption, if so it is only fit to be served with honor before a non-Jew — and anything that is not fit to be served with honor (meaning: after nullification) before every person is not called worthy of honor. Aruch HaShulchan ibid.
