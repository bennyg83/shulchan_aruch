(Seif 3 in the hagahah) And so is the custom. See Shach s.k. 7 who cites Maharil and Bach that even a counted item (davar sheb'minyan) is not so — unlike the Torat Chatat who wrote that even if nullified on account of worthiness of honor, it is not nullified on account of being a counted item. Proof can be brought from the challenge at the beginning of the chapter of mixtures to nullify in the majority; Tosafot explain the challenge from dying sin-offerings, not from a stoned ox. Mordechai records an incident in France where Rabbeinu Tam ordered discarding one chicken and permitting the rest, saying that although a chicken is a counted item and important, it is not nullified — but he did not forbid on account of being a counted item; in his place chickens may not have been sold as a counted item. Tosafot in Yevamot equate a counted item with a piece worthy of honor. In our time chickens are not sold together in a basket, so feathers and sheep skin do not nullify as a counted item — see Semak. The Rama's custom except for chicken in feathers is correct.
