"And even if it is prohibited in benefit, etc." — Prohibited-in-benefit items such as basar b'chalav and similar cases, if they are pieces worthy of honor, render their mixture forbidden in benefit as well, even in a thousand [parts]. However, it is permitted to sell them to a non-Jew, except for the monetary value of the forbidden portion within them. Shach, small note 1. Peri Chadash note 1. Minhat Yaakov on the Torat Chatat, klal 40, note 1. Beit Lechem Yehudah note 2. Chavat Da'at note 1. Siftei Da'at note 1. Chacham Adam, klal 53, note 6. Beit Yitzchak, vol. 1, in Amudei Zahav, note 1. Aruch HaShulchan ibid. Zivchei Tzedek note 3, who wrote that this is not in accordance with Beit HaLevi, who prohibits selling it to a non-Jew. And the fact that it is permitted to sell it to a non-Jew except [for the value of the forbidden portion], etc. — this applies when the situation is such that there is no concern that the non-Jew will sell it back to a Jew.
