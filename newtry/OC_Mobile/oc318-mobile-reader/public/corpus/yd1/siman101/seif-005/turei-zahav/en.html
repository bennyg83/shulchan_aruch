Gizzard, etc. Maharshal wrote in the chapter of Gid HaNasheh siman 50, and this is his language: however, it appears to me that in most places I have seen that the gizzard of a goose is considered worthy of honor, and since the Rambam wrote that everything depends on time, certainly a goose gizzard is important and is not nullified — end of his words. It implies that of a chicken it is not worthy of honor; and so in Maharil in the name of Mohar R. Yaakov Tevel — this distinction. And see what I wrote in siman 75 that specifically for this purpose the gizzard is included among intestinal organs, as the Tur's words. Maharshal wrote there, and this is his language: the sages of Krakow ruled on goose meat on the breast called bilak — even though the goose was plucked, nevertheless the bilak is called worthy of honor. And to me it appears they ruled not according to law, although in truth in those places it is fit to honor guests with its roast; nevertheless, since the goose was plucked and its glory removed, its significance was lost, and it is somewhat like a beriah from which a little was removed — its significance is nullified. And here its significance is not nullified, since when it was not plucked it was very important — end of his words. And I wonder whether these words came from Maharshal's mouth, for they are very puzzling: that in a very large and important piece, if a little was cut from it, shall we say its significance was lost? Yet we say in the next seif that significance is not lost through crushing or cutting until its form is lost, etc. And this is not comparable to beriah, for whenever something is removed from it, it is no longer a beriah upon it. Therefore one may not rely on this for practice, only as the sages of Krakow ruled — that any piece currently fit to honor, even though it was formerly more important. It is written in the Levush that those goose livers that turned white from the fat are certainly important and are not nullified.
