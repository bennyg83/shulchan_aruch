A chicken etc., and likewise etc., and likewise etc. — From the fact that [the Gemara] in Zevachim there challenges: 'let them be nullified by a majority!' — even though there are many pieces worthy of honor (chatichot ha're'uyot l'hitchabed) among them — since as it is [in its whole, intact state] it is not [considered] worthy of honor. And likewise in the Yerushalmi, which Tosafot cite in Yevamot there, [the Gemara] interprets the Mishnah of Avodah Zarah as referring to a slaughtered ox [and the Rash in chapter 3 of Orlah wrote in the name of the Yerushalmi: in a cut piece]. And even though in our Gemara in Zevachim there it appears to refer to a living animal, nevertheless it states there that it is specifically because it is a living creature (ba'al chayim) that it is not nullified.
And even though etc. — As noted in siman 99, seif 6, that in such a case [the forbidden status] does not revive [once nullified]. See there.
And some disagree with all of this — see Tosafot in Zevachim 72a, s.v. 'ela etc.,' and in Yevamot chapter 1, daf 2, [where] Rabbi Yochanan etc. And the proof is from the fact that [the Talmud] counts among the six things: beets, cabbage, and gourd — and these are not normally cooked whole, hence it must be referring to them when alive. And likewise in Avodah Zarah, which counts the birds of the metzora etc. — [this is the view of] the Rosh. And according to this, even a chicken in its feathers is like the birds of the metzora.
And so the practice is, except for etc., and likewise etc. — [This follows] the first position, except that one can say that the ox condemned to stoning is different, since it lacks a major rectification [before it could ever become permitted].
And likewise fish etc. — See Bava Batra, chapter 3, [daf] 2, in the Mishnah.
