A chicken in its feathers, etc. — it is nullified, etc. It is implied from the words of the Mechaber and the poskim that even a counted item (davar sheb'minyan) is not so — and as Maharshal wrote in his rulings and in his book Piskei HaGahot siman 53, that when it lacks a very great preparation it is also not a counted item; and this is clearly implied in Mordechai at the beginning of Beitzah — see there. And so is the opinion of the Bach, unlike the Torat Chatat who wrote in general rule 43 that even though it was nullified on account of being worthy of honor, it was not nullified on account of being a counted item — see above:
And even though after it was mixed they removed the feathers. Nevertheless, since at the beginning of the mixture it was nullified, it does not revert to being prohibited when stirred. Sefer Mitzvot Katan:
And likewise a whole sheep. Even if its skin was removed:
Except for a chicken in its feathers. And a sheep in its skin, and the like:
Except for goose feet. And the Torat Chatat wrote there in the name of Maharai that goose legs whose skin was not singed off them as people are accustomed to do are comparable to a chicken in its feathers:
