"Worthy of honor." The Shach wrote that it is also not a counted item (davar shebeminyan), since it lacks such a significant degree of preparation. This is also the opinion of the Bach, unlike the Torat Chatat who wrote that it is a counted item. And see below, siman 110.
"They removed it." Nevertheless, since at the time of the initial mixture it was nullified, it does not revert to being prohibited when stirred. Sefer Mitzvot Katan.
"Whole." Even if its skin was removed.
"In its feathers." The same law applies to a sheep in its skin, and the like.
"Goose." The Torat Chatat wrote that goose legs whose skin has not been singed off them, as is the custom to do, are comparable to a chicken in its feathers. The Taz wrote that it implies that if the feathers were already removed but the innards had not yet been taken out and the belly had not been opened, it is still considered a piece worthy of honor and is not nullified. Maharshal wrote that the head of an animal is not considered worthy of honor, except for the head of a calf — and certainly the tongue of an ox is considered worthy of honor, for Abraham honored the angels with three tongues.
