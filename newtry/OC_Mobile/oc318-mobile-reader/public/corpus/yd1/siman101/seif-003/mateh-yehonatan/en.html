(Seif 2) It does not have the status of a piece worthy of honor unless its prohibition is on account of itself, such as nevelah and basar b'chalav. One could say: how is it logical to say it is not nullified on account of a piece worthy of honor? For it can be established on this matter for the reason of each piece individually — that piece which has taste in it is forbidden and the rest are permitted. And one could answer that at first it absorbed taste from the milk, and afterward it fell into a pot that has sixty, and its taste was emitted — nevertheless that piece is forbidden, because we hold that if it is possible to squeeze it out it is forbidden; and the Mishnah that teaches "these forbid in any amount" regarding basar b'chalav comes according to the one who holds that if it is possible to squeeze it out it is forbidden. If so, Tosafot's challenge in the first chapter is from the chapter "And is a large ox worse," etc. — "and let it also teach nevelah, for it taught it there," etc. — and they answer: let it not also teach basar b'chalav, lest it teach us that if it is possible to squeeze it out it is forbidden. And it is impossible that it is not like the Mechaber in the first chapter, for if a piece fell into basar b'chalav there is a doubt whether it became forbidden, and it was mixed with nevelah or impure meat — it is impossible that it is not nullified also for benefit, or perhaps specifically for eating we say a piece worthy of honor is not nullified, but not for benefit. And it is impossible that for benefit too we say a piece worthy of honor is not nullified. One could say it deals with meat of a wild animal mixed with all of them where there is taste of milk, and therefore there is no way to establish the matter through tasting; and we properly say it is not nullified on account of a piece worthy of honor, even though nevertheless meat of a wild animal is also forbidden d'rabbanan — from where do we know for benefit? But the Mechaber in the first chapter decided the matter that we do not say a piece worthy of honor for benefit; therefore there is no reason to say it deals in such a case, and one must say as Terumat HaDeshen wrote, that even though it can be established on this matter, nevertheless we say it is nullified and tasting is not required; and if so, he properly said that in basar b'chalav it is a piece worthy of honor that is not nullified — meaning that one must taste whether there is taste in it or not. [Hagahah of the great Rav, etc., Mohar R. Shlomo HaKohen, may his light shine.]
