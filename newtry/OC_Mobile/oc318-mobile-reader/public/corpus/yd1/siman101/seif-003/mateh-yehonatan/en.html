(Seif 3 in the hagahah) And so is the custom. See Shach s.k. 7 who cites Maharil and Bach that even a counted item (davar sheb'minyan) is not so — unlike the Torat Chatat who wrote that even if nullified on account of worthiness of honor, it is not nullified on account of being a counted item. Proof can be brought from the challenge at the beginning of the chapter of mixtures to nullify in the majority; Tosafot explain the challenge from dying sin-offerings, not from a stoned ox. Mordechai records an incident in France where Rabbeinu Tam ordered discarding one chicken and permitting the rest, saying that although a chicken is a counted item and important, it is not nullified — but he did not forbid on account of being a counted item; in his place chickens may not have been sold as a counted item. Tosafot in Yevamot equate a counted item with a piece worthy of honor. In our time chickens are not sold together in a basket, so feathers and sheep skin do not nullify as a counted item — see Semak. The Rama's custom except for chicken in feathers is correct.
(There in the hagahah) except for a chicken in its feathers, which lacks great preparation. One could say: how does the Rama explain the Gemara's challenge in the chapter of mixtures, which challenges "and let it be nullified in the majority" — if one explains the challenge according to Tosafot, from dying sin-offerings, but from a stoned ox there is no place, for it is a piece worthy of honor — if so, a chicken in its feathers is also a piece worthy of honor. And if one explains according to the Rosh, that from a stoned ox too he challenges, for a large ox is worse than a piece worthy of honor — if so, why should a whole sheep be a piece worthy of honor? And one could say there is a difference between a whole sheep and a chicken in its feathers: Tosafot challenge according to what they explained, that the Gemara's challenge is from a bird sin-offering — for the Mishnah there teaches "and these forbid in any amount: metzora birds," etc. — if so, even a bird would be a piece worthy of honor. And they resolve that it deals with a sparrow, which is more a piece worthy of honor than doves and turtledoves. And the Rosh in the third chapter of Orlah wrote that metzora birds are different, for they are equated to impure birds, and therefore they are significant and are not nullified. And behold the Tur in the name of some who brought this ruling of the Rama in his name — one could say that Tosafot's explanation was considered strained, and therefore they distinguish between a bird in its feathers and a bird not in its feathers. If so, there it deals with a bird sin-offering that is still in its feathers — it is not a piece worthy of honor; but regarding metzora birds it deals after the feathers were removed — it is properly a piece worthy of honor. And incidentally, in the words of the Rosh in the third chapter of Orlah, one could say that his words settle what the world challenged in the passage in Kiddushin daf 57, where they say that from "this you shall eat," etc., we include the dispatched bird — and it is not nullified in the majority, etc. — see there at length. [Hagahah: the words of the author here are not understood to me — how is it proven from the verse that it is not nullified, for the Rosh only wrote that metzora birds, since the verse equated them to impure birds, are significant and are not nullified — and therefore he certainly means only d'rabbanan they are not nullified, but from the Torah even impure birds themselves are nullified one against two; for the world's challenge is according to those who hold that beriah is not nullified only d'rabbanan in general, etc. — end of hagahah of the great Rav Mohar R. Shlomo HaKohen, may his light shine, Vilna.]
