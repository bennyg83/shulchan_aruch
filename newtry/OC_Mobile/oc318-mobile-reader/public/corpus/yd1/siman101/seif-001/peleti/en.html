(Siman 101) A piece worthy of honor, etc. The opinion of Terumat HaDeshen: if now, when nullified, it is not important, nevertheless before nullification it was important and is not nullified. The Taz in Orach Chayim siman 473 wondered: from where do we know this — perhaps we follow how it will be after nullification, whether to be lenient or stringent? Terumat HaDeshen has proof from Tosafot in Zevachim on "all sacrifices that became mixed," where the Gemara challenges "let it nullify in the majority" — it is a piece worthy of honor. Tosafot answer from dying sin-offerings with small birds; the challenge on a mixed sin-offering does not apply, for in kodashim there is no worthiness of honor. One cannot say kodashim never have worthiness of honor, for the Mishnah in Avodah Zarah teaches stoned ox, etc. Tosafot in Zevachim and Terumat HaDeshen are correct. Proof from Pesachim: five Paschal lambs, one invalid — we cannot stipulate, for breast and thigh belong to the priest; if the invalid were nullified one against two, there would be no prohibition of eating the Paschal offering improperly; therefore breast and thigh are a piece worthy of honor. See Tosafot at the beginning of "How do we roast." Peri Toar: if benefit-forbidden mixes with eating-forbidden, the Sages did not make worthiness of honor prevent nullification for benefit. In Avodah Zarah, basar b'chalav forbids in any amount — how is nullification possible if taste can be detected? It deals with beast basar b'chalav with fowl where taste cannot be distinguished; taste prohibitions for benefit still apply. Rambam holds beast and fowl are two kinds for worthiness of honor only in its kind — see siman 110. Therefore the Gemara's challenge stands; basar b'chalav teaches both benefit and eating. One should be stringent.
And even if the prohibition is only d'rabbanan, there is room to investigate whether a d'rabbanan eating prohibition is nullified. Yevamot teaches that a counted item in a d'rabbanan matter is nullified; terumah in our time is d'rabbanan and is nullified. Tosafot and Rosh discuss bundles of clover in kilayim of a vineyard. For us, who hold only "that which is counted" is not nullified, there is doubt which Tosafot reason applies. End of Avodah Zarah challenges using chametz on Pesach to teach nullification of a d'rabbanan important item. Beit Yosef infers from the Rambam that chullin in the azara has worthiness of honor; the Rambam may hold it is d'oraisa, but after analysis this is uncertain. Rambam in Terumot rules a sealed jug of terumah is not nullified even in a thousand — even d'rabbanan important items are not nullified. Rif holds that without benefit, even beriah is nullified. One may be lenient on non-Jewish cheese in loss cases.
