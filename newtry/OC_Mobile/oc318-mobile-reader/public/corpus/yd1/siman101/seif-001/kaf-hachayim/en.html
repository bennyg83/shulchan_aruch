1) [Seif 1] "A piece worthy of honor, etc." — meaning it is fit to be served with honor before guests. Chullin 100a. Tur. Levush. Kneset HaGedolah in Hagahot HaTur, note 1. And we require that it be fit to be served with honor before guests of the sort that distinguished people are accustomed to serve it. Maharshal, Perek Gid HaNasheh, siman 23. Kneset HaGedolah ibid. Beit Lechem Yehudah note 1. Mishbetzot Zahav note 1, who wrote that this means honored and important guests. Beit Yitzchak in Amudei Zahav, note 3. Aruch HaShulchan note 1.
2) And if you ask: how is it considered a piece worthy of honor — is it not neveilah or tereifah? — this is not a question at all. For not only is neveilah and tereifah fit to be served before a non-Jewish guest, but even a piece of something prohibited in benefit, such as basar b'chalav, is also considered worthy of honor — since, if we were to say that it is nullified and permitted, it would then be worthy of honor. Therefore it is not nullified for this reason. Aruch HaShulchan ibid.
3) And specifically the piece itself is not nullified, but its taste is nevertheless nullified in sixty, as was written above (in the previous siman) regarding the law of beriah. Levush.
4) Ibid. "And even if it is prohibited in benefit, etc." — Prohibited-in-benefit items such as basar b'chalav and similar cases, if they are pieces worthy of honor, render their mixture forbidden in benefit as well, even in a thousand [parts]. However, it is permitted to sell them to a non-Jew, except for the monetary value of the forbidden portion within them. Shach, small note 1. Peri Chadash note 1. Minhat Yaakov on the Torat Chatat, klal 40, note 1. Beit Lechem Yehudah note 2. Chavat Da'at note 1. Siftei Da'at note 1. Chacham Adam, klal 53, note 6. Beit Yitzchak, vol. 1, in Amudei Zahav, note 1. Aruch HaShulchan ibid. Zivchei Tzedek note 3, who wrote that this is not in accordance with Beit HaLevi, who prohibits selling it to a non-Jew. And the fact that it is permitted to sell it to a non-Jew except [for the value of the forbidden portion], etc. — this applies when the situation is such that there is no concern that the non-Jew will sell it back to a Jew. Minhat Yaakov ibid. Zivchei Tzedek ibid.
5) However, regarding a piece that is not worthy of honor, even a prohibited-in-benefit item is nullified, as per the law of dry-with-dry, which was explained at the beginning of siman 109. Siftei Da'at ibid. Beit Yitzchak ibid.
6) Ibid. "Since if it were to be nullified it would be permitted, etc." — This applies specifically where, if it were nullified, it would be permitted for consumption. But where, even if nullified, it would not be permitted for consumption — such as a piece of basar b'chalav that became mixed with two pieces of neveilah — [the mixture] is permitted in benefit, because the Sages enacted the decree that a piece worthy of honor is not nullified only where, if it were nullified, it would become permitted for consumption. Peri Toar note 2. Chavat Da'at note 1. Siftei Da'at, end of note 1. Erech HaShulchan note 3. Aruch HaShulchan note 5. Zivchei Tzedek note 4, who wrote that this is not in accordance with Kehillat Yaakov who was stringent. And the reasoning is: since even if it were nullified the pieces would remain forbidden for consumption, if so it is only fit to be served with honor before a non-Jew — and anything that is not fit to be served with honor (meaning: after nullification) before every person is not called worthy of honor. Aruch HaShulchan ibid.
7) Ibid., in the Rama's gloss. "And if it is doubtful whether it is worthy of honor or not, we rule leniently." Because the law of a piece worthy of honor — meaning the decree that it is not nullified by a majority — is d'rabbanan, and in a doubtful d'rabbanan case we rule leniently. And this leniency applies specifically to a doubt as to whether it is a piece worthy of honor; but if it is certainly a piece worthy of honor and there is a doubt as to whether it is forbidden, it is not nullified, as below in siman 110, seif 9 — see there. Shach, small note 2. Peri Chadash note 2. Shulchan Gavoah note 3. And so was written above in siman 100, notes 1 and 2, in the name of several poskim — see there.
8) And this applies specifically when it is forbidden due to a doubtful Torah prohibition; but if it is forbidden due to a doubtful d'rabbanan prohibition, it is nullified. Taz, small note 1, and below siman 102, small note 3. Minhat Yaakov on the Torat Chatat, klal 40, note 20. Lechem HaPanim note 3.
9) Ibid., in the Rama's gloss. "And if it is doubtful whether it is worthy of honor, etc." — This is not speaking of a case where the halachic decisor is uncertain whether it is a piece worthy of honor or not, for if so it would constitute a doubt arising from a lack of knowledge, and this is not called a doubt at all. Rather, it speaks of a case where we are uncertain about what the situation was at the time of the mixture — for example, where there are many pieces here, and among them are small pieces and a piece worthy of honor, and we do not know whether the forbidden item was the piece worthy of honor, and similar cases. Minhat Yaakov ibid., note 19. However, Peri Toar, note 4, wrote that even if one is uncertain regarding a piece whether it is worthy of honor, it is permitted — and this matter is not a doubt arising from a lack of knowledge, for in any case where in the eyes of those who are ruling the matter is not considered worthy of honor, we do not enact the decree, even though it is possible that in the eyes of others elsewhere it would be worthy of honor — for we only adjudicate the matter according to the time and according to the place. Thus, even if someone comes and says that it is a piece worthy of honor, if the people of that place are in doubt, it is regarded as a matter that is in doubt for all. End of his words. And so also wrote Mishbetzot Zahav, note 2, who wrote regarding the aforementioned words of Minhat Yaakov that the wording of the author does not indicate this. And so also wrote Zivchei Tzedek, end of note 7, that this is the primary ruling, in accordance with the words of Peri Toar, as is apparent from the general implication of the words of the poskim.
