But if it became forbidden, etc. Noda BiYehudah siman 64 records a woman who lent two molds presumed dairy, baked kreplach with butter, returned them without saying so; later the borrower baked fat in nine molds including those two. He permitted nullification in the majority — kreplach are worthy of honor and a counted item, but the dough is the main substance and butter is absorbed — nullified; forbid according to the number of the two molds. He considered fat and butter not the same kind requiring sixty in dry. Shach siman 105 s.k. 23 is stringent on absorbed vessel taste; Magen Avraham is lenient. If molds were greased with forbidden fat before kreplach, there is concern of visible fat without sixty. Chatam David challenged Noda BiYehudah: taste can be detected in a spoonful. Answer: when eating one by one we say each is from the majority; Noda BiYehudah would forbid eating all together. Doubtful peel prohibition — fat taste may not be sensed.
