(Siman 101 in the Shulchan Aruch, seif 1) A piece worthy of honor has the law of beriah. And Terumat HaDeshen wrote that if after it was cooked it is not an important item — only before it was cooked it is also an important item and is not nullified. And one can bring proof for his words from the chapter of mixtures, where Tosafot challenge from the chapter "And is a large ox worse than other pieces," etc., and they explain that the Gemara's challenge is from dying sin-offerings, and among them are birds that were not worthy of honor — see there. And therefore one must say that they hold that since from the outset it was worthy of honor, it is also not nullified; for it deals with kodashim, and Tosafot already wrote that regarding kodashim there is no worthiness of honor, for the priests do not hold favor for one another; and if so, after nullification it is not worthy of honor. Or: since from the outset it was worthy of honor, it is also not nullified.
(There in the hagahah) And even if it is forbidden only d'rabbanan it is not nullified. Rabbenu Yerucham proved this from the Rambam in Laws of Disqualified Offerings that chullin slaughtered in the Temple Court is forbidden only rabbinically; and in the second chapter of Avodah Zarah we learn that these forbid in any amount — chullin slaughtered in the Temple Court, etc. — from the reason that it is a piece worthy of honor for guests. If so, it is proven that even a d'rabbanan prohibition that is a piece worthy of honor is not nullified. And one might say that therefore the Rambam must hold that chullin slaughtered in the Temple Court is d'oraisa; for wherever it says "you shall not eat," benefit is also included, and proof is from nevelah where Scripture had to permit giving to a ger and selling to a non-Jew. The Gemara in Pesachim discusses whether we derive all prohibitions from "to the dog you shall throw it"; the Rambam rules that benefit is included from "you shall not eat," and in Laws of Idolatry rules like R' Yosi that only selling to a non-Jew is permitted. It appears the Rambam derives benefit from "that" (oto) and if so there is no source to forbid chullin in the azara — unless it is d'oraisa. After investigation, however, the Rambam may derive benefit from "you shall not give them a free gift" (lo techenam) regarding a non-Jew, not from "to the dog"; and the verse "sell to a non-Jew" establishes an extra positive prohibition. Thus the entire Pesachim discussion may be according to R' Yosi who learns from lo techenam, and there is no proof to all Torah prohibitions — we still need "to the dog." And since the Rambam did not cite that verse for chullin in the azara, he may hold it is d'oraisa. The Rosh in Eruvin explains the Mishnah of chullin in the azara as teaching that a piece worthy of honor is not nullified; if it were only rabbinic, the Mishnah of basar b'chalav would suffice. Thus the Rosh may hold like the Tosefta that it is not nullified, and that even benefit prohibitions are not nullified — unlike our conclusion that for eating prohibitions without benefit, even beriah and a piece worthy of honor are nullified according to Rif. Therefore one may be lenient regarding non-Jewish cheese when there is great loss, since it is a d'rabbanan eating prohibition.
