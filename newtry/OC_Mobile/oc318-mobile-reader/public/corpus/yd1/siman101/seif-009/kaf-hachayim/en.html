"And if it is doubtful whether it is worthy of honor, etc." — This is not speaking of a case where the halachic decisor is uncertain whether it is a piece worthy of honor or not, for if so it would constitute a doubt arising from a lack of knowledge, and this is not called a doubt at all. Rather, it speaks of a case where we are uncertain about what the situation was at the time of the mixture — for example, where there are many pieces here, and among them are small pieces and a piece worthy of honor, and we do not know whether the forbidden item was the piece worthy of honor, and similar cases. Minhat Yaakov ibid., note 19. However, Peri Toar, note 4, wrote that even if one is uncertain regarding a piece whether it is worthy of honor, it is permitted — and this matter is not a doubt arising from a lack of knowledge, for in any case where in the eyes of those who are ruling the matter is not considered worthy of honor, we do not enact the decree, even though it is possible that in the eyes of others elsewhere it would be worthy of honor — for we only adjudicate the matter according to the time and according to the place. Thus, even if someone comes and says that it is a piece worthy of honor, if the people of that place are in doubt, it is regarded as a matter that is in doubt for all. End of his words. And so also wrote Mishbetzot Zahav, note 2, who wrote regarding the aforementioned words of Minhat Yaakov that the wording of the author does not indicate this. And so also wrote Zivchei Tzedek, end of note 7, that this is the primary ruling, in accordance with the words of Peri Toar, as is apparent from the general implication of the words of the poskim.
