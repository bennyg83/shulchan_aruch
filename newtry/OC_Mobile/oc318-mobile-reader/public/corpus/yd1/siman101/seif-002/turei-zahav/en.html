And basar b'chalav. Because both of them became one body, and the proof is that one receives lashes from the Torah even if one ate only approximately half an olive-measure of milk and half an olive-measure of meat.
Because it absorbed taste, etc. For that which is absorbed is not worthy of honor. Based on this, Darkei Moshe and Torat Chatat general rule 40 wrote that cheese curdled in treifah milk that became mixed is not a piece worthy of honor, since the prohibition within it is absorbed, and not like Or Zarua at all — so is rule 5, where he was stringent also in this cheese on the grounds that it is as if born in prohibition. And Maharshal, may his memory be blessed, was puzzled at the Rav Rama in the Torat Chatat, for there in general rule 43 he ruled regarding cheese of a non-Jew that a double doubt does not help, since the prohibition is on account of itself, etc.; and based on this he ruled here too like Or Zarua that it is worthy of honor. And to me it appears that there it deals with cheese of a non-Jew in which there is concern that impure milk became mixed in, and there was no nullification in a majority of kosher items, and it teaches us that regarding double doubt they were stringent because, although all cheeses of non-Jews were forbidden from the outset on account of a d'oraisa doubt, as explained in Or Zarua general rule 26 at its beginning — this is why he calls it a prohibition on account of itself, meaning a certain prohibition, and it has no permission through a double doubt; for if from one doubt alone it is obvious that it is forbidden even though it is d'rabbanan, since there is concern that impure milk became mixed in and there would be a d'oraisa doubt, and even through a double doubt it is not permitted, for the first doubt is counted as certain, since the essence of the d'rabbanan prohibition here was in place of a d'oraisa doubt — and he did not mention worthy of honor at all. But here, where it was curdled in treifah milk and mixed in a majority — meaning that it could be permitted through nullification in a majority of kosher items, but you come to prohibit it on account of worthy of honor — in this I say: since it is absorbed, meaning that there is no mixture of treifah milk as a prohibition in it, but only that it was curdled in it — therefore it was only absorbed and is nullified, as will be explained shortly regarding the distinction between curdling in treifah milk versus treifah milk mixed in. And Maharshal wrote in chapter 25 siman 48, and this is the language of Mordechai in the name of Rabbeinu Shimshon son of Rabbeinu Avraham: who knows whether cheeses are as important as a piece worthy of honor, etc. And to me it appears that certainly there a piece worthy of honor does not apply to cheeses, for if it is moist it is not important, and even dry cheese that is important in our time — nevertheless it is not so important to eat enough for satiety, for it is hard on the body, and especially since many people do not eat it even under duress — until here his language. And his words do not seem right to me in my humble opinion, for we say in the chapter of Rabbi Eliezer that cheese even one day old is fit, and so wrote the long Torat HaBayit regarding cheese made from a treifah animal, which Beit Yosef brought in siman 21, that cheese is worthy of honor; and so explained the Rama in siman 81. Nevertheless, specifically there where the prohibition was on account of itself, for treifah animal milk was mixed in there and it was absorbed — it is worthy of honor; but in cheese absorbed from the prohibition we mentioned through curdling in treifah milk, the words of the Torat Chatat are correct that it was not a piece worthy of honor; and likewise in a mixture of impure milk — it is also not worthy of honor, for the Tur wrote in siman 115 regarding the reason for the prohibition of cheeses of non-Jews: perhaps they mixed impure milk in it, even though no impure milk is actually found — we are concerned that a little remains between its holes — end of his words. And it is obvious that regarding that milk between the holes it is not worthy of honor, since the body of the cheese is not made from it, unlike a mixture of treifah milk. And why did the Torat Chatat mention in general rule 43 "perhaps impure milk was mixed in," and a double doubt does not help, and he did not mention there regarding its being worthy of honor for the matter that nullification in the majority does not help — for there it would certainly be nullified if there is a majority, since it is only like absorbed. Nevertheless it appears to me that ordinary cheese of a non-Jew that was mixed among kosher cheeses we consider as an absorbed prohibition and it is nullified, and we do not say it comes from treifah animal milk, for we follow the majority, and most animals are kosher. And proof: many reasons are stated in the chapter Ein Ma'amidin why they forbade cheeses of non-Jews, as the Beit Yosef brought in siman 115, and he did not mention this reason that it is made from treifah, only "perhaps they mixed impure milk in it" — and this was not worthy of honor, and its law is like an absorbed prohibition as we said. However, one reason he counted there is that they curdle it in the skin of a neveilah stomach — and based on this it has the status of worthy of honor, for there is a meat-and-milk prohibition here. End of my words: in a place where it is known that they do not curdle in the skin of a non-Jew's animal, it is not a piece worthy of honor — so it appears to me.
