And basar b'chalav. For since each one is permitted on its own, and through their joining together they became forbidden, the whole reverted to being as one body:
It does not have the status of a piece worthy of honor. The reason is as the Hagahot ShaDa wrote in siman 41, and Rashba, Ran, and other poskim — because from the place of absorption the prohibition absorbed in it is not considered significant.
And even a piece that was not salted, etc. See above siman 99 seif 14 and what is written there:
Every piece that was forbidden only on account of a peel, etc. It is written in Sefer Lev Chaim (daf 210b): and it requires study — from where was it forbidden on account of a peel? Only because it absorbed taste from another prohibition; and if so, even when the whole piece became forbidden — end of his words. And to reconcile this: for we find it in basar b'chalav — investigate. In any case, what the Rav brought thus from Tosafot I did not find; only that Tosafot at the beginning of the chapter Gid HaNasheh (Chullin daf 96) wrote in the name of Rambam that for a peel and the like we do not say chein nafsho — and there is no proof from this that it is not a piece worthy of honor. But there is proof, for from the words of Rabeih that Mordechai brought in the chapter Gid HaNasheh siman 1994 and all of it s.s. 104 regarding salted milk and meat, it is explained that even for a peel we say a piece worthy of honor is not nullified, even though it is his view explicitly that we do not say chein nafsho for half a piece — and as Mordechai brought in his name in Perek HaYom siman 1061 regarding a drop of milk — and so too Rabeinu Yerucham in netiv 25 end of section 25 holds that for a peel we say a piece worthy of honor is not nullified; and in section 13 he brought Ritva that for half a piece we do not say chein nafsho; however in responsum of Maymoni haMe'uhad siman 11 I found it implies that for a peel we do not say it is a piece worthy of honor, and it is cited in Hagahot ShaDa siman 33 in the hagahah beginning "an incident came before R'" etc.; and one must say Rabeih and his allies refer to when if one peels it there will remain a whole piece — end.
