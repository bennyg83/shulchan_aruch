"With milk." Because both of them have become one body — and the proof is that one receives lashes from the Torah if one ate approximately half an olive-measure of milk and half an olive-measure of meat. End of the words of the Taz.
"Taste." For that which is absorbed is not worthy of honor. Based on this, the Torat Chatat wrote that a cheese curdled with treifah milk that subsequently became mixed in [with other cheese] is not a piece worthy of honor, since the prohibition within it is absorbed. The Bach was puzzled by this, for the Torat Chatat had ruled regarding non-Jewish cheese that a double doubt (sfek sfeika) does not help, since the prohibition is inherent to the cheese itself — and if so, here too he should have ruled that it is a piece worthy of honor. The Taz answered that there the discussion concerns cheese in which there is concern that non-kosher milk became mixed in and there was no nullification by a majority of kosher milk, and it teaches us that regarding a double doubt they were stringent, since the Sages prohibited it from the outset due to a d'oraisa doubt, and it is therefore called a certain treifah — for the first doubt is counted as certain. Whereas here the discussion is where there is a majority sufficient to nullify the prohibition, but you come to prohibit it due to the rule of a piece worthy of honor — regarding this I say that it is only an absorbed prohibition, and we do not apply the rule of a piece worthy of honor to it. He further wrote: nevertheless, it appears to me that we consider ordinary non-Jewish cheese as an absorbed prohibition, and it is nullified, and we do not say that it comes from treifah animal milk, since we follow the majority — see there. However, what he wrote at the end of his words — that if there is concern that the cheese was curdled with the stomach-lining of a neveilah, due to this it has the status of a piece worthy of honor, for there is a basar b'chalav prohibition here, end of his words — does not seem correct to me. For the reason we apply the rule of a piece worthy of honor to basar b'chalav is because each one of them is permitted on its own, and this logic does not apply to neveilah. Furthermore, it contradicts what the Mechaber wrote in siman 87, seif 11 — see there, and consider this carefully. It is possible, however, to press his words to mean that the prohibition of neveilah is a prohibition inherent to itself, and we apply a piece worthy of honor to it just as with basar b'chalav, which is also called a prohibition inherent to itself. And it is straightforward.
"Peeling." The Shach wrote that this rule is only found regarding basar b'chalav, but regarding other prohibitions they were only prohibited due to absorbed taste, and it is obvious that they are not called a piece worthy of honor. However, there are decisors who hold that if one were to peel it, it would be a piece worthy of honor that is not nullified — see there.
