That which is invalid in ikkur, etc. It implies explicitly that even at the time of shechitah we do not forbid ikkur of a dangling siman except when the whole was uprooted — for in the prior seif he wrote it happened before completing shechitah, automatically ikkur occurred at the beginning of shechitah; and on this he says in this seif: kosher if any amount remained, with no distinction between during shechitah or before shechitah; and no posek divides on this, and there is no reason to divide — for since the majority was not uprooted there is no treifah, why forbid because it happened at shechitah? And so is proven explicitly from Tosafot I cited, who challenged: it is obvious shechitah does not help; and they further challenged: what connection does this ikkur have to laws of shechitah? etc. — and they had to resolve it deals when the siman slipped and afterward he slaughtered it — end; and if you say at the time of shechitah it is stricter and forbids ikkur there even without the whole uprooted, only part — they would not have challenged at all, for there is a great difference between at shechitah and before shechitah; rather it is clear that even ikkur at shechitah is not forbidden except in the whole, as in this seif; and so is explained in Ran in the name of Ramban whom Beit Yosef brings: ikkur means simanim uprooted entirely or majority in a matter making treifah — and it should have said if this happened at shechitah it would be neveilah, since once it slipped at that time it is impossible to slaughter properly, for due to ikkur the simanim sway — end; and it is simple that also when this ikkur happened before shechitah it is treifah — the mishnah is crop severance; nevertheless it is not neveilah until it is so at shechitah; and so Beit Yosef — behold in any case there is no stringency even at shechitah except when all or majority were uprooted; and I wrote this because Maharshal wrote: it appears to me if ikkur happened at shechitah he forbids even in any amount uprooted, and we do not require the whole or majority, only if before shechitah; and he wrote he found thus, and so Semak and Mahariu did not mention measure in this ikkur (♯) and I know no place for this stringency, for all early and late authorities who did not write measure wrote "the simanim were uprooted" — meaning all simanim; only in Hagahot shechitot of Mahariu he wrote: at shechitah he forbids even in a minority; and perhaps on this Maharshal hinted in "I found thus"; but the essence is that even at shechitah ikkur forbids only in the whole; and the author of Hagahot shechitot did not write well on this, and so in Sefer Zivchei Riv.
