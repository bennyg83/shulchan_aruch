Whether in the last minority — ikkur in last minority is permitted by law; and Bach wrote: only if simanim were uprooted; but a flawed knife, also ikkur — even in last minority forbidden by law. A novel prohibition without proof; we find no hint in poskim; but Tosafot, Niddah, 2b, flawed knife — Tosafot wrote several doubts: perhaps in spinal bone or last minority it was flawed — learn he holds animal kosher in last minority, not like Bach — understand.
Regarding its milk — Taz challenged: in Chullin daf 69 the Gemara says: one may say therefore ordinary milk, though from its mother, is permitted, for there is remedy through shechitah; and eiver min hachai — why need remedy through shechitah? — here regarding ikkur what of it — it is not treifah, there is no remedy — nevertheless milk forbidden from eiver min hachai. Taz answered: what they said "there is remedy" means the eiver min hachai prohibition is lighter, therefore even in ikkur milk is not forbidden. I wonder: Rashba in his chiddushim, chapter Behemah HaMekasheh, challenged: what is the question on an animal with eiver min hachai if its milk is permitted — obvious it is forbidden like treifah milk; and Rashba answered: eiver min hachai has no name of treifah, only lack of shechitah fitness — slaughtered is like eiver min hachai; therefore the question: does it enter the leniency of eiver min hachai? — see there; if so, milk is also forbidden from eiver min hachai — why permit here? — at first glance difficult. What appears to me: certainly Tosafot, Ra'osh, Rashba all spoke whether it is treifah or not — not practical difference; forced to say all thought about milk. Therefore practical difference: whether uprooted or slipped simanim can return healthy through medicines — according to Rashi impossible, treifah cannot return to fitness; if we see it healed briefly, it returns. Unlike BeHaG: no treifah, only while uprooted shechitah does not help, not connected, or Ramban's reason — impossible to slaughter properly; but if they return healthy and heal well, in every case kosher — automatically milk permitted, for more permission than shechitah for eiver min hachai — simple and true; though I did not find authors thus — nevertheless all is correct and true.
