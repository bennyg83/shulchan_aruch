From the jaw and from the flesh. It implies if flesh is still on the simanim it is kosher; and so Beit Yosef in the name of Rashba explicitly; but in Tur it implies treifah for anything slipped from the jaw; and so appears in Ashiri, who did not bring the resolution in the Gemara of agam agumim.
And one of them slipped out. In Tur it is written: the siman was uprooted or slipped, etc. — its explanation: uprooted means by human force; slipped means by the animal's own motion.
But while alive it is kosher, etc. They proved this in Tosafot, chapter 1 (daf 9), from the challenge they raised: it is obvious shechitah does not help since it was already treifah — rather certainly it did not become treifah because of this ikkur, only halacha to Moses from Sinai that shechitah does not permit it until here; and they answered Rashba: even though a bird is fit with one siman, nevertheless we require both fit for shechitah; and they answered from the Gemara, chapter Behemah HaMekasheh (Chullin daf 69), in the question of milk of a ben pekua, etc. — as I brought siman 14, seif 8: here regarding ikkur there is no remedy through shechitah; nevertheless the milk is permitted, for there they only said the prohibition of eiver min hachai is lighter than yotzei, since remedy can apply to that prohibition sometime, unlike yotzei which has no remedy at all.
