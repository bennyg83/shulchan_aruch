(g) A knife with two necks' measure slaughtering simanim with holacha or hava'ah alone — kosher; but if he removed the entire head with holacha or hava'ah alone, this measure of a full neck beyond the neck does not suffice. Peri Chadash, part 1; and Turei Zahav, end of siman 8, agreed — specifically in an animal, because Rashba wrote spinal bone is hard — not in a bird — see there; but Maharach Kazis in Chok L'Yisrael agreed to validate; and so appears Rambam, chapter 2, and Ramban, and Rashba's responsum, siman 17, and Socher Hashem understood — no distinction between bird and animal — see there. Erech HaShulchan, part 1; and Rav Zivchei Tzedek, part 6, agreed: no distinction; also removing entire head with holacha or hava'ah suffices with neck measure and full neck beyond — see there; and see below, seif 5, and our words there.
(q) [Seif 5] He was slaughtering and cut the entire spinal column — kosher. Its law is like breaking the animal's spinal column, stated below siman 67, seif 3 — see there; and so Rashba in Tur HaDeshen and Mishmeret HaBayit, daf 23 — see there; Peri Chadash, part 7; Zivchei Tzedek, part 9.
(r) There, gloss. And the custom to declare treifah even if only majority, etc. — however Shach, s.k. 5, brought many great poskim and from the Gemara that it is permitted; and wrote: study on our master's words; and concluded: perhaps the Rav in the gloss was stringent specifically where there is no great loss — and in such a case. So too Chayei Adam, general rules 7, part 8; and Peri Chadash, part 8, wrote: even in small loss the lenient did not lose; and Beit Yisrael, part 2, wrote: now slaughterers declare treifah even in great loss; and so by experience. She'eilot uTeshuvot, part 5; and in these countries (Ashkenaz) they practice to forbid, not even lenient in great loss; even without intent. And Kenesset HaGedolah in Hagahot Beit Yosef, end of part 17, wrote: we do not practice thus — many birds are slaughtered with majority of spinal column and we do not invalidate; and so Shulchan Gavoah, part 7 — except their custom validates even if entire head was cut. And so Turei Zahav, siman 91, part 6; and Rav Zivchei Tzedek, part 14, wrote: in Baghdad, if majority of spinal column was cut they validate; but if entire spinal column cut and not even a thread remained — they practice treifah; except in great loss or mitzvah need one may be lenient even if entire spinal column cut. It turns out customs differ; and Peri To'ar, part 8, wrote: where they practiced to forbid, they practiced like permitted matters, etc.; where they did not practice — do not practice; all who slaughtered with holacha and hava'ah — end.
(s) And in countries where custom was not clarified — if he intended thus in his slaughter, do not be lenient except in great loss; but if he did not intend and the spinal column was also cut — do not forbid at all. She'eilot uTeshuvot, there.
(t) There, gloss. Even if he only cut majority of spinal column — meaning majority of the cord within the spinal column. Taz, s.k. 2. She'eilot uTeshuvot, part 5; Kometz, part 7; Minchat Yitzchak, part 5.
