(Siman 24, seif 10) If after he slaughtered the majority of the simanim he inserted the knife under the minority remaining of both or one of them and paused — permitted; and there is one who forbids in this too, and fitting to heed l'chatchila — meaning Ra'osh's view; and Ribbi's view: in shehiyah, darsah, and haladah, which are the manner of shechitah and in the place of shechitah, forbid in the last minority; but in hagramah and ikkur, not in the place of shechitah and not the manner of shechitah — do not forbid in the last minority; and Ra'osh wrote thus seems correct; therefore in hagramah, seif 12, and ikkur, seif 15, Shulchan Aruch did not bring "there is one who forbids"; see Shach, s.k. 16. In my humble opinion, Ra'osh and Ribbi did not forbid all haladah, only haladah slaughtering from above downward — such as inserting between both minorities and slaughtering the lower properly from above downward; or possibly he did not insert between them at all, only slaughtered under a cloth stuck to its neck, as seif 8; but in haladah Shulchan Aruch mentioned here — inserted under minority and paused — meaning from below upward — on this Ra'osh and Ribbi did not forbid, for from below upward is not the manner of shechitah, as explicit in the sugya and Rashi, chapter HaShochet (daf 30b), Rif, Ra'osh, and Shulchan Petach; and behold in ikkur, since not the manner of shechitah, they did not forbid; and the same for haladah from below upward — not the manner of shechitah — they did not forbid in the last minority. Therefore Shulchan Aruch's words are difficult — why bring "there is one who forbids" here and not in hagramah and ikkur.
