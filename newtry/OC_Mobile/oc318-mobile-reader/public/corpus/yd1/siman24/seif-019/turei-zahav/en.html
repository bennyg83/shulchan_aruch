Whole ring from the crop — kosher. This is Agur's view: through rolling of the crop it happened thus; and Rashal wrote on this to declare treifah as Rama wrote here: behold any doubt in shechitah is invalid — who will testify it came through rolling the crop after slaughtering the majority of the simanim? Perhaps it was at the beginning or middle of shechitah; and it is reasonable to say that through pressing the knife on the crop a joint jumped from its place — end; and so Maharshal concludes; and even if the ring is not whole, and no difference whether found on the knife or on the ground — in every case it is doubt in shechitah, lest he came to ikkur any amount — end; and he goes according to his reasoning that ikkur at shechitah forbids even any amount; but it has already been clarified there is no distinction except in the whole, as in seif 16; and accordingly if less than majority of the ring is on the knife or ground, plainly kosher, for there is no treifah at all even if caused by pressing the knife; and it appears even if we doubt whether it is majority or not, we follow leniency from safek safeka: perhaps it was not majority — if you say it was majority, perhaps it happened through rolling the crop. Once a case came to me: one slaughtered a goose and after shechitah found on the knife tip one ring — cut, both cut sides on both knife sides; I validated it, for it is clear it did not come through pressing the knife on the crop, for the ring was cut; and we need not fear it was first pushed from its place and then cut thus, for we do not fear pushing at all even for the stringent — rather when we see the ring was not cut and they have some reasoning the knife forgot and pushed without strength to cut; but here we see it was cut — why hold prohibition it was first pushed? Know: if so, when two cuts are found in the trachea and one ring between them, even if not torn from there, we could say that ring was pushed and there were not two adjacent cuts — and this too would be treifah; plainly there is no basis; for it is written siman 21: slaughter in two or three places is kosher — plainly even if adjacent with one ring between; rather certainly we do not hold a presumption except when we see the ring was not cut but came whole — on this we say perhaps it was pushed without cutting. In Sefer Ma'aseh Binyamin I saw at its end in his chiddushim on laws: once a slaughterer slaughtered a goose and when covering the blood found a whole ring on the ashes and I validated it; although in She'erit Yosef, siman 47, he responded to my words and remained in doubt — nevertheless many great ones and slaughterers agreed with me to validate; and so appears the essence, for Agur and Beit Yosef permitted if found on the knife; only Rav Rama was stringent — a novelty; and do not add upon it; and forbid only if found on the knife — end; and Beit Yosef and Agur are fitting to rely on in these matters we mentioned.
