9) From here there is open rebuke for certain slaughterers who show people the training of their hands: with great speed and ease they pass the knife over the bird's neck only in the forward stroke or only in the return stroke, and usually slaughter the simanim with only a small part of the knife. They do this initially to display their strength and prowess in the skill. It is clear that this is an invalid shechitah. It is not necessary to say that initially they may not do so, for at least the whole knife must pass over the siman; with that measure the Sages knew that it is as if he both moved it forward and brought it back. But if he slaughters with a small part of the knife, and with such speed, it is impossible that they avoid the concern of derasah. It is absolutely certain that they do this only from a lack of fear of Hashem, blessed is He, and they want to display their prowess before other slaughterers. Perhaps even after the fact we are concerned for this shechitah. Anyone who eats this shechitah, besides eating a possible case of derasah, also strengthens the hands of transgressors, for when they see that their deeds are pleasing to people they continue and boast of themselves, thinking that they are acting properly; therefore their sin is too great to bear. Kesef Achar, chapter 2 of Hilchot Shechitah, halachah 9. This is the language of Rabbi Michtav Eliyahu: I know of myself that in my youth I acted so, and I thought there was nothing better than it. From the day I was chosen in the holy community of Zolkiew and was tested before the rabbi and those sitting before him to slaughter birds before them, I slaughtered with only the forward stroke, and they rebuked me: God forbid that you ever do such a thing again, only with forward and return movement, as implied by Shulchan Aruch. End quote. Esron brought this in ot 8 and wrote that one who violates their words would have been better not created. Zevach Tzedek, ot 8. Therefore one must be careful not to make the knife for birds so sharp that he will come to slaughter with only the forward stroke.
(j) That which they said: if the knife has two necks in what is slaughtered — kosher with holacha or hava'ah alone — if he begins from the knife head intending to draw or bring its entire length — then even if from sharpness it slaughtered one full neck — kosher; but if he began in part of the knife, from that part onward there must be two necks' measure from there; and if he intended not to draw or bring the entire knife, only until some point — from the start until that stopping point there must be two necks' measure. (k) And the measure of two necks — in bird and animal; do not think: I have fourteen finger-widths in the knife, though the neck is wide, it reaches two necks since he draws and brings — enough even more beyond the neck; as I heard from an expert boasting he can slaughter calves with a bird knife — they do wrong: when the knife is short it slips into the slaughter-house mid-shechitah — comes to darsah, shehiyah, and ikkur — therefore measure for each species two necks with skin and spinal column.
