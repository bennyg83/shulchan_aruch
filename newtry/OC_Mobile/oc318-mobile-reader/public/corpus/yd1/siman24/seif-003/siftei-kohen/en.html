[If there is in the knife the measure of three necks, kosher. Explanation: whether he placed the knife at the beginning of shechitah on the width of both heads and the length of the knife extended a full neck beyond both heads and he drew the knife to the other side until nothing remained protruding on that side already; or whether he began to slaughter at the edge of the knife and drew the knife in his slaughter until a full neck extended beyond the second head — then certainly, since on both heads he passed a full two necks, kosher; but plainly, if he placed the knife at the beginning of shechitah on the width of both heads and drew the knife to that side that already protruded beyond both heads a full neck — this is considered as if the knife has only the measure of two necks, and understand.]
[And if not, there is concern. This is the view of Ramban, Rashba, and Ran (see in Rashba's chiddushim daf 32a and Tur HaDeshen daf 27b); and it appears to me they deal when he placed the knife at the beginning of shechitah on the width of both heads and deal when he slaughtered toward the side that protruded — and Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe wrote according to their reasoning: since we see that in one of them he darsahed, therefore in the second too he darsahed, for behold in one act and one matter he passed the knife over both; the same applies when he began to slaughter at the edge of the knife — since half the knife passed over the second, they rule both are forbidden for this reason: at least our eyes see one was darsahed, and if so in one act he passed the knife over both, and if so the second too was darsahed; and so appears Maharshal, chapter HaShochet, siman 11 — not like Bach who distinguished between beginning at the edge of the knife and placing the knife at the beginning on the width of the heads in matters not acceptable to the heart; and it also appears to me: if he began at the edge of the knife and passed the knife to the other side until a full neck protruded on the other side in a manner that the entire knife passed over both — both are kosher according to all, and so wrote Beit Yosef, Darkei Moshe, and Maharshal, and it is simple — not like Bach who distinguished on this; and his words are not seen either in the substance of the matter or in Ra'osh's words — examine well.]
[If he slaughtered two heads, etc. It appears that b'dieved alone is meant; and in Tur it states "a person slaughters two heads as one," etc., and so it is stated explicitly in the Gemara, chapter HaShochet (Chullin daf 29a), and so too Ran — permitted even l'chatchila; and one must say "slaughtered" is not precise, for the same applies even l'chatchila; and so one must say from Rambam, chapter 2 of Hilchot Shechitah, law 9, and Kol Bo — see there and understand.]
