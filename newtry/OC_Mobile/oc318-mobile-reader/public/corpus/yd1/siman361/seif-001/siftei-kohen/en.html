Interrupting Torah study, etc. — explains that there is an obligation to interrupt; so agreed the poskim.
"That he read and studied," etc. — implies both are required; but from Ritva's words brought in Beit Yosef that there is no Jew who is not either in Scripture or Mishnah — implies they say either reading or study.
"If there are 600,000," etc. — taking Torah is like giving it: just as giving Torah at Sinai was with 600,000, so taking it requires 600,000.
"Provided ten are with him" — so they can say Kaddish and the mourners' blessing.
And one does not interrupt Torah study for the dead when there is someone to attend to him — the law is garbled, but in Tur it is clear: it refers to one who does not read and study, for it suffices when there is someone to attend to him; and if there are people in the city who can attend to him, one need not check whether they are doing so — presumably they are — and Torah study takes precedence. But one who only teaches others has no limit and must always accompany; and for one who reads and teaches, 600,000 are required, and presumably there are not 600,000 — so the Mechaber's words must be explained similarly. Aguda wrote: the custom to interrupt Torah study whenever there is a dead person in the city — lest the needs of the dead be neglected because all go to study — seems that in communities which formed groups with designated people, a person who studies for himself and is not accustomed to tend to them may study. Maharshal and Derishah. Bach wrote on this: it seems that even for one who reads and teaches he may study, but for one who only teaches others — who has no limit — he must interrupt even if they formed groups; for the baraita "they interrupt Torah study for accompanying the dead" does not mean it is permitted to interrupt, but states an obligation. I did not understand his words — Aguda speaks of when it is not the time of accompanying, that he may study even for one who teaches others; for it is only at the time of accompanying that one must interrupt, as below seif 2.
Schoolchildren are not interrupted at all — even for accompanying the dead; the reason as Tur wrote: they are not interrupted even to build a synagogue — unlike Or Zarua who wrote the reason is that they do not deal with the deceased, for they are not interrupted even for accompanying the dead.
