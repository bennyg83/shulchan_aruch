To go out. The Taz wrote: this refers to one who neither reads nor studies, who does not need to see whether there are people attending to the dead. It appears that this opinion disagrees with what Rama wrote, that nowadays one generally neglects study. If so, according to Rama's view, one should neglect study even in this case. Nevertheless, a rabbi learning with his students should not interrupt for this as a general rule; end quote. The Agudah wrote: the reason people are accustomed to interrupt Torah study whenever there is a dead person in the city is lest the needs of the dead be neglected, for everyone will go to study. It appears to me that in communities that made groups and have designated people who attend to him, a person who studies for the sake of Heaven and is not accustomed to handling them may study; end quote. The Shach wrote that this speaks not at the time he is being taken out, when one may study and need not attend even according to the one who has studied Mishnah; but at the time he is being taken out, one must interrupt for him, as written in seif 2, unlike the Bach; see there.
