(Likkut) And Torah study is not interrupted, etc.; and one need not leave, etc. Torat HaAdam, from what is written in the Yerushalmi ch. 3 of Pesachim [and ch. 1 of Chagigah]: R. Abbahu sent his son R. Chanin to Tiberias; they sent word back to him: he is performing acts of kindness. He sent back: "Are there no graves in Caesarea that I sent you to Tiberias?" The Sages had already voted in the upper chamber of Beit Lid that Torah study takes precedence over action. The Rabbis say this applies when there is someone else there to perform the action. Perhaps R. Chanin and R. Yosi and R. Ami — they brought prisoners before R. Elazar; he said to them: "If you were performing acts of kindness..." They said: "Were there no others there?" They said: "There was a stranger [who could have done it]."
(Likkut) And schoolchildren of, etc. Yerushalmi (cited by Rosh and Mordekhai ch. 3 of Moed Katan): the father of Shmuel sent Shmuel to study Torah; they sent back to him: he is performing acts of kindness. He sent: "Are there no graves here that I sent you there?" This teaches: schoolchildren of the house of study are not interrupted for accompanying the dead. And it is stated in ch. 16 of Shabbat (119b): schoolchildren of the house of study are not interrupted even for building a synagogue.
