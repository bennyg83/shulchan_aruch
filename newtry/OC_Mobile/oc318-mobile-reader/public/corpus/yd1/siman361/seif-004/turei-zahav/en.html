One must stand before him. This means before those accompanying the deceased, because they are performing kindness. The Tur cites the Yerushalmi: those who stand before the dead do not stand in honor of the dead, but in honor of those performing kindness for him. This is difficult from Moed Katan, where Yehudah told Chizkiyah to rise when Rav Huna’s bier was brought into the burial cave of Rabbi Chiya and his sons, saying it was improper for Rav Huna to remain standing while they sat. This implies standing in honor of the deceased. One must answer that our passage concerns an ordinary person who is not a Torah scholar. Nevertheless, it appears that one should stand before anyone proceeding to perform a mitzvah. Kiddushin 33 similarly states: see how beloved a mitzvah is at its proper time, for craftsmen stand before those bringing first fruits though they do not stand before a Torah scholar. This implies the principle applies to every mitzvah.
