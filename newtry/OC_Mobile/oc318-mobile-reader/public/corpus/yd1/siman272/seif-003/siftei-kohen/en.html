A sheet should contain no fewer than three columns and no more than eight. This is the correct reading.
Even a single verse. Although the final verse does not contain enough words to correspond to the number of remaining lines, he should elongate letters upward from below, so that one word occupies four or five lines, enabling him to finish at the bottom of the column. Tur. This implies that if there are many verses, he may write one word on each line, corresponding to the number of lines. All this applies at the end of the Torah, because no other arrangement is possible. In the middle of the Torah, however, one should not do this, neither elongating letters vertically nor widening them. Rather, he should write them according to their proper form, as stated below in siman 273. Nevertheless, if the scribe did so, the scroll is not invalidated on that account. The Perishah likewise implies this.
