Less than three. Tur writes: "The measure of the length of the sheet should be such that it contains three of these columns." When he says "length," he means the width of the sheet, since the measure is three columns next to one another. He calls it length because the lines run along the length of each column. Maharshal.
And no more than eight. This is the correct reading. Tur explains that the principal beauty is a sheet of four or five columns, but the actual defect applies only if there are two or nine. Semag, Hilchos Sefer Torah, writes the law that a sheet should contain three columns, etc., including all the details mentioned here, and concludes: "All this is for the ideal fulfillment of the mitzvah, but it does not invalidate." Mordechai in Hilchos Ketanos writes likewise. Therefore, if a sheet was written with only two columns, it is valid after the fact. Rambam, chapter 10 of Hilchos Sefer Torah, likewise implies this, since he does not list this among the defects that invalidate. It is surprising that Beit Yosef and Rama did not cite this. It appears that what Shulchan Aruch writes in siman 273, seif 5, "all these matters," etc., applies to this siman as well.
