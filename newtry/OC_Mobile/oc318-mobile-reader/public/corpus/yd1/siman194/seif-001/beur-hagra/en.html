Even, etc. Niddah 42b and Keritot 10a, regarding a dry birth, and derive from there. It is also explicit in Scripture.
Whether, etc., and even, etc. Mishnah, 30a, and derive from there. In Torat Kohanim: The word 'male' includes even a stillborn child. I might think only one born after nine months; from where do we know one born after eight, seven, six, or five months? Scripture states, 'and she gives birth,' meaning any birth whatsoever, etc.
Nowadays, etc. See Tosafot, 36a, s.v. 'a week,' etc., and the other authorities, based on what is stated there, 66a, 'why specifically,' etc. It is impossible for the womb to open without blood, as stated below. Although labor blood is pure when a fully formed child is born, they were concerned that she might see even a small amount of blood during the easing of labor, and therefore treated every woman who gives birth as one who gave birth while in a state of zivah.
(Addendum) Nowadays, etc. The Mordechai, siman 737, quotes a responsum of the Geonim: You asked about a woman who gave birth through difficult labor and not during an easing of labor, and who saw blood, whether she requires seven clean days. This matter was disputed by the Geonim. Mar Rav Yehudah Gaon ruled that she does not require seven clean days, in accordance with the Gemara in Niddah concerning the daughters of the Cutheans, and his disciples ruled likewise. Mar Rav Tzemach Gaon and all the members of the academy disagreed with him, saying that nowadays we are not expert in distinguishing between easing and difficult labor, and the matter involves a possible liability of karet. Furthermore, Rav Zeira said that the daughters of Israel accepted stringency upon themselves. Since Rav Zeira stated this without qualification, it may be said that women who give birth also accepted this stringency. Rav Yehudah bar Yakar likewise ruled that the statement was unqualified and therefore includes a woman who gives birth. The Rif also applies Rav Zeira's enactment even during the days of blood of purity. See there. (End of addendum.)
The days following childbirth, etc. See Tosafot, 37a, s.v. 'Abaye,' etc., and the view of Rav Saadiah, etc. All the Geonim and all the halachic authorities ruled this way, unlike Rabbeinu Tam.
And if, etc. As stated explicitly in Scripture.
And if she immersed, etc. See Pesachim 90b, where it was taught: I might think, etc., that she remains in her state of niddah for all seven days. A woman after childbirth is compared to a niddah.
And afterward, etc.; however, etc. See the Taz and the Shach.
But if, etc. The Rif and Ramban wrote that the Sages made no distinction, etc. The Rashba wrote that perhaps the child's head will emerge beyond the outer chamber, and the matter will lead to error.
Although, etc. As stated in Torat Kohanim regarding the blood of purity: although she continues to see blood. This is also stated in the Mishnah and Gemara in many places.
