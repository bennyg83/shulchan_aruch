The laws of a woman who gives birth and who miscarries; it contains 14 se’ifim. A woman who gives birth, even if she did not see blood, is tamei like a niddah — whether she gave birth to a live fetus or to a dead one, and even a miscarriage. And how many are her days of tumah? Now, in this time, all women who give birth are considered as women who gave birth in zivah, and they need to count 7 nekiyim. It follows that one who gives birth to a male sits 7 [days] for the birth and 7 nekiyim for zivah, and one who gives birth to a female sits 2 weeks for the birth and 7 nekiyim for zivah. The days of birth, which are 7 for a male and 14 for a female — if she did not see during them, they count toward her counting of her zivah. And if her 7 nekiyim were completed within the 14 [days] for a female, she is forbidden until the night of the 15th; and if she immersed before then, the immersion did not count for her. {Rama: And after 7 for a male and 14 for a female, she is permitted to her husband immediately, since she counted 7 nekiyim and did not again see. Nevertheless, there are places that practice that they do not immerse within 40 [days] for a male and 80 for a female (Beit Yosef in the name of Mahari”k, who brought the Tanya, and in Agudah, perek Eilu Perakim, and in Mahari”l); and one must not permit in a place where they practiced to be stringent (Rivash). But in a place where there is no custom, one should not be stringent at all; rather, immediately — if she did not see blood after 7 for a male and 14 for a female, and she counted 7 nekiyim — she is permitted to her husband (Terumas HaDeshen, siman 255). But if she again saw even a drop of blood like a mustard seed, she is tamei; even though by Torah law it is dam tohar, the custom has already spread throughout all Israel that we do not have intercourse upon dam tohar (Beit Yosef and Agur and Tur and poskim in the name of the Geonim), and its law is like other blood in every respect.}
