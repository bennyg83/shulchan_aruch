And he should not rent to him for less. Rashi explained that this law applies specifically to a house and courtyard, since there is no loss there; unlike a vineyard in siman 173 where sometimes there is a loss, and even so one deducts a fixed amount per year [as permitted]; and Tosafot disagree and hold that this law applies to all things. And the essential distinction: if [the lender] lent on the house it is like "zinvana" [sale] and permitted with a deduction; but here he lent not on his house — and this will be explained in siman 172 regarding the ruling. And in a courtyard that is normally rented, it is avak ribbit according to all opinions. So it seems the text should read — this seems correct to me. And it seems to me there is a further omission, and the text should read: "and nonetheless he is obligated to pay him rent for all [the time] unless he said to him explicitly: dwell in my courtyard for free" — and even if after the loan he said to him "dwell in my courtyard for free," even though it stands ready for rental, it is only avak ribbit; so it seems to me that the copyist erred from the first occurrence of "avak ribbit" to the second and skipped what Rema wrote in between — and this kind of error is common; and so it is in Darkei Moshe; and it is clarified in Beit Yosef in the name of students of Rashba: that since he did not exempt him explicitly, he must pay him his rent — for because he did a prohibited act does not weaken his right. And from here there is a puzzle on what Rema wrote in Choshen Mishpat siman 363: "one who says to his fellow 'dwell in my courtyard'" — meaning this is equivalent to saying "for free"; and here we say he must say it explicitly as Beit Yosef wrote — for "dwell" by itself implies the way people normally dwell, namely for rent — and I already wrote in siman 246 seif 17 that we do not rule according to that ruling in siman 363.
