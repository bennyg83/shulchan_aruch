He may not make use of his slave for labor. Had he not lent him money, it would be permitted, because it is beneficial to the slave's master that the slave not become accustomed to idleness, as stated in Choshen Mishpat, siman 363. Here, however, because he lent him money, it appears as interest.
And he may not rent from him for less. Rashi explains that this law applies specifically to a house or courtyard, where there is no possibility of loss. This is unlike a vineyard in siman 173, where there can sometimes be a loss, and nevertheless the lender deducts a fixed amount for each year. Tosafos disagree and hold that this law applies to all types of property. The essential distinction is that where he lent money against the house itself, the arrangement resembles a sale and is permitted through a deduction. Here, however, he lent him money independently of the house. The practical halacha will be explained in siman 172.
