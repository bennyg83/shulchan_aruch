Work. Only public, conspicuous services are forbidden. Lending tools, animals, and similar items remains permitted where one would have done so even without the loan. If the parties were known friends who commonly lent one another a courtyard before the loan, they may continue afterward, though Maharshal limits this to nonpublic benefits. A borrower should not publicly honor the lender with synagogue honors unless everyone knows it is unrelated to the loan. Shach.
For rent. Even if the courtyard is not ordinarily rented, use may still constitute avak ribbit. If it is ordinarily rented, the occupant must pay rent because he caused monetary loss. Silence by the owner does not necessarily imply free use. If after the loan the lender says, 'Live in my courtyard free,' without tying it to the loan, it is avak ribbit and rent is not recoverable. If he explicitly says, 'Live there as compensation for the loan,' it may be fixed interest. Shach distinguishes between an already extended due date and an ongoing loan payable at will. Taz questions the interpretation of an unqualified invitation to live there.
