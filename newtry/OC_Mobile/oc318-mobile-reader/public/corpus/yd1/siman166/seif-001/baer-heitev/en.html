He said that they were the words of a cow, but they were not allowed to borrow his brides or his daughter, and that he would have been allowed to do so, and that he was told that he would not have loved it as if he were not allowed to borrow any of his or her And, such as happening at a time or buying a scroll, he even knows that at the same time, he is employed by an AHP because he has a similar word to a house and a slave, if he is not known to anyone who is not an employer of the Levites:
For rent. Even if the courtyard does not stand for rent, it is still avak ribit. The Beit Yosef writes that although it is avak ribit and does not go out through judges as ribit, nevertheless he must pay rent if the courtyard stands for rent; even ordinarily he must pay rent, for he caused him monetary loss, and the fact that there is a prohibition does not weaken his right. Even if the owner knew he was living there and was silent, it does not imply that he intended him to live there free. If, when lending him, he said, "live in my courtyard," he must pay rent. However, if after lending he said, "live in my courtyard for free," and did not mention that it was because of the loan, then although it stands for rent it is only avak ribit, since he did not stipulate it, and if he lived there he is exempt from paying rent. This implies that if he said, "live for free in my courtyard because of your loan," even after the loan it is fixed ribit. It appears to me that the same applies where it does not stand for rent, if the circumstances are such that he would have had to pay rent had he not lent him, such as if he told him to leave, or the lender suffered even slight loss, such as blackening the walls, or he indicated intent to pay, as written in Choshen Mishpat there; in all such cases we extract from him. Even if he paid and the borrower did not claim it, the borrower may always claim it in court, for he did not waive it unless explicit. Alternatively, even if he explicitly waived, it is a mistaken waiver; this requires study where he explicitly waived. The Tur's view implies that after the loan, even if he said, "live in my courtyard for free because of your money," it is only avak ribit. It appears that the Tur speaks where he already extended the time of the loan, while the Beit Yosef speaks where he had not extended the time and every day was due; therefore, if he says "because of your loan," it is fixed ribit, since he can claim repayment whenever he wishes. However, where he says at the time he extends the term, "live in my courtyard for free," even if he did not say "because of your loan," it is fixed ribit according to the one who does not require stipulation at the time the money is given. The Maggid Mishneh writes that if he lived there without the owners' knowledge, and when they learned they let him continue because of the loan, it is avak ribit. Thus the Shach. The Taz asks from what the Rama writes in Choshen Mishpat siman 363, that one who says to another, "live in my courtyard," means for free, while here Beit Yosef requires him to say so explicitly, since a plain statement of "live" implies the normal way people live, with rent.
