Even in courtyard, etc. In the first version of Rava and R. Yehuda son of R. Chiya — they hold thus.
And if he dwelt, etc. Rif wrote: even though we hold like first version, nevertheless it does not come out in court, for it is only avak ribbit, as stated initially not, etc.; but second version is fixed ribbit; and Ramban wrote on this and Rosh brought there that Rif's words do not apply to our sugya, which deals without standing, etc. — for there in first version even avak ribbit it is not, and need not pay Israel; since he took nothing from him; and he brought proof from what is stated: my houses returned and we do not find he gave masters anything; and so in second version it is not fixed ribbit even for one who engages in renting, for we say in first chapter there is no ribbit without neshech; and likewise in first version need not pay Israel; and this is what he wrote: and even if, etc.
And for Rambam. From what is stated in first version: even though, etc. — he lent him, etc. — must, etc. — evidently must return to pay Israel; and Ramban wrote this "must" speaks initially that he not dwell without rent; and plain meaning of Gemara "he lent him and dwelt" implies like Rambam.
And in courtyard, etc. As above that Ramban establishes Rif's words in such case; and he said: and in courtyard, etc. — meaning even for one who does not engage in renting; and as stated CM siman 363 seif 6.
