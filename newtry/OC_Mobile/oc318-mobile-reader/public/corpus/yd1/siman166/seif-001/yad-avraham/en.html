(Siman 166, Shulchan Aruch, seif 2) If he said to him, "Lend me money and live in my courtyard," if the courtyard is ordinarily rented out this constitutes fixed interest, and if it is not ordinarily rented out it constitutes the semblance of interest. This requires further study. According to what the Rama wrote at the end of siman 160, it should constitute fixed interest even where the courtyard is not ordinarily rented out, in a case of "lend me," because the owner possesses a benefit of discretion, since he can allow whomever he wishes to live there for free. According to the one who says that a benefit of discretion has monetary value, it is fixed interest, in accordance with the Rambam's view as cited by the Tur. See Lechem Mishneh, chapter 5 of Hilchos Ishus, law 6, and examine carefully. This also appears explicitly from the Rosh in the chapter Eizehu Neshech, which is the source of the Rama's gloss at the end of siman 160, that according to the one who says that a benefit of discretion has monetary value it constitutes fixed interest. Only according to the one who says that a benefit of discretion has no monetary value is it merely the semblance of interest. This requires further study, because at the end of the chapter Ha'ish Mekadesh, daf 59, the Rosh rules like the one who says that a benefit of discretion has monetary value, whereas in the chapter Eizehu Neshech, siman 17, he implies that it has no monetary value. See there, where he also wrote this in the name of the Ramban. This requires further study, because the poskim wrote in the name of the Ramban that a benefit of discretion does have monetary value, and the Shach cites them in Choshen Mishpat at the end of siman 350. However, according to the Rambam, who rules in chapter 5 of Hilchos Ishus and chapter 2 of Hilchos Geneivah that a benefit of discretion has no monetary value, there is no difficulty as to how he can hold that with a courtyard not ordinarily rented out it is fixed interest in a case of "lend me." One can say that he follows his own reasoning, for he holds that even the smallest amount of interest is recoverable through the courts, as he wrote at the beginning of chapter 6, and certainly there is at least some amount of benefit here. Examine carefully. Similarly, the Shach wrote in Choshen Mishpat, siman 37, seif katan 9, regarding personal interest that disqualifies testimony, that since financial interest is not required and even any form of benefit disqualifies, a benefit of discretion is regarded as money according to all opinions. See Sha'ar HaMelech, chapter 5 of Hilchos Ishus, who discusses this at length. As for the Lechem Mishneh's powerful difficulty that one can find an increase without biting interest, and similarly Maharam Schiff, according to the Rambam there is no difficulty, because he derives even the smallest amount from the verse "any matter of biting interest," as the Mishneh LaMelech wrote. If so, there is indeed biting interest. According to Tosafos in chapter 4 of Kesubos, who hold that there is no prohibition in such a case where it is worth less than a perutah, and a benefit of discretion has no monetary value according to Rava, as the Shach wrote there, and as is also implied in Bava Metzia, daf 11b, nevertheless the verse necessarily does not discuss a case such as a courtyard that is not ordinarily rented out, as the Ma'adanei Melech wrote. For the verse says, "Do not take," and if you took it, return it, whereas here this does not apply, as the Rosh wrote there. Nor can biting interest exist without an increase even where he says, "Throw a dinar into the sea and I will lend you a maneh," because the Ritva wrote in the name of the Ramban in the chapter Eizehu Neshech, daf 69, that this constitutes fixed interest, unlike the Mishneh LaMelech at the end of chapter 5 of Hilchos Malveh, who was uncertain about this. There it is different, because he acted this way at the lender's command, and by the law of a guarantor it is regarded as though the dinar first came into the lender's hand, so there is also an increase.
