And if it is not ordinarily rented out, etc. Rambam holds that where the lender says, 'Lend me,' even a courtyard that is not ordinarily rented out produces fixed interest. The Beit Yosef writes that Rambam's reason is that since he stipulated this with the borrower, it is as though he rents the courtyard from him from that moment. Hagahos Maimoniyos write that this is likewise the view of the Semag and the Ra'avad. Sefer HaTerumah, sha'ar 46, part 2, likewise writes that this is the view of the Rif and Rambam. It is astonishing that the Mechaber omitted this view. As a practical matter, it appears to me to be a doubt in the law, and the burden of proof rests upon the claimant.
