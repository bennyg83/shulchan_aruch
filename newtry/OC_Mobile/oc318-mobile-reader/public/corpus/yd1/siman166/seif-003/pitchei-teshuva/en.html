This is the primary view. See Noda B'Yehuda, Tinyana, Yoreh De'ah, siman 76. He writes that Rama ruled in accordance with the latter view specifically in such a case, where the borrower claims that the amount should be deducted for him. The borrower is considered the possessor, and the lender seeks to extract money from him. Relative to the other creditors, however, they are not considered possessors against this creditor, and no deduction is made for him. See there.
