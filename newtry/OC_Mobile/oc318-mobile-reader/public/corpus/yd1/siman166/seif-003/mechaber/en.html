In a case where it is avak ribbis, some say that even if he has not yet repaid, and the borrower demands that he deduct from his debt the measure of the rent, we do not deduct for him; and some say that we do deduct for him. {Rama: And the latter reasoning is the primary one (it is the view of Rosh and the Tur and R' Ephraim and Rashba). One who received money as a loan from someone, and also learns with his son, and stipulated with him that he will give him the expenses for this: even though he would have given him the expenses without the loan because he learned with his son, even so it is forbidden, for it is like “lend to me and live in my courtyard,” which is forbidden even when it is not meant to be rented (in Mordechai, perek Eizehu Neshech, in the name of a responsum of Maharam; and Maharik, shoresh 119), and the same applies to any similar case. However, if he gives the money by way of a gift, and if the borrower wishes he can keep it for himself and not pay him, it is permitted, even though he afterward pays him. One who does not need to borrow money except for the benefit of the lender — he says to him, “Build this ruin for yourself, and everything that you spend on this is upon me to pay you; and as long as I do not pay you, live in it for free” — it is permitted, since there is no loan here, only that he did [this] for the benefit of the lender (Beit Yosef in the name of Ba’al HaTerumos).}
