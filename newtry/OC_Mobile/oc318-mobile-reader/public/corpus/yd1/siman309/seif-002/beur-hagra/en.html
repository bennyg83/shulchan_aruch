(Likkut) And if there is no, etc. Like the first tanna, as stated there in the Gemara, and there 37, and at the end of Shabbat chapter 3; learn from it.
And nowadays, etc. The Rosh and Tosafot there s.v. “pasak,” “however,” etc., because one must be a great sage and also expert in seeing blemishes. This is what is said there: regarding a vow, a sage; regarding a bekhor, an expert. See the Rosh there. The Beit Yosef wrote another reason: there is no nasi in Eretz Yisrael from whom to receive permission. This is based on the Rambam's statement at the beginning of chapter 3: one may not inspect, etc., except by an expert to whom the nasi in Eretz Yisrael gave permission, etc. It appears he inferred this from the fact that he is called “expert,” as at the beginning of Sanhedrin regarding fines, where experts are required. But it has already been written that this is no proof. There too, 5a, regarding judging alone, it says “expert,” and there it says even though he did not receive permission. It further appears that he inferred it from what is stated in Bekhorot there: permitting a bekhor outside Eretz Yisrael, etc.; this is what the Rambam wrote there: likewise a bekhor that left to outside Eretz Yisrael, etc. But Rashi there s.v. “outside Eretz Yisrael” explained: that it is not fit, etc. Nor can one infer from Sanhedrin chapter 1, 5a, which says he permits bekhorot; there too it means he issues rulings, etc., as stated there: and since he has learned, etc., because of practice, etc., specifically with a student, as above siman 242 seif 4. However, one can say from the Yerushalmi in Chagigah chapter 1 and Nedarim chapter 10, where Rav asked Rabban Gamliel bar Rabbi after his father's death to give him permission to permit bekhorot; after one's teacher's death, permission is unnecessary. In Yoma 78a they asked Rabbi Eliezer the elder, etc.; this matter they left, etc.; see Rashi there. But Tosafot in Bekhorot there s.v. “in a place,” etc., wrote that it is unnecessary; see there. We also find several amoraim in Bavel who permitted bekhorot alone, perhaps through permission. Tosafot's words too can be explained so: they said only that semikhah is unnecessary, as in Yoma there, and it is only because of the nasi's honor. Yet now that there is no nasi, perhaps it is unnecessary. This is what Tosafot there s.v. “pasak,” “however nowadays it does not appear,” etc., imply: but regarding permission there is no objection. So too the Rosh at the end of Bekhorot: nowadays, when there is no sage and expert, etc., because we are not expert in them nowadays. He says “sage and expert” as above, and as written in chapter 5 seif 11. Rabbi Yonah wrote, etc. In Derishah u-Perishah he wrote both reasons of the Beit Yosef and attributed them to the Rosh, but he erred in this and followed the Beit Yosef's words.

And a notch, etc. The Rosh there in the name of Ri inferred that it is a clear blemish, since the Gemara in several places uses this case, “one who notches an ear,” etc., in Bava Kamma chapter 9, 98a, and Bekhorot 34b; learn from it. But the Rambam wrote only the three above, and in that Mishnah only those three are mentioned. Why did they not mention notching the ear? What the Gemara says about notching the ear can be because it is the first blemish in the Mishnah. According to Ri, who permits a notched ear, the same applies to any blemish that is very recognizable and does not require expertise, even if ignoramuses do not know it is a blemish. The need for experts is not because of wisdom but because of expertise, as above. Therefore the Rosh in chapter 6 wrote several blemishes, and the Tur wrote them here; the Rosh wrote that non-obvious blemishes are such as eye films and the like. But the Rambam's view is not so; he follows his approach that it depends on receiving permission, and therefore one may permit only a blemish known to everyone. The Rosh follows his own approach. The Shulchan Aruch's words are astonishing, for he combined the Rambam's view with Ri's view and held that notching the ear is a clear blemish even according to the Rambam. This is not so. Are the other blemishes listed by the Rosh less clear than a notched ear? The Rosh mentioned notching the ear as a novelty, that even this is a clear blemish, while Shulchan Aruch omitted all the blemishes written by the Rosh and Tur and wrote notching the ear.
That it is more, etc. Meaning that nowadays it must be more recognizable to the eye; but by law, catching the fingernail is enough, as stated there 37b and in the first chapter of Chullin. Regarding the meaning of catching the fingernail, see what I wrote at the beginning of siman 18 in the name of the Rashba and the Raah, who disagree about it.

But Shulchan Aruch wrote only a notched ear, which is a defect, as above.
