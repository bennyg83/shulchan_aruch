To place it in a confined enclosure. See Responsa Noda BiYehudah, Yoreh Deah, siman 81, who explains that the prohibition is because it causes a loss to the kohen. According to this, in the case of a doubtful firstborn, which need not be given to a kohen even after it develops a blemish because the burden of proof rests on the claimant, one would be permitted to confine it until it dies. Rabbi Moshe Fishels disputed this, and the author retracted and agreed that such confinement is forbidden even for a doubtful firstborn; see simanim 62 and 83. See also Chatam Sofer, at the end of simanim 314 and 318.
