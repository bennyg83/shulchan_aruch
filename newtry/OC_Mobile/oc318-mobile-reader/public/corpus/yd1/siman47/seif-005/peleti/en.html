The hadra dekaneta, etc. — kosher for the length of a finger. Yeshuot Malko wondered that it contradicts seif 1 for all views, that it requires even for the first view without any separation, only return and become mixed at its beginning and end — see there; and Shach explained in s.k. 7: in seif 1 it deals when two emerge from the kivah, but here only one intestine emerges and afterward another kosher intestine emerges, etc., end of his words. And his words appear astonishing: for in seif 1 we say that until finger-width they are together and afterward return to split, etc.; and if so behold it emerged from an intestine that already passed a finger and was as one, and behold it is separated from the intestine and not from the kivah — nevertheless we require return and becoming mixed, and without this it is invalid. And it is strained to say that even though they are in one finger-width attachment, nevertheless it is recognizable in a crack and the like that they are two intestines that only connected at the beginning; and the matters are very forced. And it appears a great man said a word one may not divert him from: Rosh's version in Behag — "granted, according to the one who says even though they do not return and become mixed, this is what teaches 'until a finger' — rather 'like a finger'"; and "what do they end" and "until a finger," and they answer "a finger from below"; and Rosh explained thus: certainly at the place of their attachment and emergence they were first attached as one for a finger-width, and afterward split and separated and became two; and the Gemara challenges: granted according to the one who says they do not return and become mixed, this is what teaches "and they end until a finger" as he explained — first attached for a finger-width and afterward separated and are completely two intestinal children not attached at all; but according to the one who says they return and become mixed, what is "and they end until a finger" — do they not need to connect together; and they answer "what do they end" — "they end below"; he explained they return and become mixed for a finger-width near the refuse house, end of his words. And one may understand the Gemara's difficulty: since we explain "they end" until a finger even for the one who says they do not return — namely at the beginning of their root and attachment it was like a finger and afterward separated — so too "they return and become mixed" means: at least at the beginning of their attachment they must be together a finger, and this is "and they end until a finger"; and nevertheless they must return and become mixed; and in this there is no limit — in whatever place in the body it should be so for a finger-width or more; at least they must return and become one; and we require two things: from the beginning of their separation they must be as one for a finger-width, and also the splits must return and come as one; and see seif 1. And if so, what does the Gemara challenge, and it requires further study. It appears the Gemara knew that from the intestines it is common to split branches and many splits — and this is the novelty in an animal; but all splits from intestines and not from the kivah — also all splits have no mouth at their end to empty refuse, only closed without a mouth, and the moisture that enters there is what walks within the intestines because the entrance closed on it. And if so the Gemara's words are fine: granted according to the one who says they return and become mixed, one may say "they end until like a finger" above at the beginning of the intestines' separation — finger-width; and the reason: then this does not emerge from the kivah but from intestines, and such is the way in many splits; and if all splits have no mouth at all because they return to where they came — here too it deals thus that they have no mouth and do not return and become mixed, only the intestine returns to walk. And therefore we require that at its beginning it be like a finger together, and afterward it splits; and if so this is not an extra but merely the way the intestine spreads branches. But according to the one who says they return and become mixed — why need the way, for on the contrary there they do not return to become mixed and have no mouth at all; and if so we do not compare it to that case — why need at its beginning to be as one like a finger. And if it must be a branch from the intestine and not from proximity — even a branch from the intestine is considered an extra, for it is not their way that all have no mouth, and this one has a mouth. And the Gemara answers: in truth above there is no relevance whether they are as one or not, for even if it emerges from the kivah it is kosher, since below it becomes mixed for a finger-width — it is not considered an extra in any manner. And Taz's words are correct. If so, we derive: when they emerge from the intestines and not from the kivah and have no mouth at all because it is closed — kosher, per Rosh's ruling; and follow his method and examine. And further one may say: the author found a way for himself in understanding the Gemara per Behag's version — "granted according to the one who says they do not return and become mixed, this is what teaches 'and they end' — but according to the one who says they return and become mixed, what is 'and they end,'" and they answer "they end below"; and the matter is astonishing. It appears the author certainly holds like experience testifies: sometimes from the intestines a branch and splits emerge, only they are not larger than a finger — therefore the Sages gave them all the measure of a finger, for a branch's way is to be a finger. And if so the Gemara's difficulty is fine: granted according to the one who says they do not return and become mixed — only kosher, since such is the order — a branch emerges; if so it need only be a finger, and this is "and they end until a finger," and it has no greater length like the way of splitting branches. But according to the one who says they return and become mixed — if so there is no extra because of the way of branches, only because they become mixed they are one; and if so why need the length of a finger; and they answer: in truth he explained "like a finger" below, before mixing to emerge — there must be a finger that they mix together; and Taz's words are correct, and these are Shulchan Aruch's words; and even though at the beginning of Rosh's words it implies a finger is not precise, but at the end he wrote "and my teachers' words stood who permitted for one intestine like a finger emerging" — behold a finger precisely; and understand the difference: that which he permitted below is specifically narrowed like a finger, which is the way of branches; and in seif 1 it deals when they are larger than a finger; and so it is proper to rule specifically a finger that is nullified and not more.
