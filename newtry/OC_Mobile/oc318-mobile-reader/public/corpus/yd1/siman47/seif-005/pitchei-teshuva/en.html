Be'er Heitev in the name of Peri Chadash; and see in responsa Noda BiYehudah Tinyana Yoreh De'ah siman 21, where they asked about an opening found in the intestinal children near their emergence from the kivah, and it goes on a slope and is found so in many animals; and the butchers say most animals are thus and it comes from the liver's membranes; and he answered: since this opening is always in one form on a slope in one place near the kivah, one may be lenient and rely on those who say it is the vein going from the intestinal children to the liver, and its function is that the liver throws bile through this vein to the intestinal children and from there to the kivah to digest food; and this vein is also mentioned in several compositions. And one need not fear lest perhaps this vein was severed in the animal's lifetime and it is treifah from perforation of the intestines — for behold refuse would fall from there into the body cavity; on the contrary we suspend that after slaughter it was severed at the time of removing the liver; and further that even if certainly severed in the animal's lifetime there is room to be lenient and say this vein brings in but does not take out into the intestinal children to place a drop of bile there, but it takes out — it has no nature to expel refuse. However, even though we compare, we do not act to permit where it is certain it was severed from life; but where one may suspend it was done after slaughter — certainly kosher. Also it is obvious there is no obligation at all to check and search for this opening — see there.
