An animal, etc., until "and there is no extra here." All this is Rambam's language, and he explains what is written "in what matter is this stated," etc., "but," etc. — here there is no distinction between fowl and animal, for the distinction is only when they emerge from two places. And the version is like R' Shimshon Kayyara, who wrote: Rosh wrote "granted, according to the one who says they do not return," etc., "but," etc., "what is the meaning of 'they end' until like a finger" — rather "like a finger"; and (°) for the one who says they do not return and become mixed, that they emerge from one place and split like fingers from the hand — above they become mixed; and for the one who says they return and become mixed — like a finger from below, where there they become mixed like fingers from the hand. And this is what he said "but," etc., "and they end like a finger," and he ruled like the one who says they return and become mixed — stringently; and see Lechem Mishneh.
And some say, etc., "in what matter is this stated in an animal," etc. All this is Tur's view, and he disagrees on two points, per the version above, except that the version reads "until like a finger," and it refers to the measure that their mixing requires a finger — for the one who says they do not return, above; and for the one who says they return, below; and he ruled like both of them stringently, and explains "in fowl — kosher" refers to all of it, per Rashi's first explanation; and so Rashba explained in this language; and Ran wrote in the name of Behag the version is reversed: "granted, according to the one who says they return," etc., "what is the meaning of 'they end' above" — regarding their dispute; and for halacha everything is one: for the one who says they return, above the measure is a finger, etc., as above; and he also explained like both stringencies, as above.
