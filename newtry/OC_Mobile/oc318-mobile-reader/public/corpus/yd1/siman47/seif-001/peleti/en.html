An animal in which were found, etc. — we say on page 58b: two intestines emerging from the animal as one — treifah; and corresponding to them in fowl — kosher. In what matter? When they emerge in two places; but when they emerge in one place and end until like a finger — kosher. And Rashi explained: corresponding to them in fowl — sometimes in fowl it is so, and even though we do not find it so for it, they established it; and some explain that every fowl's way is thus, etc., end of his words. And Rashba brought these two explanations and wrote: if extra intestines are found in fowl, whether kosher or treifah is disputed; and he agreed with Rashi's first explanation that it is kosher, for such is the Sages' view. But Rambam, who wrote "and behold it is separated whether in animal whether in fowl" — treifah — implies he holds like Rashi's second explanation; and this is simple. And behold Shulchan Aruch in seif 1 copied Rambam's language, as stated; and yet in seif 2 he wrote that in fowl it is kosher — namely Rashi's first explanation, like Rashba; and the matters contradict. And Shach in s.k. 2 explained: that which Shulchan Aruch permits in seif 2 is when they return and become mixed; and that which he forbids in seif 1 is when they remain separated and do not become mixed. And I did not understand his words, for perforce: if you explain like Rashi's first explanation, behold the Gemara said "and corresponding to them in fowl — kosher" in what matter when they emerge in two places — behold even two places in fowl are kosher; and when they return and become mixed — according to the first view even in an animal it is kosher; and what is the novelty of fowl. And if they do not return and become mixed, nevertheless in fowl kosher — behold this is not Shach's intent, who wanted to say that even Rambam concedes in this. And Bach wanted to reconcile that the Gemara deals when they are separated in a manner that they do not end like a finger, but nevertheless they are not so separated that they are considered extra; and the matter is forced in itself — no need to elaborate. And if you explain like the second view — whence do we distinguish at all between fowl and animal. And Taz in s.k. 2 implies that the two views in Shulchan Aruch are Rashi's two explanations: the first view in Rambam copies Rashi's second explanation, and in the second view he brought the disputants' second view — Rashi's first explanation; and the matter needs study: what relevance do Rashi's two explanations on "and corresponding to them in fowl" have to Shulchan Aruch tying them to one another. Therefore it appears that certainly Rashi explained on "and corresponding to them in fowl" two explanations; and who will decide to be lenient without proof in a safek d'oraisa. However one may understand that it says "in what matter when they emerge in two places, but in one place and they end until a finger — kosher"; and the disputants on Rif who explained "and they end until like a finger" that at their beginning they must be attached for a finger-width and afterward split — it is difficult: if we distinguish between two places and one place, let us distinguish in our case too: even in one place, if attached like a finger and afterward split — kosher; and if not attached like a finger — treifah; and all the more when not attached at all — treifah. And it must be said per Rashi's first explanation that in fowl it is kosher; and if so, when it teaches where they are not attached like a finger — treifah, it should have said: in this view fowl is kosher; but when emerging in two places, even in fowl treifah — it teaches us: even in two places, specifically in an animal treifah; behold corresponding to it in fowl kosher. And if so, Rashi's first explanation is necessary; but per Rif's explanation it is the same: two places means one place, for everything depends on whether they emerge from one root like a finger — namely two places means they do not emerge from one root like a finger; and if so there is no need at all for Rashi's first explanation. And if so, Shulchan Aruch's words are correct in Rambam's law, which is like Rif — if so there is no proof to either of Rashi's explanations; therefore he ruled to be stringent that even in fowl it is treifah. But for the first opinion that requires attachment like a finger, Rashi's first explanation is proven — difficult is the challenge above to distinguish and teach in our case; and it must be said "fowl" is needed; and if so, Rashi's law that fowl is kosher is necessary, and he ruled thus; and examine well: therefore he wrote "in what matter in an animal," etc. And Taz's words are correct.
And behold both are separated in the middle — Peri Chadash explained well for this method the Gemara's words: granted, according to the one who says they return and become mixed, "they end until like a finger" means they return and the intestines become like one finger; but according to the one who says even though they do not return and become mixed, what is "until like a finger," and they answer "like a finger" — meaning like a finger splitting from the hand that does not return to become mixed to be two fingers as one; so too splitting in intestinal children. And he wrote: not like Ran who strained; and in truth Ran's main strain is because he followed the majority of versions that say in them "finger" below; but in truth even in the version "from below" Peri Chadash's words are correct: the explanation of "from below" is not the end of the splits near the refuse house, but below is called the place of splits relative to their root — the place where they come and split; this is called below in their root. And a sign for the matter: above siman 39 seif 6 — however if the hadra stuck below in its root, etc., behold the place of the root is called below; and so here the splits of the intestines — the place of their emergence from the root is called below. And Taz's words are correct per Peri Chadash's explanation.
