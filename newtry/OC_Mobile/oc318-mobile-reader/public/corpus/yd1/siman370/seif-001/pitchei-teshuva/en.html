And it is proper to be stringent. See Baer Heitev in Bach's name and Responsa Beit Yaakov, siman 130, who argues that according to Bach, if the concern is tumah, a kohen who is an expert physician should be prohibited from entering the house of a dying patient in order to treat him. In Nachalat Tzvi I rejected this and concluded that according to everyone a kohen physician may enter to provide medical care, even if another non-kohen physician is available. Chatam Sofer, siman 338, later likewise writes that even according to authorities who prohibit a kohen from entering the house of a dying person as a matter of strict law, possible pikuach nefesh plainly permits entry and treatment when the patient needs a physician. He even writes that after a person has been declared dead, if any movement or sign of life is detected, a kohen must immediately enter because of the compounded possibility of pikuach nefesh. Regarding the presence of another non-kohen doctor, Chatam Sofer expresses some hesitation because Torah prohibitions are generally considered overridden, not fully permitted, by pikuach nefesh. I do not understand why he did not invoke the rule in siman 221, seif 4, that medical treatment is not equally obtainable from every person, which makes the availability of another physician not necessarily decisive. Chatam Sofer also addresses a town whose official physician was a kohen and where civil law prohibited burial until the doctor personally examined the body and certified death. One rav proposed permitting the kohen even to touch the corpse. Chatam Sofer rejects this emphatically and says there is no halachic license for the kohen himself. He then observes that the doctor was irreligious and had not asked for a ruling; the practical question was whether God-fearing Jews may summon him, or whether that violates lifnei iver or assisting sin. He concludes that if they can have him removed from the office, that is best. If they cannot, and otherwise the dead would remain unburied in disgrace, then from the community's perspective the deceased has become like a met mitzvah with no available buriers. Although this does not excuse the kohen personally, the community may summon him because it has no realistic alternative.
