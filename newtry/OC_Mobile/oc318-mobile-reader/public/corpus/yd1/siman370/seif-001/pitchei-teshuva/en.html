And it is good to be stringent. See Be'er Heitev in the name of the Bach. See Beit Yaakov responsa siman 130, who wrote that according to the Bach, who holds that by law one must be careful, if the reason is tumah, one should forbid a kohen who is an expert physician from becoming tamei to a goses by entering his house to treat him; see his reasoning. In Nachalat Tzvi I rejected his words and concluded that according to everyone a kohen physician may enter the house of a goses to perform some treatment, even if there is another physician who is not a kohen; see there. Afterward Chatam Sofer responsa was printed, and I saw that in siman 338 he also wrote that it is simpler than an egg in kutach that even according to the poskim who hold that a kohen is forbidden by strict law to enter the house of a goses, nevertheless because of possible pikuach nefesh it is permitted, and he should enter and treat him if we see that a physician is needed. More than this: even after we have determined him dead, if they notice any movement or fluttering, the kohen should hurry, be concerned, and enter because of a double doubt of pikuach nefesh. However, regarding whether there is another physician who is not a kohen, he hesitates there because no one holds that the whole Torah is wholly permitted in the face of pikuach nefesh; it is only suspended, see the Rosh, Yoma chapter 8 siman 14. I do not know why he did not mention siman 221 seif 4, where it is clear that this does not depend at all on whether it is permitted or merely suspended, because not every person, etc. See there further regarding a city whose physician is a kohen, and by the laws of the country the dead are not buried until the physician has examined him and testified that he has despaired of life. May this kohen enter and examine him? One rabbi, whose name is hidden, thought to permit him even to touch. Chatam Sofer z"l built a siege against this and discussed at length the nullity of such a position, that Heaven forbid one even consider it, and there is no permission at all. At the end he wrote that afterward he reflected that this physician did not ask and did not seek guidance, and he is among the lawless masses; the question is for those who are God-fearing, whether they may call him or whether it is forbidden because of lifnei iver and assisting transgressors. If so, it seems to me that if they can arrange to remove him entirely, how good. If they cannot, then the dead lie in disgrace without burial, and it is like a met mitzvah who has no buriers, for whom a kohen becomes tamei. Although this is no permission or excuse for the kohen himself, nevertheless since he does not listen to teachers and it is not in our power to remove him, for us it is like a met mitzvah and is permitted. This is a clear permission; see there at length.
