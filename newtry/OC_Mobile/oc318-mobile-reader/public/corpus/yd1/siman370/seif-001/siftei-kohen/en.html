And most of the flesh with it, etc. It is implied in Chullin 21 that with an elderly person, even without most of the flesh, it conveys tumah in a tent. So wrote Maharshal, chapter 1 of Chullin siman 44. Its implication there is that if he was made like a shard, meaning cut across the whole neck or through the spine to its hollow, or split in two, he conveys tumah in a tent; and so Maharshal wrote there.
From his back, etc. But not from the front.
Nevertheless, it is forbidden to enter, etc. Meaning: although there is no tumah here, there is desecration here.
And it is good to be stringent. The Bach wrote that by law one must be careful, and there is no place here to rule leniently; see there. However, it appears that one need not wake a kohen and tell him when there is a goses, as one must wake him when there is a dead body, as below siman 372. Likewise, if they told him and he is undressed, he may wait until he dresses and then leave. It is close to saying that he need not leave at all from a house in which there is a goses, for the language "it is forbidden to enter the house," etc., implies that only entering is forbidden. Still, it is proper to be stringent and leave, because from the Mordechai and other poskim it implies that there is no distinction.
