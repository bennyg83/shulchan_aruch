Missing gall bladder. In Sefer Chamudei Daniel he wrote: once a gall bladder was found whose skin was thick and inside white moisture and it is not bitter — treifah; end; and see Peri Megadim in Mishbetzot s.k. 2 who also doubted this law and declared treifah from doubt — see there; and requires further study whether tasting liver helps in such a case per Rikanti brought in acharonim in incident: small pouch was at gall bladder, tasted and was not bitter, permitted — not two gall bladders but a blister; if so here too perhaps main gall was absorbed in liver and this is only blister; or perhaps only where one is as required do we say so, not here. Further requires further study: if all moisture is sweet and only in part bitter taste — whether to permit in such a case — requires further study; and see s.k. following.
The liver. See Sefer Bnei Chayya who wrote: bird liver partly crushed and somewhat cut and no gall bladder found; tasted liver — no bitter taste; tasted gizzard — bitter like wormwood; and some wanted to be stringent saying we have no examination other than liver; but Mahar"a HaKohen permitted from double doubt: perhaps this bitterness found in gizzard is from gall that rotted and stuck to gizzard and by handling was uprooted from liver and poured to gizzard; and if you say it came from elsewhere — perhaps halacha is removed gall kosher; and it is double doubt turning; and further because it was crushed and somewhat cut we attribute gall was in that crushed cut place; Rav Maharach in Navasht agreed for this reason that gall tore to gizzard. Per what Taharat HaShulchan wrote and Peri Megadim in Sha'ar HaDin letter he: reason kosher via tasting liver — even though pouch is lacking, meaning we say presumably created lacking and bitterness absorbed in liver since gall's action is "liver angry," etc.; or pouch entirely adhered to liver until not visible — see there this permission does not apply: for what helps attributing that via handling afterward bitterness poured to gizzard — nevertheless pouch is lacking; cannot say case where pouch never lacking only empty; and on this he says we attribute bitterness poured to gizzard; if so kosher even if no bitterness at all as Peri Chadash s.k. 12 and all acharonim. However if we resolve our doubt in previous s.k. and say even if only part moisture bitter and rest sweet — kosher — you find this law here where gall was sweet and not whole and we attribute there was also part bitter moisture and via handling poured to gizzard; especially since question came that part liver was missing and can attribute gall was in that missing part and absorbed in that piece; and if before us bitter taste would be found — end; per his method: he holds if liver lost before tasting — kosher as written below s.k. 5.
And taste with his tongue. See responsum Zichron Yosef siman 4 who brought in name of Rashal: correct to rinse liver at tasting place from visible blood; and wrote on this it is only loss of Israel's money, for Rikanti wrote (brought in Ba'er Heitev s.k. 3): if tasted and bitter found and tasted again and no bitterness sensed — kosher; we attribute first licking removed all bitterness; and therefore concern perhaps he removes bitterness by rinsing and afterward will not sense bitter taste and will declare treifah improperly; therefore rely on Peri Chadash that here it suffices with ordinary licking — see there. And see responsum Shevut Yaakov chelek 1 siman 23 who wrote: that we taste liver is even if pouch too removed (meaning lacking — see Peri Megadim) — not like responsum Hilchot Ketannot — see there.
And if not — treifah. Ba'er Heitev and what he wrote: in responsum Orach Chaim he permits if liver lost before tasting — already Peri Megadim seized on him: on the contrary his view is to declare treifah — see there; and see Sefer Bnei Chayya who wrote in name of Rav Maharach in Navasht to permit — see there; and see responsum Shevut Yaakov chelek 2 siman 66 who also wrote in name of Orach Chaim to permit; and so he ruled practically; and wrote further he stood on experience many times no bitter taste in liver and saw gall stuck in intestines and fat; and sometimes small gall from fattiness of bird and not visible; and sometimes bitterness poured via stripping intestines from them; therefore it seemed simple: if intestines and fat lost and afterward no gall in liver — attribute stripped with intestines — see there.
And even now. See Shach: it is likely one will taste bitter taste; and for this reason Shevut Yaakov chelek 1 siman 23 wrote: if no gall found — permitted to roast liver on Yom Tov to taste, for we rely on majority — see there.
We may rely. See responsum Chinuch Beit Yosef siman 86 middle of answer who wrote: nevertheless he admits one may not rely on women and the like, etc. — see there; and so responsum Zichron Yosef chelek 14 end siman 4; and see Nekudot HaKesef what I wrote on this.
