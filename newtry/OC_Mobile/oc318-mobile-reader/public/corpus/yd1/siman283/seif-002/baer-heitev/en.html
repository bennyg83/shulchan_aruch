A scroll. Rif and the other poskim permit this because it is a time to act for Hashem, for not everyone knows the passages by heart and not everyone has chumashim available in the synagogue. Otherwise they may come to neglect Keriat Shema and prayer. Bach writes that this is the widespread custom in all countries. Shach adds that for the same reason one may not write portions of the Torah as separate scrolls, one may not write a single Torah passage as a scroll even for a child to learn from. A complete chumash as an independent volume is permitted, but not isolated scrolls. Taz writes that Bach's statement that the custom is to permit applies to material written for study because of "it is a time to act for Hashem." Those who write verses on a wall merely as a good-luck sign do not act properly, because the Torah was given as a complete, sealed work. See siman 17.
