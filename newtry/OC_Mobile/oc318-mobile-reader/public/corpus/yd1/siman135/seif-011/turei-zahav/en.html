One must rinse it three times. So Rabbeinu Tam explained what they said in the Gemara: cup the first time and second time is forbidden — this refers to rinsing that they rinsed only two times; and if so bedi'eved it is forbidden if they did not rinse three times, since the Gemara used the language "forbidden"; and even those who disagree with Rabbeinu Tam in the simple explanation admit to him regarding the law, as Beit Yosef brought — except it is possible specifically lechatchilah they are forbidden, but bedi'eved it is possible it is permitted even without rinsing at all when dry, as we will mention in siman 137 seif 1; and also according to Rabbeinu Tam's explanation it is possible the language "forbidden" in the Gemara means specifically lechatchilah; and see above siman 137; and from Rama's language here it implies if he did not rinse at all it is forbidden even bedi'eved, and we do not find here a prohibition bedi'eved according to Rama's view, except if he held kosher wine in a forbidden vessel me'ol le'ol, as written siman 137, since it is not put in for permanence and there is also no 60 in kosher wine against the coating of forbidden wine on the vessel's surface that was not yet rinsed; and in this way Rama also wrote nearby; and there are those lenient bedi'eved — it implies that without rinsing at all it is forbidden bedi'eved, namely in the manner I mentioned; and nevertheless it seems to me one should not forbid bedi'eved, as I wrote in siman 137 seif 1.
