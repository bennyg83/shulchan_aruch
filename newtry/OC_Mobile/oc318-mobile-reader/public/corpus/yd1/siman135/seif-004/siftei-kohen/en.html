Earthenware vessels, etc. Even if he does not put them in for permanent storage — and so is the conclusion of most poskim; and so it is in Atzei Zahav.
Filling and emptying. Although regarding hagalah we rule that an earthenware vessel can never be fully purged of what it absorbed, as explained above in siman 121 and in Orach Chayyim, siman 451, the present case is different. Yayin nesech was absorbed through cold use, and repeated filling with water weakens the taste of the wine. This is the language of the Levush. See also above, siman 128, seif katan 15.
If he used them, etc. Specifically when there was no yayin nesech in it for 24 hours; but if there was for 24 hours — there is no distinction between sated and not sated; rather it always requires filling and pouring — as above siman 93 seif 6.
Some say that it requires, etc. This is difficult. The Mechaber first cites the Tur that from the second use onward the vessel is already saturated, while Beit Yosef writes that Sefer HaTerumah and Hagahos Maimoniyos, who are the 'some say,' explain the sugya differently. If so, he should have written: 'Some say that if water was placed in it only twice, it requires filling and emptying.' It appears that even the Tur agrees that water does not saturate the vessel after only two uses; only wine does. Beit Yosef's statement that Sefer HaTerumah and Hagahos Maimoniyos explain the sugya differently may concern interpretation rather than practical law, unlike Darkei Moshe's understanding that they disagree in practice. Because the plain language of the Tur and poskim does not clearly make this distinction, the Mechaber presented the position of Sefer HaTerumah and Hagahos Maimoniyos as 'some say' specifically regarding water. Had he held there was no distinction between water and wine, he could simply have said that two uses never suffice.
And they remained in them, etc. Beit Yosef writes that this applies specifically to a vessel not ordinarily used for permanent storage. A vessel used for permanent storage requires filling and emptying even if the non-Jew placed wine in it only briefly. The Bach, however, writes that an earthenware vessel already saturated by Jewish use needs only rinsing when a non-Jew later uses it, even if it is ordinarily used for permanent storage. Wooden and stone vessels differ because they never become saturated in the same manner. In my humble opinion, however, an earthenware vessel that was not yet saturated requires filling and emptying even if the non-Jew used it only briefly; this is also implied by Rashba as cited by the Tur.
Even with ours that the gentile touched them, etc. Namely according to what Beit Yosef explained in Tur's words; but Maharshal challenged on him and explained Tur's words that even l'chatchilah the practice is to permit drinking in it — and it is not comparable to wooden vessels in seif 1; for earthenware, since they are already sated, they no longer absorb; and Derishah brings it; and so Bach wrote.
