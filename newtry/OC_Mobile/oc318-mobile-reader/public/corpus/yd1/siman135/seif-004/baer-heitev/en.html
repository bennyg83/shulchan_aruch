Used. Shach wrote: specifically when it did not contain yayin nesech for a full day-and-night period; but if it did contain yayin nesech for a full day-and-night, there is no distinction between sated and not sated, as above siman 93:
Irui. The Shach writes that this is difficult, for at first he wrote that from the second time onward they are saturated. It appears to me that he holds that they become saturated after two times specifically with wine, but not with water. Large. The Beit Yosef writes that this is specifically where one does not place it in for long-term storage, but if it is used for long-term storage, even if the non-Jew placed wine in it only temporarily it requires irui. The Bach, however, writes that with an earthenware vessel, even if used for long-term storage, where it was already saturated from absorbing in the Jew's possession, it does not require irui when a non-Jew later used it; rinsing suffices. But whenever it was not saturated from absorbing in the Jew's possession, even if the non-Jew used it only temporarily, it requires irui. And bedieved. Maharshal writes that even lechatchilah the practice is to permit drinking from it, and it is unlike the wooden vessel in seif 1, for earthenware vessels, once saturated, no longer absorb. The Taz rules like the Rama, that this is only bedieved and not like Maharshal.
