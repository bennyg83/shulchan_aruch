Pitched. Even if wine is not stored in it permanently. Although earthenware ordinarily cannot be kashered through hagalah, here the forbidden wine was used cold, and filling with water weakens the wine's taste. Levush.
Was used. Shach writes that this applies only where yayin nesech had not remained in it for twenty-four hours. If it remained for twenty-four hours, there is no distinction between a vessel already saturated and one not yet saturated, as stated in siman 93.
Filling and pouring. Shach asks that earlier it was said that from the second use onward the vessel is already saturated. He answers that it becomes saturated after two uses of wine, but not after two uses of water.
Large. Beit Yosef explains that this applies only where wine is not stored permanently. If it is used for permanent storage, filling and pouring is required even if the non-Jew used it only briefly. Bach writes that an earthenware vessel already saturated through Jewish wine needs only rinsing when a non-Jew later uses it, even if ordinarily used for storage. If it was not yet saturated, filling and pouring is required even for brief non-Jewish use.
After the fact. Maharshal writes that common practice permits drinking from it even initially. Earthenware differs from wood because once saturated it no longer absorbs. Taz rules like Rama that the permission is only after the fact.
