And b'dieved their law is like wooden vessels. This is according to what Tur wrote, and the practice is not so regarding the law of earthenware that is pitched; and Beit Yosef explained the practice is not to forbid b'dieved, for l'chatchilah behold Tur already concluded in pitched wooden vessels that filling and pouring is required l'chatchilah; and Maharshal explained it is permitted even l'chatchilah in our earthenware since they are already sated, end quote; and this is according to the distinction Tur wrote in the name of Rosh in earthenware between sated and not sated; and if so, sated earthenware vessels would be preferable to wooden vessels; and in my humble opinion it is impossible to explain Tur's words so, for when Tur wrote the law of earthenware that is in pitch, he refers to the place that forbids, namely it is not sated; and on this he wrote it appears to compare it to a leather vessel that requires filling and pouring; and on this he wrote the practice is not so, it implies in that manner it deals before this; and how can we say the practice refers specifically to our sated earthenware vessels? Rather it appears primary according to Beit Yosef's explanation that b'dieved he speaks, specifically the practice is not to forbid it.
