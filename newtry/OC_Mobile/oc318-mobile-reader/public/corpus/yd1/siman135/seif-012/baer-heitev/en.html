Twenty-four hours. Beit Yosef cites a responsum of the Rosh. If one cannot measure exactly, he should leave the water in for more than twenty-four hours. Shach.
Forbid. Shach writes that only plain water is treated stringently initially. Other beverages, such as beer and honey, are permitted. Mordechai implies that even water is forbidden only when placed in the vessel for the purpose of kashering it, because drinking that water would constitute intentionally nullifying a prohibition. If water was placed there incidentally and remained a long time, it is permitted. This resolves Rama's ruling in siman 137:4 permitting water and other beverages: the distinction is whether the liquid was placed there to kasher the vessel.
Consecutive. Even initially, the three days may be nonconsecutive. Tur and Rashba.
Counted. Beit Yosef cites Rashba that the partial day counts, provided the missing hours are completed on the following day. Later authorities disagree and hold that the partial day does not count at all, and we rule stringently. Taz writes that after the fact, if one relied upon Rashba and then used the vessel permissibly, the contents are not forbidden. Nekudot HaKesef explains that immersion is not established by intermittent twenty-four-hour periods; nevertheless, after the fact the wine is permitted, as with wine vessels used without kashering, because there is ordinarily sixty against the surface layer.
One third. Nekudot HaKesef writes that this is a printing error. It should read two-thirds salt and one-third water, as in Shabbat 108b and Orach Chaim 321:2.
