Those waters do not count for him. These are the words of the Rosh, and the Tur concludes that he requires 3 additional times apart from these. This implies that they do not help even if he wishes to combine other water with them; the period during which this water remained does not count. The Beit Yosef cites a responsum of the Rashba, who wrote: Moreover, even if on the first day he filled it for 6 hours, and on the second day he filled it for 6 hours with fresh water, and so on in separate intervals until the full measure of 3 days was completed, it is permitted, end quote. The Beit Yosef reconciles the Rosh with the Rashba by explaining that the Rosh intended only to exclude counting it as an entire day when even 1 hour was lacking. This appears to me to be the primary explanation, for the Rosh brings proof from a mikveh lacking even a minute quantity. Had his intent been that the partial period cannot be combined at all, he would have no proof from a mikveh. This is the very proof that the Rashba also cited for the ruling that a period lacking even 1 hour is insufficient. The wording of the Tur, however, disagrees with the Rashba. The Beit Yosef wrote that we rule like the Rashba, but in the Shulchan Aruch he did not mention the Rashba's ruling, which implies that he rules stringently like the Tur. In my humble opinion, if after the fact one performed the kashering in accordance with the Rashba and subsequently used the vessel for permitted wine, we do not prohibit it. In truth, I do not know the reason for the Tur's stringency. If we say that the interruption during the filling and pouring process of 1 day causes the moisture that had begun to emerge to become reabsorbed, so that the vessel must remain moist for 24 consecutive hours, then for the same reason we should require all 3 days to be consecutive. The Gemara does not explicitly permit separated days, and from where do we derive leniency? Rather, this reasoning is plainly unsound. Whatever was expelled, even a little, combines with what will be expelled afterward. The same is true on the first day. There is no distinction between separating one day from another and separating the hours within a single day. Since we permit separated days by logical reasoning, why should we not also permit separated hours? Perhaps the Tur's reason is maris ayin, because an observer might think that this counts as a full day. Therefore, after the fact it certainly combines. If, however, during a single day one empties the water and immediately fills it with other water, this is plainly even better and is permitted even initially, provided that he intends the total period to be slightly more than 24 hours, to compensate for the brief interval between emptying the first water and adding the second. Perhaps even this is unnecessary, for the Beit Yosef already cited the the Rosh's responsum that even if one cannot be exact to the very moment, the Torah was not given to ministering angels. It is possible, however, that there he was compelled because exact precision is impossible, whereas here he deliberately creates a short delay between one water and the next. Therefore, he should leave it afterward slightly longer than 24 hours, and there is then no concern at all. So it appears to me.
