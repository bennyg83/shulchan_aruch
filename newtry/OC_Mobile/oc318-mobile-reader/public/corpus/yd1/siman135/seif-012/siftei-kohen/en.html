Water. If he wishes to perform the filling-and-emptying procedure with wine, it accomplishes nothing. The Tur writes this, and Beit Yosef cites Orchos Chaim in the name of Ramban. Darkei Moshe writes that regarding mere rinsing all may agree that one may be lenient, implying that for the full filling-and-emptying procedure the Rama concedes that wine is insufficient, unlike the Levush, who permits b'diavad kashering through rinsing or repeated filling with kosher wine. This is the strict law where wine mixed with wine is prohibited in any amount. For us, if there are not sixty parts of wine against the thickness absorbed in the vessel, the same rule applies. If there are sixty parts, it is still prohibited l'chatchilah to place wine in the vessel for this purpose; but if wine containing sixty was already placed in the vessel and afterward wine was placed there a second time, the later wine is permitted b'diavad, because the absorbed taste was already nullified into the first wine, as explained below in siman 137, seif 5, seif katan 18.
Twenty-four hours. Beit Yosef cites a responsum of the Rosh: if one cannot measure to the exact instant, the Torah was not given to ministering angels; but if on one of the three days he emptied the water two hours early, that day does not count at all. Two hours is not literal; the same applies to half an hour or less whenever it is definitely known that the water did not remain for twenty-four hours. I nevertheless find Beit Yosef's formulation difficult: why say that exact measurement is impossible and invoke the principle that the Torah was not given to angels, when one can simply leave the water in the vessel for somewhat more than twenty-four hours?
And some prohibit the water. It appears that this stringency applies specifically to plain water, which is not a food, and therefore one is stringent l'chatchilah, similar to what I wrote in siman 94, seif katan 19. Another beverage, such as beer or honey, is permitted, as stated in the Gemara and poskim. Even regarding water, Mordechai implies that it is prohibited only when placed in the vessel in order to kasher it. Since one intentionally performs the nullification, drinking the water would violate the rule that one may not nullify a prohibition l'chatchilah, and similarly one should not place such water into food l'chatchilah. But if water happened to remain in the vessel for a long time incidentally, it is permitted. Beer and similar beverages are in any event permitted because one does not kasher the vessel through them. This resolves the difficulty raised by Maharam of Tiktin in his gloss to Mordechai. It also explains why the Rama writes here that some prohibit the water, while below in siman 137, seif 4, he follows the Tur and Shulchan Aruch that water and other beverages may be placed in the vessel: there he discusses using them not for the purpose of kashering.

Those waters did not count for him. Beit Yosef cites Rashba that the day itself still counts, except that on the following day he must make up the number of hours missing from the first day. Beit Yosef writes that the Rosh's responsum also agrees. The Derishah, however, holds that the Rosh and Tur disagree, and the Bach likewise rules that the deficient day does not count at all, because perhaps specifically the twenty-fourth hour causes the forbidden taste to be expelled. The Bach concludes that we should follow this stringent view, and this is also implied by the Shulchan Aruch's wording, which appears to retract Beit Yosef's earlier explanation. This is unlike the Levush, who follows Rashba.
