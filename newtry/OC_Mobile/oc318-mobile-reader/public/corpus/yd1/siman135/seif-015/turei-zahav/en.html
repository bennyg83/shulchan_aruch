but when pitched, weak hag'alah like this does not help; and it implies from his words that good hag'alah helps without peeling the pitch; and Rabbi Yitzchak required heating each board separately — he puts the vessel into the pot when it is boiling and pours on each board, etc. — end quote; what he wrote regarding Rabbeinu Tam's view to pour boiling water — apparently it means even boiling water of a second vessel, since he used plain "boiling water"; and in Rabbi Yitzchak's words he said "he puts the vessel into the pot," etc. — and it is not so, for it is explicit in Rosh's responsum general rule 19 siman 11, whose language is: and the barrel Rabbeinu Tam permitted that he take a pot full of boiling water and pour it into the barrel, etc.; and even though this is not a first vessel, since the water does not reach in all of it except through rolling — nevertheless since the absorption was through cold he was lenient, etc. — behold even for Rabbeinu Tam one must pour from a first vessel specifically; and even though through rolling of the barrel it helps even from one board to many boards — nevertheless the essence of the heat must come into the barrel from a first vessel specifically; and that which Rabbi Yitzchak afterwards wrote "he puts the vessel into the pot" — it did not come to exclude Rabbeinu Tam's words on this, but to inform us of a leniency of Rabbi Yitzchak, since Rabbi Yitzchak is stringent to pour on each board separately and rolling from board to board does not help — if so pouring would not help even from that vessel itself, since boiling ceased after removing it from the fire; it informs us it is still called a first vessel; and so is explicit in Tosafot that Beit Yosef brought; and what Tur wrote in the name of Rosh that in pitched weak hag'alah like this does not help — do not err to say specifically in this manner that was through rolling; but if he pours on each board separately it helps even through pouring from a first vessel without peeling pitch — and this is the good hag'alah he mentioned afterwards, namely pouring over the whole barrel not through rolling, which helps even without peeling; this is not so, for it is explained in Rosh's responsum this siman and that — his language; and what Rabbeinu Tam was lenient to pour, etc., is specifically in a wooden barrel that is not pitched; but pitched absorbs more, as is in the Gemara "if they were pitched they are forbidden"; and after I wrote this it seems to me from your words that you asked how they can be made fit after peeling this pitch — it is a simple matter that it suffices to pour boiling water into it from a large vessel, and he need not fill water for three days; and what you said they cannot because the pitch does not adhere well after water — for this I knew no remedy — end quote; behold before you they required peeling pitch specifically; and even though he knew no remedy and the vessel would be ruined, he did not say to pour water from a large vessel in any place on the barrel in a manner that would not require rolling; rather it is clear that whatever is pitched has no remedy at all through any pouring — it must be put into a first vessel on the fire; and this is Avraham the son of David; and the good hag'alah Tur cites means plain hag'alah that everywhere one puts what is being kashered into the pot where possible, and there they need not remove the pitch; and so it implies from Beit Yosef's explanation who wrote good hag'alah — meaning from a first vessel helps without peeling pitch — and it is simple — end quote; and do not err in what Beit Yosef wrote "from a first vessel" — meaning through pouring from a first vessel; on this he would not write that it is simple, for almost most poskim hold pouring does not help at all, but one must put it into the pot; and as Shulchan Aruch wrote here "they are permitted through hag'alah in a first vessel"; rather Beit Yosef intended that the hag'alah comes from a first vessel that was put into it; and this language Rashba wrote in Tur Zahav HaKatzar that Beit Yosef brought siman 138 in the paragraph beginning "Rashba wrote" regarding winepress vessels, etc. — and this is his language: and there are great teachers who taught that winepress vessels are made fit only from a first vessel, etc.; and there it deals that one puts it into a first vessel — end. I wrote all this because I saw in Perishah that he wrote that according to Rabbeinu Tam one need not pour from a first vessel into the barrel; and I already proved this is not so; and he further wrote that in the good hag'alah Tur cites he means one pours on each board without rolling — and this is permitted even in pitched — Tur's language in the name of Rosh misled him, who wrote weak hag'alah like this — he holds such is specifically; but in truth in Rosh's responsum general rule 19 siman 8 he did not write this word "like this" but plainly "weak hag'alah does not help"; and we already proved that in pitched nothing helps except peeling pitch; and to make it fit afterwards, if it is impossible to put it in a first vessel, and if he does not do so even bedi'eved it is forbidden; and through this understand Rama's words who wrote to permit through pouring that one rolls, etc., in the details of laws we mentioned with Heaven's help.

Behold we say in the braita of flasks that rinsing with pouring suffices. Rather it is clear the Gemara there did not need to exclude flasks from the decree of pouring, but comes to say if he did not do pouring and waited twelve months it is also permitted; and likewise Rambam perek 11 of Laws of Forbidden Foods: gentiles' flasks are permitted when old twelve months and through pouring; and he did not need to mention pouring at all, since it is already a variant reading; unlike the incident of Bar Ada the Arab there, which is not mentioned anywhere that its law is through pouring — if so, that which the Gemara cited that it is permitted after twelve months is specifically, and rinsing with pouring does not suffice — therefore one should not explain like Perishah.
