more than a peel. It appears this means presumably we say so because it is not known that wine was kavush in it a full day; but if it was known that yayin nesech was in it a full day, this is kavush and kavush forbids the whole vessel; and as written below siman 137 s"k 9; and it further appears that in the whole siman and everywhere we say wine vessels are made fit through filling and pouring and the like — this is presumably; but if it is known that yayin nesech stood in it a full day, obviously complete hag'alah is required for each according to its law as explained in siman 121 as if used hot, for we hold kavush is like cooked; and even for Ramban who holds filling and pouring is preferable to hag'alah — filling and pouring helps in earthenware and not hag'alah; and if so it would appear that just as hag'alah helps in other vessels that were impaired through kavush, all the more so pouring — namely presumably; and for the reason that if it were absorbed in it, hag'alah would not help for earthenware that never leaves its mouth, or for the reason Beit Chadash wrote so that one not come to err to heat earthenware even in other prohibitions; but pouring the Sages established, since it is absorbed only through cold like cold into cold; but if it is known that me'ol le'ol stood in it, it does not help for anyone — for earthenware neither filling and pouring nor hag'alah; and likewise filling and pouring does not help in other vessels; however still it requires study, for it is possible to say that even if it is known that me'ol le'ol stood in it — nevertheless since it was forbidden only through cold we say regarding it like cold into cold, and filling and pouring help for it; and also one can say the Sages were established that through filling and pouring the taste of wine absorbed through cold is weakened — and it requires study; and nevertheless it appears that other prohibitions absorbed through cold through kavush — making-fit through filling and pouring does not help, and they did not say so except regarding wine, where the water that one fills and pours in the vessel weakens the taste of wine, unlike other prohibitions; and this is because making-fit through filling and pouring in other prohibitions was not mentioned anywhere in the Gemara and poskim, except that Taz in Orach Chaim siman 451 in the name of Ra'aviah and Beit Yosef there in the name of "there are poskim" challenged; and likewise in Shulchan Aruch there s"k 21 regarding an earthenware barrel into which barley beer was put — they are permitted through hag'alah or pouring for 3 days; and it is possible they were lenient only when they are not ben yomo, for there is no longer a Torah prohibition; and in such a case the author of Ittur wrote, and Tur brings it siman 121, that an earthenware vessel that is not ben yomo — since there is only a rabbinic prohibition, hag'alah helps for it even though it never leaves its mouth; and even to those who disagree with him it is possible he admits regarding a barrel whose use is cold, as is explicit from Ra'aviah's words there that he distinguishes so; but on ben yomo pouring does not help; and in wine vessels it is not fit to distinguish between ben yomo and not ben yomo, as below siman 137; also one can say regarding chametz there is room to be more lenient in hag'alah because the permitted absorbed; and in such a case we say above siman 93 s"k 3.
