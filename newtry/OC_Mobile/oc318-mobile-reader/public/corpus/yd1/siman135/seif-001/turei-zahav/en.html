Vessels that are not, etc. The vessels of the winepress were not included in this siman. They are more stringent, even though wine is not placed in them for long-term storage, because they are constantly occupied with wine. Their law is explained in siman 138. So wrote the Beit Yosef, and all the poskim mention that winepress vessels are more stringent than other vessels.
If they were pitched. The reason is that the pitch causes the wine to become absorbed. The prohibition is not because of the wine that they place in them at the time of pitching, for that wine is considered thrown away. Rather, they subsequently place wine in them, and this is not readily discernible. So wrote the Beit Yosef in the name of Tosafos. The Tur wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Yitzchak that this applies specifically in their times, when they commonly put wine into the pitch, so that it was not discernible whether other wine was placed in them after the pitching. Nowadays, however, when it is not customary to put wine into them at the time of pitching, as long as they are new their appearance remains yellow until wine is put into them. Therefore, one may purchase them from non-Jews as long as they are yellow, end quote. The primary prohibition in their times was when wine was put into them at the time of pitching, because afterward it would not be discernible whether wine had been placed in the vessel. A pitched vessel is comparable to a vessel coated with clay.
Filling and emptying them, etc. It seems to me he cited this kashering because it helps in all of them, even in wooden vessels; but in truth in stone vessels wiping mentioned in siman 138 seif 1 regarding a winepress helps, and all the more so in other vessels if wiping applies; and Tur and Shulchan Aruch who did not mention here except filling and pouring followed the Gemara the second chapter (daf 33), Bar Ada the Arab forced hides from Israel and put wine in them, and they ruled in the study house that one empties them 3 days, and this is in leather or earthenware that absorbs even more than wooden vessels; but in vessels that are not put in for permanence the Gemara and poskim do not deal, and they included them with wooden vessels regarding that rinsing does not suffice, and they cited in them one kashering that helps for all; and they did not descend here to distinguish between wiping and filling and pouring, for in this they relied on siman 138; also in Perishah siman 138 he distinguishes between stone and wood in this, except he was forced to say that here in the kashering of filling and pouring it does not refer to stone, and this is unnecessary; and further the author of Perishah sought distinctions to be stringent in other vessels more than winepress vessels, these are astonishing matters, for it is simple in all the poskim that winepress vessels are more stringent since they are preoccupied with wine, as Tosafos wrote in perek Ein Ma'amidin.
The same applies to our vessels. Meaning, there is a distinction between pitched vessels and vessels that are not pitched.
While the wine is still moist. Meaning, moist enough to moisten something else. So wrote the Maharshal. Otherwise it does not have the capacity to prohibit, as written in siman 123, seif 17, regarding grape pits.
If they are of wood or stone, etc. This comes to exclude only leather vessels, which are soft, and the pitch causes the wine to become absorbed in them. So wrote the Tur in the name of Rabbeinu Tam.
