Vessels. Taz and Shach write that the vessels of a winepress are not included in this siman. They are more stringent even though wine is not stored in them permanently, because they are used with abundant wine, as explained in siman 138.
Their usual manner. Tur writes that if it is not customary to place wine in them during pitching and their appearance remains yellow, this shows they were not used with wine after pitching, because wine use would be visibly recognizable.
Ours. Taz explains that pitched and unpitched vessels differ. 'Moist enough' means enough to moisten another object. Maharshal. Less than this cannot forbid, as stated in siman 123:17 regarding grape seeds.
To be lenient. Shach writes that it is permitted even for drinking. This follows Talmudic law, or the case is where there is not sixty. If there is sixty, it is plainly permitted nowadays because ordinary non-Jewish wine is nullified in sixty, and ordinarily the contents have sixty against the vessel's surface layer, which alone became forbidden. This applies to wooden vessels and the like, but not soft leather vessels, where the pitch causes the wine to penetrate more deeply; those require filling and pouring according to all.
