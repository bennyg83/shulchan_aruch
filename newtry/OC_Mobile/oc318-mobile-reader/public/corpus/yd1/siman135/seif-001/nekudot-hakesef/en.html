(Siman 135, Taz, seif katan 9) And his words are astonishing, etc. I say that, on the contrary, his own words are astonishing. What he cites from Shulchan Aruch, siman 138, is difficult, because Shulchan Aruch follows its own view, as stated in Beit Yosef there. But the Perishah cites the words of Beit Yosef there and disagrees with them. The wording of the Tur there compelled him, for why did it not also state this regarding filling and emptying, as it did regarding rinsing? See there. What he further wrote, that Terumat HaDeshen states, etc., presents no difficulty, because Terumat HaDeshen there explicitly discusses a stone winepress, which requires only rinsing. See there. The Perishah also agrees in that case, for his entire intent is only to say that they did not decree so extensively where only rinsing is required. But where filling and emptying are required, they did not impose that requirement merely because a non-Jew touched the vessel. What he further wrote, that even regarding vessels not used for long-term storage, etc., also presents no difficulty, because there the reason is that the pitch causes absorption. Nevertheless, I have a difficulty with the words of the Perishah in siman 131. Even regarding wooden vessels not used for long-term storage, if they are pitched, the Tur cites authorities who require filling and emptying for our vessels that were touched by a non-Jew. He writes that the Rosh concludes that their use is initially forbidden, though after the fact, if one used them, the contents are permitted. If so, how can it be that the pitched winepress vessels discussed below in siman 138, belonging to a Jew and touched by a non-Jew, do not require filling and emptying? The permanent resident stands upon the earth while the sojourner reaches the heavens. Nevertheless, the words of the Perishah here, regarding vessels used for long-term storage that are not pitched, may be correct. It is possible that the Mechaber also agrees, because here, since the vessel is not pitched, they did not decree so stringently concerning mere contact as to require filling and emptying. Examine this carefully.
