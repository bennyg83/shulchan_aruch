(There, seif katan 15) In truth, I do not know the reason for this stringency, etc. There is no difficulty. The reason is that whenever it remained in the vessel for a full twenty-four hours, the liquid causes the vessel to discharge what it absorbed, and it resembles pickling. Rosh writes likewise in chapter Ein Maamidin: pickling is like pouring, because over twenty-four hours the water draws out whatever it can, but not in less than twenty-four hours. Plainly, intermittent periods do not combine into one twenty-four-hour pickling period. For this reason we do not require three consecutive twenty-four-hour periods, but three separate soaking processes one after another. Nevertheless, even apart from this, it may be that if the vessel was afterward used for permitted food, we do not prohibit the food after the fact. This is not limited to this case, but applies to all wine vessels used after the fact without kashering, because ordinarily there is sixty times the absorbed surface layer, and for us the food is permitted, as explained below in siman 137.
