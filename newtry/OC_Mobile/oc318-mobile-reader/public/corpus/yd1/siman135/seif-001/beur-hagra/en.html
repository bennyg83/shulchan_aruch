(Likut) Between, etc. ; excluding earthenware; and as written in Shulchan Aruch "earthenware," etc.; and according to the Geonim's explanation that all earthenware vessels have equal law, unlike the Rosh there who wrote specifically a cup that is from the known earth type ; but all earthenware is so; and even though they said there "these jugs," etc. "these lids," etc. ; Rashba and Ran wrote specifically those whose pottery is hard and they do not absorb much, and it is not like other earthenware; and Shulchan Aruch omitted the law of jugs and lids because their pottery is not known to us; and likewise Tur Zahav HaKatzar omitted it. Beit Yosef ; end.
Thoroughly. See above, siman 121, seif 1, and below, seif 11.
Between, etc. From the aforementioned; and further the girsa of Ri'f and Rosh there ; Rabbi Yehudah: "these Aramean jugs," etc.; and there 74b ; he holds all earthenware, etc. ; it implies otherwise even for a long time we do not decree; and Tosafot there 32b s.v. "kach," etc., and in Rosh and Ran there, and unlike Rashi.
Were, etc. According to Daf Talmud's girsa and explanation there ; that of hides that one does not put in for permanence [Likut: were pitched, etc. ; girsa of Rabbeinu Tam and Tosefta: hides of gentiles ; scraped are permitted, new pitched are forbidden; flasks of gentiles ; new are permitted, old pitched are forbidden; and "scraped" means even if there was pitch in them and even if old, only that the pitch was peeled off they are permitted; but pitched that has not yet been peeled off ; even if new they are forbidden; new flasks in which there was no pitch at all, recognizable that wine was never in them ; permitted; old and pitched ; meaning old or pitched, even if they look new, since they are pitched it is not recognizable; and the reason of flasks: since one puts them in for permanence even temporarily, etc., and pouring is required; and Tosafot and Rosh ; end].
The same applies to vessels, etc. This is stated there in Tosafot. According to Rabbeinu Tam's approach, etc.; and even according to Rabbeinu Yitzchak there, who permits a non-Jew's vessel, he forbids this case, as stated there: ‘Rabbeinu Yitzchak would,’ etc., ‘nevertheless, ours,’ etc.
Some say, etc. This is stated there, ‘and Rabbeinu Tam himself,’ etc. At first glance, this should apply all the more to new vessels belonging to a non-Jew, as above. However, according to Tosafot there, who wrote, ‘Rabbeinu Baruch,’ etc., and as stated here, one should not forbid it after the fact. This applies specifically to a Jewish vessel, and we do not rely on Rabbeinu Yitzchak's leniency.
Moist. Moist enough to moisten another surface, as stated there, 60b, and as above, siman 129, seif 13. See also siman 126, seif 5, in the gloss.
If they are, etc. This excludes wineskins, which are soft. This is stated there in Tosafot.
