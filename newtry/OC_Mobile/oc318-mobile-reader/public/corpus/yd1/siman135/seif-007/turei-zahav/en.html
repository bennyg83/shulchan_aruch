And a non-Jew used them even temporarily. See in Perishah, specifically when he used them; but if he touched at the time of smearing it is not forbidden, for they decreed lest the non-Jew use it and put wine in for permanence; and in siman 138 seif 1 it is explained regarding a winepress that there is no distinction whether a non-Jew pitched it or a Jew, and a a non-Jew's touched it; and Perishah wrote there to distinguish that here regarding other vessels where he ruled filling and pouring is required, they did not decree on touch; but there in siman 138 which deals with requiring wiping, they also decreed on touch, end quote; and his words are astonishing from all sides: one, from where do we have to distinguish in this? Behold there Shulchan Aruch wrote "and of wood, if a non-Jew pitched it, etc., or a Jew pitched and a a non-Jew's touched, etc., filling and pouring is required without a peel," etc., behold in the law of filling and pouring Shulchan Aruch also wrote they decreed on touch; and so is at the end of siman 138 in Tur in the name of Shulchan Aruch, that in ours a a non-Jew's touched, he was in doubt whether hag'alah is required, etc., learn from this not like his words that he wrote specifically regarding wiping they decreed. And further, behold he wrote in Tur Zahav that Beit Yosef brings it siman 138 regarding a winepress, whose language is: and that which teaches "a non-Jew pitched it", the same if a Jew pitched and a a non-Jew's touched when there is enough to smear, etc.; and the reason for this: a winepress, since if its first use had been in a non-Jew's hand wiping would be required, they also decreed on it temporarily, and as we also said regarding putting in for permanence that even temporarily the rabbis decreed, end quote; behold before you he learned the law of a winepress from the law of a vessel put in for permanence regarding this a non-Jew's touch that forbids; and what he wrote that here it does not deal with wiping but with filling and pouring is astonishing, for certainly here too rinsing suffices in stone vessels, as I wrote in seif 1, and it is simple. And further, behold even when not putting in for permanence and they are pitched we say in this siman that a non-Jew's touch forbids, all the more so regarding putting in for permanence even if not pitched, for behold Beit Yosef wrote we learn the law of putting in for permanence from the law of pitched when not putting in for permanence. And according to this I forbade one who sold his wine and the barrel remained empty with only sediment remaining in it and he sells them to a non-Jew, lest the non-Jew take the sediment from there, since the barrel has tofe'ach with intent to smear, and also among the sediment itself a bit of clear wine remained; it turns out the non-Jew forbids the barrel, for wine is put in it for permanence, and the barrel will need hag'alah or filling and pouring; therefore the Jew must remove the sediment and the non-Jew must not touch it; thus it appears here too even though he used the language "a non-Jew used it," the same applies to touch, they have one law in all cases; for regarding a winepress below siman 138 we forbid a non-Jew's touch even though in the Gemara they only said "a non-Jew pitched it", rather it is one matter, and by simple reasoning we say so, as Beit Yosef wrote there; and here too it is so, and we have no [basis] to invent distinctions and laws from our hearts, especially to be lenient in my humble opinion; and the Rav author of Perishah did not examine here as needed; also Maharach disagrees with him on this.
