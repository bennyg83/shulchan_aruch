And a gentile used them even temporarily. See in Perishah — specifically when he used them; but if he touched at the time of smearing it is not forbidden, for they decreed lest the gentile use it and put wine in for permanence; and in siman 138 seif 1 it is explained regarding a winepress that there is no distinction whether a gentile pitched it or Israel, and a gentile touched it; and Perishah wrote there to distinguish that here regarding other vessels where he ruled pouring is required, they did not decree on touch; but there in siman 138 which deals with requiring drying, they also decreed on touch — end quote; and his words are astonishing from all sides: one — from where do we have to distinguish in this? Behold there Shulchan Aruch wrote "and of wood — if a gentile pitched it, etc., or Israel pitched and a gentile touched, etc. — pouring is required without a peel," etc. — behold in the law of pouring Shulchan Aruch also wrote they decreed on touch; and so is at the end of siman 138 in Tur in the name of Shulchan Aruch, that in ours a gentile touched — he was in doubt whether hag'alah is required, etc. — learn from this not like his words that he wrote specifically regarding drying they decreed. And further — behold he wrote in Tur Zahav that Beit Yosef brings it siman 138 regarding a winepress, whose language is: and that which teaches "a gentile pitched it" — the same if Israel pitched and a gentile touched when there is enough to smear, etc.; and the reason for this: a winepress — since if its first use had been in a gentile's hand drying would be required, they also decreed on it temporarily, and as we also said regarding putting in for permanence that even temporarily the rabbis decreed — end quote; behold before you he learned the law of a winepress from the law of a vessel put in for permanence regarding this gentile touch that forbids; and what he wrote that here it does not deal with drying but with pouring is astonishing, for certainly here too rinsing suffices in stone vessels, as I wrote in seif 1, and it is simple. And further — behold even when not putting in for permanence and they are pitched we say in this siman that gentile touch forbids — kal v'chomer regarding putting in for permanence even if not pitched, for behold Beit Yosef wrote we learn the law of putting in for permanence from the law of pitched when not putting in for permanence. And according to this I forbade one who sold his wine and the barrel remained empty with only sediment remaining in it and he sells them to a gentile — lest the gentile take the sediment from there, since the barrel has tofe'ach with intent to smear, and also among the sediment itself a bit of clear wine remained; it turns out the gentile forbids the barrel, for wine is put in it for permanence, and the barrel will need hag'alah or pouring; therefore Israel must remove the sediment and the gentile must not touch it; thus it appears here too even though he used the language "a gentile used it," the same applies to touch — they have one law in all cases; for regarding a winepress below siman 138 we forbid gentile touch even though in the Gemara they only said "a gentile pitched it" — rather it is one matter, and by simple reasoning we say so, as Beit Yosef wrote there; and here too it is so, and we have no [basis] to invent distinctions and laws from our hearts, especially to be lenient in my humble opinion; and the Rav author of Perishah did not examine here as needed; also Maharach disagrees with him on this.
