(Siman 135, Taz s"k 9) And his words are astonishing, etc. And I say that on the contrary his words are astonishing — for what he brought from Shulchan Aruch siman 138 is astonishing, for Shulchan Aruch follows his reasoning that he so wrote in Beit Yosef there; but Perishah behold brings there Beit Yosef's words and disagrees with him; and they forced the language of Tur there — why did it not also teach regarding pouring so, as it taught regarding drying — see there; and what he wrote "and further that he wrote in Tur Zahav," etc. — to my mind in Tur Zahav there it deals explicitly with a stone winepress that needs only drying — see there, that Perishah also agrees that his entire intent is to say they did not decree so much only where drying alone is needed; but where filling and pouring are needed they did not decree regarding gentile touch alone; and what he wrote "and further that even when not putting in for permanence," etc. — to my mind there the reason is because pitch absorbs in them. However it is simple regarding Perishah's words in siman 131, for even in a wooden vessel that is not put in for permanence and they are pitched, Tur brings the poskim's view that pouring is required in ours that a gentile touched them; and he wrote that the Rosh's conclusion is forbidden lechatchilah and bedi'eved if one used them it is kosher — if so, how is it possible that winepress vessels below siman 138, pitched in ours that a gentile touched them, do not require pouring — a bastard stands on the ground and a convert in Heaven's name; and nevertheless Perishah's words here regarding vessels put in for permanence and not pitched can be true, and it is possible the Mechaber also admits to this, and the reason here is since it is not pitched they did not decree so much regarding touch to require pouring — and study it.
