Wineskins are not considered “used for long-term storage,” for they are made only to transport wine from the winepress to the barrel. But the flask of travelers — since the wine is always in it, for immediately when they pour out, they return and fill it — its law is like one used for long-term storage. But cups, even though one drinks from them regularly, nevertheless the wine does not remain in them always; rather, he fills them for a time, drinks, and empties, and therefore they do not have the law of “used for long-term storage.” (A vessel into which they place wine to stand in it for 3 days is called “used for long-term storage,” and therefore the iruy as well is 3 days) (Mordechai, perek Ein Ma’amidin; and Kol Bo; and Arukh, rule 28, law 69).
