Leather flasks, etc. The Tur writes: Some say that they are considered vessels into which wine is placed for long-term storage, etc. The explanation of his words appears to be that people sometimes keep wine in a leather flask for several days. Therefore, some maintain that it is considered a vessel used for long-term storage. The Tur disagrees and holds that a vessel is considered designated for long-term storage only when it was made for that purpose from the outset. By contrast, when it was not made for that purpose and wine is kept in it only incidentally, the Sages did not decree regarding temporary use by a non-Jew. On this basis, the Tur cites the responsum of the Rosh concerning leather flasks used to transport wine from the winepress to the barrel. The Rosh permitted them through rinsing and was not concerned that perhaps wine had occasionally remained in them for several days. He proves this from the daily practice regarding earthenware vessels, for which rinsing suffices. Meaning, it happens every day that a non-Jew drinks wine in a Jew's house from the Jew's earthenware vessels, and they rinse them afterward. We do not say that because wine is sometimes kept in them for several days, they are considered vessels used for long-term storage and rinsing would not suffice. Rather, as I explained, the Sages did not decree in such a case. This resolves the Beit Yosef's difficulty with the Tur. I am astonished, however, that the Rosh permitted through rinsing the leather flasks used to carry wine from the winepress to the barrel, for they are winepress utensils like the strainer and funnel in siman 138, and they require wiping, as written there. It therefore appears that the Rosh intended only that they do not require filling and filling and pouring like vessels used for long-term storage. For this he brings proof from ordinary earthenware vessels used throughout the year, which require only rinsing. The same applies here, but wiping is nevertheless required in addition to rinsing.
A vessel into which wine is placed to remain for 3 days, etc. Meaning, it was originally designated for that purpose, as I explained immediately above, but not where wine merely happened to remain there for 3 days.
