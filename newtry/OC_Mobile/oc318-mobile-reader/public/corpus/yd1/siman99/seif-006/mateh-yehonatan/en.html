(There in hagahah) Forbidden item that was nullified, etc., stirs and is forbidden, etc. Ran challenged: if so if an olive-sized piece of chelev or nevelah fell into the dish, why does sixty help to nullify it — say it absorbs and returns and emits until there is no sixty; from this difficulty he derived we do not say it stirs without knowledge; if so Rambam in Ma'achalot Assurot even without knowledge we say it stirs — Ran's difficulty in Ma'achalot; one must say as Peleti wrote it is only one absorption and one emission. See Shach s.k. 21 who wrote in dry in dry we do not say it stirs and they challenge him from the mishnah: a se'ah of terumah that fell into a hundred of hullin and he did not manage to remove it before another piece fell — forbidden; R' Shimon permits — how much may he increase until permitted up to the measure of its majority; it appears specifically up to like its majority, but equal equal we say it stirs; accordingly even for Ran there is room to challenge from the mishnah he wrote we do not say it stirs — for if an olive-sized forbidden fell into permitted, say it absorbs and emits and returns and absorbs and emits so there would not be majority permitted; one may say that which he explained up to how much he may increase until the measure of its majority — that is only d'rabbanan, but d'oraisa even less than majority we do not say it stirs; if so here with an olive of nevelah that fell into permitted, since it cannot be otherwise, the Sages placed on d'oraisa that we do not say it stirs even less than majority permitted; but Shach who rules thus for us is difficult; one may say forced: behold the poskim disagree on this that we establish dry in dry one in two nullified — some poskim hold since there is one more permitted than forbidden, even if a hundred of forbidden fell into a hundred and one of permitted, still they say nullified since there is one more than forbidden; some hold on the view that there are two times permitted — e.g. two of forbidden one must say four of permitted; if so one may say Shach holds certainly even in dry in dry we say it stirs if forbidden and permitted are equal without majority permitted, even one; and that he wrote we do not say it stirs — he holds two times permitted is needed; and on this he wrote if it mixed so there is no majority against forbidden — majority that is two times permitted — nevertheless we do not say it stirs; but nevertheless there must be majority against forbidden, only one need not have two times against the forbidden; if so that which the mishnah teaches up to how much he may increase — that there must be at least majority permitted and not equal; investigate.
(There seif 6) Rabbinic prohibition, etc. See Shach what he challenges from permitted Chanukah oil; it appears to me to resolve: behold Tosafos on egg there challenges from why not add and nullify, and answers this case is different — there the essence is d'oraisa and Shulchan Aruch did not disagree thus, and Tosafos's difficulty from terumah; one may say here briefly: Hagahat Asheri wrote if a rabbinic prohibition mixed one in one it is permitted for two people; Rosh in Beitzah chapter 1 disagrees; if so one may say Tosafos's difficulty another way: even one in one forbidden also in rabbinic prohibition we do not say safek l'kula since the prohibition is established — nevertheless it is called sixty; accordingly Shach's difficulty from Chanukah oil is resolved — granted regarding wood since already mixed one in one there is no doubt and in sixty of rabbinic prohibition we say increase and nullify; but certainly rabbinic prohibition cannot increase and nullify; accordingly there in terumah they needed 101 to nullify; if so with less it is certainly rabbinic prohibition that does not help to nullify in the majority until there are 101; if so without less than 101 this is not rabbinic sixty but certainly, and certainly does not help [however from what Rosh is forced to rule like Maharam of Rothenburg until here — because he does not wish to disagree between essence d'oraisa and no essence d'oraisa, therefore Tosafos's difficulty from terumah; or that one may not increase where there is forbidden and not mukal kli; if so until here one must say one in one is not called rabbinic sixty, otherwise not forced to rule like Maharam — Tosafos's difficulty without resolution; or that he does not hold like Or Zarua but that it is not rabbinic sixty] — all this in dry in dry where there is no rabbinic enactment in its nullification; but in wet in wet that needs sixty even rabbinically in a mixture — if so certainly wherever there is no sixty here there is no sixty at all, like terumah I mentioned, and forbidden to increase; accordingly Shach's difficulty from Chanukah oil is resolved — there it is wet in wet like certainly rabbinic prohibition and forbidden to increase; investigate. It is stated in Beitzah chapter 1 daf 2 side 2: an egg born on Shabbos and one born on Yom Tov may not be moved, etc.; safek forbidden — granted according to Rav because preparation — safek d'oraisa to be stringent; but according to Rav Yosef and R' Yitzchak it is safek d'rabbanan to be lenient, etc.; Tosafos wrote but according to R' Nissim who says because muktzeh does not apply and muktzeh is severe like d'oraisa; one may say Tosafos on branches from the palm that muktzeh without essence d'oraisa is lighter than other rabbinic prohibitions with essence from Torah — contradicts what he wrote here that muktzeh is severe; one may say Tosafos wrote muktzeh is severe for R' Yehuda who holds muktzeh is certainly severe; but for us who establish like R' Shimon who has no muktzeh except on Yom Tov we are stringent like R' Yehuda — certainly muktzeh is not so severe and is lighter than other rabbinic prohibitions with essence d'oraisa; all this muktzeh due to prohibition or muktzeh by intent; but muktzeh for a mitzvah that even R' Shimon has this muktzeh is severe and is like d'oraisa; accordingly Shulchan Aruch ruled well regarding permitted oil of Chanukah lamp that is muktzeh for its mitzvah like d'oraisa — forbidden to add to it; no difficulty.
