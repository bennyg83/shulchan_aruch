(There Taz s.k. 9) If he thinks it is permitted to increase upon prohibition he is called b'shogeg. One who errs in law is called b'shogeg and not called ones, even regarding oath and liable for a sacrifice, as Rashba wrote in his novellae chapter 7 of Shabbat. And it appears not specifically an ignoramus who did not know there is prohibition in this; for even a Torah scholar who erred in a matter worthy of doubt is called b'shogeg; only regarding examination he is called b'mezid in Even HaEzer siman 13, as written in Birkei Yosef in Choshen Mishpat siman 34. And so in Yerushalmi Gittin it is stated there: as one who thinks it is permitted to render terumah impure; and likewise a kohen who thinks it is permitted to make piggul is b'shogeg; and in Panim Me'irot vol. 2 siman 163 he brought proof from Bava Kama (daf 95) and one who acts on a sage's ruling who erred is called b'shogeg even regarding examination, as Maggid Mishnah chapter 1 of Laws of Divorce wrote plainly: there is no greater b'shogeg than this; and unlike what Choshen Mishpat wrote in Even HaEzer that he is b'mezid; and likewise in Tosafot Eruvin (29) it is proven he is b'shogeg and we do not penalize b'shogeg as b'mezid for one who does not penalize; and so Magen Avraham in Orach Chaim siman 318, and so Tosafot in Sanhedrin (33), and behold proof, for one who acts on the ruling of a court brings a sacrifice, as Taz wrote in Even HaEzer end of seif 17; and so Beit Meir there in siman 13. And what the hagahah wrote there that he is ones — wrote in Birkei Yosef that this is only regarding ruling of the Great Court. And I saw in responsa R' Akiva Eiger siman 206 who rejected the words of a sage who permitted a woman bound to yibum who married because she erred in law and thought she was not bound; and that sage brought proof from Mahari"k general rule 68 regarding a designated maidservant who committed adultery through error in law — he forbade because nevertheless she transgresses with a man — it implies that without this she would be permitted; and Ra'avad answered him that we establish like Rava in Makkot who says permitted is close to b'mezid, and he did not mention that the sage's words are Taz's words here; and there are further proofs for this from Tosafot Bekhorot and Tosafot Eruvin.
