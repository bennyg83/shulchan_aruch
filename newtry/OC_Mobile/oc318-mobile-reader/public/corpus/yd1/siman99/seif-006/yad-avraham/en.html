(Seif 6) A rabbinic prohibition one does not mix by hand. [See Ran chapter 3 of Pesachim regarding how we separate] But if it fell by itself and there is not enough permitted food to nullify it, one adds — so Tur in the name of Rashba; except he wrote rabbinic prohibition that has no Torah root; and Beit Yosef wondered on Tur: since even though Rashba initially wrote thus, behold he concluded one must be concerned for those who are stringent; and I wondered on Beit Yosef who wondered on him in vain, for one who examines well Rashba's words that Beit Yosef brings — it appears to the eyes that this did not enter Rashba's mind, not to add to a prohibition whose root is rabbinic — only he brought one view that even l'chatchila permitted to mix by hand; and on this he wrote one must be concerned for those who forbid mixing l'chatchila, but to add in this he did not say to be stringent, as Tur wrote; and again after I wrote the Rav Peri Chadash also challenged Beit Yosef on this, and so in Ma'aseh Rokeach, and so Rambam chapter 15 of Laws of Forbidden Foods wrote — and these are his words: milk that fell into a pot that has fowl meat and gives taste in it — one increases upon it other fowl meat and nullifies it, and he considers it has no Torah root; as Mordechai wrote and so R' Simchah whom Hagahot Maimoniot brings there and Beit Yosef brings. [And this appears Peri Chadash omitted in Orach Chaim siman 496 — see there; and removed what he challenged there on Ran end of chapter 2 of Pesachim from beginning of R' Elazar on milah and chapter Keitzad]. And it appears to me from Rambam's words that when one increases upon a rabbinic prohibition, one must increase from something that also has no Torah deficiency together with the mixed prohibition — and this is what he said "one increases upon it other fowl meat" in order to nullify the taste of the milk; it implies that with animal meat forbidden from the Torah it is forbidden to increase upon it to nullify the taste of milk — for it would be nullifying something of Torah; and even though it does not come to prohibition, nevertheless l'chatchila forbidden if possible. And it appears to me: if nullified in animal meat that he increased — permitted and not called nullifying Torah prohibition at all, since still no prohibition came at all — similar to what Rambam wrote chapter 3 of Laws of Forbidden Foods and Shulchan Aruch above end of seif 7: if he curdled the milk in a valid stomach with noten taam — as long as the meat does not give taste in the milk it is still permitted. And it appears this is specifically here where possible to nullify in fowl meat — forbidden l'chatchila to add animal meat; but where impossible otherwise and if not nullified in animal meat the dish would be forbidden — permitted to add animal meat, for not called nullifying Torah prohibition l'chatchila since still no Torah prohibition here; and through this is well resolved the commentators' wonder on Rambam chapter 9 of Laws of Forbidden Foods regarding a drop of milk that fell on one piece in the pot — he stirs the pot so the taste of the drop mixes in the entire pot, as written siman 92 seif 2; and they wondered: behold one does not nullify prohibition l'chatchila; and to resolve: since he hurries to mix before the drop gave taste and it is still permitted there is no prohibition and not called nullifying prohibition l'chatchila. And such a case is stated below siman 109 regarding dry mixtures dry in dry — permitted to add upon it in order to cook because still permitted that did not give taste and he intends not to come to prohibition at all; and this unlike Tzemach Tzedek siman 80 who forbids nullifying a little milk in wine and compares it to nullifying prohibition l'chatchila that one drinks wine with meat, even though now permitted; and see Magen Avraham and Taz in Orach Chaim siman 467; and in Peri Chadash there siman 452 and 453; and see above what I wrote to resolve Rambam's words otherwise; and see in Sha'arei Mitzvah Laws of Forbidden Foods who challenged Rashba's words against themselves in chapter kli sheni that it appears he forbids even when he does not intend to nullify, and in chapter Ein Ma'amidin permits in such a case; and according to Peri Chadash in siman 453 to resolve, as I wrote chapter 4. And see in responsa Radbaz vol. 3 siman 547 who forbids buying from a gentile something nullified in sixty, for at the time of buying from the gentile it is as nullifying l'chatchila — he forbids in a matter that has Torah root; and according to Mishnah Berurah beginning general rule 35 Binyamin Zeev brought siman 108 that acquisition is not considered l'chatchila; and see Avodah Zarah (23) calls it b'dieved, and so Ran in Chullin (daf 6) and in Yad HaMelek he also challenged Radbaz; and nevertheless his heart was not full to disagree in practice and the above Acharonim are not mentioned; and also from Gemara Avodah Zarah. However Radbaz also concludes one does not forbid because of l'chatchila except here regarding nullification — a decree lest he say to nullify.
