(Seif 2) This is stated when it fell while it was raw; but if it was cooked first, the bones in it combine with the prohibition because they absorbed from the nevelah meat. It appears that for permitted food too it combines to nullify the prohibition, for Rashba wrote and Beit Yosef brings it in this siman and in siman 106, and so in Shulchan Aruch shortly seif 4 — according to R' Efraim we do not say chein nafsho in other prohibitions: a piece that absorbed prohibition combines to nullify the prohibition; and likewise for us, and even for us here regarding bones we do not say chein nafsho, as Taz and Shach wrote; and if so, even though they absorbed prohibition, nevertheless they combine to nullify, as Mechaber wrote in this siman; and likewise according to the hagahah in a place of loss — investigate. After I wrote I found thus in Lechem HaPanim in the name of Peri Chadash — praise to God.
