(Siman 99 in Shulchan Aruch seif 2) In what matters is this stated that forbidden bones combine with permitted food, etc.; but if it was cooked first and afterward fell, etc. [The taste in this is explained in Rashba Terumot chapter 5 and Rosh chullin 98 and Tur because we are concerned lest the forbidden item diminished and was absorbed in the bones; accordingly if we saw the forbidden item when it was raw, certainly sixty against the meat seen while the piece was raw suffices, and so Bach explicitly; and it appears that even for Rama who holds we say chein nafsho in other prohibitions and even in something without taste of its own we say chein nafsho — nevertheless here he agrees, as Shach wrote s.k. 3, that a bone is like a metal vessel; if so, likewise if it was cooked first with the bones too the law is thus that bones combine with permitted food if they knew how much meat there was at first — end of words of Rav R' Shlomo Cohen from Vilna first chapter.]
