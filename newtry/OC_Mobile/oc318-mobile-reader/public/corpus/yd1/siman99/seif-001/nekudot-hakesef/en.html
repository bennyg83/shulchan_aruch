(There) This is very puzzling, for we also require sixty against the pot itself. It requires study, for Beit Yosef wrote above siman 93 that it is praiseworthy that the pot's width be large enough to hold water so there is sixty against the entire pot — e.g. thin wide copper — if so, here we deal with a pot not full but only partially cooked. Also regarding what Beit Yosef wrote there that the known measure of meat cooked in it — he deals with Ba'al HaItur, etc. — also requires study; one may say here too it was known before the dish was put in the pot what its measure was, and therefore certainly one need not now estimate more than was put in the pot; requires study.
(There s.k. 8) And therefore Tur learned from Semag's view regarding stirring the pot, etc. — all this is strained; also in siman 92 itself we do not say the pot stirs, etc., for stirring afterward does not help — its explanation there is a different matter as Shach wrote there s.k. 8; but in my book I wrote another resolution that is correct and true: Semag's language implies not because of chein nafsho do the water added afterward not combine, but because for all prohibitions that one increased upon them it does not help — we penalize b'shogeg as b'mezid; and that we need throughout the Talmud the reason of chein nafsho is where even b'shogeg there is none — e.g. it fell by itself as R' Yehuda explained (if so, we deal with dry in dry; but in wet in wet Semag holds we do not say they increased b'shogeg are permitted, as Beit Yosef brought this distinction in the name of Hagahat ShaDa). Now for Semag, even where chein nafsho does not apply one must inquire — e.g. where it became forbidden through a slight amount and the like, as above siman 92 in Tur and Ran; therefore Tur challenged him, and it appears one need not inquire — for we establish that all prohibitions that one increased upon b'shogeg are permitted; investigate.
