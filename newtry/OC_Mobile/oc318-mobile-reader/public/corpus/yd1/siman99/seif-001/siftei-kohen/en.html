The bones of the prohibition, etc. For now l'fanei denu in Bavli and Yerushalmi like Ra'ah in Beit HaBechirah (96b) and Ran chullin 98: soft bones combine with the prohibition because of marrow in them and because of their softness moisture emerges; but dry bones or eggshells and the like — even those of prohibition combine to nullify the prohibition; and so is the view of Issur VeHeter klal 28 in the name of Or Zarua, and so Bach.
There are those who are stringent not to combine the bones of the prohibition with the permitted food to nullify it. And they do not combine them even for the prohibition; but the bones of permitted food combine with the permitted food, and so it is in Hagahot Sha'arei Dura siman 55 in the name of Or Zarua.
For this is the essence. Bach wrote: it is astonishing — one should rule that bones of prohibition combine with prohibition, since they adhere to meat they first received prohibition taste and became nevelah; and he holds (above siman 98 seif 5) that even in something without taste of its own we say chein nafsho. There is no astonishment here, for presumably the Rav did not write above siman 98 seif 5 to be stringent in a new earthenware vessel that became nevelah even though it has no taste of its own — only because it has no remedy through hag'alah and the prohibition cannot be separated, as written there; if so a bone vessel that has remedy through hag'alah, as explained in Orach Chaim siman 451, certainly is like a metal vessel, for the Rav holds we do not say chein nafsho unless it is old, when what is absorbed in it became nevelah.
