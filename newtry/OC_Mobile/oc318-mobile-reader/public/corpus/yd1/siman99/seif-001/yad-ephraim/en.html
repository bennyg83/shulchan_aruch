One does not nullify. See Ba'er Heitev; She'elat Ya'avetz vol. 2 siman 131 wrote that if one wishes to make a forbidden item spoiled in order to mix it into permitted food, that too is included in the prohibition of nullifying forbidden food l'chatchila — and through this the Shach's difficulty in siman 95 regarding a copper vessel is resolved (see there). In truth this is not sufficient to resolve the Shach's difficulty according to our ruling that ein mivtalin is rabbinic while we are dealing with a d'oraisa case. However, the law itself that he wrote — that this is included in ein mivtalim — requires study in my view; even for the Ra'avad and his camp who hold ein mivtalim is d'oraisa, the source is Chullin 99 regarding the cooked foreleg, and it appears this is specifically regarding nullification where the taste of the forbidden item in its substance is still in its glory and the prohibition has not departed from it, but because it is eaten through a mixture its taste is not sensed by the eater — therefore l'chatchila it is forbidden; unlike when one makes it spoiled, where its prohibition departs from its substance and when it enters the mixture its taste is already spoiled and permitted, and ein mivtalim does not apply — what causes the forbidden item itself to become permitted. So it appears from the poskim. I also saw in responsa Chacham Tzvi siman 101 who wrote explicitly that cooking something that spoils is not called nullifying a forbidden item, for there is no doubt that d'oraisa it is permitted to spoil it since one does not benefit from its taste or quantity — the taste emitted from the vessel walls is not sensed at all (see there); I rejoiced and wondered at the Gaon Ya'avetz how he wrote thus after his father the elder Gaon had already ruled the opposite. Noda B'Yehuda Mahadura Chadashah siman 45 wrote: even according to Tosafos who hold ein mivtalim l'chatchila is rabbinic, this is when one mixes a forbidden piece and removes it and only the taste is permitted d'oraisa — like the cooked foreleg; unlike nullifying dry in dry where it remains visible, which is forbidden even d'oraisa since if the forbidden item could be recognized it would not be nullified and now it is recognizable (see there). Nevertheless it appears that for a rabbinic prohibition, according to those who permit nullification or who hold ein mivtalim and permit in a lenient manner, even when the forbidden item is visible it is permitted — as proven from the case of branches that fell into the oven (see Noda B'Yehuda siman 56 regarding making yayin sheretz with pears and raisins and putting nevelah or unsalted meat into it at once when certainly there is not sixty at boiling, only afterward more yayin is added and it may be that combined there is sixty; the activity of the meat passing through and negating the sharpness of the yayin — he answered that a Jew is forbidden l'chatchila through nevelah or unsalted meat even though they say their intent is not to nullify the forbidden item; nevertheless l'chatchila it is forbidden to mix by hand, and Magen Avraham siman 447 also agrees that there the permitted food still exists unlike here; therefore the Jew must salt the meat first and rinse it and then even if there is not sixty at first it is permitted, but he should ensure there is sixty with what he adds afterward, for even though one does not usually drink yayin with milk, one does soak baked goods in butter and milk — nevertheless since there is now sixty it is permitted even if there was not sixty at first, for heter b'heter does not apply to chein nafsho, and there is no ein mivtalim l'chatchila since he does not intend to soak dairy foods at all. Israeli yayin: if it is known there will be sixty in the end it is permitted in any case as b'dieved; if there is not sixty — if a majority mix it is forbidden in places where they are accustomed to do so, but if the majority do not mix, it is forbidden to buy from them in their homes as it is fixed for them, but what the shopkeeper buys from them and brings to sell in his fixed place is permitted through kol d'parish (see there).
