Forbidden bones combine. Since they are not edible they are not in the category of prohibition; nevertheless they absorb through cooking — therefore they combine to nullify the prohibition, for the prohibition spreads into them and they also have moisture to blend the prohibition absorbed from the forbidden meat. Maharach challenged Rama from what Turei Chayim wrote that I brought in siman 98 seif 5, that even pottery in a vessel needs sixty against it — if so, here too one should say the bones are nevelah, since they cling to the forbidden meat; and what differs from a piece of meat to which the heart clings and liver above, that that piece is nevelah? So too he challenged in Perishah. I say: Moses is true and his Torah is true — this bone is truly like a vessel, for we do not say chein nafsho in it if it is metal according to Mordechai, since it has a remedy through hag'alah; likewise this bone, for it is possible to separate what was absorbed in it now through cooking with meat upon it through hag'alah, for we establish in Orach Chaim siman 451 that bone vessels are permitted through hag'alah — therefore chein nafsho does not apply here, and it combines with the permitted food.
And the body of the pot, etc. Explanation: that same pot in which one is now cooking prohibition and permitted food; but plainly, a pot that was already forbidden and afterward one cooks permitted food in it — the body of the pot certainly combines with the prohibition; and on this Tur wrote above siman 98 to be stringent that the pot combines. The reason the body of the pot does not combine at all is that in the Gemara there are two versions regarding what combines to nullify: one says in the pot itself, in the pottery of the pot; and one says in what the pot absorbed and not in the pottery — automatically the latter version is stringent, as it appears from Rashi and as I wrote on this; and since they disputed the versions and it is a Torah prohibition we go to stringency and do not estimate the pot — and the essence of the matter we estimate as it comes before us and do not estimate what the pot absorbed, etc. — end of his words; it appears we establish not like both versions; but Rashba brings some commentators who hold that what he wrote "in the pot" is a stringency, meaning we estimate only what is inside the pot, i.e. the contents and gravy, but not what was absorbed in the pot; and the latter version is lenient, that even in what was absorbed we estimate; and we establish like the latter version, and that is like Rambam whom Shulchan Aruch brought in seif 4 that we estimate with a good estimate in what was absorbed; and this is also Rashba's view to decide thus in min b'mino; if so what Shulchan Aruch wrote "and the body of the pot does not combine" — that is the pottery alone and like the latter version; except that in the latter version we rely only in its kind; but in Tur one must say thus — or perhaps the pot does not combine at all, even what was absorbed; except that afterward he brings that Rashba disagrees and decides on this.
