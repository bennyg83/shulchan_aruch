(Shach s.k. 15) And perhaps the Rav also retracted. It appears to me there is no doubt — certainly he retracted, for above (siman 73 seif 6) we find liver clinging to fowl, etc., and he wrote in the hagahah; therefore if the liver is whole and clinging to the fowl the fowl becomes chein nafsho and needs sixty from other things against the fowl — and we certainly find it implies without prior knowledge that the liver clings to it, yet nevertheless all is forbidden; even the law of davuk we say in it from kli sheni — if it was cooked and there was not sixty at first we say the piece itself is chein nafsho. I actually saw Turei Chayim (general 5 law 5) one who brought Issur VeHeter's words that even if it mixed first and without knowledge permitted food was added afterward — it is permitted; he wrote and these are his words and requires study — if so why are clinging prohibitions forbidden as explained above regarding liver, etc. However one may say that in a clinging prohibition it does not combine — end of his words; I did not merit to understand, for in davuk the taste that quickly absorbs from before, or lest it was once outside — if so all the more so when it mixed first in wet without sixty, certainly the absorption entered first before the mixture; may God enlighten my eyes.
