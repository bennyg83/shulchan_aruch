(Hagahah in Shach s.k. 15) And if so it should resolve that there we deal without knowledge, etc. It appears to me to distinguish: granted this law he wrote the reason is we consider as if the forbidden item or piece of meat fell now into milk and there is now sixty — therefore it is nullified in sixty without knowledge; but with milk whose taste was absorbed in meat — the milk is as a piece of meat whose taste is in milk, and milk in milk is min b'mino — therefore it is not nullified; investigate. Also what he wrote "if so" regarding what we taught in chapter 5 of Terumot — also here it appears to explain: what we taught in chapter 5 of Terumot means if b'shogeg — i.e. without knowledge of the mixture — and this mixed b'shogeg is permitted; if b'mezid, even if the mixture was not known, only that it mixed b'mezid for another reason — nevertheless forbidden, for we decree b'mezid lest one mix even with knowledge; but b'shogeg we do not decree b'shogeg with knowledge since b'shogeg with knowledge is only a general stringency and we do not decree; and through this the Gemara's difficulty on Rama is resolved. End of hagahah.
