One does not nullify prohibition l'chatchila. Rashba wrote in Taharat HaBayit bayit 4 beginning of sha'ar 3 and Ran chullin 98 that this is d'rabbanan according to Tosafot; but according to Raavad it is forbidden d'oraisa; however b'dieved even Raavad's view is as Mechaber wrote, and as Rashba and Ran wrote in his name — see there; and from the words of Rav R' Menachem Azariah in responsum siman 27 it is clear he holds like those who say it is d'rabbanan.
And even if it fell, etc. That is to say: it is not only forbidden to mix actual prohibition into permitted food in order to nullify it; even if it fell into permitted food, etc.
If b'shogeg it is permitted. And this is specifically in other prohibitions according to Tur, for we do not say chein nafsho in other prohibitions; but in basar b'chalav that became nevelah, even b'shogeg it is forbidden unless he increased sixty against all that became nevelah; and the Rav who holds chein nafsho in all prohibitions had to write specifically when dry mixed with dry, etc.
Forbidden for one who nullifies for himself, etc. And in Beit Yosef — it appears that if it was not his and he also did not intend to nullify for himself, it is permitted for him, since he did not permit for one for whom it was nullified for him and it was also not his — his bad act does not help — end of his words.
And also to the one for whose sake it was nullified. For if you permit it to the one for whose sake it was nullified, we are concerned lest he come to say to a non-Jew or a servant to nullify it; therefore we penalize him.
They are forbidden to sell it to a Jew. Although the prohibition itself was permitted for benefit, it was still forbidden to eat it, and now he would be benefiting from this addition so as to permit even eating it; therefore one must give it to another person for free, as Issur VeHeter wrote there. It seems to me that only when the Jew pays a premium over the non-Jews is it forbidden; otherwise there is no reason to forbid selling it to a Jew, for his wicked act did not help. And perhaps the phrase "one must give it to another for free" is not literal.
According to the view that one does not say chein nafsho in it. We rule likewise in other prohibitions in a case of major loss, as above in siman 92 seif 4 in the gloss.
But a piece that absorbed prohibition, etc., for we say chein nafsho. At first glance this is difficult — what connection does this have to chein nafsho? For even according to those who do not say chein nafsho in other prohibitions, nevertheless a piece that absorbed prohibition and permitted food was added afterward — the piece itself remains in its prohibition, as Rashba and Mechaber wrote below siman 106, that the prohibition in it is not entirely emitted; rather the Rav comes to teach us that a piece that absorbed prohibition and permitted food was added afterward — even the addition is forbidden because there is not sixty against the entire piece; but if he increased b'shogeg sixty against all that became nevelah, even in basar b'chalav wet in wet all is permitted; but in a piece that absorbed prohibition, even if he increased sixty against the entire piece, the piece itself remains forbidden, as below siman 106 — see there. Nevertheless what is written here in Shulchan Aruch — Rashba's responsum siman 495 — is true, for so he wrote in dry mixture; see Rashba there that we do not say chein nafsho only in a piece absorbed from prohibition, but in wet in wet we establish that all prohibitions that one increased upon b'shogeg are permitted; and through this Tur's words are resolved, that he wrote one need not investigate if water was added after the prohibition was put in — for in basar b'chalav chein nafsho, and one would have to explain that in basar b'chalav one must investigate; only the reason is in wet in wet we do not say chein nafsho — end of his words. But one cannot say thus, for in Bavli chullin (108b) regarding what they challenge there — why is milk permitted, it is nevelah milk, etc. — it is proven explicitly that even wet in wet chein nafsho applies in basar b'chalav; and so Rashba wrote in Taharat HaBayit 98 explicitly, and so it appears explicitly from Rosh's words end of chullin regarding hag'alah of a metal vessel and end of Avodah Zarah regarding yayin nesech that fell into a pit — we do not distinguish thus regarding chein nafsho — see there; and even though there are poskim who distinguish thus, as explained above siman 92 seif 4 — that is in other prohibitions, but in basar b'chalav one cannot distinguish thus; and furthermore Rashba and Tur presumably do not hold thus, as written; and what is written in the name of Rashba's responsum is not so, for otherwise he would contradict his own words in Taharat HaBayit and also be against Bavli; but one who examines Rashba's responsum there will see he wrote only regarding other prohibitions where we do not say chein nafsho, distinguishing between wet and piece — in wet, if afterward added up to sixty all is permitted, and in a piece it remains forbidden because the prohibition is not entirely emitted, as explained below siman 110; and this matter is not relevant here (and I settled Tur's words in my book another way — see there); if so the Rav's words require study.
And some say that even in a place, etc. Issur VeHeter klal 24 law 10 wrote explicitly that even where we say chein nafsho — such as wet in wet or wet in dry or dry in wet — there is also a distinction between known and not known; for even though it became nevelah once, since there was not sixty in permitted food against it because it was not known to him at that time and he does not taste it now — the taste of prohibition — we can say well that all permitted food combines to nullify it — end of his words; and Darkei Moshe and Turei Chayim klal 85 law 8 ruled like him; and accordingly what is written here in the hagahah — even in a place where we do not say chein nafsho, etc. — means: not only in a place where we say chein nafsho is there a distinction between known and not known, but even in a place where we do not say chein nafsho do not say that even if known it is permitted as long as he increased b'shogeg — rather whatever is known is forbidden; and so Issur VeHeter writes there, that in a place where we do not say chein nafsho there is also a distinction between known and not known; nevertheless Issur VeHeter's words and the Rav require study, for since we establish chein nafsho, immediately when it mixed it became nevelah — what difference if it was not known? Nevertheless immediately it is considered a nevelah piece, and we do not find any posek to distinguish between known and not known; and so it appears in Bavli chullin (108) that they challenge: why, when Rav said an olive's bulk of meat fell into a cauldron of milk — meat is forbidden and milk permitted — why is milk permitted, it is nevelah milk? Rashi explained: behold a small amount of milk absorbed in meat became nevelah since it was forbidden, and when it returned and was emitted in the rest of the milk it became min b'mino — prohibited milk in permitted milk, and Rav holds min b'mino is not nullified — end of his words; and they answer: in a boiling cauldron it absorbs absorption, emits does not emit — see there and investigate; and so it is proven from all poskim who challenged what we establish chein nafsho in all prohibitions from medumei and menasech, that they forbid only by calculation — and many such challenges, resolved with difficulty; and some ruled from this that in other prohibitions we do not say chein nafsho; and if so they should have resolved there that it deals without known — and so it is proven explicitly from what Semag and Tur wrote siman 94 seif 2: if one inserts a dairy spoon into a meat pot twice and it is not known in between — one needs sixty both times, because the first time the entire spoon became nevelah; and since it was not known in between it was not nullified and one needs again a second time to nullify, as written there; and so from Beit Yosef siman 92, Maharshal chullin siman 57, Bach, Derishah end of siman 98, and other Acharonim who wrote from what Rosh wrote in responsa on a pot that had twenty-nine olives of permitted food and an olive's bulk of blood fell in, and another pot that had thirty olives of permitted food and an olive's bulk of milk fell in and they became mixed b'shogeg — all is permitted because each nullifies the taste of the other; and it is proven he holds we do not say a piece became nevelah in other prohibitions; and if so Rosh came only to say each prohibition nullifies the taste of the other prohibition, but regarding chein nafsho he does not deal — perhaps he deals when they mixed together before the mixture was known, as Issur VeHeter himself explained there that responsum of Rosh — thus Or Zarua and not so; and so it appears explicitly from Beit Yosef in this siman regarding what Semak wrote that the sage must investigate, etc. — see there and investigate; also what Turei Chayim writes there proves Issur VeHeter's words, that even min b'mino that requires sixty d'rabbanan is not permitted except when increased b'shogeg — meaning it deals with wet in wet, for in dry one in two is nullified — end of his words; I did not reach the end of his view, for Tur follows his reasoning that he holds we do not say chein nafsho in other prohibitions; and so it appears from Maharai in Hagahat ShaDa and Maharshal chullin 99 who concluded and wrote that what we establish — all prohibitions that one increased upon b'shogeg are permitted — is specifically in a dry thing one should practice thus, but not in a moist thing that has noten taam, for we establish chein nafsho — end of his words; and we do not distinguish between known and not known (and what Issur VeHeter wrote that even wet in dry and added before the mixture was known — even the piece itself is permitted without hag'alah — is not so, as we establish below siman 106 that the prohibition is not entirely emitted from it — end of his words); and perhaps the Rav also retracted and therefore did not write this law in his hagahot — requires study. Also what Issur VeHeter writes there, that even in a place where we do not say chein nafsho, if known it is forbidden even b'shogeg, and he explains what Tur wrote: it is written in Sefer HaMitzvot that the sage must investigate if water was put in the pot after the prohibition was put in, that that water does not combine to nullify the prohibition — and it appears one need not, for we establish all prohibitions that one increased upon b'shogeg are permitted, and we need not be concerned lest he intended to increase b'mezid, for if we come to be concerned for this we also do not believe him when he says he did not increase — end of his words; that is without known in between, but if known Tur also concedes it is forbidden — and it is not clear; and furthermore if so why did he challenge Sefer HaMitzvot — perhaps Semak was concerned water was put in b'shogeg after it was known, as Issur VeHeter himself explained at the end of klal 37 Semak's words thus; and even though in Darkei Moshe he strained to resolve this, for Tur holds we are not concerned that if it were known in between he would immediately come to the sage to ask — nevertheless what Tur wrote "and we need not be concerned lest he intended to increase b'mezid," etc., is not said according to this — investigate (and in another version siman 11): Or Zarua — even if known in between it is permitted since he increased b'shogeg, and so it appears from poskim; and furthermore say: if you say even where chein nafsho is not relevant it is not permitted when increased b'shogeg except without known — if so the mishnah in chapter 5 of Terumot: a se'ah of terumah fell into less than a hundred of hullin and afterward more hullin fell in — if b'shogeg permitted, if b'mezid forbidden — and Yerushalmi says the same for all prohibitions increased b'shogeg permitted, b'mezid forbidden — and we say in many places all prohibitions increased b'shogeg are permitted, b'mezid forbidden — if so this b'shogeg must be explained before it was known, and if so how does it apply again to him? And if b'mezid — how is b'mezid relevant when he did not know? We must strain and say b'shogeg is before the mixture was known and b'mezid is after the mixture was known, for b'shogeg is like b'mezid — and this the mind cannot bear; Or Zarua as we said, and so Raavan explained end of Avodah Zarah (57b): Explanation — if they fell b'shogeg without intending to nullify, permitted; b'mezid intending to nullify, forbidden, for one does not nullify prohibition l'chatchila — end of his words; and so Semag negative commandments 87 (30a): Or Zarua — we do not distinguish at all between known and not known, but between b'shogeg and b'mezid, as it appears from Tur and all poskim.
And accordingly, etc., and they did not practice thus. Meaning: accordingly, since the addition does not help — if so according to those who hold wet in wet chein nafsho, or a piece that absorbed prohibition for all, or the mixture was known according to Issur VeHeter — one would have had to investigate; only they did not practice to investigate in all this; and in Taz it appears he understood that this "and they did not practice thus" refers only to the first case — but this is certainly not so, for what difference is there?
