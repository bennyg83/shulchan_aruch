(Seif 5) One does not nullify a prohibition l'chatchila. Noda B'Yehuda second edition part 14 siman 45 wrote: even those who permit nullifying a prohibition l'chatchila from the Torah — that is only such as foreleg in Shiloh where only taste is mixed, but to mix the substance of the prohibition such as dry in dry — for everyone forbidden from the Torah [and see in Ra'avad's explanation and Rosh chapter 1 of Orlah mishnah 6 it appears even mixtures dry in dry visible are forbidden only because of rabbinic decree]; and so in Mishnah Lamelech Laws of Me'ilah. But what he brings proof from Chullin (100) is no proof that forbidden in majority is stated below — for there it is majority not before us, as written in Yad HaMelech. Further one may say the sugya in Chullin according to what Ritva wrote in Chullin (daf 98) on that which he said regarding foreleg in Shiloh: it is a novelty that one nullifies prohibition l'chatchila — for even for those who permit to nullify, etc., generally from the Torah — nevertheless it is a novelty in foreleg in Shiloh that its mitzvah is to nullify prohibition l'chatchila, unlike generally which is optional — until here; and through this the sugya (on daf 100) regarding sending the bird of the metzora is resolved: if it were forbidden, Torah would not command to send; and in Sefer Sha'arei Mitzvah Laws of Forbidden Foods he challenged Tosafot who permit nullifying prohibition from the Torah from Chullin (100) and did not mention the above distinction; and likewise one may say (on daf 115) regarding sending away the nest. And it appears also for everyone difficult, for we establish if he does not intend to nullify, for everyone permitted (and so in chapter 1 of Terumot, except Peri Chadash in Orach Chaim siman 473 forbids if the prohibition is recognizable and he benefits from it — but here he does not benefit from it itself; and perhaps Torah commanded to send so he should not intend to nullify). And according to what I wrote, all also I wrote what he challenged in Lekutei Shoshanim: why do I need the verse "every pure bird you may eat" to include the sent one that it is permitted — logically I know Torah did not command to send for harm; and see in Orach Chaim siman 626 that regarding a mitzvah we do not say ein mivtalin issur l'chatchila, since he intends for the mitzvah we do not decree lest he do the entire mitzvah in prohibition; and further because mitzvot were not given for benefit. And it appears difficult from foreleg in Shiloh: why is it heter that comes from a prohibition, that he nullifies the foreleg — behold it is a mitzvah to cook together; but according to what I wrote nevertheless why did Torah command specifically to nullify it; and further one may say there in Orach Chaim the reason is mitzvot were not given for benefit, as Agudah wrote — and this does not apply where there is bodily benefit.
