One does not nullify a prohibition l'chatchila, etc. In siman 84 seif 13 it is explained that if his intent is only for another matter, such as to fix honey, it is permitted; and in siman 137 seif 2 I wrote this is specifically when it cannot be done otherwise. And what he wrote here "he transgressed and it was nullified" or "he increased," etc. — it appears this is repetition, for nullification is increasing; and it appears his meaning is: he transgressed and nullified — he threw a small amount of prohibition into abundant permitted food where there is sixty against it, or it fell by itself when there was not sixty in the permitted food but he increased afterward. And what he wrote "if b'shogeg permitted" — one must say his meaning is: he transgressed b'mezid except that he thought this matter is permitted; and this supports what I wrote shortly that one who errs in law is called b'shogeg.
If b'shogeg permitted. Tur brought this in this language, that we establish all prohibitions that one increased upon b'shogeg are permitted; and Beit Yosef brought a source for this dispute of R' Meir and R' Yehuda and R' Yosi and R' Shimon regarding "they fell and were crushed," which Beit Yosef brings afterward on this page; and he challenged Tur's words against themselves, for here he ruled like R' Yosi and R' Shimon leniently that we do not penalize b'shogeg as b'mezid, and in siman 110 he rules specifically when it fell by itself, but if he cast it, even b'shogeg we penalize as b'mezid; and in my view it appears the source of this law is not from there but an explicit statement in Yerushalmi Terumot chapter Sa'ah Terumah — and these are his words: R' Avahu said in the name of R' Yochanan: all prohibitions that one increased upon b'shogeg are permitted and b'mezid forbidden — and accordingly there is no difficulty, for certainly R' Yochanan rules like R' Yosi and R' Shimon explicitly, and his reason is that wherever the permitted food depends on increase; and likewise in that case of "they fell and were crushed" — when they come before us we see crushed ones, and when he comes to ask it is permitted automatically since already increased or the significant matter was crushed; and if he was silent and said nothing we permit him, for perhaps at the time the prohibition fell there it was so and did not increase — only he revealed to us that it increased afterward; and therefore certainly he is believed that he was b'shogeg, for if he wished he could be silent — unlike that case in siman 110, when the mixture comes before us we forbid it, only he wishes to permit based on his testimony that he cast one to the sea — in this we do not believe him to be lenient on a clear prohibition based on his testimony; found his silence was not permitting; and proof for this distinction we find in Bekhorot (daf 36) where we say there: what if you say there, if he wished he would not say, etc. — see there; and likewise in that case of "they fell and were crushed" we establish like R' Yosi leniently, for it is literally like this one who increases upon the prohibition; and this appears to me Taz's intent in Tur's words where he wrote "and it appears one need not that even if he added," etc., and we need not be concerned lest he intended to increase b'mezid (so copied the text in Rashal chapter 3 siman 59 and Turei Chayim general rule 5); and it appears to me Tur learned from Semag's words that certainly he is not concerned lest it increased after the prohibition fell there and stood a while — for if so the piece itself would have become nevelah like basar b'chalav and afterward adding water would not help unless there is sixty against the entire dish and also against the pot, and this is impossible; and in Beit Yosef it is written: if there was sixty in that water against all that is in the pot, Semag concedes it is permitted if he put them b'shogeg — and this is very astounding, for we also require sixty against the pot itself; and because of this Tur learned Semag's view that for this certainly we are not concerned, for we do not establish presumption anywhere to say it was in a way that would be forbidden, as I wrote above in this siman shortly; except that here there is concern in a matter where there is remedy to nullify the prohibition through increase — in this we suspect him that b'mezid he made the remedy; found here was the concern lest immediately when the prohibition fell he increased water there before the prohibition spread — like what we say in siman 92 regarding a drop of milk regarding stirring the pot; and therefore Semag said one must investigate afterward whether increase was made there; and on this Tur challenged: for what need to investigate? Granted if we penalized b'shogeg as b'mezid there would be reason to investigate if he says he increased b'shogeg — we tell him that even so it is forbidden, for we penalize b'shogeg as b'mezid; but in truth it is not so, rather b'shogeg is permitted for the reason we mentioned above; and on this he said we establish all prohibitions, etc.; and he said afterward "lest he intended to increase b'mezid" — meaning: that which we say all prohibitions that one increased b'shogeg are permitted — that is when we know it was done b'shogeg, such as done by another, or it is clear to us he did not know of the fall of the prohibition and increased — then permitted and we do not penalize b'shogeg as b'mezid; but here where it is not known to us — therefore even if he says it was done b'shogeg it is forbidden, lest he intended b'mezid and lies and says he is b'shogeg; and therefore we need to investigate whether any increase was made in order to forbid even if he says he was b'shogeg — this is not so, for if so you hold him a liar at the time of investigation — what does investigation help? Even if he says it did not increase he would not be believed if we suspect he lies. Therefore there is no need for investigation at all, even in basar b'chalav and a neighbor's, in other prohibitions; except that in other prohibitions we need not say it deals with increase immediately after the fall, for according to Tur's view we do not say the piece itself becomes nevelah — if so increase b'shogeg afterward also helps to remedy the prohibition; and in this Semag requires investigation of a sage lest he made the remedy of increase afterward; and on this Tur disagrees, for b'shogeg is permitted, as I mentioned regarding basar b'chalav if he increased immediately — found resolved the great wonder: how did Tur write b'shogeg permitted — behold in basar b'chalav we say the piece itself becomes nevelah, and Tur should have said but in basar b'chalav one needs investigation of a sage; and further resolved what he challenged in what Tur wrote "and we need not be concerned lest he intended to increase b'mezid," etc. — why do I need this, since he already brought we establish for permission from the Gemara — why challenge Semag here more than the Gemara; and according to what I wrote all works with heaven's help, and it is an explanation like a bolt and flower.
And if b'mezid, etc. It appears from Tur's language where he wrote here "if he did not intend to nullify the prohibition," etc., and likewise afterward where he wrote "but one who intends to increase," etc. — the essence depends on intent to increase: if he intended in this, even though he erred in law and thought it is permitted to increase to nullify the prohibition — nevertheless he is called b'mezid since he intended b'mezid to nullify the prohibition; and so it appears from Rashal's words which I will mention shortly s.k. 10; but in truth it does not appear so, for if so Tur is difficult where he wrote here that if we come to be concerned for this we also do not believe him when he says he did not increase, etc.; and if it were so that one who errs in law is called b'mezid since he increases with intent to nullify the prohibition — you would still challenge Tur on Semag: let us say one must investigate whether he did it b'mezid, that he intended to increase upon the prohibition to nullify it and erred in law and thought it is permitted — in this he would not lie and would say the truth, for there is no suspicion he committed an offense but a mistake — and why do we not investigate whether he erred or not? Rather it must be that this too is called b'shogeg; and proof from Shabbat chapter Kol HaGadol where we say in the first mishnah there: if he forgot there is prohibition of melachah in this, even though he knows today is Shabbat, he is called b'shogeg — likewise here; and one should not say there is some distinction between b'shogeg of Shabbat and b'shogeg of other prohibitions, for in chapter HaNizakin (Gittin daf 53–54) they bring b'shogeg of Shabbat for other prohibitions in this. And further it appears to bring proof from responsa Mahari"k root 167 who ruled regarding a woman who committed adultery and did not know whether there is prohibition — if she is considered b'shogeg; and he toiled to find reasons she is not considered b'shogeg because in any case she intends to transgress with a man and commits adultery under him — for it is not written "if a man causes his wife to go astray" regarding intent specifically for prohibition, but "and commits adultery with her" is written — end of his words; learn from this in other places this reason does not apply and properly called b'shogeg if he erred in law; and therefore it appears to me what Tur wrote "but one who intends to increase" means he knows there is prohibition in the matter and nevertheless does it — this is called b'mezid; so it appears to me. Again I found thus in Tosafot Bekhorot (daf 23) in the statement beginning "hold," etc., that even for one who holds we penalize b'shogeg as b'mezid in nullifying a prohibition — nevertheless b'shogeg who thinks it is permitted to nullify is permitted.
And likewise for one for whom it was nullified for his sake. Rashal explained there in chapter 3: specifically when this one knew it was nullified for him and it is pleasing to him even if he did not command to nullify it; but if he did not know from him it is permitted for him, for he is like b'shogeg; and where forbidden for him, forbidden also for his household members — so Rashal explained there.
And they did not practice thus. And this is Tur's reason disagreeing with Semag who wrote one must ask if permitted food was added afterward; and Tur wrote one need not, etc.; and Rashal wrote Semag's reason: if added even b'shogeg it is forbidden where the piece itself became nevelah — if so what helps what was added? One needs afterward to nullify also the permitted food; and so Beit Yosef — except he corrected Tur's words who holds we do not say the piece itself becomes nevelah except in basar b'chalav — in this he did not correct, for nevertheless in basar b'chalav one would need to investigate if added afterward; but in my humble opinion Tur's words are correct from what I mentioned shortly, and we need not be concerned lest it was added after it became nevelah, for we do not establish prohibition presumptively, and as it is found so it was; and Semag was concerned only where the increase helps, as I mentioned, that in this a person is suspected to remedy b'mezid since in any case there is no prohibition; and Tur disagrees, as we mentioned; and so is the essence. However, if the teacher knows it was added but it is not known when — in such a case certainly one must investigate when it was added; so it appears for practical halachah, even though Rashal is stringent in every matter to ask afterward — nevertheless after the custom nothing except as I wrote in my humble opinion.
