(There seif 5) One does not nullify a forbidden item, etc.; if b'shogeg permitted, if b'mezid forbidden. Peri Chadash challenged: this is against the Gemara in Gittin 53 and 54 that R' Yosi disagrees with R' Yosi who said regarding cooking on Shabbos b'shogeg he may not eat — if so we penalize b'shogeg as b'mezid; we taught Medamei and Menasech — R' Yosi exempts b'shogeg, liable b'mezid, and we do not penalize; and we answer this is from Torah that one, this from rabbis, etc.; if so here too, why do we not penalize b'shogeg as b'mezid? It appears to me to resolve: there regarding cooking on Shabbos, even b'shogeg cooking, nevertheless desecration of Shabbos — the essence of the prohibition is the cooking — he did melachah on Shabbos; likewise Medamei and Menasech — if it were d'oraisa we would penalize b'shogeg as b'mezid because to render terumah impure or to medamei in hullin is itself nullifying a prohibition even without intent; but regarding nullifying a forbidden item there is no prohibition at all when intending to nullify; but if nullifying b'shogeg without intending to nullify at all — only that it fell by itself — there is no prohibition and no room to penalize b'shogeg as b'mezid; see Taz s.k. 8 what he challenges on Tur contradicting himself — there siman 100 specifically if it fell, but if he cast it, even b'shogeg we penalize, etc., and here wrote we do not penalize; I say there is a great distinction: granted there the beginning of the decree was if he cast it does not help, otherwise what did the Sages accomplish in their enactment of something important 32 — if casting helps, how say something important 32; and since the essence of the decree was from the start not to cast b'mezid, well we decree b'shogeg as b'mezid; if so here in nullifying a forbidden item the entire decree was not to nullify l'chatchila, but the prohibition to eat is only because of penalty in such a case — we do not penalize b'shogeg as b'mezid, for we decree b'shogeg, etc., we say, but penalizing b'shogeg as b'mezid we do not say; investigate there. Granted there we well penalize b'shogeg as b'mezid because the entire reason is in falling one of them into the sea — permitted because we say the permitted fell on the surface that it should have fallen from the majority — behold there is much permitted, nevertheless each says we suspend the forbidden fall to the sea and the permitted remains; all this when it fell by itself; but casting we penalize b'shogeg — they said and they said lest the forbidden fell; but in nullifying the forbidden item the reasoning permits, for the forbidden is not sensed — only we penalize him because they enacted not to nullify l'chatchila on the surface that it is permitted now since the forbidden is not sensed; all this b'mezid; but b'shogeg some say we do not penalize, for the taste of the forbidden is not sensed at all; investigate. And what Taz wrote that if it is a prohibition nullified in the majority, even for those who penalize b'shogeg as b'mezid, in such a case all agree we do not penalize — it appears to me proven not better than b'shogeg who wrote one need not inquire if it increased afterward, for if it is not known there is forbidden in it — permitted, for we establish all Torah prohibitions if one increased b'shogeg permitted — we do not penalize; if so it appears for those who penalize forbidden, or that it is not better than b'shogeg; and what he wrote and so from Tosafos's words see there that it appears he was explaining what Tosafos wrote that for those who hold b'shogeg permitted and the difficulty that he says "I nullified them" appears b'mezid — on this he wrote that even nullifying b'mezid, since he thinks it is permitted to nullify, is considered b'shogeg, and there is no place for Taz's words at all; investigate.
