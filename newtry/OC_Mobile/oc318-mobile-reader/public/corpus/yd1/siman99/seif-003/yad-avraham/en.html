(There) But if it was cooked first, the bones in it combine with the prohibition because they absorbed from the nevelah meat when it was cooked alone. The Acharonim wrote likewise if the nevelah was already salted — the bones absorbed from the prohibition through the salting. And in my humble opinion specifically in bones that have marrow inside that are soft and absorb through and through; and as proven explicitly in Rashba whom Tur and the hagahah bring end of seif 1 — salting the bone helps for the marrow within it; and see in Mishnah Berurah general rule 15 s.k. 23. But in bones that have no marrow, behold they are like bone vessels, as Taz and Shach wrote; and it was already explained in siman 98 seif 4 in the hagahah that a vessel is not forbidden through salting except for the measure of a peel, and if cooked one need not estimate except against the peel; and see what I wrote there seif 4 similar to what Mechaber wrote here — see there and investigate. And in what I wrote it works well with what the poskim wondered: that which we say in the Gemara Pischei HaGolah (daf 98) regarding the foreleg in Shiloh of a nazirite ram — according to one who holds the measure of prohibitions is in sixty, he holds meat and bones of the foreleg against meat and bones of the ram — we estimate and there is sixty and permitted; and these poskim wondered: why need to estimate against bones of the foreleg — behold in Yerushalmi the opposite is stated, that peels of prohibition raise the permitted food, as Shulchan Aruch wrote in this siman; and what Ran wrote that bones of the foreleg have marrow is insufficient according to Shulchan Aruch, for even bones that have marrow combine with permitted food — only the marrow in them combines with the prohibition; and Beer HaGolah's words here are not precise — see there. And according to what I wrote it is correct: in foreleg in Shiloh it must deal with that it was salted first because of blood, like the law of salting meat for a pot because of blood; and this is plain — if so, already the bones absorbed from the marrow within them and from the meat, for blood cannot be without grease — see Zevachim (daf 26) and Rambam chapter 1 of Laws of Shechitah Chullin (daf 8); and therefore one must estimate against them; and there is no proof at all from here to forbid if they were not salted in bones that have marrow — unlike Shach — see there; and all this appears clear and plain — investigate well.
