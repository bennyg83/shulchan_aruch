If a small amount of prohibition was absorbed, etc. Rashba in Taharat HaBayit daf 126 learned this law — and these are his words: from that which is stated in chapter Ein Ma'amidin: gentile vessels — gentile puts wine in them, Israel puts water in them, meaning and drinks them; and they asked there: what about putting beer in it? And we conclude it is permitted, for the text there is: R' Yitzchak son of Rav Yehuda permitted Rav Chiya to put beer in it and he went and put wine in it, and nevertheless he was not concerned to forbid beer because of wine — he said it is merely incidental; and the reason for this matter is that wine absorbed in a vessel is a small matter, for whatever is used in cold, its absorption is only small; and since so, and this vessel is used with permitted food abundantly, it is impossible to come to noten taam — therefore even l'chatchila permitted; and therefore a slight prohibition absorbed in the pot or in jugs and the like is permitted to use them l'chatchila even b'ben yomo, since it is impossible ever to come to noten taam; but if absorbed in a vessel whose way is to use it sometimes in a small amount, such as a bowl and the like — forbidden to use it even abundantly, a decree lest one use it in a small amount and come to noten taam — end of his words. One may challenge this: whence does he learn thus from this? Perhaps it is different here, for permitted to put other liquids there because they spoil wine, as Rashi explained in chapter Ein Ma'amidin (daf 33) that beer nullifies the taste of wine; and Tosafot wrote there in the name of R' Yitzchak that blood and all liquids are permitted like beer; and it appears one may say Rashba holds liquids do not spoil wine and disagrees with Rashi and Tosafot; and so wrote in Sefer HaPerishah — and this is not correct, for Rashba himself in Taharat HaBayit daf 155 wrote — and these are his words: all vessels, if one comes to use them with other liquids before they were kashered — permitted, provided he rinses first the wine residue on the mouth of the vessels, as taught: jugs, etc. — end of his words; and here the permission is not because wine is a small matter and the vessel's use is abundantly specifically, for all vessels equal to permitted implies even small ones; rather certainly because they spoil wine was the permission — if so the challenge returns to its place: whence does he say the reason is because what was absorbed is small? And Ra'ah in Bedek HaBayit challenged Rashba on this: if so, wine too let Israel use it; and some say: wine in wine forbids in whatever amount, for we say there in the Gemara that jugs are not forbidden in benefit, and whatever is not forbidden in benefit does not forbid in a minute amount. And this is not a challenge on Rashba, for already proven from his words in Taharat HaBayit daf 149 that even where not forbidden in benefit it forbids in a minute amount. And Ran in chapter Ein Ma'amidin brought these words of Rashba, for so wrote in the name of R' Yonah and Ra'avad — and these are his words: however we hear that water of filling and pouring are permitted for drinking; and Rashba challenged: if so we find nullifying prohibition l'chatchila, for they put water or beer in this vessel in order to nullify the taste of wine — and the water and vessels are permitted. And R' Yitzchak resolved for him that absorption of wine is only slight, and since impossible to come to noten taam they nullify it l'chatchila; and from here proof for what some rabbis of France permitted a wooden vessel used with yayin nesech that needs pouring of mere shell, like any slight prohibition that suffices with shell; and so Ra'avad wrote that from this we hear nullifying slight prohibition l'chatchila, even in other prohibitions such as milk and blood that fell into a pot — and specifically when the prohibition that fell there is slight that impossible to come to noten taam, or fell in a vessel in which one does not cook except extensive cooking, etc. And I do not see their law has proof from here, for kashering of filling and pouring is not because they emit the vessel from wine, for we do not find anywhere that cold emits — rather certainly water does not kasher except because water spoils wine absorbed in the vessel and nullifies taste and does not mix it with permitted food, but it is as if burning it in its place; and further, even if you say they extract it and mix it with permitted food — nullifying prohibition l'chatchila is not forbidden except for one who intended to nullify it in order to benefit from it, etc.; but intending to kasher the vessel and not benefiting from the prohibition — permitted; and therefore no proof from here that absorption of wine should be only slight that shell suffices — end of his words. And behold from this a challenge arose for me on Tur who wrote in siman 122 on Rashba's words, and it does not appear to permit by kal v'chomer, for even a pot that is not ben yomo which is permitted is forbidden because of a decree lest ben yomo — all the more so in that vessel itself, even though its way is not to use it in a small amount, we should decree lest one use it in a small amount — end of his words; since he disagrees with Rashba, automatically it appears to him like Ran that there is no slight prohibition alone — automatically shell does not suffice for gentile vessel; and why did he bring in siman 135 Rashba's words that shell suffices — behold these matters contradict each other: if shell suffices stands, automatically it is only slight prohibition; if stands that he disagrees on what he permitted slight prohibition in a vessel used abundantly. And it appears for practical halachah to resolve everything correctly, also what Beit Yosef challenged on Tur in siman 122 — his challenge is no challenge at all on Rashba, for perforce permitted only because not common in a small amount — and what relevance has this to ben yomo pot, etc., common to cook in this and that — end of his words; and certainly proper to resolve Tur's words from Beit Yosef's challenge: how did our master Tur compare them to each other, and the distinction is before us at the beginning of investigation. Therefore it appears to me Rashba's method that we have two challenges: one, why did they permit putting other liquids in gentile jugs — in this one may resolve because they are noten taam for spoilage; except there is another challenge: after all one does not nullify prohibition l'chatchila, and in that you mix prohibition in permitted food and spoil it you nullify it to prohibition and this matter is forbidden; and on this he resolved that here there was only slight prohibition and impossible to come to noten taam — therefore they nullify it; and since we came to this resolution it appears to Rashba and his teachers that we need not the resolution we mentioned in what spoils, but even what does not spoil, such as milk and blood, permitted in this since impossible to come to noten taam since it is a large vessel; and we do not need resolution of spoilage except in a small vessel where we decree lest one use permitted food in small amount — in this the spoilage resolution helps; and therefore Rashba wrote all vessels that gentile put wine in are permitted with other liquids, meaning even small ones, because they spoil; and there it is not relevant to decree lest one use in small amount and nullify prohibition l'chatchila, for there is no decree from this prohibition that one does not nullify — rather the decree is lest one come to prohibition of noten taam; and in this the spoilage resolution helps; and therefore Rashba wrote here "and the reason for this matter is that wine absorbed in a vessel is a small matter" — and concluded with this; and therefore permitted even l'chatchila — his intent here: regarding taste of permitted food we could have said because they spoil, except that if so it would in any case be forbidden l'chatchila since one does not nullify prohibition l'chatchila; therefore I explain the reason is only slight prohibition — therefore it helps even l'chatchila in this since it is a large vessel; and on this Tur challenged in this way: certainly a challenge that one does not nullify — you spoke well, since the vessel is large in this no decree applies, for in any case it spoils; except you come to resolve also the first challenge regarding permission, and practical difference for other prohibitions such as milk and blood — this reasoning helps; and this is not so, for since you have no proof from the Gemara on this — the Gemara deals with something that spoils — whence do you say in what does not spoil that large vessel helps? Perhaps there too we decree because of small permitted food, as we decree in a pot that is not ben yomo; and even though one may distinguish between these decrees regarding common use as Beit Yosef wrote — nevertheless difficult whence do we distinguish; granted if proven from the Gemara I would be forced — what is not so now, for certainly we say as we decree in not ben yomo a distant decree, kal v'chomer for such a distant decree — and whence do we distinguish between them; found according to this Tur also holds Rashba's reasoning that large vessel helps regarding that one does not nullify prohibition l'chatchila since it is only slight — and this is in something that spoils; but in something that does not spoil it does not help, since in any case we must decree; therefore well wrote in siman 135 that shell helps since it is only slight and shell in any case we require lest one use in small amount, for there it deals with wine in wine that does not spoil; and since Tur and Ran disagree with Rashba and they are later, proper to be stringent like them that large vessel does not help except where it spoils — in this Tur and Ran agree; also Ramban holds like Ran as I bring in Taharat HaBayit daf 145 that shell does not permit except that Taz wrote on him "and in their words we listen to be lenient" — and this does not apply here regarding Torah prohibition such as milk and blood where there is no spoilage — for in this Rashba and his teachers permitted, and for Ramban and Ran and Tur forbidden, and we listen to them since it is of Torah; and in truth Tur's reasoning we mentioned is very correct, for from the Gemara there is no proof to permit even in large vessel in prohibition that does not spoil — for in the Gemara large vessel helps only regarding spoilage, and lest the challenge "behold one does not nullify l'chatchila." And so we establish stringently that large vessel does not help where there is no spoilage, like Ramban and Ran and Tur and Ra'ah in Bedek HaBayit, and so Maharach and Levush.
