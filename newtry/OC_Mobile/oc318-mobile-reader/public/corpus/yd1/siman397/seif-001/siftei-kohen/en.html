They observe mourning. Ramban states the rule: whenever evidence would be accepted to permit a married woman to remarry, relatives observe mourning on that evidence. Bach rules likewise; see Even HaEzer 17. Bach further writes that where a letter reports a relative's death and it is uncertain whether the death occurred within thirty days, one must mourn, because the deceased is presumed alive until recently and we say he died only now. Responsa Mahari ibn Lev agrees. If the letter is written by a scholar, however, one may assume more than thirty days have passed, for otherwise the writer would have specified the date. Maharam Mintz 95 also requires mourning based on the presumption of recent death. If a ruling was initially given not to mourn because of doubt and it later becomes known that the death was within thirty days, it appears that mourning must then be observed. See the beginning of siman 396.
