Two say. See Baer Heitev. Responsa Yerios HaOhel, siman 38, rules like the authorities who require mourning when one does not know whether the report was received within thirty days or after thirty days. Responsa Radbaz, new edition, siman 62, rules likewise, as do Responsa Yad Eliyahu, siman 44, and Bechor Shor in his novellae to Moed Katan 20b. See also Responsa Chacham Tzvi, siman 33; Responsa Teshuas Chen, siman 17; Atzei Arazim, Even HaEzer 17, seif katan 114; Noda BiYehudah, first edition, Even HaEzer, siman 31; Noda BiYehudah, second edition, Yoreh Deah, siman 208; Mutzal MeEsh, siman 25; and Shaar HaMelech, Laws of Marriage 4:17. Responsa Beis Ephraim, Yoreh Deah, siman 77, discusses a person who received a letter stating that his father had died 'eight days ago.' It was uncertain whether this meant that eight days had already elapsed before the letter was written, in which case thirty days had passed by the time the letter arrived and the report was distant, or that the day of writing was the eighth day, in which case thirty days had not passed and the report was recent, requiring mourning. Although Bach and other authorities maintain that where it is doubtful whether a report is distant one must mourn, here mourning is unnecessary. The wording naturally means that eight days had already elapsed, as in the phrases, 'These twenty years I have been in your house,' and, 'These two years the famine has been in the land.' In particular, since the writer was a scholar, he presumably chose the Scriptural expression carefully. One may also combine the view of certain Geonim that a report heard on the thirtieth day is already considered distant. Although the halachah does not follow that view, it may serve as an additional factor here. Responsa Yad Eliyahu, siman 44, discusses one who received a distant letter reporting a death, but the wording could mean either that his teacher died or that his father died, and more than thirty days had passed. He rules that in either event the recipient must tear his garment, and that tear suffices even if it is later learned that the deceased was his father, similar to siman 340, seif 24. Regarding laundering and haircutting, however, he need not observe the restrictions, because the doubt concerns rabbinic law. He further writes that if the uncertainty is between two people, where the teacher of one is the father of the other, so that it is uncertain whether the deceased was the father of this one or of that one, and the report is within thirty days, both must mourn. Although uncertainty in mourning is generally resolved leniently, here one of the two is certainly obligated, and therefore both are obligated because of the doubt. This is also the reason for the Rama's ruling in siman 374, seif 8, regarding a child whose paternity is uncertain between a first and second husband. The wording of the question there further indicates that if there were also a doubt whether the report was within thirty days, there would be two uncertainties and both would be exempt. See there.
