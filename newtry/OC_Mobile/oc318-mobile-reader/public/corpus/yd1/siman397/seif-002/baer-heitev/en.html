And see in Even HaEzer siman 17 where testimony on the basis of which a woman is remarried is explained. And the Bach wrote: where they wrote to someone that his relative died and there is doubt whether it is within thirty days, he is obligated to mourn — for as long as we can uphold him in his presumptive status we do so, and we say that now recently he died. However, where it is a letter in the handwriting of a sage, we say that presumably it is after thirty days — for if it were possible that the letter would reach his hand within thirty days he would not have written ambiguously but would have clarified. And so too R. Moshe Mintz wrote in responsa that one must mourn based on the principle of presumption as stated above. And he wrote that if it happened that the decisors ruled he need not mourn out of doubt, and then it became known to him retroactively that he died within thirty days — the matter requires analysis whether he must go back and mourn or not, see there. And in my view he must mourn in such a case, and see above at the beginning of siman 396 and analyze carefully — end of the Siftei Kohen (and see in the responsum I mentioned) (and in Nekudot HaKesef he corrected his words, that one should read: and in my humble opinion it seems that one need not mourn, etc.) And in my view he must mourn, and not like he does not need to go back and mourn:
