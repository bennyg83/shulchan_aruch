(Siman 397 in the Taz se’if katan 2) elaborated to prove that we do not uphold him in his presumptive status (chazakah), and all the proofs he brought must be rejected. First proof he brought from a fallen wall — one can say that there it is different, since there is a weakening factor before us in that the wall fell upon him; moreover, when we say retroactively from the time the wall fell he died, there is a known time — but here there is no known time for you to say retroactively from this day he died, therefore until now we uphold him in the presumption of being alive. Second proof he brought from one who exchanged a cow for a donkey — one can say that there there is a countervailing presumption, namely: uphold the original presumption that the cow was not yet acquired by the owner of the donkey, only now with her being pulled he acquired her, and therefore one needs to clarify that it is a complete and clear acquisition; and so Tosafot explicitly distinguish in chapter HaSho’el folio 100a s.v. this is the view of Sumchus, see there. The third proof he brought from a slain person — one must say on the contrary: if we say there we uphold him in his presumptive status, then one can say he is still alive, which is the very matter we came to discuss — whether he is alive or dead. Moreover, there too there is a countervailing presumption — the presumption of a married woman, that until now she stands in the presumption of being a married woman, and one does not remove her from that presumption; and this does not apply here regarding mourning. And furthermore one can distinguish in all those cases in other ways, and analyze carefully. And what Bach wrote — that if the decisor ruled that one need not mourn, and then it became known retroactively that he died within thirty days, he need not go back and mourn — he wrote well, and so I wrote at the end of Siftei Kohen se’if katan 1, and his words are: and in my humble opinion it seems that one need not mourn in such a case, see above at the beginning of siman 396, and analyze carefully — end of his words; and this is how it should read, and not as it was printed: "and in my humble opinion it seems that one must mourn," for from the beginning of siman 396 it is implied that after thirty one need not go back and mourn:
