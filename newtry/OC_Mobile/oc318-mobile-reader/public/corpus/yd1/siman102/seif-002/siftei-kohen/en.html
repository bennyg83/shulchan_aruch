There are those who say, etc. I do not know why the Mechaber wrote this law as "there are those who say," for Rashba and the Tur hold thus and none disagree; and so is implied in the Gemara at the beginning of Beitzah, which challenges: if one says an egg laid on Yom Tov is forbidden d'rabbanan — from where? For a baraita teaches and its doubt is forbidden; and if mixed in a thousand all are forbidden — why is its doubt forbidden, for it is a d'rabbanan doubt? And it answers that it deals with a doubtful tereifah egg; and it challenges: if so, say the latter clause "if mixed in a thousand all are forbidden" — if not, let it be a doubt of Yom Tov and a doubt of weekday, which is something that has a permitted aspect, for even in a thousand it is not nullified; if not, let it be a doubtful tereifah, which is something that does not have a permitted aspect and is nullified in its majority, etc. — now what is the challenge? Perhaps that clause dealing with mixing in a thousand refers to a doubtful tereifah egg? Rather certainly in all such cases it is not something that has a permitted aspect — investigate.
