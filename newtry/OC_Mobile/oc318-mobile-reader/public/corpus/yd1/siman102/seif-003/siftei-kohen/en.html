Vessel, etc. "Because one must," etc. Maharshal wrote in Yoreh Deah siman 88 rule 6 that he did not need this reason of spending money, for even without that, since permission does not come automatically through time it is not considered something that has a permitted aspect — end of his words; and I did not grasp his intent, for even if permission comes without time it is considered something that has a permitted aspect, such as vows and the like; and Raah in Beit HaBechirah daf 128b challenges Rashba and says here there is another reason — it has no nullification except on account of something that has a permitted aspect, for wherever the prohibition is recognizable it has no nullification; and since one can remove the prohibition through hagalah it is as if recognizable — and he elaborated there that in these vessels the prohibition must impart taste in the permitted — see there where he elaborated; and Maharil in responsum siman 194 also disagrees with Rashba, for hagalah outlay is a small loss, as we reckon in chapter HaZahav (Bava Metzia daf 53) and in chapter 7 of Nedarim — maaser sheni has something that has a permitted aspect through bringing to Jerusalem, etc. — see there; and one should be concerned for his words where there is no great loss; and it appears one should wait until vessels are not ben yomo, then they are permitted from the Torah; and so at the end of responsa of Ben Lev — however he wrote there that this is only to use them all together, but alone not, since there is only taste it is not something that has a permitted aspect — see there; and in my humble opinion even each alone is forbidden to use until after twelve months, for Rashba and the Tur did not write it is not something that has a permitted aspect only because outlay is required; when there is Torah permission without outlay, all forbid. And Ben Lev ruled a dairy bowl mixed with meat bowls is not nullified in the majority since not in its kind — all forbidden; the Bach seized on this and was stringent to remove one vessel as mere stringency like Maharam with meat pieces — but all poskim agreed in siman 109 that one need not remove even a piece; Maharam did so because he was righteous and feared eating prohibition disgracefully, etc. — not applicable to vessels. Nevertheless, without loss, be stringent like the Bach; see siman 69 seif 10. The Taz also ruled one may kasher all vessels — see there.
