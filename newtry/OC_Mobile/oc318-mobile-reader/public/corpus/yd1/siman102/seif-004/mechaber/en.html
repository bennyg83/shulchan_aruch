There are those who say that something that has a permitted aspect does not apply where the food spoils: {Rama: This that something that has a permitted aspect is not nullified applies only if the prohibition is in existence or there is still substance of the prohibition in the mixture; but its taste is nullified (Tur Orach Chayim siman 513 and Arukh and Hagahot Alfasi end of Avodah Zarah and Mahari in the name of Mahariach); and likewise if its prohibition is not on account of itself it is nullified — therefore a piece that was not salted within three days — even though some say it is called something that has a permitted aspect since it is permitted for roasting — nevertheless it is nullified, for it is forbidden only on account of blood absorbed in it (Taz siman 110); every prohibition that was not recognizable before it was mixed is nullified even though it is something that has a permitted aspect (Arukh general rule 25 in the name of seif 3 and Mordechai chapter 1 of Shabbat); one who vowed from one matter and it was mixed afterward — for him it is called something that has a permitted aspect (there and in Maimonides chapter 8 of Nedarim and in Ran chapter 1 and Or Zarua and this is Gemara Arukh chapter 7 of Nedarim daf 59 and Yerushalmi chapter 6 of Nedarim and consensus of poskim); for it is possible to ask about his vow. Something that has permission and becomes forbidden again, such as chametz on Pesach — is not called something that has a permitted aspect (Mordechai chapter Kamma); and there are those who disagree in this (Rambam chapter 15 of Forbidden Foods); and it is only called something that has a permitted aspect if it was permitted for the one who was forbidden, but if it remained forbidden for one forever even though permitted for others, such as one who cooks on Shabbat — it is not called something that has a permitted aspect (Beit Yosef in the name of R' Yerucham in the name of Ramah).}
