See in responsa Tzemach Tzedek siman 69 on a doubtful tereifah egg laid on Yom Tov that was mixed — the questioner ruled it is not something that has a permitted aspect, since even after Yom Tov there will be no permission without nullification on account of tereifah; and he, may his memory be blessed, disagrees, for nevertheless it will certainly come to permission somewhat, for after Yom Tov the inclusive prohibition of being born will be removed and everything possible to do in permission we do — see there; and so agreed Peri Chadash and Menachem Tziyon general rule 74 sign 12; and see Peri Megadim who wrote similarly regarding one who said "konam on me eating this nevelah" and it was mixed — vow prohibition is in any case something that has a permitted aspect and is not nullified even in sixty; one can say vow prohibition does not apply at all since it is not in a mixture — nevertheless there are two prohibitions, only that there is not one prohibition from two sources, meaning to receive two sets of lashes or two sins; or it is called something that has a permitted aspect — see there; and see in responsa of Rabbeinu Akiva Eiger siman 65 who was asked on this law and initially also wanted to say that nevertheless vow prohibition is something that has a permitted aspect; however not for Peri Megadim's reason but since there is a dispute of the rabbis whether a vow takes effect on something already forbidden (see below siman 276 seif 5) — its doubt is stringent — if so it is something that has a permitted aspect; and he again questioned this, for nevertheless there is room to permit in our case, since that vows are something that has a permitted aspect is explained in the Gemara because there is a mitzvah to ask — if so in our case it is obvious there is no mitzvah, etc. — see the full responsum.
