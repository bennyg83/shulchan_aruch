See in responsa at the end of his Shabbat siddur responsum 3, where he was uncertain about something that has a permitted aspect that was mixed, and afterward one of them explained whether we say "everything that separated," etc. — that it is not relevant to say "until you eat it in prohibition eat it after Yom Tov in permission," for we presume there is no prohibition at all; or we do not say at all regarding something that has a permitted aspect that we follow the majority even in such a case — and he elaborated on this; and in the end he concluded that even in such a case we do not say "everything that separated," etc.; and this is also within the rule of something that has a permitted aspect; and so is implied in responsa of Tzemach Tzedek siman 69 in the middle of the responsum — see there; and so wrote Magen Avraham in siman 513 s.k. 13; and see in Tzelach on Beitzah 4 Yom Tov 2b s.v. "and since he elaborated to bring proof that we say regarding something that has a permitted aspect 'everything that separated returns to the majority'" — see there; and so appears to be the opinion of Chavot Yair in siman 233 — see there.
