That something that has a permitted aspect is not nullified, etc.; but its taste is nullified. And that if one whitened the food or filled it with chicken it is not nullified (in the first seif) — because the substance of the prohibition is considered present and it is not considered dissolved and not in existence except when not recognizable in appearance but only in taste — end of Torat Chatat there rule 2 in the name of Maharai; however it requires investigation also in this, for in my humble opinion from the Gemara Nedarim (daf 98) and Yerushalmi chapter 6 of Nedarim and Tosafot and Rosh and Ran in Nedarim there — something that has a permitted aspect, even its taste is not nullified in its kind — see there; and so is implied from what Maharam and Rosh wrote in responsum beginning general rule 2 on beer of new grain — the grain is nullified in sixty in water, and what we say that something that has a permitted aspect is not nullified refers to in its kind, etc.; and so is implied from Rambam, Semag, and Mechaber below siman 116 seif 10 regarding wine forbidden by vow mixed in other wine — even a drop in a barrel forbids all because one can ask about his vow; also what Torat Chatat wrote there that so is Taz Orach Chayim siman 513 in the name of Rabbeinu Tam is not necessary, for in my humble opinion the Tur there only said that in not its kind we do not say something that has a permitted aspect is not nullified, as implied in Beit Yosef and Bach there — see there; and this is what the Tur wrote here in the name of Rabbeinu Tam: that something that has a permitted aspect is not nullified specifically when mixed in its kind, but not in its kind taste is nullified in sixty — end of his words; thus in its kind even taste is not nullified; also what the Rav wrote in Torat Chatat that so is Orach Chayim general rule 25 — it is not so, for he ruled there in rule 4 like Rambam regarding wine above; and likewise regarding vessels absorbing prohibition he wrote there rule 19 like Rashba and Mechaber seif 3; and what he wrote there in rule 17 that where the substance of the prohibition is not in existence and there is only taste it is nullified — refers specifically to not its kind: "and that something that has a permitted aspect is not nullified refers specifically to in its kind; but in not its kind it is properly nullified in sixty even if one does not recognize it and the substance of the prohibition remains inside, etc.; and all the more so where the substance of the prohibition is not in existence and there is only taste it is properly nullified in sixty" — and so we learned in Yerushalmi that the Sages gave a measure for something that has a permitted aspect only in its kind in any amount and not in its kind in sixty — end of his words; thus it deals specifically with not its kind; and at first glance one could reconcile his words in the hagahah to say wherever the substance of the prohibition is still in the mixture, even if not in existence, such as wine in wine, etc., it is not nullified; but where a piece of prohibition fell and they threw it out whole — we hold one must nullify its emission as above siman 98 seif 8 on account of taste exiting from it — in such a case it is nullified; and now we can reconcile some proofs above dealing where the body of the item was mixed there and therefore not nullified; but from his words in Torat Chatat there it appears there is no distinction, for he brought so from Orach Chayim siman 513 dealing explicitly where the body of the item was mixed — see there; and so is implied from Maharai's words he brought there that I brought at the beginning of s.k. 7 — investigate; and he also brought this rule from Hagahot Alfasi chapter 2 of Avodah Zarah in the name of Mahariach — and there it states explicitly specifically where there is substance of the prohibition, but where there is no substance that dissolved it is nullified in sixty — end of his words; and so is implied in Darkei Moshe; and in truth for practical law it appears even if removed whole its taste is not nullified, as implied from all those proofs above; and so is implied from Rabeinu Yerucham part 4 that one who vowed from meat and it was cooked with meat — eggs are forbidden in sixty, for although it is something that has a permitted aspect, since it is not in its kind — end of his words; and otherwise it is strained to distinguish whether taste remains there or not — until you eat it in prohibition you eat it in permission. And even if there is doubt whether it was laid on Yom Tov, etc. All that the Bach wrote here is not necessary at all — see there; also what he relied on regarding something that has a permitted aspect in a doubtful tereifah egg that was mixed is not correct, for it should be said this case is different since there is permission for its prohibition — and his words would make Semag and Semak contradict one another; rather as explained, and so is the opinion of the Rav in Taz general rule 43 to distinguish in this; and so I found explicitly in Mordechai chapter Kol HaTzalmim; also what he brought from Ran is nothing, for he only wrote there that a doubt regarding prepared on the second day of Yom Tov is permitted — and the Mechaber himself wrote so in Orach Chayim siman 497 seif 4; not so here, as below siman 110 s.k. 55; also what he wrote that in two mixtures one may permit — it should be said for all it is not so, as written in siman 110 seif 6 — see there.
And if it was mixed not in its kind, etc. It appears that here we follow the name and not the taste — if it is equal in name, even though not equal in taste, it is its kind and is not nullified; and if it is not equal in name, even though equal in taste, it is not its kind and is nullified — for we hold like Rava at the end of Avodah Zarah (daf 66) that we follow the name and not the taste; and so is implied explicitly from what Tosafot Pesachim (daf 97a) and the Rosh end of Avodah Zarah and other poskim wrote — that for us, who hold all prohibitions whether in its kind or not require sixty, what Rava said that in its kind we follow the name means regarding yayin nesech and tevel and tevel that is not nullified because it is something that has a permitted aspect, as in chapter 7 of Nedarim (beginning daf 58) and in Rambam chapter 15 of Forbidden Foods and Rashba in Torat HaBayit and in all other poskim; and so is implied in Ran end of Avodah Zarah that regarding something that has a permitted aspect it is not nullified in its kind — we follow the name — see there and investigate; and so Maharshal wrote explicitly in Orach Chayim siman 84 and Yoreh Deah siman 88.
Not in its kind it is nullified in sixty. Even though regarding beriah and a piece worthy of honor there is no distinction whether mixed in its kind or not, as above siman 101 seif 6 in the hagahah — this case is different, for the importance is not on account of the prohibition itself but because there will later be permission — the Sages did not want it nullified; and everything mixed not in its kind — the permission is not called on the forbidden item but on the name of what it was mixed into, and it is as if it has no permission. So too Torat Chatat general rule 40 rule 6.
However, if one whitened it, etc. Since it is for appearance it is not nullified, as in Orach Chayim siman 513 seif 3 — see there.
Or one placed it in a pot to fix, etc. The reason Maharai wrote there: everything placed one in another to fix is called its kind, like water and salt in dough — considered its kind since they fix the dough — as Tosafot chapter 2 of Beitzah (beginning daf 39) wrote.
