Wine that is forbidden only for drinking forbids only with 60. If stam yeinam became mixed with permitted wine, behold it forbids with any amount for drinking, and one should sell it all to a non-Jew, and take the value of the forbidden wine that is in it and cast it into the Dead Sea, and he may benefit from the rest of the money. And likewise, if a barrel of yayin nesech became mixed among barrels, all are forbidden for drinking and permitted in benefit, and he should take the value of that barrel to the Dead Sea when he sells everything to a non-Jew; and likewise with a barrel of stam yeinam. {Rama: That a barrel of yayin nesech forbids all the barrels with which it became mixed is specifically regarding large barrels, which are important and are not batel; but if they are small and are not important, 1 in 2 is batel, like other prohibitions (Tur, netiv 17). And even with large ones, if 1 of the mixture fell into the sea or was burned, all the others are permitted, like other prohibitions. And some say that all stam yeinam in our times is batel in 60, and so is the practice to be lenient. And in a place where it became mixed through nitzok chibbur, as above siman 126, the upper wine does not help the lower wine to be mevatel the prohibition (Beit Yosef).}
