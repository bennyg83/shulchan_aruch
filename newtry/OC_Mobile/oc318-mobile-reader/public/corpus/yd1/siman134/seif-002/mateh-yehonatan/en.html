(There seif 3) Water that became mixed with wine or wine with water in noten ta'am, etc. See Shach s"k 16, what he challenged against Rashi's approach — why not say chatichah na'aseh neveilah, etc. — see there. And behold all his difficulty is according to the Rosh's approach that he brought above seif 1, that in one vessel even min b'mino it is permitted; and necessarily here it deals with one after another, unlike Tosafot who hold min b'mino — permitted fell into forbidden, even 60 at once they are not batel — to my mind one can say that here it deals with water falling at once in a manner where chatichah na'aseh neveilah does not apply; but according to the Rosh, that if it fell at once it is permitted even min b'mino, we say chatichah na'aseh neveilah. And one can say that chatichah na'aseh neveilah is from two reasons: one — since it was absorbed in the piece, the prohibition can no longer be squeezed out, and even if afterwards there is 60 against the prohibition, the prohibition never emerges from it and 60 is needed against all the prohibition; and one — even though we say it is possible to squeeze it, nevertheless since it fell once, prohibition was called upon the piece and it no longer returns to permitted. And Rambam wrote perek 9 of Laws of Forbidden Foods: if a drop of milk fell in a pot and it is not known on which piece it fell, he shakes the pot so all the taste mixes in all of it; and they challenged him — what does it help if he shakes, since prohibition fell on the piece, this piece is neveilah and 60 is needed against the whole piece; and now that it is not known which it is, 60 is needed against the largest piece in the pot; and they resolve: since it is not known which piece it fell on, we do not say chatichah na'aseh neveilah — we do not say chatichah na'aseh neveilah except where the piece was clarified and prohibition was called upon it; but where it was not known and the piece was not identified, we do not say chatichah na'aseh neveilah; and accordingly here chatichah na'aseh neveilah does not apply at all from the reason that it cannot be squeezed — granted regarding a prohibition absorbed in a piece through cooking and the like, there it is fit to say it cannot be squeezed; but where the matter was absorbed and mixed, it is certainly possible to squeeze it; and if you say the reason is because it was already called prohibition upon it and no longer returns to permitted — this too does not apply here, for the piece was not identified when the prohibitions were absorbed, and it is similar to a pot into which a drop of milk fell, where Rambam wrote chatichah na'aseh neveilah does not apply — and study it.
(There seif 3) And if the forbidden beverage increased, etc. His words here contradict one another — see Taz and Shach s"k 14 who want to resolve Rama's difficulty, and their words are forced; and it seems to me: behold the measure of nullification of wine in water is one in six as the Mechaber wrote below seif 5; and even though sometimes there is no taste of wine at all even in less than six, nevertheless the rabbis did not distinguish — each time six parts water are needed even if there is no taste of wine at all. And behold that which the poskim dispute regarding whether it returns and revives or not is regarding min b'mino where there is no taste at all; unlike min b'she'eino mino where there is natan ta'am — certainly we say it returns and revives, for taste is like the essence from the Torah; accordingly certainly the Mechaber holds that where there is natan ta'am we say it returns and revives, as the Mechaber wrote above siman 99; and here he explains so: but if the forbidden beverage fell into the permitted beverage, the first is batel — meaning even if it increased until there are not four parts water; and it deals where there is no natan ta'am, and it will not be forbidden except due to stam yeinam — nevertheless six parts are needed; and in such a case it is permitted since there is no natan ta'am — in such a case we do not say it returns and revives, and it is similar to min b'mino where we do not say it returns and revives; but if it increased so much that there is natan ta'am in it, we properly say it returns and revives — and this is what the Mechaber wrote: if it increased until there is enough to impart taste, it is forbidden — and study it, for the matters are clear.
