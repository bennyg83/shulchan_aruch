For drinking, and he shall sell, etc. Meaning: but in benefit there is a remedy — that he shall sell, etc.; but certainly anything where he did not send benefit to the Dead Sea is forbidden in all benefit.
And he shall sell all of it, etc. And accordingly, where there is no substance of prohibition — such as when he placed wine in gentile vessels — all is permitted in benefit, for there are no money of prohibition here. Tur; and see above siman 137.
And he takes his money, etc. The same applies when he sells to a gentile except for the money of the prohibition in it — it is permitted, the same as sending benefit to the Dead Sea, as stated in seif 10 and in the poskim; and so Beit Yosef and later authorities ruled; and Tur wrote in the name of Rashba that even if he sells anonymously it is permitted, and he need not say to the gentile at the time of sale: “I sell it to you except for the money of the libated wine.”
To the Dead Sea. Or he shall burn it or bury it — as above siman 133 seif 1; and likewise Agudah in the last chapter of Avodah Zarah; and it is simple; and it seems likewise any place it is completely lost, such as a deep river and the like — as above siman 110 seif 42.
A barrel of yayin nesech. Certainly so; but if the body of stam yeinam itself mixed in wine — neither sending benefit to the Dead Sea nor selling to a gentile except for the money of the prohibition in it helps — as stated in Tur and poskim; and so Beit Yosef and later authorities ruled.
When he sells all to a gentile. But when he sells to a gentile except for the money of one barrel — it is permitted and he need not again send to the Dead Sea; and as was explained; and see above seif 8.
In large barrels, etc. See above siman 110 seif 5.
If he fell, etc. See above siman 110 seif 7 and what is written there.
Null in 60. And one need not send benefit to the Dead Sea or sell to a gentile except for the money of the prohibition in it — Bach. And I hesitate on this: since where there is no loss we hold there is no distinction between our time and Gemara time, as above siman 123, 124, 128 and many places; and if so here — by law stam yeinam is not null in 60; so in our time, that it is null and permitted even for drinking, that is because of loss; but how can he benefit l'chatchilah from the yayin nesech that fell there? Rather he should send the benefit to the Dead Sea, and then it is permitted for drinking — so it seems to me.
