And casts it away. Or he may burn it and bury it. It appears that the same applies to any place where it will be destroyed, such as a deep river and the like, or he may sell it to a non-Jew excluding the value of the prohibited portion. Even if he sells it plainly it is permitted, and he need not say to the non-Jew at the time of sale, “I sell it to you excluding the value of the yayin nesech in it.” But as long as he has not taken the benefit to the Dead Sea, all of it is forbidden for benefit. However, where the prohibited substance has no physical presence, such as where one placed wine into non-Jews' jugs, everything is permitted for benefit, for there is no value of the prohibited matter here. End quote from Shach. See above siman 137.
The value. When he sells to a non-Jew excluding the value of one barrel, it is permitted and he no longer needs to take anything to the Dead Sea. Shach.
In sixty. He need not take the benefit to the Dead Sea or sell it to a non-Jew excluding the value of the prohibited part. So wrote the Bach. But the Shach hesitates and tends to stringency: the reason it is nullified nowadays and permitted even for drinking is because of loss, but how may he lechatchilah benefit from the yayin nesech that fell there? Rather, he takes the benefit, etc., and it is permitted for drinking. End quote.
