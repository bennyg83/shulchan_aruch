Wine, etc. This follows the statement there on 74b: This is understandable regarding yayin nesech because, etc. In the present case, however, there is no connection to idolatry. See Tosafot 29b, s.v. 'Wine,' etc., and the Ran and Rivash.
Ordinary gentile wine became mixed, etc. This is the view of Rashba, Ran, and the other authorities, unlike Rabbeinu Tam. See the Be'er HaGolah and the authorities' proofs. See Tosafot there on 74a, s.v. 'Wine with wine,' the Rosh there, Ramban, Ran, and Ritva; Tosafot 71b, s.v. 'The case is,' etc.; and Tosafot 56b, at the end of s.v. 'But,' etc. This is the meaning of the gloss, 'Some say,' etc., and the accepted practice is to rule leniently. Since the Rama states this without qualification, it accords with the Bach rather than the Shach in seif katan 15, because Rabbeinu Tam's view is also combined.
(Likut) Ordinary gentile wine became mixed, etc.; when he sells, etc.; likewise, etc. This too is entirely Rambam's language, following the Rif. Rambam explains that Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel's ruling and Rabbi Eliezer's statement, 'He casts the value of the benefit,' etc., are one ruling, one master stating the beginning and the other the conclusion. Kesef Mishneh and Beit Yosef explain this, and Rambam writes likewise in his Commentary to the Mishnah at the end of chapter 3: The halachah follows Rabbi Eliezer in every case, even a barrel, etc., and one may sell it, etc. He likewise writes in chapter 5 regarding the Mishnah, 'These are forbidden,' etc.: He sells everything to non-Jews, etc. This is the meaning of 'he casts the benefit,' etc. Tosafot Yom Tov writes likewise. (End of Likut.)
And he takes, etc. There we say: Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel says, etc., except for, etc. The Tur writes likewise: Or he sells the entire mixture to a non-Jew and takes, etc. This accords with the discussion there on 49b. See Torat HaBayit, which cites several approaches.
(Likut) And he takes, etc.; likewise, etc. This follows the Rif's view that redemption of yayin nesech is equivalent to selling it. See what I wrote in the Likutim to siman 142, seif 4, seif katan 8. (End of Likut.)
Likewise, if, etc. Meaning as Rav Nachman said there: The practical halachah is, etc.
That a barrel, etc. See Rashi 74a, s.v. 'Yayin nesech,' and s.v. 'And idolatry.' Tosafot there, s.v. 'These,' writes likewise, as implied by the Gemara, which explains that the Mishnah discusses an item counted individually. See Tosafot Yevamot 81b, s.v. 'Rabbi Yochanan.' Ran, however, writes that anything connected to idolatry is never nullified, even if not counted individually, as stated in the Yerushalmi: yayin nesech, idolatry, and hides with a heart-shaped tear, because the verse says, 'Nothing from the banned property shall adhere to your hand.' See Tosafot Zevachim 72a, s.v. 'Rather,' etc. Ran further brings proof from the Mishnah there, 'Yayin nesech that fell into a pit,' etc., showing that it forbids even when no longer distinct, and we do not rule like Rav Dimi and Rabbi Yitzchak. According to the Tur and Shulchan Aruch, however, who rule like Rabbi Yitzchak, that Yerushalmi rationale must have been rejected, and the Yerushalmi disagrees with Rabbi Dimi and Rabbi Yitzchak.
(Likut) That a barrel, etc. Tosafot Zevachim 72a, s.v. 'Rather,' and the Rash in Orlah, chapter 3, were uncertain about this. In Avodah Zarah 74a, s.v. 'This,' however, Tosafot adopts the view stated here, writing: If you ask, what of yayin nesech, etc.; one must answer, etc. This is also implied by the Gemara, which says that it listed only an item, etc., although the inference can be rejected. Rambam follows Ran's reasoning and the Yerushalmi, and therefore did not list yayin nesech, idolatry, and hides with a heart-shaped tear among the first three categories in Hilchot Maachalot Assurot, chapter 16, as explained above at the beginning of siman 110 in the Likut. (End of Likut.)
Even with large barrels, etc. Torat HaBayit and Ran, as stated regarding a ring of idolatry in Zevachim 74a.
