Water that became mixed, etc. The Rama asks that the Mechaber's rulings contradict one another. In my humble opinion, the Shulchan Aruch is correct, and even had these words not been written they would deserve to be written. The principal difficulty is that the Mechaber first makes the law depend upon whether prohibited taste is imparted, and then says, 'But if the prohibited substance fell,' implying a leniency even where taste is imparted. Moreover, he writes that when prohibition falls into permission, each first increment is nullified, which appears to apply even if the cumulative amount eventually exceeds the threshold of imparting taste. Otherwise, why would he limit the rule to pouring from a small pitcher? A large stream would be forbidden even below the taste threshold, whereas with other prohibitions an unlike-kind mixture is forbidden only when taste is imparted. Rabbeinu Tam's statement that when prohibition falls into permission we do not say each first increment is nullified if the stream is large, even when the permitted liquid could nullify that stream, concerns yayin nesech falling into wine, a same-kind mixture that forbids in any amount; it is no proof for an unlike-kind mixture. The first-increment rule here must therefore allow even a cumulative amount capable of imparting taste, which seems to contradict the later clause. The correct explanation is that the first seif is Rambam's language in chapter 16 of Hilchos Maachalos Assuros, and the present seif through the words 'little by little' is likewise Rambam's language; the Shulchan Aruch added the clause, 'and if it increased,' precisely to resolve Rambam's wording. In a same-kind mixture, when prohibition falls gradually into permission, the first increments are nullified even if a very large total amount is eventually poured, because no new taste is created and the prohibition rests on the special stringency of yayin nesech. In an unlike-kind mixture, however, once the cumulative prohibition reaches the point of imparting taste, the permitted food is certainly forbidden even though the prohibition entered little by little, as Tosafos explicitly demonstrate. Thus a same-kind mixture has one stringency and one leniency relative to an unlike-kind mixture. Where permission falls into same-kind prohibition, even a minute amount of prohibition prevents nullification. Where same-kind prohibition falls gradually into permission from a small stream, each increment is nullified even if much is ultimately poured. In an unlike-kind mixture, ordinary law follows taste. Yet with idolatrous wine there is an additional stringency: when the stream of prohibition entering permission is large, even an unlike-kind mixture may be forbidden below the ordinary taste threshold, because the large continuous stream prevents the incremental nullification that would otherwise occur. The sequence of the Shulchan Aruch is therefore exact. The phrase 'the first is nullified' teaches that a small stream is treated incrementally; but if the prohibition eventually accumulates until it imparts taste, an unlike-kind mixture is forbidden, unlike the same-kind case above. With a large stream, even less than the taste threshold may be forbidden because of the special stringency of idolatrous wine. Thus the practical conclusion agrees with the Rama, but the Mechaber additionally teaches this special stringency of a large stream in an unlike-kind mixture. Concerning the Shulchan Aruch I apply the verse: 'The secret of Hashem is for those who fear Him.'
