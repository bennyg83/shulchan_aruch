Taste. “And if the water fell into the wine of the water, it was a bit short that the water had been destroyed until there was no wine, it is permissible for the mother to say that he was hard, and that he would not say to him, and that he would not say anything to him, and that he was not in the water.”
Contradict. The Taz seeks to resolve the Mechaber's words and at length explains them, but in Nekudot HaKesef he challenges him and inclines to what he wrote in the Shach, that it appears an error fell into the books and the text should read: "and some say, if the liquid increased," etc. This view is primary, for taste is never nullified, even with the same species, and even with other prohibitions, whenever there is enough to impart taste against it in a different species, it is not nullified and it reawakens. Thus the Shach. To me it appears that the Mechaber's words, "and if the liquid increased," mean that he did not pour from a small jug, but from barrels where the stream descending from the mouth of the barrel is large, as in seif 1; so too the Bach writes. See the Taz.
