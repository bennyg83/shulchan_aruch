Taste. If water falls into wine, even gradually, once the water becomes so abundant that the wine no longer imparts taste, it is permitted. Shach questions why the rule of chatichah naasit neveilah does not apply. According to those who do not apply that principle to cold mixtures, there is no difficulty. According to the Rosh, who applies it even cold whenever the prohibition is not one that forbids in any amount, the answer is that the Rosh applies chatichah naasit neveilah only to meat and milk.
Contradict one another. Taz labors at length to reconcile the Mechaber. Nekudot HaKesef disagrees and follows Shach, who believes a textual error occurred and the text should read, 'Some say that if the permitted liquid increased,' and so forth. This view is primary: taste is never nullified, even within the same species. Whenever the amount could impart taste into a different species, the prohibition revives. Another explanation is that the Mechaber refers not to a small pitcher but to barrels whose descending stream is large, as in seif 1.
