However it is difficult — why do we not say chatichah na'aseh neveilah? And for Ran there it is satisfactory, for he wrote: and the reason is that we do not say chatichah na'aseh neveilah except in things that cook, like meat in milk, for the Torah forbade through cooking; or cold into hot, which is also a way of cooking; but in another manner, no; and here, since chatichah na'aseh neveilah in other prohibitions is rabbinic merely due to taste of meat in milk, therefore we do not say it except similar to meat in milk — end quote; and likewise for poskim who do not say chatichah na'aseh neveilah in wet into wet (and the practice is so in a place of great loss, as the Rav wrote in siman 92 seif 4) it is satisfactory; but for the Rosh who wrote that that which we say (in seif 4) when a pitcher of water fell at the end it is permitted and we do not say chatichah na'aseh neveilah — this is because forbidden wine did not fall to the measure of natan ta'am in the permitted but was forbidden in any amount (and it deals with falling from the vat to the pit, for otherwise the Rosh there holds the first is batel since there is no natan ta'am — and examine) (and likewise Or Sameach wrote general rule 24, law 3, that something forbidden in any amount — we do not say chatichah na'aseh neveilah when there is no 60 — see there) (and one can say: since at first it was not to the measure of natan ta'am, such as a piece and the like that was forbidden in any amount due to chametz on Pesach or shiluach haken and afterwards fell into the pot — even though if the prohibition had fallen at first into the pot it would have forbidden in any amount, nevertheless now it is permitted since there is no body of prohibition in any amount b'ein; and likewise it is explicit in Shulchan Aruch and Tosafot and Rosh perek 3 in the name of Rabbeinu Tam, unlike what appears from Derishah siman 92 who forbids afterwards also in any amount as if the prohibition itself fell, which forbids in any amount there, and we do not say it did not become neveilah in any amount except regarding whether the prohibition in any amount would not forbid in mixtures of 2 even if it fell there b'ein — but it is not so, rather as explained) — and anything forbidden in any amount we do not say chatichah na'aseh neveilah — until here his language; evidently we say chatichah na'aseh neveilah even when cold, whatever is not forbidden in any amount; and here in water in wine it is forbidden to the measure of natan ta'am; and it appears the Rosh follows his reasoning that he holds we do not say chatichah na'aseh neveilah only in meat in milk, as above siman 92; and that he needed here to resolve that it was forbidden in any amount is as Perishah wrote siman 11, that regarding yayin nesech which is stringent and forbids in any amount — if not for this reason that since it was forbidden in any amount we do not say chatichah na'aseh neveilah, it would be chatichah na'aseh neveilah; but water in wine does not forbid except to the measure of natan ta'am for prohibition — there is no chatichah na'aseh neveilah, for it is similar to other prohibitions; or also for clarity of the matter the Rosh wrote so to resolve even for one who learns chatichah na'aseh neveilah in all prohibitions.
דבריו בכאן כו'. והאחרונים כתבו ליישב דברי המחבר ולפי שדבריהם דחוקים לא הבאתי דבריהם ונ"ל לתרץ דמה שכתב ואם נתרבה המשקה האסור כו' היינו שנתרבה אח"כ כ"כ בפעם אחת עד שיש בו לבדו כדי ליתן טעם בהיתר אוסר ואשמעינן דל"ת דאפ"ה תשתרי דנימא דגם האיסור שנפל בתחלה יצטרף עם ההיתר וכסברת הראב"ד שהביאו הרשב"א והר"ן קמ"ל ויותר נראה דטעות נפל בספרים וצ"ל וי"א אם נתרבה המשקה האסור כו':
סברא האחרונה היא עיקר. כי כן הסכימו התוס' והרא"ש וטור והרמב"ן והרשב"א והר"ן דלעולם טעמא לא בטיל ואפילו במין במינו ואפי' בשאר איסורים כל שיש בו בכדי שיתן טעם בכנגדו בשאינו מינו לא בטיל וחוזר וניעור ובעט"ז כ' ויש להחמיר ולא נהירא דלא מטעם חומרא קי"ל הכי אלא דכך עיקר לענין הדין כמבואר בדברי הפוסקים וכן לעיל סוף מן צ"ט קיי"ל בפשיטות (וכן כתב העט"ז גופיה שם) דאיסור שנתבשל ואחר כך חזר ונפל עד שכשנצטרף עם השני אין ס' חוזר וניעור אפי' מין במינו ומבואר בדברי כל הפוסקים דיי"נ שלא במינו שוה לשאר איסורים עיין שם: **** ENGLISH **** The latter reasoning is primary. For so agreed Tosafot and Rosh and Tur and Ramban and Rashba and Ran — that taste is never batel, even min b'mino, and even in other prohibitions — whatever has enough to impart taste in its counterpart when not its kind is not batel and returns and revives; and in Eliyahu Rabba he wrote one should be stringent, and it is not clear that we practice so not due to stringency of yayin nesech but because such is primary regarding the law, as explained in the poskim's words; and likewise above end of siman 99 we practice plainly (and likewise Eliyahu Rabba himself wrote there) that a prohibition that was cooked and afterwards fell again until when combined with the second there is no 60, it returns and revives even min b'mino; and it is explained in all the poskim's words that yayin nesech when not its kind is equal to other prohibitions — see there.
