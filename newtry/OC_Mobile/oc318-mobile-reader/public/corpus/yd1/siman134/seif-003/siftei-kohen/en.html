His words here, etc. And later authorities wrote to reconcile the Mechaber's words; but since their explanations are strained I did not bring their words; and it seems to me to resolve: that which he wrote "and if the forbidden liquid increased" — means that afterwards it increased at once so much that it alone could impart taste in the permitted, it forbids; and he informs us that one should not think that even so it is permitted because we say the forbidden that fell first is combined with the permitted, like Raavad whom Rashba and Ran brought — it informs us otherwise; and more likely a scribal error entered the books and it should say "and some say: if the forbidden liquid increased," etc.
The latter view is primary. For so Tosafot, Rosh, Tur, Ramban, Rashba, and Ran agreed: taste is never nullified, even with its own kind, and even with other prohibitions; whenever it contains enough to give taste against it in a different kind, it is not nullified and it reawakens. Ateret Zekenim wrote, "one should be stringent," but this is not correct, for we do not hold this as a stringency; rather, this is the primary law, as is clear in the words of the poskim. So too above at the end of siman 99 we hold simply, and Ateret Zekenim himself wrote there, that a prohibition that was cooked and then fell again, so that when combined with the second there is not sixty, reawakens, even with its own kind. It is clear in the words of all the poskim that yayin nesech not with its own kind is equal to other prohibitions; see there.
