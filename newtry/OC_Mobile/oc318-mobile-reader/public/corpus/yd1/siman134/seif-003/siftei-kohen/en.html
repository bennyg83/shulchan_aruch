In natan ta'am, etc. And if the water fell into the wine, even little by little — since the water increased until the wine does not impart taste in them, it is permitted — end quote Tur; and it seems to me that this is proven from Tosafot and Rosh and Ramban that Ran brought (that in wine in water we always follow natan ta'am and do not say the first is batel in any case), according to what the Gemara establishes for Rav Dimi — the whole mishnah deals with permitted falling into forbidden (and Rosh ruled like Rav Dimi), unlike Beit Chadash seif 5 who was forced to say that ultimately this establishment does not exist. However it is difficult — why do we not say chatichah na'aseh neveilah? And for Ran there it is satisfactory, for he wrote: and the reason is that we do not say chatichah na'aseh neveilah except in things that cook, like meat in milk, for the Torah forbade through cooking; or cold into hot, which is also a way of cooking; but in another manner, no; and here, since chatichah na'aseh neveilah in other prohibitions is rabbinic merely due to taste of meat in milk, therefore we do not say it except similar to meat in milk — end quote; and likewise for poskim who do not say chatichah na'aseh neveilah in wet into wet (and the practice is so in a place of great loss, as the Rav wrote in siman 92 seif 4) it is satisfactory; but for the Rosh who wrote that that which we say (in seif 4) when a pitcher of water fell at the end it is permitted and we do not say chatichah na'aseh neveilah — this is because forbidden wine did not fall to the measure of natan ta'am in the permitted but was forbidden in any amount (and it deals with falling from the vat to the pit, for otherwise the Rosh there holds the first is batel since there is no natan ta'am — and examine) (and likewise Or Sameach wrote general rule 24, law 3, that something forbidden in any amount — we do not say chatichah na'aseh neveilah when there is no 60 — see there) (and one can say: since at first it was not to the measure of natan ta'am, such as a piece and the like that was forbidden in any amount due to chametz on Pesach or shiluach haken and afterwards fell into the pot — even though if the prohibition had fallen at first into the pot it would have forbidden in any amount, nevertheless now it is permitted since there is no body of prohibition in any amount b'ein; and likewise it is explicit in Shulchan Aruch and Tosafot and Rosh perek 3 in the name of Rabbeinu Tam, unlike what appears from Derishah siman 92 who forbids afterwards also in any amount as if the prohibition itself fell, which forbids in any amount there, and we do not say it did not become neveilah in any amount except regarding whether the prohibition in any amount would not forbid in mixtures of 2 even if it fell there b'ein — but it is not so, rather as explained) — and anything forbidden in any amount we do not say chatichah na'aseh neveilah — until here his language; evidently we say chatichah na'aseh neveilah even when cold, whatever is not forbidden in any amount; and here in water in wine it is forbidden to the measure of natan ta'am; and it appears the Rosh follows his reasoning that he holds we do not say chatichah na'aseh neveilah only in meat in milk, as above siman 92; and that he needed here to resolve that it was forbidden in any amount is as Perishah wrote siman 11, that regarding yayin nesech which is stringent and forbids in any amount — if not for this reason that since it was forbidden in any amount we do not say chatichah na'aseh neveilah, it would be chatichah na'aseh neveilah; but water in wine does not forbid except to the measure of natan ta'am for prohibition — there is no chatichah na'aseh neveilah, for it is similar to other prohibitions; or also for clarity of the matter the Rosh wrote so to resolve even for one who learns chatichah na'aseh neveilah in all prohibitions.
