In accordance with Tosafot there, even where one kind was mixed with a different kind; the Rosh there in his rulings; and Ramban and Rashba. It appears to me that all the more so, in seif 1, concerning yayin nesech poured from a small container into a wine pit, if the forbidden wine ultimately increases enough to impart flavor, it forbids the mixture. Rashba in the name of Razah, and Ran in the name of Raavad, write that where yayin nesech falls into wine we do not say that each first amount is successively nullified; rather, the mixture becomes forbidden again when the forbidden quantity increases.
Regarding wine falling into water, however, they write that each first amount is nullified, becomes entirely permitted, and not only no longer joins to forbid but even joins the permitted liquid. Beit Yosef rules like those who disagree with them regarding wine in water, and all the more so regarding wine in wine. Meaning: at the beginning of his words the Rambam writes, "When does this apply? When it fell," and so forth, which plainly implies that if it fell from a small container, it does not forbid when it merely imparts flavor. Rambam thus follows Raavad and Razah, as I wrote above. One should not infer from the Mechaber's words in seif 1, "even throughout the entire day," that the mixture can never later become forbidden. That phrase is taken from Rav Yitzchak's statement in the Gemara, and Tosafot explains that it means only that so much forbidden wine did not fall that the permitted wine no longer contained sixty times its quantity.
