According to Tosafot there — even min b'she'eino mino — and the Rosh there in his rulings, and Ramban and Rashba; and it seems to me that all the more so in seif 1 regarding yayin nesech — a small tzinzror into the pit: if it increased to the measure of imparting taste it forbids, for Rashba wrote in the name of Razah and Ran in the name of Ra'avad that in yayin nesech in wine we do not say kama kama batel but it returns and is forbidden when it increased; and in wine in water likewise they wrote that the first is batel and is nullified and behold it is complete heter, and it does not suffice that it no longer combines to forbid but even combines to permit, etc.; and Rav Beit Yosef rules like the disputants against them in wine in water, and all the more so in wine in wine [meaning: that at the beginning of his words he wrote "in what case" that it fell, etc., which implies explicitly that if a tzinzror fell it does not forbid with natan ta'am, and this is Rambam's language, and he holds like Ra'avad and Razah as I wrote above; but one should not infer from the Mechaber's words that he wrote in seif 1 "even all day" to say it does not return and forbid — this language is Rabbi Yitzchak's language, etc. in the Gemara, and Tosafot there explained it means it should not fall so much that there would not be 60 of heter].
