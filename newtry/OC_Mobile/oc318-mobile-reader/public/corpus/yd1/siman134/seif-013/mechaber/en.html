All beverages of a non-Jew, whose way is to mix into them wine or vinegar, are forbidden. {Rama: And see above siman 114. And all this is regarding beverages in which the wine and vinegar improve them, or in which it does not spoil; but if it spoils — such as dye to which they put vinegar, or salt that non-Jews cook and put wine or blood into it to whiten it (Mordechai, perek Ein Ma’amidin), or ink of a non-Jew (Rabbeinu Tam) — it is permitted, for all this is a spoiled taste, and the same applies to anything similar. And some are stringent regarding the ink of a non-Jew, because sometimes he puts his quill into his mouth and the ink is upon it (Terumat HaDeshen siman 129; Kol Bo in the name of R' Peretz). And see above siman 123, that the practice is to permit something into which they put vinegar, since it is a spoiled taste; and it appears that the same applies to ink — even if there was certainly yayin nesech in it, it is only a spoiled taste and it is permitted; so it appears to me.}
