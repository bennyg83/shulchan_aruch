When the permitted wine is poured, etc. Even if a large quantity of permitted wine fell at one time upon a single drop of yayin nesech, so that there is sixty times as much permitted wine, it is nevertheless forbidden. So wrote the Ran: the reason is that the substance standing in its place is regarded as more significant than that which comes upon it. Therefore, when the prohibited substance remains in its place, even the smallest amount forbids the wine that comes upon it, even if an entire large barrel is poured upon it at once, because of the special stringency of yayin nesech. But when the permitted substance remains in its place and the prohibition comes upon it, the permitted substance nullifies the prohibition unless an amount sufficient to impart taste falls at once. This also appears from the Rambam. End of the Ran's words. This is the primary interpretation of the Gemara. Moreover, many poskim hold that even when prohibition falls into permitted wine, yayin nesech forbids in the smallest amount. Nevertheless, in a case of great loss and the like, one may rely on the Ramban, the Rosh, and the Tur, who hold that even when permitted wine is poured into prohibited wine, if it falls all at once and there is sixty times as much, it is permitted. Only when it falls little by little do we say that each first quantity is successively nullified, and even if more permitted wine is later added until there is sixty times as much, the first prohibition does not reawaken. Although with other prohibitions, and likewise wine mixed with water, the prohibition would reawaken, as explained below in seif katan 12, regarding yayin nesech mixed with its own type the Sages were stringent. See there. Olat Tamid confused the issues, beginning with the words of the Mechaber, which are the Rambam's view, and concluding with the words of the Tur. All this, however, concerns definite non-Jewish wine. Regarding ordinary non-Jewish wine nowadays, we rule that in every case it is nullified in sixty, as will be explained.
From a small flask. Rashi and Ran explain: a small flask.
The first is null. Ran wrote: even though we hold stream is connection — stream is only connection to say that the wine above has the same status as the wine that mixed below, and behold it is like it; and since that which falls below is nullified and becomes permitted, how can the wine above be forbidden? And unlike Raavad who explains it deals with dripping — end; and Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe bring it briefly; and likewise Rosh wrote in the last chapter of Avodah Zarah: even though stream is connection, here it is not connection — since even if it dripped all day in this manner and all mixed, the first would be null; and it is not logical to be strict about stream more than mixture — end; and see above seif 16.
Null. It implies even if there is no 60 — as below seif 3; and that is Rambam's view; however we do not hold thus, rather we require 60 against all that fell — for otherwise it returns and revives and is forbidden, as below seif 18.
From the barrel, etc. Ran wrote: I did not know in what regard the distinction between a small flask and a barrel — how did he assign measures to this? And it seems to me the resolution: since it is customary to pour wine into a pit by means of a large barrel, and since the end of the wine in the barrel will descend through this vessel and it is its way — one cannot say the first is null, for whatever stands to fall and mix is considered already mixed; unlike a small flask — since it is not customary to pour into a pit with a vessel like it, we do not view it as fallen and mixed except what has already descended; therefore we say in every vessel like it the first is null — end.
Because the column descending, etc. It is implied explicitly from Ran's words there that specifically when the stream was not interrupted; but if the stream was interrupted, the wine in the barrel is not connected to the wine in the pit; and since in that which fell alone there is not natan ta'am, it is null as in a flask — see there.
