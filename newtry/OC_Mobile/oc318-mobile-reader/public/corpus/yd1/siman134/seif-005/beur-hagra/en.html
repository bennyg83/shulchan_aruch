If there is not, etc. This is like wine mixed with wine, as above in seif 2. Rashba writes that the Raavad held that if it became mixed to the point that the water is fit to drink as wine, even only with difficulty, it may not be sold. He derives this from the statement on 38b regarding pickled foods, etc.: Chizkiyah said, this was taught only, etc. The same applies to ordinary gentile wine mixed with wine if the amount mixed in would render an equal amount of water fit to drink, etc. Support may be found in the Yerushalmi regarding the dispute of Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel and the Sages. Rabbi Yose said: One of the Sages left the study hall and said that Rabbi Yochanan and Reish Lakish disputed; one said the halachah follows Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel and one said it does not. Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel concedes that wine intended for cooking is forbidden. Thus he concedes regarding all substances used to prepare food. Rashba rejects the Raavad's view regarding wine mixed with wine, since the statement, 'Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel concedes,' implies that regarding wine mixed with wine he disagrees even in such a case. Furthermore, the Gemara's phrase, 'the halachah,' etc., 'wine with wine, but,' etc., implies a case comparable to a barrel, etc. Rashba's words imply that regarding wine mixed with water he agrees, even though he rejects the proof from pickled foods because Chizkiyah merely explains the Mishnah and does not rule like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel. The Yerushalmi cited above nevertheless supports the Raavad. Ran writes likewise and states that this is also Rashba's view. Beit Yosef, however, writes in siman 114 that the abridged Torat HaBayit rules against the Raavad, and the gloss there, seif 10, rules accordingly.
Nevertheless, one may not, etc. As stated there on 55b and in seif 8. See Tosafot there 49b, s.v. 'He wove,' and Tosafot Yevamot 81b and Zevachim 72b at the end of s.v. 'They became mixed.' Tosafot holds that the same applies to wine mixed with water, but adding small amounts successively is permitted, as stated in Orach Chaim, siman 467, seif 1. See Magen Avraham there, seif katan 2, who challenges this.
