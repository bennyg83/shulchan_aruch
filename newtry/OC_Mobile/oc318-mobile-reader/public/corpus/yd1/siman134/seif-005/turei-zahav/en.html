If he placed wine in prohibited vessels, etc. The Beit Yosef cites a responsum of the Rashba that if one places kosher wine that already contains water into a non-Jew's vessel that is forbidden because of yayin nesech, the water in the kosher wine nullifies the absorbed prohibition, meaning the amount absorbed in the thickness of a peel from the vessel. L'chatchila, however, this may not be done, because one may not intentionally nullify a prohibition. The Beit Yosef writes concerning this that it requires study, for the water had already been added, etc. It appears to me that Rabbeinu Tam permitted pouring water into the vat at the time of the grape harvest so that if prohibited wine should later fall into it, it would be nullified, only because it was uncertain whether prohibited wine would fall in. Here, however, where he knows that he will certainly pour the wine into this prohibited vessel, it is certainly forbidden l'chatchila. Furthermore, even if he did not originally know that he would pour it into a prohibited vessel, now that he pours it and relies on the water in it to nullify the prohibition absorbed in the vessel, he is intentionally nullifying a prohibition, and one may not intentionally nullify a prohibition. See what I wrote in siman 136, seif 2. According to our practice, even if there is no water, the kosher wine itself nullifies the amount absorbed in the thickness of a peel, as above in seif 2, where the custom is to be lenient. The difference is that if he nullifies it with water, six parts are sufficient, whereas if he nullifies it with kosher wine, sixty times the prohibited amount, namely the amount absorbed in the thickness of a peel, is required.
