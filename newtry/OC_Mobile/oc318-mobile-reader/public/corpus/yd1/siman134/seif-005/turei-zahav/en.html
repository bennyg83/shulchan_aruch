If he poured permitted wine into forbidden vessels, etc. — Beit Yosef cited a responsum of Rashba: if one pours permitted wine that already has water mixed into it into a vessel of a gentile forbidden due to yayin nesech — the water in the permitted wine nullifies the absorption, i.e., what was absorbed in the surface layer of the vessel; but not ab initio, since one may not nullify a prohibition ab initio. Beit Yosef wrote on this: this requires examination — for the water was already added before reaching the vessel. And it seems to me that R' Tam only permitted pouring water into the vat during the vintage season in order to permit wine that might fall in afterward — and that only if it is doubtful whether forbidden wine would fall in; but here, where it is certain that he is pouring it into the forbidden vessel, it is certainly forbidden ab initio. Moreover, even if he did not know at first that he would pour it into the forbidden vessel — now that he is pouring it in and relying on the water within it to nullify the absorbed prohibition — this constitutes nullifying a prohibition ab initio, and it is prohibited. And see what I wrote in siman 136 seif 2. And according to us Ashkenazim, even without water the permitted wine itself nullifies the surface-layer absorption — as stated above in seif 2 where the custom is to be lenient — except that if nullified by water, sixty parts of water against the vessel's absorbed prohibition suffice, whereas if nullified by permitted wine, sixty parts of the wine against the absorbed prohibition of the surface layer are required.
