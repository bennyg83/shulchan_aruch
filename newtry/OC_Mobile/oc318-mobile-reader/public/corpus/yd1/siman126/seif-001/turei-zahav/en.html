And even if he can sell it to a gentile. The reason there in Terumat HaDeshen siman 200: even though he can sell it to a gentile cheaply and this is not called great loss, as proven there from terumah mixed with non-sacred produce — nevertheless here we consider this great loss wherever one must sell cheaply, for regarding libation wine we are not so precise in our time to be stringent like other prohibitions; and further that if you consider it minor loss and sell it to a gentile, leniency will result, for many authorities hold it is forbidden to sell to a gentile wine forbidden through touch of a gentile — until here; and the latter reason is not applicable for us, for we establish in the beginning of siman 123 that stam yeinam is permitted in benefit in our time — all the more in the law of nitzok which is only a stringency; and further his words here are astonishing, for it is plain there is greater prohibition when a Jew drinks it than when he sells it to a gentile, even according to those who forbid; if so it is better he make a minor prohibition than a major prohibition; nevertheless the first reason of Terumat HaDeshen permits us, for from the leniencies of wine in our time we learned here; and we consider this great loss — what requires selling cheaply to gentiles; and it appears there that even if it is somewhat cheap in selling to a gentile it is called great loss regarding this wine.
