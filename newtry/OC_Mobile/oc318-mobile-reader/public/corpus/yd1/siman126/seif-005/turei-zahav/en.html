One who pours wine, etc. Tur wrote: some say nitzok even to stam wine, etc.; Beit Yosef raised many difficulties on Tur; and it appears according to Rabbenu Meir that this is the explanation of Tur's words: some say nitzok even to stam yeinam — everything forbidden in benefit, for he holds the stringency in the lower is itself applied to the upper and we do not consider them mixed to nullify in sixty, for in truth they are not mixed; rather we say the stringency of the lower is on the upper literally and as if it is stam yeinam by itself. And Rashba disagrees on this in truth, as explained in his language that Beit Yosef brings, who wrote it is only by the law of connection to the lower and as if mixed with it — and it is well nullified in sixty, for thus Rashba holds regarding stam yeinam that it is nullified in sixty, or he carries benefit to the Dead Sea, as Tur wrote nearby; and likewise Rashba in his explanation in Terumat HaDeshen daf 151 side 1 disagrees with these "some say" that Tur brought, and this is Raavad's view; and on daf 152 he wrote further, and these are his words: nevertheless for everyone nitzok is not like touch of a gentile but like mixtures of wine; and after Tur brought the view of "some say" who are very stringent, he brought Rashba's view who is very lenient, for he holds they did not say nitzok is connection except in complete libation wine — meaning clear wine visible, whether definite stam yeinam or stam — connection applies each according to its law: definite stam yeinam — everything forbidden; and in stam yeinam he carries benefit, etc.; and likewise in Talmud Yerushalmi daf 151, and these are his words: except for stam yeinam forbidden in benefit, which is complete libation wine, they said connection; and libation wine is called only what is forbidden in benefit — end of his words. But when pouring into a vessel that was not rinsed where there is no complete wine but only absorption alone — Rashba is lenient, for here there is no connection at all and the upper is permitted even for drinking; and Tur disagrees with both of them, for in complete libation wine — meaning definite stam yeinam — the upper has no remedy even though Rashba also mentioned language of complete libation wine, and we explained it includes also stam yeinam — this is not astonishing, for each learns from his context; from the end we hear the beginning — Rashba wrote in the end "but one who pours into a vessel of a gentile" — we learn the beginning also deals even with stam yeinam; but here he afterward wrote in explanation "and if to stam yeinam" — automatically the beginning deals with definite stam yeinam; it is found that Tur disagrees with "some say" and also Rashba; but in what he wrote that when pouring into an unrinsed vessel there is no connection at all — Tur disagrees with him, for it is connection in every manner; and on what Tur wrote "if to stam yeinam he carries benefit" — Beit Yosef challenged: what benefit does he have, for nothing mixed there; I wondered why he did not challenge thus on what Tur wrote afterward in Rashba's name "if there is in the water in the barrel sixty," etc.; and likewise he ruled in Shulchan Aruch in the next seif; and why did Beit Yosef not challenge how nullification in sixty applies in a winepress against the splash, for the splash is not mixed there; rather it is plain the sages made nitzok connection in libation wine like permitted absorbed prohibition when it is hot — we establish even though the prohibition is whole we require sixty against all of it; so too the sages made stringency here and sixty are needed to nullify the splash as if it is literally there; and likewise regarding if there is not sixty we view as if part of the permitted above became prohibition literally and he must carry benefit, etc.; also Rema in Darkhei Moshe brought proof for Tur's view from Mordechai chapter of the hired worker that we require sixty against libation wine in a vessel from which the gentile pours — we learn it is as if mixed; therefore Rama ruled well here "and if it is stam yeinam it is nullified in sixty"; and in what I wrote all the difficulties Beit Yosef raised are resolved without any forced explanation — what some strained in this; also Rashba's language that Beit Yosef brings is well explained on this path.
