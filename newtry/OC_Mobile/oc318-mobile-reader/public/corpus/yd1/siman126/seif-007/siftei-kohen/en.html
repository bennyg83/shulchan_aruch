rather all the wine, grape seeds, grape skins, grapes, and lees combine to nullify in sixty; and likewise Taz challenged; and perhaps he holds that specifically stam yeinam is nullified in sixty but not wine forbidden through touch of gentiles; but one cannot say thus, as written siman 123 s.k. 40; and one may resolve that here it deals with the law of the Gemara — specifically in our time the rabbi says in siman 134 it is nullified in sixty — see there (except requires study from the words of Hagahot Maimoni, Pri To'ar of Forbidden Foods, and Beit Yosef and Darkhei Moshe below siman 134: it appears explicitly that even according to the one who says stam yeinam is not nullified in sixty — that is specifically wine that a gentile touched and became mixed; but nitzok is nullified in sixty for everyone — see there; and one may say the rabbi holds not like Hagahot Maimoni but like the Mechaber and his camp); and nevertheless what Rashba holds that if he touched the splash they nullify in sixty — Taz was drawn to his view — requires study for halachah according to Tosafot last chapter of Avodah Zarah (daf 72 side 2) who wrote, and these are their words: if a Jew drew from the barrel into a vessel that has stam yeinam, if there is in the barrel sixty against the wine in the vessel — permitted, even according to the one who says nitzok is connection, for it is no worse than if all were mixed in the barrel and were nullified in sixty in stam yeinam, as Rabbenu Tam explained; and nitzok that became forbidden — for example the wine was spurting from the barrel and the gentile came and touched the splash which is the nitzok — now it is as if the gentile touched the barrel, for this became forbidden — end of his words; and likewise Mordechai and Agudah there; and see there siman 134 s.k. 3 and seif 2 in the hagahah, further laws of nitzok.
