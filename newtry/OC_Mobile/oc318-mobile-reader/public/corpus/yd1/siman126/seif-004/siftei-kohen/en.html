To mortar, etc. But on the ground — they too sprinkle on the ground before their idolatry — Rashba, and Beit Yosef and Perishah bring it; and in Taz he wrote: if one pours wine on the ground and a gentile touched that splash, the splash is forbidden from the touch and what is inside is forbidden through nitzok — end of his words; and he did not explain as plainly as what is written outside is forbidden because of his force — and he properly intended that if not so it would be forbidden only because of his force for drinking, as above siman 125, and what is inside would not be forbidden because of nitzok, for nitzok forbids only wine forbidden in benefit, as in the next seif; and even though from Rashba's words there it appears that even when pouring without touching it is forbidden because of nitzok — according to his reasoning he goes, who forbids his force in benefit — unlike for us.
Including what went outside. Taz wrote that even if the gentile who pours it to mortar touched it, everything is permitted, for since he himself pours it to loss, his intent is nullified and it is like mere water and there is no intent of libation at all — end of his words; and this is from Rashba's words in the name of Raavad, and Beit Yosef brings it.
