If the gentile, etc. As written 73a and here in the times, etc., and according to Raavad's explanation there: he put wine into it and Jew on Jew — meaning he put the Jew's wine into it; and nevertheless whenever the gentile pours, even though he does not touch — forbidden, as written there 72b regarding a gentile who pours, etc.; and that is what we challenge: for Jew on Jew, etc.; and they answer: in a time when it is permitted, etc.; and that we require Jew on Jew lest he libated it from the outset before he pours it; and even though we say regarding a gentile who pours — that is on the ground which is not repulsive; and likewise in Yerushalmi there; and one should be concerned lest he libated — it is not fit for libation on a repulsive thing, etc. Ran there and Talmud Yerushalmi.
But, etc. Specifically when the gentile pours it to nullify it — he is not libating; unlike in such a case.
Everything forbidden. For what is inside is forbidden because of nitzok, as written in the beginning of siman 125; and Mordechai's view that nitzok going to loss is permitted. Beit Yosef.
