The conclusion is: if we interpret Rambam's words as the Tur understood them, he has no support from any of the decisors before us — for the Tur himself wrote that the Rosh did not make this distinction, and we see that no decisor wrote so; and Raavad explicitly wrote this view — that the matter depends on the verbal statement within the time of speech; and so it is in Darkei Moshe in the name of Raavad — that we are not concerned at all about the time of speech within the vow. If so, the Shulchan Arukh should not have established the law that we require within the time of speech for the vow, since he himself wrote in Beit Yosef the words of Raavad who wrote about Rambam "he ruled correctly," etc. — and it is based on Raavad's words that he wrote here in the Shulchan Arukh "and he should say in his heart that he relies on the condition"; and according to this there is no need for within the time of speech of the vow. And one cannot say the Shulchan Arukh rules like both — requiring the fulfillment of the condition within the time of speech of the vow, and that "he should say in his heart" also refers to within that time — for actually we do not require fulfillment within the time of speech of the vow but within the time of speech of the awareness; and this requires further study.
