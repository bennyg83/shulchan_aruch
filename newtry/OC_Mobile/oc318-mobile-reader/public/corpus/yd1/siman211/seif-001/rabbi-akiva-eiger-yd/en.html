(Siman 211, Shach s.k. 2.) At the time of the vow. Words of the heart we require evident — it appears his intention: even if he first says whatever I vow shall be void — nevertheless when he vows afterward it only helps if also at the hour he vows his intention in his heart is that the vow be void; and if so it should be included in words of the heart; therefore he must first say publicly evident to the many that in his heart it is so at the time of the vow — in such a case, revealed to all, it is not called words of the heart. But truly one must explain — why do we need intention in his heart at all, for without it the speech itself is void from what he said before it shall be void; and even if not so, also at the time of the vow it would be uprooted to the condition — meaning he remembers the condition and nevertheless vows — then he annuls his condition; unlike if truly in his heart it stands for his condition — automatically the vow is void from the outset and not a matter of words of the heart at all; one only needs the opposite — not to uproot the condition. Proof — for the Talmud challenges straightforwardly: since he remembers, he uprooted his condition — perhaps in his heart to fulfill the condition; and if words of the heart — for listeners otherwise it is not evident there is substance in his utterance, for they do not know he remembers the condition; and regarding himself — in his heart to fulfill the condition; and also from what they ruled — remembering the condition and not knowing what he conditioned — he must say with his lips he wants the condition; and why does what is in his heart not help — nevertheless as written, whoever remembers the condition — automatically proper speech; only that upon him comes from the law of his thought in his heart to annul the vow — even though we call this words of the heart — investigate.
