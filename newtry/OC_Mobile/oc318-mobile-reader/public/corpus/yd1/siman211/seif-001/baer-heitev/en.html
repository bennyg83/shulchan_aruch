It is not. Shach writes that even if he remembers the prior condition at the time of the vow, the vow ordinarily stands, as stated in seif 2. Here it is void because the condition was formulated together with the specific vow. Perishah explains that the decisive point is whether he explicitly annulled this particular future vow, rather than making a general declaration concerning any vow. Taz disagrees and understands the case as one in which the condition was made at the time of the vow itself. Nekudot HaKesef agrees with Perishah.
In a whisper. Bach limits the invalidity to a declaration not audible to his own ears. If he heard himself, it counts as verbal annulment, as with Shema and prayer. Shach disagrees and understands Rosh and Rabbeinu Yerucham to require public annulment in a loud voice.
