Honey of Ishmaelites, etc. Explanation: the Ishmaelites are accustomed to take grapes and put on them known white earth one hour or a little more before treading them, so the wine coming from them will quickly congeal and stand in its sweetness; and afterward they tread them and cook that wine with good cooking until it thickens and becomes like honey, and they call it honey — so in responsum Ran siman 5. But plainly honey of Ishmaelites from bees or dates has no concern of prohibition; and in Turei Zahav it appears he did not understand thus, for he omitted that honey of Ishmaelites is forbidden and wrote that we practice to permit drinking with non-Jews honey even though it intoxicates; and he expanded there on its taste in vain, for its permission was explained plainly above siman 114.
Forbidden. In Beit Yosef he brought Ran's responsum, that there is no intermarriage concern here; and on this the Ra'ah relied regarding a non-Jew who cooked for an ill person on Shabbat, that it is permitted for a healthy person on a weekday, for there is no intermarriage concern here; and I also say it is possible to judge for halachah that honey is permitted, for it has no status of wine, and from the beginning of its treading they intended for this; but in practice I forbid, for I saw some great ones disagree with the Ra'ah, and Ba'al HaTerumah includes them; and therefore it is fit to practice prohibition regarding honey — end of his words; and so Darkhei Moshe copied below end of siman 134. And accordingly there was a difficulty: why did the rabbi rule above end of siman 113 regarding a non-Jew who cooked, etc., that it is permitted; but in truth it is an error in Beit Yosef and Darkhei Moshe; and in Ran's responsum it is also found: the Ra'ah also permitted a non-Jew who cooked for himself, whether on a weekday or Shabbat, things permitted regarding vessels to use them, for there is no concern here of intermarriage; but in practice I forbid, according to his words; and it implies in the first law everyone agrees; and one should not say it refers to honey of Ishmaelites.
