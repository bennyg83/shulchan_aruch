Honey. Turei Zahav explained that its beginning is pure wine, and afterward they cook it with spices until it becomes honey; and had this been done by another non-Jew it would be a complete prohibition since its beginning was pure wine. But since Ishmaelites are not non-Jews like other non-Jews, there is no prohibition here except because of intermarriage; therefore it forbids only for drinking. But regarding mead (made from bee honey or dates) it is obvious that the practice is to permit drinking with a non-Jew, as Maharam wrote in siman 114. And I heard many who are lenient in practice even regarding wine, saying that nowadays intermarriage does not apply, since non-Jews keep us distant — and this contradicts his own words, for on the contrary, since non-Jews keep us distant, drunkenness will cause complete denial, God forbid, and one will marry his daughter; therefore there is no reason to distinguish for leniency — end of his words.
