Honey of Ishmaelites. Explanation: its beginning is pure wine, and afterward they cook it with spices until it becomes honey; and had this been done by another non-Jew it would be a complete prohibition since its beginning was pure wine. But since Ishmaelites are not idolaters like other non-Jews, as the Tur wrote in siman 124, and even in pure wine there is prohibition only for drinking, and there is no prohibition here except because of intermarriage with avodah zarah, Ran judged that perhaps there is no intermarriage concern here, like cooked wine of a Jew that is permitted to drink with non-Jews; and he wrote that intermarriage is not forbidden in every matter, etc.; and for this reason the Ra'ah permitted a non-Jew who cooked for an ill person on Shabbat, that it is permitted for a healthy person on a weekday; but one should forbid in practice since Sefer HaTerumah and many great ones disagree with the Ra'ah, etc., all this in Beit Yosef. And from this I am very astonished at Rama, who ruled at the end of siman 113 like the Ra'ah regarding a non-Jew who cooked for an ill person, etc., behold it is one law with honey here; and since here it is forbidden, so too there it is forbidden for a healthy person on a weekday; and see what he wrote there also. I again saw here in Levush that he wrote this law regarding stam honey of non-Jews, and he thinks it refers to mead; and in the place of Ishmaelites he wrote non-Jews and gave a reason for this: since there is no intermarriage concern for us, for on the contrary non-Jews also keep us distant, besides in wine there is prohibition from the side that it was forbidden by a count and requires another count, etc.; and he greatly erred in this, for behold Beit Yosef was before him and he did not examine Ran's responsum on this, that it refers to another matter, as I quoted; also what he wrote that now non-Jews keep us distant, let this matter be silent from his saying, for many are lenient in practice from this even in wine; and I heard from the great one of the generation, Maharal of Prague, that one who mocked his words on this and said: since non-Jews keep us distant, drunkenness will cause complete denial, God forbid, and one will marry his daughter, therefore there is no reason to distinguish for leniency. But regarding the essence of the law of drinking mead with non-Jews, its law was already explained in siman 113, that there is no intermarriage concern, see there.
