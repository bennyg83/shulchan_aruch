One may not tread with the non-Jew in the winepress. These are Rambam's words, chapter 11 of Forbidden Foods; and it deals with a winepress that is not closed and there is drawing there; and on this the reason he wrote "lest he touch," etc., applies. But in a closed and full winepress, where drawing is not relevant, it does not forbid even if he touched with his hands; but the Tur wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Tam that even in a closed winepress one may not tread with the non-Jew, since in any drawing whatsoever it becomes yayin nesech, we decree lest [one come to act] after drawing; and when treading in a vat where drawing is not relevant, etc., the reason is: in a vat there is no slope in its floor, and therefore even though it is lacking there is no drawing in the empty place because of moving grapes from the force of treading; and all that is squeezed by treading is stopped among the grapes since there is no slope, and there is no drawing here. But if there is a small spigot there from which clear wine comes out, certainly there is drawing there; but not if it is large, for then grapes also come out there; and likewise if he turns all the grapes to one place in the vat and wine from the side, this is properly drawing. But Mordechai in the name of Rabbeinu Tam was stringent also in a vat in the treading of non-Jews; and so it appears in Hagahot Ashiri, from the reason that in any case since there is relevance of drawing in some manner we are concerned for it lest there be drawing. But in a vat that has no hole with a spigot at all, it is certainly permitted with the non-Jew's treading, for there is no relevance of drawing at all; one should not say lest he turn aside the grapes until the edges of the vessels and wine will be drawn there clear, for what purpose would he do so? We do not concern ourselves lest this come about, and it is an uncommon matter; and likewise it is not common that he takes out a little clear wine, as Beit Yosef wrote, all this is not common. And further, Beit Yosef wrote that according to Terumat HaDeshen he does not forbid what he takes out a little clear wine, only what is in the cup is forbidden; and even though we establish stringently, as he wrote in seif 18, nevertheless for a doubt we are not stringent on this. And nevertheless there is a slight inconsistency in Rama's words, who wrote "or in a vat", namely even while there is still a spigot inside it; and this is better than a closed winepress, namely from the reason of decree lest after drawing, as we mentioned. And these words of the author are in an open winepress specifically, since he concludes "lest he touch," etc., as we proved. And regarding practical halachah one should rule as we mentioned: in every vessel that has a spigot, even if the spigot is large, one should be careful about the non-Jew's treading there; and even though Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Tosafot to permit with a large spigot like the mouth of a barrel, nevertheless from Mordechai's words it appears to be stringent also in this, as Rama wrote here, namely since there is some drawing there, the spigot moves slightly and clear wine comes out, and this is a common occurrence as I have heard. But in a vessel that has no spigot at all, there is no prohibition there even with the non-Jew's treading, as it appears to me; and so I practiced in the region of Mehrin in Galilee when I was there at harvest time: they did not at all turn the grapes to one side in the vat to the edges to make clear wine in the vat; therefore there is no decree lest after drawing. And Maharik wrote that nevertheless there is no prohibition of the touch of a non-Jew in grapes in a vat, even though treading is forbidden according to Rabbeinu Tam, for also in treading it is explained in Tosafot that the ancients did not heed this stringency of Rabbeinu Tam, see there. And it appears the same is in a closed winepress; but in an open winepress it is obvious that touch is forbidden even in grapes. And Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Semag that whenever there is a basket in the winepress and clear wine enters there, it is called drawing and is forbidden even if the non-Jew touched the grapes.
Lest he touch with his hands. And even though with the foot there is also prohibition, as he wrote in seif 24, nevertheless here it is different because a Jew treads with him, and this is explained in siman 125 regarding a Jew assisting; even by hand there is leniency there.
But in a kneading trough, etc. One may question this leniency, for perhaps those crushed grapes had already been in a place where drawing off the wine occurred, and the non-Jew knew that the Jew would not purchase them from that vessel, and therefore he emptied them into this kneading trough. Indeed, I have heard from many scholars and pious men who enacted that crushed grapes should not be purchased from a non-Jew in any vessel, and they ruled accordingly in actual practice. It is proper to conduct oneself this way wherever it is possible.
