And if there are in grape-skins and pulp, etc. Meaning that even though we establish below siman 134 that yayin nesech is forbidden in any amount — it was explained there that this is specifically in its type; but not-its-type in sixty parts — namely in sixty — and grape-skins and pulp are not the type of wine; and even though there is also wine there that is its type, we say "remove its type" as though it were not present, and not-its-type dominates and nullifies it (as above siman 98 seif 2); and so in the poskim. And accordingly, for what the rabbi wrote in siman 134 seif 2: the practice is to be lenient that all stam yeinam nowadays is nullified in sixty — so too here, for certainly the touch of a non-Jew is no worse than stam yeinam, as appears from the rabbi's words in this siman — they have one law; and so in responsum Maharil siman 38 (and see below siman 124 seif kaf 21). And so it appears explicitly in Tosafot (Avodah Zarah 56 side 2, "But if," etc.) and Mordechai there, who wrote: according to Rabbeinu Tam, who holds stam yeinam in sixty — even min b'mino is not forbidden — here in returning the basket to the winepress, since presumably there are sixty in the winepress, it is proven from their words explicitly that everything in the winepress, whether wine or grape-skins and pulp, all combines to nullify — see there; and so it is proven explicitly from Tosafot's and Mordechai's words and Agudah last chapter of tractate Avodah Zarah; and I brought their words below siman 126 seif kaf 13: wine forbidden by the touch of a non-Jew is also nullified in sixty like stam yeinam; and likewise Rabbeinu Yerucham 17:1, Rabbeinu Tam wrote: specifically yayin nesech, but stam yeinam in sixty; and likewise the touch of a non-Jew; and likewise Ramban, etc.; and so in Tosafot and the Rosh explicitly in the name of Rabbeinu Tam (last chapter of tractate Avodah Zarah in the mishnah "yayin nesech is forbidden and forbids in any amount"): the touch of a non-Jew — its law is like stam yeinam, that it is nullified in sixty; and so it is proven explicitly further from their words there and Ran's words there and Rashba in Terumat HaDeshen regarding what they challenged Rabbeinu Tam from the case of agradim — see there; and likewise in Orach Chayyim kelal 23 law 14: there is no difference between wine of a non-Jew and the touch of a non-Jew. And there is great difficulty on the rabbi: why did he not emend here that everything combines, and even when there are no grape-skins and pulp it is nullified in the wine (and one may say: because it appears simple he relied on the essence of the law to below siman 134; and also one may say grape-skins and pulp is not precise). And on Turei Zahav there is greater difficulty, for he explicitly wrote that wine does not combine, since it is its type, and only in grape-skins and pulp it is nullified; and he explained the mentioned reason — and behold this is not so for us, as I explained; and see end of siman 126.
One may not tread, etc. As he wrote the reason in the hagahah: we decree before drawing lest [one come to act] after drawing — as Beit Yosef; and it implies Rabbeinu Tam did not forbid a closed and full winepress except that the non-Jew treaded in it, but his touch in it he agrees does not forbid — as Rashba's and Ran's words; and so it appears from the Tur's words; and so Maharik precisely deduced from Semag's words — end of his words; and Darkhei Moshe brings it. And in Kesef Mishnah chapter 11 of Forbidden Foods law 11 he understood that the Rambam (whom the author copied here) forbids in a covered press even in benefit, for we decree covered lest not covered; and there is a difficulty: why should covered be worse than a Jew guarding, where he wrote afterward and the author copied in the next seif that it is forbidden only for drinking; and likewise Beit Yosef in siman 124 seif 11: covered is better than a Jew guarding. Rather the matter is clear: at the beginning he wrote that even in a covered press it is forbidden l'chatchila to tread with non-Jews, as Maharik and Kolbo below seif kaf 43; and afterward he wrote that b'di'eved, even a Jew guarding suffices, for there is no concern of touch but because of his force, and his force is permitted in benefit, as below siman 125; and this is clear. And see below siman 124 seif 19 in the hagahah.
Lest he touch with his hands, etc. Kesef Mishnah wrote in chapter 11 of Forbidden Foods there: from the Rambam's wording here (in this seif and the next seif) one can learn that he holds libation by foot is not called libation to forbid in benefit, for he wrote "lest he touch with his hand and pour a libation" — and if so, let it be forbidden because he touches it with his foot when he treads. And further, that he wrote nearby "a non-Jew who treaded the wine and did not touch it," etc. — "this is forbidden for drinking" — it implies even though he treaded with his feet it is not forbidden in benefit. And one should not reject that all these deal when he did not touch the grapes in the winepress, such as when there are boards on the grapes and he treads on them; for if so he should not have closed the matter — end of his words. And it is astonishing, for here he copied the Rambam's wording, and below siman 124 seif 11 he ruled that libation by foot is called libation to forbid in benefit; and it is possible that specifically from the Rambam he deduced, because he should not have closed the matter; but the author here explained below that libation by foot is called libation; if so, perforce here it deals when he treads on boards — this is the author's view in my opinion. But in truth it appears to me there is no proof from the Rambam's words, for behold the Rosh and Tur siman 124 hold that libation by foot is called libation, and nevertheless hold that if he treaded with his feet it is permitted in benefit because he is occupied with treading; also what he brought in Kesef Mishnah there and in Beit Yosef below siman 124 — Rashba's responsum, who wrote: from the Rambam's wording in chapter 12 of Forbidden Foods, "How is touch that the non-Jew forbids in it — that he touched the wine itself, whether by his hand or by other limbs accustomed to pour libations" — it implies he holds touch by foot does not forbid in benefit — end of his words; this is not conclusive, for it is possible he holds like the Rosh's and Tur's view and their support, that the way to pour libation is by foot (I again found in Be'er HaGolah that he retracted and wrote that perhaps the Rambam also holds libation by foot is called libation). And see below siman 124 seif kaf 18 and seif kaf 53; and see there seif kaf 71: I wrote that wherever it is forbidden by law for drinking, for us nowadays it is permitted even for drinking in a place of loss, since non-Jews nowadays are not idolaters.
And what came outside is forbidden. But what is in the winepress or vat, even though it was drawn, is permitted, for the non-Jew's force forbids only in wine that came outside — so Beit Yosef and Darkhei Moshe; and see below siman 125; and this is when he did not touch the grapes in the winepress or vat, such as when he placed wood on the grapes and the non-Jew went up on the wood and they were treaded from his force, as is found in Beit Yosef and Darkhei Moshe; but if he touched them, it is obvious it is forbidden because of touch when it was drawn; but when it was not drawn, it is permitted b'di'eved even if he touched — so Maharik root 32, and Beit Yosef and Darkhei Moshe bring it and conclude with it; and so everything in it: even if the non-Jew treaded, the wine is not forbidden b'di'eved — see there.
Is forbidden. — For drinking — for the force of the non-Jew forbids only for drinking, as below siman 125.
But in a trough, etc. — See below siman 125 seif 6; and see in the responsum of Binyamin Ze'ev siman 350.
