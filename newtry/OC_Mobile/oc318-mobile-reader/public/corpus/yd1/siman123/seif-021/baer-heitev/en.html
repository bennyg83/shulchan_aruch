Stepping. Shach wrote that treading is prohibited specifically in a full and closed winepress, but touching it is not prohibited, and in a covered press it is prohibited only for drinking. And Shach challenged: here it implies that libation by foot is not called libation to forbid in benefit, for behold he wrote "lest he touch with his hand and pour a libation" — and if so, let it be forbidden because he touches it with his foot when he treads. And below siman 124 seif 11 he ruled that libation by foot is called libation to forbid in benefit; therefore it must be that here it deals with when he treads on boards, or that here it is permitted in benefit because he is occupied with treading. And Turei Zahav distinguishes: here it is different because a Jew treads with him — and this is explained in siman 125 regarding a Jew assisting, even by hand there is leniency. And Shach concluded that wherever it is forbidden by law for drinking, for us nowadays it is permitted even for drinking in a place of loss, since non-Jews nowadays are not idolaters. And Turei Zahav wrote that for practical halachah one should rule that in every vessel that has a spigot, even if the spigot is large, one should be careful about the non-Jew's treading there — because since there is some drawing there, the spigot moves slightly and clear wine comes out, and this is a common occurrence as I have heard. But in a vessel that has no spigot at all, there is no prohibition there even with the non-Jew's treading. And so I practiced in the region of Mehrin in Galilee when I was there at harvest time: they did not at all turn the grapes to one side in the vat to the edges to make clear wine in the vat; therefore there is no decree lest [one come to act] after the drawing. And Maharik wrote that nevertheless there is no prohibition of the touch of a non-Jew in grapes in a vat, even though treading is forbidden according to Rabbeinu Tam — and it appears this is in a closed winepress; but in an open winepress it is obvious that touch is forbidden even in grapes. And Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Semag that whenever there is a basket in the winepress and clear wine enters there, it is called drawing and is forbidden even if the non-Jew touched the grapes — end of his words.
