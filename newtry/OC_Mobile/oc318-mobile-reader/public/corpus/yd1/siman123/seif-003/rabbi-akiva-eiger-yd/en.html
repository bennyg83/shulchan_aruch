(A) and as a result of Hashem’s promise. Even in the derini pocket:
(Turei Zahav seif kaf 3) That even though it is not fit for libation they forbade it entirely. I do not understand: whence did the Rosh know that perhaps the non-Jew's way is to pour a libation of mixed wine — similar to what the Rosh challenged against the Rambam regarding putting a little leaven into it. And even according to Rabbeinu Yonah, who also regarding the touch of a non-Jew in temed depends on "he poured three and four came" — namely where it is not wine for purposes of birkat ha-gomel it does not enter at all within the decree of the Sages, for they decreed only on wine. And especially since the main decree was because of their daughters; and they did not decree on this except on wine. Nevertheless one may say that the non-Jew's way is to pour a libation of mixed wine. And mainly I did not succeed in understanding the Rosh's difficulty: whence did the Rosh know that the touch of non-Jews was because of their daughters? Behold plainly regarding touch their daughters are not mentioned — only since they forbade stam yeinam in benefit, which is as though it was poured as a libation, therefore they were also stringent regarding their touch from concern for libation. And on the contrary, the Rosh according to his view, who holds that the touch of a ger toshav is permitted for drinking and his wine is forbidden for drinking — therefore regarding touch their daughters are not relevant; and so it also appears from the Rosh's words, who wrote on Rashbam's words regarding our non-Jews: "these reasons suffice for our wine that a non-Jew touched; but their stam wine that they forbade because of their daughters," etc. — behold that regarding touch their daughters are not mentioned; and especially why the Rosh afterward suggested that one may say: initially they did not forbid stam yeinam except for drinking, but afterward when they were accustomed to pour libations they forbade it in benefit — one may say that within one general decree there was no prohibition in their touch; only afterward when they were accustomed to pour libations they also forbade their touch. If so, regarding our cooked wine, which deals only with the touch of a non-Jew, there is not so much place for the Rosh's difficulty. And investigate.
