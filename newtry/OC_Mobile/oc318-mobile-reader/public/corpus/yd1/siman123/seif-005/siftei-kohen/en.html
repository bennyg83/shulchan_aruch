And wine that congealed, etc. It is written in Hagahot Asheri from the Or Zarua, Perek 2 of Avodah Zarah, that wine which congealed is not subject to the prohibition of contact by non-Jews, etc.; and likewise, if a non-Jew touched it after it dissolved, it is subject to the prohibition of contact by non-Jews. This is cited by the Darkei Moshe. It appears to me that according to what we hold, that contact by non-Jews prohibits wine that has soured because we are not expert in determining when it is called vinegar, as below in s.k. 13, the same applies here to wine that has formed a film or begun to congeal, for we are not expert, and contact by a non-Jew prohibits it. So too it is implied by the words of the Rosh, who wrote regarding vinegar that Rabbenu Tam became angry at R. Meshullam for permitting contact by a non-Jew with vinegar, because we are not expert in the nature of vinegar: some call it vinegar while others drink it and recite kiddush over it. And regarding wine that has formed a film, the amoraim dispute in Perek Ha-mokher Peirot when it is called vinegar; therefore, if one permits contact by a non-Jew with vinegar, a mishap will result. Rashi also explains in Perek 2 of Avodah Zarah, regarding chamra de-akrim, that it means it soured, and Rashbam explains likewise in Perek Ha-mokher Peirot, and so it is proven in the Gemara there. It appears that for this reason the Rav omitted these words of the Hagahot Asheri that he had brought in the Darkei Moshe. It further appears that there is a scribal error in the gloss and in the Darkei Moshe: instead of "wine that formed a film" it should read "wine that congealed," with a shin, as is implied from what he says afterward, "after it dissolved," etc. So too the Bach in seif 13 copied the wording of this gloss as "wine that congealed."
