Because. Beit Yosef understands from the authorities that it is permitted even for drinking. He was troubled by Rashi's expression, 'permitted for benefit,' regarding aluntit, which implies that drinking is forbidden, and he strained to reconcile Rashi with permission to drink. Taz writes that according to Rashi it is indeed forbidden for drinking. This is not compelling, however, because Rashi uses the same expression concerning cooked wine, though the Gemara explicitly permits cooked wine for drinking, as Shmuel drank it with Avlat. Rather, this supports Beit Yosef: whenever Rashi says it is not forbidden for benefit, he means that it is permitted even for drinking.
Honey. Meaning, it contains enough honey that, if mixed with wine alone, the honey would alter the wine's taste. Darkei Moshe. This proves that the essential criterion is a change of taste. Accordingly, wine infused with wormwood, called vermouth wine, should not be subject to the prohibition of non-Jewish touch, though other responsa ruled stringently.
