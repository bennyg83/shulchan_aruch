A cooked dish that contains wine, and a non-Jew touched it — even before it was boiled — it does not have the status of yayin nesech. {Rama: This is specifically a cooked dish in which the wine is not recognizable as a substance; but regarding fat or mustard into which wine was put, and it is present as a substance floating on top, it has the status of yayin nesech (Tosafot in the name of Ritzba). And it appears that this is specifically if its taste was not changed because of what was mixed into it. And if it became mixed with cooked wine: if it has honey, everyone agrees it is permitted, for it is no worse than if it were honey alone (Hagahot Ashiri, Perek Elu Metziot, and it appears so). And if it has no honey, there are those who appraise it as though the cooked wine were water (Beit Yosef in the name of Rashbatz and Hagahot Ashiri there), and there are those who say that if the cooked wine is the majority it does not have the status of the touch of a non-Jew (Beit Yosef in the name of the great ones of the generation). And wine that congealed does not have the status of the touch of a non-Jew (Chiddushei Agudah). And all the more so if he touched earthenware that absorbed wine (Hagahot Ashiri).}
