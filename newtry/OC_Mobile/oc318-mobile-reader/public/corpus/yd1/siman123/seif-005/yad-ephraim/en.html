Even after they removed it. See Baer Heitev and responsa Zichron Yosef, simanim 5 and 6, concerning wine that was pressed after the outlet of the winepress had been plugged. Non-Jews climbed onto the press to arrange the mound, while the Jew positioned the boards and beams. Several times they took grape seeds and skins from the press and placed them upon the mound. Likewise, after the first pressing ended, they came with axes to split the grapes for a second pressing, and similarly for a third. He discusses at length grounds for leniency. There is sixty times the small amount of wine adhering to the seeds and skins. Where the quantity is uncertain, the prohibition is rabbinic, and particularly nowadays it is comparable to a case where the prohibited substance was spilled, as discussed in siman 98. There is also a compound doubt: perhaps the non-Jew did not touch it for libation but only for the pressing; and even if he touched it, perhaps the skins were not wet enough to moisten something else. There is also the possibility that the halachah follows the opinion that only the place of contact becomes forbidden. One may therefore permit the wine even for a Jew to drink in a case of substantial loss and pressing need. However, wine removed from a straining basket that had a small spigot and stood in the non-Jew's house is absolutely forbidden. In the future, they must warn the workers not to allow non-Jews onto the winepress at all once it has been opened even once to remove clear wine before pressing, even if the outlet was then plugged and even if a straining basket surrounds the mouth of the outlet. See there. He also writes that he witnessed a serious breach. After the first pressing, they swept all the crushed grapes and scattered material in the press into the mound, then arranged everything with a saw so that the boards and beam could be set in place. They did the same for the third and fourth pressings. Since the grape skins had been cleared away, clear wine collected in the press, constituting drawing forth of the wine. They therefore proclaimed throughout the country that no non-Jew should approach the winepress after the outlet had been opened, even once, to remove the first wine, even if the outlet was plugged afterward.
