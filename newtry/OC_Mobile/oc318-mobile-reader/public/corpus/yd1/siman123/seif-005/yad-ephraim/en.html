Even after they removed it. See Baer Heitev and Zichron Yosef, responsa 5 and 6, concerning wine being pressed after the pipe of the winepress had been plugged. Non-Jewish workers climbed onto the press to arrange the mound of grapes, while Jews placed the boards and beams. The workers repeatedly took grape seeds and skins from the press and placed them on the mound, and after the first pressing they returned with poles to crush grapes for a second and third pressing. The responsum develops several grounds for leniency: there was sixty times the quantity of the small amount of wine adhering to the seeds and skins; where the matter is doubtful, the prohibition is rabbinic and, especially nowadays, resembles a case of spilled mixture in siman 98; and there is a double doubt, perhaps they did not touch for idolatrous libation but only for pressing, and even if they touched, perhaps the grapes were not wet enough to moisten another surface. There is also a possibility that the halachah follows the view that only the place of contact becomes prohibited. Therefore, in a case of substantial loss and pressing need, the wine may be permitted even for Jewish drinking. However, wine removed through a strainer fitted with a small tap that stood in the non-Jew’s house is completely prohibited. Going forward, they must be warned not to allow non-Jews to climb onto the press at all once it has been opened even once to draw off clear wine before pressing, even if it is later plugged and even if a strainer surrounds the mouth of the pipe. The responsum further condemns the practice whereby, after the first pressing, workers sweep all the pressed and scattered grapes toward the mound and rearrange everything with a saw-like implement before replacing the boards and beam, repeating this for a third and fourth pressing. Once the grape residue is moved aside, clear wine gathers in the press and is considered drawn wine. The authorities therefore proclaimed throughout the region that no non-Jew may approach the press once the pipe has been opened, even once, to remove the first wine, even if the pipe is afterward plugged.
