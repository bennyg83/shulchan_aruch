(Seif 8 in the gloss) The taste of wine is forbidden. In the Ran it is written: as long as it has the taste and appearance of wine. See the Beit Yosef in the name of the Tashbetz regarding raw wine mixed with cooked wine.
(Taz s.k. 4) It does not help for drinking if a non-Jew touched it — these words are astonishing, for it is explicit in the Talmud that Shmuel and Avlat drank cooked wine together; see the Prisha to distinguish that in the home of a non-Jew it is forbidden, as it is no better than other beverages.
(Shach seif kaf 18) Rather he means more than three and a half. I am astonished, for we say on this in the sugya: Rava, according to his reasoning — "all wine that does not carry," etc. — behold here it depends only on the measure of mixing; and so it appears explicitly from Ran's words brought in Beit Yosef, who first wrote that some compare mixed wine to actual temed, which requires a quarter-log of wine; and he contradicts this, for there it is mere foam, but in mixed wine it depends on taste; and some hold it is nullified in six; and likewise explicitly in Rashba. And behold explicitly: if one compares mixed wine to temed, six are not required; for in temed specifically a quarter-log of wine is required, and so explicitly in Rashbam. And what Shach brought as proof from Rashba regarding "stopped in a wheel," etc. — there is no proof, for there it explicitly deals with grape-skins of non-Jews, and likewise grape-skins of non-Jews here, which are already forbidden and are worse. And know that Rashba wrote in that case of "stopped": according to those who forbid in mixed wine up to sixty, here also sixty are required; and behold in temed it is explicit that he poured three and three and a half came — it is permitted.
