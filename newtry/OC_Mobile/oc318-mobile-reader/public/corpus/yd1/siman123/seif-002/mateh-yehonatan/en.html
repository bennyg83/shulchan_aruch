(There, seif 20) A winepress that is closed and full, etc. This law is from the Rosh, and he holds that which Rav Huna said, "I only said this when he returned the press-boards," etc. — also according to the latter mishnah; therefore he wrote that also according to the latter mishnah it is not wine, etc. And one may say: if so, why did the Gemara need to answer and establish the mishnah "he descended to the pit — what is in the pit is forbidden and the rest is permitted" — specifically according to the first mishnah? For behold also according to the latter mishnah the rest is permitted, for according to the Rosh what is drawn from the winepress to the press-boards has no stringency and is forbidden only what is inside the press-boards and below. If so, even according to the latter mishnah it can be established. And certainly, if its pit was diminished — for then there is drawing from the winepress to the pit — it must be established according to the first mishnah. And there is a difficulty: why establish "and the rest is permitted" specifically according to the first mishnah? And there is an answer: since we conclude that it deals when its pit was diminished, therefore certainly it is forbidden according to the first mishnah also for what is inside the winepress, for the whole reason of the Rosh is that since wine is not drawn from the winepress to the pit except from the press-boards, therefore what is inside the winepress is not called wine. But if its pit was diminished so that wine from the pit itself reached the press-boards, it is as though wine was drawn from the winepress without any intervening vessel; therefore also what is inside the winepress is forbidden, for this is drawing from the winepress itself to the pit; therefore it is established according to the first mishnah. And behold all this is according to the one who holds that a stream is not a connection — who must establish the mishnah when its pit was diminished. But according to the one who holds that a stream is a connection, it appears that it works even if its pit was not diminished, since the Gemara said "learn from this that a stream is a connection" — and still it did not occur to him that it deals when its pit was diminished; and nevertheless it works. And this is what one should say: for it appears that even according to the one who holds that a stream is a connection, it must be established that its pit was diminished; for otherwise there would be a difficulty: it should have said and established the mishnah also according to the first mishnah, as above — that since its pit was not diminished there is no drawing from the winepress to the pit, only the main drawing was from the press-boards. And it must be that certainly this appears to the Gemara as strained to explain "and the rest is permitted" as referring to above the press-boards, for the language implies that everything above the pit is permitted; therefore it must be established according to the first mishnah. And one should not challenge: behold also according to the first mishnah wine descending from the press-boards is forbidden because of the stream, since a stream is a connection — one may say that the tanna did not descend to explain except where there is wine upon which it would be forbidden because of touch itself; but above the pit it is not wine even if a non-Jew touched it, and even though it is forbidden because of the stream which is due to mixture — from this the tanna did not speak at all. But according to the first mishnah, also the dripping descending from the press-boards is forbidden because of touch itself, for it is called wine like what is in the pit; if so, it should not have said "and the rest is permitted"; therefore it must be established according to the first mishnah.
