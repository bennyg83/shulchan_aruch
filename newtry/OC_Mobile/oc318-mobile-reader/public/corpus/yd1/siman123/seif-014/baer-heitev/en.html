Taz writes that this is Rabbeinu Tam's view and questions it from Chullin 110a, where Rami bar Tamri used grape marc he found to roast an udder, and answered Rav Chisda that it had aged twelve months. According to Rabbeinu Tam, who told him that the marc had first been soaked in water? One must say it was known that in that city they discarded grape marc only after first soaking it.
That was drawn. It is not called wine for purposes of prohibition until it begins to be drawn toward the bottom of the vat. Shach. Beit Yosef cites Orchot Chaim that if grape marc and skins were burned within twelve months, their ashes are nevertheless permitted. Shach questions this from Rivash and the Gemara, which imply that ashes of ordinary non-Jewish wine are forbidden, like wood of idolatry. Perhaps Beit Yosef refers to marc that had first been soaked in water.
