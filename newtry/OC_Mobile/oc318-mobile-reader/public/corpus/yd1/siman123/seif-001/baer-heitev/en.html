Chavot Yair writes that if a soldier threatens a Jew to drink ordinary non-Jewish wine or have his ear cut off, and the threat is mere bluster, he should not hasten to drink. If he can save himself with money, he must spend nearly all his property rather than transgress. If he truly fears for his life, however, it is permitted and he incurs no sin. Bach writes that nowadays, even if a non-Jew pours wine before an idol in the manner of libation, since non-Jews outside Eretz Yisrael are not considered true idolaters but merely continue ancestral custom, it is not called libation. Beit Yosef writes that where non-Jews do not drink wine until a priest comes and casts their water into it, this would seem to be libation, but one may say it is merely a mixture of forbidden water, which is nullified in the wine.
