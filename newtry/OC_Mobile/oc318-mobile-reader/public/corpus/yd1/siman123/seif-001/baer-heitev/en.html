The decree — the Taz explains that because of their daughters they forbade it for drinking, and because of the decree of yayin nesech they forbade it even for benefit. (A soldier who told a Jew he must drink yayin nesech with him or he would cut off his ear: if the things were said merely as an exaggeration, certainly one should not rush to drink; and if he can save himself by giving money, he is obligated to scatter almost all his money rather than transgress; but if he was truly afraid for his life that he would do as he threatened, it is permitted and he bears no sin — Chavot Yair siman 183.) And nowadays the Bach wrote that even if they poured it before their idol as the manner of libation, since we establish that non-Jews outside the Land are not actual idolaters but merely follow their ancestral custom, therefore it is not called libation. And the Beit Yosef wrote: however those non-Jews who do not drink wine until the priest comes and sprinkles their water on it with the wine — it appeared that this would be libation; but one may say this is only a mixture of forbidden water, and water in wine is nullified.
