And some are lenient even in this. See Baer Heitev and responsa Peri Tevuah, siman 1, concerning the widespread practice of tavernkeepers selling ordinary non-Jewish wine; he writes that the established practice may be left in place. See there. See also Toras Moshe, s.k. 8, Hilchos Yayin Nesech, and Noda BiYehuda, second edition, siman 69. A non-Jew was walking past two vessels of wine vinegar standing before us, lifted them, and set them elsewhere, thereby lifting and shaking them. In a case of loss, one may permit this, for it is no worse than a non-Jew's contact through another object, which is not forbidden for us. This is especially so with vinegar, for although we are not expert in determining vinegar, it nevertheless remains at least doubtful vinegar. One may therefore be lenient where he did not touch it directly, but merely lifted and shook it, since one should not rule stringently in a case of doubt. See also siman 70, where he writes that if it was wine and the vessel was full, one should be stringent out of concern that he may have touched it, since the Jew did not intend to guard it and ensure that he would not touch it. This is especially so at a wedding, when people are occupied and drunkenness is common. See there.
