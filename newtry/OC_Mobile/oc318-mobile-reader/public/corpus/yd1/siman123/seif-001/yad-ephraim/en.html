And some are lenient even in this. Be'er Heitev and responsum Pri Tevuah siman 1 regarding the practice that spread of merchants mixing stam yeinam — we established for them a custom — see there. In Turei Zahav seif kaf 8, section 5 of yayin nesech; and see in Nachalat Binyamin, Ma'amar Torah siman 69: a non-Jew was walking, and two vessels of vinegar-wine stood before us; he lifted them and placed them elsewhere in a manner where there was lifting and rubbing — in a place of loss there is reason to permit, for it is no worse than the touch of a non-Jew through something else, which is not forbidden for us; and all the more so vinegar, for even though we are not expert, nevertheless from a doubt of vinegar one does not emerge [to forbid], and one should be lenient at least when there is no actual touch, only lifting and rubbing unintentionally, not to be stringent in a doubt — see there siman 70: if it was wine and the vessel was full, one should be stringent from concern lest he touched with intent — not that the Jew intended to guard it and watch that he not touch; and especially at weddings when people are busy and drunkenness is common, one should be stringent — see there.
