(Siman 123, Shulchan Aruch, seif 18) If he filled a cup of wine from the winepress, etc. It appears to me that all this applies specifically when we saw him fill from the winepress, but if we did not see this, we do not ordinarily suspect that perhaps he filled a cup. The Beit Yosef holds that this law applies even according to Rabbeinu Tam, who explains that "he began to draw" means that the wine began to descend into the pit. Nevertheless, Rabbeinu Tam came to dispute only what Rashi wrote, that drawing within the vat itself constitutes drawing. But if one filled a cup of wine from the winepress and intended to strain it, Rabbeinu Tam also agrees that this constitutes drawing. According to this, there is no difficulty with what Tosafos wrote on daf 55b, s.v. Amar Rav Huna, that according to Rabbeinu Tam's explanation we do not read "and it was filled." One might ask that perhaps according to Rabbeinu Tam as well it must be full, for otherwise we should be concerned that perhaps he filled a cup from the winepress and intended to strain it. Rather, it must be that ordinarily, if we did not see him fill a cup, there is no concern at all that perhaps he did so. Tosafos therefore correctly wrote that we do not read "it was filled," etc.
