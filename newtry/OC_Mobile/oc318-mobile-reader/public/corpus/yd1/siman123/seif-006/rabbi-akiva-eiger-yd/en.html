Thus, here the matter depends only on the degree of dilution. This is also explicit in the Ran, cited by Beit Yosef. He first records that some compare diluted wine to grape-water, which requires one-quarter wine, but rejects the comparison because there the result is merely a sharp taste, whereas diluted wine depends on taste. Some hold that it is nullified in six parts, and the Rashba likewise states this explicitly. It is therefore clear that if diluted wine is compared to grape-water, six parts are not required, because grape-water specifically requires one-quarter wine. This is also explicit in the Rashbam. The Shach's proof from the Rashba concerning grapes pressed in a wheel is no proof, because there the discussion expressly concerns a non-Jew's lees and grape skins, which had already become forbidden and are therefore more stringent. Indeed, the Rashba writes regarding that case of pressing that according to those who require sixty parts for diluted wine, sixty are likewise required there. Yet concerning grape-water it is explicit that if three measures were poured in and three and one-half emerged, it is permitted. It must therefore be that the present case is more stringent because the substance had already become forbidden. See the Rashba, and this is clear.
