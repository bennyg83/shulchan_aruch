And wine that solidified, etc. And it is written in Haggahot Asheri from Or Zarua, chapter 2 of Avodah Zarah, that wine that solidified has no [issur] of a non-Jew's touch, etc.; and likewise if a non-Jew touched it after it melted, it has [the issur] of a non-Jew's touch — end of his words. And Darkei Moshe brings it. And it appears to me that according to what we rule that a non-Jew's touch forbids in wine that turned to vinegar, because we are not experts when it is called vinegar, as below in se'if katan 13 — the same law applies here regarding wine that soured, for we are not experts, and a non-Jew's touch forbids in it. And so it is implied from the words of the Rosh, who wrote regarding vinegar that Rabbeinu Tam was angry at R' Meshullam who permitted a non-Jew's touch in vinegar, because we are not experts in the nature of vinegar: some call it vinegar and some drink it and recite Kiddush over it; and regarding wine that soured the Amoraim dispute in the chapter "HaMocher Peirot" when it is called vinegar; and therefore if you come to permit a non-Jew's touch in vinegar, ruin would result from it — end of his words. And Rashi too explained in chapter 2 of Avodah Zarah (daf 30, end of amud a) on "chamra de'akrim" — it turned to vinegar; and so Rashbam explained in the chapter "HaMocher Peirot" (Bava Batra daf 95, end of amud b); and so it is proven in the Gemara there. And it appears that for this reason the Rav omitted these words of Haggahot Asheri that he brought in Darkei Moshe. And further it appears that there is a scribal error in the gloss and in Darkei Moshe: "wine that was kerem" should say "that solidified" with a shin; and so it is implied from what it says afterwards "after it melted," etc.; and so the Bach copied in siman 13 the language of this gloss: "and wine that solidified."
