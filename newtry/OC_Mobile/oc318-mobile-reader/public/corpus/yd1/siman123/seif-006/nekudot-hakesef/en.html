However, this is difficult for me: if the case here is one in which it is forbidden even though he did not touch it, etc. The Tur explicitly discusses a case in which, even though the non-Jew did not touch it, the wine that flowed out is forbidden because it came out through his force. Nevertheless, the Taz was not precise. Being occupied with treading permits the wine only for benefit, because one who is occupied with the treading does not intend to pour a libation. Thus, even if he touched the wine unintentionally with his body, it is permitted for benefit. With regard to force exerted unintentionally, however, we require that he not intend the wine to emerge. If he intends that the wine emerge through his force, then although he is occupied with treading and does not intend to pour a libation, the wine is forbidden for drinking, since he knows that it is wine. What difference does it make that he is occupied with treading? Even without intent to pour a libation, it is nevertheless forbidden for drinking. This is what the Tur and the Mechaber write below, at the beginning of siman 125: if it came through his force unintentionally, because he did not know that it was wine, it is permitted even for drinking. This shows that the permission applies specifically when his force was exerted unintentionally and he did not know that it was wine. All the more so, if it emerged through his force without any intention to exert force, it is permitted even for drinking. It is not permitted, however, when he knew that it was wine and merely lacked intent to pour a libation. A clear proof is found in the case of a small beam press, namely, a winepress in which the grapes are pressed with a beam. If a non-Jew operates it, the wine that flows outward is forbidden, as stated explicitly in the Gemara and by all the poskim, and as cited below at the beginning of siman 125 in the Tur and Beit Yosef; see there. Yet he is occupied with pressing and does not intend to pour a libation. If so, this should have been considered force exerted unintentionally. One cannot distinguish that the case there is different because he presses with his hand, for the Rosh and the poskim explicitly indicate that even bodily contact without intent is permitted for drinking, just as when he treads with his feet. Rather, the explanation is certainly as I have stated, and in my opinion it is straightforward. I later found that the Taz himself gives this answer in siman 125, paragraph 17, although his wording there makes the answer appear strained. In truth, it is simple and is proven by the case of the small beam press, as explained. It appears that he forgot that case and did not examine the beginning of the Tur in siman 125, but only its conclusion. That is why he found it necessary to ask from the Tur's statement at the end of siman 125 that force exerted unintentionally is permitted for drinking, which is the wording of the Rashba, instead of asking from the Tur's plain statement at the beginning of siman 125, which is an explicit Gemara accepted by all the poskim. Moreover, he should not have directed his question against the Tur in siman 123, but against the Gemara's own ruling concerning the small beam press and against all the poskim. Evidently, he did not examine the beginning of the Tur in siman 125, and everything the Tur wrote there escaped his notice.
