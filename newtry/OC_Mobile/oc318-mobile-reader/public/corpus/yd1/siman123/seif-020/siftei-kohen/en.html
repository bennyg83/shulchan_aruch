What is in the winepress does not have the status of wine. — Since the grapes and wine flowed together into the basket, and from there only the wine flowed to the pit — for the matter is similar to one who takes grapes and wine together from the winepress and puts them in another place, and from there the wine flows from the grapes; it is clear that what is in the winepress is not called wine just because wine flowed from that place — so the Rosh.
And if a non-Jew touched the basket it is forbidden. — And the reason what is in the winepress is not forbidden even if he did not return from the basket to the winepress — because of nitzuk [unbroken stream] — see Tosafot (Avodah Zarah daf 56 side 2) and Darkhei Moshe; and see above end of siman 126.
And if there are pits and skins, etc. That is, although we hold below in siman 134 that yayin nesech forbids in any amount, it is explained there that this is specifically with its own kind; but not with its own kind it depends on noten taam, namely sixty. Pits and skins are not the same kind as wine. Although there is also wine there, which is its own kind, we say: remove its own kind as though it were not there, and the different kind is greater than it and nullifies it, as above in siman 98 seif 2. So it is in the poskim. According to this, since the Rav wrote in siman 134 seif 2 that the practice is to be lenient, that all stam yeinam nowadays is nullified in sixty, the same applies here, for certainly contact of a non-Jew is no worse than stam yeinam, as is implied by the Rav at the beginning of this siman, that they have one law. So too it is in responsum Mahari"l siman 38; see above siman 124 s.k. 21. This is also explicit from Tosafot, Avodah Zarah 56b, s.v. aval, and from Mordechai there, who wrote that according to Rabbeinu Tam, who holds stam yeinam is nullified in sixty and even its own kind does not forbid, here, when returning the basket to the winepress, presumably there is sixty in the press. It is explicit from their words that everything in the press, whether wine or pits and skins, combines to nullify; see there. So too it is explicit from Tosafot, Mordechai, and Agudah in the last chapter of Avodah Zarah, and I brought their words below in siman 126 s.k. 13, that wine forbidden by contact of a non-Jew is also nullified in sixty like stam yeinam. So wrote Rabbeinu Yerucham, netiv 17 part 1: Rabbeinu Tam wrote that specifically actual yayin nesech forbids, but stam yeinam is in sixty, and so too contact of a non-Jew; and so wrote Ramban, etc. Likewise Tosafot and Rosh explicitly write in the name of Rabbeinu Tam in the last chapter of Avodah Zarah, in the Mishnah that yayin nesech is forbidden and forbids in any amount, that contact of a non-Jew has the law of stam yeinam, which is nullified in sixty. This is also explicit from their words there and from Ran there and Rashba in Torat HaBayit regarding what they challenged Rabbeinu Tam from the case of the inspectors; see there. So too Isur VeHeter, kelal 23 din 14, wrote that there is no distinction between wine of a non-Jew and contact of a non-Jew. It is somewhat difficult why the Rav did not gloss here that everything combines, and that even if there are no pits and skins it is nullified in wine. One may answer that since this appeared obvious to him, he relied on the primary law below in siman 134; also one may say that pits and skins are not meant specifically. But the difficulty on Ateret Zekenim is greater, for he wrote explicitly that the wine does not combine since it is its own kind, and that it is nullified specifically by pits and skins, and he explained the reason mentioned; but this is not so for us, as I explained. See the end of siman 126.
