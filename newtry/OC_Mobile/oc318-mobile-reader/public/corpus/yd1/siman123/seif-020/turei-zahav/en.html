Maharalbach wrote in his responsum siman 48: if one makes wine from dry raisins — namely by crushing them and adding water to them until it ferments, at which point it is called wine — even if the dry grapes remain in the water, after having soaked there for three days: for it is only with wine pressed in a winepress, where it is not customary to leave the grapes there together with the wine, that [leaving them in] is considered as still pressed within the grapes; but here, since it is customary to leave the dry raisins in the barrel where the wine is produced, it is fully considered wine — and drawing [hamshacha] is not required.
