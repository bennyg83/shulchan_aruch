A winepress that is closed, etc. This is from the Tur's words; and to understand the matters we will quote the Rosh's words in perek R' Yishmael, Rav Huna said: I only said this when he did not return the press-boards, etc.; even though Rav Huna said that once it begins to be drawn it becomes wine, and if so even without returning the press-boards the winepress in which one treads is forbidden — Rav Huna here spoke to the matter of the first mishnah and informed us that even though the winepress has no status of wine to forbid by the touch of a non-Jew, nevertheless it is forbidden in mixtures; and there is a difference for the latter mishnah in a closed and full winepress. And it further appears that Rav Huna here properly refers to the latter mishnah, and wine in the winepress is not forbidden by drawing when grapes and wine together were drawn to the press-boards and from there wine alone to the pit, for the matter is clear that what is in the winepress is not called wine because wine was drawn from that place; and now Rav Huna's matter is according to its plain meaning: wine in the press-boards is forbidden by the touch of a non-Jew because it began to be drawn, or because of the non-Jew's force according to Rashbam; and Rav Huna holds that a stream is not a connection. And because his words were said in Bava Batra after they learned that mishnah "he descended to the pit," the listener erred that he spoke according to the first mishnah and said "learn from this that a stream is a connection"; and according to his words he answered him — end of his words. The explanation of his words appears in the first answer: he thought that if the winepress is open, even though grape-skins go from it with the wine, the beginning of drawing is called drawing since afterward in that dripping it goes to the basket and there wine alone is drawn — he considered also what is in the winepress as this; and so is the implication of Rashi's words in the Gemara (daf 55): "likewise in a closed winepress," etc., "and the wine descended and it is drawn immediately" — the word "immediately" indicates this that I mentioned. It is impossible to explain Rav Huna's words according to the latter mishnah from what he said "he returned what is in the basket," etc. — it implies the winepress is not closed but discharges to the basket; and on this was the difficulty: behold it was already forbidden what is in the winepress without return. Therefore he explained that he refers to the first mishnah, that even though it is open there is no drawing here; and there is a difference for us in closed and full, that there is no drawing from itself, as above; and afterward there is no drawing from it outside, for behold it is closed; and if a drop of yayin nesech fell there, it forbids what is in the winepress because of a mixture of yayin nesech; but Rav Huna cannot deal with this, for he said "he returned it" — it implies he deals with an open one; and afterward he wrote "and further," etc., that drawing does not begin in an open one except from the press-boards; and if so it works well in a closed and full winepress at the time of the non-Jew's treading — from itself there was no drawing; and afterward he opened it and grape-skins and wine went from there, and still there is no drawing on the basket; and this explanation works better, that it deals plainly according to the latter mishnah, and he informs us that nevertheless there is prohibition because of a mixture of wine in the basket; and this that the Tur said "a winepress that is closed and full — from itself there was no actual drawing; and afterward he opened it and grape-skins and wine descended from there, and nevertheless drawing is only from the basket" — this is exactly the Rosh's last answer; except there is still reason to challenge: behold there is prohibition for the winepress from the side of "a stream is a connection" to the basket. Granted according to the first explanation I would say the reason for the basket's prohibition is because of the stream's connection to the pit — from itself the winepress would be a fit stream for libation, as Tosafot wrote there; which is not so now, that the basket is forbidden because of wine where drawing begins there — if so, let us say the winepress is forbidden because of the stream to the basket; and even though Rav Huna holds a stream is not a connection, as the Rosh wrote, nevertheless we establish "a stream is a connection" in siman 127. This is not a difficulty, for here the Tur did not come to rule only whether there is prohibition of the touch of a non-Jew from the wine itself, as he is precise in his wording that he wrote "the winepress has no status of wine" — namely from itself touch of a non-Jew does not harm it; but because of the stream to the basket the Tur has no dealing with this now, for its law is explicit in siman 127, where there is a dispute; and here everyone agrees that there is at least prohibition from the side of a mixture of yayin nesech if he returned it to the winepress. And there is great astonishment here that Tehillim HeChasidim daf 332 plainly mentioned this: "But I wonder at Rav Huna's words: why did he say they decreed upon it the status of wine for the first mishnah more to forbid in mixtures of wine from the touch of a non-Jew? On the contrary, if in touch itself it is not forbidden, how will it be forbidden in a mixture of wine forbidden because of the touch of a non-Jew? The matter stands on earth and the convert in heaven" — end of his words; and in Perishah he did not descend to what we mentioned and toiled to expand on this in vain. And there is reason to challenge: since according to the Tur there is no drawing at all in the winepress — neither inside, since it is closed and full, nor when he opens it afterward, since clear wine does not come out — what is this that the Tur wrote above from Rabbeinu Tam in a closed and full winepress, that Rabbeinu Tam wrote to decree before drawing lest [one come to act] after drawing? For in a winepress drawing will never come, ever. Granted according to Rabbeinu Tam himself — investigate, for he holds the main drawing is outside the winepress, as Tosafot and the Rosh wrote in his name; and it is possible he holds that immediately outside the winepress drawing begins, as I wrote above in the name of Rashi — therefore this decree properly applies in one place, namely in the winepress itself, since even in a closed one one must open it afterward and there will be drawing; which is not so according to the Tur's words, that in a closed winepress drawing will never come at all — what decree applies here? For it is certainly not reasonable at all to decree from place to place, namely winepress lest basket where there is drawing; therefore it appears to me the main text is as some books bring in Beit Yosef: decree lest it is not closed; and with this we properly decree lest not closed, where there would be drawing in the winepress itself, as above. It is found that they decreed winepress lest winepress; and this is reasonable to decree, as it appears to me.
