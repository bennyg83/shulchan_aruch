(Seif 20) There is no [prohibition] on what is in the winepress. Investigate, for he did not bring any dissentient on this; and it appears the Rosh's view on this is singular, for all poskim wrote that Rav Huna refers to the first mishnah. And for us there is a difference according to the first mishnah in a closed and full winepress when he put other wine into it — see there how they strained themselves on this — therefore he does not hold like the Rosh; also in Ramban's words that Rashba brought in Terumat HaDeshen (daf 32a) it is explicit that he disagrees with the Rosh.
(Shach seif kaf 43) But in truth it appears to me. From the Tur there is no proof, for since measuring by hand is permitted in benefit, so too by foot; but according to the Rambam, measuring by hand is rabbinically [forbidden], and as Beit Yosef concludes — if so, it appears that even by treading with the foot it should be forbidden in benefit. However, since the Rambam did not mention the law of the foot, if so we must say that measuring is called libation; therefore he included it in the wording whether by his hand or by other limbs that are accustomed to pour libations, and the foot is included — and as Shach himself wrote; if so, it appears it is like the hand and being occupied does not help.
(In Be'er Heitev) Because he is occupied with treading. This is according to his view (siman 114 seif kaf 50) that he disagrees with Beit Yosef, and his view is like the Rambam that measuring by hand is permitted in benefit. And I have a difficulty: if the Rambam holds that measuring by hand is permitted in benefit, and a fortiori this regarding what the Rambam omitted — measuring by hand — for he relied on the case of oil, as Shach wrote there; if so, the Rambam must hold that unintentional wine and unintentional touch are equivalent; if so, it is impossible to say that treading is forbidden because of his force, for behold his force unintentionally regarding wine is permitted for drinking, as is explicit in Noda B'Yehuda. And investigate.
