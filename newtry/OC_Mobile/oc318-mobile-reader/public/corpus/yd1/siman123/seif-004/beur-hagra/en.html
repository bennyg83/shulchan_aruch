Wine into which one mixes, etc. Yerushalmi ; R' Yitzchak bar Nachman in the name of R' Yehoshua ben Levi said: sweet and bitter together ; they do not have status of uncovered wine; R' Simon in the name of R' Yehoshua ben Levi: bitter, sharp, and sweet ; they do not have status of uncovered wine and not of yayin nesech; R' Simon explains: sharp is conditum, etc.; and we say in Pesikta parashat Yitro: R' Abba compared Torah to conditum ; just as conditum has wine, has honey, has pepper, etc.; and it is called anomalin in the Gemara there; and Ramban wrote: specifically one-third honey and one-third pepper, as Yerushalmi wrote: Bar Yudna made conditum ; he asked the rabbis and they forbade it; and not so R' Yehuda in the name of R' Yehoshua ben Levi: bitter, sharp, and sweet have no status of uncovered wine ; rabbis of Caesarea in the name of R' Yehuda bar Titoi: in that which one sharpens one to three; but Rashba wrote: there is no such opinion, rather whenever its taste changed; and he wrote there: one who is stringent ; is praiseworthy.
(Likkut) If it was changed etc. Not like the Rambam who wrote even a little honey, because it is not fit to libate; and it is not so, for nevertheless it is fit for them, for they offer even dates of a palm, which is forbidden for the altar; and they said mixed wine has the law of yayin nesech even though it is not fit for the altar, as is written (in the Sifrei) "you shall live" etc.; and in chapter 6 of Bava Basra (97b) to exclude mixed etc. Ran and Rosh (thus far).
And the same law, etc. From what is written in the Gemara: one wine and pepper ; therefore pepper alone suffices; and they did not say in Yerushalmi honey and pepper except because that is their custom. There in Terumat HaDeshen; and from our Gemara, that they did not give a measure, it implies like Rashba's words above; and Rashi there, "one," etc.; and Beit Yosef wrote: the same applies to honey alone; and likewise in Rashba's responsum.
