Be'er Heitev in the name of Shach, who challenged Orach Chayyim from what Rivash wrote, etc.; and see in Tiferet Yisrael, who wrote that grape-skins are different, since if they dried they are permitted because no moisture of wine remains; if so, if one burned them to ash certainly no moisture of wine remains, and this is better than temed that stood twelve months, which is permitted; and accordingly it appears that also the coals from grape-skins and the like are permitted, for certainly no moisture remains inside the coals — end of his words — see there. [And likewise he challenged him in Dagul MeRevavah — see there; and see in responsum Chatam Sofer siman 117, who wondered at him from Gemara Avodah Zarah 4:65b, where it is explained that Rashi's words are correct, and from there is also a contradiction to the words of Magen Avraham siman 500 seif kaf 11 in his explanation of "he wants its existence." And see further the discussion of oil made from almonds growing on grape-skins of grapes (the whole craft is explained there), which are yayin nesech; and he wrote: I know no place for prohibition — if because we say the force of wine in grape-skins was transformed and made into oil, there is reason to permit; and Magen Avraham siman 216 seif kaf 3 — investigate; and he properly ruled in Chak Yakov siman 467 (seif kaf 16): everything that changed to a permitted thing, such as meat to honey, was permitted; and here wine changed to oil. And indeed the Gaon Mekor Chayyim there challenged the measure and brought from Gemara Kerisos 5b; and in Machatzit HaShekel he was not precise, etc. (and he already mentioned this also at the end of siman 114 — see there). And Chak Yakov's words are correct and true; and if because of a mixture of wine — behold many Jews tasted it and did not detect wine taste — end of his words, the simple meaning is that it is complete heter. And it appears there is a difficulty from below seif 24; and it is possible to distinguish — and see further in that responsum siman 70.]
