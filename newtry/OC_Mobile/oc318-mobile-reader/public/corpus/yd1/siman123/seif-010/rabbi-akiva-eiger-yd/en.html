But in truth, it appears to me that there is no proof from the Tur. Since the Tur holds that when a non-Jew measured the wine with his hand it remains permitted for benefit, the same would apply when he treaded it with his foot. According to the Rambam, however, who holds that measuring with the hand renders it forbidden for benefit, as Beit Yosef concludes, it would appear that treading with the foot should likewise render it forbidden for benefit. Nevertheless, since the Rambam did not mention the law of the foot, if we say that he considers measuring to be a form of libation, we must conclude that he included it in his general wording, whether with the hand or with another limb customarily used for libation. The foot is included, as the Shach himself writes. It therefore appears that the foot is comparable to the hand, and preoccupation with the act does not provide a leniency.
