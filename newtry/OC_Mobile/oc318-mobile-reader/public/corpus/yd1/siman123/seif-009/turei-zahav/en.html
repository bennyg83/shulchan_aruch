The first and second steeping are forbidden even, etc. The Tur writes: “It always remains subject to the law of a non-Jew’s contact until it becomes so inferior that people do not ordinarily drink it.” End quote. Maharshal explains that “always” means even when only a very small amount of wine was found, but nevertheless this refers specifically to the first and second steepings. End quote. It appears to me, however, that this is what he means: It always remains subject to the law of a non-Jew’s contact, even if its quality as a beverage is poor, until it becomes so extremely inferior that people do not drink it at all. The Prishah explains that this applies even if it was steeped many times, but this is imprecise.
Because it may be confused, etc. This implies that the same law applies even to tamad made from wine dregs, since the reason is identical. This is the view of the Raavad cited by the Tur, and the Drishah ruled accordingly. If so, his ruling in seif 11 that the second steeping is permitted is astonishing. According to what we rule in seif 11, which follows the view of Rabbeinu Yonah, we do not follow this “there are those who say.”
