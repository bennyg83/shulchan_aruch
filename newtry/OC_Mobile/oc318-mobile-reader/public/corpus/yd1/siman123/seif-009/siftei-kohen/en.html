First and second time forbidden. For the grape-pulp absorbs from the water and wine comes out from them; therefore there is always status of the touch of non-Jews until it is inferior that people do not drink it — end of his words; Tur in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah. And Maharshal wrote in his hagahot to the Tur: explanation — always, even if he did not find its full measure, only a tiny amount; but nevertheless it deals specifically with the first and second time — examine carefully — end of his words. And in Perishah seif kaf 27 he challenged him, for the wording "always" does not imply this; also nearby the Tur wrote (and the author in seif 11) in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah: grape-skins of non-Jews are always forbidden, and he explains there that in every case it deals, even if soaked many times — end of his words. And to me it is like Maharshal's words, for otherwise he should not have said "first and second time forbidden," since it is always forbidden. And further, behold in Rabbeinu Yonah's words that the Rosh brought, the wording "always" is not mentioned; and from what Rabbeinu Yonah wrote regarding grape-skins, on the contrary from there is proof, for there he explained and wrote "always forbidden, even if soaked many times"; and here he did not explain except on the contrary he wrote "first and second time forbidden" — it implies first and second time specifically. And this appears to be the author's view, who wrote "first and second time forbidden" and omitted the word "always"; and so it appears explicitly in Turei Zahav: first and second time specifically forbidden and no more. Nevertheless it appears this is specifically when he found its full measure; and Maharshal deals with such a case. But if he found more than its full measure, even after the first and second time it is forbidden, for behold there is wine in it. And it is astonishing, for in Derishah itself seif kaf 23 he wrote that after one and two times Rabbeinu Yonah holds like the Raavad, who holds that there is no status of the touch of a non-Jew unless he put three measures of water and found four; if so, perforce what he wrote regarding grape-skins "always forbidden" means specifically there, for there he explicitly explains thus, which is not so here; and as I explained.
