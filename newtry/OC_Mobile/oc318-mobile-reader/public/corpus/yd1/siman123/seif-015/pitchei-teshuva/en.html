And full. See in Derishah, who wrote for one explanation that "full" is not precise, because even according to Rashi drawing is not called on the winepress until he turns aside with his hands the grape-skins and pulp to one side. And see in responsum Zikaron Yosef siman 6, who wrote that he relied on this, and did not protest against the people of his country who practiced that non-Jews brought grapes in baskets and poured them on the closed winepress that has a slope, and the non-Jews touched here and there with their hands even though the winepress is not full and wine is drawn on the slope toward the hole — because according to Derishah's words there was no prohibition here, and for them it is like a time of pressure. And he further wrote: nevertheless I saw their wickedness, that after the first treading they swept with a broom all the grapes and grape-skins scattered in the winepress onto the press-board; and they again arranged, etc.; and since he turned aside the grape-skins to the side of the press-board, behold clear wine was gathered on the side of its slope and this is drawing on the winepress; and nevertheless they were not careful about the touch of non-Jews in grapes to put them on the press-board, and therefore the wine was forbidden. And nevertheless there is reason to side with permitting, because even according to the commentators who follow Rashi's view, that through drawing a little wine the status of wine applies to the whole winepress — this is specifically on wine mixed with grapes and grape-skins on them, not on the clusters and grape-skins and pulp and wine stored in them, for those have no status of wine at all; and that everything was forbidden was because there is much wine in the winepress and we are concerned lest there are not sixty in grape-skins and pulp and wine stored in them against what was forbidden of the clear wine (it appears also cold-in-cold applies, and then rinsing suffices; one may say that presumably there are leaves, and the Rosh according to his view who holds in Chullin 112 regarding "if there are leaves in it, all of it is forbidden" — even in a living creature — and Shach brought this above siman 91 seif kaf 21; but there is a difficulty, for behold also Ran's view is so, that everything was forbidden, as explained in Beit Yosef, and he wrote that also Rashba's view inclines thus; and they do not hold so there — and one should investigate this) — and this is specifically in the first treading; but if there are not certainly sixty, nevertheless since stam wine is from their words, one may say it is similar to what spilled in the second treading, where only a little wine remained that certainly has sixty against it; and even in siman 98, since no person in the world can know. There are further branches to permit — see there, where he expanded on this.
