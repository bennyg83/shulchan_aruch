It constitutes drawing — see Baer Heitev; and responsum Pri Megadim Ashel Avraham part 2 siman 111: homeowners bought a treaded vat from a non-Jew to make kosher wine, but the lord’s servants first took the tithe owed to the lord by inserting a basket into the vat and drawing clear wine from it as the required amount. As is explained in siman 123, if one places a basket it constitutes drawing, since clear wine enters the basket and separates from the grape-skins. He concluded that l’chatchila it is strictly forbidden to rely on this to make kosher wine from such a vat into which a basket was placed to take the tithe; but b’dieved, if a Jew witnessed that at the time they placed the basket into the vat they did not touch the wine by hand, one may permit it in case of great loss, combining the consideration of all the people involved — even though for each individual it is not a great loss — and one can also say that for each individual it is a great loss, since he would need to buy kosher wine at high cost; and in this one may rely on the Rama in siman 124, that any touching of theirs is considered unintentional, etc. But if no Jew was present when they placed the basket, and one must be concerned and it is likely they touched the wine by hand, one should not be lenient even b’dieved. And see there, where he wrote that in Tzemach Tzedek siman 12 he ruled that one may not permit b’dieved where it is the practice to place a basket in the vat to draw clear wine, except in a place where they have no winepresses among the vineyards, etc.; and as for the doubt lest priests come there who customarily request to be given wine and have small vessels to draw clear wine — one should not forbid, for that is merely a doubtful entry, etc. And see there where he was asked about one who bought wine from a vat that had a spigot, and he ruled leniently in case of great loss or pressing need when no other wine is available; but from that point forward it was proper to ban and excommunicate those who buy wine from such a vat unless they seal the spigot and also place a cloth over the vat and guard and seal it so that the non-Jew cannot draw with small vessels — see there.
