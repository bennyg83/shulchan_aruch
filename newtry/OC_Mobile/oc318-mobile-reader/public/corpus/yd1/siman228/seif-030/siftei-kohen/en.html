And above I wrote, etc. Meaning, above in seif 21 Rama wrote that according to some, four people who swore together to perform an act are considered to have sworn with public consent. Rama holds that even if all later regret it, they cannot be released because their regrets are not identical. See above, seif katan 57. Ramban follows his own position, as Rivash explains, distinguishing a ban from an oath. Rivash, however, writes that the established practice permitting communal bans without formal release rests on the fact that the community imposes them according to its custom. That reasoning applies equally to a communal oath. Even if they say, 'We impose a ban and swear,' they themselves may release it without an opening or regret, because that was the implicit basis of the oath. Rama rules this above in seif 25 even where an oath was mentioned together with the ban. Here he follows the Mechaber and states that even if everyone regrets, they must find an opening and have three outsiders release the oath. It appears that Rama distinguishes between an oath proclaimed collectively in the usual communal formula, such as when the prayer leader says on the community's behalf, 'We swear and impose a ban,' even if everyone answers amen, and individual members each saying explicitly, 'I swear regarding this matter.' The former follows communal custom, as Rivash writes. The latter is an oath with public consent and requires formal release. Ateret Zekenim must be understood this way.
For there is one who says, etc. Ateret Zekenim writes that even this authority agrees in the case of a community, because every communal act is treated as though it was initially made subject to this condition.
Nevertheless, some disagree, etc. According to Rama's understanding, Ramban appears to contradict himself. The dissenting opinion comes from Rivash, siman 461, who writes that Ramban ruled likewise in Mishpat HaCherem, while the Mechaber's first view is also from Responsa Ramban. It therefore appears necessary to distinguish. Rivash discusses the entire community swearing together in the ordinary communal manner, which is not treated as public consent because they act according to established custom. Responsa Ramban concerns each person swearing individually, which is public consent, and therefore release is impossible unless everyone agrees. The matter still requires study.
