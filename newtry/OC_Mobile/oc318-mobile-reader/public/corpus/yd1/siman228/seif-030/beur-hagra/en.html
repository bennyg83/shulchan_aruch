A community, etc. The Rivash wrote that because in their times they were not accustomed to mention language of oath in their bans, when they swore and placed under ban using language of oath, it was a new matter; therefore they require release by a sage. See above seif 25.
And they will release them, etc. For none of the townspeople can release it, since it is like his own vows, which one cannot release, as written in chapter 2 of Negaim. In the Yerushalmi: if one said, “Konam, my benefit is forbidden to my townspeople,” he may not ask release from an elder who is there; and as above, seif 5 and 6. It says there: if a woman vowed and her husband heard and did not annul it, it is obvious that he cannot annul it as husband; but may he annul it as an elder? What are we dealing with? If with vows between him and her, they are his own vows, etc.
And those releasing may, etc. For the question in Nedarim, regarding one man, whether the husband, etc., was only because it is written “her,” etc.; see Rivash siman 461.
And see above, I wrote, etc. It appears one can prove what is written here from Gittin 46a: what is Rabbi Yehudah’s reason, etc., and the Sages, etc., although many swore, as written, “for they swore, etc.”
However, etc. For they are like individuals, as above; see Rivash siman 185, and see Shach, who erred, for it is known that the responsa attributed to the Ramban are Responsa Rashba.
