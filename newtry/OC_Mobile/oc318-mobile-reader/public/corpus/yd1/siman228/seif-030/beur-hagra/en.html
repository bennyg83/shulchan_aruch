A community, etc. Rivash writes that in earlier generations communal bans generally did not include explicit language of an oath. Therefore, where they added an oath, it was a distinct personal obligation requiring release by a sage. See above, seif 25.
And they should release for them, etc. No member of the city may release it alone, for this resembles releasing one’s own vow, which is forbidden, as taught in Nega’im chapter 2 and in the Yerushalmi: one who says, “Benefit from me is forbidden to the people of my city,” may not seek release from a sage of that city. Therefore outsiders or a duly constituted authority not included in the ban must release it.
Those who release may, etc. The question in Nedarim 73a, whether a husband may annul two vows simultaneously, arises only because Scripture says “her,” in the singular. No comparable limitation applies to a sage releasing vows. See Rivash, siman 461.
Above I wrote, etc. The ruling here may be proved from Gittin 46a, which discusses Rabbi Yehudah and the Sages even though many had sworn, as Scripture says, “for they had sworn to them,” etc.
Nevertheless, etc. A community is in this respect like individuals, as above. See Rivash, siman 185. Shach erred because the responsa attributed to Ramban are known to be responsa of Rashba.
