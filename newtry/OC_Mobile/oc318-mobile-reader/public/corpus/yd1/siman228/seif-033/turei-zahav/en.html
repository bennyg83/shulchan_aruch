He has violated the cherem of their enactment. This means that he must seek release from his oath and accept the communal cherem. The Beit Yosef cites this from a responsum of the Rambam. It resembles one who swore against a rabbinic mitzvah, whose oath takes effect, as ruled in siman 239, seif 6. The Rivash likewise discusses one who swore not to serve as one of the trustees selected by the community for tax matters, after the community had placed them under cherem. An individual may not exempt himself from communal taxation. If he swore beforehand, he is forbidden to sit with them until he seeks release. If no opening can be found, the community’s selection is void because it selected five and now has only four. This appears to conflict with the Mordechai in the name of Rashi, who wrote that one who swears not to obey a forthcoming communal enactment has sworn in vain, because he swore to nullify a mitzvah and is already sworn from Sinai to obey communal regulations. That implies the oath does not take effect at all. The distinction is as follows. If the communal enactment safeguards against transgression, the individual’s oath does not take effect, because the Torah grants the community authority to erect fences around prohibitions, as it states, “You shall guard My safeguards.” If, however, the enactment concerns financial benefit, taxes, or appointments, the oath takes effect, and the communal regulation cannot uproot it without release. Accordingly, when the Rama writes that one who swore not to reveal information must nevertheless testify when placed under cherem, no release is needed if he initially swore to violate the obligation to testify, for he was already obligated by the Torah. Indeed, when formally summoned to testify he must testify even without a cherem, as otherwise he violates, “If he does not testify.” The case must therefore be one where he was not formally summoned. In siman 239, seif 7, the Rama writes similarly concerning one who swore not to reveal information that would save another from loss and states that release is required. There, the obligation is not as absolute as formal testimony, and therefore he was stringent to require release.
