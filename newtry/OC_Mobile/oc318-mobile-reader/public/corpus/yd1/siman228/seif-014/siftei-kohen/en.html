And the reason for which, etc. Perishah writes that it is surprising that even meticulous scholars do not customarily require the cause of the vow to be stated. He suggests that most vows brought before a sage concern fasting, abstaining from meat or wine, or similar self-affliction, whose cause is ordinarily understood to be a desire for greater righteousness. Therefore they do not ask the cause, unlike vows concerning other matters, such as withholding benefit from another. This explanation is unsatisfactory. The Gemara and authorities' requirement to specify the vow means to explain its cause, as is evident in Gittin 35b and Bechorot 45b. Furthermore, the practice is not to state the cause of any vow, and according to Perishah's explanation it should be even more necessary, lest the sage wrongly assume a pious motive. Rather, specifying the vow is the petitioner's obligation, not an obligation upon the sage to question him. One who does not state a cause may simply have vowed without one. If he vowed because of a cause and conceals it, the sin is his, and no proof can be drawn from fools who act improperly.
