But one who swore, etc. Perishah notes that the passage begins with vows and ends with oaths. He suggests that because Rashba and Rosh dispute whether repeated vows take effect one upon another, the ruling concludes with oaths, regarding which all agree that a second oath does not take effect. Above in siman 229, end of seif 3, where one twice prohibited a loaf and obtained release, he remains forbidden until seeking release a second time. This does not prove that one release can never suffice. There the petitioner sought release only from the first prohibition. If he seeks release from all repetitions together, one proceeding may suffice.
He requires release for each one, etc. Mahari ben Lev, responsum 139, rules that even where a vow and oath concern the same act, separate proceedings are unnecessary. He may release all at once. If he releases only the first, the second and third then take effect and require release. The same applies if he releases only the third while the first two remain. He proves this from Rambam, Hilchot Shevuot, chapter 6. Bach rules likewise. Ran, responsum 17, cited by the Mechaber below in siman 229, seif 5, concerns specifically one who said that the second vow should take effect only after he seeks release from the first, and the third only after release from the second. Until release from the first, the second vow does not yet exist, and likewise the third. If one simply swore or vowed several times about the same act, saying, 'I will not eat today, I will not eat today,' all the oaths already exist. The rule that a vow is not released before it takes effect therefore does not apply, and one release suffices. This appears primary, though initially one should be stringent where possible. Mahari ben Lev himself records that he saw someone release each separately, and Responsa Menahem Azariah, siman 48, also appears stringent.
