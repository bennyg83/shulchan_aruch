But one who swore, etc. Written in Darkei Moshe this section — precise in his language begins with vows and ends with oaths — appears because dispute between Rashba and Rosh in vows whether one applies to other (as above section 215) — why end only with oath where all agree do not apply; see siman 229 section 3: if forbade loaf to him and they released — forbidden until ask second time — implies not released with one release — end quote; no proof from siman 229 for there deals when asked only on first; but if comes to ask on all at once suffices.
Need release for each and every one, etc. Maharal ben Chaviv in responsum siman 139 ruled: even vow and oath on one matter — need not release for each; if wishes releases all at once; if releases first alone — second and third take effect and need release; likewise if releases third alone — first and second take effect; proved from Rambam chapter 6 Laws of Oaths — see there; likewise Bach and Ran responsum siman 17 (Mechaber wrote below siman 229 seif 5) — deals specifically when said second vow not until asked on first; likewise on third after asked on second; as long as not asked on first — second vow not in world; likewise third; but one who swore or vowed two or three times such as says "I will not eat today, I will not eat today" — since all oaths in world cannot say here do not release vow until takes effect and one release suffices — end quote; so appears primary except should be stringent initially since possible; also Maharal ben Chaviv himself wrote there saw one who practiced release each by itself; also in responsum Ma'aseh Rokeach siman 48 appears he was stringent on this.
