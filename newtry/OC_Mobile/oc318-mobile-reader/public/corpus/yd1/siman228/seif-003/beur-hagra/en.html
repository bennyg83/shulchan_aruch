Three times. This is the Tur’s ruling. Rambam appears to hold that one declaration suffices, as Beit Yosef notes. In his Commentary to the Mishnah, at the end of chapter 10, Rambam writes twice; but the Rosh at the end of Yoma agrees with the Tur, writing, “Nevertheless, it appears,” etc., “and therefore,” etc.
(Supplement) Three times. As stated at the end of the chapter Four Death Penalties: “The vow is released,” etc. Although the formula there is stated only twice, that is not exact, for two repetitions alone never have independent significance. Compare the end of chapter 3 of Parah: “cedar wood,” etc., “hyssop,” etc.; and the first chapter of Niddah (11a): “she jumped and saw,” etc. Although Tosafot emend the latter passage to read three times, the emendation is not necessary, as Tosafot themselves indicate at 39b, s.v. “this proves,” and as explained above and in many other places. (End.)
(Supplement) Three times. As stated at the end of the chapter Four Death Penalties, “the vow is released,” etc. Likewise, in the chapter HaSefinah (74a), the Ein Yaakov version reads, “It is annulled for you, it is annulled for you.” Although only two repetitions are written, the intent is three, as in chapter 3 of Parah, “this hyssop,” etc., and in Menachot, chapter Rabbi Yishmael (65a), and elsewhere. A formula of only two repetitions is never found. (End.)
