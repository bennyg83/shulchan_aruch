In whatever expression he uses. This means that even in a foreign language he may use an expression of pardon or release whose meaning is to uproot the vow retroactively. The Rambam concludes: If, however, he says, “It is annulled for you,” or, “Your oath is uprooted,” or a similar expression, he has said nothing, for only a husband or father annuls. A sage uses only language of release or pardon. The Beit Yosef cites the Ramban that language of release and pardon is like untying a knot, returning the matter to its prior state, like one untying a bound rope. Language of annulment, however, refers to something that exists but is prevented from reaching completion, as in, “He frustrates the plans of the cunning.” Thus, from the perspective of the one who vowed, the vow remains in existence, but the person who says language of annulment means that he does not wish it to be fulfilled. Only a husband regarding his wife has that power.
And even while standing. This excludes what we find in monetary judgment, where judging while standing, by relatives, on Shabbos, or at night is invalid.
And the custom is to release communal cherems. The Rama likewise wrote in Orach Chayyim, siman 306, seif 12, that this is considered a communal need. However, one may not impose a cherem on Shabbos if it is unrelated to a Shabbos need. The same applies when it is a major matter affecting the entire community, as found in a responsum of the Rashba, who imposed a cherem on Shabbos upon the residents of Provence who devoted all their days to the study of natural philosophy, as mentioned in his responsa.
