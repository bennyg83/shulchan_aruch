He swore not to serve as collector or trustee for the town. Rashba writes that he benefits from municipal authority, one of the offices to which gentiles and converts are not appointed, while the public also benefits because its work is performed through him. Since he benefits, he may not seek release from a sage of that town. Darchei Moshe explains that even though he vowed only not to serve as collector or trustee and did not prohibit the town's benefit, he still may not ask a local sage. He prohibited the office and trusteeship, which bring him benefit from the town, and if the sage's release restores that benefit he violates his vow. Accordingly Rashba follows Rambam's position, as Beit Yosef and Ateret Zekenim explain, unlike Perishah. In any event, according to the authorities who forbid because of personal interest, as stated in seif katan 9, he cannot seek release here regardless.
