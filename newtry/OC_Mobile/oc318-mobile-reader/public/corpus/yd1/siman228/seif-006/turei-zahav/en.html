That he should not serve as a communal collector, etc. The Beit Yosef cites the Rashba that this appointment is considered a benefit, because the position is disqualified for a non-Jew and for converts and is therefore regarded as an office of authority. I wrote regarding this in the previous seif. In truth, however, this requires study. The Beit Yosef copied from the Rashba that by forbidding himself from the office, he forbade this benefit to himself and to the townspeople, and concluded that since he benefits, he may not seek release from the town’s sage. This is difficult. Although he forbade himself from one particular benefit, namely, the office, he did not forbid all other benefits. Is this different from one who forbids himself from the wine of the townspeople? Would he thereby be forbidden to seek release from the town’s sage? If the concern is that the town’s sage benefits from the release and is therefore interested, that is the explanation I gave above, but the Beit Yosef rejected that reasoning. Furthermore, the Rashba should have said, “Since they benefit,” not, “Since he benefits.” It appears that there is a scribal error and the text should read, “Since they benefit.” This still requires study. According to this, a town rabbi would be forbidden to release any vow concerning a communal appointment when one swore not to accept it, yet it appears that the custom is not so.
