That there should be no treasurer, etc. In Beit Yosef in the name of Rashba it is explained that this constitutes a benefit, since this appointment is invalid for a non-Jew — and for a convert it is considered a position of authority. And in the previous seif we wrote about this. But in truth this requires examination to understand, for in Beit Yosef he cited this in the name of Rashba — that by forbidding himself the treasurership, he forbids this benefit to himself and the townspeople; and he concluded: and since he benefits, one may not seek annulment from the town's sage. This is difficult: even though he forbade himself from them in a specific benefit — namely the treasurership — he did not forbid himself from other benefits. Is this not comparable to one who forbids himself from the wine of his townspeople? Would he therefore be forbidden to seek a sage of the town for annulment? Indeed it would be exactly so. And if we say one may not seek the town's sage because the vow involves a forbidden benefit for them and he is personally interested in the ruling of permit — as I wrote above in explaining the Tur's words — this is difficult, for Beit Yosef does not accept that reasoning. Furthermore, why did Rashba conclude "and since he benefits" — he should have said "and since they benefit." It appears to me that there is a scribal error in Rashba's words, and it should read "and since they benefit," etc. This requires further study. Furthermore this requires examination: according to this, the town's rabbi would be forbidden to annul any vow regarding all communal appointments, if someone swore he would not want to accept it — and it seems this is not the practice.
