It is impossible, etc. Even those already born cannot be made subject to an oath unless they consent and answer amen, as stated at the end of chapter 3 of Shevuot, 29b. Rivash, siman 185, cites Ramban that one who did not answer amen is not personally bound by the oath. Nevertheless, communal enactments may bind future members through the community’s legislative authority, custom, and acceptance, not because the unborn were literally sworn. The force of such enactments depends on the authority of the community and the terms of the enactment.
A congregation, etc. As stated at the end of chapter 3 of Shevuot, 29a: “An oath that I will eat this loaf; an oath,” etc. See the Rosh there in the name of the Yerushalmi.
Some are stringent. Darkei Moshe wrote this based on a responsum of the Rosh, rule 7, siman 4, concerning a later enactment made against an earlier ban. Documents were executed under the later enactment, and the Rosh ruled that they were not invalidated. This is unlike the principle in Temurah 4b that an act done in violation is ineffective, because communal enactments operate through the authority and intention of the community. Nevertheless, where the first enactment expressly barred later repeal, some are stringent unless the repeal is made by an equal or greater authority and with the required consent.
