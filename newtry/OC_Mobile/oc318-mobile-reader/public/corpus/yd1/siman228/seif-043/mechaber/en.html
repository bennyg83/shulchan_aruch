One who swore to give his daughter to so-and-so as a wife by a certain time, and within the time the match became disqualified (meaning: those who betroth each other are called meshudakhim from the verse "and found them rest," for the Targum of "and the land was quiet" is "and betrothal of the land") through his deeds — the other party is exempt from his oath; and the same if the matchmaking sister became disqualified. {Rama: And some say specifically if he remained in his disgrace or came to beit din while still in his disgrace and it is impossible for her to marry him — such as he converted his religion; but if he returned in repentance and afterward the one who swore came to beit din — he needs release for his oath, even though he is exempt from the fine he obligated himself if he transgresses; nevertheless he needs release for his oath (Hagahos Mordechai on Shevuos, not like Beit Yosef; and see in the Rav's responsa siman 100 question 14). But if he came to beit din while in his disgrace, even release is not needed — for it is impossible she marry an apostate; and since it was permitted at that time, it is permitted forever even if he repents afterward; and such seems to me to be primary (there).}
