His conduct. Noda BiYehudah explains that 'corrupt conduct' means conversion, habitual sexual immorality, or repeated ugly transgressions, not one isolated incident. See Darkei Noam and Shemesh Tzedakah.
The betrothed's sister. Rosh permits breaking the match because of serious family corruption. Noda BiYehudah extends this a fortiori to the grandfather, citing the enduring effect of family taint across generations.
And she cannot marry him. Noda BiYehudah concludes that Rama does not truly dispute the Mechaber: conversion or notorious corruption in the betrothed or close family creates a clear presumption that the match was never accepted on such terms. After genuine repentance, however, the defect is less decisive.
