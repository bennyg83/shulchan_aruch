Such as if he forbade them from his property, etc. Gemara reason: since he was brazen he will be brazen — since he already brazened his face to forbid his father, also at time of regret if he has no complete regret he will brazen to say he would not have refrained for their honor but certainly has complete regret. This law's reason is like the reason I mentioned seif 10 — any sin that is common we do not say he is ashamed to say that even knowing the prohibition he vowed; we say since he truly regrets, likewise this sin releasing maintenance obligation — even though not common, since even without Torah command this reason applies to honor father as nations do and is considered sin by all — he brazened and breached the world's fence — certainly no shame now to say had he known he is obligated in their maintenance he would not have vowed; he will not act from shame for he has no shame but truly regrets. Maharach tied the distinction in these laws of "do not hold a grudge" and honoring father is explicit in Torah — since he already brazened to transgress Torah he will brazen now and not be ashamed; unlike opening where sin in vow is not revealed to all — his words are astonishing: if he already knew prohibition of "do not hold a grudge" or honoring father — what opening applies to release his vow from "had you known"?
