And they do not intend to return. Meaning, even though they do not intend to return, the enactment does not bind them if the breach that caused it is absent there. If they do intend to return, however, they must observe the safeguard even where the breach is not found, as stated in Orach Chaim, siman 574, that one who intends to return must observe the stringencies of the place he left.
Therefore, etc. The wording is imprecise, because the present case is more stringent. Beit Yosef also implies this. Here the enactment was issued by the supreme beit din, and the place did not cause the ban. It rests upon each person's head. See below, seif 35, seif katan 92. Responsa Mabit, part 2, siman 104, discusses a man who married in a place governed by Rabbeinu Gershom's enactment and stipulated that he would not marry another wife. After moving elsewhere and losing the ketubah, he wrote a replacement. He may not marry another woman because he married subject to Rabbeinu Gershom's enactment, especially if the condition was written expressly in the ketubah. This implies that without such a condition he could marry elsewhere, and the ban would not automatically follow him. The case must be where he did not reside in the place of the enactment but merely married there, so the enactment never attached to him.
