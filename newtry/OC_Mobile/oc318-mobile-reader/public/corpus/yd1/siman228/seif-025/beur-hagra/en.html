A decree and excommunication. See above, seif 8, and what I wrote there.
Therefore they may vow, etc. The Tur writes this. Tashbetz explains that it is only like a ban, as above, and a community may release its own ban. Thus the brothers, when they found Yosef, released themselves from their ban. Rivash writes likewise. This supports what I wrote in seif 8.
Even if they mentioned, etc. Their intent is only to strengthen the sanction against one who violates their enactment. Scripture similarly says, “There is a ban in your midst, Israel,” and “Yehoshua adjured,” etc. See Shevuot 36a. Tanna Devei Eliyahu says that “the oath is the ban,” meaning in this type of case. Since the language functions as a communal sanction rather than a personal oath, the community may release it according to its original authority.
Some say, etc. So states a responsum of the Rosh, and it necessarily disputes the first view. According to the first view, release by a sage is required because this is unlike ordinary communal bans. It is not as Shach understood. According to the second view, even a ban made on the public’s understanding may be released by the community itself without a sage, since whatever was enacted by communal consent may be rescinded by comparable communal consent.
