Therefore they impose vows and release them, etc. Their mention of Hashem's consent and an oath was intended only to make the matter more severe and deter frivolous treatment. Ultimately, everything depends on the community's decision and whatever they agree upon.
And if a ban or vow was imposed. This refers to the additional ban that they added to their custom.
Or it was vowed with public consent. Meaning, they said that it was with the consent of distant communities, which constitutes public consent. This appears in Responsa Rashba cited by Beit Yosef.
And some say that even if they stipulated, etc. Every communal enactment is treated as though this condition was made from the outset. It is nevertheless possible that Rosh agrees that if they expressly said it was with public consent or with the consent of distant communities, it has no release. The Mechaber understands that Rosh does not dispute the first opinion. When Rosh says that even if they stipulated they may later release it, he means through formal release. The Mechaber therefore cites Rosh's words below in siman 229, seif 4.
