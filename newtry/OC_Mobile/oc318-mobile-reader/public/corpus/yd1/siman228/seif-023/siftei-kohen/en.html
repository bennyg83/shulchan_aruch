A vow that was imposed publicly. Meaning, it was made in the presence of many people, but he did not say that it was made with their consent.
It may be released without their consent. Beit Yosef cites Responsa Rashba that it need not be released in their presence, as is proven in several places in HaSholeach. His words imply that the release may occur even without informing them, because only a vow made with one person's consent requires notice. This is also evident in Gittin 36a and at the end of Mumim. Nevertheless, this applies only when the public receives no benefit from the vow. If it benefits them, they are no less than an individual, and release without notice is forbidden because of suspicion, as above in seif katan 41.
