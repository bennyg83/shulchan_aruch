Baraita, Nedarim 65a. In my commentary to the Mishnah and in the conclusions and related laws I composed in exile, which I called Kli Golah, I proved that one word was omitted from the copied text. It should read: 'the person for whose benefit he vowed, with his knowledge and consent,' meaning voluntarily, because he vowed in exchange for a benefit received from him. Kesef Mishneh, Beit Yosef, Rambam, Rosh, Raam, Raavad, Tosafot in the name of Rabbeinu Tam, Rashba, Mordechai, Tur, Ran, Semag, Maharik, and the other authorities support this, apart from Ramban and one answer in Tosafot to Gittin. The Mechaber's wording, 'but if he vowed on his own, without the other's knowledge,' implies that the other's consent is unnecessary, though he must be informed, because of suspicion, as the Yerushalmi, Rambam, and other authorities state. Although the Taz explains that the Mechaber requires the other's consent, in my humble opinion the Mechaber intended to clarify the law, and the distinction stated above is necessary.
