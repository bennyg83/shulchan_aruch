One who vowed with the consent of his fellow — they do not release him unless they informed that one upon whose consent he vowed. {Rama: (And some say even if they informed him, they do not release except with his knowledge and will) (Maharik root 22, Tur, and Rosh) (and such is the essential ruling)} And specifically when he vowed with his consent for some benefit that [the fellow] did for him. {Rama: (for the sake of the oath) (Beit Yosef in the name of Rivash siman 186 and Shulchan Aruch)} Like Moshe who vowed with Yitro's consent because he gave him his daughter in marriage. {Rama: (and the oath was for Yitro's benefit) (Rivash siman 461)} But if of his own accord he vowed with his fellow's consent, they can release him without his knowledge. {Rama: And some say he must inform him that they released him so he should not suspect him of transgressing his vow (Mordechai chapter Shevuos shtayim in the name of Yerushalmi, and Maharik root 52 and Afarkasta); and if [the fellow] already died so it is impossible to inform him, they release him — for there is no longer suspicion (Ramban's responsa siman 207)} And even if he vowed with his consent for a benefit [the fellow] did for him — if b'dieved they released him without his knowledge, it is a release; and this is when he specified the vow and the sage knew it was done for his fellow's benefit, and immediately the beit din that dealt with this should punish and rebuke him. And some say it is not a release. {Rama: Since he did him a benefit and swore to him on account of this; but if he swore to his fellow to do some thing and did not do so — on account of a benefit [the fellow] did for him — although they do not release l'chatchila without his knowledge, nevertheless if they released b'dieved it is a release (thus is implied in Maharik root 52 in the second division)} And if he swore to his fellow that he would not move from a certain city and he transgressed and left — they release him even without his knowledge, since there is no longer benefit to the one who imposed the oath in the matter. And some say there is no distinction between [whether the fellow] did him a benefit or did not do him a benefit — never do they release except with his knowledge. {Rama: And if that one upon whose consent he vowed died, he can release — for there is no [longer his] knowledge; however if the oath was for his fellow's benefit, such as he swore to give him something or not to engage in commerce without his permission, and it was for his fellow's benefit — "in his absence, his heirs are in his place" (Ramban's responsa siman 249). Everywhere the one sworn accepted benefit from the one who imposed the oath — even though the imposer accepted more benefit than him, nevertheless it is called benefit — for behold Yitro accepted more benefit than Moshe who married his daughter, and even so it is called benefit (Rivash siman 390). Some say that for the sake of a mitzvah they release a vow one swore with his fellow's consent, even without his fellow's knowledge (Beit Yosef in the name of Tosafos). A man and woman who accepted cherem or who swore to each other to marry each other — they do not release one without his fellow's knowledge, for this is called benefit, for each wants to marry his fellow and they swore on account of this to each other (Hagahos Mordechai on Shevuos, Maharik root 52, and Maharam Padua siman 70). But if the woman says she hates him and gave a good excuse for her words — they release her without his knowledge; for even if she already married, one who says "he is repulsive to me" is obligated to divorce (Beit Yosef in the name of Mahari Ve-Yahel siman 137 and 186); and see above siman 239 — one who swore not to marry a woman over his wife.}
