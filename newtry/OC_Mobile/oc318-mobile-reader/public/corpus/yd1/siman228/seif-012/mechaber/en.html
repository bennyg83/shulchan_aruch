They open only regarding a matter that exists and is common and occurs frequently; but they do not open regarding a matter that does not occur frequently — such as if he said "konam that I benefit from so-and-so" or "that I enter the house of so-and-so," and that so-and-so became a Torah scholar whom everyone needs, or that house became a synagogue — they do not open for him to say "If you had known so-and-so would become a Torah scholar" or "that house would become a beit k'nesset, you would not have vowed" — for through petach the vow is uprooted from its root, and such a petach does not uproot it, for even if he had considered it he would not have refrained from vowing because it does not occur frequently. {Rama: And likewise death does not occur frequently, but poverty occurs frequently (Beit Yosef in the name of the Gemara chapter Rabbi Elazar and Hagahos Maimoni chapter 5) (and likewise a woman's pregnancy occurs frequently and is not called 'born') (Hagahos Maimoni ch. 6)}
