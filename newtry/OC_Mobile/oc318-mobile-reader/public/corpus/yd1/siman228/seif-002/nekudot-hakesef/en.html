(There, seif katan 27) Rather, an erring student wrote them. I say that the words of the Perishah are clear. The discussion concerns even a case where the other person did not formally affirm the vow, but the vower made it with the other’s wishes in mind, meaning to satisfy him, although the other remained silent. Since he vowed to satisfy the other, it is a complete vow that does not depend on the other’s consent; he merely undertook it for the other’s benefit. This is also implied by the halachic authorities, and it is plainly the position of the Perishah.
(There, seif katan 45) This does not appear so, etc. It seems to me that Ran's view is that if one vows without qualification 'with his friend's intent,' the friend cannot release the vow, because that phrase does not mean that the friend may annul it. But if the vower says that this was his intention, namely that his friend could annul it, it is as though he expressly stipulated so, because vows follow the vower's intention. Rivash likewise holds this view. Examine carefully.
