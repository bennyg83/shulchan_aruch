They do not release, etc.; how, etc. See Ran there, s.v. “nevertheless.” Regarding release, some authorities hold one way, while others hold the opposite; Rambam and Shulchan Aruch rule according to the second view.
(Supplement) They do not, etc. This is Rambam’s language. He holds that the rule applies even where the vow depends on a fixed time. Others disagree regarding a time-dependent vow, because the prohibition will take effect automatically, whereas where it depends on an act, it may never take effect. (End.)
Even, etc. This is unlike seif 15, “if there is,” etc. There, the act is intrinsically permitted and forbidden only by the vow; moreover, it involves a transgression, and where a stumbling block exists it is a mitzvah to obtain release. Here, however, the act is intrinsically forbidden until the condition occurs, and an attempted release does not permit it. See there.
All the more so one who vows, etc. This is the very law stated in the Gemara there.
But, etc. This is there, according to the Sages. See Ran there, s.v. “regarding the law,” etc.
Likewise. Annulment too operates this way, as stated there, 7b: “and he may annul for himself,” as above.
