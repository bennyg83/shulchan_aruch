From Rosh Chodesh Iyar. This is the Rambam’s formulation. In the final chapter of Nedarim, Rav Chisda holds that whether the vow depends on days, as here, or on an act, such as, “If I perform a certain act, meat shall become forbidden to me,” release is unavailable until the prohibition actually takes effect. Abaye distinguishes. When it depends on days, the time will certainly arrive automatically, and only time is lacking, so release may be granted immediately. When it depends on an act, perhaps the prohibition will never arise, so release is unavailable until the act occurs. The Ramban’s responsum, siman 271, explains their dispute this way. The Rambam rules that even where it depends on days, release is unavailable until the prohibition begins. He may understand the dispute this way and rule like Rav Chisda, unlike the Ran, who distinguishes between days and an act.
But regarding annulment by a husband. The Gemara explains that the verse states, “He frustrates the plans of the cunning,” implying that annulment can apply even while the vow exists only in thought and has not yet taken effect. Release by a sage is different, for the verse states, “He shall not profane his word.” So long as it is merely speech and has not yet taken effect, it cannot be released.
Regarding release of communal cherems, etc. It appears that the reason is that this release is not included in “he shall not profane,” for the community itself imposed the cherem and itself releases it. “He shall not profane” refers to others releasing him, as Shmuel said: He may not pardon himself, but others may pardon him. The Beit Yosef later cites the Rashba that communal cherems are considered to have an implicit condition that the community may release them whenever it wishes, even before they take effect, for they are imposed initially with that understanding.
