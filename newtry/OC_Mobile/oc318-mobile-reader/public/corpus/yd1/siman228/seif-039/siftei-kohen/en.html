But to extend the time while the obligation remains. The creditor cannot merely extend the deadline while leaving the oath in force, unless they stipulated at the time of the oath that he could do so. The matter can be repaired by the creditor saying, 'I consider myself as though I have received payment,' and the debtor then swearing to pay by the newly agreed date. If the creditor fears that after saying this the debtor will mock him and refuse the new oath, he should say, 'I consider myself as though I have received payment on condition that you swear to pay me by the specified date.' Beit Yosef.
To extend it while the obligation remains, etc. Orchot Chaim writes that when part of a vow is released, the entire vow is released, and the same applies to an oath. Therefore, if Reuven owes Shimon one hundred zuz under oath by a fixed date, and at that time asks Shimon to extend payment to a later date, Reuven is no longer bound by the original oath. The sages of the generation ruled this way. The plain sense of Responsa Rosh concerning one who swore to his wife not to leave her without permission, and Responsa Ramban below, also appears to support this. Nevertheless, it is proper to take Rashba's view into account. Bedek HaBayit. The cited responsa do not clearly prove the point, and Orchot Chaim's reasoning requires study. The rule that release of part releases the whole does not apply to a ban or a husband's annulment, as stated in siman 229, seif 2, because they cut off the prohibition prospectively rather than uprooting it. A fellow likewise does not uproot an oath retroactively. The matter depends upon his permission and is a condition that he may formulate as he wishes. Rabbeinu Yerucham and the Mechaber below, siman 229, end of seif 1, imply this. Orchot Chaim must therefore refer to a case where the creditor said, 'I consider myself as though I have received payment.' Then the entire oath is released, and Rashba's ruling does not disagree.
