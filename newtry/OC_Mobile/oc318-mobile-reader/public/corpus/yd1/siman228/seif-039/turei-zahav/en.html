As though I had received it. The Beit Yosef explains that when the matter is not truly for his own direct benefit, he cannot say this. For example, if one vowed to his father-in-law, according to the father-in-law’s understanding, that he would not travel with his daughter without permission, although the father-in-law derives some satisfaction, release by a sage is specifically required and his pardon is ineffective, unless the wording was, “I will not go without your permission.”
But to extend the time. Many people stumble in this. They bind themselves to others to fulfill an obligation, sometimes even with a handshake oath, by a specified time. When the time arrives, the other person says, “I extend the time for you,” but does not pardon the oath. The obligated person then violates the oath. The Beit Yosef provides the remedy: The creditor should say, “I regard myself as though I have received payment, on condition that you swear to pay by such-and-such time,” and the debtor should then swear.
