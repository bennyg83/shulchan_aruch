Ambiguous language, etc. As above, seif 31. So too here, as stated on 18b: “Unspecified vows are interpreted stringently, but their explanations leniently.”
If they stipulated, etc. As stated there, 20a: one vowed “by a ban” and then explained “by his net,” etc. Ran explains that in the latter clause the ordinary meaning inclines more toward stringency than leniency. Certainly one may not interpret the language contrary to the plain meaning of the communal enactment.
A congregation, etc. As stated in Avodah Zarah 36a: “Our masters relied,” etc. See Rashi, Chullin 6a, s.v. “and they accepted,” and Shabbat 14b: “Shammai and Hillel decreed,” etc.
