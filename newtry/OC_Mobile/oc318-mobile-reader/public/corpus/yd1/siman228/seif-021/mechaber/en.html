A vow that is al daat rabim (with the consent of the many) — it has no release without their knowledge. {Rama: And some are stringent that even with their knowledge one may not release regarding a matter of reshus (optional/permitted matter) (Beit Yosef in the name of Ramban's responsa, Rashba, Semag, and Rivash); and such is proper to be stringent l'chatchila} except if there is a mitzvah in its release. {Rama: One who swore his fellow would not enter his house, and there is a synagogue in his house — it is called a matter of mitzvah and they release him that his fellow come to pray with the multitude (Semag, Hagahos Maimoni, and Maharik brought root 22, and R' Yitzchak's responsa; and likewise to make peace between man and wife (Meyuchas's responsa); or on account of communal needs — it is called a matter of mitzvah to release on their account a vow made al daat rabim (Rivash siman 461); and likewise for his livelihood (Rashbatz); and specifically if it is impossible to fulfill the mitzvah without releasing the vow (there in Maharik). And if those many say not to release him — even for the sake of a mitzvah they do not release him (Beit Yosef in the name of Rivash and Maharam Padua siman 62). One who swore not to benefit from his father and regrets — it is called a matter of mitzvah and they release him (Beit Yosef in the name of Rashba's responsa)} And they are not called rabim with fewer than three; such as if he specified them — "with the consent of so-and-so, so-and-so, and so-and-so." But if he said stam (unqualified) "al daat rabim" — he has release [without specifying]. And some say if he vowed before three, even if he did not specify them but said stam "al daat rabim" — he has no release, for presumably he meant those rabim standing before him. {Rama: And the same applies if he swore stam and we know whom he intended — such as he swore al daat ha-kahal, or said he swore al daat three rabbis of the city and there are only three rabbis there, that certainly he intended these — it is called al daat rabim (Beit Yosef in the name of Rivash). Some say that four who swore together to do one thing are called al daat rabim, for each swore to three fellows and with their consent he swore (Maharik root 162); and see below seif 30. Some say a vow al daat rabim that they released b'dieved was released (there root 52 in the name of R' Avigdor); and one should not rely on this except in a time of pressure and there is need (Beit Yosef in the name of Rashba's responsa). If one swore al daat rabim regarding a matter that has a hint of sin — it is permitted to release him without their knowledge, for the mind of rabim does not agree on a matter that involves sin (Beit Yosef in the name of Ran's responsa siman 42). One who swore not to do a certain thing except with the permission of three men specified by name — it is not called al daat rabim; on the contrary, these many were mentioned to lighten for him that he may do with their permission without release; and likewise all similar cases (also this in Beit Yosef beginning of daf 473 in the name of Ran siman 17).}
