Without their consent. So writes the Tur, and Tosafot, Gittin 35b, s.v. “but if she married,” and Mordechai in the name of Rabbeinu Tam. A vow made on the understanding of the public cannot normally be released because their consent is required and they are presumed unwilling. For a mitzvah, however, they are presumed agreeable. If the public expressly object, release is ineffective.
Some explain, etc. Rashba, Semag, and other authorities explain that the members of the public do not share one identical regret, since each regrets for a different reason. Where release serves a mitzvah, however, all concur in a single intent. For this reason a husband may annul in every case, as stated in seif 24, because annulment does not require an opening or regret.
One who swore, etc. Prayer is heard particularly in the synagogue, and one who has a synagogue in his city yet does not attend is censured in Berakhot 6a, 7b, and 8a. Communal prayer also sanctifies God’s name publicly and enables responses of Kedushah, Kaddish, and Barekhu. It is therefore a mitzvah need for which a public-dependent oath may be released. The same applies where the oath prevents Torah study or another communal mitzvah.
