(Supplement) One who vowed and regretted, etc. — how so, etc. For [a vow] is only dissolved through regret, as written in Gittin 83b: "this is what R. Yochanan said: a sage [may not dissolve a vow] without, etc." — and so it is implied in many places. (End.)
That he has learned, etc. and it is, etc. This is the view of the Rosh in chapter 3, seif 3; and see Ran, 8b, s.v. "and excommunication, etc."; 23a, s.v. "and the dissolution, etc."; 78a, s.v. "he said, etc." — unlike Ramban who requires [the petitioner to appear] specifically before [the sage who declared the ruling]. And Rambam's view is that laypeople do not need [a sage] since they explain [the reason] through reasoning, etc. See Beit Yosef. And they also know, etc. The Rosh there, and Ran, 78a, in the name of the Jerusalem Talmud.
