And even if he vowed, etc. Meaning, he swore, for saying 'by the God of Israel' constitutes an oath. Bach, and this is obvious.
And they have been taught and understand. Meaning, others teach them the halachot and they understand them. This implies that even if none of them learned independently, but all were taught by others and understand, they may release the vow. This differs from Choshen Mishpat, siman 3, regarding judges, where if none is learned and understands legal reasoning they are unfit to judge, because releasing vows is not a judicial proceeding, as will be explained. This is unlike the Perishah here. The Ateret Zekenim writes that they must have been taught and understand somewhat, enough to know what they are releasing. This implies that complete mastery is unnecessary; some understanding sufficient to know what they are releasing is enough, and this is the practice.
And they also know, etc. Although an opening is not required for every vow, they are not fit to release even through regret unless they also know how to release through an opening. A vow that requires an opening may come before them, and they may not know how to distinguish one vow from another. Perishah.
And nowadays, etc. Release may be performed only by three who have been taught and possess some understanding, as stated. If this condition is lacking, even the presence of one great sage is ineffective. Likewise, if there are fewer than three, even if both are great sages, their release is invalid even after the fact, and the vow must be released again before three. Bach writes likewise, and this is obvious.
