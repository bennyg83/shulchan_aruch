Since he was obligated, etc. I do not understand the Rama’s view. The responsum attributed to the Ramban, siman 259, states that one who swore to pay by a specified time, or to bring his daughter to marry a certain man on a specified day, and failed to do so, may remain obligated afterward and violate the oath each day. These are two matters: first, he swore to perform the act without making it dependent upon time; second, he accepted an additional obligation to perform it by a specified time. If, however, he swore to do the act on a particular day and that day passed, the oath no longer obligates him, though he is liable for having violated it. The responsum proves this from one who vowed to fast. If he accepted an unspecified day and ate, he must fast another day. If he accepted this specific day, he merely completes that day. The Rama distinguishes the two clauses by saying that in the first he was already obligated independently, unlike the second. This distinction is exceedingly difficult, for it is nowhere implied. The first clause also speaks of one who swore to perform an act. The Shulchan Aruch’s wording in the latter clause, “the act,” clearly refers to the same act mentioned in the first. If the Rama’s distinction were correct, the proof from fasting would be irrelevant. Moreover, the Shulchan Aruch writes, “There is no further obligation through the oath,” implying that some obligation may remain apart from the oath. The responsum attributed to the Ramban is in fact a responsum of the Rashba, as the Beit Yosef explains in his introduction, and the Rashba states explicitly that the first case creates two distinct obligations: he swore to perform the act and separately fixed a time. Maharam Padua, siman 72, explains that when the vow and time are stated together, they form one matter. Thus, if he first swore to perform an act and then added that it should be completed by a certain time, the oath on the act remains after the time. If he said in one statement, “I will do this act on such-and-such day,” the oath ends when the day passes. The Rosh disagrees regarding payment of an existing debt. Even when he says in one statement, “I swear that I will pay you on such-and-such day,” the oath primarily concerns payment, while the time merely urges prompt payment. The Rosh himself distinguishes this from one who swore to eat a loaf today and did not, for there he had no prior obligation to eat the loaf. Thus, even the Rosh is stringent only where an independent obligation already exists. Regarding something not otherwise obligatory, he agrees with the Rashba. As practical halacha, we should rule stringently like the Rosh concerning an existing obligation, that the oath continues after the time. Where no prior obligation exists, we distinguish between a separate oath to perform the act followed by a time limit, and a single oath to perform it on a specified day. Maharam Padua therefore ruled concerning one who vowed during distress to travel to Eretz Yisrael in Iyar, but war arose then, that after Iyar there was no continuing oath penalty, though he was stringent to require release. This is the proper practice.
