Since he was obligated, etc. I did not understand Rema's view here: responsum to Ramban siman 259 — one who swore to pay by set time or invite daughter to Ploni's marriage on set day and did not do so — judge and say he is obligated to do so after time and every day transgresses oath, for these are two matters: one swore to do thing without tying to time; second accepted to do by set time — not dependent on each other; but if swore to do thing on set day — if he passed and did not do, no further oath obligation and he is lashed for transgressing oath and exempt from doing. Brings proof from one who vowed to fast and forgot and ate — if said unspecified day lost fast and must fast another day; if said this day — does not lose fast but completes that day. Rema made distinction between first and second section — in first obligated to do without oath unlike second — very astonishing from where is this distinction hinted; if from language in second "to do on set day" as different matter not in first — not so, for also in first responsum language "swore to do thing"; rather clear both of first section refer and abbreviated language; especially Shulchan Aruch wording here with definite article on thing mentioned in first; if distinction as Rema thought — fasting proof irrelevant; also from Shulchan Aruch wording astonishing on Rema — wrote in second "no further oath obligation" then added "not obligated from his oath" implying some obligation not from oath — if not so "and not obligated" would be entirely superfluous. More astonishing — responsum to Ramban is Rashba's as Beit Yosef wrote in introduction; explicitly in Rashba responsum siman 84 same law; Rashba wrote: one who swore to do thing and fixed time for completion — even if did not do in that time still obligated to do, for two matters: swore to do thing without tying to time; accepted additionally to do by set time — not dependent; but if swore to do on set day — if passed and did not do no further oath obligation. This exact law Shulchan Aruch wrote from Ramban responsum — wrote in first two distinct matters formed — swore on substance and on time as explicitly "swore to do and fixed time"; Maharam Padua siman 72 brought this Rashba responsum: explicitly if he says vow and time fixation at once — one matter; implies in first section vow and time fixation not at once — matter depends; appears clear in responsum to Ramban he intended this — if says "by set day" with lamed not called one oath on payment substance and time — applies only if says "on set day" with bet — language proves; also wrote "some say" which is Rosh's view. Rashba: if made two matters — swore to do and to do by set time — oath obligation on substance even after that time; if said at once "I will do on set day" or "by set day" — once time passed oath no longer applies; Rosh who disputes brought here seif 7 — even if said at once oath on payment and time is merely spur, general 8 law 6; unlike swore to eat loaf today passed day did not eat — transgressed oath not obligated to eat that loaf — there not obligated to eat loaf, oath essence on time; here obligated to pay money and swore to pay — essence on payment, time to spur payment. Rosh did not stringency except on payment already obligation without oath; on matter without obligation without oath — (like Rosh) [like Rashba]; Maharam Padua siman 72 wrote thus but did not bring Ramban responsum words Shulchan Aruch brings implying distinction between "by day" and "on day" — so appears; Rema's words astonishing for reasons stated; per his Hagahah like Rashba first view in seif 7 — per disputant Rosh even without obligation oath applies afterward — not so — resembles swore eat loaf today as Rosh explicitly wrote. For halachah rule like Rosh who stringencies: on matter with obligation without oath oath applies even after time; on matter without obligation distinguish "by set day" vs "on set day"; therefore Maharam Padua ruled there one who vowed in distress to ascend Israel in Iyar and war arose afterward — no further oath penalty but stringency to release oath; likewise practice stringency.
