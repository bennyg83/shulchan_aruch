"Obligated," etc. — Taz and Shach challenged the Rav on this, that the matter does not depend on whether he is obligated to do so without the oath or not; but rather: if others imposed the vow on him and set the time at once — it is one matter, and if the time passes the vow is void. But when he swore to do a thing and set a time for completing it — these are two matters, and one does not overlap the other. Taz's conclusion for halacha: one should be stringent and rule that for a matter in which there is obligation anyway, the oath applies even after the time; and in a matter where there is no obligation otherwise, one distinguishes between one who says "by day such-and-such" and one who says "on day such-and-such" — for if he says "by day," there are two oaths: the first on the essence of payment in general, and the second on completing payment by that day; therefore he transgresses also after the time from the force of the first oath. But if he said "on day such-and-such," the main oath was on payment that day; since that day passed, the oath does not apply afterward. Nevertheless, one should practice stringency to release the oath — end of his words.
