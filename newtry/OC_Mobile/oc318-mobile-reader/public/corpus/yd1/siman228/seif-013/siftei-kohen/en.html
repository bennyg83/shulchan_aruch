And he was lying, etc. The petitioner had thought that perhaps the statement was true. If he knew it was false, such as where he lived in Jerusalem and could not possibly have been told that it had then been destroyed, this cannot be used as an opening. The same applies in similar cases. Perishah, and this is obvious.
It is considered regret, and they release him. He could have been released through initial regret without an opening, as above in seif 7. For this reason, the present practice is to formulate an opening even from an unforeseen development. According to this, they must ask, 'Had you known that you would later regret it,' as Rama writes there.
