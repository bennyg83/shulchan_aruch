And since it requires, etc. The one releasing the vow must be composed and deliberate, but the person seeking release need not be seated. Certainly he need not stand, since this is not a judicial proceeding. The present practice is that those releasing sit while the petitioner stands. A proof appears in the Yerushalmi at the end of HaNa'arah HaMe'orasah, which says that vows are addressed only while wrapped and seated, that the one consulted sits, and the petitioner must stand. Although the Bavli and the halachic authorities allow release while standing, that refers to release through regret, whereas an opening requires seated deliberation. The Yerushalmi may discuss an opening, and nowadays the practice is to release all vows through an opening, as stated below in seif 7 in the gloss. Eruvin 64b, in the incident of Rabban Gamliel, indicates that just as one who drank wine may not rule, as below in siman 242, seif 13, he may not release vows. Keritot 13b indicates likewise regarding Rav Acha's vow. This may apply specifically to release through an opening, which requires examination and composure, as the Gemara concludes regarding Rabban Gamliel.
