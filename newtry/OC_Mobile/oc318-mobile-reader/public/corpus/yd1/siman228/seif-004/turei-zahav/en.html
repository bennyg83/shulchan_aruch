Therefore, one must find an opening for him. This means that the opening must contain a strong reason on account of which he would have refrained from making the vow, even though he was satisfied with the vow when he made it were it not for this opening. A difficulty was raised: Since everything depends on the opening, and we know that he was initially satisfied with his vow, how can we believe him? Regarding regret, at least we see that he presently regrets it, and we see no reason to assume that he did not regret it from the outset. With an opening, however, that is not so. The answer is that if he lies, the release is entirely ineffective, and we inform him of this. He will therefore certainly be careful not to lie. Since the matter of an opening requires careful consideration and a strong rationale, the person releasing him should be seated. The posture of the person seeking release is irrelevant, for this is not equivalent to a judicial proceeding, as explained.
