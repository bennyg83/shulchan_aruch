Unless he says, etc. Meaning, he states that when he swore, his intent was that the vow depend upon his fellow's agreement. If the fellow does not agree, there was never a vow. The statement that the fellow can 'release' him means that the fellow can withhold consent. Ran at the end of HaNa'arah HaMe'orasah writes that if one expressly says, 'I vow if my fellow wishes,' the fellow can permit it, and no special formula is needed. Saying, 'I do not wish it,' suffices. Since this is a conditional vow, the fellow need not say, 'It is permitted to you' or 'It is forgiven to you.' The gloss below in siman 239, seif 2, implies this. If, however, he made an absolute vow and merely said that his fellow would have the power to release it like a sage, the statement is ineffective. The vow takes effect immediately, and one cannot override the Torah's rule that only a sage or three laymen may release it. Alternatively, 'he can release it' may mean actual cancellation that works if performed immediately at the time of the vow before it takes effect, as below in siman 231 in the gloss. This appears to be the Mechaber's legally sound intent, though Beit Yosef's discussion requires some study.
