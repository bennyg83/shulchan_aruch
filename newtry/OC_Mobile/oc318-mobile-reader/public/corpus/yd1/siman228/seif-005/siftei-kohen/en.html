He prohibited himself, etc. If he prohibited his benefit to the townspeople, Ramban, Tur, and Rivash, siman 406, write that he may not seek release from a sage of that town because the sage has an interest in the matter. If two others from another town join him, however, it is permitted, because there is then no concern of interested judgment. See Responsa Mahari ben Lev, who explains their view correctly. Perishah and Bach likewise challenge the Beit Yosef. This stringent ruling is primary, unlike Darchei Moshe and Ateret Zekenim, who follow Beit Yosef.
The benefit of the townspeople, etc. If he prohibited himself from the benefit of all Israel, he may seek release, because this is comparable to an after-the-fact situation. Responsa Mahari ben Lev writes this according to Rambam, and it appears correct.
From a sage, etc. Even if two sages from another town join the local sage, it is forbidden, because the petitioner nevertheless provides him benefit. Beit Yosef. Responsa Menahem Azariah, end of siman 48, writes that under no circumstances may the releasing judges be townspeople, because all have an interest. Their withdrawal from the benefit is ineffective. Withdrawal helps only regarding monetary testimony or judgment, but here the matter is a communal benefit and withdrawal does not help, like a Torah scroll in Choshen Mishpat, siman 37, since it is intended for communal hearing.
