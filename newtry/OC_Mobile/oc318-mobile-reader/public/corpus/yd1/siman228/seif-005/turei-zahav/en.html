It is forbidden to ask the sage concerning his vow, etc. The Yerushalmi in the chapter “The partners” states this, and the Tur cites it. It concludes that if he forbade himself from benefiting from the townspeople, he may ask the sage of that town. One Tanna taught that even in the first case he may ask the town’s sage, because this is not truly like annulling one’s own vows. It is implied that the first Tanna forbids it because it resembles annulling one’s own vows. The Beit Yosef cites the Rivash, siman 406, accordingly. The Beit Yosef objected that annulling one’s own vow is different because the verse states, “He shall not profane his word.” There is no difficulty, for the comparison is not exact. Since the town’s sage benefits from the release, because after the release he may benefit from the person, he is an interested party and there is concern that he will not examine the matter adequately. We have already learned that release requires careful consideration. It is therefore like monetary law, where a judge with a personal interest may not judge. Rabbi Akiva’s release of the vow of his father-in-law, Ben Kalba Savua, is no objection, for the Rivash answers that he did not release it alone but joined with others. Nevertheless, after the fact the release is valid, since it is not literally a judicial proceeding. Regarding the Yerushalmi’s conclusion that one who forbade himself from the townspeople may ask the town’s sage, the Beit Yosef asked that he benefits from the sage’s release and therefore explained it in the opposite manner. This is forced and unnecessary. It is no different from siman 221, seif 2, where one prohibited from benefit may learn halachos and aggados from the person who imposed the prohibition. We do not forbid that because the ruling may enable him to benefit from something he had thought forbidden. Teaching him what is forbidden and permitted is not considered benefit. The same applies here. However, the Beit Yosef shortly afterward cites a responsum of the Rashba regarding one who swore not to serve as a communal collector, as discussed in the next seif, and that appears to disagree with the Yerushalmi and Tur.
