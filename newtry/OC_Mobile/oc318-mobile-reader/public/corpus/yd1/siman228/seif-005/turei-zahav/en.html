Forbidden to lend on his vow to a sage, etc. Yerushalmi chapter HaShutfin states thus and Tur brings it, concluding: one who forbade benefit of townspeople to himself asks the sage of the town — a tannaitic source taught: even initially one asks the sage of the town who is not like nullifying his own vows; from this implies the first tanna who forbids — his reason is it would be like nullifying his own vows; likewise Beit Yosef in name of Ribash siman 406; Beit Yosef challenged him — nullifying one's own vows differs for Scripture says "he shall not profane his word"; no difficulty, for he does not say he truly nullifies his own vows but like nullifying with kaf of comparison — meaning since the town sage benefits from this release, after release he will be permitted to benefit from this — it is like having interest in the matter and concern he will not examine well, for we already learned he must be settled in the matter; difficult likewise here; it is literally like monetary laws where any judge with personal interest does not judge that case — likewise here in this matter it was like law; Rabbi Akiva who released ben Kalba Savua's vow — Ribash answered he did not release by himself but through joining others; nevertheless after the fact permitted since vow was not truly like law as we wrote nearby.
