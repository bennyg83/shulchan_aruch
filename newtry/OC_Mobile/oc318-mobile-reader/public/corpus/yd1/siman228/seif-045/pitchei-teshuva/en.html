In a time of distress. Noda BiYehudah permits a husband to annul his wife's vow, made during difficult labor, to donate her garment as a synagogue mantle. Even if it was her melog property, he may annul because replacing it would impose expense upon him.
It may not be annulled. Mahari ben Chaviv distinguishes a conditional distress-vow, 'If Hashem saves me, I will do such-and-such,' which should not be annulled, from an unconditional vow uttered during distress, which may be annulled.
Like a vow made on the understanding of the public. Chatam Sofer explains that the comparison is only approximate. The distress-vow is regarded as made for Hashem's benefit after divine rescue, like a vow benefiting another who conferred favor. Nevertheless, for a true mitzvah, divine will is presumed to consent to annulment.
