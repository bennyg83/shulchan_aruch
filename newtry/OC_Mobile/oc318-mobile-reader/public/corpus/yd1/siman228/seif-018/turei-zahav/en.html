And it appears to me that it is proper, etc. The Beit Yosef explains the reason. Since the prohibition will exist when Shavuos begins, one should not wait until Shavuos. According to the Ran, in a vow dependent on days, once Pesach passed without payment the later prohibition at Shavuos is already considered to have taken effect because Shavuos will certainly arrive. It is therefore proper to release it after Pesach, though not necessarily immediately. Even the day before Shavuos is sufficient, provided he avoids violating it when Shavuos begins. According to the Rambam, however, the vow does not take effect until the prohibited day arrives, so he should be released again after Shavuos begins. The Levush wrote that release must occur immediately after Pesach because perhaps he is then obligated to leave the city so that he will not be there on Shavuos. This is not precise. No prohibition exists until Shavuos, so he may seek release whenever he wishes before then. It is preferable to release it immediately before Shavuos and again on Shavuos itself. The Beit Yosef cites the Ran regarding one who swore not to be in a certain city on Shabbos. There is doubt whether the vow takes effect immediately before Shabbos, permitting release then, or only after Shabbos begins. The Ran concludes that he should be released shortly before nightfall. According to the Shulchan Aruch’s ruling here that release should occur twice, the same procedure should be followed there, once before Shabbos and again on Shabbos. The same is proper for Shavuos.
