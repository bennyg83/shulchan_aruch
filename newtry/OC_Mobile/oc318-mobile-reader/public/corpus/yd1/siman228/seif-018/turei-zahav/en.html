And it appears good to me, etc. Reason explained in Beit Yosef: since there is prohibition at that hour when Shavuot festival enters — do not wait until Shavuot since some say he holds regarding vow dependent on days — after Passover arrived and he did not fulfill, prohibition applies automatically at Shavuot even though Shavuot not yet arrived since it will certainly come as Ran above — therefore good to release after Passover, not specifically immediately after Passover but even day before Shavuot, only beware not to transgress at entry of Shavuot day; since some say like Rambam vow does not take effect even on days until prohibition day arrives — therefore release him second time after Shavuot day entered. Levush wrote reason must release after Passover — perhaps immediately after Passover obligated to leave city so not in city at Shavuot therefore must release immediately after Passover; not precise in source and no prohibition until Shavuot therefore need not release immediately after Passover but whenever he wishes before Shavuot; appears better to release close to Shavuot itself and on Shavuot itself; Beit Yosef brings Ran's responsum one who swore not to be in city on Shabbat — doubt if vow takes effect close to Shabbat and they release then, or not until part of Shabbat entered and then release; depends on verse "but on first day you shall rest" — when first day partially entered; "seven days leaven shall not be found" — verses contradict; therefore said first day refers to Passover eve; likewise here forbidden at Shabbat entry; some say first day meaning not contradictory. Ran concludes release close to darkness of Shabbat entry; per Shulchan Aruch here requiring two releases — likewise release close to Shabbat and on Shabbat itself; likewise appropriate at Shavuot we mentioned — release first time close to Shavuot and second on Shavuot itself — so appears correct.
