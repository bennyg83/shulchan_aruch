A husband may annul, etc. Since annulment does not depend on the wife's wishes, and a husband may annul even without her regret, just as her view does not prevent annulment, neither does the public's view. The authorities write this. Rosh in HaSholeach further suggests that even if the husband waived his authority and allowed her to vow with the consent of others, that consent is ineffective. A woman ordinarily vows subject to her husband's authority, and Scripture entrusted annulment to him, so he may retract the permission he gave her. Rabbeinu Yerucham writes similarly. Although Rosh introduced this as a possibility, he appears to rule this way, for in his abridged rulings he states it plainly. Otherwise, Rav Huna's ruling that she loses her ketubah would be difficult, because the husband could simply authorize such vows. Tosafot, Rosh, and Ran therefore explain that a husband's annulment works even against public consent. This proves that the husband's prior permission is ineffective. All authorities who rule like Rav Huna imply the same. Bach's statement at the beginning of siman 234 that Rosh remained uncertain is incorrect, and he also overlooked Tur's explicit ruling in Even HaEzer, end of siman 96, that a husband may annul even after giving such permission.
