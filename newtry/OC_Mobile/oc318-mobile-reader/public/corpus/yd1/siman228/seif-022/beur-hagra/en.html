He vowed, etc. As stated in Gittin 46a: “How many are considered many?” We rule like Rav Nachman, as stated at the beginning of chapter 8 of Nazir, 57a.
Of his own accord. Meaning as above in seif 20. If he vowed on his own initiative, etc.
On the understanding of one person. Proof comes from a woman, who vows on her husband’s understanding, yet a sage may release her vow. So writes the Rosh in Gittin.
Or on the understanding of, etc. This is unlike Tosafot in Gittin, who appear to distinguish an oath made on God’s understanding. In Shevuot 29b, however, Tosafot write that one cannot derive such a rule. See the Rosh there. Ran writes that even a vow made on God’s understanding is irrevocable only when it concerns a mitzvah, like the oath at Sinai.
