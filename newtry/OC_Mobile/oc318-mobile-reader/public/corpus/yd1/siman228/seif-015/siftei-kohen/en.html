And some prohibit, etc. Bach objects that if release is ineffective even after the fact, why does Rav Nachman in Gittin 35b not require the vow to be specified out of concern for a prohibited matter. This objection itself is difficult. One could equally challenge Rav Pappa, who does require specification because of a prohibited matter. If release of a prohibition-related vow were always ineffective, the concern would be unnecessary, and the petitioner would not seek release at all. The Gemara also asks, according to the opinion that specification is unnecessary, that perhaps a sage will release the vow, which likewise proves that release can be effective after the fact. That entire discussion, however, concerns a vow whose prohibition is contingent upon whether the person committed a transgression, such as, 'Produce is konam upon me if I committed or commit a particular sin,' or, 'Food is konam upon me if I benefited from my ketubah.' There the sage is not permitting the transgression itself, but only the food that the petitioner prohibited. Here the vow itself binds him to avoid a prohibited act, and releasing it enables gambling or another transgression. There is no proof that such a release is effective even after the fact. Responsa Rabbi Eliyahu Mizrachi, siman 50, likewise appears to hold that a vow against gambling or a similar prohibition has no release even after the fact.
And if there is an obstacle, etc. See above, siman 208, seif 2, seif katan 12.
They release him, etc. Rama follows his position in responsum 103 that no one disputes Rabbi Toviah in this matter. Rabbi Eliyahu Mizrachi, however, writes that regarding a prohibited matter, even one prohibited only rabbinically, such as gambling, one does not release him. Even if he cries that he cannot uphold his oath, we disregard him. Beit Yosef cites responsa of Rashba and Ramban, siman 252, that disagree with Rama, and Kol Bo cites Rashba likewise. Perishah and Bach write in Maharam of Mintz's name that this applies specifically to a habitual gambler who has no other occupation and is thereby disqualified as a witness, as stated in Choshen Mishpat, siman 34. A person whose livelihood is elsewhere and who gambles only occasionally may be released.
