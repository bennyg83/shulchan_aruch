On the first day of Rosh Chodesh. With vows we follow ordinary speech, and in ordinary speech the first day is also called Rosh Chodesh. Although on Rosh Chodesh itself we do not obligate him to pay at the very beginning of the day, this case is different because the two days are distinct and a Torah doubt is treated stringently. So wrote the Beit Yosef in the name of the Rivash. It appears to me that according to the Rambam cited above in siman 220, seif 8, if he knew the year was a leap year he is forbidden until Rosh Chodesh of the second Adar. Here too, if he knew at the time of the vow that Rosh Chodesh Adar would last two days, he need not pay until the second day. This is a kal vachomer. There, R’ Meir and R’ Yehuda dispute which month is called unspecified Adar, and we interpret his words as the second Adar although ordinary people call the month after Shevat simply Adar. All the more so here, where everyone agrees that the second day is the principal Rosh Chodesh for all purposes, should we say that he intended the second day. There is then no doubt.
