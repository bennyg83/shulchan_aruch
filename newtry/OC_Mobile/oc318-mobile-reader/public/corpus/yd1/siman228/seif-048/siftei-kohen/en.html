One who swore, etc. The same applies to every Rosh Chodesh that begins with two days. This differs from one who swore to pay on Rosh Chodesh Nisan in a full month, whom we do not necessarily obligate at the very beginning of the day. Here the two days are distinct, and each is independently called Rosh Chodesh. Rashba therefore holds that one must be stringent because of a possible Torah doubt. Sema, siman 73, seif katan 27, cites this without qualification. It appears, however, that the rule does not depend merely upon doubt. The Gemara at the beginning of Konam holds even leniently that the first day is called Rosh Chodesh in ordinary speech, as explained above in siman 220, seif 4. Rashba may have intended a strained distinction, but it is unpersuasive. Rosh likewise writes that in ketubot and documents one writes 'on the first day of Rosh Chodesh,' showing that documents follow the same ordinary-language rules as vows. Accordingly, even without an oath, if a document obligates payment on or before Rosh Chodesh Adar, the wording is interpreted as in vows, as Rabbeinu Yerucham and Shulchan Aruch rule in Choshen Mishpat, end of siman 42. Beit Yosef in Choshen Mishpat, siman 73, also cites Rashba to this effect. He must therefore pay before the first day of Rosh Chodesh.
