Regarding 'You shall not take revenge,' etc. Here we do not say that a person would be too brazen to admit the truth, because these prohibitions are not so severe and therefore will not prevent him from speaking truthfully. Beit Yosef. Bach explains instead that because these prohibitions are written in the Torah, one who nevertheless vowed was certainly brazen.
But one does not open with the honor of the Omnipresent, etc. Likewise, one may not ask him, 'Had you known that your vow was a transgression, would you have vowed.' This follows from what is written afterward, and Bach rules likewise. The Mechaber's statement that one does not open with the honor of the Omnipresent because one who vows is as though he vowed by Hashem's life teaches the general rule. Thus, if one vowed about a matter that itself affronts Hashem's honor, one may not open by saying, 'Had you known that you were diminishing Hashem's honor, would you have vowed.'
