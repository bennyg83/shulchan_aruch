He vowed, etc., or, etc. See the Rosh there, s.v. “that you were,” and s.v. “upon,” etc.
That the vow is as though, etc. See Ran, s.v. “Rabbi Tzadok said,” and s.v. “they said,” especially his words, “nevertheless, granted,” etc.
Or that he should say, etc. See the Rosh, s.v. “they open,” where he writes “and they say,” etc.; and s.v. “they said,” where he explains that it is not exact, etc.
For a person does not, etc. In seif 11, for the same reason, the phrase “that he does not,” etc., follows Abaye’s explanation, since the discussion on 22a accords with him. Even where an opening is valid, all the more so in this case, as stated below, “and for this reason,” etc.
