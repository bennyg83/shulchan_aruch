One who vowed not to lend his vessels to his fellow, or not to inquire of his welfare — they open for him "If you had known you transgress 'do not stand idle' or 'do not hate your brother in your heart,' you would not have vowed." But they do not open regarding the honor of Heaven, to say "If you had known you would be lenient regarding the honor of Heaven" — for the vow is as if one vowed by the life of the king; or to say to him "If you had known that one who vows is evil in the eyes of Heaven" — for no man is brazen to say he would not have refrained for this reason, and he says he would have refrained even if it is not true, and thus the vow is not uprooted from its root. And for this reason they do not open with that [petach of] "If you had known they would open your ledger and scrutinize your deeds"; nor with that [petach] "everyone who vows deserves to be stabbed with a sword"; nor with that [petach] "everyone who fulfills it is as if he offered a sacrifice upon it."
