In a column it is written to say if you knew that you were committing an offense in Nadricht you would not have been arranged as a writer and made it difficult for May that the truce that was going on will not be raised and written here that you were cursed with the King's honor, but I will do so that it is not possible to say anything that he really regrets And Mr. Shapa was arranged and described in this section:
That a person is not brazen, etc. It appears to me: since the reason is perhaps it is not in his heart — therefore this opening does not work when others open for him thus, for he is ashamed to answer the truth in his heart; but if one person comes of himself and says "I ask you release my vows because it has now become clear there is sin in the vow," or when the one releasing the vow is like building in what was an opening — good and effective, for brazenness to say he would not have refrained does not apply — since if he wished he could have remained entirely silent; and Talmud and poskim language is precise — they said "we do not open with honor of Heaven" implying we do not open, but he himself can open; likewise regarding "we open only in present matter," etc. — wrote in seif 12 likewise so, as Rambam wrote explicitly Laws of Oaths chapter 6: we do not open in nolad, etc., concluding: but if he regretted of himself due to nolad and changed his mind — they release him; Rema wrote in seif 13 but if he regrets from the outset, etc. — one matter with "we do not open with honor of Heaven" — so appears to me.
