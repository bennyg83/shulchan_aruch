**(seif 5) Mumar to provoke.** There 13: heretic—even Israelite is worse than non-Jew and his shechitah forbidden; and Avodah Zarah 26b—and the version “kasever,” etc., “it was stated,” etc.; and Rambam ruled in chapter 9 Hilchos Aveidah and chapter 4 Hilchos Rotzeach as stated that he is heretic because R. Avahu and R. Yochanan hold like him; and so Shulchan Aruch below siman 158 seif 2.
**Even for one matter—R. Eliezer.** Avodah Zarah there baraita: “ate,” etc.
**Or that he is, etc., even except these two.** Chullin there baraita, etc.—the first part, etc.—apparently, etc., mumar to entire, etc.; and therefore except these two; and so in the whole sugya there.
**And one who is not concerned, etc.** So Beit Yosef inferred from Rosh who wrote: mumar to provoke—R. Yitzchak explained: since he is not concerned to slaughter properly he is like non-Jew who is not fit to slaughter; and this is his view as stated siman 1 derives from “and you shall slaughter”; and per first view in Gitin 45b and Rashi there s.v. mumar and moser, etc.; but per Rambam siman 1 specifically for provoking is called heretic as stated.
**(Likkut) And one who is not concerned, etc.** And Rabbenu Chananel permits—only he explains that Avodah Zarah “mumar to provoke” means in such a case; but to provoke literally—all agree he is heretic; and wrote poskim rule leniently as those who say he is mumar. Therefore per Rambam who ruled stringently it would be forbidden in Hagahot. But Maharshal challenged him—for he said there “ate a flea,” etc.—impossible to say such a case. Rashba in Terumat HaDeshen permits b'dieved even with “others see” even for provoking—only he was uncertain from what is written “except mumar,” etc., “what is the difference,” etc., and explains “mumar to provoke”; and Razah's view to permit with “others see” even l'chatchila—and he disagrees—examine (end quote).
