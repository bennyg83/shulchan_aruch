**(in gloss) One who slaughtered and brought out treifah, etc.** Per my view if while animal not yet removed still on presumption of kashrut witnesses came he slaughtered improperly and he denies he slaughtered properly—in this he is not disqualified. One can say he does not want to feed treifot; since he knows they do not permit on his word because witnesses contradict him—therefore he denied them to maintain himself as artisan who did not spoil his shechitah; if witnesses had not come he would not truthfully say animal kosher—especially if first one witness said he slaughtered improperly and he denied slaughtered properly; afterward another witness joins first testifying not slaughtered properly. Per Maharshal with one witness contradicting animal forbidden—one can say his denial since he knows they do not permit on his word with one witness contradicting—therefore denied so he need not pay if hired—for regarding money he is believed against witness.
