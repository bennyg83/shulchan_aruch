**And he says he slaughtered with the fine one, etc.** That is, when we know these knives were with him at the time of shechitah, then he is believed that he slaughtered with the fine one—for he does not leave the permitted and eat the forbidden. But if after shechitah they found these knives with him, his law is as explained in seif 7—examine. That which here we require “and he says,” and likewise what is written afterward “believed to say an expert”—Perishah wrote: in such a case we believe him in general even if he says nothing, only because wherever one can stand on the matter and clarify more, we clarify—necessarily. And per what is written in seif 15 regarding expert—that it must be “expert so-and-so”—if so he must say explicitly—and this requires study.
**And even if meat is found, etc.** It implies the Mechaber’s view that there is no distinction between poultry meat and animal meat; and in any case if there is an expert in the city he is believed to say an expert slaughtered for him. So Perishah seif 1; so explicit in Mishmeret HaBayit to Rashba explicitly (6a); Bach in last pamphlet wrote Rashba to the contrary and therefore ruled that with animal an expert does not help—unless as stated. And regarding animal: if there are Jewish butchers he is believed to say he took it from the slaughtered ones, as explained in Tur—examine Rashba. R. Yerucham wrote end section 32: specifically when slaughtered meat was commonly found that day cheap like nevelah—necessarily; brought in Beit Yosef and Bach.
**Expert, etc.** Specifically when he says “expert so-and-so,” as below end siman 119 (and see there seif 36); and so explained in Mishmeret HaBayit to Rashba there explicitly; so Bach—not like Perishah.
