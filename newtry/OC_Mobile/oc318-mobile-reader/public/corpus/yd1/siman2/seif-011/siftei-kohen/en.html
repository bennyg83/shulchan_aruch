**And needless to say if each held his knife.** Some say: if Israelite shechitah is kosher only through combination (meaning they slaughtered in siman 1 one opposite the other around the circumference as below siman 22 seif 3) and majority is only through combining both—but even if each slaughtered the majority of a siman (meaning this at the head side and this at the body side) it is disqualified—up to here Rashba’s wording. See siman 4.
**There were Israelite and unfit, etc.** In Or Zarua and gloss beginning Chullin they validate; so in Tosefta; so Maharshal there siman 7. He wrote: unlike below siman 5 with two holding the knife and one slaughtering for the name of an unfit matter—for there disqualification is the slaughterer’s intent, but here with an ordinary non-Jew or mumar who are forbidden only because they are not “children of shechitah”—when there is one who is fit to slaughter besides him, that suffices and it is kosher. So Bach; and so appears from Rambam who wrote the law of two holding the knife in chapter 2 and did not wait to write it until after all the disqualifications in shechitah in chapter 4—examine. Nevertheless Bach was concerned to be stringent.
