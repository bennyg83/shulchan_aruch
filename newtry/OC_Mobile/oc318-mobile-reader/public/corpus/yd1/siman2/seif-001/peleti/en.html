Even if he does not worship idols, etc.: Rambam wrote in chapter 4 of Hilchos Shechitah halachah 12 a great fence he fenced regarding a matter that even a non-Jew who does not worship idols—his shechitah is nevelah. From this Kessef Mishneh learned that a non-Jew who does not worship idols—his shechitah is forbidden only d'rabbanan. But Shach in seif 2 wrote the correct girsa in Rambam is regarding Kutim, but an idol worshipper is forbidden d'oraisa; he brought proof from Rambam end of chapter 2 of Hilchos Avot haTumah and from the Gemara that says “from you exclude”—mumar: even an Israelite mumar to the entire Torah except idol worship and Shabbat. Peri Chadash disagrees with him. And I say proof from the Gemara is not decisive—for it is obvious in this that Israel is more severe than a non-Jew; what relevance has mumar to a non-Jew if he does not worship idols? And Rambam in Hilchos Maaseh haKorbanot chapter 3 wrote we accept a bird offering from a non-Jew even an idol worshipper; and in halachah 3 wrote we do not accept from an Israelite mumar—if so a non-Jew is lighter than an Israelite. And in chapter 2 of Hilchos She'ar Avot haTumah there is no need; and that which is written “and you will ask—is it not forbidden to eat from the Torah,” etc.—one may say it refers to a complete idol worshipper; nevertheless Rambam holds his tumah is d'rabbanan, and therefore he distinguishes, etc. And in Rambam's Yad HaChazaka there is no decisiveness; but in the commentary on the Mishnah he wrote explicitly the reason it is forbidden with a non-Jew: stam machshavah of a non-Jew toward idol worship—see there; behold with a non-Jew who does not worship idols it is permitted d'oraisa, only Chazal forbade because lo pelug. And the matters are clear—not like Shach. See Kesef Hagadol on Yoreh Deah who cited in name of an early great authority that his view is also as I wrote, and so it is correct. And there is some proof for the words of Tosafot and Rosh from that which we say regarding mumar to idol worship his shechitah is invalid—the Gemara challenges from the ravens that brought bread and meat—bring the slaughterer of Ahab; and explain according to the conversation we are different; and they challenged Tosafot: per your view we also need according to the conversation, for meat hidden from the eye is forbidden; and explain this he knew according to the conversation they do not bring from non-Jews—but this is not the view that the conversation comes to permit the issur of Ahab's slaughterers. End quote; and still there is difficulty: perhaps according to the conversation he knew the slaughterers—even though they are mumarim to idol worship, nevertheless at the time of shechitah their thought was not toward idol worship and there is no heter for the issur here; and certainly the reason is not machshavah toward idol worship but because he perek on bar zevicha—all the more a non-Jew. (And Maharsha challenged: perhaps according to the conversation it came from Ovadyah's slaughter—and it is not a difficulty, for Ovadyah was not a fixed slaughterer for Ahab as the verse states—and in truth in Tosafot's difficulty one may question: behold meat hidden from the eye is only a gezeirah plain, and in Elijah's days perhaps they had not yet decreed; and tzarich iyun, for it appears the poskim hold in chapter 2 of Metzia that a bird everyone takes is forbidden, yet ravens brought.) If so there is no difficulty here for Rambam if he does not hold this. And in the words of Rambam there is proof from that which we say regarding gid hanasheh: they slaughtered a slaughterhouse for them and so took also gid—see there Tosafot s.v. kama de'amarin that they were careful then in shechitah even though they were not commanded; and it is difficult: did they know Yosef and his household were Israelites—they thought gentile; what helps that the man of the house slaughtered—gentile slaughter is nevelah and not bar zevicha; proven they knew Yosef's household are not idol worshippers and d'oraisa their shechitah is kosher. See Maharsha who resolved because of this difficulty Pharaoh gave them a slaughterhouse that they slaughter—and this is not the language of bearing payment. For Tosafot and Rosh the second version is not specifically a slaughterhouse but piercing—examine.
