Eats nevelot for appetite: I am doubtful whether one who slaughters for free without any benefit and without any presumption of benefit at all—if there is no permission even though he did not examine the knife—for from Torah he would transgress lifnei iver by slaughtering with a damaged knife, for he has no benefit at all from it and does not eat from it, and a person does not sin when it is not for himself—and all the more here, where all his corruption is for temporary appetite and there is no appetite here; therefore tzarich iyun, for I did not find the author write any rule on this.
Therefore one must forbid—and provided they know about him, etc.: this is Ran and Rambam's view. Tur wrote in name of Rashba one need not know—for he too is among majority found at shechitah, etc. (In truth per Tosafot 3b s.v. bodek who challenged why not say presumably he does not slaughter with damaged knife from fear they will examine afterward—for he is not afraid since from Torah they will examine after him, he is complete Israelite per his view; therefore among majority found experts fits well: since he stands to slaughter, presumably expert; if he belittles issur he fears they will examine him; if he does not think they will examine—this does not apply in shechitah laws, for even every Israelite not established expert—although majority experts—nevertheless per Geonim examination required at least after shechitah, therefore he fears. Per Ran: when Israelite stands over him, since standing here he could say “give your knife to examine”—he fears; without standing here, although obligated to examine, not so much fear—strained. Apparently proof for Rashba: Rav Ashi establishes mishnah “everyone slaughters” even mumar who eats nevelot for appetite; we say like R. Eliezer, not like R. Yehudah, from difficulty; “and all who slaughtered” refers to mumar—if no other knife, what helps others; if there is, why need others. R. Eliezer said not like Ravina who holds majority at shechitah are experts—hard to establish mishnah so “everyone slaughters” even Israelite mumar (and agree mishnah does not speak as now that knife not before us needs expert—speaks he is expert). He knows shechitah laws; if they do not know he knows—after he slaughters they test him; “all who slaughtered” and he is not before us to test—others see him, kosher; knife not before us not before us—speaks his knife examined and kosher; thereby all settled; all can say like R. Eliezer; R. Eliezer too without difficulty from “and all.” Proof for Rashba that now majority found, etc., includes mumar—can be settled with strain. In any case one should make poskim and Rashba a branch if another matter to be lenient. Perhaps resolve above difficulty: certainly those amoraim who disagree with Ravina and hold majority are experts in shechitah—also experts per Rashba's reasons. Only since Ravina disagrees and holds every person not presumed expert—we do not hold like him (some authors ruled like him); but for Ravina's matter regarding reckless mumar without saying majority found and without establishing him expert—one may be stringent like Ravina since he is stringent for every person. Difficulty thereby settled: amoraim themselves do not distinguish him from others—examine.
