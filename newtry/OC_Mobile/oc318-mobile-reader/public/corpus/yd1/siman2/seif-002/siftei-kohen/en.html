**An Israelite examines, etc.** The reason is that he does not leave the permitted and eat the forbidden; therefore when he examines the knife and gives it to him it is permitted even l'chatchila, for since there is a fine knife in his hand he will certainly slaughter with it and presumably will slaughter well and not delay and not press. Beit Yosef ruled that when he examined the knife before shechitah he need not examine it after shechitah even l'chatchila; after him followed the Taz and so Maharshal siman 6. And even per their words—the reason we say is that presumably he examined and found it fine; but if we know for certain he did not examine after shechitah, he must examine l'chatchila, for he is not better than a fit Israelite who must examine l'chatchila also after shechitah as below siman 18. But R. Yerucham wrote in the name of the Geonim and agreed, and so appears Ran’s view, that one must examine the knife after shechitah also l'chatchila, for we suspect perhaps on the hide it was damaged—for whatever can be checked we check, as above siman 1 seif 2 in the gloss. So too Bach. But b'dieved it is obvious it is kosher even if he did not examine after shechitah, for it is no worse than a lost knife after shechitah which is kosher as below siman 18—for since they examined before shechitah we establish him on his presumption.
**Even if he slaughters, etc.** Tur’s wording: “And even if he does not eat from it first”—meaning that even though they gave him a examined knife, he made it damaged or made other kinds of treifot in order to cause stumbling, and he will not eat but before the blind—he did not transgress “lifnei iver” placing a stumbling block, as the poskim wrote.
**Provided they know, etc.** For otherwise we would not say of him that the majority found at shechitah are experts; and even b'dieved, when it is not known clearly that he is expert, it appears one must test him first before shechitah whether he is expert, and one does not give him relying that they will examine after shechitah—analogous to the knife nearby.
**Until he examines it at the end.** Then he is believed to say he slaughtered with this knife and we do not suspect he slaughtered with a damaged knife and after shechitah this one happened to him—specifically in an hour or two when he had no leisure to go on his way and find another in the market; but when he had leisure a day or two on this we do not rely on him, and even in an hour or two one may be stringent. But if we come at the completion of shechitah, or two or three slaughtered animals were before him and he is still slaughtering and proceeding—since he is still engaged in shechitah and his knife is in his hand, we say he certainly slaughtered everything with the one in his hand. All this from Rashba’s novellae 5a, brought by Beit Yosef.
**And one does not give him l'chatchila, etc.** The reason: these forgot and did not examine the knife neither at the beginning nor at the end—they eat issur, for we suspect he may forget and not examine after shechitah and eat from his shechitah; unlike above siman 1 where it is permitted to give him l'chatchila to slaughter relying that they will examine at the end if he is expert—for there even if they do not examine at the end he does not eat issur, for the majority found at shechitah are experts—so all poskim and Ran wrote. Here we suspect lest onlookers think we need not examine him and they will entrust him and not examine after him. Bach wrote: per my view it appears that even if he examined the knife between himself and himself, one does not give it to him before onlookers unless he returns and examines it before them; and it is fit to be stringent as his words. And it appears the same if he says before onlookers “I examined this knife”—it is permitted to give him.
**Even if a fit person stands over him.** For an Israelite mumar does not fear, for he thinks they will not examine after him since he holds himself as Israelite.
**Without examining for him, etc.** Below siman 18 it is written that even a fit Israelite when slaughtering must examine the knife at the beginning before shechitah—and this is Rashba’s words in Terumat HaDeshen; he wrote the reason: we suspect he may forget to examine after shechitah. That which the Taz teaches here regarding an Israelite mumar—because we would rely on his examination that he will examine—we teach that we suspect lest he find it damaged and nevertheless slaughter with it, for he will not trouble to fix it. So Rashba and Bach; except Bach afterward added further to resolve that with a fit Israelite the reason is not “lest he forget to examine” but “lest it be damaged and he bless a blessing in vain.” Therefore per this view, if he already heard the shechitah blessing from another and went out, he need not examine—even so in the name of chapter 1 Chullin (17b) regarding “whence do we know knife examination from the Torah,” etc.—it implies the reason is not concern for blessing in vain—examine.
**One who slaughtered and brought out a treifah, etc.** It implies even when it is known for certain this time that he brought out treifah we do not remove him—and this law the Rav learned from Maharik; per my view Maharik’s words show explicitly the opposite, and so the meaning of all poskim, as I will explain. Maharik wrote (shoresh 33 ad 1) that one who testified on the slaughterer that he slaughtered treifah and he denies it—the witness is not believed against him, and even he himself is permitted afterward to eat from his shechitah; for even if it is true he did not slaughter well now as the witness says, nevertheless he was not established to be disqualified forever for this. Nevertheless everything is according to what sort of person he is—if that slaughterer already failed and there are indications, they remove him—end of his words. It appears the Rav did not examine Maharik’s words that Beit Yosef brought siman 1, which copied this wording, and from that the Rav inferred thus; but it is not so—for Maharik himself wrote that even if it is true as the witness says, nevertheless one is not absolutely disqualified forever for this; and at minimum if he wears black and does the teshuvah incumbent on him he returns to fitness. He did not make himself a piece of issur in his testimony at all, since the matter depends on the slaughterer’s thought whether for bad or good. Nevertheless everything is according to what sort of person he is—this implies explicitly that one who did not do so is disqualified if it is true as the witness says, even once, as this case of Maharik; except Maharik came to give reason and say that in the matter of the question where witness 1 contradicts him, he himself is permitted to eat from his shechitah from now on (unlike Yoreh Deah siman 1 seif 41, that if wine was libated as the witness says it has no remedy—which is not so here, for there is remedy when he does teshuvah); therefore the witness himself is permitted to eat from his shechitah from now on. On this he also wrote that if there are indications he already failed, even with one witness they remove him; but when we know for certain he brought treifah from beneath his hand, it is obvious it is forbidden to eat from his shechitah until he does proper teshuvah, as Maharik’s words prove and as stated; and so appears more to one who examines Maharik’s responsum itself; and so Maharshal Chullin chapter 1 siman 9—examine. Rambam too wrote chapter 10 Hilchos Shechitah: if treifah came out from beneath his hand they would excommunicate him and remove him, etc.—implies even once. So Taz end siman 65: a butcher after whom chelev was found—even once they remove him. So Maharshal Perek HaGadol simanim 16–17—examine (and above siman 18 seif 9). So Rivash siman 113; so appears siman 119; and Shach end seif 30—examine. Taz’s words here also appear to contradict what he wrote siman 1; and it appears he distinguishes: only when two witnesses saw he brought treifah from beneath his hand do we say since he was so brazen and was not careful before witnesses we suspect him and remove him; but not before witnesses—e.g. his mouth caused him to stumble and from his words it became known he brought treifah—we do not remove him. This is not clear, as stated. One must say what Rabbenu Yerucham wrote—“one who slaughtered and brought treifah”—means the matter is not clear that he brought treifah but there is concern he brought treifah, e.g. one witness testified—and this is strained. Uncertain.
