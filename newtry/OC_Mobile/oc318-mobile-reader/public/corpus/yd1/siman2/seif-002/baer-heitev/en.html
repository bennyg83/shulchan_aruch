**knife.** For he does not leave the permitted and eat the forbidden. Hagahot Beit Yosef wrote: in our time, one suspected of eating nevelot for appetite—one does not eat any food from him, how much more one does not give him an examined knife to slaughter and eat from his shechitah; one who did this is considered an absolute mumar.
**from his shechitah.** Taz and Shach wrote: if the knife is before us, one must examine the knife also at the end—for he is not better than an Israelite, lest on the hide it was damaged. But they dispute if he examined the knife for him and he goes to slaughter elsewhere so the knife will not come before us—then it is like a lost knife and permitted; and such is the halachah, for we follow leniency.
**himself.** Shach wrote: even if he will not eat from it first.
**shechitah.** Shach wrote: one must test him before giving him to slaughter; one does not rely on his examination at the end; and even b'dieved one should forbid if it is not known clearly that he is expert.
**at the end.** Shach wrote: on condition he did not have leisure to go after a knife.
**relying.** For these forgot and did not examine—they eat issur; unlike the earlier siman where it is permitted to give relying afterward—for there, even if he forgets and does not examine at the end he does not eat issur, for the majority found at shechitah are experts.
**at the end.** Shach wrote: this is a great rule—not to rely on examination at the end in a place where if he forgets and does not examine it will be forbidden. Ran wrote: here we suspect lest onlookers think we need not examine him and entrust him and not examine after him. Bach wrote: per this, even if he examines the knife one does not give it to him before onlookers unless he returns and examines before them, or says before onlookers, “I examined this knife.” That it is forbidden to give him even when a fit person stands over him—the reason is he does not fear, since he holds himself as Israelite.
**from his shechitah.** Shach wrote: all this speaks only when it is not clear that he brought treifah from beneath his hand, e.g. one witness testified; but otherwise it is obvious they remove him.
