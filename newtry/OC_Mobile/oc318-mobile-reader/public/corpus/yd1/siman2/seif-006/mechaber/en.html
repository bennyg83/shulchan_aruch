A mumar for one of the rest of the transgressions—one need not examine the knife for him; and according to the Rambam one must. And specifically a mumar for a transgression—but one who is disqualified for testimony for a Torah transgression—one need not examine the knife for him even according to the Rambam (Beit Yosef and thus wrote the Rambam). {Rama: But if he is disqualified for testimony because he ate nevelot although he is not a mumar for that—since he is suspected to eat nevelot his law is like a mumar for that (reasoning of Beit Yosef and Rambam).}
