And there are those who permit, etc. This is Levi, who argues with Rav; the Tur brought Rashba's view, who holds that even if he left ten and found nine — forbidden, for we are concerned perhaps those were taken and these are others. And one may ask: what is different from chametz on Pesach in Orach Chaim siman 439 seif 3, where some say this applies only when tied together? Here it is different, for since the Sages saw fit to decree on meat that disappeared from sight, they decreed in every place where we see that some change occurred to this meat in the world — we are concerned afterward for everything one might fear; they strengthened their words, as explained above:
And the custom is to be lenient, etc. This is when he finds it in the place where he left it; but if he found it elsewhere, even in his house — forbidden if he has no siman or teviyat ayin. Mordechai wrote in Perek Gittin, and thus: an incident came before me with a slaughtered chicken that they sent in the hand of an idolater without a guard, and they were in doubt to say perhaps it was another and was exchanged; and they went to the slaughterer and asked him if he slaughtered this chicken, and he said: I have no teviyat ayin upon it; however, I have just now slaughtered one chicken; but the chicken's owner recognized it and said this is it, and I permitted it, since the owner recognizes it like Rabbenu Tam, who said the halacha is not like Rav who said meat that disappeared from sight is forbidden, etc., end of his words. And Beit Yosef challenged: behold Rav too permits when there is teviyat ayin, for teviyat ayin is preferable to siman, as stated in the Gemara, end of his words. And Rashal wrote on this: besides his honor he did not understand, for according to Rav teviyat ayin does not help regarding a chicken, lest the Israelite did not slaughter it but an idolater slaughtered it — for shechitah is not a siman; if so, according to Rav who is concerned lest they exchanged it — here too we are concerned for such a case. But since it is settled like Levi, who holds we are not concerned lest they were exchanged — here too we are not concerned for such a case, since we do not see a suspicious circumstance, and we say: certainly it is the chicken that the Israelite slaughtered, since he says: I have just now slaughtered one chicken, end of his words — it implies he explained Mordechai's words that they sent the Israelite with the chicken via an idolater to bring it to an Israelite to slaughter, and he said perhaps the idolater slaughtered this one; and his explanation does not appear at all, for behold Mordechai wrote regarding a slaughtered chicken that they sent in the hand of an idolater, etc. — it implies it was already slaughtered. And further, why did he say they were in doubt if it was exchanged — they should have had concern perhaps this is it; rather that the idolater slaughtered it. And further, why did he write that for Levi it is kosher — certainly Levi agrees we are concerned lest the idolater slaughtered it, as long as it is not clarified that an Israelite slaughtered it; if an idolater brings a slaughtered chicken of an Israelite, even though we know an Israelite slaughtered one — nevertheless who will say this is it, for there is no majority of Israelite slaughterers here; and therefore one must say that the slaughterer explicitly says he slaughtered one for this idolater, and since the owner recognizes this one with certainty — certainly we do not say the idolater gave another to an Israelite to slaughter and slaughtered this one himself to trap the Israelite; we are not concerned, for the halacha is widespread in siman 118 that we are not concerned for exchanges by idolaters except where he has benefit from it — and this is permitted even according to Rav, for all hold like that law in siman 118, even Rif and Rambam and Shulchan Aruch who ruled here like Rav; if so, the challenge returns to its place against Mordechai. And further, Mordechai did not write that the slaughterer says 'now' to this idolater — and Maharshal also followed Rashal in explaining Mordechai's words, and explained that it is forbidden according to Rav because one cannot recognize it by teviyat ayin, for when he brought it to slaughter it was alive and now it is slaughtered, etc. And this too is difficult, for then according to Levi we should also be concerned because of concern for shechitah. And further, we say explicitly in the Gemara Perek Elu Metziot with several amoraim that they found their birds slaughtered among a majority of Israelite slaughterers; and likewise in a baraita: his kids were stolen and he found them slaughtered, etc. — and it implies teviyat ayin helps for them; and this is simple. If so, Beit Yosef wondered well and understood well; only it appears to me to answer Mordechai's words on this matter: certainly it deals according to the plain meaning of the matter, that this chicken was already slaughtered permissibly; only that one sent his fellow the slaughtered chicken via an idolater without a guard, and Mordechai holds that for Rav siman or teviyat ayin does not help except where there is no suspicious circumstance and reason for exchange, but from mere general stringency — such as when some meat is found in the hand of an idolater who took for himself and most sellers are Israelites — why should we say it was exchanged; they were stringent in this that a siman is needed, according to Rambam who rules like him in seif 1. But if an Israelite sends via an idolater slaughtered meat or a bird where there is concern the idolater exchanged for his benefit, leaving another worse than it — here we descend a level according to Rav, that neither any siman nor teviyat ayin helps, lest the idolater forged and also made this siman, such as that siman Rav relied on, that he cut the meat in three corners — certainly if the idolater wants to forge for his benefit he will trouble himself also in this; what craftsmanship is there in this cut. And likewise teviyat ayin does not help, lest he troubled himself and brought another that resembles this one in appearance — this is common. But Levi was not so concerned, and according to him even when sending via an idolater teviyat ayin suffices; and so is implied from Mordechai's words who wrote before this incident, and Rabbenu Tam: the halacha is not like Rav, etc.; therefore if he left a piece of meat, etc. — but if he found it in another place we are concerned until he has simanim and recognizes it by teviyat ayin (meaning: or recognizes it); and likewise if he sent it via an idolater the law is thus, for we say in Perek Ein Ma'amidin (Avodah Zarah daf 39): barrels are forbidden with one seal, end of his words — for at first glance it is difficult why he wrote 'and likewise,' etc., and what did he innovate here. And according to what I wrote it is settled: for Rav it is not permitted here by teviyat ayin, and since it is settled like Levi — permitted by teviyat ayin. And if so, Mordechai wrote well here regarding sending a slaughtered chicken via an idolater: at first they wanted to seek permission because of the slaughterer if he would recognize it, and it did not succeed in their hands, and they permitted because of the chicken owner's recognition by teviyat ayin — and this is Levi, as I wrote above:
