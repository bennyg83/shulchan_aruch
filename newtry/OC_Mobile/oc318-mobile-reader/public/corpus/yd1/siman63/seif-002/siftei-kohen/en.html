If he found it in the place, etc. And Taz wrote that if he returned and found it, even though he did not pay attention whether it is in the place where he left it or not — they are not accustomed to forbid it, even in a place where most slaughterers are idolaters, etc., end of his words. And it requires further study from where he derived this; for from the Rosh's words, who wrote the reason that we are not concerned for a raven that exchanged it and brought it to that very place — it implies: but otherwise it is forbidden; and so is the implication of the poskim who wrote that ravens from their locale take:
In a place where all the sellers are Israelites who sell kosher meat. Meaning: it is known that they never sell except kosher meat; if not so — such as when they sometimes sell treifah meat, in a place where they announce that there is treifah and they announced — then even because of one treifah they forbid all the pieces; and when meat is found there it is forbidden because it has the status of kavu'a. And likewise if meat is found in the hand of an idolater — forbidden; and even though most animals are presumed kosher — nevertheless it is a rabbinic enactment not to buy from the idolater l'chatchila in a place where they do not announce, or in a place where they announce and announced thus — so wrote Tosafot Chullin (daf 95a) in the section 'and regarding found,' etc. — see there:
That all the sellers, etc. 'All' is not precise — the majority is like the whole. (Payment of these laws is in siman 1 seif 4 and siman 110 and 118 and what is written there):
And there are those who permit. Thus wrote the Ran in the name of Sefer HaTerumah: presumably it is permitted only regarding meat found in a city whose slaughterers are Israelites, for we are not concerned for ravens of the world; or alternatively, in a city whose slaughterers are idolaters — such as when he left it in his house and found it in the place where he left it — for in every such case we are not concerned for a raven; but in a city whose slaughterers are idolaters, and he found it in a place where he did not leave it — we are concerned, lest ravens from their locale took it, and these idolaters require siman or teviyat ayin and did not find it in the place where he left it, end of his words. And he means: a city where most slaughterers are idolaters — and so wrote Rashba in his novellae in the name of Sefer HaTerumah, and ruled thus. And it is explained in Rashba that the same applies if pieces of meat are found on the road — and likewise if most passersby on the road are Israelites — permitted, and we are not concerned for a raven as long as we do not see that a raven took it; and all the more so if he found meat in the market in a place where most slaughterers are Israelites — permitted. All this is the view of the aforementioned poskim (and so wrote Rokeach siman 97, that one who finds an animal and the like in a place where most are Israelites — permitted, and we are not concerned for meat that disappeared from sight; and so Issur VeHeter general rule 22 din 7: where he left meat in the street in a place where most idolaters are found, and he went and returned and found it in the place where he left it — permitted, end of his words). And it appears that such is also the Rosh's view in the name of Rashbam in all this — for behold he comes to settle why Rashba ruled like the plain meaning of the mishnah, that it deals with meat found in the market where most are Israelites; and further that he wrote (to settle that incident in Perek Elu Metziot that deals with a majority of Israelites). But where ravens took and returned it — we are concerned perhaps they exchanged; it implies: presumably when we did not see that it was taken we are not concerned in a majority of Israelites. And further that he wrote that these incidents in the Gemara that required simanim or teviyat ayin — one may say they were in a city that is mostly idolaters; and since it was lost there is greater concern — and these incidents imply that he did not find it in the place where he left it, as the aforementioned poskim wrote. And also from the Rosh's words, who wrote 'and since it was lost,' etc. — it implies thus; and so Bach, that the Rosh holds those incidents were where he did not find it in the place where he left it — and nevertheless they forbade only in a majority of idolaters. And further, presumably that which the Rosh wrote 'and since it was lost there is greater concern' comes to say that it was lost and he found it not in the place where he left it — for otherwise why say it at all; and 'and since it was lost,' etc. — it should have said only that it deals with a majority of idolaters; rather, certainly he holds that in a majority of idolaters alone they did not forbid except where there is also the circumstance that it was lost. And what the Rosh wrote at the beginning of his words, that that which we are not concerned for meat that disappeared from sight is where he found the meat in the place where he left it — that we are not concerned perhaps ravens exchanged it and brought it to that very place — he means: we are not concerned at all for meat that disappeared from sight, even in a majority of idolaters. All this is clear according to the Rosh and Rashbam; and so is proven explicitly in Asheri Perek Elu Metziot — see there; and so in Darkei Moshe, that the Rosh's view is like Sefer HaTerumah; and so is implied in Maharshal Chullin siman 22; and so in Derishah and Perishah in this siman and siman 1 — not like Bach in this siman seif 4, who went outside the line of reasoning and did not understand the Rosh's words thus; and therefore he entered great forced explanations and challenged Derishah and Perishah in the last section improperly. And the matters are clear as I wrote. Also that which Bach wrote that such is the Tur's and Rabbenu Yerucham's view like his words — it is not so — see there. (And he explained that incident of Rav Kahana where ravens threw kidneys, etc., and they found it in the place where he left it — and this is a matter that has no dawn; and in Perek Elu Metziot the Rosh explained explicitly like Tosafot Chullin, that incident of Rav Kahana was in all places around which most were Israelites — see there. He also forced to explain that Rashbam did not need to say according to his view that those incidents were mostly idolaters, etc. — all these are forced and far-fetched.) The Tur wrote in the name of Rashba that even if he left ten and found nine — they are forbidden, for we are concerned perhaps those were taken and these are others; and in Taharat HaKodesh they learned from chametz. And the Rambam wrote in Taharat HaKodesh at the end of general rule 32 that the custom is not like Rashba when he left ten and found nine, but rather they permit in every case; and so Issur VeHeter general rule 22 din 10 wrote that it is permitted when he left ten and found nine, for we say a cat or mouse dragged it; and even a complete half-rib, lest a mouse stole it, end of his words. However, one may say that the Rav in Taharat HaKodesh did not say that they are accustomed to be lenient except regarding pieces that are not tied — for such is the custom to leave several pieces together without being tied; and in such a case perhaps he holds like Tosafot's and the Tur's view in Orach Chaim siman 439, that regarding chametz too it is forbidden only when tied together; and the Mechaber brought it in Orach Chaim there. But when tied one may not be lenient, since in such a case regarding chametz all forbid; and Rashba and Ra'ah daf 122 forbade here where there is no majority of permissibility; and so Taz wrote anonymously that when he left ten and found nine it is forbidden; and so Maharshal Chullin siman 22, that when tied it is obviously forbidden, but when not tied it appears to him to permit, since the Rambam testified that such is the custom; and Issur VeHeter permits entirely; and so Maharshal there permits when not tied. And it is possible that Rashba and Ra'ah themselves forbade only when tied, like Tosafot's view regarding chametz; and so Perishah in the name of his teacher, that Rashba deals with such a case — not like Bach in the last section seif 5, who challenged Derishah and wrote: 'And in my composition I brought that Rashba himself wrote that here even when not tied,' etc. — and he erred in his own words, for in his composition he wrote thus from his own words after Rashba's words, and understood that it is the conclusion of Rashba's words; and it is not so, for it is not found at all in Rashba's words — see there:
