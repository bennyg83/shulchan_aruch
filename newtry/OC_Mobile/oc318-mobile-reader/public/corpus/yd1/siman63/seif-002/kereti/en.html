And there are those who permit, etc. — this is if he left it in the place where he found it without change — permitted even if most slaughterers are gentiles, for we say: it is the same piece he took; but if he did not find it in the place where he left it — there is suspicious circumstance, we are concerned for a raven that exchanged it — not from a far place, only from a place near that city; and if in the city most slaughterers are Israelites — permitted; if not — forbidden unless with siman and teviyat ayin as above — so is implied from Beit Yosef's words; see Shach s.k. 8:
In the place where he left it; and if he left ten and found nine — Shach's and later authorities' view: if they are not tied — certainly we are not concerned; but if tied (and it should say: the usual tie, for otherwise it is a tie-siman) and he left nine and found eight — there is reason to be stringent in a city where most slaughterers are gentiles:
