For everything that separates, etc. — See above siman 110 seif 3 and what is written there:
If most of the sellers, etc. — It is stated in the Gemara Chullin (daf 95a) that regarding cooked meat one follows the majority of meat eaters: if they are idolaters — forbidden; if they are Israelites — permitted. And if it is found in the hand of an idolater — forbidden, for presumably it is his own; and likewise in the hand of an Israelite — permitted:
And it is not known from whom he took, etc. — And the Rambam wrote below siman 110 seif 3 that specifically when he did not see the idolater take it; but if he saw the idolater take it, it is as if he took it from there and it is forbidden — see there:
This is Torah law. — For it is settled everywhere that we follow the majority, as it is written: 'After the many to incline':
And the Sages already forbade all meat, etc. — It implies that whether found or in the hand of an idolater they forbade it because of meat that disappeared from sight; and so is implied from the words of the Rav HaMagid. And accordingly, even if it is known with certainty that the idolater took kosher meat from an Israelite — nevertheless it is forbidden because of meat that disappeared from sight — not as it appears at first glance from Taz, who in such a case permits, as he wrote: 'But the Sages already forbade, etc., even if they saw the idolater take it from an Israelite, lest he sold him nevelah or treifah, and there are those who permit if he saw,' etc. — it implies: but if it is known with certainty that he sold him kosher meat — permitted; and this is not so, but rather as I explained. And further, one may ask: if there is concern that the Israelite sold him treifot, how is it possible that 'there are those who permit if he saw'? What difference does it make that the sellers are Israelites — in the end the Israelites also sell treifot? Rather, certainly we require that the Israelites sell kosher meat and not treifot at all, as will be explained in s.k. 10. And it appears that Taz also holds that we require specifically that the Israelite sell kosher meat — only that for stringency he says: even though he certainly sells kosher meat, we say perhaps it happened that he sold him treifot, and 'there are those who permit if he saw' since they always sell kosher meat; and nevertheless he did not need this reason, but rather the main reason is as the Rav HaMagid wrote — because of meat that disappeared from sight. And also where most sellers sell kosher meat with certainty — one does not need that he saw, for we follow the majority; and this is proven from that which it is settled that meat found in the hand of an idolater is permitted specifically when he did not see the idolater take it — as written in s.k. 3:
Forbidden. — The reason the poskim wrote is that we are concerned perhaps creeping things and birds exchanged it. And the Rosh wrote that even regarding whole animal flesh we are concerned perhaps ravens exchanged it, as is proven in the sugya — and it is an extra stringency, end of his words. And he means in the incident stated there, that Rav went to the house of Rav Chanin his son-in-law, saw an animal hanging (meaning: an animal hung up) and did not remove his eyes from it. But according to what Tosafot wrote there, that incident was mostly idolaters — nothing is proven from it that wherever there is concern for idolaters it is forbidden; but for creeping things on whole animal flesh we are not concerned. And so ruled in Sefer Taharat HaKodesh. And further, it appears that when hung on a nail we are not concerned for creeping things — all the more so on whole animal flesh — not like Bach seif 1, who wrote in the name of Tosafot like the Rosh, and it is not found thus in Tosafot; and incidentally he added the Rosh's words into Tosafot's words. And Derishah's hagahot challenged: behold above siman 1 the Tur wrote that one who finds a slaughtered animal in the market is permitted; and he answered that here it deals when it is whole without a head, and above it is with the head, therefore shechitah is a siman, end of his words — and he was not precise, for behold the Tur wrote below siman 118 anonymously in the name of Rashba that shechitah is not a siman, and this is proven explicitly from the Rosh's words in Perek Elu Metziot — see there; and see Mordechai Chullin regarding what is written and from the view of Rabbenu Tam — it appears to me that shechitah is not a siman. But in any case it requires further study, for the Rosh and Tur hold that in a majority of Israelite slaughterers, or in a majority of Israelites, it suffices wherever we do not see that a raven took it — as written in s.k. 8; and so answered Derishah itself siman 1 seif 22 — and Derishah's hagahah omitted his father's words:
