A kohen shepherd. Meaning a shepherd who is a kohen.
The owner is considered in possession. Rabbi Tarfon reasoned that an owner grants the kohen a share in his courtyard so that the kohen will immediately acquire firstborn animals born there, because a person wishes a mitzvah to be performed with his property. The courtyard would then be jointly owned, like property deposited by two people, and the animals divided. The law follows Rabbi Akiva: because granting a share would cause the owner loss, he does not do so, and the case is like one claimant seeking to remove property from the owner's possession. The burden of proof therefore rests on the claimant. [Citation 5.]
