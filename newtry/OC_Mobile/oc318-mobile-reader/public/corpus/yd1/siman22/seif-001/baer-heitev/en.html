To puncture them. And Shach wrote: even if at the time of shechitah his intent was to cut it limb by limb—nevertheless, since it is its way to roast it whole we are concerned lest he reconsider about it and roast it whole; and at least he must slaughter two veins. Pri Chadash wrote: therefore when he slaughters he should tilt the knife slightly to the sides in order to slaughter the veins.
Whole. And Shach wrote: however, if he did not puncture them at shechitah, it is permitted to roast it by cutting the veins with the flesh around them from the neck; and so appears from that we remove the head in a bird—end.
Finger. Meaning the width of a finger; and Shach wrote: and even for us who hold roasting spreads throughout until 60—nevertheless here removal suffices; and see below siman 65 seif 1 and siman 76 seif 3 from the law of picking out other threads; and specifically in veins in the neck we say thus that removal is required, unlike threads in the neck where peeling suffices; and the distinction will be explained there in the gloss siman 65.
The threads. And Shach wrote: and even this peeling is only a general stringency, and not like Bach who forbids in salting the measure of removal without reason; and furthermore it is astonishing on Bach, for behold in siman 72 regarding heart he agreed with Rama that peeling suffices; and in this veins are certainly not worse than heart.
Permitted. And Shach wrote: since we do not know how much emerged, one must estimate against all the threads; and see below siman 65 seif 1 in the gloss; and behold Taz wrote: for us who hold the piece itself becomes neveilah even in other prohibitions—here there must be 60 in that bird alone; and the same law applies below siman 72 regarding heart; but Shach wrote it is not similar here to heart—see there; and Shach wrote: according to those who are stringent above, here he would have required 60 also against the peel, for it was already forbidden in salting; but since it is only a general stringency, 60 against the blood suffices.
