At the time when it is convulsing. In Beit Yosef he brought the view of some poskim that if he did not slaughter all the simanim but only a majority of them and wants to roast it whole, he must cut them too like the veins; and he rejected them that one need not do so; and in Darkei Moshe he wrote that there is prohibition in the matter, for it appears like pausing in shechitah as below siman 23 seif 5; and even in a way where it is not fitting to say so, such as puncturing them, I have not seen anyone be concerned for this; and it is possible because also regarding veins they did not practice thus—end of his words.
The measure of removal. Specifically regarding veins we say thus, and not regarding other threads that require cutting, as explained siman 65—see what I will write there on this.
Like the thickness of a finger. Meaning the width of a finger, as written siman 105.
Afterward one removes the threads and it is permitted. For we say: as it absorbs, so it expels; and some are stringent—he holds it is like blood in the eye and is not called emitted blood; and see siman 72 from this law.
In everything in the pot, etc. It appears that according to what we hold in siman 92 that the piece itself becomes neveilah even in other prohibitions—here there must be 60 in that bird alone; and if not, 60 is required against the entire bird; and so it is in siman 64 seif 12 regarding the membrane on the liver—see there.
To nullify the blood. According to those who are stringent that nearby he would have required 60 in cooking also against the peel, for it was already forbidden in salting; but since it is only a general stringency, as written siman 72, therefore 60 against the blood in cooking suffices.
And therefore they practiced, etc. In Darkei Moshe he cited in the name of Mordechai, and thus: nevertheless it is astonishing—for we cook every day and roast birds without cutting, even though he did not slaughter and did not puncture the veins; therefore it appears as Rabbenu Tam that specifically with the head is it called whole, but we who cut off the head do not need to cut the pieces of the veins; and that which is said that he cuts it limb by limb is not precise; or alternatively, because an animal's neck is large one must cut the neck in two pieces, but a bird whose neck is thin, removal of the head suffices—end; and he wrote in Derishah on this; and from here is an answer to those who cook a bird whole with the head except that Rama wrote at the end of the siman that an unspecified bird—its veins were cut at shechitah, etc.—end. And he did not correct anything in this, for the reason we say an unspecified bird, etc., is from the force of presumption that he certainly did properly as we cited there; and behold now we stand that veins are not cut—if so, what permission is there when the head was not cut? Therefore one must be careful that anyone who wants to cook the bird whole with the head shall cut its veins at shechitah—that is, all the time it is convulsing; and in truth I heard from men of action who were at such a meal and did not want to eat from that bird; and Rashal wrote: and we practice to pick out after the veins and cut them, only we are not careful to puncture them or slaughter them at shechitah; and we need the words of Rabbenu Tam that removal of the head suffices—end.
