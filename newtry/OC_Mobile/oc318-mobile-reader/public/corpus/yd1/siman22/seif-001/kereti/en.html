One must slaughter, etc.—even if his intent is not to roast it as one piece but only to cut it into limbs—nevertheless, since the way is to roast it as one piece, veins must be cut, lest he roast it as one piece—Shach in the name of the poskim; and it must be said: even though it is only a rabbinic doubt regarding blood in its cooking, which is rabbinic—nevertheless, since it is common to roast it as one piece, even in rabbinic matters we are concerned; if so, for us who do not commonly roast it with the head and it is cut into limbs, there is no concern l'chatchila to slaughter the veins.
The veins—see Shach who wrote that cutting two veins suffices; and see Peleti that I wrote for us regarding every vein.
While it is convulsing; and if he cut only a majority of the simanim, he need not cut them all, for blood is not absorbed in them like in veins; and on the contrary there is reason to prevent, for it appears like pausing—Beit Yosef and Taz.
And if he did not do so, etc.; and Ra'osh's view is that if he cut the veins from the neck and removed the flesh around them with them, there is no further concern—so Shach and Pri Chadash; and in Tur HaAroch daf 69 it does not appear so, for the blood already spread in all the limbs—if so, what will removing them help; and so several authors; nevertheless Ra'osh is fit to rely on, and it is a rabbinic prohibition as written.
The measure of removal—since it is roasted and since vein-threads of the neck are not so thick, we require removal; unlike other blood threads that are thick—the view of Tur HaTerumah that peeling suffices; see Peleti.
And it is permitted—even though in roasting removal is required, in salting it is entirely permitted even without peeling; and Shach gave the reason as it absorbs so it expels; and see Peleti that I wrote the gidin themselves are in place of peeling.
