The veins—Shach wrote ninety veins; and in truth so it is in the authors, and it is from the Tosefta; but it appears not every vein, only designated ones; and so the Tosefta wrote that veins implies known and recognizable ones, not every vein; and Rambam explained for us in his explanation of the mishnah: veins are two gidin pulsing in the neck on the two sides of the trachea, etc.—end; behold specifically these, for they are the source from which blood spreads to enter and come; and therefore we who are not expert in them so much must slaughter all of them; and therefore Shulchan Aruch ruled anonymously and did not write like R' Yitzchak two veins; and he too wrote at least two veins and intended the aforementioned veins—and it is simple.
The measure of removal; and below siman 65 regarding threads that were roasted he forbids only a general stringency—Darkei Moshe there—they are very thick; and according to the simple meaning of the authors, if he cut veins in the neck he need not puncture any thread more, and the like, for everything depends on the vein-threads of the neck; and in this manner it deals below siman 65; and since without this many permit, he was lenient with peeling.
And it is permitted—Rama; Shach wondered: since roasting requires removal, in salting there should at least be peeling—see there that he distinguishes in roasting we do not say as it absorbs so it expels, for the moment he removes from heat it absorbs and does not expel, unlike salting—and this is strained; as Pri Chadash wrote; and to me it appears certainly blood in threads is blood in the eye enclosed in a vessel; and salting forbids only a general stringency; if so, the threads or veins themselves are the peel, as said regarding scales siman 70 and so here; but in roasting that requires removal like finger-thickness, truly the thickness of threads joins within that thickness; and the piece becomes neveilah does not apply, for the forbidden cannot forbid—and I wonder at the latter ones who overlooked this.
In everything, etc.—the acharonim wondered that in the bird alone there must be 60 adhesive threads and it is prohibition of adhesion, and they extended—and everything is strained; and what appears to me: that which R' Yehuda said one must puncture threads at shechitah—and if this is certain that if not done at shechitah then when blood is hot it certainly will not emerge anymore—this is impossible to be lenient, only a reasoning perhaps it will not emerge and is enclosed in threads; but there is no certainty here; and R' Yitzchak's view is to disqualify b'dieved—for R' Yehuda said only l'chatchila; if so, this is a rabbinic prohibition doubt regarding blood in its cooking, which is only rabbinic; and for all this we are not stringent in rabbinic prohibition; and with this also is resolved what Darkei Moshe wondered in Beit Yosef that we estimate only against blood and not against the threads; and Rama wrote: are we to know how much blood they absorbed; and Shach agreed with him; and according to what Tosafot Chullin daf 97b wrote in the eyelid that we say plainly in the piece itself we estimate—for if from what emerged from it we would know—and they wrote in the Tosefta: since shechitah blood is rabbinic, it is a rabbinic doubt and permitted; if so, even in threads where we do not know how much was absorbed and emerged from them, since it is rabbinic prohibition there is to permit; and with this one can defend our teacher Beit Yosef—and understand.
In all the threads; and Bach's view: since it was salted first and forbidden as a general stringency, 60 is required also against the peel; and see what Shach wrote that one need not be so stringent—see there; and I did not understand Bach's words at all, for even though the peel of threads in a bird is flesh, the blood inside the threads is blood and there are two types here; if so, if 60 in the pot nullifies the blood so it is not Torah-forbidden, all the more so there is 60 to nullify the taste of flesh forbidden in the peel—go here there is no taste and go here there is no taste; therefore prohibitions nullify one another—and this is explained below in laws of mixtures at length; and in truth otherwise I did not know what our rabbis Bach and Shach grappled with—behold it is explained below Tosafot's view that a thing forbidden in a peel—even if afterward nullified—does not forbid a mixture and 60 is not required at all; if so, what is this wonder; and see below siman 69 on this; and see Shach on this siman 69 to distinguish between peel standing in existence and everything requires study as written above; and Shach extended in vain.
