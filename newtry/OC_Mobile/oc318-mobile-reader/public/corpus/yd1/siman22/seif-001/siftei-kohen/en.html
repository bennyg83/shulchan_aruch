In a bird one must slaughter, etc. — Even if at the time of shechitah his intent is to cut it limb by limb—for since it is its way to roast it whole we are concerned lest he reconsider about it and roast it whole—so all the poskim.
The veins. — Rabbenu Yerucham wrote one must slaughter at least two veins—thus; and so in the Tosefta, and Mordechai brings it chapter HaShochet, and so Bach, and so in the chiddushim of Rashba chapter HaShochet (Chullin daf 30) and chapter 5 daf 95, and in Tur HaAroch daf 68 end of seif 1.
While the blood is still, etc. And if he did not do so, etc. — And it is explained in Ra'osh chapter HaShochet that if he did not puncture them at shechitah, it is permitted to roast it by cutting the veins with the flesh around them from the neck—see there; and so wrote Rabbenu Yerucham; and so appears from that we remove the head in a bird in the gloss.
And he shall cut around them the measure of removal. — And 60 parts are not required, for roasting does not spread throughout like salting—so Ra'osh, Tur, Rabbenu Yerucham Netiv 15 beginning of sign 25, and Rashba in Tur HaAroch; and above s.k. 6 I wrote that even for us who hold roasting spreads throughout until 60, removal suffices; and see below siman 65 seif 1 and siman 76 seif 3 from the law of picking out other threads.
The measure of removal. — Specifically in these veins in the neck we say thus that removal is required; but in threads in the neck, behold Rama wrote in the gloss siman 65 that peeling suffices; and the distinction will be explained there, with Heaven's help (and so it is required to distinguish for Ra'osh and Tur—see there); and so in Darkei Moshe siman 65 he distinguished explicitly between veins and threads in the neck, and Perishah brings it siman 65 seif 3; and I wonder at Bach who understood from Rama's words in Tur HaAroch end of general rule 34 that there is no distinction between veins in the neck and threads in the neck regarding whether if roasted without their removal; and therefore he challenged him—and behold this is certainly not so, for in Tur HaAroch there he deals specifically with other threads that are equivalent to threads in the neck regarding whether if roasted without their removal, as explained siman 65—see there, for so it is explained explicitly in his words.
And if he salted thus, etc. — It requires study—where did he find to distinguish between roasting and salting, for we hold everywhere salting is like boiling, and it is obvious to one who forbids roasting the measure of removal, he forbids in salting the measure of peeling—as below siman 105 and many places (and see siman 72 s.k. 9). And also Agur in the name of Maharil wrote plainly that in roasting removal is required and in salting peeling. And I saw written in Tur HaAroch general rules 34 beginning law 3: and if he did not remove those gidin, afterward remove the threads and it is permitted—so appears from Ohel Moed general rules 19—and so is simple to the poskim, for we say regarding salting: as it absorbs, so it expels; but Agur wrote in the name of Maharil that even regarding salting one must peel around the threads—end; and it requires study, for it appears Ohel Moed and Agur do not disagree at all, for Ohel Moed deals explicitly with threads in the neck where even Ra'osh agrees that even in roasting b'dieved it is permitted without peeling as written in chapter 5 and Tur siman 65 and so Ohel Moed there explicitly on his name that both in salting and roasting it is permitted without peeling; and Agur deals explicitly in veins—see there—and Darkei Moshe brings it: since they require removal in roasting, in salting peeling is required; and it appears Rama holds: since by law even in roasting removal is not required, for we hold as it absorbs so it expels regarding veins as Rama wrote siman 72 seif 2 as Rashba and Rabbenu Yerucham wrote and we do not hold as Rashba wrote in Tur HaAroch daf 28 that since the threads themselves do not entirely leave blood status after removing from fire the threads still emit some blood in the flesh and the heat of the flesh lacks power to expel what was absorbed after removal from fire and it is hot into hot and blood does not spread—therefore one must remove the threads and remove their place—end; if so, 60 would be required for us who hold below siman 105 and many places blood spreads and all the latter ones and Maharil himself wrote removal suffices; rather we hold that even after removal from fire we still say as it absorbs so it expels as below siman 76 seif 4; therefore by law it is entirely permitted as Tosafot and Ra'osh in the name of R' Yitzchak and so Semag and Sefer HaTerumah and so appears Agudah and so Maharshal chapter HaShochet siman 4, and R' Yitzchak's reason Maharil wrote in responsum siman 58—that is because of as it absorbs so it expels; only stringently we require removal due to concern for Rashba's words and his supporters; therefore specifically in roasting one can say thus, for even Rashba did not say except in roasting because after removing from fire one cannot say further as it absorbs so it expels, unlike salting where this cannot be said; therefore Agur in the name of Maharil who requires peeling in salting is only a general stringency even for Rashba and supporters; but by law peeling is not required for anyone, for as it absorbs so it expels regarding salting; and proof—that heart and veins have one law, as Rashba wrote in Tur HaAroch there and so other poskim; and on the contrary many poskim hold heart is worse than veins as below siman 72, and regarding heart Maharil himself wrote in his Ohel Moed that if salted with the flesh it is permitted by rinsing—apparently what he requires here in veins as peeling is only general stringency as Rama wrote in Tur HaAroch there and here in the gloss; therefore certainly in neck threads peeling is required from the force of stringency; therefore Ohel Moed who did not write this stringency holds even in veins peeling is not required; and now the matter that Rama wrote so appears from Ohel Moed, etc.—behold it is explained explicitly in Ohel Moed; rather he meant so appears from his words that one need not be stringent at all since he did not write this stringency—it appears to me, and understand well.
To peel. — And even this peeling is only a general stringency, as written in the previous s.k. that not like Bach who forbade in salting the measure of removal without reason and proof; and what he wrote that so appears from responsum of Maharil siman 58—I examined there and found no such implication; and furthermore it is astonishing on him, for behold in siman 72 regarding heart he agreed with Rama that if salted thus with peeling it suffices; and it is explained that veins are not worse than heart; also what he wrote that Tur agrees that in salting he forbids the measure of removal and Agur holds like Rashba that below siman 105 in roasting he forbids the measure of removal and in salting the measure of peeling—end—is astonishing, for behold Tur siman 105 ruled like Rashba and so in many places and so is the view of Ra'osh and the poskim in many places—see siman 70 and siman 91 and siman 105 and what he wrote there.
In everything in the pot, etc. — It requires study, for here Rama ruled anonymously like the Mechaber, and so in Tur HaAroch there; but in prohibition of adhesion he ruled below siman 72 regarding heart that there must be in the bird itself 60 against the heart, and so we hold in siman 73 and 92 and many places; if so, here too why does the entire pot combine to nullify—let us say there must be specifically in the bird itself 60; and so Mahari ben Naim challenged in responsum siman 89 and ruled thus; and so appears the view of Perishah seif 8, who wrote that what Tur wrote that in the entire pot we estimate—that is because he goes according to his reasoning that he holds we do not say if the prohibition is adhesive that it must be in that thing to which it adhered 60—end (or alternatively he goes according to his reasoning that he holds in siman 92 we do not say the piece becomes neveilah in other prohibitions—it appears to me); if so, for us the pot does not combine; and it appears Rama's view that since the veins themselves contain no blood, therefore since they separate between the blood and the bird it does not hurry to absorb in the bird from other things in the pot—for the reason of prohibition of adhesion is because it was absorbed first in the piece that adhered, as below siman 72 s.k. 18—not so here; and proof for this from what Maharai wrote in his rulings siman 98 on a kidney that was cooked in its fat, that 60 is required against the entire kidney, for the kidney becomes neveilah from the start since the prohibition is adhesive in it, and we do not rely on the variant reading that the membrane separates, because even if the kidney was salted it became forbidden—for the membrane does not separate; also examination is required whether the membrane was perforated—end; if so, regarding veins that certainly separate—for they are hard—and furthermore because of this reason the blood in them is considered emitted blood regarding which we say as it absorbs so it expels as written in s.k. 6 (for regarding blood in the eye we do not say as it absorbs so it expels as below siman 69 and 70 and 72 and 76 and many places)—if so, the same if it was cooked, for they separate so that it does not hurry to absorb more than other pieces in the pot; and it is similar to what we say siman 72 s.k. 7 that even if there is 60 against the heart the heart itself is forbidden because sometimes the blood dries inside it and does not emerge outside, and it is not forbidden from the reason the piece becomes neveilah—for regarding this its separation helps (meaning its portions) that it does not hurry to absorb more than other flesh—and the same here; for although regarding heart adhered to the bird we require there 60 from the bird itself—that is because there is blood in the flesh of the heart itself too, which is not so in veins—and understand well.
The blood, etc. — The language of Rashba and Tur: if there is in it enough to nullify all the threads in 60, etc.; and Beit Yosef wrote: but Ran wrote if there is enough to nullify the blood in all the threads it is permitted—and his words appear correct; therefore Rama in Darkei Moshe challenged him, for Rashba's reason is because we do not know how much blood emerged from them (and as explained in Tur HaAroch explicitly, and Beit Yosef brings it); if so, Ran also agrees to this; and the blood of the threads that he mentioned is not precise, but the essence of the prohibition is because of blood; and if it is possible to estimate the blood within the threads, Rashba also agrees that one need not estimate except against the blood; and that which he mentioned against the threads is because we do not know how to estimate without the threads—so it appears to me—end; and his words are correct—and that is what he wrote in Tur HaAroch end of general rule 34 that Ran's view is like Tur; therefore he wrote below siman 65 seif 1 in the gloss that if he did not remove them and cooked it whole without cutting, 60 is required against all the threads—thus; and I wonder at Lechem Hapanim chapter HaShochet who wondered at Rama that he did not emend here that 60 does not suffice to nullify the blood in the threads—and he overlooked the words of Darkei Moshe cited above.
In all the threads, etc. — Bach wrote: for us who hold below siman 92—and what he wrote in Tur HaAroch there that so is Tur's view—must be like Tur's view—see there, and understand well: the piece becomes neveilah in all prohibitions requires 60 also against the peel that was forbidden first in salting; and so Agur in the name of Maharil, and Darkei Moshe brings it; and it does not appear so from Rama in Tur HaAroch there and from what he ruled anonymously here like Tur; and so appears the view of Pri To'ar and other acharonim; and it appears he holds we do not say the piece becomes neveilah except where the piece itself was forbidden by Torah law, but not in a peel the sages forbade, which is only a stringency; and so in Ohel Moed general rule 29 law 2 in the name of Rabbenu Tam and Maharam—see there; and so Rabbenu Yerucham Netiv 15 sign 13 in the name of R' Yitzchak Meir, and Beit Yosef brings it end of siman 105, that we do not say the piece becomes neveilah except when it stands by itself, but not in a peel, etc.; and also Tosafot chapter 5 daf 96 in the name of Ra'em and Ra'osh there mentioned this reasoning—see below that we do not say it becomes neveilah in salting as Tur HaAroch general rule 38 daf 2 wrote; and although it appears there that he permits only in a place of great loss specifically—nevertheless here, since there is another side to permit and also poskim permit b'dieved even if he roasted the bird whole, and all the more so in salting as written in s.k. 6; and even for those who forbid if roasted, it is permitted in salting as written there—if so, there is to permit even not in a place of great loss; and so Ran end of chapter 25 explicitly, and Beit Yosef brings it siman 92: that which in all prohibitions the piece becomes neveilah is because they decreed on account of meat in milk, and they did not decree except similar to meat and milk that the Torah forbade through cooking, and not in salting; and the same (as below section 87)—and see below siman 92 s.k. 14 and siman 134 s.k. 16.
