In what manner one must slaughter the veins. And in it are 2 seifim.
In a bird one must slaughter the veins (meaning: threads from which blood emerges—and Yonatan in a foreign tongue) or puncture them at the time when it is convulsing, while the blood is still warm so that it will emerge and not congeal inside; and if he did not do so, he may not roast it whole; and if he roasted it whole, he shall discard the veins and cut around them the measure of removal, which is the thickness of a finger: {Rama: And if he salted it thus, afterward one removes the threads and it is permitted (and see s.k. 6); and some are stringent to peel around the threads (Agur in the name of Mahari Weil).} And if he roasted it whole, he scrapes and picks out the threads; and the rest, if there is in everything in the pot enough to nullify the blood in all the threads—60 parts—it is permitted: {Rama: And if he removed the head from it, it is not called whole (Mordechai, Hagahot Ashiri, Agur, and responsum of Maharil siman 58 there); and therefore they practiced removing it from whole birds; and see below siman 76 whether the veins were not slaughtered.}
