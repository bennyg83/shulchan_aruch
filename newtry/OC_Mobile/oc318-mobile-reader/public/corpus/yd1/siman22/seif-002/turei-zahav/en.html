An animal need not puncture. In Tur he brought the view of BeHaG that one must cut the veins also in an animal, and this is against Rav Chisda in the Gemara as Beit Yosef wrote; and he answered on this two answers; and it appears to me that in BeHaG he explains: since in a bird, even if he certainly intends to cut it limb by limb, he must slaughter the veins because the way of the world is to roast it as one piece—automatically what he said in an animal that one need not cut the veins deals also in this way, that he certainly will cut it into pieces; then one need not cut the veins, for such is the matter; and the reverse automatically: if he is not certain, he must also in an animal, since sometimes he roasts it as one piece too—as said, it is correct; and so I found from my teacher, may his memory be blessed.
And an unspecified bird, etc. For they certainly did according to law and cut the veins at shechitah.
