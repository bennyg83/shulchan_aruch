An animal, etc.—see what I wrote in Kereti that only one who cuts into limbs many pieces and splits it—if it depends on the animal and was not entirely detached from it, nevertheless it is called whole; and it is possible BeHaG does not hold so, and therefore he ruled even in an animal veins must be cut; and all wondered at BeHaG—he himself cited R' Chiya's words that R' Yitzchak said only in a bird; see Taz s.k. 8 what he wrote on this; and it appears certainly BeHaG holds thus—for there are kids and lambs that are roasted as one like birds; only R' Yehuda holds one must cut the thigh and remove gid hanasheh and the kink of the gid—for R' Yitzchak holds in several places there is Torah prohibition in gidin; if so, it does not suffice that he not take it before roasting and cooking, and behold this is like cut into pieces; but according to what we hold there is no Torah prohibition in gidin, and also its fat according to Rabbenu Tam they were not more stringent than the body of the gid—if so, he can leave it thus in the thigh until after roasting and cooking and afterward remove it, for its emission does not forbid; if so, he may roast it whole properly and therefore he forbids; or it is possible how is it common that he roasts kid and lamb as one—for he must cut foreleg and hind leg to give priestly gifts, and ways of exempt animals are not common; and most animals are obligated, and therefore it is not common to roast them as one; therefore R' Yehuda who spoke in Eretz Yisrael where they are obligated in priestly gifts—it is properly not common; but we outside Eretz Yisrael who have no priestly gifts—it is properly common to roast them as one; and this is BeHaG's view.
