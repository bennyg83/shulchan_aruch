A seal. The reason is that because of its frequent use, the non-Jew will trouble himself and forge the seal. Bach writes that after the fact, the authorities who permit one seal are sufficiently authoritative to rely upon. See above, siman 130, seif 2.
And he sent them. This applies even if they remained in the non-Jew's possession only briefly. Bach writes that the Jew may leave them for twenty-four hours, after which they are permitted even initially, because the absorbed taste is nevertheless detrimental; this concludes his words. Shach writes that this is astonishing. We say that pots and bowls that remained in a non-Jew's house may be left for twenty-four hours, but regarding wine vessels, the principle of detrimental taste does not apply even if they remain for many days, as stated below in siman 137; this concludes Shach's words. Taz writes that earthenware vessels are not included in this rule. If their first use was by a non-Jew, they require iruy even though wine was not stored in them permanently. If they initially belonged to a Jew, they require only what is explained in siman 135, seif 4. Taz also rules that even after the fact all types of vessels must be prohibited, unlike Rama. See above, siman 122, seif 9.
