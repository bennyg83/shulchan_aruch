And he sent them, etc. And if it even stayed one hour in the hands of the gentile, kashering is required — as the matter is — Tur; and so Rashba also explains regarding gentile leather bottles — scraped new ones are permitted, old tarred ones forbidden etc.; and so is implied from Mordechai there regarding "when he sends out flasks" etc., from which that one who sends an empty barrel etc.; and so Rabbi Yerucham — kashering is required even if it did not stay in his hands but only briefly; and so is implied from Rashba in Tosafot HaRosh 144b; and Bach also brought more poskim who hold so, and not like Ra'ah and Ran and the opinion of Tosafot and Semak Avodah Zarah 148 end of 53 (and Agudah). Bach wrote that even be'di'eved they require kashering — in my humble opinion they have no decisive ruling; on the contrary, it is more plausible to explain their language that they speak only lechatchilah — see there. Also what Bach challenged from Mordechai in the name of Rabbeinu Tam regarding knives, writing that Rabbeinu Tam does not hold like the Tur and the aforementioned poskim — this is not so; specifically there Rabbeinu Tam permits because he wrote "perhaps they were used for something that spoils," but here they are wine vessels; and so Bach wrote that the Tur and poskim agree that food is not forbidden be'di'eved if used without kashering — one can say perhaps they were used for something that spoils etc. — this is not so, for they are wine vessels; rather one can say they permit for another reason: since it was not in the hands of a gentile except briefly, therefore certainly no prohibition was absorbed in the vessel, and also certainly it was not used — it suffices that they decreed the vessel requires kashering because briefly it is considered as putting it for permanent use; but certainly be'di'eved the food is permitted. Also what Bach wrote that a Jew can leave them overnight and afterward they are permitted even lechatchilah because nosen ta'am lefegam etc. — this is astonishing; for specifically regarding pots and bowls that stood in the house of the gentile Ra'ah says one leaves them overnight, but regarding wine vessels nosen ta'am lefegam does not apply even if one leaves them many days, as below siman 137; also above siman 122 seif 7 I wrote on this — see there.
