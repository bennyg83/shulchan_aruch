If her husband is in the city, etc. This applies even according to the opinion cited by the Tur that immersion at its proper time is not a mitzvah. Nevertheless, because of the mitzvah of procreation, it is certainly a mitzvah.
And she is permitted to immerse on Friday night. The Tur cites the opinion of Rabbeinu Chananel, who holds that immersion at its proper time is a mitzvah. He brings proof from the Yerushalmi, which says: "A woman who has a fixed period, her husband calculates the days of her period and has relations with her. This teaches that a woman is forbidden to remain in her state of impurity." End quote. My father-in-law, of blessed memory, wrote that this is not a proof, for one can say that the reason she is forbidden to remain impure is lest her husband have relations with her. It appears to me that this can be answered as follows. When they said that her husband calculates the days of her period and has relations with her, they did not say this only where the woman knows with certainty when her husband will return, but in every case, even where she thought he would not return at that time. It is therefore proven that a woman is forbidden to remain impure independently of the concern regarding her husband, because immersion at its proper time is a mitzvah. The husband relies upon this and has relations with her, for she certainly acted according to the law. According to this, immersion on Shabbos should be permitted. Rabbeinu Chananel, however, holds that the halacha is that immersion at its proper time is not itself a mitzvah, but it is a mitzvah because of procreation. Therefore, the Rama ruled that it is permitted specifically when her husband is in the city, as we will write below.
After childbirth, etc. Terumas HaDeshen, siman 295, was asked about a woman after childbirth who immersed after 80 days following the birth of a girl, or within that period, in a case where, because of her clean days, she could have immersed 7 or 8 days earlier, but chose to wait because she still lacked sufficient strength. May she immerse on Shabbos, even though she could have immersed earlier, or not? He answered that the Mordechai wrote that immersion on Shabbos was permitted only because immersion at its proper time is a mitzvah, whereas this case is not at its proper time, since she could have immersed earlier. He afterward wrote that when it was impossible for her to immerse earlier, such as because of circumstances beyond her control, one can say that even according to Rabbeinu Tam, who rules that immersion at its proper time is not a mitzvah, she may nevertheless immerse because of the mitzvah of fulfilling the marital obligation. Even when it is not the scheduled time for that obligation, it is considered a mitzvah for a man to gladden his wife through marital relations, as stated in the chapter Eilu Devarim in Pesachim, etc. He concludes there that women have practiced not to immerse on Shabbos unless the husband is in the city and it was impossible for her to immerse earlier. In such a case, the immersion is for a mitzvah because of the marital obligation. Although the second chapter of Beitzah states that any impure person may immerse on Shabbos because it appears as though he is merely cooling himself, nowadays it is different, since people do not commonly cool themselves in any body of water on Shabbos, and therefore it does not appear as though she is merely cooling herself. These are his words. Thus, he permitted it only when she could not immerse before Shabbos, such as because of circumstances beyond her control, and her husband was with her on erev Shabbos. He excludes only a case where the husband is not in the city at the time of her immersion. It is obvious, however, that it does not help if he did not arrive until erev Shabbos when she could have immersed earlier. The fact that her husband was not in the city during the week does not help her, for she nevertheless should have immersed during the week. The Agur and Mahariv, cited by the Darkei Moshe, and my father-in-law, of blessed memory, were more stringent, holding that even if she was prevented by circumstances beyond her control and could not immerse before Shabbos, and her husband arrived on erev Shabbos, she may not immerse on Shabbos. Their proof is from the Mordechai cited above. The Beit Yosef disagrees with them and concludes leniently even when immersion was possible before Shabbos, because it appears as though she is cooling herself. He does not cite the words of Terumas HaDeshen above at all. The Darkei Moshe writes: Since the practice is to immerse on Shabbos, we see that the halacha does not follow the Mordechai. If so, it should be permitted even when she could have immersed earlier. Nevertheless, it appears proper to uphold the custom by saying that whenever she could not immerse earlier, even because of a stringency the women accepted upon themselves, it is called immersion at its proper time, and she may immerse on Shabbos; but where it was possible, she may not. Nevertheless, it does not appear to me proper to be stringent in this where the custom is not to be stringent. End quote. The Rama ruled accordingly here, making the matter dependent upon local custom. His words are astonishing. The Mordechai, the Agur in the name of the great authorities, Mahariv, and Maharil entirely forbid immersion on Shabbos, while Terumas HaDeshen permits it only when it was impossible earlier, but forbids it when it was possible, even if her husband arrived on erev Shabbos. Why, then, did the Rama rule leniently where there is no contrary custom, when her husband arrived on erev Shabbos, even if she could have immersed earlier, and likewise after childbirth? This is contrary to all these great authorities. My father-in-law, of blessed memory, also disagrees with the Rama and is stringent everywhere. He writes, however, that according to Terumas HaDeshen above, it is implied that she is forbidden on Shabbos specifically when her husband was in the city and she could have immersed earlier, but if her husband was not in the city and arrived on erev Shabbos, she may immerse on Shabbos even though she could have immersed earlier, because women do not immerse when their husbands are not in the city. This is imprecise, for Terumas HaDeshen certainly did not intend to permit such a case. Rather, his intent is that her husband must be in the city at the time of immersion. There is no distinction whether he arrived on erev Shabbos or earlier. In every case, it must also be one in which it was impossible for her to immerse earlier. We should therefore rule according to Terumas HaDeshen, whose words are intermediate between those who forbid entirely and the one who permits entirely. Namely, whenever she could have immersed before Shabbos without any circumstance beyond her control and did not immerse, she is forbidden to immerse on Shabbos, even if her husband arrived on erev Shabbos. Whenever she could not immerse because of some circumstance beyond her control, similar to the case of Terumas HaDeshen that we cited, she may immerse on Shabbos in every place.
One should not separate the chafifah from the immersion. This means that we are concerned for Rashi's opinion, cited by the Tur in siman 199, that she must perform chafifah during the day before her immersion and not at night. Here this is impossible because it is Shabbos. She would therefore have to perform chafifah on erev Shabbos, creating a separation between the chafifah and immersion. Although it is ruled below that after the fact it is sufficient for her to perform a thorough chafifah at night, that applies specifically when her immersion falls on Motzei Shabbos. Here, however, she was negligent by waiting until Motzei Shabbos.
For it is forbidden to have first relations with her on Shabbos. This is because he acquires her through these relations, as stated in Orach Chayyim, siman 339, and Even HaEzer, siman 63. Therefore, a widow's wedding should not be held on Shabbos when her immersion falls on Friday night. If she immersed before Shabbos, he must have relations with her specifically on erev Shabbos after the chuppah. Yichud in one room does not suffice to permit him to have relations with her for the first time on Shabbos. So wrote the Mordechai, chapter Mashilin, in the name of Maharam. So too wrote Maharil in the laws of Shabbos, in the name of Maharash, the teacher of Maharil, that complete relations are specifically required. Although Responsa Masas Binyamin, siman 90, writes that yichud also effects acquisition in a place suitable for relations, one should nevertheless be stringent, since great authorities forbid it. This will be explained further in Even HaEzer, siman 63.
And there are those who are lenient and permit, etc. It appears to me that this refers specifically to the immediately preceding case, namely, one who marries a widow, where by law first relations are forbidden on Shabbos, her time for immersion then arrived, and she was not negligent in any respect. In the preceding law, however, namely, in a place where the women practiced stringency after childbirth, or where her husband was not in the city until Shabbos, it is certainly forbidden even on Motzei Shabbos. Since she could have immersed before Shabbos, why should she immerse on Motzei Shabbos and needlessly separate the chafifah from the immersion? So it appears to me.
