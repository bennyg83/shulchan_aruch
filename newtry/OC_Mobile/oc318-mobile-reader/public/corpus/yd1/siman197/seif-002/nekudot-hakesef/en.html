(Siman 197 in Taz s.k. 4) He was astonished at Rama and those who follow his view; and in Siftei Kohen s.k. 3 I upheld Rama's words, only that I wrote briefly. And now I am compelled to elaborate and copy what I said in my book Vayivchanu — examine my words. And this is my language: Bach Chadash wrote that one should be stringent, for this stringency is from the law according to the plain meaning in Mordechai, per the view of Mahari"v that tevilah at its proper time is a mitzvah — see there. And per my humble view it appears that one should not be stringent, as I will explain. And it appears to me that the main point is that we hold tevilah at its proper time is not a mitzvah, as Tosafot wrote in chapter HaMapeles (Niddah page 30) and in the first chapter of Yoma page 8 side a: ma'asim b'chol yom — you have no woman who immerses at her proper time, and not at the time of immersion she keeps day against day for zavah. And in the last chapter of Niddah we also say: since nowadays we made them all doubt zavot, we immerse them on the daytime of the seventh — implying that they never immersed by day at all until here is his language. And what Bach Chadash said on this — per my humble view this is not a difficulty, for since nowadays, after R. Zeira's stringency, this is her time — after she sees a drop of blood like mustard she counts seven clean days; if so, now it is incumbent upon her to immerse immediately, and if she does not immerse immediately after her count she transgresses the mitzvah until here is his language. He did not intend well, for certainly Tosafot do not prove from what they immerse after counting seven clean days, but from what they do not immerse at their proper time — namely, keeping day against day for zavah and niddah — and on this one should not answer anything at all, for certainly because of R. Zeira's stringency she will not transgress the mitzvah by not immersing at her proper time. And further, it is possible for her to fulfill both. And so ma'asim b'chol yom — you have no woman who immerses at her proper time when her husband is not in the city; and so widows and virgins who are niddot do not immerse at all at their proper time. And so in all these countries, where even if she did not have relations she does not immerse until after five and seven days, as explained in the previous siman seif 11 — and this is an extra stringency — if so, how would she transgress the mitzvah by not immersing at her proper time? From all this it appears that we hold tevilah at its proper time is not a mitzvah. See there. Nevertheless, even so it is permitted to immerse on Shabbat, as Terumas HaDeshen wrote siman 255, and Beit Yosef — for from Beitzah page 18 it is proven that even when not at her proper time it is permitted to immerse on Shabbat, for there is no prohibition in the matter because it appears like heating water (miykar). And certainly so it is proven there, for Beit Shammai hold that a person and vessels immerse on erev Shabbat — obviously even erev Shabbat is their time, and even so Beit Hillel say regarding a person on Shabbat. And so it is proven per my view in Tur Orach Chayyim, that in siman 327 he wrote a tamei person is permitted to immerse on Shabbat from his tumah because it appears like miykar; and in siman 613 he wrote similarly, that all those obligated in immersions immerse in their usual manner on Yom Kippur. And Rav Tam wrote that one does not immerse — and the baraita "they immerse" means specifically according to the one who says tevilah at its proper time is a mitzvah; but we hold it is not a mitzvah, therefore let him wait until tomorrow end quote. And in chapter 2 of Beitzah we say: satisfactory on Shabbat, on Yom Kippur (meaning that miykar is not relevant, for bathing is forbidden on Yom Kippur) — what is there to say? Rava said: is there anything permitted on Shabbat (and it does not appear like fixing a person, because it appears like miykar), and on Yom Kippur it is forbidden? Rather, since on Shabbat it is permitted, on Yom Kippur too it is permitted (and see Bach siman 554 end of seif 3). But certainly it is clear to the Tur that on Yom Kippur, since there is also a prohibition of bathing, it is not permitted except for the reason that tevilah at its proper time is a mitzvah; however, for this reason alone it would not be permitted, because in any case it appears like fixing a person. And so Tosafot wrote in Shabbat page 111 side a, and these are their words: and if you say, let us establish this baraita "they immerse in their usual manner" according to the one who says tevilah at its proper time is a mitzvah, and Rav according to the one who says it is not a mitzvah — one may say that because of tevilah at its proper time being a mitzvah alone, the prohibition of fixing a person would not be permitted end quote. But since on Shabbat it is permitted because it appears like miykar, on Yom Kippur too it is permitted. However, on Shabbat it is permitted even when not at her proper time; but on Yom Kippur it is not permitted except because of mitzvah. And so it is proven in Tosafot in Beitzah there — for why do we say there "since," etc. — we challenge: perhaps it is not so, that only on Shabbat it is permitted and not on Yom Kippur? And we answer: it cannot enter your mind, for we taught "all those obligated in immersions immerse in their usual manner." And Tosafot wrote, and these are their words: "all those obligated in immersions immerse in their usual manner" means specifically for those who hold tevilah at its proper time is a mitzvah; but now, since all our women's immersions are not at their proper time, they do not immerse on Yom Kippur until here is their language. And at first glance there is a difficulty in their words, for the mishnah of Beit Shammai and Beit Hillel deals with immersion not at its proper time. And further, if so, why does Rava need a reason to distinguish between a person and vessels — because a person appears like miykar? Let it emerge from the fact that regarding a person tevilah at its proper time is a mitzvah. And further, in chapter HaMapeles and Yoma Tosafot wrote that Rabbi Yossi bar Rabbi Yehuda holds that both Beit Shammai and Beit Hillel hold tevilah at its proper time is not a mitzvah — if so, there is a difficulty from the mishnah in Beitzah, and it is forced to say that he also argues on that mishnah; if so, we should not rule like him. Rather, it is certainly simple that on Shabbat, even when not at her proper time and even when it is not a mitzvah, it is permitted; but on Yom Kippur it is not permitted except because of mitzvah. And now the difficulty is resolved, that Tosafot wrote so regarding immersion on Yom Kippur, and did not write so regarding the mishnah of Beit Shammai and Beit Hillel regarding immersion on Shabbat — but certainly as I separated. And so it is proven from the words of Semag, lavin 65, page 22 end side b, and Rashba, who wrote anonymously in the laws of Shabbat that a tamei person is permitted to immerse on Shabbat — implying even when not at his proper time. But Mordechai wrote that Rashi ruled that tevilah at its proper time is not a mitzvah, and from this he forbids immersing on Shabbat (and Mahari"v followed him), and he is alone against all the poskim, and the judgment does not follow the halachah — and not like what Darkei Moshe wrote, that one may say to Mordechai that what we say in Beitzah, "a tamei person is permitted to immerse on Shabbat," means specifically according to the one who says tevilah at its proper time is a mitzvah, etc. — and this is certainly not so. See there, and further it is proven that Tosafot wrote at the end of Niddah, and the Rosh there, and Semag lavin 111 page 38 side b, and Semak, and Agudah, and other poskim in the name of Rashi, that one should not comb on Friday evening and immerse on Shabbat, because it is two leniencies end quote. And it is explained below siman 198 seif 7, explicitly, that it is permitted to immerse on Shabbat according to Rashi, even though Bach wrote that Rashi wrote so only according to the words of Rabbi Meshulam, and he himself does not hold so — this is not acceptable to the mind. And it is more reasonable to say, as Terumas HaDeshen wrote, that Mordechai in the name of Rashi also deals only when her husband is not in the city end quote. And further, in chapter Kol Kitvei (Shabbat page 121) Tosafot wrote that the version of Rashi and Rashi is "one need not immerse," etc. — that even according to the one who says tevilah at its proper time is not a mitzvah, it is permitted to immerse, as implied at the end of tractate Yoma, etc. See there. If so, it is clear to Rashi that even when it is not a mitzvah one may immerse, even on Yom Kippur; and so Tosafot conclude in Beitzah there, and in chapter Shmonah Sheratzim (Shabbat page 111 side a). And it is certainly proven from the whole sugya at the end of Yoma. See there; examine. General principle: it emerges that even though tevilah at its proper time is not a mitzvah, it is permitted to immerse on Shabbat. But Terumas HaDeshen wrote that nowadays they practice to be stringent regarding a yoledet and the like, and he forced a reason, writing and these are his words: since the custom now is to be careful and prevent heating on Shabbat, even though there is no reason in the matter — nevertheless one cannot say that it appears like miykar, for they are careful about heating, and it is proven that their intent is to immerse, and it appears like fixing. And for this reason women practice to be careful not to immerse on Shabbat unless her husband is in the city and it was not possible for her beforehand, for in such a case it is a mitzvah because of onah end quote. It is implied from his words that when her husband is not in the city and he comes on Friday evening, even though it was possible beforehand — nevertheless, since she did not neglect by failing to immerse earlier, it is permitted to immerse. And so Beit Yosef ruled that it is permitted to immerse on Shabbat in every case — and this is the main halachah, as I separated. Therefore, in a place where her husband is in the city one should not be stringent, as Rama wrote. And so too Maharam Lublin in responsum siman 53 and siman 71 — see there until here is my language in my book. Behold, it has been clarified that Rama's words here in the hagahah are clear and correct. The ordinances of Hashem are upright.
