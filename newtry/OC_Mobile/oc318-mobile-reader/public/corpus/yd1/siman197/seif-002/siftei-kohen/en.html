If she could not, etc. Maharam of Lublin rules that a woman who immersed on Thursday night and afterward discovered that the immersion was invalid, such as dirt beneath a fingernail, may immerse Friday night, because this is an unavoidable circumstance and is unlike a woman after childbirth. If a woman immersed on Shabbos and upon returning home discovered that she had forgotten to cut one fingernail, with no dirt beneath it, it appears that although requiring a second immersion in such a case is only a stringency, she should nevertheless return and immerse. By strict law immersion is permitted on Shabbos. Once the custom has required a second immersion, it is an immersion for a mitzvah; moreover, Raavan holds that the fingernail itself can invalidate the immersion. Even if by strict law the first immersion was valid, she is then already pure, and a pure person may immerse on Shabbos because there is no appearance of effecting a halachic repair. Thus either way she may immerse: if the nail invalidates, this is the required mitzvah immersion; if not, she is already pure and her immersion effects no repair. The same reasoning applies to any genuine doubt whether her first immersion was valid. Therefore, even where Maharam of Lublin rules that after spending a night with her husband she need not immerse again for a forgotten nail, according to the view that a second immersion is still required she may do so on Friday night. There is no stringency leading to leniency, because if no second immersion is legally required, she is a pure woman for whom bathing and immersion are themselves permitted on Shabbos.
After birth. Specifically when she immerses after birth and did not see impure blood; but if she saw impure blood and the time of her immersion is Friday night, she is permitted to immerse because it is at its proper time — so Mahariv in his rulings siman 93, brought by Darkei Moshe. And it appears that impure blood is after 33 days for a male and 66 days for a female, as above siman 194.
But where there is no custom, one should not be stringent, etc. The Bach argues that the stringency is required by law and that immersion at its proper time is a mitzvah. I do not understand his position. The settled law follows the poskim that immersion at its appointed time is not independently a mitzvah. Thus women do not immerse when their husbands are away, and widows and unmarried women who are niddah do not immerse at all. Nevertheless, immersion on Shabbos is intrinsically permitted because it appears like ordinary cooling bathing. Terumas HaDeshen explains that the later custom to avoid unnecessary immersion on Shabbos arose only because nowadays people generally avoid such bathing, so immersion visibly appears to effect a halachic repair. He therefore writes that women customarily immerse on Shabbos when the husband is present and earlier immersion was impossible, because then there is a mitzvah of marital relations. This implies that if the husband arrives on Friday she may immerse even though an earlier immersion would theoretically have been possible, because she was not negligent. The Beit Yosef rules more broadly that immersion on Shabbos is permitted in all circumstances, and that is the essential law. Therefore where there is no contrary custom one should not be stringent. Maharam of Lublin rules similarly. As to a virgin bride whose immersion falls Friday night, Maharam permits it even where the custom is not to consummate the marriage until after Shabbos, because other forms of marital closeness are also a mitzvah. This differs from a widow, from whom all closeness is avoided until the first intercourse. Nevertheless, it is preferable for the bride to immerse before Shabbos where possible.
And in a place where they are stringent, etc. The relationship between these stringencies requires explanation. The meaning appears to be: in a place where the custom is to prohibit Shabbos immersion unless it is literally at its proper time, and likewise to avoid Shabbos-night immersion because it separates the immersion too far from the washing, while on Shabbos itself she cannot immerse because it is not considered timely, one should not be lenient.
Since, etc. That is to say, on Motzaei Shabbat she cannot perform chafifah — for according to Rashi and other poskim below siman 199 s.k. 6, she must perform chafifah specifically by day; and one cannot arrange that she perform chafifah on Friday, since one does not distance the chafifah from the immersion where possible; and so Bach.
