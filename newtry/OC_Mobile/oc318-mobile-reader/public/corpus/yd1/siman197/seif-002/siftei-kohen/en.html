"If she could not, etc." And it is written in a responsum of Maharam Lublin siman 71: one who immersed on the night of the sixth and afterward found that she had not immersed properly — for example, she found dirt under the nail — she is permitted to immerse on Friday night, for it is an oness; and one should not compare her to a yoledet. And one who immersed on Shabbat, and when she came home from the mikveh she saw that she had forgotten to cut one nail, and there is no dirt under the nail — it appears that even though the need for a second immersion in such a case is a general stringency, as I wrote below siman 198 s.k. 25 — nevertheless she should return and immerse; and we do not say that it is an immersion that is not of mitzvah, for by law all immersion is permitted on Shabbat, as I wrote below shortly. And even though we practice to be stringent in such a case, they did not practice so; and further, since they already practiced to be stringent to require a second immersion for her — if so, it is an immersion of mitzvah. And further, Rav Hai holds that the nail impedes, and I brought him in siman 198 there. And further, below s.k. 25 it is explained that even immersion not at its proper time is permitted — only Terumas HaDeshen wrote that they practice to be stringent because in the Gemara it says that it does not appear like fixing a person, because it appears like miykar; and in this time, when they are careful about heating, it appears like fixing a person. Obviously a pure person is permitted to immerse, for it does not appear like fixing a person at all — and so it is simple in the Gemara and poskim; and Tosefta in Orach Chayyim siman 326, that a pure person is permitted to wash his whole body and immerse on Shabbat (and even one who forbids a niddah to immerse when not at her proper time agrees regarding a pure person, as is proven in the Gemara and all the poskim). If so, this woman is permitted to immerse — either way: if the nail impedes, then it is tevilah at its proper time for the need of mitzvah; and if it does not impede, then she is pure and one does not fix a woman at all, and she is permitted to immerse. So it appears to me clearly, not like one who wished to be stringent that she should not immerse until after Shabbat. And per this it appears to me that the same applies in every other safek whether she immersed properly or not — she is permitted to immerse on Friday night. And for this reason it appears to me regarding one who immersed on the night of the sixth, and the next day found that she had forgotten to cut one nail — that in the responsum of Maharam Lublin there he ruled that she needs no immersion at all, for they were not stringent in such a case in the law of forgetting a nail, since she passed one night with her husband. And below siman 198 s.k. 25 I wrote that even in such a case she needs another immersion — if so, the questioner's question there remains in its place. And these are its words: since by the law of the Gemara and the poskim she needs no other immersion for forgetting a nail — if so, it is not considered that she delayed her time, for she did all that was in her power to do, and the prohibition was not known to her until the morrow; or let us say all the more that she is forbidden to immerse — for if we permit her to immerse, it is a stringency that leads to a leniency, for that woman had a valid immersion by law, and if so she stands in the prohibition of bathing on Shabbat like any other person. But the later authorities were stringent upon her and required another immersion; and if we permit her immersion on Shabbat, it is a stringency that leads to a leniency, for we permit the prohibition of bathing on Shabbat end quote. And it appears to me that there is no stringency here that leads to a leniency; on the contrary, when there is no need for immersion — for the nail does not impede — if so she is pure, and there is no prohibition of bathing on Shabbat for a pure person, for it does not appear like fixing. And if so, this woman is permitted to immerse on Friday night either way — and this is clear; and so agreed the Gaon, my master and teacher, may his memory be blessed.

"But in a place where there is no custom, one should not be stringent, etc." And Bach wrote that one should be stringent, for this stringency is from the law, etc.; also tevilah at its proper time is a mitzvah. See there — he elaborated in his words and I did not reach the end of his view in all his words; but the matter is clear that we hold tevilah at its proper time is not a mitzvah, as all the poskim wrote. And therefore women do not immerse at their proper time when their husband is not in the city; and so widows and virgins who are niddot do not immerse at all at their proper time; and so too, even if she did not have relations, she does not immerse until after five and seven days, as above in the siman before this, seif 11. Nevertheless, she is permitted to immerse on Shabbat, as Terumas HaDeshen wrote siman 255 and Beit Yosef, and as is proven in the Gemara and poskim that there is no prohibition in the matter because it appears like miykar; and so it is implied below siman 199 seif 7. Only Terumas HaDeshen wrote that nowadays they practice to be stringent regarding a yoledet and the like, and he forced a reason, writing and these are his words: since the custom now is to be careful and prevent heating on Shabbat, even though there is no reason in the matter — nevertheless one cannot say that it appears like miykar, for they are careful about heating, and it is proven that their intent is to immerse, and it appears like fixing. And for this reason it appears that women practice to be careful not to immerse on Shabbat unless her husband is in the city and it was not possible for her beforehand, for in such a case it is a mitzvah because of onah end quote. It is implied from his words that when her husband is not in the city and he comes on Friday evening, even though it was possible beforehand — nevertheless, since she did not neglect in what she failed to immerse earlier, it is permitted to immerse on Shabbat. And so Beit Yosef ruled that it is permitted to immerse on Shabbat in every case — and this is the main halachah, as I explained. Therefore, in a place where there is no custom one should not be stringent, as the Rav wrote. And so Maharam Lublin wrote in responsum siman 53 and siman 71. See there. And regarding what he wrote about a widow, etc. — Maharam Lublin wrote in responsum siman 53: a virgin kalah whose immersion occurred on Friday night — it appears that one should permit, even though they practice not to have intercourse of mitzvah until after Shabbat, because other kinds of closeness are also mitzvah. And it is uncertain regarding a widow, for since it is forbidden to come upon her with first intercourse, they separate her from all other closeness — which is not so regarding a virgin. Nevertheless, it is good to precede her immersion before Shabbat. See there — he elaborated; and so they practice.
"And in a place where they practiced to be stringent, etc." It is uncertain what relation this stringency has to that. And one may say that this is the meaning: and in a place where they practiced to be stringent not to immerse on Shabbat except exactly at her proper time; and likewise in what he wrote, for the reason that one should not distance the immersion from the chafifah; and on Shabbat she cannot, because it is not at her proper time — one should not be lenient.
