See in responsum Chemdat Tzvi siman 11, who forbids women to immerse on Friday night in hot water, because it is forbidden to wash one's whole body in hot water on Shabbat, as written in Orach Chayyim siman 326. [And he wrote that one who transgresses and immerses on Friday night in hot water has certainly done a prohibition, and it is permitted to call her a transgressor, etc. And what one sage ruled and permitted them to immerse on Friday evening while it is still day on their seventh day — he certainly did not act well, and he transgressed a rabbinic prohibition, etc. And he elaborated on this and concluded, writing at the end: halachah lemaaseh I say that they should not immerse in hot water on Friday night, nor on Friday evening that is their seventh day; rather, let them immerse in cold water on Friday night, or only by cooling their heat until the name "hot water" is not called upon them; or let them postpone the immersion until Motzaei Shabbat. And if it is impossible in all that was said, let them permit them to immerse during bein hashemashot, and let them not come to their homes until night — and more than this we have no authority to permit at all end quote. See there. And see in Bach at the end of siman 190, who brought this and challenged "or postpone the immersion to Motzaei Shabbat" — for Rama wrote that in a place where they practiced to be stringent, also on Motzaei Shabbat she should not immerse, etc. See there. Also in Levushi Serad, in novel laws at the end of the book, he addressed this. And it is possible to reconcile it, for Rama concludes that since it was possible for her to immerse beforehand, etc. — and here it is called impossible, for she cannot bear the coldness of the water.] And see in responsum Divrei Yosef siman 64, who brought his words — and these are his words: he came to justify the custom that the daughters of Israel practiced, to immerse in hot water on Friday night; and at the end he left the matter uncertain. See there. And see in responsum Noda Biyhudah, second part, Orach Chayyim siman 24, and similarly that he would warn the bath attendants to put hot water on Friday evening into the mikveh while it is still day, so that when it grows dark they will be only lukewarm; and in lukewarm water it is permitted — only in hot water it is forbidden. See there. Further, regarding Yom Tov that falls on Friday evening — whether it is permitted to kindle under the pot for the need of a Jewish bather in the bathhouse, and the rest he leaves to drip into the mikveh for the need of women's immersion on Friday night. See there. And see in responsum Tiferet Tzvi, part Orach Chayyim siman 9, and in responsum Maor HaGolah, Rabbi Akiva Eiger siman 17, further on the aforementioned case. And see in responsum Chut HaShani siman 83, on the matter of women's immersion on Friday night — he hesitates to mention immersing while it is still day in a place where there is no custom; and in a place where they practice so, one should do so only on Shabbat after the recitation of Barchu, when the name Shabbat has already been called upon it; but not on a weekday, even after Barchu. See there. And see in responsum Chinuch Beit Yehudah siman 82, who challenged the custom that even on Shabbat after the recitation of Barchu it is not correct to immerse while it is still day, and one should protest and abolish this custom. And if she fears concern of a fall, let them stand a candle in a closed lantern in the mikveh building voluntarily; and even during bein hashemashot it is permitted to kindle via a gentile, for telling a gentile is shevut, and all shevut is permitted during bein hashemashot for the need of a mitzvah. And we are not concerned lest the candle tilt — for there are at least two women who will remind one another. See there.
