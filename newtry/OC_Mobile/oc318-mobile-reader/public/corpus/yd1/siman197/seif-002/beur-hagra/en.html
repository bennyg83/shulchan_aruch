If, etc. The Gemara, 15b, states: A woman who has a fixed veset, her husband, etc. The Yerushalmi comments that it is forbidden for a woman to remain in her impurity. This was explained according to the view that immersion at its appointed time is a mitzvah, although the wording does not imply that limitation. See Be'er HaGolah and the Zohar, in Sava.
She is permitted, etc. Mishnah, second chapter of Beitzah, 17b.
But otherwise it is forbidden. We rule according to the view that immersion at its appointed time is not a mitzvah. See Tosafot to Niddah 30a, s.v. 'and derive from there,' etc., and Yoma 8a, s.v. 'everyone agrees,' etc. At the end of Yoma, however, it appears that even according to this view immersion is nevertheless permitted even on Yom Kippur. See Tosafot to Shabbos 121a, s.v. 'this is the correct reading,' etc. It is certainly permitted on Shabbos, as stated in Beitzah 18b: This is understandable regarding Shabbos, etc. Tosafot there, s.v. 'every,' wrote that this applied specifically in their days, when immersion was needed for matters of purity, etc. Nevertheless, they stated this limitation only regarding Yom Kippur, in accordance with Rav.
And if it was, etc. The Gemara in Beitzah there implies that it is permitted even when immersion could have been performed earlier, because the Mishnah distinguishes between a person and vessels. Concerning vessels, when immersion could not have been performed before Yom Tov, they may be immersed on Yom Tov, as stated in the Gemara, 18a: A vessel that became impure on the eve of Yom Tov, etc. This is unlike what is written in Darkei Moshe. This is why the ruling here continues: But in a place, etc.
And in a place where they practiced, etc. This follows from the statement on 68a: There is no difficulty; this case is where it was possible, etc. Likewise, regarding a widow, etc., because it is forbidden, etc. See what our master wrote in Even HaEzer, siman 64, seif katan 11.
