If, etc. In the Gemara, 15b: "a woman who has an established pattern [of niddah] — her husband, etc." — and the Jerusalem Talmud says: "this is the [teaching] that it is forbidden for a woman to stand in her impurity"; but this should be interpreted as according to the view that immersion at its proper time is a mitzvah — for the language does not imply otherwise. See Baer Heitev; and see the Zohar in the Saba section. And she is permitted, etc. Mishnah, chapter 2 of Yom Tov (17b).
But otherwise it is forbidden. For we rule like the view that immersion at its proper time is not [specifically] a mitzvah; and see Tosafot, Niddah 30a, s.v. "and we learn, etc."; and Yoma 8a, s.v. "that all agree, etc." But at the end of Yoma it is implied that even according to that view it is nevertheless permitted, even on Yom Kippur. And see Tosafot, Shabbat 121a, s.v. "this is the correct version, etc."; and all the more so on Shabbat — as written in Yom Tov 18b: "granted, on Shabbat, etc." Except that Tosafot there, s.v. "all, etc.," wrote that [this was permitted] only in their days for ritual purity purposes, etc. — but even so, they wrote this only regarding Yom Kippur, and following Rav. And if it was, etc. But in the Gemara of Yom Tov there it is implied [it applies] even when it is possible [before Yom Tov] — from the fact that the mishnah there distinguishes between a person and vessels, and for vessels when it was impossible before Yom Tov, they immerse on Yom Tov — as stated there in the Gemara (18a): "a vessel that became impure before Yom Tov, etc." — unlike what is written in Darkei Moshe. And that is the meaning here: "but in a place where, etc." And in a place where the custom is, etc. From what is stated, 68a: "there is no difficulty — the case where it is possible, etc." (And likewise a widow, etc. because it is forbidden, etc. See what our master wrote in Even HaEzer, siman 64, seif katan 11.)
