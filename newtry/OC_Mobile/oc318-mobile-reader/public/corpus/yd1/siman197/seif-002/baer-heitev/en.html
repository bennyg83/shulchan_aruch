Birth. Specifically when she immerses after childbirth and did not see impure blood — that is, after thirty-three days for a male and sixty-six for a female. But if she saw impure blood and her time of immersion falls on Friday night, she is permitted to immerse, for it is at her proper time — so Mahari"v in his rulings. And regarding when her husband was not in the city, Bach wrote that it is forbidden to immerse on Shabbat only when it was possible for her to immerse before Shabbat and her husband was in the city; but if her husband was not in the city and he comes on Friday evening, even though it was possible for her to immerse beforehand (since she did not neglect by failing to immerse earlier), she is permitted to immerse on Shabbat — and so Shach. But Taz ruled that there is no distinction at all between whether her husband was in the city or not and only came on Friday evening; everything depends on whether she was able to immerse beforehand without any oness — then she is forbidden to immerse on Shabbat, even if her husband did not come until Friday evening (and in Shulchan Aruch he elaborated on this and upheld his words in Shach).

Because he acquires her through this intercourse, as written in Orach Chayyim siman 39 and Even HaEzer siman 63 — therefore one should not marry a widow on Shabbat when her immersion falls on Friday night. And if she immersed before Shabbat, he must come upon her specifically on Friday evening after the chuppah; yichud in one room does not help so that he may have first intercourse with her on Shabbat. So too Mahari"l — we require complete intercourse specifically. Even though in Sefer Masat Binyamin siman 90 it is written that yichud also acquires in a place fit for intercourse, nevertheless, since many great ones forbid, one should be stringent end quote Taz.
Prohibition. Taz wrote: and this is specifically regarding one who marries a widow — for by law she is forbidden on Shabbat, and then her immersion time occurred and she sinned in nothing. But in the aforementioned law — namely, in a place where they practice to be stringent after childbirth, or her husband was not in the city until Shabbat — certainly it is also forbidden on Motzaei Shabbat, since it was possible for her to immerse before Shabbat; why should she immerse on Motzaei Shabbat and distance the chafifah from the immersion for no reason? And Maharam Lublin wrote in responsum siman 53: a virgin kalah whose immersion occurred on Friday night — it appears that one should permit, even though they practice not to have intercourse of mitzvah until after Shabbat, because other kinds of closeness are also mitzvah. And it is uncertain regarding a widow, for since it is forbidden to come upon her with first intercourse, they separate her from all other closeness — unlike a virgin. Nevertheless, it is good to precede her immersion before Shabbat. See there — he elaborated; and so they practice. Shach.
