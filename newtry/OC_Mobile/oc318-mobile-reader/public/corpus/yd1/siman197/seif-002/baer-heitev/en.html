Could. Maharam of Lublin writes that a woman who immersed Thursday night and later discovered that the immersion was invalid, such as dirt beneath a nail, may immerse Friday night because she was prevented. If she immersed on Shabbat and upon returning home noticed that one nail had not been cut but was clean beneath, she should immerse again that night: if the nail invalidates, the immersion is timely and for a mitzvah; if not, she is already pure and no corrective act occurs. Shach extends this reasoning to every genuine doubt whether the immersion was valid.
Childbirth. This applies only when she immerses after childbirth without later impure bleeding, such as after thirty-three days for a male or sixty-six for a female. If she saw impure blood and her proper immersion night falls on Shabbat, she may immerse because it is timely. Bach permits Shabbat immersion where the husband was away and returned Friday, even if she could technically have immersed earlier. Taz disagrees and makes the rule depend solely upon whether she could have immersed earlier without unavoidable impediment. Shach supports Bach.
They separate. Because washing and combing should ideally be done by day. Here Shabbat prevents it, so she must prepare on Friday and a long interval will separate preparation from immersion. The after-the-fact permission to prepare thoroughly at night applies where immersion falls after Shabbat, not where she negligently delayed.
The first. First relations effect acquisition, as explained in Orach Chaim 339 and Even HaEzer 63. Therefore a widow should not marry on Shabbat when her immersion falls Friday night. If she immersed before Shabbat, full relations must occur Friday after the chuppah; mere seclusion does not suffice according to the stringent authorities. Taz.
Prohibition. Taz limits the permission to a widow whose first relations are legally forbidden on Shabbat and who was not negligent. Where the restriction arose only from local postpartum custom, or because the husband was away until Shabbat, she should not delay immersion until after Shabbat if she could have immersed earlier. Maharam of Lublin permits a virgin bride to immerse Friday night despite the custom to delay first relations until after Shabbat, because other forms of closeness are also mitzvah-related. It is nevertheless preferable to immerse before Shabbat.
