Question: A kalah whose day of chuppah is the seventh day of her sight — may she immerse by day, so that the chuppah not be a chuppah of niddah, for one should not make a chuppah of niddah lechatchila, as is explained in Even HaEzer siman 61 — for so is the view of Rambam; and Shach in siman 196 wrote that even though we are not meticulous about this, as is explained in Even HaEzer there — nevertheless, certainly where possible in greater purity is preferable. See there.
Answer: Behold, in Shulchan Aruch siman 197 Rama wrote in the name of Mahari"l that kalot who immerse before the chuppah may immerse by day, for they do not come to the chassan until the night. And Shach there wrote: it appears that this is specifically for us, where kalot do not immerse on the seventh day — as is clear in Mahari"l, who deals with such a case. But in a place where kalot immerse on the seventh day, or for us if she had a mishap during her counting days and then immerses immediately after seven — she cannot immerse by day, for on day seven it is forbidden not only because of sarach batah alone, but lest she see and annul and be found a zavah retroactively end quote. And in Minchat Yaakov he was astonished at this, for according to the reason that they do not come to the chassan until the night, there is no concern lest she annul — for then she will truly not have relations. And he wrote to reconcile it with difficulty. And similarly Ba'er Heitev challenged Shach; and in responsum Pischei Mevinim volume 2 he also attacks Shach on this and rules to be lenient. And so in responsum Even HaGra siman 20 he wrote on Shach that he erred in what he saw, for in Hagahot S.D. it is written explicitly in the name of Mahari"l that there is no concern for the view of Rashi, nor for sarach batah. And so in Sefer Seder Taharah; and the Gaon Maharaz, may his memory be blessed, elaborated greatly on this and concluded at the end: the whole matter is heard that if the seven clean days are completed on the day of the chuppah itself, she should not immerse by day, since she will be with the chassan that night — there is in the matter concern of sarach batah; and even if it is day eight, it appears that sarach batah also applies, though one may distinguish, as Maaseh Yom wrote on the words of Shach, that on day eight one may be more lenient, for it is like a decree upon a decree. Nevertheless, it is not compelled that Shach said so, because he relied on Rama's reason that they do not come to the chassan until the night — which he wrote according to his version; and according to what is written, this is not so. And also, in any case the chuppah must be at night, as implied by Rama's wording; and it is not reasonable to be lenient when she goes to her house after the chuppah and does not come until the night. But per my humble view, even in such a case, where the chuppah will be at night, one should not be lenient — for I did not find that Mahari"l is lenient regarding immersion on the day of the chuppah, except when the chassan will not come upon her on that night at all — which is the night before the chuppah. But if the chuppah will be in the evening, it does not appear to be lenient for this reason, for this is not found like Mahari"l; and all the more on day seven one should not be lenient, even if the chuppah will be at night. And in Sefer Degul MeRevavah he also wrote to challenge Shach, that on day seven too one should permit, for the concern of "she will see and annul" does not apply. And he wrote that nevertheless the heart of Minchat Yaakov was not filled to disagree with Shach, even though he sensed this attack. Nevertheless, in a pressing situation it appears to be lenient even on day seven — but only on condition that they not establish the chuppah until the stars actually emerge. But to establish the chuppah by day and rely on the fact that they do not seclude them until the night does not help, per their understanding — that Mahari"l is lenient even though intercourse will be at night, since they do not come until the night. And they explain that what is meant by "they do not come until the night" is because the chuppah is at night; or he speaks of his own reasoning, saying that on day seven, where the concern is because of zavah intercourse, there is greater reason to be concerned, and one should not rely on not secluding them until the night — for this only helps on day eight, where the concern is only because of sarach batah. But for day seven it does not help, except that she needs to wait after the chuppah until the night. And according to what is written, Mahari"l does not be lenient at all regarding immersion on the day of the chuppah. If so, one may say that if she did not immerse on the day before the chuppah, she again needs to immerse at night, whether the chuppah will be by day or at night. And according to the custom of these countries, where on the night before the chuppah they make a feast in the kalah's house, which they call the chassan's meal, and they also bring the chassan there with the youths — after the feast they seclude the chassan and kalah in a special room. Nevertheless, it appears that she may immerse properly by day, for sarach batah is not relevant here — for even though they seclude them, nevertheless it is known that the yichud is not for intercourse, but only in order to fulfill the words of Chazal: do not sanctify a woman until he sees her, and also so that his heart be bold toward her. And in such a case there is no concern of sarach batah, as in Hagahot S.D. that I brought above. But on the day of the chuppah it appears that one should not be lenient. But per my humble view, in a place where women are insistent about this and are ashamed that the kalah is not pure — because the matter is publicized at the time of giving kiddushin and when they return from the chuppah, and their joy becomes grief for them — one should permit her to immerse on the day before the chuppah, since otherwise it is fitting to act in purity, as Shach wrote in siman 193 that so is preferable; and so Beit Shmuel. But she needs to immerse again at night, since there are several great ones who hold that even if she immersed on day seven, bediavad her immersion did not count and she needs to return and immerse at night — as Shach wrote there, and so is the view of Mahari"l. If so, one should not be lenient in this immersion. And so in responsum Chakham Tzvi it is explained, that he wrote regarding one who permitted immersing on Friday evening voluntarily on the seventh day — he did not act well and transgressed a rabbinic prohibition; and for some of the great ones, even bediavad her immersion did not count, etc. And he concluded that one should not permit except that they immerse in cold water on Friday night, or by cooling their heat, etc. See there. And so I ruled halachah lemaaseh. But where the day of the chuppah is day eight, and the hour requires that she immerse on the day before the chuppah — then she does not need to return and immerse again at night, for on day eight all agree that bediavad her immersion counted. However, one should be stringent not to make the chuppah until the night; and in particular according to the custom of these countries, where immediately when they return from the chuppah they seclude them alone in a room — sarach batah applies here. Therefore one should make the chuppah only at night. And if because of some reason and necessity it must be made by day — in any case one should not seclude them after the chuppah until the night. Per my humble view I wrote the words of Ze'ira Kodesh Efrayim Zalman Margoliyot.
