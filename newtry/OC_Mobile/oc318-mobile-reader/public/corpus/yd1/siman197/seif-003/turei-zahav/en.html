She is forbidden to immerse by day, etc. Explanation: the law of the Torah is that a niddah who immerses on her seventh day — even if there were no clean days — immerses specifically at night; regarding her, it depends specifically on days. But a zavah who counts seven clean days in her counting — we say "part of the day is like the whole day," since she counted part of the seventh day in cleanliness, she immerses even by day; and we derive this from the verse, "and afterward she shall be pure" — after the act she shall be pure. Only the Sages forbade immersing by day, lest she have relations while it is still day, and afterward on that same day see and annul retroactively — and it be found that he came upon her in prohibition. Therefore the Tur wrote: our women, even though they are doubt zavot — meaning that they count seven clean days — and if so there would always be at least eight days with the day of sight, and she would have permission to immerse by day, since the seven days have already been completed — nevertheless she should not immerse by day. And with this the words of Shulchan Aruch here are explained; and the reason is because of sarach batah — meaning, her daughter will see that her mother immersed on day eight, and will think that on day seven she immersed, and she too will do so.
