Because of sarach batah. That she thinks her mother immersed on the night of the seventh, and she will do so too. But on day seven it is a complete prohibition, as the Tur wrote — lest she see and annul retroactively (and see Taz s.k. 8 in his explanation of the Tur's words — his words are somewhat astonishing; uncertain). And so it is implied and explained in the Gemara and all the poskim. However, I saw in Mahari"l at the beginning of the laws of Niddah that he wrote that even on day seven the reason is because of sarach batah; and he also wrote that for us, whose wives are not doubt zavot, etc. — and his words are astonishing, and it is uncertain; and perhaps an error fell into the books. And Shach wrote that one who immerses on day eight, where it is forbidden because of sarach batah alone, they practice to be lenient to immerse before dark, only that she comes home after it grows dark — unlike one who immerses on day seven, where one should not be lenient, but only immerse after dark; and one should protest against one who does not act so. And Shach wrote further that there is no distinction whether she has a daughter or not — and this is simple end quote.
And kalot. And Shach wrote: and specifically for us, where kalot do not immerse on the seventh day; but in a place where kalot immerse on the seventh day for their sight, or for us if her immersion occurred on the seventh day — for example, she had a mishap during her counting days and then immerses immediately after seven — she cannot immerse on day seven. It is not only because of sarach batah alone that they are forbidden, but because lest she see and annul everything retroactively, and be found a zavah retroactively, as stated in the Gemara and poskim end quote. And his words are astonishing at first glance, since the reason is that they do not come to the chassan until the night — if so, even if she annuls retroactively there is no concern of prohibition. And it is possible to say that Shach's intent is that nevertheless on this seventh day we decree, lest she come to the chassan by day — unlike day eight, where it is only a decree because of sarach batah; we do not decree so much, decree upon decree; examine.
