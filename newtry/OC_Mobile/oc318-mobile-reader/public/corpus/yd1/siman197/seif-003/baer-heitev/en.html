Her daughter. And even if she has no daughter — the law is the same, for we do not distinguish. And in a responsum, Maharam Lublin was asked about a woman who did not immerse properly in a case where after two or three days she needs to return and immerse — whether she may immerse by day, and whether she needs to guard zavah days because of sarach batah, lest she say she saw blood and immersed after three days — and Maharam did not answer on this. It appears that regarding the first question one should be stringent lechatchila, and bediavad it is permitted; and regarding the second question the matter is simple that she may immerse after two or three days, and we do not find a decree of sarach batah except regarding immersion by day. See siman 196 seif 12 end quote Shach. And Bach wrote that even to go from her house to the mikveh voluntarily is forbidden — namely, when the woman bathes and combs at home and goes to the place of immersion. But when there is a bathhouse and mikveh in one place, and a woman goes voluntarily to the bathhouse an hour or two before dark and comes home after dark — even though the bathhouse and mikveh are close to her home, there is no sarach batah here, for the daughter knows that she lingers bathing and combing in the bathhouse and does not immerse until after dark end quote. However, in Agur it is written that the Ashkenaz custom is to immerse near dark; and Shach wrote that this means she comes home after it grows dark — and it seems to me that so they practice. Nevertheless, one should be stringent. However, it appears that this is specifically on day eight, where immersion is forbidden only because of sarach batah alone — in such a case they are lenient near dark. But on day seven one should not immerse near dark at all, and one should protest against those who do so.
And kalot. Shach wrote: it appears that this is specifically for us, where kalot do not immerse on the seventh day — as is clear in Mahari"l, who deals with such a case. But in a place where kalot immerse on the seventh day, or for us if she had a mishap during her counting days and then immerses immediately after seven — she cannot immerse by day, for on day seven it is forbidden not only because of sarach batah alone, but lest she see and annul retroactively and be found a zavah retroactively end quote. And in truth his words are astonishing, and it is uncertain — for the concern lest she annul is in this manner: that the Sages were concerned lest she have relations while it is still day, and afterward see again on that same day and annul retroactively, and it be found that he came upon her in prohibition. And this is specifically regarding an ordinary woman. But regarding a kalah, per what Rama wrote — the reason that they do not come to the chassan until the night — this concern does not apply to her at all. Uncertain; examine.
