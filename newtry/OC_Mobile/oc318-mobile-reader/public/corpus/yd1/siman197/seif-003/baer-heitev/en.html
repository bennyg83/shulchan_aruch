By day. Shach implies that even immersing shortly before dark and returning home after nightfall is forbidden.
Her daughter. Even if she has no daughter, the decree applies uniformly. Shach is stringent initially where she must repeat an immersion two or three days later, but permits after the fact. The concern of misleading a daughter applies only to daytime immersion, not to the interval between the first and repeated immersion. Bach forbids even leaving home for the bathhouse before dark where the immersion site is separate, but permits going early to a combined bathhouse and mikveh because it is evident that the time is spent washing and preparing. Shach records the Ashkenazic custom to immerse close to dark and return after nightfall, but advises stringency, especially on the seventh day.
And brides. Shach limits this to contemporary brides who do not immerse on the seventh day. Where a bride does immerse on the seventh day, he forbids daytime immersion because of concern that she may later see and retroactively invalidate the clean days. The Baer Heitev questions this, since a bride does not meet the groom until night, removing the concern of daytime relations.
The chuppah. Meaning that if she immerses after the chuppah, even though this is her first immersion for this husband, she has the law of other women.
