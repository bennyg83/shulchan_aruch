And in a pressing circumstance, etc. This is also stated in Semag, positive commandment 12, which concludes, 'This matter is forbidden to state before an ignoramus.' However, Hagahos Maimoniyos does not cite this conclusion, and the Rav followed it. The reason appears to be that we learn in Menachos 99b: Rabbi Yochanan said in the name of Rabbi Shimon ben Yehotzadak that even if a person recited only Shema morning and evening, he fulfilled 'It shall not depart,' and this matter is forbidden to state before an ignoramus; Rava said that it is a mitzvah to state it before an ignoramus. Rashi explains that he will reason that if one receives such great reward for Shema, then if he studied all day his reward would certainly be greater, and he will accustom his sons to Torah study. Alternatively, those scholars could have exempted themselves with Shema, yet they sat and studied all day, showing that the reward is great. Since Rava said that it is a mitzvah to state it, we rule like him. This is especially so according to the version of Yalkut at the beginning of Yehoshua, which states that Abaye cursed one who says it before an ignoramus, while Rava said it is a mitzvah; we rule like Rava against Abaye. Midrash Tehillim likewise states without dispute in the name of Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi that even one who recited only Shema morning and evening fulfilled 'and in His Torah he meditates day and night.' Yalkut cites this at the beginning of Tehillim; see there.
