A mamzer Torah scholar, etc. In Yerushalmi at end of Horayot and in Shabbat chapter HaBoneh — they thought permitted to redeem and to sustain and to clothe; but for sitting in the study hall — no; Rabbi Avin said: even for sitting in the study hall; and the Semag brought it positive mitzvot 162 end page 207.
To a priest who is an am haaretz. It is written in Be'er Sheva page 21 side 3 — not specifically am haaretz but one who is not equal in wisdom to an Israelite, etc.; but nevertheless one may say specifically for sitting in the study hall and to bless first and all similar where nothing is fixed because of ways of peace; but not regarding opening first in Torah that they enacted because of ways of peace, etc.; and see there where he elaborated; and in Orach Chayyim siman 135 seif 4 it is explained that the common custom is that even a priest who is am haaretz precedes reading before a great Israelite sage — and this is when the priest knows to read with the prayer leader word for word; and see further in Be'er Sheva where he elaborated and wrote in conclusion: we find there are five divisions in the matter — there is no Torah commandment to precede the priest by force of "and you shall sanctify him" except specifically when there is no Israelite sage greater than him; and if the priest is a Torah scholar — even if the Israelite is a great sage with us — it is a merit to precede the priest because of the priesthood, as we see in Rabbi Perida; and it is not forbidden because of "and those who love Him are as the sun going forth in its might" since he is not am haaretz; but if the priest is am haaretz the Israelite Torah scholar is not permitted to precede him to bless before him by law and statute because of the priesthood — because of "and those who love Him are as the sun going forth in its might"; but if the Israelite Torah scholar gave permission to a priest who is am haaretz to bless the blessing over bread or grace after meals — there is no concern here, for a sage may give permission even to an Israelite lesser than him to bless grace after meals, as Rabbi said to Rav "precede — wash your hands"; and if the priest is a Torah scholar and the Israelite is not a greater sage than him — the priest may give permission to the Israelite to bless before him at a meal, and even at a feast of circumcision and weddings where many are present; since the Gemara concludes in chapter HaNezikin that ways of peace — as the Mishnah teaches — is according to the master who said one divides honor to one greater than him; but only at a meal; but in the synagogue — no; and since it concludes that in the synagogue too we only said specifically on Shabbatot and festivals where many are present — we automatically know that at a meal it is permitted even when many are present, because contention does not arise — the host gives to bless to whomever is right in his eyes — until here.
