A mamzer Torah scholar, etc. In Yerushalmi at end of Horayot and in Shabbat chapter HaBoneh, they thought permitted to redeem and to sustain and to clothe; but for sitting in the study hall, no; Rabbi Avin said: even for sitting in the study hall; and the Semag brought it positive mitzvot 162 end folio 207.
To a kohen who is an am haaretz. It is written in Be'er Sheva folio 21c, not specifically am haaretz but one who is not equal in wisdom to an Jew, etc.; but nevertheless one may say specifically for sitting in the study hall and to bless first and all similar where nothing is fixed because of ways of peace; but not regarding opening first in Torah that they enacted because of ways of peace, etc.; and see there where he elaborated; and in Orach Chayim siman 135 seif 4 it is explained that the common custom is that even a kohen who is am haaretz precedes reading before a great Jew sage, and this is when the kohen knows to read with the prayer leader word for word; and see further in Be'er Sheva where he elaborated and wrote in conclusion: we find there are five divisions in the matter, there is no Torah commandment to precede the kohen by force of "and you shall sanctify him" except specifically when there is no Jew sage greater than him; and if the kohen is a Torah scholar, even if the Jew is a great sage with us, it is a merit to precede the kohen because of the kohenhood, as we see in Rabbi Perida; and it is not forbidden because of "and those who love Him are as the sun going forth in its might" since he is not am haaretz; but if the kohen is am haaretz the Jew Torah scholar is not permitted to precede him to bless before him by law and statute because of the kohenhood, because of "and those who love Him are as the sun going forth in its might"; but if the Jew Torah scholar gave permission to a kohen who is am haaretz to bless the blessing over bread or grace after meals, there is no concern here, for a sage may give permission even to an Jew lesser than him to bless grace after meals, as Rabbi said to Rav "precede, wash your hands"; and if the kohen is a Torah scholar and the Jew is not a greater sage than him, the kohen may give permission to the Jew to bless before him at a meal, and even at a feast of circumcision and weddings where many are present; since the Gemara concludes in chapter HaNezikin that ways of peace, as the Mishnah teaches, is according to the master who said one divides honor to one greater than him; but only at a meal; but in the synagogue, no; and since it concludes that in the synagogue too we only said specifically on Shabbatot and festivals where many are present, we automatically know that at a meal it is permitted even when many are present, because contention does not arise, the host gives to bless to whomever is right in his eyes, until here.
