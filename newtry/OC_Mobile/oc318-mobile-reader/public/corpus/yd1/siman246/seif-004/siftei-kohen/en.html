And some say, in the Gemara, etc. The Derishah writes that some householders customarily study Gemara, Rashi, and Tosafos every day, but not the other halachic authorities. They bring proof from the teaching at the end of Niddah: Tanna Devei Eliyahu taught that whoever studies halachos every day is assured that he is destined for the World to Come. In my view, however, one must study the works of the halachic authorities and the laws of the Torah, such as the Rif, Mordechai, Rosh, and similar works, for this is the root and foundation of our Torah. They do not discharge their obligation at all through studying Gemara, Rashi, and Tosafos alone. Regarding the teaching of Tanna Devei Eliyahu, Rashi already explains there that 'one who studies halachos' means settled halachic rulings. Rabbeinu Tam's statement here that the Babylonian Gemara is a mixture of all subjects refers to the Tur's preceding discussion of one who studies nine hours a day. Since he has much available time, he should study Gemara. But householders who study only three or four hours should not study Gemara alone. End quote.
But not in study of other wisdoms, such as books of philosophers and astronomy and Kabbalah wisdom and similar; and the Rav's language is not precise when he wrote "and this is called among the sages strolling in the orchard (Pardes)," etc., it appears that all other wisdoms besides Gemara are called strolling in the orchard, and this is not so; for the Rambam wrote in chapter 4 of Laws of Foundations of the Torah that matters of maaseh bereishit and maaseh merkavah are what the sages call Pardes, as written (in chapter "one may not expound") "four entered the orchard"; and even though great ones of Israel and great sages they were, not all of them had strength to know and grasp all matters clearly; and I say it is not fitting to stroll in the orchard except one whose belly is filled with bread and meat, and bread and meat is knowing the forbidden and permitted and similar from the rest of the commandments; and even though these matters are a small matter, the sages called them so; for they said (Bava Batra folio 134a) a great matter is maaseh merkavah, a small matter is the disputes of Abaye and Rava; nevertheless they are fitting to precede, for they settle a person's mind first; and also they are the great good that the Holy One, blessed be He bestowed to settle this world in order to inherit life of the World to Come; and it is possible that everyone knows, small and great, man and woman, broad-hearted and narrow-hearted, end quote. Also in derush he was entangled in this language when the Rav wrote and did not look only at the words of the Rambam in chapter 1 of Laws of Torah Study where he concluded there: and matters called Pardes are included in Gemara; and he wrote on this: and his words are astonishing in my humble opinion; for in my humble opinion Pardes mentioned means natural sciences, how did he include them in Gemara; and it seems to me Pardes means as they said four entered the orchard, and it is profound divine investigation; and even so the Rambam wrote that even though they are included in Gemara, nevertheless we do not teach them except after he filled his belly with Mishnah and Gemara, end quote. And had he seen the words of the Rambam in chapter 4 of Laws of Foundations of the Torah that I brought, he did not write so. Also the Kabbalists and other later authorities exaggerated the matter not to study Kabbalah wisdom until after he filled his belly from Gemara; and some wrote not to study Kabbalah until he is forty years old, as written "at forty for understanding"; and also one needs holiness and purity and alacrity and cleanliness for this; and most who breach to ascend in this wisdom before the proper time, their arms were bound untimely, as written, all this in the four sages of truth.
