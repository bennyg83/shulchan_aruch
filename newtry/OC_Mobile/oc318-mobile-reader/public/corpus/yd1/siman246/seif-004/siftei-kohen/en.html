And some say that in the Gemara, etc. — Derishah wrote: some householders practice learning Gemara every day and not other poskim; they bring proof from what we say at the end of Niddah: Tanna deVei Eliyahu — whoever repeats halachot every day is assured he is a child of the World to Come. But to my mind one must learn books of poskim, laws of the Torah, like Rif, Mordechai, Rosh and the like, for this is the root and essence of our Torah; and they do not leave at all to study Gemara alone, for that which Tanna deVei Eliyahu, etc. — Rashi already wrote there "who repeats halachot" means halachot of verses; and what Rabbenu Tam wrote here "Gemara" means Bavli mixed, etc., refers to what was written before this — nine hours a day of study; since he has much free time he should learn Gemara; but those householders who learn only three or four hours should not learn Gemara alone, as above — end.
But not in study of other wisdoms — such as books of philosophers and astronomy and Kabbalah wisdom and similar; and the Rav's language is not precise when he wrote "and this is called among the sages strolling in the orchard (Pardes)," etc. — it appears that all other wisdoms besides Gemara are called strolling in the orchard, and this is not so; for the Rambam wrote in chapter 4 of Laws of Foundations of the Torah that matters of maaseh bereishit and maaseh merkavah are what the sages call Pardes, as written (in chapter "one may not expound") "four entered the orchard"; and even though great ones of Israel and great sages they were — not all of them had strength to know and grasp all matters clearly; and I say it is not fitting to stroll in the orchard except one whose belly is filled with bread and meat — and bread and meat is knowing the forbidden and permitted and similar from the rest of the commandments; and even though these matters are a small matter, the sages called them so; for they said (Bava Batra page 134 side 1) a great matter is maaseh merkavah, a small matter is the disputes of Abaye and Rava; nevertheless they are fitting to precede, for they settle a person's mind first; and also they are the great good that the Holy One blessed be He bestowed to settle this world in order to inherit life of the World to Come; and it is possible that everyone knows — small and great, man and woman, broad-hearted and narrow-hearted — end quote. Also in derush he was entangled in this language when the Rav wrote and did not look only at the words of the Rambam in chapter 1 of Laws of Torah Study where he concluded there: and matters called Pardes are included in Gemara; and he wrote on this: and his words are astonishing in my humble opinion; for in my humble opinion Pardes mentioned means natural sciences — how did he include them in Gemara; and it seems to me Pardes means as they said four entered the orchard — and it is profound divine investigation; and even so the Rambam wrote that even though they are included in Gemara — nevertheless we do not teach them except after he filled his belly with Mishnah and Gemara — end quote. And had he seen the words of the Rambam in chapter 4 of Laws of Foundations of the Torah that I brought — he did not write so. Also the Kabbalists and other later authorities exaggerated the matter not to study Kabbalah wisdom until after he filled his belly from Gemara; and some wrote not to study Kabbalah until he is forty years old, as written "at forty for understanding"; and also one needs holiness and purity and alacrity and cleanliness for this; and most who breach to ascend in this wisdom before the proper time — their arms were bound untimely, as written — all this in the four sages of truth.
