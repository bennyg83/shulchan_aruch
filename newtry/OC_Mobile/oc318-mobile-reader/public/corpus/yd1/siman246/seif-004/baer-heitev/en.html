Babylonian. The Perishah wrote: some householders practice studying every day Gemara with Rashi and Tosafot, and not the other poskim, and bring proof from what we say at the end of Niddah, "whoever studies halachot every day is assured that he is a member of the World to Come." But it does not appear so to me. One should study books of the poskim, the laws of the Torah, such as the Rif, Mordechai, Rosh, and those like them, for this is the root and essence of our Torah; and they do not fulfill their obligation at all through studying only Gemara with Rashi and Tosafot, for Rashi already wrote there that "one who studies halachot" means decided halachot. What the Rav wrote here, that the Babylonian Talmud is mixed, etc., refers to one who studies nine hours a day; since he has much time, he should study Talmud. But these householders, who study only three or four hours, should not study Talmud alone; end quote.
In Pardes. The Shach wrote: some wrote not to study Kabbalah until one is forty years old, since holiness, purity, diligence, and cleanliness are needed for this, and most who break through to ascend in this wisdom before the proper time are cut down before their time, as all this is written in the words of the sages of truth; end quote.
