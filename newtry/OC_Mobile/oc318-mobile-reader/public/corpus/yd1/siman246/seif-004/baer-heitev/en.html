Babylonian Talmud. The Perishah wrote: some householders are accustomed to study Gemara every day and not other poskim, and they bring proof from the statement at the end of Nedarim [Niddah]: whoever reviews halachot every day is assured he is a son of the World to Come. But in our opinion one must study books of the poskim; laws of the Torah such as the Rif, Mordechai, Rosh and similar; for this is the root and essence of our Torah, and they do not fulfill their obligation at all through studying Gemara alone; for Rashi already wrote there "whoever reviews halachot" means halachot in precise form. And what Rama writes here about Babylonian Talmud mixed with Scripture, Mishnah and Gemara refers to one who studies nine hours a day; since he has much free time he should study Gemara; but these householders who study only three or four hours should not study Gemara alone; end quote.
In Pardes. Shach cites authorities who advise not studying Kabbalah before age forty, because it requires exceptional holiness, purity, diligence, and cleanliness. Many who entered this wisdom prematurely were harmed before their time.
