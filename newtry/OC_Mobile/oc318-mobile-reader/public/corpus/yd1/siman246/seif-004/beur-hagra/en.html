That is, etc. As stated in Kiddushin 49a: 'I have read Scripture,' etc.
That is, etc., and the meaning, etc. There: 'I have learned,' etc. See Rashi there, 49a, s.v. Midrash, etc.
That is, that he understand, etc. There: 'I am a sage,' etc. See Rashi to Sukkah 28a, s.v. Gemara, etc.
When does this apply, etc. This follows from the end of chapter 2 of Bava Metzia: 'One who engages in Scripture,' etc. Tractate Soferim, chapter 15, likewise teaches in the name of Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai: 'One who engages,' etc. Greater than this is one who has read and studied Mishnah, etc. They also said that it may be compared, etc.; but fortunate is the person, etc., provided that he studies, etc. See there. (Additional note) When does this apply, etc. See the Be'er HaGolah. The Ran, however, writes in the first chapter of Avodah Zarah that dividing one's study into thirds is not literal; rather, one should assign to each branch the amount of time appropriate for it. See there.
And some say, etc. This is derived from Sanhedrin 24a: 'Babylon,' etc. See Tosafot there and in the parallel passage.
A person should not, etc. As stated in Menachot there, where Ben Dama asked, etc. The Sifrei expounds: 'You shall speak of them,' make them primary and do not make them secondary. Your dealings and conversation should be in them, and you should not mix foreign matters into them. One should not say, 'I have learned the wisdom of Israel; I will now study the wisdom of the nations.' Scripture therefore says, 'to walk in them,' not to become exempt from them. Likewise it says, 'They shall be yours alone'; 'When you walk, it shall guide you,' etc.
Nevertheless, it is permitted, etc.; and this is called, etc.; and a person should not, etc. These are the Rambam's words. He explains that this was the matter of the four who entered the Pardes, and that they were punished because all of them, except Rabbi Akiva, were young. Regarding Acher, the Gemara says in Chagigah 15b that books of heretics were found with him, etc.; and Rabbi Elazar said, 'I am not old,' in Chagigah 13a. Yet neither the author nor the Rambam saw the Pardes. The wording of the author is imprecise: if this is the Pardes, how can he then say 'but one should not,' etc.? There is no greater reception of reward than this, as stated in Sukkah 28a and Bava Batra 134a: 'A small matter' means the discussions of Abaye and Rava; 'a great matter,' etc. See the Shach. It is nevertheless true that a person should not stroll through these matters casually, as Chagigah 13a states: 'The secrets of Torah are not transmitted,' etc., as above.
Provided that, etc. Sanhedrin 100b, as explained above regarding Acher.
