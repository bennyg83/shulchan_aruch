And some say that even when healthy, etc., it is permitted, and therefore they practiced, etc. — see Kesef Mishneh chapter 3 of Laws of Torah Study, who expanded to reject Rambam's words and expanded to bring proofs for those who permit; and Bach wrote that Beit Yosef too left his words unspecified and did not explain the rejections of Rambam's proofs, nor show the place of proof, etc.; and in truth, even though Beit Yosef did not explain, they wrote in Be'ur in Choshen Mishpat and his conclusion there: the general rule is that whoever has no means to support himself may take his wage to teach, whether from the students themselves or from the community. After the Lord informed us of all this, one may say our Rabbi's intent here is that a person should not cast off the yoke of work to live from people in order to learn, but should learn a trade that supports him; if it suffices, good; if not, he takes his sustenance from the community and there is nothing wrong; even if we say Rambam's view is not so but as appears from his words in Mishnah commentary — behold we hold wherever halachah wavers in your hand, follow the custom; and we saw all sages of Israel before and after our Rabbi practice taking wages from the community; and also it is known halachah is like Rabbenu in Mishnah commentary; it is possible all sages of the generations agreed because of "a time to act for the Lord, they have voided Your Torah," for if sustenance of learners and teachers were not available they could not toil in Torah properly and Torah would be forgotten, Heaven forbid; and when it exists, the community is obligated to support them as they see fit.
But he may not accept gifts, etc. — and in Kesef Mishneh there it implies even gifts are permitted to accept; and so Maharshal there, that there is no decisive ruling, and whoever is strict is strict, for it is not written in the verse "and one who takes gifts shall die," etc.; see there. And Bach too expanded that it is permitted for a rosh yeshivah or av beit din to accept gifts until he becomes wealthy, like a nasi and kohen gadol; see there, and so they practiced.
