Words of Torah are not established in one who eases himself upon them, nor in those who learn from joy and from eating and drinking, but in one who mortifies himself over them and afflicts his body always, and does not give sleep to his eyes nor slumber to his eyelids: {Rama: And a person should not think to engage in Torah and acquire wealth and honor with the learning, for one who raises this thought in his heart does not merit the crown of Torah; rather he should make Torah fixed and work occasional, lessen business and engage in Torah, remove worldly pleasures from his heart, and work each day for his living if he has nothing to eat, and the rest of day and night engage in Torah; great is one who sustains himself by his hand, as it says, "When you eat the labor of your hands," etc.; whoever resolves to engage in Torah and not work but sustain himself from charity — behold he profanes God's name and disgraces Torah, for it is forbidden to benefit from words of Torah in this world; and any Torah without work brings sin and ends in robbing people (language of Tur). All this is when healthy and able to engage somewhat in his craft or in some worldly occupation to sustain himself (Rabbeinu Yonah, Rabbeinu Yerucham netiv 2, and so Maharal); but an elder or sick person may benefit from his Torah and they provide for him (all agree); and some say even when healthy it is permitted (Beit Yosef in the name of Teshuvat Rashbatz); therefore the custom in all Israel is that the city rabbi has income and provision from the townspeople so he need not engage in craft before people and Torah be disgraced before the masses (Abarbanel on Avot); and specifically a sage who needs this, but a wealthy person is forbidden; and some are further lenient to say a sage and his students may receive support from givers so they can engage in Torah comfortably — nevertheless one who can sustain himself well by his hand and engage in Torah is piety and God's gift; but this is not every person's measure, for not everyone can engage in Torah, become wise, and sustain himself (same responsum); all this when taking a fee from the community or fixed support — but he may not take gifts from people; and that they said whoever brings a gift to a sage is as if he offers first fruits — this is small gifts, as people customarily bring a small gift to an important person even if an am ha'aretz (Rabbeinu Yerucham in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah); and a Torah scholar may taste something from his ruling to clarify his ruling, but to take a valuable gift from what he permitted is forbidden (Hagahot Ashiri, first chapter of Avodah Zarah); and one who uses the crown — removed (Hagahot Maimoniyot, Semag); and some say this is one who uses names (there in Avot de-Rabbi Natan); and it is permitted for a young scholar to make himself known in a place that does not know him if he needs this (Nedarim 62, Semag, Hagahot Maimoniyot there, Teshuvat Rashba siman 84).}
