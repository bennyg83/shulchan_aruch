But he should not accept gifts from people. The command to elevate the High Priest “from among his brethren” refers to an established communal obligation to support the office, equivalent to salary, not voluntary private gifts. The same applies when Rabbi Yochanan was appointed communal head. The fundamental permission for scholars to receive support is to preserve Torah study and prevent livelihood burdens from interrupting it. Even one with some assets may still need support because Jewish obligations, including educating sons and marrying daughters to scholars, are extensive. Maharshal permits scholars in genuine need to benefit from others. A person who is not wealthy may even accept substantial support until he becomes established. Once he has sufficient capital to sustain himself, he should no longer accept gifts. The claim that an av beit din should enrich himself through gifts so his authority will be respected is dangerous, because litigants may give advance gifts to influence him. One who does not truly need support should distance himself from this ugliness, all the more from soliciting gifts, which disgraces Torah and its students. “The righteous shall live by his faith.” [Citation 100.] [Citation 186.]
