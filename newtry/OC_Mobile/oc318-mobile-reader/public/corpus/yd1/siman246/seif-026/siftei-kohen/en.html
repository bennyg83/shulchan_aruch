When he finishes a tractate, etc. And it is written at the end of responsum of Maharam Mintz that when they come to the end of a tractate he should leave a little at the end until the proper time — a day fit to make a feast on it, etc.; and therefore all householders are accustomed to go to the yeshiva at the beginning of the term; and therefore the custom is to inform them when the rabbi comes to finish the tractate so that the householders also come to the siyum; and then they are there at beginning and end and return after the minyan to say Kaddish deRabbanan and the ten sons of Rav Pappa; and it is a seudat mitzvah — for even a mourner within twelve months for his father and mother may feast there, etc.; and see there also Maharshal wrote in chapter Merubah siman 37 that siyum of a tractate is a seudat mitzvah; and he concluded that nevertheless one should not bless "whose dwelling is in joy"; and at the end of the chapter he wrote that even one who did not finish a tractate — great mitzvah to rejoice with the one finishing; and even a single one who finished — others are obligated to finish with him, etc. — see there; and in responsa of Maharil siman 172: on the day his father or mother died — the world is accustomed to fast — it is forbidden to eat at siyum of tractate, for it is like permitted things that others were accustomed to forbid (as above siman 214 — see there).
And nevertheless permitted to enter, etc. Also in Turei Zahav siman 41 he wrote that it is permitted to enter synagogue and bathhouse even while [contemplating] a halachah that is not explicit; and specifically regarding prayer (Orach Chayyim siman 92) it is forbidden because he cannot concentrate well — for certainly thoughts of halachah will distance him to analyze it; and if so necessarily his heart will be troubled — if he analyzes the halachah he cannot concentrate in prayer; and if he forces his heart's thought to remove that halachic analysis — that itself is a distraction for him, for he must remove from his heart what he desires to think, and he will lose through this the concentration of prayer. And furthermore one may distinguish: specifically regarding prayer we are concerned lest he contemplate in it words of Torah because both are holy matters; but we are not concerned he will contemplate in a filthy place such as bathhouse and bathroom — end quote; but afterward he wrote: nevertheless it is known it is very difficult to remove from one's heart thoughts of Torah even in a filthy place — one whose heart is hasty and eager to analyze pilpul of halachah; and even though we say in chapter Mi SheMeto Rabbi Huna said: a Torah scholar is forbidden to stand in filthy alleys because he cannot be without thoughts of Torah — apparently we are concerned for this — difficult — end quote; and he wrote on this in Darkei Moshe: and it seems to me we must distinguish between filthy alleys and bathhouse — for otherwise a Torah scholar would never be permitted to enter a bathhouse, as it is forbidden to enter filthy alleys; but necessarily it must be that he does not contemplate in the bathhouse at all and is careful according to what one is careful in filthy alleys; and if so even from an halachah that is not explicit is also permitted — end quote; meaning: since with intent he goes to the bathhouse to bathe there — if so distraction of study turns away from him until he leaves the bathhouse; and likewise in the bathroom; and it appears also the opinion of Maharai is so — for he elaborated there in responsum that it is permitted and proved so from the Gemara chapter 5 of Kiddushin; and he did not write this conclusion "nevertheless it is known," etc. — except to warn Torah scholars when they enter synagogue or bathhouse to turn their thought from thought of study; and likewise in Maadanei Melech chapter Mi SheMeto page 27 side 1 anonymously in name of Turei Zahav that it is permitted to enter synagogue even from halachah that is not explicit; and see in Orach Chayyim siman 85.
