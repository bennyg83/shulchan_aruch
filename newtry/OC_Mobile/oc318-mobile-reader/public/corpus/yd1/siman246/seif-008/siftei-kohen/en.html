They do not learn from him, etc. This law is learned from chapter "these shave" (Moed Katan folio 17a), that they excommunicated that young Torah scholar even though the rabbis needed him, because Ravah in Bava Batra said Rabbi Yochanan said: what is written "the lips of a kohen shall guard knowledge and Torah they shall seek from his mouth, for he is an angel of Hashem of hosts", if the rabbi is like an angel of Hashem they shall seek Torah from his mouth, and if not they shall not seek Torah from his mouth. And it is difficult: in chapter 1 of Chagigah (folio 12b) they say Rabbi Meir, how did he learn Torah from Acher; and Rabbi in Bava Batra said Rabbi Yehoshua said: what is written "the lips of a kohen," etc., Reish Lakish found a verse and expounded "incline your ear and hear the words of the sages and set your heart to my knowledge", to their knowledge it does not say but to my knowledge; the verses contradict each other, no difficulty: here regarding a great one, here regarding a small one (Rashi explained: a great one who knows to be careful in his deeds may learn Torah from his mouth; and in Ein Yaakov explained in name of Chiddushim in one opinion). And if so the Rambam and his supporters must distinguish between great and small; and one may say he holds as Tosafot wrote there: and this that he expounds in the last chapter of Moed Katan that they excommunicated him, etc., one may say they were small ones who studied before him and they were concerned lest they be drawn after him, end quote. If so we see that even in the days of the Gemara there were small ones, all the more in our time when all are considered small, as written "if the early ones were like angels we are like people; and if the early ones were like people we are like donkeys; and not like the donkey of Rabbi Pinchas ben Yair," etc.; and if so in our time there is no distinction. And the Gaon may he live long wrote: he holds that since Reish Lakish said Rabbi Meir found a verse, etc., it implies Rabbi Meir holds so and we do not hold like him; and likewise Tosafot and the Rosh and other poskim wrote in chapter 1 of Chullin (folio 11) regarding Rabbi Meir "he would not eat an unsupervised vegetable," etc., see there; and this is what they say there in the Gemara: the Holy One, blessed be He does not say a tradition in name of Rabbi Meir, since he learned his tradition from the mouth of Acher, see there.
