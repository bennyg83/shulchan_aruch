One does not say to him "heal," etc. — it is written in Perishah: it is possible this applies specifically in their days when they did not show their books outside, all the more so they did not read [by heart]; but now when in any case people are not careful, they say "heal" — end. And he should not have written this to invent a leniency from his heart, for even though we find in Orach Chayim siman 70 a similar idea regarding a groom reading Shema nowadays, since other people too are not careful — that is because a good thing follows, that he will read Shema; which is not so here, where something improper follows, that the listener thinks: since it is now permitted to say "heal," the strap is permitted, and he will also engage in idle talk; and we are already warned of this with punishment of glowing coals, Heaven forbid; therefore one should not heed this to be lenient; on the contrary, the rabbi should warn about this and they will learn a fortiori regarding prohibition of idle talk; and one who increases honor of Torah is honored and praised.
