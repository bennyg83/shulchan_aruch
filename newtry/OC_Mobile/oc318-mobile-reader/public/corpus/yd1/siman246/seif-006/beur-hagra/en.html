A woman, etc. The reading 'from a non-Jew' should be omitted, as is evident from Bava Kamma 38a. See there.
But not, etc. Sotah 21a.
The Sages commanded, etc. This follows Rabbi Eliezer and Rabbi Yehoshua, unlike Ben Azzai. Rabbi Elazar ben Azariah likewise states in the first chapter of Chagigah: 'The men come,' etc., as Tosafot there cite from the Jerusalem Talmud.
(Additional note) When does this apply, etc. This follows from Nedarim 35b: 'But he may teach his sons and his daughters,' etc. Although the Rambam omitted 'his daughters,' because this is only after the fact, it remains forbidden initially.
Nevertheless, etc. This follows from the statement that women come, etc. See Tosafot to Sotah there, 21b, s.v. Ben Azzai, who write: 'It appears,' etc.
And a woman is not, etc. Kiddushin 29a.
Nevertheless, etc. The Gemara in Sotah there.
