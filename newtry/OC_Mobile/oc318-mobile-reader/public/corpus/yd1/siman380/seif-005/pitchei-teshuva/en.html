See Mishkenot Yaakov responsa siman 68, who wrote that the view of the Rif and Rambam seems more correct, for they do not distinguish and forbid partners in every case; see the Shach s.k. 23. So too it appears from the Yerushalmi, which makes no distinction at all. As for the Rosh's challenge against the Rif, where he wrote that our Gemara disagrees with the Yerushalmi, it seems to me that they do not disagree, for the two incidents are not comparable. In the incident of Maryon, each of them had one ox that was not fit to plow alone, and they formed a partnership to plow each one's field with both animals. When mourning occurred, we stop the team, and he did not give it to his partner to do his work, for it was pledged and hired to him, etc. This has no connection at all to partners in a store, where they are partners in the work and it is the work of both of them, and the other is acting as his agent; that is forbidden. According to this, one should be stringent with partners in a store even privately, while in the case of the camel or ox in the Gemara one may permit even publicly; see there.
