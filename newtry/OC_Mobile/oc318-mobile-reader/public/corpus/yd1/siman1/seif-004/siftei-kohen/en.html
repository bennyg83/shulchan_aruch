**Or they were stolen.** The Tur’s wording and the poskim: even if he is suspected regarding theft, he is not suspected regarding shechitah. See below siman 2 seif 18.
**That most of Israel.** Even if most of the city and market are gentiles—and likewise if most gentiles are commonly found there—even if most of the city and market are Jewish, it is forbidden, for everything depends on who are commonly present. If gentiles and Jews are equally commonly present, we follow the majority of the market found there; if equal in the market, we follow the majority of the city; if equal in the city, it is forbidden. All this appears from the poskim and Beit Yosef. See below siman 129 seifim 11, 17, 18, 19; and Choshen Mishpat siman 259 seif 3.
**Commonly found.** The same applies where most gentiles are commonly found: if most butchers there are Jewish, it is permitted—and specifically for livestock the majority of butchers helps, but not for fowl and the like, whose practice is to slaughter them at home; rather we require specifically that most Jewish fowl-hunters slaughter immediately when they hunt. So Rosh and Rabbeinu Yerucham; so Beit Yosef. What Choshen Mishpat siman 259 seif 6 writes—a place where most are gentiles and most butchers Jewish, an animal or bird found slaughtered there is permitted—speaks of large birds.
**And also most of the city’s thieves, etc.** This refers to “stolen”: for stolen animals we require specifically that most of the city’s thieves be Jewish; we do not follow the majority of Jews in the city. If most of the city’s thieves are Jewish, even if most of the city are gentiles, it is permitted; and if most of the city’s thieves are gentiles, even if most of the city are Jewish, it is forbidden. So is proven in Ashrei and Beit Yosef; so Darchei Moshe implies. See below siman 129 seif 1. In Levush it appears that in practice one requires both that most of the market be Jewish and most of the city’s thieves Jewish—for he did not mention earlier only “one who finds an animal,” etc., but afterward wrote “and most of the city’s thieves are Jewish”—which is surprising; perhaps there is a scribal error in his words.
**They are permitted.** This speaks of cases where there is no concern for meat that left sight of the eye, as will be explained in siman 63—see there.
**In a garbage heap in the market, etc.** The reason is that people are apt to throw their carcasses there. The Bach wrote that when the garbage heap is less than four cubits it is not considered a garbage heap and is not necessarily forbidden.
