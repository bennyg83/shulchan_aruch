**That it became invalid.** See Beer Hetev (what he wrote “and Taz disagrees,” etc.; see responsum Mishkanot Yaakov siman 4 what he wrote on this); and what he wrote “butcher who sold,” etc.—see in Hagahot there regarding utensils cooked in meat bought from that butcher retroactively, and see from this below siman 69 seif 12. And see in responsum Azriel Panim Me'irot part 3 siman 27, who ruled regarding a slaughterer they asked about an animal he slaughtered and examined whether it is kosher, and he said before witnesses it is kosher; afterward they found a large adhesion by which it became treifah through this adhesion—that one should not remove him, for he can say “I was unintentional” and from haste as such a stumbling block is common; and even a butcher from whose hand treifah issued has no excuse to say “I was unintentional,” as written below siman 119 seif 17—for here there is proof and assessment on the slaughterer that if he wanted to deceive intentionally he would have severed the adhesion in a way they would not notice at all; and he brought the words of Maharshal in chapter Gid HaNashe siman 16 who wrote this reasoning (and likewise ruled in responsum Shivat Tzion end siman 15 on an actual case like this—see there). And he wrote further: but since the rabbi of the city removed him from his craft for a fence— and we find that great Rishonim removed the slaughterer for thirty days, and the slaughterer acted improperly by not accepting the rabbi's decree—proper to forbid utensils of what they cooked from his shechitah after his removal—see there. And see in responsum Nodah B'Yehudah second edition part Yoreh De'ah siman 48, who wrote regarding a slaughterer who was insolent against the rabbi and the rabbi forbade his shechitah, and some baalei batim scorned this prohibition and ate from his shechitah; afterward that rabbi died—there is room to doubt whether meat remaining from his shechitah during the rabbi's life is now permitted, for perhaps the prohibition expired retroactively. Regarding utensils—for those who did not accept the rabbi's words they are permitted, since once he died the prohibition expired from the slaughterer and also from the utensils; but for those who accepted the rabbi's words it is like a vow, and there is doubt whether kabbalah is like the meat and wine explained in siman 216 seif 9 and forbidden for imparting taste—therefore they should leave the utensils twenty-four hours so there not be sixty—forbidden taste—to resolve all doubts—see there. And see responsum Beit Yaakov siman 4, responsum Basem Rosh siman 299, Hagahot Kesef Niskar there, responsum Pri Tevuah siman 13, Keter Kehuna simanim 28–29, Beit Ephraim part Yoreh De'ah simanim 4, 5, and 8 (and responsum Chavat Shaul simanim 2–3) who expanded on this matter; and see responsum Brit Avraham part Yoreh De'ah from siman 1 to siman 11, who raised many novel laws regarding a slaughterer from whose hand treifah issued, and in several matters disagrees with Acharonim on this—since this matter is not so common I abbreviated.
