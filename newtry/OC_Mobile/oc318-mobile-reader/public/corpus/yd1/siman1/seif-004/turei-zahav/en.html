**If his kids were lost.** I say: he ruled in siman 63 that meat that left sight of the eye is forbidden even where all sellers and slaughterers are Jewish—for he wrote there “in some places permitted”; here too it speaks of that. Therefore he said here “his kids and his chickens,” implying they are known to be his—only coming to permit here due to shechitah presumption. The Tur, who wrote “one who finds an animal slaughtered properly,” implies even another’s animal is permitted due to yeush, as Choshen Mishpat siman 259—he follows his view in siman 63 that the Rosh concludes like Rashbam; per him issur is only where most are gentiles, as Beit Yosef there in the name of Rosh; Tosafot wrote that even for theft we do not suspect regarding nevelah.
**In a garbage heap in the market.** Magen Avraham wrote: this “garbage heap” is like that in Choshen Mishpat siman 210 seif 15—“he sold the garbage heap” means he sold refuse; Rashbam in the Gemara: a garbage heap is a place three deep or three high where refuse is regularly thrown. Therefore he wrote that here too anything less than four cubits is not called a garbage heap and is permitted. In my view it appears that specifically there for sale one must give all the refuse—perhaps only in a place always considered a garbage heap; not so here for throwing a carcass temporarily.
