(Shach s.k. 5.) **Thus wrote Bach**, etc. It appears Ra'avad emerged thus in order to resolve Tosafot's question on Ravina from the baraita of a found chicken; therefore he wrote that Ravina concedes when they are not before us and we cannot pursue after him—it is permitted. Nevertheless, per this it is difficult on that which we say "all" like Ravina, one version, etc.—from where does the Gemara learn that they hold leniently regarding "most found at slaughter are experts" and established? Perhaps conversely they hold only that most found are experts and not established; if so, what does "all" come to include? For Tosafot it fits, since for the first tanna who is lenient regarding "his kids were found"—perforce they hold two matters in the baraita, only Ravina holds like the other tanna who forbids. If so, one may say the tanna who forbids does not hold one majority; but from Torah to say regarding experts and established to forbid, even though it is a majority, and he disagrees with the first tanna in two matters—therefore we say the other Amoraim hold leniently like the first tanna. But for Ra'avad it is difficult—perhaps all hold that l'chatchila and b'dieved, when we can pursue after him, one must know he is expert and established. One may say: **And it appears** further that he comes to answer the above question of Tosafot; therefore he distinguishes: there they are not before us and it is impossible to clarify—unlike here, where we can pursue after him. Even for Ravina, b'dieved when we cannot pursue after him—it is permitted. If so, why do we not give him to slaughter on the basis of examination afterward? If Ravina disagrees with the other Amoraim—for Ravina holds that even if he forgets afterward it is permitted—nevertheless we do not give on that basis. The other Amoraim hold we give on that basis; if so, the Gemara should have said "all" like Ravina, one version—that they hold it is permitted to give on that basis. One must strain that it depends on this: for Ravina the majority is not so strong, and it is permitted only in true b'dieved without being able to pursue after him; therefore the law is that we do not give him l'chatchila on the basis of examination afterward. But the other Amoraim hold "most found" and the majority is strong, and there is no need to pursue after him; therefore it is fit to give l'chatchila on the basis of examination afterward. **However**, per this it is difficult on that which we say "all" like Ravina, one version—"most found"; from where does the Gemara learn—perhaps they hold only most found are experts and not most established, and it is forbidden to slaughter only when it is known he is expert and established? Without the words of Ra'avad, one may say they disagree only on one matter, and they say only most found are experts, or only most found are established. But to say they disagree on both matters—regarding majority of experts and majority of established—from Torah, and therefore it could not be said the Amoraim require expert and established. Also one may say: since in truth it was for them in that sugya that they disagree only regarding "found in the garbage heap," therefore all hold found slaughtered animals are permitted—for we say majority of experts and established; therefore they establish the Amoraim's reason accordingly, that it accords with the halachah. But when we can pursue after him, one may say they do not rely on majority of experts nor on majority of established. If so, from where does the Gemara learn the other Amoraim disagree leniently with Ravina—perhaps conversely they disagree stringently, that he may not slaughter unless it is known he is expert and established—and think.
