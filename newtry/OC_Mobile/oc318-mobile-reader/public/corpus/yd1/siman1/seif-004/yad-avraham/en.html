(In Shach s.k. 29.) **That blessing does not impede b'dieved**, etc. In Ba'er Heitev at the beginning of Chullin another reason appears: regarding all blessings one fulfills b'dieved if he did not make it heard to the ear, only he blessed in his heart—and likewise here he fulfills even though he does not hear—see there. It appears there is a practical difference between the two reasons according to Geonim and Ra'avyah, as Mordekhai wrote in chapter 25: if he acted intentionally and did not bless, one should fine him; if so, per this view, if a deaf-mute who speaks and does not hear slaughtered intentionally—it is forbidden according to us; but according to Rosh's reason it is kosher even according to us; and so is the view of Taz who wrote "and if he slaughtered, his slaughter is kosher"—this language is for everyone, as we say in the Gemara beginning of Chullin 15 regarding one who slaughters on Shabbat and Yom Kippur—and think.
See Shulchan Aruch siman 6 who proved from Tur's words regarding one who became mute: even where he is not fit to bless, even hirhur helps to hear from another—and we say "one who hears is as one who answers"; and likewise regarding naked— forbidden, sheomea keoneh; and think—if so a mute should perform chalitzah and hear the reading from another, for it is keoneh and speech.
(In Shach s.k. 27.) **That a minor is commanded regarding shechitah**, etc.—meaning he is called bar zevicha, and it is forbidden to feed him by hand, and we are commanded not to give him nevelot; as Peri Megadim wrote and likewise in responsa of Maharil—and this is if we say forbidden to feed him by hand from Torah; because of derabbanan he is not called bar zevicha, just as he is not called bar keshira regarding tefillin because chinuch is derabbanan. One may bring proof from what Tosafot wrote in Avodah Zarah 28—that he is called bar brit because we are commanded to circumcise him; otherwise it should be difficult: perhaps a minor is among them, as Tosafot wrote regarding apostate for orlah. And likewise one may say deaf-mute and shotah are ben brit—for otherwise let us say behold there is among them. What Peri Megadim wrote here that they are warned and sons of mitzvot is against the sugya at the beginning of Mi sheHechshich; and Rambam chapter 9 of testimony and beginning of Yevamot that Beit Din is not commanded to separate them; and likewise Magen Avraham siman 489—and it implies there they are worse than minors with da'at.
