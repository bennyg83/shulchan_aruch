(In Shach s.k. 29.) **That blessing does not impede b'dieved**, etc. In Ba'er Heitev at the beginning of Chullin another reason appears: regarding all blessings one fulfills b'dieved if he did not make it heard to the ear, only he blessed in his heart—and likewise here he fulfills even though he does not hear—see there. It appears there is a practical difference between the two reasons according to Geonim and Ra'avyah, as Mordekhai wrote in chapter 25: if he acted intentionally and did not bless, one should fine him; if so, per this view, if a deaf-mute who speaks and does not hear slaughtered intentionally—it is forbidden according to us; but according to Rosh's reason it is kosher even according to us; and so is the view of Taz who wrote "and if he slaughtered, his slaughter is kosher"—this language is for everyone, as we say in the Gemara beginning of Chullin 15 regarding one who slaughters on Shabbat and Yom Kippur—and think. See Shulchan Aruch siman 6 who proved from Tur's words regarding one who became mute: even where he is not fit to bless, even hirhur helps to hear from another—and we say "one who hears is as one who answers"; and likewise regarding naked— forbidden, sheomea keoneh; and think—if so a mute should perform chalitzah and hear the reading from another, for it is keoneh and speech.
