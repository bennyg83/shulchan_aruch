His kids were lost, etc.—and he recognizes by their nature that they are his, or he has a sign on them—for otherwise it is forbidden because of meat removed from sight, per Rambam; see siman 61 that in this even an average sign helps, and we do not require a prominent sign.
Or stolen—even though he is suspected regarding theft, he is not suspected regarding slaughter—poskim.
Where majority of Israel are found—see Peleti: the essence is if in that market where it was found the majority of passersby and returners are Israel—even if the whole city is majority idolaters—since in that place the majority are Israel, it is kosher; and likewise the reverse; see Peleti, where I wrote that if it is found near an idolater neighborhood, even though passersby are majority Israel—majority and proximity—one should be stringent—see there.
Majority of thieves of the city are Israel—in Peleti I clarified: this is where there is no market at all whose passersby are Israel, only the whole city is in doubt; therefore regarding lost we follow majority of people of the city, and regarding stolen we follow majority of thieves; but where there is a market with passersby, and it was found there—we no longer pay attention whether majority of thieves are idolaters or Jews; everything is only according to passersby—if they are Jews, kosher; if not, forbidden—for from majority of passersby it fell, and we do not pay attention to the thief at all.
They are permitted—and Acharonim wrote that even majority of idolaters are found and majority of Israelite butchers—it is permitted; and likewise the reverse; see Choshen Mishpat siman 259; this is in an animal and large birds, but in small birds there is no relevance of butchers, since they slaughter in their homes; see Peleti, where I clarified that in this case of lost or stolen kid—majority of Israelite butchers does not help; one needs only majority of the city or majority of Israelite thieves—and this is correct; one should not lighten and rely on Israelite butchers.
Whether in a market—see Peleti, where I brought Rambam's view that he is stringent in a market, and I settled him with good reason and sense for the practical difference; therefore regarding stolen or lost one should be stringent when found in a market; but when found unspecified, we rely on majority of Israelite butchers—even when found in a market it is kosher—see there.
