**In a place where most of Israel are found**—everything depends on the place where passersby and returners are found; likewise in Choshen Mishpat siman 259 seif 3 everything depends on majority of passersby in that place whether idolaters or Israelites. Semag and Prisha wondered there—what of it, in that market they are common, why do we not say majority and proximity follow majority (Peri Chadash in siman 60 challenged this too and did not see Semag preceded); Semag's answer I did not understand—for he wrote since the lost object is in its place this does not apply—for in a corpse in a fallen heifer likewise the corpse is in its place and we judge who is the murderer who killed there in that place. And so too is good. Nevertheless I read in it majority and proximity; but in truth I did not understand their wonder—for certainly if we say in this market idolaters dwell, nevertheless the passersby and returners are mostly all Israelites and we want to say since it was found near idolaters' neighborhood we follow them and from them it fell—majority and proximity apply; but here we do not pay attention at all to houses and their neighborhoods, only who are the passersby and returners in that place—we follow what is found, and that is the majority, as they said regarding eglah arufah—we follow majority of the world and answer regarding one who dwells between mountains where the foot of the majority of the world does not pass—what is found there, we follow majority of towns where the foot of passersby is common there; and likewise this matter—all follows majority found in this market, place of its finding. They are the majority and majority of the world does not combine since their foot is not found there—it is not in the majority. And they said regarding locked city gates—and this is that the foot of others is not so found; we follow what is found there whether for leniency or stringency as Shach wrote; except I am inclined to be stringent since Rambam did not decide this law whether majority and proximity follow majority—for in that finger found in the orchard of orlah Rambam gave reason that if from that orchard it would not be hidden inside it—and this is the reason for the view we do not follow majority in place of proximity; but if we follow majority, in any case permitted; and likewise in laws of orlah in year 3 he wrote it is safek prepared, and if majority and proximity follow majority certainly they come from the world, etc.; and so in several places it teaches with a finger that he holds it is safek; and in my novelties on laws of orlah I advocated for him that the Gemara should not challenge him; if so in such a case one should be stringent from Torah and be concerned for Rambam's words and forbid—if found in proximity clearly to idolaters' neighborhood—and think.
**Majority of Israel.** Shach wrote: likewise if there is no majority of Israel, only majority of Israelite butchers—in animal except bird—all is permitted; likewise Peri Chadash; see Choshen Mishpat siman 259 seif 6 what Semag wrote there. In my heart it does not seem so—for not for nothing did Shulchan Aruch and Rama omit this law to distinguish between Israelite butchers as written in Choshen Mishpat. Therefore it appears in chapter 2 of Bava Metzia we say R' Chanina found a slaughtered kid between Tiberias and Tzippori and they permitted him because of finding like Rashba, and because of slaughter like R' Chanina son of R' Yosi haGelili—for we taught: if his kids were lost and he found them slaughtered, R' Chanina permits and R' Yosi forbids; the Gemara challenges: since they permitted him because of slaughter, etc.—does Rashba say even in majority of Israel; and it answers majority of idolaters and majority of Israelite butchers. Rosh there wrote regarding stolen—that it deals with majority of Israelite thieves; Me'adanei Melech wondered: since it deals with majority of Israelite butchers, if so even if the thieves are mostly idolaters it is also permitted. He wondered well apparently; but there is room to say that which majority of Israelite butchers helps is because homeowners do not raise animals for slaughter in their homes and buy everything from butchers—even idolaters buy from butchers, therefore permitted when Israelite butchers slaughter. However this is unspecified animal and we say certainly from butchers it came; but his kids were stolen that he recognizes and they are his—what need for butcher, can he not pass a knife on its neck and slaughter it—who prevents; in this we do not pay attention to majority of butchers, and they are like poultry per Ra'avad who does not pay attention to butcher. Therefore R' Chanina who found slaughtered kid—there is presumption there is a butcher; we follow majority of butchers as explained in the Gemara; but one whose kids were lost or stolen—specifically here there is no relevance to majority of butchers—what does butcher want there; therefore we need majority of Israelite thieves; if so presumption they slaughtered it, for they are not suspected regarding slaughter. The matter is necessary—for in Gemara Chullin it infers from this that "most found at slaughter are experts"—and it is difficult, behold his majority is majority of Israelite butchers and from them this came, and Israelite butchers obviously they are experts—who doubts them, only the question on unspecified person whether they are in presumption of experts; per words of Magen Avraham the Gemara's words are strained; but the correct view regarding when his kids were lost we do not pay attention to butchers at all—for we say in his house he slaughtered, and one must say "most found" are experts. One should not say it is not from kid only from chicken it infers, and regarding them Israelite butchers do not apply—for then for those rabbis who do not hold Ra'avad's view to distinguish between animal and bird—what would they answer; and furthermore regarding chicken even Ra'avad concedes, as Semag wrote there siman 259. This is straightforward and correct; therefore they did not mention here Israelite butchers, only in Choshen Mishpat regarding slaughtered kid—and this is true and correct; and with this it is resolved what is difficult for Ravina who holds we do not say "most found at slaughter are experts"—if so he ruled not like R' Chanina bar Yosei haGelili, only like R' Yosi—for with R' Chanina who found slaughtered kid they ruled in practice like R' Chanina bar Yosei haGelili and ma'aseh rav as Rashba wrote on Rambam; per what is written it is resolved that there regarding found kid in general there must be majority of Israelite butchers, and no longer need "most found are experts"; and Gemara Chullin regarding his kids lost—in this Israelite butchers do not apply, and one must say "most found," and Ravina holds they disagree and rules like R' Yosi—and there is room for question; however in hints it says his kids and chickens, and nevertheless requires majority of Israelite butchers—and this contradicts what is written above. Nevertheless truth guides his way as written regarding when his own kids were lost Israelite butchers do not apply.
**Majority of Israelite thieves**—I did not understand: if he found it in a place where most of Israel are common and we say from them it was lost—certainly what difference if majority of thieves are idolaters, behold the passersby and returners in that place are majority Israel and from them it was lost—and if so certainly lost from Israelite thief, and what difference if majority of thieves are foreigners; consider yourself: if lost and in a city majority idolaters, only in a market where found majority Israel passersby and returners—is it not kosher; and likewise the reverse—and this is what we say it fell clearly from those passersby; if so also regarding stolen—what of it that majority of thieves are foreigners, nevertheless since it was found in a market where their majority are Israel passersby and returners, we say it fell clearly from Israel. This is correct. Rosh said his words without market—only unspecified city whether majority idolaters or majority Israel; therefore regarding lost we depend on majority of city idolaters or Israelites, unlike regarding stolen—we do not follow majority of city, only majority of thieves—if they are Israelites permitted, if not forbidden; but when found in a designated market where many are found, we depend on majority of passersby and returners in the market—we no longer pay attention to majority of thieves—and this is clear.
**Whether in a market**—Rambam forbids when found in a market and wrote perhaps it became ruined and he threw it. They challenged him: why did he rule halachah like R' Yosi against R' Chanina bar Yosei haGelili—for with R' Chanina who found slaughtered kid they permitted him like R' Chanina his son and ma'aseh rav. Peri Chadash sought to say because of meat removed from sight he forbids—and this is not so, his words do not bear in Rambam who wrote it became nevelah and therefore was thrown; also this deals where he recognizes it by its nature; and to resolve it appears per what is written that there in R' Chanina's incident regarding found kid there must be majority of Israelite butchers; but when his kids were lost majority of butchers does not help, only "most found at slaughter are experts"—per this it is uncommon that one throws nevelah, and anonymously we say it was kosher, for majority of animals are not nevelot; only here there are two minorities—"most found at slaughter are experts" and there is a minority who are not experts—and if so here are two minorities, or he slaughtered not according to law, or he threw it when it was nevelah—and if so the majority became defective, therefore forbidden; but there with Israelite butchers they are certainly experts; and for Rambam "majority of Israelite butchers" is not precise, only all Israelite butchers (and likewise implied in Chullin regarding the tradition of leniencies—see there); if so there is only one minority that perhaps it was nevelah and kosher; and more straightforward one may say there we say it came from butcher—for it is not an idolater's way to slaughter in his house; if so tamei nevelah and threw does not apply—for a butcher does not throw nevelot, only sells to idolaters, therefore they were permitted to announce it fell among Israel so an idolater would buy from them—see there; if so no concern he threw it because of nevelah, unlike here that does not deal with Israelite butchers, only the finder, idolater, or thief slaughtered it, and butcher is not needed; if so obvious regarding one who finds nevelah he throws it and does not trouble to sell it, therefore forbidden—and if so there is no proof from there; he rules halachah like R' Yosi as Maggid Mishneh wrote; think.
