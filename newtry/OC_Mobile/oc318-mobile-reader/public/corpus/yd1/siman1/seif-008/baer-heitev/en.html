**Like a shoteh.** Meaning: l'chatchila he does not slaughter even with others al gavav; b'dieved kosher when others al gavav specifically.
**Pressed.** Shach wrote: Rama wrote thus for good advice; but by law he is like a sharp person for all matters and permitted b'dieved if others al gavav, or without others if he did not reach Lot’s drunkenness—as in Choshen Mishpat siman 235. Taz disagrees and wrote the reason in a drunk: his limbs are heavy on him and this does not depend on a person’s mind; proper to practice that all whose nature is strong on him—even if he did not reach Lot’s drunkenness—does not slaughter. Beit Yosef expanded and was very stringent on slaughterers habitual in this—to remove them because their limbs are heavy from much drinking; so too proper even if he did not reach Lot’s drunkenness. Semag wrote: an elder and one whose hands tremble from weakness or from his nature—their hands are also heavy; most of their shechitot are pressing. Although they said clearly they did not press—they are not believed because they are prone to this—end quote.
