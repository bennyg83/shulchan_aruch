**On the permitted it is forbidden.** Beer Hetev in the name of Beit Yaakov: even one who says on permitted “forbidden,” also there is room to remove him. And behold there Beit Yaakov wrote the reason—that there is concern he will cause damage if he sanctifies with it a woman, and since it is forbidden it will not be worth a perutah, etc.; and Peri Megadim in Mishbetzot s.k. 7 wrote the pleasant [explanation] that damage will come in two pots—see below siman 111—see there. It is clear from their words that even though the error is not from forgetfulness but from his confused mind that so appears to his distorted mind—also there is room to remove him until he repents. And it appears there is a difference between these two reasons: if he says on permitted that it is forbidden d'rabbanan—for Beit Yaakov there is concern even in this; see Pesachim 7 and Even HaEzer siman 28 seif 21; and for Peri Megadim there is no concern, for we do not attribute to something forbidden only d'rabbanan, as Menachem Nesiach wrote in siman 190 s.k. 43. And likewise if he says on a d'rabbanan prohibition that it is d'oraita—it is the reverse: for Beit Yaakov no concern, for Peri Megadim concern. However, his colleagues already disputed Menachem Nesiach (and I expanded on this below siman 111) and raised that we attribute even in something forbidden d'rabbanan—therefore in this manner, saying on derabbanan that it is d'oraita, there is no concern for Beit Yaakov nor Peri Megadim; but in the second manner there is concern for both.
