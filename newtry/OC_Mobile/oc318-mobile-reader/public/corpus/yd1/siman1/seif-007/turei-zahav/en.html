**If another recites the berachah.** Beit Yosef in the name of Rosh derives from one who speaks but does not hear that he may not slaughter l'chatchila from terumah; Beit Yosef asked: why not derive also one who became mute to forbid l'chatchila from terumah, for a mute and naked may not separate terumah? Answer: the tanna did not want to change the enactment. I wonder—for the Mishnah implies the enactment does not help, as it taught similar to naked where another’s berachah does not help; therefore Or Zarua cited in Beit Yosef from Hagahos Ashrei that a mute may not slaughter and another blesses. First resolve Or Zarua’s difficulty from “they saw him in court—all berachot even if he answered, he fulfilled”—Rashi: all Israel are guarantors for mitzvot except bread berachah—meaning birkat hana’ah, where there is no mutual guarantee for one not obligated to benefit or bless; if so, why may another bless for terumah and shechitah? It appears we distinguish mitzvot one must perform with his body, not via another—only if he cannot bless may another bless; not so terumah and shechitah which can be done via another—then the one performing the mitzvah blesses, not splitting mitzvah to one and berachah to another. Rosh also seems to hold mute may not separate terumah and another blesses for the reason stated; for shechitah his reason is that another may bless because shechitah berachah is not on the slaughter itself—no obligation to slaughter if he does not wish to eat; the intent is praise to God for forbidding meat without shechitah—all Israel share that berachah, but there is no place to bless except at slaughter of any animal. Example: Beit Yosef siman 265 in the name of Rabbeinu Tam on circumcision berachah—praise and thanks always for that sanctity—see there. Therefore erusin berachah fits: groom betroths and rabbi blesses—blessing on forbidden relations to all Israel; but for terumah separation the berachah is on the mitzvah of separating, not on issur of tevel—for terumah is obligatory even if he does not wish to eat grain—like other mitzvot; since he is not bodily obligated, for he can separate via agent, we do not split mitzvah and berachah—the separator blesses; therefore “mute may not separate terumah” fits—he truly has no enactment to bless. With what I wrote, Darchei Moshe’s difficulty is resolved—why may another bless here if birkat hana’ah when answering does not count? He answered as above. Magen Avraham also wrote that “if another blesses” for shechitah means if another slaughters his animal for himself and blesses, as Hagahos Ashrei—but I do not see this, for then he would have stated explicitly “if another blesses on another’s slaughter”; rather as I wrote—no birkat hana’ah here, only praise on the issur like erusin berachah.
