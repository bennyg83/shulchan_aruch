(There.) **Thus it is**, etc. One should examine from where this is—for we say: where it is possible to clarify, we clarify; but regarding examination of the lung, where there is no presumption of issur, one may say there is no need to clarify—see Magen Avraham siman 337 s.k. 2. Also here there are two majorities—majority of animals are kosher and majority of examiners are experts—no need to clarify. Per the words of Magen Avraham, it appears difficult that we must check the esophagus l'chatchila—for there is presumption of issur where it is possible to clarify, we clarify; and behold, regarding "most found at slaughter are experts," Rosh wrote the minority is an uncommon minority; nevertheless, when they are before us, examination is required—if so, the esophagus too should be checked. One must say Chazal required this only if there is a safek whether he performed the act of slaughter properly—for they are concerned perhaps he is not expert and delayed or pressed. But where he certainly performed the act of slaughter properly, only the concern is from elsewhere—that perhaps there was a hole in the esophagus, whereby it becomes invalid in the slaughter; since the animal is in presumption of wholeness and in presumption that if they slaughter it according to all laws of slaughter it will be permitted—in this there is no need to clarify. What must be said per the intent of Shach siman 25 s.k. 2: without checking, regarding dislocated—it is kosher, for a live animal is not in presumption that the simanim were uprooted, and it is not comparable to one who did not see that most were slaughtered—for there a live animal stands in presumption it is not slaughtered—see there. And this is even though regarding dislocated the slaughter does not help and it is also "not slaughtered"; nevertheless this is only if the dislocation is before us and there is safek whether it slipped before slaughter or after. But all cases where we do not see a defect are not called presumption of issur—for for this reason it was in presumption it is not dislocated, and that if they slaughter it according to laws of slaughter it will be permitted. It is different without seeing whether the simanim are mostly slaughtered—for there the safek is perhaps he did not perform the act of slaughter; nevertheless, regarding dislocated they need to check l'chatchila, since it is common, like examination of the lung. But regarding a hole in the esophagus—no need to clarify.
