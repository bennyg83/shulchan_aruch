**Expert, etc.—if another, etc.** According to Tur siman 63: one whom they know does not know the laws may slaughter l'chatchila if another stands over him—if so, why is “expert” needed here? One can say: here it speaks where that other is not expert but only knows how to recite the berachah; or that other recites the berachah and leaves, or stood there and did not see that he slaughtered but only made his berachah heard—and the like.
**If another recites the berachah.** Specifically when the other also slaughters; if not, he cannot recite the berachah—Hagahos Agudah, and Beit Yosef and Darchei Moshe bring this; so Shechitat Mahariv and Bach and other acharonim. So the law reads “if another recites the berachah,” not “and another recites the berachah”—meaning the other recites the berachah in any case. See below siman 19 that that other must intend to extract the slaughterer.
**If he is an expert.** That which he did not write regarding one who speaks but does not hear “if he is an expert” is because here we are taught that he slaughters even l'chatchila—therefore he had to say “if he is an expert,” which is obvious in my view. The Bach wrote in his last booklet that since he does not speak he is not among “most commonly found at shechitah are experts”—I do not know why his presumption should be lesser than other people, for we say at the beginning of chapter 1 of Chagigah that he is like a competent person for all matters, as explained in Choshen Mishpat siman 235. Further, we say there (3a): shall we say that when he does not speak he did not learn? Yet these two mutes were found to have learned halachah, Sifra, Sifrei, and the whole Shas—and we conclude he also learned from one who speaks but does not hear; also from Darchei Moshe nearby it is clear he is among most commonly found who are experts. In Darchei Moshe he answered that a deaf person who speaks but does not hear—even if expert—does not slaughter l'chatchila; b'dieved, even when before us, it is as when not before us, for he cannot be examined since he does not hear—and his shechitah is valid even if not expert; therefore we derive nothing there from “expert.” A question arises, for one can examine him through writing, as they examine for gittin and gifts, as Tur wrote in Choshen Mishpat siman 235 and Orach Chayyim siman 121.
