**Some say his slaughter is forbidden.** See Peri Chadash; Turei Zahav siman 2 holds one should not forbid slaughter when they did not forbid explicitly; Tiferet Tzvi siman 1: slaughterer who swore not to slaughter without a receipt from the leaseholder of the community and transgressed and slaughtered—do not forbid his slaughter as Peri Chadash wrote; Turei Zahav siman 2 agreed, since the community did not decree slaughter invalid, only regarding the oath alone—for this he is not suspected on slaughter, and one should not suspect him for benefit, for behold he slaughters for others and is kosher to slaughter from now on; also his law is not like one who transgresses an oath, since he excuses himself that he thought there is no objection of the leaseholder when he informs him immediately; and in particular since in truth the butcher had a receipt, only the slaughterer sinned that he was not commanded to see it and take from him—and his intent was to slaughter without receipt—there is no difference, and it is like a transgression without lashes; nevertheless fit for punishment and fine and to dismiss him for some time from descending before the ark, and fine him in money according to his ability, and accept repentance in Beit Din with fasting as fit—but do not disqualify him from slaughter, since by law he is not disqualified and dependents rely on him; what the community does not wish to trust him for the future—the community may forbid what he slaughters without receipt as nevelah, and for this he is not suspected to feed nevelot, and one should not dismiss him, for it appears not in rebellion and over ten—see there. He also wrote there siman 2 regarding slaughterers written in their document that they accepted upon themselves in cherem and have an excuse—they are trusted as explained in Yoreh De'ah siman 232 seif 12 in gloss and Choshen Mishpat siman 73; it does not apply "he made himself one" for kabbalah that he not be slaughterer—not that his slaughter is forbidden; and because of community cherem to dismiss him—if they already transgressed they fulfilled their vow and can return him, only if the cherem was that he not be their slaughterer—forever forbidden as siman 219—see there. He also wrote there siman 20: slaughterer who swore before Beit Din not to drink wine, and if he transgresses he will not slaughter again—and he transgressed and drank and slaughtered afterward; some permitted the slaughterer when informed he drank only on Motza'ei Yom Kippur and it is considered Yom Tov—and he expressed in his oath Shabbat and Yom Tov; one may say as in Orach Chaim siman 579 that Chanukah and Purim are not Yom Tov; nevertheless if the one who vowed says he intended them—he is believed; likewise Motza'ei Yom Kippur—in derashot of Maharil it is called Yom Tov language—see Orach Chaim in Beit Yosef siman 624; Rama also wrote it is somewhat Yom Tov. But per what Taz wrote he drank also on other days; and according to Rambam he is like apostate requiring knife examination; most poskim disagreed; but what he said he did not forbid himself drinking as all the land, only not to become drunk—not believed, for if so why mention Shabbatot and Yom Tov—and then he wishes to become drunk—it is abomination to be drunk even on Shabbatot and Yom Tov "who gives wine to eyes," etc.; his thought is recognizable that he forbade himself drinking even less than revi'it per Taz—for on wine even less than revi'it one blesses after-blessing, for such is way of drinking; and in vows follow language of Beit Din. But one who slaughtered after Beit Din forbade his slaughter—it is as nevelah and he did not turn to this and slaughtered—he is as one who fed nevelot and is suspected to feed nevelot and not trusted on slaughter; and even though only rabbinic issur as Taz siman 217—nevertheless certainly in slaughter there are issurim and he is suspected on them—who will take such a slaughterer—see there. See Noda BiYehudah Mahadura Tinyana siman 5: slaughterer whom community forbade to eat from his slaughter—if written in document forbidden like slaughter of idolater—even if examining knife and giving to him, forbidden to eat from his slaughter; but if written simply his slaughter is forbidden—one may lighten through examining knife and giving to him, as law of apostate slaughter—see there; and see there regarding community decree in cherem not to eat from his slaughter: if the matter is doubtful in the enactment—we go to lighten, for cherem from words of Kabbalah is derabbanan and its safek is lenient (see there he retracted from Noda BiYehudah part 1 Even HaEzer siman 77); therefore if majority of city agreed, and in particular scholars, to declare slaughterers kosher—they have power to declare him kosher if he did not feed treifot to Israel—see there. See Shevut Yaakov part 2 siman 58: what is close—that some rabbis enacted in their communities to proclaim all meat brought from a province as if eating treifah and forbid even utensils cooked in that meat—this is not correct, for one should fear enmity; fit for all Israel to be as one man regarding eating and drinking and not separate and make factions; and great urging needed regarding examination of knife and feeling, and to appoint for this kosher known people as siman 18; and so fit for every rabbi and leader in his city and province to supervise. So I practiced in Serbian lands under my jurisdiction—I examined all slaughterers and examiners in laws of Shulchan Aruch and examination and feeling and sharpening knives; urged them very greatly; all struck hands in actual deed to be zealous in examining knives, even if in their settlement one expert slaughterer or teacher to show his knife before him once a week, also not to declare any animal kosher that has a defect until he joins one expert slaughterer or teacher. Also to investigate when buying meat whether that slaughterer is expert and God-fearing and another will eat. What will he do if the slaughterer transgresses in rebellion—the collar hangs on that slaughterer's neck and all Israel are clean and no harm will befall, etc. But to forbid generally meat of people of the province and villages and even utensils—this is certainly great enmity, etc. Also from my fathers and teachers the Geonim I did not hear such—see there. It appears this is in general; but if known in villages and towns around there is concern because they are not expert or light-minded—a blessing on the teacher who comes to fence in his city to raise a stumbling block from the people's way; all this deals only if coming to forbid from concern for zeal in slaughter and examination; but not included is because of meat tax called taks in many places—enmity does not apply and it is straightforward. He wrote in Noda BiYehudah siman 1: enactment by city rabbi with community that proclaimed in synagogue two slaughterers may not slaughter alone without companion; proclaimed explicitly what one slaughters alone is treifah—forbidden to them from kabbalah they accepted; but permitted to people of another city; and even people of that city there is remedy even retroactively; and even though community cherem does not help except for the future as siman 228—nevertheless when asked at the opening and regret uproots retroactively; and even if this is called community opinion as Rama there—nevertheless for a mitzvah matter with their consent all may permit as explained there; and since there is dispute in the city—this is for a mitzvah matter and great is peace—see there. He also wrote there: slaughterers who struck hands to the rabbi not to declare any animal kosher until he places a seal on kosher sign—for butchers are suspected in his eyes; they transgressed the enactment; though fit for punishment to dismiss them from here onward if they do not repent—do not forbid retroactively, for the enactment is not in the matter of slaughter; and even if we say intent is on examination—if so they are suspected on examination—nevertheless if lung was lost, permitted in place of loss of minority, and here even with loss of minority permitted since only examined—they do not believe the slaughterer—from safek it is permitted. Further: in examination, since he only testifies the lung is kosher—even suspected for that matter he is believed regarding others—see there. See She'elot Ya'avetz part 2 siman 196: one who slaughtered without permission and kabbalah from rabbi Av Beit Din of the province; proclaimed in synagogue with rabbi's knowledge and community that his slaughter is forbidden, utensils cooked in that meat forbidden—he did not supervise and slaughtered afterward all he slaughtered after proclamation—meat and utensils forbidden until Beit Din and community return and permit him to slaughter; before that if appointed shatz must remove him—see there (in my responsa Beit Efrayim part 2 in manuscript I wrote regarding a butcher clarified several times sold treifot to city people; also possible he sold milk where fat—for failing in issurim all city people may not lighten to permit all utensils through waiting from Torah, even though in responsa siman 2 I was lenient; nevertheless here majority have forbidden utensils accustomed to cook daily and became forbidden through hot anger which is like cooking issur—only permit through waiting from Torah—this is like making safek derabbanan l'chatchila; see my responsa Beit Efrayim printed Orach Chaim siman 6 and I brought from Noda BiYehudah Mahadura Tinyana siman 91; I wrote if owner has many utensils where there is great loss—permit at least those not accustomed to use with heat, only Shabbat—for those utensils transgressed waiting from Torah at birth of safek; there I wrote more and abbreviated here).
