**And knows to be careful.** Beer Hetev in the name of Beit Yaakov what he wrote “mourner,” etc.; and see there he wrote he wonders on the reason “mourner does not slaughter,” and it is not mentioned in any posek; and it is possible he holds that from pain he will not put his heart to slaughter with intent—end quote. Tzarich iyun—for if so, even if his ship sank at sea he also should not slaughter, for in the Talmud Berakhot 11a they compare him to a mourner—see there; rather certainly for this we are not concerned at all; only the reason forbidden for a mourner to slaughter appears to me because he is forbidden in melacha, as written below siman 380; therefore in a case where melacha is permitted, as explained there seifim 2 and 5—in truth a mourner is permitted to slaughter, and this is clear. And what he further wrote “and an elder of eighty years,” etc.—see responsum Meir Netivim siman 76, who wrote that nowadays in these generations when strengths weakened, it is fitting and proper for all who have ability to protest and repair a fence that at least from forty years and onward no person should slaughter even if he still feels—because of lo pelug—and also until seventy years needs great examination; rabbis are obligated to supervise that slaughterers coming in days come before them periodically to test them, for it is likely to become defective and they themselves do not feel if their hands tremble—see there; and see responsum Teshuvah Me'ahavah part 1 siman 118.
