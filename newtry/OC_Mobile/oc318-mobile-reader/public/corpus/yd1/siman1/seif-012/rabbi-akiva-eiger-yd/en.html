(In the response.) **For in Gittin**, etc. This is not such a proof—for one may say: just as for R' Chanina here it is preferable to mikveh, which is safek, likewise one may say even for us, who forbid without bones having entered; nevertheless it is not fully comparable to mikveh—for there we judge it certainly impure even in the public domain, as explicit at the beginning of Niddah. But here, since there is only safek—and safek does not help against presumption of issur—nevertheless it is certainly not vadai. See Shach siman 110, laws of safek safeka, letter 28: in three doubts it helps where there is presumption—the first doubt is like vadai, and again safek safeka helps; if so, in safek safeka it is like one doubt in the body of the issur, forbidden only from doubt and not vadai. It is also possible to say further: the thin stringency is derabbanan—for safek is like majority, and min haTorah it helps even where there is presumption; only derabbanan it is forbidden, as minority against presumption. If so, one may say: therefore in mikveh the purities are forbidden, since we judge it certainly impure; and even for R' Shimon, who holds regarding mikveh it is only safek—for him automatically the thin stringency of public domain impure applies; therefore presumption of purity does not help, like in all safek tumah in private domain presumption does not help. But here, where it is only safek, one may say the vessels are permitted because of presumption of heter. However, from reasoning it appears presumption of heter does not apply to a vessel—for the earthenware of the vessel is still permitted, and we judge regarding the absorption from this slaughter that entered them; regarding the absorption there was never presumption of heter. Only regarding vessels that are not earthenware—and in particular if the defect was not known until after the vessels were made as forbidden vessels—there is room to lean lenient—and think.
