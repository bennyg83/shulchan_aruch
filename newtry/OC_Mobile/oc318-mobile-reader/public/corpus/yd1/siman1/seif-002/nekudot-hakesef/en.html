(s.k. 6) **He expanded** to disagree with Agudah and Rama from mikveh—and I already sensed this in Shach s.k. 8, that there is no question from mikveh—see there. Now I will explain more: it appears Agudah derives that since we hold "most found at slaughter are experts," therefore the majority assists the slaughterer, and perforce we do not require "he broke bones in it"—only because there there is no presumption from the force of majority. But here, since there is "most found at slaughter are experts," all concede; and it is close to say that even if he did not take kabbalah, Agudah declares kosher for this reason—and therefore he did not mention that he took kabbalah. Rather Rama must equate him with Rashba, and he holds it deals when he took kabbalah; and when he took kabbalah it is obvious it is not comparable—for now there is presumption and majority of presumption, for we establish him on his presumption, since he knew from the outset; and as also implied from Rashba's responsum. "Most found at slaughter are experts"—and it is not comparable to mikveh or knife found defective—for there there is no majority at all. Agudah derives from a fortiori: since there we say "the knife became defective, the animal did not become defective," all the more so here—even R' Huna concedes from the reason I separated. Also one may say: perforce there we do not require "he broke bones in it" only because perhaps he did not slaughter at the place of the defect—it is not a great safek, for typically the knife goes through the whole animal. But here one may say it is safek safeka: perhaps he knew, and perhaps he did not know—perhaps he slaughtered properly. Also what he brought as proof from Talmud in Mordekhai chapter Mi sheMet—is not proof at all, from all the reasons I wrote; all this is clear—and think. There is further room to distinguish in several aspects.
