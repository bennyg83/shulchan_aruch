(Siman 1, Taz in introduction.) **"And you shall slaughter as I commanded you"—teaching that Moses was commanded**, etc. Rambam in Yad, beginning of Hilchot Shechitah, counts shechitah as a positive mitzvah; and likewise Tosafot Beitzah 25 and Chullin 37—that "and you shall slaughter" is a positive commandment. Raavad in his hasagot wrote it has no reason; Maggid Mishneh explained Raavad's reason: it is not an obligatory mitzvah—for if he wishes not to eat meat or not to slaughter himself he is permitted; rather it is a lav haba michlal aseh that you eat slaughtered, not a limb from a live animal and nevelah. According to Rambam, Maggid Mishneh answered: nevertheless it is a positive commandment for one who wishes to eat meat—that he slaughter, whether himself or another slaughter for him. It is wondrous that Maggid Mishneh contradicts his own words, for he wrote in chapter 1 of Hilchot Berachot that netilat yadayim and shechitah are optional matters, for it is possible for him not to eat. It appears to me that the reason of Rambam and Tosafot who counted shechitah as a positive mitzvah is the reason Rosh wrote at the beginning of Ketubot: therefore one blesses on slaughter and "He commanded us" even though he is not obligated to slaughter and eat—for they expounded the verse in language of command, as it is written "and you shall slaughter and eat"; and likewise the precision of Rambam's language there, law 4: "on all these matters we were commanded in the Torah, and He said 'and you shall slaughter as I commanded you'"—implies that because He said it in language of command it is a positive mitzvah; and likewise according to Tosafot there. See Peri Megadim in the introduction: a positive commandment that is not slaughtered applies in any amount; this does not appear so in Tosafot Chullin 33.
