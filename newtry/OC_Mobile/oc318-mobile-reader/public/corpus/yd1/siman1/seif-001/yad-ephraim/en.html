**Even** women. See Eshel Avraham in the name of Semak. See She'elot Ya'avetz siman 157. A woman who learned laws of shechitah is kosher to slaughter even for herself; and likewise if he examined the knife and gave it to her and stood al gavav she may slaughter; but they do not appoint her as slaughterer for the public at all—see there.
**Unless he took kabbalah.** See Ba'er Heitev; see Chavat Ya'ir siman 178—in a village where the slaughterer fell ill near Yom Tov and one person learned knows laws of shechitah and examination, only he never slaughtered or examined—he may not slaughter except for the joy of Yom Tov: permit to slaughter birds with the sick slaughterer, even though the joy of the festival does not come out in birds—nevertheless the custom of important people who sustain themselves with birds. (In my humble opinion it appears this is not so except for complete joy; but joy of Yom Tov certainly exists, as in Beitzah regarding stocking dovecotes—they come to diminish joy of Yom Tov; see Tosafot Ketubot 5 who wrote that in four chapters they were accustomed to increase in animals and not in birds—see there.) Likewise permitted to slaughter an animal, only if some safek arises that requires removal, and the sick one cannot attend to the whole treifah; see Lechem HaPanim who copied this. What he wrote regarding kaparah—see Chavat Ya'ir who wrote he should accept some fast for kaparah and some sum for charity for publicity that it was not permitted except in an hour of pressure. See there Chavat Ya'ir brought in Lechem HaPanim: regarding one who must give meat to idolaters—one may perform nehirah through an idolater, for there is no concern of mistake here, unlike if he slaughtered without kabbalah—see there.
**Until he knows him.** Ba'er Heitev; see responsa Shevut Yaakov who challenged Lechem HaPanim: since an idolater is believed regarding issurim even a relative—from Ketubot 22—for the father is trusted to feed him terumah because it is in his power to feed him. Rambam: when it is known he is his father, he is trusted. See Even HaEzer siman 3; nevertheless he wrote regarding slaughter: since "most found at slaughter," etc.—only later enactments to take kabbalah; also a matter of tel appearance—whether he is expert or not he does not lie; somewhat a proof from Mishnah Sanhedrin 5; nevertheless he should take kabbalah from another since he is not his relative—see there. Ba'er Heitev in the name of Chavat Ya'ir—not this law but the law of permitting a bechor—see there. Peninei Me'orot part 2 wrote: if he is before us and they examined that he knows laws of shechitah—it is obvious he is trusted, also established in presumption; this is only revelation of the matter. Greater than this—it appears even if they examined him and he does not know, kabbalah helps to declare kosher, like one who took kabbalah from another and we say "now he became defective"; where he is not before us and we know he had kabbalah from his father—it is obvious to rely on "most found," etc. Already permission went out from gedolei hador as Lechem HaPanim—see there. See responsa at the end of Sefer Beli—Shevut Yaakov brought and wrote it is not a matter of tel appearance: all who take kabbalah are not examined afterward. He also answered his proof from Sanhedrin; therefore he concludes: when found that he has no sense of feeling—kabbalah from his father does not help, for certainly he did not examine well; must kasher utensils; in any case delay from Torah; but if Beit Din give him kabbalah, the father may be one of them—see there. From Peri Megadim that I brought it is clear kabbalah helps not to declare treif retroactively. It appears to decide: if the slaughterer is expert for the public in feeling and well established in kashrut—kabbalah helps greatly; unlike unspecified—be stringent; all according to the eyes of the instructor.
**Or** stolen. See Ba'er Heitev; Rambam wrote in responsa Avodat HaKodesh (called Pe'er HaDor) siman 112: appointed for the public for slaughter and examination, established for the public that he steals meat and was found with it publicly—even if he repented, you have no greater desecration of God's name than this; therefore he should not slaughter for the public. If he wishes to slaughter in a private house—the permission is in his hand—see there (see Noda BiYehudah part 14 siman 37) and he brought from Peninei Me'orot.
**And there are those who are stringent**, etc., and he should know to be careful. Ba'er Heitev; Noda BiYehudah Mahadura Tinyana siman 1: regarding a slaughterer whose hands tremble and he senses a fine defect, and also says "it is clear to me I did not fail in anything in his craft through delay or pressing," and his trembling does not harm him—one may dismiss him that he not slaughter; but what he slaughtered retroactively do not forbid; and utensils that are in his slaughter are permitted to use (see there what he wrote on Lechem HaPanim who brings in Semag in the name of Mahara Stein and Mordekhai Zaken—or whose hands tremble, etc., and poured on him great wrath—and it is wondrous, for the matter is simple that Lechem HaPanim does not mean Semag wrote so in the name of Mahara Stein, only that in the margin of Semag he found written in the name of Mahara Stein and Mordekhai Zaken—meaning in old Mordekhai, one whose hands, etc.; the Gaon z"l understood the word zaken refers to what follows, therefore wrote the witness lied in the name of Mahara Stein—for behold from his words zaken at eighty years is only a general stringency, and these words were not given to be written and are like an error escaping before the ruler—for it is simple the word zaken refers to the name and means old Mordekhai as this language is mentioned many times in poskim, and need not expand; per this it is possible the Gaon z"l accepted his testimony here that hands tremble to forbid b'dieved because of pressing even when saying "it is clear to me," since found written so in the name of Mahara Stein and old Mordekhai; and to be stringent at least where there is no great loss, and to kasher utensils—for hagalah helps them; and utensils not subject to hagalah—and Peri Megadim likewise—do not permit except after waiting from Torah; and think). See Me'ir Netivim siman 76 who also brought responsa Beit Yaakov siman 105 and wondered on him that words of Maharik of Beit Yosef are mentioned in Mordekhai in the name of Eldad HaDani, and wrote that in truth they are wondrous—see there how many stringencies and we do not practice so; but regarding this old man it is fit to enact that at least from seventy years onward no person should slaughter even if he still senses and is in his strength, because of lo pelug—for since majority of majority in those years already the power of sensing was nullified. Also until seventy years teachers of rulings need to supervise slaughterers coming in their days to test them periodically whether they stand on their presumption in sensing, for it is close to spoil—and I testify I saw slaughterers who thought they sensed and their hands were trembling; I showed them eye to eye and they swore they did not sense at all themselves; they held me in esteem and ceased slaughtering; fit to fence this breach—see there.
**Everything according to whether he is a person.** Ba'er Heitev; wrote in Degel Machaneh Efrayim: even for Acharonim—only when not properly at all forbidden from Torah; but if according to words of contradictor it was not proper from stringency, e.g. less than measure or delay in minority—the slaughterer is believed; also room to distinguish one who says "I slaughtered improperly"—whether they contradict him in what he says he slaughtered at such hour and witness says he did not slaughter this animal for it did not move, etc.—slaughterer is believed, for regarding body of slaughter he is believed like two—see there.
