**Giving him kabbalah.** See in Shmila Chadasha 324, who wrote that in these countries they do not slaughter without kabbalah; if one transgressed and slaughtered without kabbalah, even though he is expert and established—nevertheless his shechitah is forbidden, etc.—see there; and see in responsum Brit Avraham part 4 siman 15 who disagrees and raised that one need not forbid b'dieved—see there. And see in responsum Chavat Yair siman 178: in Kfar Sderim there were some baalei batim who always kept a slaughterer and examiner with them; near Pesach he fell ill, and one of the baalei batim is expert and knows hilchos shechitah, only he never slaughtered, examined, or took kabbalah—whether permitted to slaughter; he answered that by law it is permitted to slaughter since it is a time of pressure and honor of the festival; nevertheless in my heart I hesitate to permit in these generations when the stumbling block has grown greatly, etc.; however he is permitted to slaughter birds that do not require lung examination with the sick slaughterer-examiner—for in such a case there are those who permit l'chatchila, and for honor of the festival certainly permitted; and even if they have meat only salted more than two days ago—for there is no simchat hag in it—permitted to slaughter; and although we hold one does not fulfill simchat hag with bird meat, nevertheless it is preferable to old salted meat. The same applies that it is permitted to slaughter an animal—only that if any change or any doubt occurs in lung examination and the sick one cannot deal with and press on this, it becomes invalid—for certainly in bedikah one needs skilled hand, habit, and sensitivity, and one should be more stringent in it than in shechitah—see there further. And see in responsum Shevut Yaakov part 1 siman 23 that a slaughterer who never slaughtered should not slaughter l'chatchila on Yom Tov—see there.
