(1) [Seif 1] **All may slaughter l'chatchila.** For we taught: Rabbi says "and you shall slaughter as I commanded you"—teaching Moses was commanded regarding the esophagus and windpipe, and majority of one in a bird and majority of two in an animal (Chullin 28a). Rashi explained "as I commanded you"—learn the mitzvah of shechitah was explained to him orally, for where was he commanded in writing—end quote. Tosafot there wrote: Rabbi expounded the spelling of "as" (ke'asher)—alef one in bird, shin two in animal, reish majority of one like him—majority of one in bird and majority of two in animal. Rabbeinu Tam: retroactively of "as" (ka'asher)—majority of one like him—end quote; and likewise Ran. Tur brought this baraita; Bach wrote to teach that bird shechitah too is min haTorah, and every safek in shechitah is invalid even in bird—not like R' Eliezer ben Yaakov who said bird has no shechitah min haTorah, only midivrei sofrim; per his view one could be lenient where there is safek in bird shechitah, for the halachah is not so, rather like this baraita Rabbi taught—end quote. Likewise Rambam chapter 1 of Hilchot Shechitah law 1 that bird shechitah is min haTorah—see there. Likewise Semag beginning of Hilchot Shechitah; Sefer HaChinuch parashat Re'eh mitzvah 451; Rashba in Torat HaBayit HaKatzar at its beginning; Beit Yosef siman 13; Levush beginning of this siman and beginning of siman 13. Kaf HaChayim part 1 or 1; Minchat Yosef siman 1 or 1; Chochmat Adam kelal 1 or 1; Zivchei Shem or 1, Zivchei Tzedek or 1; Aruch HaShulchan or 1. Likewise Peri Megadim in introduction to Hilchot Shechitah root 1 that bird shechitah is min haTorah, and we follow its safek to be stringent—see there. Likewise the custom: do not distinguish in laws of shechitah between animal and bird, and follow their safek to be stringent. See further below siman 13 or 1.
(2) **And if** this mitzvah of shechitah must be fulfilled like the other 613 mitzvot, or optional—only one who comes to eat meat must slaughter. See Peri Megadim in introduction to Hilchot Shechitah what he wrote in explanation of Rishonim's dispute on this; he wrote there is a practical difference regarding half-measure and issur chal al issur; he sides on this in several aspects and does not decide the law—see there. Beit David of Rabbeinu Tam or 1—see there. From words of poskim we wrote in the previous or 1—it implies there is a positive mitzvah; therefore be stringent; and if he does not know how to slaughter and wishes to fulfill the mitzvah, give an animal or bird to a slaughterer and say to him: you be my agent to slaughter this, and it is as if I myself fulfilled the mitzvah of shechitah—to repair the root of this mitzvah in the supernal place. "And let the pleasantness," etc.—for the agent of a person is as himself.
(3) **There.** "All may slaughter l'chatchila." And you may ask: how did the Holy One blessed be He permit us to cause pain to living creatures to slaughter and eat them—behold it is written "and His mercies are on all His works"? Behold this question was investigated by Ramban, and he answered: this animal that is slaughtered for its benefit was slaughtered—for it ascended from the rank of animal body to the rank of human body. Likewise in Yalkut Reuveni parashat Shemini, siman 138 in chapter "This is the law of animal and bird" in the name of Sha'arei Orah 59a; Beit David Yafeh Lalev part 3 or 1—see there.
(4) **Good** to be careful not to say about any animal, beast, or bird before you "slaughter for me this animal or this bird"—rather as they say somewhat: "place the knife on its neck for me." Pele Yoetz part 2 or 200 s.v. rachmanut; Yafeh Lalev there or 2.
(5) **There. All may slaughter l'chatchila.** A man of Israel who comes to begin engaging in slaughter—what is his law? First he should learn all laws of shechitah until he is expert in its halachot. Semak or 2.
(6) **There. All may slaughter l'chatchila.** Meaning: even alone and others do not see. Kereti or 2; Aruch HaShulchan or 3—and this is after he became expert and proficient in laws of shechitah.
(7) **There. Even women.** Likewise Tosafot at the beginning of Chullin—that women slaughter even l'chatchila, not like laws of Eretz Yisrael—see there. Likewise Tur and Beit Yosef; Taz s.k. 1. Likewise Rambam chapter 4 of Hilchot Shechitah law 4: even women and slaves, if they are experts—these slaughter l'chatchila. Likewise Rashba in Torat HaBayit HaAroch 7b; and in Torat HaBayit HaKatzar house 1 gate 1; likewise Rosh, Ran, Mordekhai, Hagahot Ashri in the name of R' Yitzchak, and Mahariach at the beginning of Chullin. Likewise Semag positive commandments chapter 3 end of Hilchot Shechitah. Likewise R' Yeruham netiv 15 or 5 part 1. Likewise Sefer HaGan. Beit David Hekdesh HaGan in Hagahot Beit Yosef letter 2—see there. Found: per the law, women are permitted to slaughter l'chatchila; only from custom some are stringent, as written below or 11—see there.
(8) **There, gloss. Some say women should not be left to slaughter**, because their minds are light lest they faint. Beit Yosef in the name of laws of Eretz Yisrael. Likewise Levush—the reason is because most of them have soft hearts and faint from seeing blood.
(9) **There, gloss. Some say women should not be left to slaughter.** And likewise slaves—they do not leave to slaughter. Beit Yosef Levanon or 2. But it does not appear so per the poskim, as written below or 12—see there. Likewise in responsa Makom Shmuel at its end, in novelties of laws for Yoreh De'ah, he challenged the words of Beit Yosef Levanon—see there.
(10) **There, in the gloss. And likewise the custom**, etc. Likewise Agur; Beit Yosef challenged him that "we have not seen" is not proof. In my humble opinion it appears Agur's view is as Maharik wrote in root 172: regarding custom and the like, "we have not seen" is proof—as the rabbi wrote in Choshen Mishpat siman 37. Shach s.k. 1. And in Choshen Mishpat siman 37 Shach expanded there to resolve his words—for here they challenged him—and see what he wrote there in Urim VeTumim that the correct view is as Beit Yosef wrote—see there. Kereti or 4; likewise in responsa Makom Shmuel there that the correct view is as Beit Yosef wrote—see there. And so is the practice: when poskim write "the explanation is: we have not heard and we have not seen"—this is regarding their country and neighboring countries they heard from, but not the whole world; however sometimes possible regarding the whole world, per the appearance from that posek's words.
(11) **There, in the gloss. And likewise the custom that women do not slaughter.** From the essence of the law, women are like men regarding shechitah; but already practiced in these countries that women do not slaughter—whether for their own need or for others' need—even if expert and established; and no sage should give them kabbalah at all. Semak or 13.
(12) **There, in the gloss. And likewise the custom that women do not slaughter.** So agreed most Acharonim; likewise from there Sefer HaKaneh. Peri Chadash testified he saw women slaughtering; likewise in Sefer Gur Aryeh in his gloss, who wrote: in these Galilee regions I saw women slaughtering with permission, and no one protests them—end quote; river by river and it spread. Birkei Yosef or 4; and the custom here in the Holy Land Jerusalem is that women do not slaughter; and such is the custom in all cities of Eretz Yisrael, Syria, Bavel, India, Media, and Persia.
(13) **And if** it happened she slaughtered between herself and herself in a place where they slaughter without kabbalah—she needs examination whether she is expert and says "it is clear to me I did not faint"—then her slaughter is kosher. But if she is not before us, her slaughter is invalid, since there is a defect before us that she deviated from the custom—we do not rely on "most found at slaughter." Semak or 13; Zivchei Tzedek or 2.
