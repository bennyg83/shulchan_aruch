Who are fit to slaughter. In it are 14 seifim. Everyone may slaughter l'chatchila—even women. {Rama: Some say one must not allow women to slaughter; the practice has already been established not to have women slaughter, and so the custom is that women do not slaughter (Beit Yosef in the name of the Agur).} And slaves and any person—even if we do not recognize him—as long as he has a presumption that he slaughters without fainting, and also we do not know about him that he is expert and knows the laws of shechitah—it is permitted l'chatchila to give him to slaughter, and it is permitted to eat from his shechitah, for most people who regularly slaughter are presumed experts and established in that regard. This applies when he is not before us: then it is permitted to eat from his shechitah and we rely on the presumption. But if he is before us, one must test him whether he is expert and knows the laws of shechitah—but one need not ask him whether he ever fainted (from fear). {Rama: Some say one does not rely on presumption except b'dieved, but l'chatchila one does not rely on presumption where one can ascertain (Mordechai; Hagahos Ashrei; Agur in the name of Or Zarua; Sheiltos on parashas Behaalosecha). All this speaks of others who are not expert and cannot tell whether this slaughterer is expert or not. However the slaughterer himself may not slaughter although he knows the laws of shechitah and is expert until he has slaughtered three times before a sage expert in the laws of shechitah who knows that he is accustomed and alert so that he will not faint (Tur in the name of the Rambam). Therefore the custom is that a person does not slaughter unless he first received kabbalah before a sage (Agur in the name of Hilchos Eretz Yisrael). The sage does not give him kabbalah until he knows about him that he knows the laws of shechitah and is expert by hand. Therefore the custom is that all who come to slaughter are relied upon l'chatchila and we do not check them, not at the beginning nor at the end—for everyone commonly found among shechitah has already received kabbalah before a sage (it is evident). In some places they are additionally stringent that the one receiving kabbalah takes a letter from the sage as evidence that he gave kabbalah. Every slaughterer, even after receiving kabbalah, should see to review the laws of shechitah from time to time so that they are fluent in his mouth and in his heart that he not forget them (Mahariv Yaakov haLevi in the name of Maharam) (°). And as is the law in the laws of shechitah and in one who comes to slaughter, so is the law in the laws of inspecting the lung and in one who comes to inspect: their law and custom are the same in all this (it is evident). The rabbinical court should investigate and make inquiry after inspectors and slaughterers and see that they be proficient, expert, and fit (Mahariv siman 50), for the issur of causing others to stumble in shechitot and inspections delegated to the public is severe. If they inspected some slaughterer or inspector and found that he does not know: if he once received kabbalah, we do not retroactively declare treifah what he slaughtered—for we say he is now incompetent (Chidushei Aguda). But if he never received kabbalah at all, everything he slaughtered is treifah, and also all utensils in which they cooked what he slaughtered need kashering (Rashba siman 218).}
