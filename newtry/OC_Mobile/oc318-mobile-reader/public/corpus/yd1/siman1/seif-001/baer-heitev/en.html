**Women.** The Rishonim [also permit slaves] in Orach Chayyim. Peri Chadash in siman 101 of Chullin disagrees.
**And slaves.** Shach wrote: Tur wrote “freed slaves” (see Peri Chadash who disagrees); it appears his view is that ordinary slaves have all evil traits.
**To slaughter.** Meaning: on the basis they will examine him after shechitah whether he is expert; we are not concerned lest he become drunk and eat without examination—for for the benefit of the matter we ask him, as Rashba wrote in Terumat HaDeshen 6a.
**And established.** Shach wrote: see Choshen Mishpat siman 306 seif 7 regarding an expert butcher who erred—must bring proof he is expert; we do not say in such a case “most found at shechitah are experts.”
**Before a sage.** Taz wrote: specifically we require both sage and expert; not enough to take kabbalah from an expert in shechitah alone—only from a sage, for he knows how to test him well; those who take kabbalah from slaughterers who are not sages in any case do not act properly.
**Kabbalah.** Shach wrote: custom is to begin slaughtering with three chickens and within the three there should be a rooster; forbidden to take wages for this. Beit Yosef brought proof that before shechitah wages are permitted; but the chickens are forbidden to the rabbi since it is after the act—give to the poor. Even one proficient at chickens may not slaughter sparrows and small pigeons except one who already slaughtered them and received authorization; what they do not give at first for authorization is because they are uncommon—therefore do not slaughter sparrows alone except with a chicken, due to concern for berachah in vain—all from Maharshal. Nevertheless I saw slaughterers practice slaughtering two sparrows or small pigeons together, for according to all views we are not concerned both become invalid.
**Until he knows about him.** A father may rely on his son in shechitah since a single witness is believed for issurim—Chayei Adam siman 40; Chavat Yair siman 121.
**Periodically.** Meaning: every thirty days—Beit Yosef; see Be'er HaGolah what he brought in the name of Maharil.
**To all.** Sefer Yirah warns not to give kabbalah to anyone light-minded, only God-fearing; especially habitual heavy drinkers—even if not drunk, for they do not sense if drunk or not and their hands are heavy from much drinking; also warn them about knife inspection with awe and fear.
**That it became invalid.** Taz disagrees and brings proof that even one who took kabbalah may be forbidden retroactively; concludes to be stringent—forbid meat still extant from his shechitah from eating, and known utensils cooked in from his shechitah. Nekudat HaKesef disagrees. But if through negligence he issued treifah from his hand we do not forbid retroactively—see Shach and Peri Chadash. Bedek HaBayit siman 119: if he slaughtered or nikkur cheilev and afterward apostatized—even that day all is kosher. A butcher who sold meat not rinsed and not salted more than three days after shechitah and did not tell buyers not to eat except roasted—by law need not remove him unless for a fence—Avodat HaGershuni siman 15.
**Kosher.** Shach wrote: not comparable to an animal found treifah where siman 81 says cheeses from it are kosher if we can say “now it became treifah”—there from birth it was kosher and now became treifah; here from birth he knew nothing. More answers there. Also: one who was inspecting and afterward treifah issued from his hand through negligence—not from lack of knowledge—utensils are kosher by law; we uphold his presumption that until now he was kosher. Even one who apostatized is not suspected retroactively—if he slaughtered and apostatized that day it is kosher (however if they tested him and he is not expert, certainly forbid all his shechitah retroactively).
