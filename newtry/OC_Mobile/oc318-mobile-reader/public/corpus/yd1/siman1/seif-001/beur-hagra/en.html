**Some say there is no such thing.** See Tosafot Kiddushin 76b s.v. ein etc., and it may be said that l'chatchila we do not leave them even though they are kosher. The same applies in Chullin because of negligence.
**(Likkut) Even if there is no such thing, and also etc.** For the halachah is not like Ravina even though he is later, because the conclusion of the Gemara there 12a is “most found at shechitah are experts”—the Rif. Also in the final version the halachah is not from that sugya there, for “they were lost,” etc.—therefore we are not concerned for both of them—Rosh (end quote).
**Permitted etc.** Meaning: permitted l'chatchila on the basis they will examine him afterward—for Ravina forbade even in such a case, and he holds we do not say “most found at shechitah are experts,” and even if he is before us it is forbidden, as written there “not before us” that he should examine him, and we are concerned lest he become drunk, etc., as Tosafot wrote there regarding a Kuti and apostate 3:2 s.v. bodek; and “and he said to him,” etc. But for us it is permitted in such a case.
**But if etc.** As written in the first chapter of Pesachim 4a—his presumption is examined or not; remove doubt, ask him, etc.—therefore where he is before us we do not rely; the reason is because it is a minority commonly found greatly, as with lung treifot—and see below siman 39 seifim 1 and 2. And this is what the Gemara says “most found at shechitah,” etc.—for the short ones we are not concerned; and he also did not say for those not expert we are not concerned—therefore they are not comparable, for there even if he is before us we are not concerned, and this is what it says here “but one need not,” etc.
**(Likkut) In another matter etc.** And not like Ra'ah who wrote that it is not specifically “most found” but “all found”—because he has difficulty with the question of the Rif and Rosh, who challenged from Bereishit Rabbah that we say an animal while alive stands in presumption, etc.; and though the Rif and Rosh answered that since “most found” it is as one who became known how it was slaughtered—it does not appease him, because we say in the last chapter of Yevamot “bring the minority,” etc., and lean on presumption, etc. And it is not so—for only regarding R' Meir do we say this; see Kiddushin chapter 1, and see Tosafot there and there; and proof from what we say in Chullin 11—from where do we learn we follow the majority, etc., even though an animal while alive, etc.; and there “if so, for R' Meir,” etc.—for R' Meir specifically. (end quote)
**(Likkut) In another matter etc.** Since it is a minority commonly found; but Rosh wrote in s.v. 17 in the name of Rabbeinu Tam (and likewise Tosafot Bekhorot 20b) that therefore we do not say we rely on the minority against presumption, because it is a minority not commonly found; and even R' Meir agrees we are not concerned for the minority, and he holds that sugya in Yevamot is even for Rabbanan (and see below siman 396 s.k. 5); and Tosafot go according to their view that they do not hold Rabbeinu Tam's view. (end quote)
**And some say etc.** Their view: if he slaughtered b'dieved, even if he is before us he need not be examined; but l'chatchila to give is forbidden in every case. And even though the sugya there does not imply so, as Tosafot wrote there s.v. rov metsuyin, etc. They ruled like the sugya there 12a “he asked him,” etc., that the baraita “behold,” etc., implies there specifically b'dieved; and likewise it implies there that even if he is before us—as they said there “unless he does not know,” etc. They did not rule not like Ravina because the other Amoraim do not hold like him—for he is later—but because of that Gemara sugya that is not like him.
**(Likkut) And some say there is no such thing.** And the question of Tosafot s.v. rov, etc., and Rosh—it appears there is no contradiction at all, for it may be said: why did Abaye and Rava need to say regarding a Kuti a remedy b'dieved via an examiner, and likewise R' Elazar regarding an apostate, and likewise for all the resolutions there; and why did Ravina need to say “all expert slaughterers slaughter”—why mention experts, for it is unnecessary except in the siyafa; and likewise in all the resolutions—only because it implies “all slaughter and their slaughter is kosher” deals with one matter, and this is what refutes “all l'chatchila,” etc.; otherwise it could be reisha Kuti and siyafa apostate, or one of those; see Rashi there s.v. ushechitatan kesheirah b'dieved, etc.; and if the siyafa, etc.; therefore Ravina had to say b'dieved specifically regarding examiners, for it is one matter—we expand experts even though not established, specifically when they examined at the beginning or end; and “all experts” sometimes l'chatchila if they examined at the beginning, and b'dieved if they examined at the end; and likewise in all the resolutions, e.g. Kuti l'chatchila and b'dieved that it be clear he slaughtered properly, or via others watching, or via an examiner; and likewise apostate; and likewise expert that they know he is expert; and likewise established—unlike the conclusion “most found,” etc. (end quote)
**But the slaughterer himself etc.** So too Rambam; and as written there—one who knows laws of shechitah and slaughtered before a sage until he became regular is called expert; and we say “most found at shechitah are experts”—therefore we require experts only that most etc.
**Therefore the custom that all etc.** For the reason that “before us” requires examining him is because the minority commonly found is as explained—not so in such a case where it is a minority not commonly found—Darchei Moshe.
**And in some places etc.** So that he need not examine each one individually, as he has.
**And every slaughterer etc.** As written below—even b'dieved it is forbidden.
**And if they examined him etc.** Agudah learned from what is written there 10a “the knife became defective,” etc.—likewise a person became defective, an animal did not become defective, and we say “once slaughtered it is permitted.” Rashba siman 218 wrote: in such a case do not say “most found,” etc., since he was examined and does not know; and likewise do not establish on his presumption except one established from the beginning—like a knife they examined at the beginning and slaughtered with it and afterward it was found defective; and likewise a mikveh that was measured and they still argued; and Darchei Moshe divided as written here “if,” etc., “but if,” etc., as Rashba wrote, and likewise do not establish, etc. Tzarich iyun—for this version “knife,” etc., is Rav Hisda, and we hold like Rav Huna; and Rashba who wrote “likewise do not establish,” etc., wrote for benefit of the matter also R' Chanina as written, and nevertheless they argued. Also what Agudah wrote “once slaughtered permitted”—on the contrary, an animal while alive, etc.; and likewise Rashba in siman 219 that all is forbidden retroactively, even utensils cooked in them—for an animal while alive stands in presumption, etc., and like Rav Huna, and even if he examined his knife at the beginning and it was found defective, etc.—see there; and Taz who expanded further to challenge from the words of Tosafot there s.v. sakin “wonder,” etc., and here the three answers there do not apply—and likewise Peri Chadash, and he challenged Shach.
