**Since he gave an excuse for his words—he is believed.** See Ba'er Heitev; wrote in words of Shmuel siman 122: slaughterer and examiner fixed for him wages equal from kosher and treifah; found he profits on the animal to declare treif—for he profits in its sale; slaughtered animal and declared kosher then retracted and gave excuse for treif—do not lighten and say therefore he declared treif to profit somewhat; rather rely on his last words to declare treif since he gave excuse—see below siman 18 in Ba'er Heitev s.k. 25 who copied this briefly. Shevut Yaakov part 1: slaughterer who slaughtered animal, examined, said it is kosher, and he himself took meat; after a day said it was treifah that he delayed in slaughter—if he is not believed in his heart it is forbidden as siman 127; likewise if he gives excuse, e.g. says he slaughtered another kosher and confused it with this and now recognized it is not this—such cases; but if he says excuse that he lied initially for some reason like that sugya in Ketubot—possible it does not help here except "he made himself one," but not for others, since he acted in the body and took meat like wearing garment of niddah—and this is worse than act of sign on head of lamb; and even if one says stringently excuse helps such—this is that for trustworthiness of witness leniency is preferable to stringency, for we hold one witness believed to permit and not to forbid—if so regarding excuse likewise—see there (somewhat think: since he says he delayed, the excuse strengthens presumption of issur; there one witness believed to permit is when not in presumption of issur. Possible he holds also here: since already went out kosher and permitted, stands in presumption of heter and again not believed with excuse—see responsa siman 1 at length on this law).
