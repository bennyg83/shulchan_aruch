**Since he gave a justification for his words, he is believed.** Beit Yosef brings thus in the name of Rashbatz; proof from Ketubot chapter 2—a woman who told her husband “I am tamei” is believed afterward to say “I am tahor” when she gives justification for her words, as below siman 187 (♯). Question: Ramban wrote there if she was established a niddah in her affliction—i.e., she wore garments designated for her niddah—justification does not help; Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Rashba the reason: it is called ones, and she says—but to do such an act as wearing niddah garments she would not wear—end of his words; we see he distinguishes only speech from act: whatever is not speech but act, justification does not help. Why is this slaughterer’s act—mark on the ram’s head—different between act and act? Also wearing niddah garments is an act—for wearing her special apron suffices. One who examines Maharik responsum 87 sees wearing niddah garments is not a complete sign—for perhaps her tahor garments were unavailable—see there: justification still does not help. On Rashbatz himself—a question: perhaps he holds like Tur siman 187 that even established niddah justification helps, but we rule like Ramban above—if so we do not rule like Rashbatz. After writing this I found Darchei Moshe siman 127 on Rashbatz: “one must examine siman 185 on niddah who wore niddah garments—justification does not help; perhaps one can distinguish—uncertain”—therefore it appears we should not be lenient in Shulchan Aruch here, since Rama left it uncertain.
