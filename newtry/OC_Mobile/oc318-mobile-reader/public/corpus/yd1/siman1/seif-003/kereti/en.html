Since he knows laws of shechitah—and if it were pressed, etc.—he would not say it is kosher, for he knows the laws; Shach wrote likewise regarding a mute who eats—presumably kosher, for otherwise he would protest; likewise Peri Chadash wrote.
From the beginning of slaughter to the end—even if he saw that he slaughtered one siman properly, we do not say so—for from one slaughtered properly we learn the other was also slaughtered properly; it teaches he did not finish; and if he saw a majority of years in an animal and in a bird a majority of one—even though we are stringent regarding a minority of two, nevertheless for this matter it helps and they declare kosher—for the majority is like the whole.
And there are those who are stringent—this is the view of our rabbi R' Baruch, and Peri Chadash doubted him; see Peleti, where I clarified R' Baruch's words precisely.
And thus is the practice—but if they do not know whether he is an expert, it is obvious he may slaughter l'chatchila with others seeing him—Shach and Peri Chadash.
