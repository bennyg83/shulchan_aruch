**One who knows**, etc. R' Baruch proved from that which we say to Ravina: if they do not know whether he is expert or not—they examine him after slaughter; the Gemara challenges: but it teaches "and all who slaughtered and others see him—it is kosher"—if it refers to one who forgot hilchos shechitah, and if he is expert examining suffices; and it answers they are not before us. And it is asked why it teaches "who slaughtered"—an act; rather certainly l'chatchila in any case forbidden. This view that declares kosher—one may say certainly if it taught "and all" it would refer only to one who forgot hilchos shechitah and it applies well, and it should have taught "and if they slaughtered"—but if it also refers to non-expert, "and all" may apply even to one who forgot hilchos shechitah—for "and all" is taught. And if so one may say an act because of one who forgot hilchos shechitah it teaches, as we find in chapter Kol HaPesulim that teaches "who slaughtered" b'dieved because of tamei—even though the others listed are kosher l'chatchila—and think.
**There are those who are stringent**—this is the view of R' Baruch mentioned above. Shach wrote: but from anonymity that it is not clear he did not learn, permitted to give to slaughter l'chatchila with one al gavav—and I did not know what pilpul is needed here—for we hold "most found at slaughter," etc., and they are permitted to slaughter; and if to examine afterward—behold others see him, and this is straightforward. Peri Chadash argued from the weak text: do not hand over to slaughter chullin l'chatchila; Rashi explained with one al gavav lest they ruin, for their actions are ruined—learn with another permitted to give initially to slaughter with one al gavav. I did not know who suffices for Peri Chadash—if R' Baruch holds like Rabbeinu Tam that one does not hand them over even to feed dogs, as written below seif 5; if so there is no proof except with another permitted to hand initially to slaughter to feed dogs, but not for a person. One must understand in Tosafot on the Mishnah s.v. shema yekalkelu: they challenged Rashi's weak text "do not hand them over," etc.—this is l'chatchila to slaughter, and others al gavav are unnecessary—for from the siyafa we learn "and all who slaughtered" b'dieved not l'chatchila, etc.; Tosafot wrote unnecessary that it teaches an act because of tamei in kodshim or non-experts, but one who forgot hilchos shechitah—even l'chatchila they slaughter when others see him—for Rava who is precise holds "and all" refers only to one who forgot hilchos shechitah; and they wrote there is a variant Rabbah later. One must understand how could it enter one's mind that non-experts are forbidden to slaughter l'chatchila when others see, and one who forgot hilchos shechitah would be permitted l'chatchila—and is non-expert worse than one who forgot hilchos shechitah; and think. What one may say: Tosafot hold regarding one who forgot hilchos shechitah that everyone knows they are prone to ruin—even the observer standing there supervises more lest they ruin the slaughter, therefore even l'chatchila kosher; but one not established in presumption who slaughters—the observer thinks: since he slaughters he is expert in laws of slaughter and does not supervise so well, therefore forbidden. If so Peri Chadash's question is resolved—even with another forbidden l'chatchila; nevertheless it teaches regarding one who forgot hilchos shechitah—for one may say according to Tosafot's view here he is worse. However according to this view R' Baruch has no proof from the Gemara for his law—for the Gemara deals when they are not before us and we do not know if expert or not—if so one should fear the observer will not supervise so well as written, he will think he is expert, therefore l'chatchila forbidden; but according to what we hold "most found," etc., and it deals when it is clear to us he did not know—one may say here the Gemara is precise that even l'chatchila permitted. Only one should not lighten with reasoning without basis; and in particular per Rabbeinu Tam in any case this is unnecessary. And from Tosafot 3b s.v. delaisa, etc., they challenged: behold they are before us and he does not know—his slaughter is not kosher even when others see him. And later we say regarding one known not to have learned, and others see—kosher; there is apparently proof against the view I wrote—for later they know he did not learn, unlike here. Only one may say Tosafot hold certainly l'chatchila without handing for slaughter, and certainly there is room to distinguish—but that because of this reasoning disqualify b'dieved—this Tosafot hold out of strain. Rather the issue itself is difficult: why does Tosafot need proof from later Ravina's statement and law of one who forgot hilchos shechitah—for certainly they did not learn and nevertheless b'dieved kosher; therefore one must say Tosafot hold the entire reason forbidden l'chatchila or b'dieved is not because the observer will not supervise, only lest he err and they think his slaughter is good and eat from his slaughter and there is no observer—and if so regarding one who forgot hilchos shechitah there is room to err, for everyone knows they are not slaughterers by themselves.
