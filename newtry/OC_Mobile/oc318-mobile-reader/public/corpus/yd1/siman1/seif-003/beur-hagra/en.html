**Even if they asked him, and even etc.** Avodat HaGolah s.k. 12; and as written in chapter 3 of Sanhedrin (29b “every matter that,” etc.; and perhaps should read “chapter 3 of Bava Batra” 39a) and chapter 4 and chapter 6 of Shevuot; and proof from what is written in Chullin 3b—to Ravina in the first version “and if he slaughtered they examine him,” and in the final version “and he said ‘it is clear to me,’” etc.; and for us the law is certainly like a doubt for Ravina, and this is what the gloss says “and one whose way is,” etc. **(Likkut) And even if he said ‘it is clear to me,’ etc.** From what is written “all” like Ravina, etc.; and if so, let it say “all slaughter,” even though they do not know, etc.; but they know he may not slaughter—and if he slaughtered and said “it is clear to me,” etc., “and all of them,” etc.; and regarding “not before us” below that they should ask him—Terumat HaDeshen (end quote).
**And some are stringent etc.** From that it challenges there “they examine him” is enough, and it does not challenge “even l'chatchila” as well; and likewise in the answer “not before us,” etc.—nevertheless the aforementioned question remains. And the first reasoning is Terumat HaDeshen and Tur, and they hold that without this we do not hold like that sugya—for there it implies that if he is before us and does not know, others watching him do not help; see Tosafot there s.v. delo lei, etc. And in chapter 3 of Sukkah “knows how to train his hands,” etc., and Rashi explained there—even though he is not expert, and permitted even l'chatchila, as Ran wrote there. And one may ask: why “knows how to train hands”—but we taught “and all who slaughtered,” etc.? Answer: there b'dieved, and here even l'chatchila; and likewise Rosh at the beginning of Chullin that permitted l'chatchila is implied; and even though he wrote “minor expert,” Beit Yosef wrote not that opinion, rather for precision that even though he is expert he needs others al gavav, as written above in seif 5—and not like Tosafot there in Sukkah; and the sugya at the beginning of Chullin they strained for Tosafot there to explain b'dieved specifically—for l'chatchila even an adult is forbidden as explained, and they push aside the sugya as explained; and that which R' Nachman said “one saw,” etc., and challenged “how so,” etc., “obvious,” and did not challenge “even l'chatchila” as well [and likewise Mordechai there and Shulchan Aruch]—because it teaches us that if he did not see at the beginning, even b'dieved it is forbidden.
