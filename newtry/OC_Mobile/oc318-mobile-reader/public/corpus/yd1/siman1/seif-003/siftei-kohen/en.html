**And even if he says, “I am certain,” etc.** Meaning: when they ask him and from his answer it appears he slaughtered properly, it is obvious that counts for nothing; but even if he says, “I am certain I did everything you ask”—nevertheless, since at the time of shechitah he did not know, it is not his knowledge, and he thinks he did what they ask. In the Bach he explained that this means: even after they taught him, he says “I am certain,” etc.—and one need not, however, for his law is true as Ran wrote in the name of Tosafot and Kesef Mishneh chapter 4 in the name of the poskim.
**He is believed, etc.** Since he knows the laws, surely if he had paused or pressed he would not have fed it to us (Beit Yosef). Therefore it is so even if he is silent and says nothing—for since he knows the laws, surely if he had paused or pressed he would not have been silent and would not have fed it to us, and would have said he paused or pressed.
**From the beginning of shechitah until its end.** Meaning: even if he saw that he severed one siman properly, we do not say that since he severed this siman properly he also severed the second siman properly; rather we are concerned that perhaps he paused or pressed on the second siman.
**And some are stringent, etc.** So too it appears in Shas (3b) regarding that which he answers “when he is not before us to examine him,” and so is the Bach’s view. However, from the outset, if it is unknown whether he knows the laws or not, it appears permitted l'chatchila to give him to slaughter when others stand over him—so Maharshal, first chapter of Chullin siman 5. Only that R' Baruch in Hagahos Ashrei does not hold thus; in my view R' Baruch also agrees to this—see there.
**His shechitah is invalid.** For since he does not know the laws, perhaps sometimes he paused or pressed and did not know—meaning he does not feel it, for since he is not proficient in its laws he does not know if it happened to him. The Rosh.
