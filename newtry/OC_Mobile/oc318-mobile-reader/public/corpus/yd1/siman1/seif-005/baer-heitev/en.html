**Speaks.** Bach and Shach wrote: even if sharp and became deaf in the meantime.
**Folly.** Meaning: if he does them in a foolish manner he is called a shoteh—even in one of these; but if he does not act foolishly, even if he does all of them we do not establish him as a shoteh—Shach; see Choshen Mishpat siman 35: even if he acts foolishly, since it is not his usual way he is not called a shoteh.
**To dogs.** Taz wrote: even if expert—the reason in Shach: lest they come to eat from his shechitah, erring to say it is kosher because they give him to slaughter; but for others to throw to dogs all agree it is permitted when he is expert and knows how to train his hands. All the more a grown man who does not know laws of shechitah—permitted to give him to throw to dogs when others stand al gavav—not like Bach who is stringent here.
**From his shechitah.** Shach wrote from Beit Yosef: implies a minor who knows how to train his hands or an expert slaughterer may l'chatchila when others al gavav; where he does not know how to train hands and is not expert—permitted b'dieved when others al gavav; where there are no others al gavav—even expert who knows how to train hands—his shechitah is invalid even b'dieved, for he is not believed. But Maharshal and Derishah rule: even one who knows how to train hands alone or expert with others standing al gavav—not permitted except b'dieved; l'chatchila forbidden until expert, knows how to train hands, and others al gavav; where others do not stand al gavav, or he does not know how to train hands and is not expert—even with others al gavav, even b'dieved forbidden—so Bach ruled.
**Shechitah.** Reason in Shach: no credibility for a minor. Taz wrote: although some poskim say if expert and skilled with his hand we may declare kosher—“slaughtered before you” except concern lest he did not slaughter properly; since he knows hilchos shechitah we say presumably he slaughtered properly—but other poskim do not have this leniency. Bach was stringent on those who send to slaughter via an idolater and give him a sign what the slaughterer should write on it that he slaughtered it—and said this is forbidden since the mark must be on the body of the thing, e.g. on the flesh; I say certainly the stringent act piously, but I do not know prohibition in the matter—for we require a mark on the flesh lest it be switched, and this does not apply here since he recognizes his bird and the idolater has a sign that a kosher person slaughtered—what prohibition is there? Nevertheless concern for those who write as a sign the word “kosher”—lest the idolater drew that word after him as is common; rather he must write some other sign that is not common. Nevertheless the stringent act piously lest there not be good recognition on his bird—see below siman 62 regarding a chicken sent to slaughter via an idolater, etc., there.
**To be careful.** Taz wrote from Maharshal: everything according to the youth’s sharpness and knowledge; so I saw practiced—not to be strict beyond a son of thirteen years, all who see in him he is expert and diligent in training hands and strength of a man—so Shach. Beit Yaakov siman 58 found written: “A mourner and elder of eighty years and a youth before eighteen years do not slaughter”—end quote. Bach wrote: all according to what kind of person—if weak in strength he does not slaughter even from fifty years—see there siman 1.
