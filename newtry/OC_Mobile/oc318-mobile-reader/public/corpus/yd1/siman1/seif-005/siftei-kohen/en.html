**A deaf person who does not hear, etc.** It appears that even a competent person who became deaf in hearing is included—so the Bach wrote.
**And a fool—that is, etc.** Beit Yosef wrote in Choshen Mishpat siman 35 that the intent is not that doing one of these once makes him a fool, but when his way is to do so—see there. And the intent is: even if he once did something foolishly, since his way is not to do so, he is not called a fool.
**Even one of these.** Meaning: only when he does them in a deranged manner is he called a fool—even one of these; but if he does not do them in a deranged manner, even if he did all of them, we do not establish him as a fool, as stated in Shas—therefore he doubled the wording in his language.
**And a minor, etc.** Know that from Beit Yosef’s words it appears: a minor who knows how to steady his hands, or an expert, slaughters l'chatchila when others stand over him; where he does not know how to steady his hands and is not an expert, it is permitted b'dieved when others stand over him; where others do not stand over him, even an expert who knows how to steady his hands—his shechitah is invalid even b'dieved, for he is not trustworthy. On this basis interpret his words: throughout the seif he speaks when he is not an expert—examine this. But Maharshal, first chapter of Chullin siman 5, and Darchei Moshe ruled at length that even one who only knows how to steady his hands, or is an expert with others standing over him, is permitted only b'dieved; l'chatchila it is forbidden until he knows how to steady his hands and is also an expert and others stand over him. Where others do not stand over him, or he does not know how to steady his hands and is not an expert—even with others standing over him—even b'dieved it is forbidden. So too the Bach.
**Even if they wish to feed dogs, etc.** The reason is lest they come to eat from their shechitah, erring that it is kosher because they handed them animals to slaughter; but with others standing over him one can say that all agree with the Tur that it is permitted to hand him animals to throw to dogs when he is an expert or knows how to steady his hands, because then b'dieved his shechitah is kosher—and all the more so a grown person who does not know the laws, that it is permitted to hand him to throw to dogs when others stand over him. Tosafot and Rabbeinu Yerucham agree on all this regarding what Rabbeinu Yerucham forbade with a minor even when others stand over him—lest he err and eat from this shechitah he slaughtered; that speaks when he is not an expert and also does not know how to steady his hands, for then even b'dieved it is forbidden. The Bach understood that Rabbeinu Yerucham argues on Tosafot here and therefore ruled to forbid even for a grown person—and it appears to me as stated. Further proof: Rashba in his novellae and Terumat HaDeshen 8, and Ran end of chapter Lulav HaGazul, and Tur siman 63 ruled that it is permitted to give him (and likewise a minor who knows how to steady his hands) to slaughter l'chatchila even for eating when others stand over him—even though we rule there that it is forbidden; nevertheless for throwing to dogs their law is certainly true—examine this.
**Even though he knows the laws and knows how to steady his hands.** So Or Zarua and Hagahos Agudah wrote; the reason the Raa wrote in Bedek HaBayit and Hagahos Agudah and Maharshal there is that a minor is not trustworthy—not as Levush strained, that since he has not yet reached the Torah obligation of shechitah his shechitah is not shechitah, as it is written “and you shall slaughter”—meaning one who is commanded, as will be explained in siman 2—end of his words. That is surprising, for in siman 2 a gentile is not commanded on shechitah at all; but certainly a minor is commanded, for it is forbidden for him to eat without shechitah; and also sometimes his shechitah is kosher.
**And some are stringent, etc.** Maharshal wrote that the practice not to give kabbalah to one under eighteen is all according to what kind of youth he is—for sometimes one finds a youth who is a Torah scholar and trembles in fear of Heaven; even poskim are accustomed to authorize him and establish him as expert. On the presumption I say: therefore.
