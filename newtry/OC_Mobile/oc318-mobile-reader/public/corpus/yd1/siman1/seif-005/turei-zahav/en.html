**Even one of these.** Many challenge the Tur: why was this needed—he already wrote “or in each of them,” etc., and entered forced answers. It appears there is no question: without writing “even one of these” one might think that “if he does them in a deranged manner” refers only to the preceding case—losing what is given to him, where we require specifically deranged conduct; but for the earlier cases, even if we did not know he did them derangedly, therefore he wrote “even one of these” to teach it applies to all those. One might ask: why not write “he does them” in plural and avoid “even one of these”? Because he did not want such wording, which contradicts what he wrote first—“or in each of them”—which implies all together.
**Even if they wish to feed dogs.** This speaks even if they are experts—for here there is greater reason to decree lest they rely on their shechitah without others; so Agudah states.
**To steady his hands if others, etc.** He follows what was difficult for him in Beit Yosef on Rosh: “minor in the Mishnah who does not slaughter l'chatchila even with a grown person standing over him” means a minor not yet educated; education for shechitah means knowing how to steady hands—but a minor who is expert and steadies hands slaughters l'chatchila with a grown person over him, as in chapter Lulav HaGazul: a minor who knows how to slaughter may eat from his shechitah—and “may” includes l'chatchila—end of his words. Beit Yosef challenged: first he required only steadying hands, then both steadying and expert. Answer: truly steadying hands suffices l'chatchila; at the end mentioning expert is for precision. His words here are surprising—for Tur who wrote the leniency of l'chatchila permitted only with both. It appears Rosh was challenged from Sukkah that “knows how to slaughter—permitted l'chatchila with others over him”; here in Mishnah “all slaughter” also speaks of experts as in Gemara “all experts slaughter”—yet others help only b'dieved. Answer: that Mishnah minor, even if expert, is not skilled by hand; Sukkah speaks of both skilled and expert—therefore even l'chatchila permitted with others watching—as above, simple. Tur can be answered: first teaches l'chatchila he may not slaughter if not skilled even if expert; end teaches even with both qualities forbidden l'chatchila without others—as above, simple. So Magen Avraham ruled.
**Even though he knows the laws of shechitah.** Tur wrote: if he is expert and skilled by hand, valid b'dieved without others—question: why is a minor trusted on shechitah unlike siman 108 seif 14—no trusting a minor on immersing utensils? Answer: if we did not know the kashering occurred except from the minor’s words we do not believe him; but here it is known it was slaughtered—it is slaughtered before you; we only fear perhaps improper shechitah or he gave to another who may not slaughter—we do not fear since he knows and is expert—we say presumably he slaughtered properly, per Tur. I again saw thus in Mishmeret HaBayit 9: sharp minors who take meat and wine in hand are not apt to bring from issur—this is not testimony but presumption—end of his words; he elaborates there. Other poskim do not have this b'dieved leniency even with expert and skilled hands. Magen Avraham was stringent on those who send birds to slaughter via a gentile and give a sign for the slaughterer to write that he slaughtered it—he said this is forbidden since the mark is not on the thing itself like on flesh or sack. I say the stringent are praiseworthy, but I do not know the issur—for we require a mark on flesh lest it be switched—what help is a sign not on the flesh? Not applicable here—he recognizes his bird and the gentile has a sign that a reliable person slaughtered it—what issur is there? Nevertheless for those who write the word “kosher” as a sign—fear the gentile may forge that common word; need another uncommon sign to prevent forgery—so I have seen practiced. Nevertheless the stringent are praiseworthy lest recognition of his bird not be clear—see siman 62 regarding a chicken sent to slaughter via gentile.
**Less than eighteen years.** This is in Mordechai in the name of Hilchos R' Eldad HaDani; he wrote we do not rule thus, but Darchei Moshe wrote in the name of Hagahos Alfasi that some poskim were concerned about this. Maharshal wrote at the beginning of Chullin that everything follows the youth’s sharpness and knowledge; so I have seen practice not to be stringent beyond thirteen years, whenever they see he is proficient and alert in steadying his hands with adult strength.
