**Treifah.** Beit Yaakov wrote at end of siman 4: that which we forbid his shechitah retroactively is certainly prohibition, since it is a double evil—both the slaughterer became defective and the animal stands in presumption of prohibition—see there. And see in Sefer Nachalat Azriel Niddah 4:3 s.v. vehineh; and see responsum Givat Shaul siman 17, asked regarding a slaughterer who came before a court and confessed that according to the majority he slaughtered while drunk, and because of this did not slaughter properly, and did several times disqualifying matters in shechitah—delay, pressing, etc.—and asked them to instruct him in order of repentance for the evil deeds he did—what is the law of the meat and fat still extant from this slaughterer's shechitah; they answered to permit, for he is not believed because ein adam mesim atzmo rasha even in such a case—see there at length. And see responsum Chavat Yair end siman 72 regarding a woman who says in way of repentance she committed adultery, and likewise one who confesses as boel—it is explained there that if he says in way of repentance ein adam mesim atzmo rasha does not apply—but he did not bring proof thereon; and in truth it is explained so in Tosafot Bava Metzia 4a s.v. mah im yirtzeh—see there (and see responsum Chatam Sofer siman 4 who wrote from Tosafot there there is no proof, but he brought another proof there; and on what Chavat Yair wrote that the same applies to confession of boel—he disagrees—see there; and see responsum Nodah B'Yehudah part Even HaEzer siman 71 and Nodah B'Yehudah second edition siman 12—I brought them in Pitchei Teshuvah EH siman 115 s.v. raglayim ladavar; and see further Chavat Shaul siman 22, and brought a little below siman 2 s.k. 8, where he did not decide to say one is believed when confessing in way of repentance, even though he confessed while ill—see there further). I again found in responsum Shivat Tzion from the Gaon Maharash Landau z"l siman 23 who expanded on such a matter (I will bring his words below s.k. 18; and his view too is that in such a case ein adam mesim atzmo rasha does not apply, and one must forbid all meat still extant and every kli rishon absorbed from this meat—see there). And it appears that even according to the aforementioned words of Givat Shaul—specifically if he said he did matters disqualifying in shechitah d'oraita he is not believed; but if he said he did matters disqualifying d'rabbanan—there is room to forbid according to what Tumim wrote in kelalei migo os 105 that in derabbanan a person mesim atzmo rasha—see there; however I saw in responsum Brit Avraham part Even HaEzer siman 13 who expanded to disagree and raised that even in derabbanan ein adam mesim atzmo rasha—see there; if so, here too there is room to permit; nevertheless it is possible that if he said he did something uncertain where the prohibition is not clear—there is room to forbid. And see further responsum Shivat R' Eliezer siman 15.
