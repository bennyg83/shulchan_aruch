**And there are those who are stringent**, etc., and he should know to be careful. Ba'er Heitev; Noda BiYehudah Mahadura Tinyana siman 1: regarding a slaughterer whose hands tremble and he senses a fine defect, and also says "it is clear to me I did not fail in anything in his craft through delay or pressing," and his trembling does not harm him—one may dismiss him that he not slaughter; but what he slaughtered retroactively do not forbid; and utensils that are in his slaughter are permitted to use (see there what he wrote on Lechem HaPanim who brings in Semag in the name of Mahara Stein and Mordekhai Zaken—or whose hands tremble, etc., and poured on him great wrath—and it is wondrous, for the matter is simple that Lechem HaPanim does not mean Semag wrote so in the name of Mahara Stein, only that in the margin of Semag he found written in the name of Mahara Stein and Mordekhai Zaken—meaning in old Mordekhai, one whose hands, etc.; the Gaon z"l understood the word zaken refers to what follows, therefore wrote the witness lied in the name of Mahara Stein—for behold from his words zaken at eighty years is only a general stringency, and these words were not given to be written and are like an error escaping before the ruler—for it is simple the word zaken refers to the name and means old Mordekhai as this language is mentioned many times in poskim, and need not expand; per this it is possible the Gaon z"l accepted his testimony here that hands tremble to forbid b'dieved because of pressing even when saying "it is clear to me," since found written so in the name of Mahara Stein and old Mordekhai; and to be stringent at least where there is no great loss, and to kasher utensils—for hagalah helps them; and utensils not subject to hagalah—and Peri Megadim likewise—do not permit except after waiting from Torah; and think). See Me'ir Netivim siman 76 who also brought responsa Beit Yaakov siman 105 and wondered on him that words of Maharik of Beit Yosef are mentioned in Mordekhai in the name of Eldad HaDani, and wrote that in truth they are wondrous—see there how many stringencies and we do not practice so; but regarding this old man it is fit to enact that at least from seventy years onward no person should slaughter even if he still senses and is in his strength, because of lo pelug—for since majority of majority in those years already the power of sensing was nullified. Also until seventy years teachers of rulings need to supervise slaughterers coming in their days to test them periodically whether they stand on their presumption in sensing, for it is close to spoil—and I testify I saw slaughterers who thought they sensed and their hands were trembling; I showed them eye to eye and they swore they did not sense at all themselves; they held me in esteem and ceased slaughtering; fit to fence this breach—see there.
