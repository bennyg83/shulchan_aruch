**A deaf-mute who speaks**, etc.—one should not challenge to establish the Mishnah thus: "all slaughter"—healthy, and if they are mute they do not slaughter because of blessing, and if they slaughtered their slaughter is kosher—for blessing does not impede (see above that the Mishnah does not deal only l'chatchila and b'dieved alone unless there is remedy or other aspects between them—and think). And according to Maharshal one must say what does it teach us that it teaches "if they slaughtered"—blessing does not impede, behold from the siyafa we learn "and all who slaughtered," etc., and there is a deaf-mute who does not hear and does not speak among them—learn blessings do not impede; however for Tosafot it is difficult what they argued on Rashi "lest they ruin" that they do not slaughter l'chatchila—behold from the siyafa we learn "who slaughtered," etc.; and it is difficult: perhaps the siyafa teaches because of deaf-mute specifically an act because of blessing, but regarding one who forgot hilchos shechitah one could say l'chatchila they can slaughter; and it is possible the question: why need altogether "and all slaughter," only it should have taught "if others see them—it is kosher"; and why teach altogether "who slaughtered"; and if to teach forbidden l'chatchila because of blessing—this we already heard from elsewhere regarding terumah and the like; perforce to teach forbidden to slaughter l'chatchila because of deviation—and think, for it is strained.
