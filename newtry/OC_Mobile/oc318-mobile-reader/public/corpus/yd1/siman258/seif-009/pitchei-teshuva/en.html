Into the collector's hand. Authorities debate whether transfer to someone other than an established charity collector blocks annulment. Noda BiYehudah distinguishes a named poor recipient, where the intermediary may remain only the donor's agent, from money handed over for discretionary distribution among the poor, where the recipient becomes a true collector. Merely separating a coin among one's own charity money does not prevent annulment. He also permits annulment where donated merchandise was returned privately by the intended agent and the donor has a credible claim through migo.
