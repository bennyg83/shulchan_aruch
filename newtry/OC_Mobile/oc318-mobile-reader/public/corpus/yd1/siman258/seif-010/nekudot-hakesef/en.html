(Siman 258 Shulchan Arukh seif 10) One who vows charity by asmakhta, e.g. if I do such and such I will give such and such to charity, etc. (and similarly Rama wrote in gloss Choshen Mishpat siman 207 seif 19, and so I wrote in Siftei Kohen seif 21 and Rama ruled in his Shulchan Arukh). Since he wrote without qualification and did not write explicitly "if I do a mitzvah matter specifically and not an optional matter" — it implies he does not distinguish whether what he will do is a mitzvah matter or optional matter. In truth Beit Yosef brings two views in this siman — responsum of Maharam and responsum of Ramban and Hagahot Maimoniyot — and Mahar"a'i ruled in his writings like Maharam; and above from him Beit Yosef brings Mahari"v who wrote that if he conditioned on an optional matter, e.g. he said if I do not go to such a place I will fast or I will give such to charity — it is asmakhta, since going was an optional matter; and since Shulchan Arukh did not bring the distinction whether the condition was a mitzvah matter or optional matter — it implies he holds like Maharam that there is no difference between optional and mitzvah matter. And you may say: in Orach Chaim siman 562 in Shulchan Arukh seif 19 he brings the words of Mahari"v above — and his words contradict each other, for here it implies he does not distinguish between mitzvah and optional matter as stated, and there in Orach Chaim he distinguishes as stated; and one cannot say there is a difference between fast and charity, for Mahari"v mentions both; also one cannot distinguish that there in Orach Chaim he said "if I do not go and he did not go" therefore it is asmakhta because he did nothing, unlike here where he said "if I do" and he did — since he did an act it is not asmakhta — this is not so, for in Mahar"a'i cited explicitly that whether he said "if I do not do such" or "if I do such" it implies there is no difference. And one may wonder on the later poskim who did not notice this difficulty — requires investigation.
