It is nothing. Since it is not in his possession, he is not obligated because of a vow, for he did not say, “I will consecrate it when it comes into my possession,” but rather, “It shall be consecrated,” meaning that it should become consecrated now, when it is not in his possession. If he said, “This field that I am selling to you shall become consecrated when I purchase it back from you,” it becomes consecrated when he purchases it, because he presently has the ability to consecrate it conditionally. If he had already sold it and then said, “This field that I sold to you shall become consecrated when I purchase it back from you,” and he later purchased it, it does not become consecrated, because at the time of the declaration he lacked the ability to consecrate it. If he said, “This field that I pledged to you shall become consecrated when I redeem it from you,” it becomes consecrated, because he has the ability to redeem it. Even if he said, “This field that I pledged to you for ten years shall become consecrated when I redeem it from you,” it becomes consecrated when he redeems it. If he said it should become consecrated immediately, it does not, because it is encumbered to the lender. All of this is explained in the Gemara, Kesubos 59a, and is codified by the Rambam at the end of chapter 7 of Hilchos Arachin and by Rabbeinu Yerucham in netiv 19. See there for additional distinctions regarding hekdesh and charity, and see above, siman 251, seif 7, and what is written there.
But if he said, etc. The same applies even if he made such a declaration regarding something that has not yet come into existence. He must fulfill it, as explained in Choshen Mishpat, at the end of siman 212, seif 7, in the gloss.

And in the presence of the charity collector, etc. We are considered the hand of the poor, and it is therefore as though the property has already reached the poor. This implies that he cannot retract even through release of the vow. Responsa of the Rashba, siman 656, states explicitly that even if he regrets and seeks release from the consecration, he may not do so, because he already transferred the money to those two men with immediate effect, and after transfer nothing remains to be done. Since we are the hand of the poor, anything already delivered to one who acquires it, or to another person who acquires it on behalf of the poor, cannot be retracted. See there. This is also implied by Responsa of Rabbi Betzalel, siman 15. One should not challenge this from the question of the Rif and Tosafos in Bava Kamma 36b. There, Rav Yosef said, “We are the hand of the poor,” and they asked why it was necessary, since a declaration to hekdesh is equivalent to delivery to an ordinary person. They answered that the coin was not in his possession, so acquisition for the poor through the law of ma'amad sheloshtan was necessary. Some authorities give other answers. They could not answer that a declaration to hekdesh could be released through a sage, whereas delivery to the hand of the poor could not, because there the coin was not in his possession and he could not have consecrated it even without the rule that we are the hand of the poor. Furthermore, that passage does not discuss release from a vow. The words of the Rosh in Responsa, kelal 12, siman 2, require study. Nevertheless, it appears that the Rosh also holds that once it has reached the hand of the poor, release is unavailable. Even if his wording is somewhat difficult, his first answer, that it had not yet come into his possession, clearly implies this. This appears to be the primary ruling.
