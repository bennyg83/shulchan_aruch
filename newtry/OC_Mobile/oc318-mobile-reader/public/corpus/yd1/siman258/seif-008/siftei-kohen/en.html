Since it is not in his possession; and because of a vow he is not obligated, for he did not say he would consecrate it when it comes to his hand but says "it shall be consecrated" — meaning now it shall be consecrated, and now it is not in his possession to consecrate it. If he said "this field that I am selling you — when I take it from you it shall be consecrated" — it is consecrated when he takes it from him, since it is in his power to consecrate it from now; but if he already sold it and said "this field that I sold you — when I take it from you it shall be consecrated" and afterward took it from him — it is not consecrated, for it was not in his power to consecrate it then. If he said "this that I pledged to you — when I redeem it from you it shall be consecrated" — it is consecrated since it is in his power to redeem it; and even if he says "this field that I pledged to you for ten years — when I redeem it from you it shall be consecrated" — it is consecrated when he redeems it; but if he said "it shall be consecrated from now" — it is not consecrated, for behold it is encumbered to the lender. All this is explained in the Talmud Ketubot chapter Afi Al Pi (Ketubot 59a); Rambam wrote chapter 7 of Arakhin and Rabbeinu Yerucham netiv 9; and see there several distinctions in laws of consecration and charity, and above siman 251 seif 7 and what is stated there.

And before the gabbai, etc. For "we are the hand of the poor" and it is as if it came to the hand of the poor; and it implies that for this reason he cannot retract even by asking permission. And so is implied explicitly in responsum of Rashba siman 256 who wrote: and moreover regarding this matter, even if he regretted and came and was asked about the consecration he is not permitted, because he already handed the money to the hand of these two men and now there is no further transfer — for "we are the hand of the poor", and whatever he already handed to the hand of the acquirer or to another who acquires on behalf of the poor he cannot retract, etc. — see there. And similarly in responsum of R. Betzalel siman 15. And do not challenge according to this: what did the Rif and Tosafot challenge in chapter 4 of Bava Kamma (36b) regarding one who retracted and said "give it to the poor" — for R. Yosef said "we are the hand of the poor" — let it emerge that his statement to the Exalted One is like his transfer to a common person? They answered that it is different there because that coin was not in his possession, and therefore he needed to acquire for them by the law of standing of three; and some poskim answered otherwise. And why were they not satisfied: if because statement to the Exalted One, etc., he could permit by asking permission? But because "we are the hand of the poor" and it is as if it came to the hand of the poor he cannot permit — one can say that there, since that coin was not in his possession, even if it were not "we are the hand of the poor" he could not; and moreover there it does not discuss permission but requires investigation what the Rosh wrote in responsum kelal 12 siman 2: and what troubled you according to the explanation of R. Yonah who explained that a vow to the poor has permission and I do not know what your difficulty is, etc. — and that of Bava Kamma had not yet come to his hand, therefore the poor acquired it; and also it does not discuss permission but retraction — to retract without permission he cannot — end quote. And Maharal of Prague brings it siman 4; it seems to say: why did this man not permit in Bava Kamma? And he answers that it does not discuss permission, etc. — it seems he could permit even though "we are the hand of the poor"; and it seems to me that also the Rosh's view is that he cannot permit. And what he wrote "and that of Bava Kamma", etc. — the explanation of that of Bava Kamma that is clear to us there that the poor acquired even if not for "we are the hand of the poor"; and that we needed "we are the hand of the poor" is because that half-dinar was not in her possession, as the Rif, Tosafot and other poskim wrote; and similarly the Rosh himself in his rulings to Bava Kamma that there it had not yet come to his hand, etc.; and also it does not discuss permission, etc. And even if your soul desires to say this strains the Rosh's wording — nevertheless in the first version he wrote "had not yet come to his hand" — it implies explicitly he cannot permit; and so seems primary.
