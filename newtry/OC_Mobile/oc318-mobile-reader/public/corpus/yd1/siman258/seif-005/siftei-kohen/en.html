To the poor of that city. This applies when he lives in that city. If he does not live there, they return and bring the funds with them to their own city, as above, siman 251, seif 6. The Mordekhai writes likewise, and the Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe cite him. The Bach writes regarding one who has no fixed residence in any city, but sometimes stays in one city and sometimes in another, that the funds should be given to the poor of the city in which he was located when he vowed, as written in Choshen Mishpat, siman 253, seif 23: “They shall give it to the poor of that city; and if he regularly stays in both, etc.” It appears that the Mordekhai also agrees with this ruling.
And even if that charity was deposited, etc. Even if he has a migo through which he could be believed that he vowed this charity, and even if he is a charity collector whose hand is considered the hand of the poor, they give it to the heirs and inform them that this is what he vowed. Mordekhai, ibid.
