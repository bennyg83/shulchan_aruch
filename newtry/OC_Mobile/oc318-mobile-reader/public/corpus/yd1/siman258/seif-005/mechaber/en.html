One who says, "Give 100, 100 zuz to the poor" — they give to the poor of that city where he lives: {Rama: And see above siman 251 seif 5; and all this is unstated; but in a place where they know his intent was not only for the poor of that city alone — such as places where the custom of the wealthy is to give much charity at the time of death and the custom is to distribute those charities to all poor — they follow this; and even if he vowed unstated they follow the custom and his heirs distribute the charity to all poor who are good in their eyes, even if they are not in their city; and even if that charity was placed and deposited in another's hands, they give it to the heirs and inform them that so he vowed (Mordechai, first chapter of Bava Batra). One who obligates himself with a fine to charity and transgressed must give them to the poor of the city and cannot say they should give them to other poor; and the collector can claim him, for it is called money that has claimants (Mordechai, chapter Bnei HaIr, in the name of Maharam). But one who consecrated much money and appointed his heirs over it to distribute as good in their eyes, even to poor of another city — it is called money that has no claimants, and the collectors of the city cannot compel them and claim they fulfill the deceased's will — and even though they transgress and rob the poor, nevertheless it is called money that has no claimants since they can distribute to whomever they wish (Beit Yosef in siman 259 in the name of Teshuvat Rashba); and even if there were two trustees and one died, the community cannot appoint another but the survivor does as he wishes, as the donor commanded (there and there). And see Choshen HaMishpat siman 301. And even in charity of the townspeople, a person cannot claim charity unless he has authorization from the collector or the town notables. One who consecrates a mina to charity and does not have to pay it in installments like other creditors (Beit Yosef in the name of Teshuvat Rashba, gate 1).}
