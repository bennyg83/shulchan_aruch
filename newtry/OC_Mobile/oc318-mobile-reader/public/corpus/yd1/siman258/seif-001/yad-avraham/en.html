(Siman 258, seif 5, Rama's gloss) Even if there were two trustees and one of them died, the community cannot appoint another; rather, the survivor shall act as he wishes and in accordance with what the donor instructed. This law is from a responsum of the Rashba. The Taz expressed astonishment at it, because since the testator appointed two, perhaps he did not trust either one without the participation of the other, and why should the survivor now be trusted alone? It appears that there is no difficulty at all, because the Rashba follows his own approach. In another responsum he wrote that whenever a person says, "So-and-so and so-and-so," it means even either one of them individually unless he explicitly specifies that they must act together. For this reason he ruled that if one placed his son or friend under oath not to lend out his money except with the permission of Reuven and Shimon, and one of them died, he may lend it with the permission of the one who remains. This is as the Rama wrote above in siman 216, and the Mechaber in siman 228 wrote the same law even when both are alive. The Taz there, however, asked from where we derive a leniency, since perhaps we should assume that he intended the more straightforward meaning, namely both of them together. He raised a further difficulty there and explained the Rashba's words differently. His words, however, are not compelling, as I wrote there. The primary understanding is that the Rashba's words are literal: even for purposes of leniency he rules that the formulation means either one of them. Accordingly, the words of the Rashba here are well understood.
