Like one who attaches his vow. As above, siman 229, seif 3.
If he said, without specification, that it is for hekdesh, etc. See above, siman 256, seif 1, in the gloss.
It has no remedy. Through redemption, and it becomes forbidden to everyone. Ibid. However, this applies specifically to consecrated movable property, but land can be redeemed. Responsa of Rabbi Eliyahu Mizrachi, siman 53, likewise cites the Or Zaru'a and adds that according to the Rif and Rambam, even movable property can be redeemed for a small amount. See there.
And they release him. They release his vow through an opening and regret. They ask him, “Had you known that through your declaration it would become forbidden to everyone, would you have vowed?” If he says no, they release him immediately. Ibid. If he says, “I knew, and nevertheless I vowed,” they release him through regret or other openings, as above, siman 228. In Responsa of Rabbi Betzalel, siman 15, he extensively proves that some authorities hold that vows of charity can be released only through an opening that renders the vow mistaken from its inception, but not through regret. See there.
