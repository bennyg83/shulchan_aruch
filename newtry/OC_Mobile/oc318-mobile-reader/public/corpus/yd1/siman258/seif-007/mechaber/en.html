A person does not consecrate a thing not in his possession: How so? He had a deposit in another's hands and that one denied it is with him — the owners cannot consecrate it; but if he did not deny it, behold it is in the owner's possession wherever it is. When do we say this? With movable property; but land that one robbed and denied — if he can extract it through judges, behold he can consecrate it even though he has not yet extracted it, for the land itself is in the owner's possession. One who robs his fellow and the owners did not despair — neither can consecrate it: this because it is not his, and this because it is not in his possession; and likewise all similar cases: {Rama: If he has collateral in his fellow's hands for what is against the loan, he cannot consecrate it; and the remainder he can consecrate (Mordechai, chapter HaMocher). And some say even the remainder he cannot consecrate (Teshuvat Or Zaru'a, Niddah 59). One who consecrates a debt document requires writing and delivery like an ordinary person (Beit Yosef siman 259 in the name of Teshuvat Rashba).}
