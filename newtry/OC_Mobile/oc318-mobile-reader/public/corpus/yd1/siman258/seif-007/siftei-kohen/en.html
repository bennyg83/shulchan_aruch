But if he did not deny it, etc. This applies specifically to a deposit, because wherever it is located, it remains in the owner's possession. A loan, however, even if the borrower did not deny it, is given to be spent and is therefore considered outside the lender's possession, so he cannot consecrate it. This is implied by the Rif and the authorities, and also by the words of the Tur and Shulchan Aruch in the following seif.
He cannot consecrate it, etc. See above, siman 221, at the end of seif katan 44.
As with an ordinary person. As explained in Choshen Mishpat, siman 66. It appears that one who consecrates a promissory note and subsequently forgives the debt has effectively forgiven it, just as one who sells a promissory note to an ordinary person may forgive it, as stated there, seif 23.
