For so-and-so when he arrives. Chatam Sofer discusses a community that pledged future business profits to build a synagogue, then after the profits materialized delivered them to the collector and later took them back. Even if the original pledge concerned property not yet in existence, the later delivery created a new and valid dedication. Since the vow was already fulfilled by delivery, it cannot be annulled, and the collector may be liable if recovery from the takers is impossible.
