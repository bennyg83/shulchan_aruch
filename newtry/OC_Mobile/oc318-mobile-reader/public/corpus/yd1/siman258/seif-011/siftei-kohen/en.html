Moreover, even if it had substance they did not vow this to charity except if one of them claims his fellow — e.g. one retracts and the other does not retract from him — but if both agree and neither claims, why should they be obligated — end quote. And his words do not seem correct and he became entangled in the words of Beit Yosef, for Beit Yosef did not write in the Talmud from Mahar"i that when the kinyan is nothing the penalty is also nothing, but by establishing penalties one to another and not to charity; for to charity what does it matter that the kinyan is nothing — it should be without kinyan, statement to the Exalted One acquires. And Mahar"i did not exempt in Teshuvot siman 311 by establishing penalties to charities but because they reconciled with one another; and so wrote Taz siman 26 in the name of Beit HaTalmud on a borrower who obligated himself by kinyan to go to judge with the lender in gentile courts — it is nothing, for it is only a kinyan of words; and just as the kinyan is nothing, also the penalty he conditioned on himself is nothing, except if he conditioned on himself to obligate himself thus and thus to the poor, etc.; and so wrote the author there seif 63 — it implies regarding penalty to charity he is obligated even if the kinyan is nothing; and it is clear.
