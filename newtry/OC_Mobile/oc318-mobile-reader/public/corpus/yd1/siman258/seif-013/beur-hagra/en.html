(Likkut) For every, etc. The Ran asks in Kiddushin 28b why acquisition by money is needed for sacred property, since a verbal commitment to Heaven is binding. He answers that the rule of verbal commitment to Heaven applies only where there is a benefit to hekdesh, as seen at the beginning of chapter 8 of Arachin: if one retracts from a fifty-unit bid, etc. But regarding the balance, where hekdesh gains nothing, the rule does not apply. The entire sugya there implies this. This also resolves Tosafos's question in Kiddushin: at the moment of the transaction there had been no gain to hekdesh. The Ran likewise explains that the statement 'he redeemed it for two hundred' means specifically an actual redemption, whereas mere speech would not suffice. This is also Tosafos's second answer. Charity is learned from hekdesh, because the verse 'that which your mouth has spoken' includes charity, and the first chapter of Nedarim discusses yad for charity. The early authorities rule according to that conclusion. End.
But, etc. Kiddushin 29a: if they drew it at a value of two hundred, etc., 'the power of a layman should not be greater,' etc. The rule is specifically where meshichah occurred, because of that principle. See Tosafos there.
If he thought, etc. As stated in Shevuos 26b and at the beginning of chapter 2 of Kiddushin. The statement in Kiddushin 28b, 'his declaration to Heaven,' refers specifically to speech; the Baraisa there likewise says, 'How so? If one says,' etc., because that passage concerns a case where beit din compels him to give, and his thought is not known until he expresses it. Mordechai, first chapter of Kiddushin. This is the Rama's intent in writing, 'unless,' etc., though I do not know why he needed to state it.
And some say, etc. They hold that the rule applies specifically to terumah and sacred property, as stated there. The first opinion holds that charity has the same status as consecrated property, as explained above in seif 1. See Choshen Mishpat, siman 212, seif 8.
