And he wrote in Shevut Yaakov part 2 siman 67: a needle found in the thickness of a chicken's thigh — the dispute of recent poskim in siman 51 is known, and one may not be lenient in flying birds; but in domestic birds such as geese and chickens whose way is to rub on the ground within ash-heaps to warm themselves — one need not be stringent; and see in Sulam LaMinchah general rule 54, see there; and Maharach agreed with this, not like Beit Lechem Yehudah, see there. And he wrote in Pri Megadim part 3 siman 14: in a goose that lay in the intestines like a shell and was similar to a dried egg — I cut the shell and found it entirely full of small and large, long and short feathers; and Taz already spoke of this in siman 51 and Peri Chadash; and according to this view they permit — only Shevut Yaakov doubts the matter; and it appears one should not rely to be lenient only where it appears the geese swallowed sharp feathers like a thorn and punctured the intestinal children and they came into the body cavity, and through moisture skin formed above them. And I thought and searched and found within the skin a small thorn about half a cubit long — I rejoiced. And again a question came to me from my sons: they found within intestinal children of a goose called (tripe giderim) long feathers and small feathers beneath them, and from their great growth it became literally hard as a stone; and at the other end something protrudes like a thick needle — to declare treifah only if found within the intestines and did not go out to the body cavity — there is to declare kosher like a needle found lying in the intestines; but found in the body cavity — treifah; see there, for it implies in his incident he declared treifah because of the feathers even though the shell lay within the intestines; and it is possible that because it was moist he wrote "in the intestines" and meant in the cavity. However, for practical law it appears to me: if such an incident occurs that a shell with feathers like a dried egg is found — declare treifah, for there are legs to the matter that the intestines were ruined and through crusting from a blow the shell crusted on the feathers and they stuck together — everything according to what the inspector sees. And see responsum Chinuch Beit Yehudah siman 44, 45, 46, and 47 in name of several great ones to declare treifah in blisters and within them feathers with pus; but in Shevut Yaakov part 2 siman 65 he wrote regarding feathers found inside stuffed geese in their fat — the view of great acharonim to be lenient; and in Chavot Yair siman 44 he wrote in name of several great ones to declare treifah; and although the lenient are majority in number and majority in stature — nevertheless the inspector must before declaring kosher examine well how the feathers are: if they have a recognizable sign that they came from outside, or grew inside the fat from abundance of fat; and in any case he should inspect the esophagus and all intestinal children on the side of possibility — if there will be no defect that one may say it perforated and sealed — he should not rely on those who permit except in great loss; see there. And see in Or Ne'elam siman 16: in a piece of goose fat called schmaltz, found inside the fat about the size of a nut and within it something like pus and appears like threads or hairs from feathers, and within it a small bone, and at one end sharp and one head wide and somewhat bent and drunk from this bone — it was recognizable to me near certainty that it is the bone at the end of the ribs of geese where is the place of connection and growth of the schmaltz; in all ribs no bone is found like this except in the last rib; if so, one may say the goose fat in which the bone was found — the last rib broke in the place of growth of the schmaltz and stood in its place and became entangled in fat; and what appears like pus — perhaps at the time of breaking it was still wrapped in a little flesh at the end or between ribs and appears like pus; and we are not concerned perhaps it swallowed a bone — we hang on permissibility; and we also find that the bone came from outside to inside, for fat geese sit, and it is possible that from the weight of sitting it entered through belly skin from outside to inside and stood in its place and did not perforate from inner organs; if so, here there is twofold safek to permit; see there.
