Or needle, etc. If so — if a needle stuck before us from outside into the body cavity and the perforation returned and healed well — the animal is treifah, for we see certainly the needle reached the body cavity and inspection is required; and it is simple. And accordingly one may say: that which the Gemara said in chapter HaTa'aruvot — certainly treifah; in what — if he knows, let him come and take; if he does not know, from where does he know — and one may challenge: it should have taught in such a case, and it is treifah and impossible to know; and the answer to this and its counterpart — see below siman 57 where we elaborate this investigation.
And if it is a safek, etc. The Rav Taz in siman 49 disagrees on this; and in truth this twofold safek — apart from that Taz wrote it does not reverse — regarding this there are arousers who uprooted below in the general rules of twofold safek that we found many twofold safekot that do not reverse; only I think both safekot are really one: whether a perforation was made in inner organs; and what difference to me if there was a needle inside the animal or not — in the end the safek is whether the intestinal children and remaining organs were perforated or not; and what difference if I say safek that there was no needle and organs were not perforated, or that there was a needle and nevertheless intestinal children were not perforated — here there is only one safek: perforated or not perforated, whatever the reason — whether from absence of needle or that the needle did not reach there and did not perforate — in any case it is one safek; therefore it is clear Ohel Mo'ed did not intend a complete twofold safek, only his intent: it was slaughtered, it is permitted — establish the animal in a presumption of permissibility; and if there is a defect before you — Taz says there is no defect, since here there is a safek: perhaps there was no needle, and safek when there was — whether it perforated; if so, defect does not apply; if so we say: it was slaughtered, it is permitted — establish the animal in presumption of permissibility. And one may reconcile: thorn in esophagus — there "it was slaughtered, it is permitted" does not apply, as written above siman 33, see there.
