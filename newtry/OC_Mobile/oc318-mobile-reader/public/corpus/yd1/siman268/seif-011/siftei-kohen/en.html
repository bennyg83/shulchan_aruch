However, he is believed, etc. The Rosh writes: The statement that he is believed to disqualify himself does not mean that, if he has relations with a Jewish woman, he disqualifies her, for we rule that a person cannot render himself wicked through his own statement. Moreover, it would be astonishing for him to be disqualified while his children remain legitimate. Therefore, his testimony has no effect even regarding himself in that respect, and if he has relations with a Jewish woman, he does not disqualify her. Rather, his testimony is effective for himself only in that he has rendered the matter forbidden to himself, and he is therefore forbidden to marry a Jewish woman. End quote. See the Bach, and see above, seif 3.
