He can protest when he grows up. See Sefer Tiferet Moshe who wrote that if his conception was not in sanctity and his birth in sanctity — such as a pregnant gentile woman converted — he cannot protest — see there at length [and in responsum Chasam Sofer siman 253 he raised that even if his birth was not in sanctity, as long as one of his ancestors converted with him he cannot protest, since the Rif in chapter HaChalitz holds he can never protest even if the court converted him; and in Beit HaGadol that all his words are words of acceptance — he ruled at least when his ancestors brought him even if they did not convert with him he cannot protest; and it is nearly certain that Rambam's opinion is likewise; and in Shitah Mekubetzet he brought many poskim who ruled that at least when they converted with him he cannot protest; and in the Rosh first chapter of Ketubot we do not find he explicitly disputes this — if so there is no doubt this is the halachah. He also wrote: where he can protest — certainly the root of conversion is uprooted retroactively; and the remaining wine that touched him before his protest is like any contact of a minor gentile with our wine; and if he had relations with a Jewish daughter while he was a nine-year-old he disqualifies her from kehunah, etc.; but we need not be concerned for this at all lest he protest; therefore we bless at the time of immersion and allow him to touch wine or marry a man or marry a Jewish woman, etc. — see there.]
