They say to him: What did you see that moved you to convert, etc. This is so that, should he wish to withdraw, he will withdraw, for converts are as difficult for Israel as a lesion. It is also so that he will not later say: Had I known, I would not have converted. The Beis Yosef wrote that if they did not inform him, this does not invalidate the conversion, and the later authorities cite this ruling.
They do not excessively burden him. This refers to mentioning the punishments.
They do not scrutinize excessively with him. Meaning, even regarding the few punishments that are explained to him, they need not inform him of every detail of those punishments. Rather, they tell him in general: Before you entered this status, if you ate forbidden fat you were not liable to kareis, and if you desecrated Shabbos you were not liable to stoning. Now, if you eat forbidden fat, etc. They need not inform him of every detail and stringency relating to the prohibition of forbidden fat and the prohibition of Shabbos, for perhaps his intention is for the sake of Heaven. So wrote the Bach.
Without any intervening substance, etc. The Gemara states that anything that constitutes an interposition for immersion also constitutes an interposition for a convert. See above, siman 198, regarding substances that interpose during immersion. A valid mikveh, fit for the immersion of a niddah, is also required, as stated above in siman 201 and in the Gemara. The Rambam likewise wrote in chapter 13 of Hilchos Issurei Biah, halachah 13: In a mikveh valid for the immersion of a niddah, converts, slaves, and freed slaves are immersed, and anything that constitutes an interposition for a niddah also constitutes an interposition for converts, slaves, and freed slaves. End quote.
Some say that he should shave, etc. This implies that even if his hair presents no interposition, such as where he washed and combed it thoroughly, or cut away the few knotted hairs that interpose, he should nevertheless shave all his hair because he is entering the community of Israel. The Bach likewise wrote that perhaps the removal of a convert's hair is based on the teaching of Rabbi Moshe HaDarshan cited by Rashi in Parashas Behaaloscha. This is also the custom for apostates who return to the Jewish faith. Nevertheless, it appears that after the fact, if he did not shave his hair but there was no interposition, the immersion is valid.
