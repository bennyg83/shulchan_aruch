And he comes to convert. This implies that he comes of his own volition. The ruling teaches that although a minor lacks legal capacity, we heed his request, because conversion is a benefit for him, and one may confer a benefit upon a person in his absence. However, if he does not wish to convert, he is not a convert, even when Jewish authority is dominant over the non-Jews, as stated. The Bach wrote likewise.
He may protest when he reaches majority. The Ran wrote that although a minor's protest is not legally effective in the sense that, if he later consents, his earlier protest is of no consequence, it is nevertheless effective in this respect: if, after reaching majority, he maintains his protest, it is effective. Hagahos Derishah wrote likewise.
