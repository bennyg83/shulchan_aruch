When he comes, etc. This follows from Yevamos 24b: “The Sages taught: We do not accept,” etc. Rambam wrote that in the days of David there was concern that converts returned because of fear, and in the days of Shlomo because of the sovereignty, prosperity, and greatness enjoyed by Israel. Although the Gemara says there, “The halachah follows the words of,” etc., this refers only to the status after the fact, as stated below: even if it became known, etc., he has the status of a convert, etc. Rambam writes there that he is nevertheless not among the righteous converts. He further writes that converts did convert during the days of David and Shlomo before ordinary people. The Great Beis Din remained concerned about them; after they had immersed, the court neither rejected them nor drew them close until their ultimate conduct became evident. Since Shlomo converted women and married them, and Shimshon likewise converted a woman and married her, and it was known that they had converted only for an ulterior motive, Scripture regards them as though they were still non-Jews, etc. For this reason the Sages said that converts are as difficult for Israel as a scab, because most such converts return for an ulterior motive and mislead Israel, while it is difficult to separate from them after they have converted. Go and learn from what occurred in the wilderness, etc. See Tosafos 47b, s.v. “They are difficult because,” etc. Regarding Rambam's statement that converts nevertheless converted, see Tosafos 24b, s.v. “They did not,” etc., and their answer. This is also the meaning of the ruling below: even if it became known, etc., we remain concerned about him, etc.
And if it is a man, etc.; and if, etc. This is stated there, and follows the explanation above that the ruling, “The halachah is,” etc., applies only after the fact. Menachos 44a likewise states: “Perhaps your eyes,” etc.
And even if he reverted, etc. This follows from seif 2 above. Rambam writes there that for this reason Shlomo and Shimshon retained their wives, even though their secret became known and their later conduct demonstrated what their original intent had been, as explained above.
An apostate Jew, etc. As explained above, even a convert who returns to his former conduct remains a Jew in every respect.
Only rabbinically, etc. As stated in Avos DeRabbi Nosson, chapter 8: There was another incident involving a certain young woman, etc. He said to him, “During all those days,” etc.
And to accept, etc. Bechoros 31a states: “The Sages taught: And all of them,” etc. See Tosafos there, s.v. “And all of them,” where Rabbeinu Tam states, etc., concerning a convert who accepted upon himself, etc. The Gemara there, 30b, states that acceptance must take place before three scholars, as is likewise stated in Yevamos, as explained above.
