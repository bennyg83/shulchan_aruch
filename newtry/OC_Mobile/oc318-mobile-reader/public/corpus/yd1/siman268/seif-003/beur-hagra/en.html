All matters, etc. Yevamos 46b states in the name of Rabbi Yochanan, etc., “judgment,” etc.; and one is not a convert without circumcision and immersion. Likewise, Yevamos 47b states: Rabbi Chiya bar Abba said in the name of Rabbi Yochanan, “A convert,” etc.
However, this applies specifically, etc. This follows from Yevamos 45b: “Did she not immerse for her state of niddah?” Immersion for niddah is performed at night, and men are not ordinarily present with her. This is the meaning of the ruling that the immersion is effective even if three were not present, etc.
Except, etc. This follows from Yevamos 47a: “The Sages taught: ‘You shall judge,’” etc.; “between him and himself,” etc.; “You are believed to disqualify yourself,” etc. Kiddushin 62b likewise asks: “A convert also is not,” etc. If conversion performed privately were valid after the fact, conversion would be within his own power. Tosafos answer that the obstacle is the acceptance of mitzvos. See Tosafos in Yevamos 45b, s.v. “Did she not,” and in Kiddushin there, s.v. “A convert.” This also resolves the Gemara's question there, “If so, then,” etc., because a freed slave does not require a new acceptance of mitzvos, as stated in Yevamos 48a. See also Tosafos in Kiddushin there, s.v. “Rather,” etc.
And according to Rif, etc. Rif answered the preceding difficulty in this manner: when they called him “the son of an Aramean woman,” they sought to disqualify her son. Nevertheless, Yevamos 47a remains difficult, for it states, “But you are not believed,” etc., whereas the children would not be disqualified in any event. Ramban answered that the prospective convert must nevertheless accept before three judges that he will circumcise, immerse, and be informed of some of the mitzvos, etc.
Or at night. This is not mentioned by Rif, but the Tur wrote it in his name, deriving it from the requirement of three judges, since both requirements share one reason, namely that conversion is a judicial proceeding. See Tosafos 46b, s.v. “They do not immerse,” etc. Ramban likewise wrote that since we rule that a judgment rendered at night is invalid, it is invalid even after the fact. Rambam, however, wrote that conversion performed at night is valid after the fact, as stated in seif 4. Rashba ruled likewise, explaining that immersion resembles the completion of judgment, and the stringency applies only initially. Shulchan Aruch, while discussing the requirement of three, also applied Rambam's ruling to nighttime conversion. It appears that Rif also agrees with Rambam, since his only difficulty with the cited incident was that three were not present; he did not object that it occurred at night.
