That he immersed for his keri. Meaning that we knew this clearly; and likewise with a woman — then this immersion ascends even for the sake of conversion even though he did not mention explicitly thus; and so the Rif — for if he were not a convert he would not immerse; meaning if it were not in his mind then to become a convert he would not immerse even for keri. And what he wrote in seif 11 that he is not believed to say "I converted between myself and myself" until he immerses before the court — there we did not see from him any immersion for keri, and now he wants to immerse for the sake of conversion — certainly lekhatchilah he needs three. And Maharshal z"l wrote it appears to him the Rif says thus — from that he immersed for keri we derive he already immersed before three; and it does not imply thus at all; also from Rashi and Ran it is explained explicitly that that immersion alone suffices for him even if there was no other; and as the meaning of the Tur and Shulchan Aruch.
Except, etc. As written 47a — "teachers and you shall judge between a man and his fellow and between a man and himself" — you are believed to disqualify etc.; and in Kiddushin 62b a convert too is not etc.; and if after the fact it is fine, it is in his hand; and resolve Tosafot that it is in acceptance; and Tosafot Yevamot 45b s.v. mi etc. and Kiddushin there s.v. ger etc.; and with this the difficulty is resolved "if so now" etc. because a freed slave needs no acceptance as in Yevamot 48a; and Tosafot Kiddushin there s.v. ela etc.

This is not mentioned in the Rif, but the Tur wrote thus in his name and learned from what is written regarding three — one reason for both — judgment, etc.; and as Tosafot 46b s.v. ein matbilin, etc.; and so Ramban — we rule if they judged at night it is not valid even bedieved; but Rambam wrote that at night bedieved it is doubtful; and as written in his words; and so Rashba, and he wrote that immersion is like completion of judgment — lekhatchilah we are stringent; and Shulchan Aruch, by force of the flow of years, wrote Rambam's words also for night; and it appears the Rif also holds like Rambam, since he was not challenged on the previously mentioned matter except that there were not three — he was not challenged from night.
