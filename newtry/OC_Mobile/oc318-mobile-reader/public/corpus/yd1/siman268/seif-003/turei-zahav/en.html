Before three who are qualified to judge. The verse states, “One law shall be for you and for the convert,” requiring a beit din.
Who immersed because of a seminal emission. This applies where we know with certainty that he immersed for that purpose, and similarly where a woman immersed from niddah. Such immersion also counts for conversion, because a non-Jew would not ordinarily immerse for those Jewish obligations unless he intended to live as a Jew. The requirement below that one who claims private conversion must immerse before a beit din applies where no such prior immersion was witnessed.
Except for acceptance of the mitzvos, etc. Acceptance of the mitzvos is the essence and beginning of conversion and must be before three by day. Circumcision and immersion are comparable to completion of judgment and are valid by Torah law even at night.
We do not disqualify him. The Gemara discusses one whose mother did not expressly immerse for conversion but had immersed from niddah. That immersion is effective after the fact, because a non-Jewish woman would not immerse for niddah. The Rif explains that the practical result concerns a son born from a Jewish woman: he is not stigmatized as the son of a non-Jew. Although the child would be valid even if his father were a non-Jew, distinguished families might otherwise avoid marrying him.
