Before three who are fit to judge. For it is written, “One law shall be for you and for the convert.”
Who immersed for his emission. This means that we knew this matter with certainty, and likewise regarding a woman; then this immersion counts even for the sake of conversion, even though he did not mention this explicitly. So wrote the Rif: “For if he were not a convert, he would not have immersed,” meaning that if he had not intended then to become a convert, he would not have immersed even for his emission. What he wrote in seif 11, that he is not believed to say “I converted privately” until he immerses before a court, is where we saw no immersion for an emission from him, and now he wants to immerse for conversion; certainly, ab initio three are required. My teacher and father-in-law, of blessed memory, wrote that it appears to him that this is what the Rif means: from the fact that he immersed for his emission, we infer that he had already immersed before three. But this does not sound so at all. Also from the words of Rashi and the Ran it is explicit that that immersion alone suffices, even if there was no other, as implied by the Tur and Shulchan Aruch.
Except for acceptance of the mitzvot, etc. For this is the essence of the matter and its beginning; but circumcision and immersion are like the conclusion of judgment, which by Torah law may be even at night. Asheri.
We do not disqualify him. In the Gemara it says of one whom they called “son of an Aramean woman” because his mother did not immerse for the sake of conversion, so wrote the Ran, that Rav Asi said: “Did she not immerse for her niddah?” Rashi and the Ran explain that that immersion counted for conversion, for a gentile woman does not immerse for niddah; and likewise regarding a man, immersion for an emission. The Rif wrote regarding this that it is after the fact, namely that if he fathered a son from a Jewish woman, we do not disqualify him. This means that one should not call him “son of a gentile,” but the pedigreed families of Israel would not marry him, even though he is valid. For even where a gentile or slave has relations with a Jewish woman, where the child is valid, nevertheless pedigreed families do not marry him, like the case in Perek HaCholetz where Rav validated him and wanted him to give him his daughter, and Rav punished him so that he became a pile of bones. Regarding this the Rif says here that there is no disqualification of gentile status at all. This is also the intent of Rabbeinu Chananel in the Tur at the beginning of the siman, where he wrote that the children enter the congregation.
