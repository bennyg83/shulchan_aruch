They must be three qualified [judges]. For it is written regarding a convert: "one law shall be for you" — and there is no [valid] judgment with fewer than three qualified persons.
But after the fact, if he did not circumcise or immerse, etc. However, regarding acceptance of the commandments — if he did not accept them before three, even after the fact it is an impediment, as Tosafot and Rosh wrote, for acceptance of commandments is like the initiation of judgment, as explained in Choshen Mishpat siman 5. But immersion and circumcision are like the conclusion of judgment, as explained there — that they may be completed at night. And one must examine somewhat, for the Rav wrote there seif 2 that some say if they went ahead and judged at night the judgment is valid; if so according to this, certainly even regarding acceptance of commandments it would suffice after the fact at night, and the Rav should not have left it ambiguous. Possibly specifically regarding monetary laws the Rav was concerned for the view of "some say" because "the court's declaration renders [property] ownerless"; and so Bach wrote in Choshen Mishpat siman 5 — that even though regarding chalitzah one should rule stringently so that even after the fact the chalitzah is invalid at night, nevertheless "some say" is sufficient to rely upon. End of his words. And the same applies here, for it is comparable to chalitzah.
But before two. Not specifically two — even before one. And so it is proven in Tosafot chapter HaCholetz s.v. yesh lecha edim (44b). However one can say he mentioned two because if we know him as one who was a non-Jew, he is not believed to say "I converted on my own," as below — and therefore they need at least two who know that he converted properly. And even for a woman, it appears that when she immersed herself, men who saw her immerse were needed to be witnesses to the matter. Perisha.
But if he married a Jewish woman, etc. And even though we rule that even a non-Jew who had relations with a Jewish daughter — the child is valid; nevertheless there is a difference, for the child is valid for kehunah, unlike one born from a non-Jew, as explained in Even HaEzer siman 7; alternatively the difference is regarding pidyon haben, as below siman 305 seif 18.
