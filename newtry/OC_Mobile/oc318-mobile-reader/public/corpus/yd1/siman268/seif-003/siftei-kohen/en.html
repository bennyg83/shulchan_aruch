There must be three qualified judges. It is written regarding a convert: “One law shall there be for you,” and judicial proceedings require no fewer than three qualified judges.
However, after the fact, if he did not circumcise or immerse, etc. Regarding acceptance of the mitzvos, if he did not accept them before three judges, this invalidates the conversion even after the fact, as Tosafos and the Rosh wrote, because acceptance of the mitzvos is comparable to the beginning of a judicial proceeding, as explained in Choshen Mishpat, siman 5. Immersion and circumcision, however, are comparable to the completion of a judicial proceeding, which may be concluded at night, as explained there. This requires some study, for the Rama wrote there in seif 2 that some say that if judges transgressed and adjudicated at night, their judgment is valid. According to this, even acceptance of the mitzvos should certainly suffice after the fact at night, and the Rama should not have ruled without qualification. Perhaps only regarding monetary law did the Rama take the dissenting view into account, because property declared ownerless by beis din is ownerless. The Bach likewise wrote in Choshen Mishpat, siman 5, that although regarding chalitzah one should rule stringently that even after the fact chalitzah performed at night is invalid, nevertheless the dissenting opinion is sufficiently authoritative to rely upon in monetary law. End quote. The same applies here, for conversion is comparable to chalitzah.
Rather, before two. Two is not precise; even one would suffice. This is evident from Tosafos in the chapter HaCholetz, s.v. “Do you have witnesses,” folio 44. Nevertheless, one may say that two are mentioned because, when people recognize him as one who had been a non-Jew, he is not believed to say that he converted himself, as explained below. Therefore, at least two people must know that he converted properly. Regarding a woman as well, it appears that when she immersed herself there had to be men who saw that she immersed, so that they could serve as witnesses to the matter. Perishah.
However, if he married a Jewish woman, etc. Although we rule that even when a non-Jew has relations with a Jewish woman the child is legitimate, there is nevertheless a practical difference, for the child is eligible for the kehunah, unlike a child fathered by a non-Jew, as explained in Even HaEzer, siman 7. Alternatively, there is a practical difference regarding redemption of the firstborn son, as explained below in siman 305, seif 18.
