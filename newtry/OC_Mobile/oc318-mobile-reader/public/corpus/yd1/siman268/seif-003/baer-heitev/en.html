Two. This is not necessarily two; one may suffice. Nevertheless, two are mentioned because at least witnesses are needed to know that the conversion was properly performed.
For his seminal impurity. Had he not intended at that time to become a convert, he would not have immersed merely for seminal impurity. Taz.
That it invalidates. Shach questions whether acceptance of mitzvot at night should invalidate after the fact. Rama in Choshen Mishpat cites a view that a judgment rendered at night is valid after the fact. Perhaps that leniency is limited to monetary law because beit din can transfer property, whereas conversion resembles chalitzah, which remains invalid at night even after the fact.
And he fathered. Although a child born to a non-Jewish father and Jewish mother is valid, the difference is that the child of this convert may be fit for the priesthood, unlike a child fathered by a non-Jew. Alternatively, the difference concerns redemption of the firstborn. Shach.
