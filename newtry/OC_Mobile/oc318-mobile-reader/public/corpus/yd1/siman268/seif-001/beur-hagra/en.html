And if he was circumcised, etc. There, in the statement, “Behold, one who comes,” etc. We rule in accordance with Rabbi Yose, as stated there: Rabbi Yose said, “In every case,” etc.
Or if he was born, etc. This is comparable to the case of a minor in siman 263, seif 4, where we are concerned that perhaps the foreskin was crushed, etc.
And no blessing is recited, etc. Rabbeinu Chananel ruled that no drawing of covenantal blood is required, as indicated in Shabbos 135a: “They did not disagree,” etc.; “Regarding what did they disagree,” etc. See Tosafos there, s.v. “They did not disagree,” and in Yevamos, s.v. “For Rabbi Yose,” etc. Therefore, Rambam and Rosh ruled stringently that covenantal blood must be drawn, but no blessing is recited, since in a case of doubt concerning blessings we rule leniently.
And if it was severed, etc. Tosafos in Yevamos, in the previously cited passage, compare this to a woman, as stated there: “And Rabbi Eliezer should likewise derive it from the matriarchs,” etc.; rather, the discussion concerns one who immersed, etc. The Sages disagree even where he immersed but was not circumcised. The text must therefore mean that we do not derive a possible case from an impossible one, for the difficulty is directed specifically against Rabbi Eliezer. The Gemara's response, “And should you say,” etc., applies here as well, since circumcision is impossible in this case.
If he immersed, etc. Initially, circumcision certainly must precede immersion, as stated there: “Until he is circumcised and immerses.” Likewise, Yevamos 47b states: “When he has recovered, they immerse him,” etc. After the fact, however, Ramban wrote that the immersion is effective, based on Yevamos 78a concerning a pregnant non-Jewish woman who immersed, etc. Tosafos in Yevamos 47b, s.v. “They immerse,” disagree, as does Ra'ah. He writes that since the fetus is not fit for circumcision, immersion alone is effective for him, just as it is for a woman, as explained above. Once he is born, he is a full Jew even before circumcision; he is circumcised only as a Jewish infant is circumcised. This is the basis of the view introduced by “Some say,” etc.
