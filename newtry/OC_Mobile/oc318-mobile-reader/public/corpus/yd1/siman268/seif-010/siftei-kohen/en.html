But one who comes, etc. Meaning: it is specifically one who was presumed to be a non-Jew and says "I converted," etc. — about him these statements were said. But one who was not presumed to be a non-Jew and says that he is a non-Jew who converted in court — he is believed by means of a miggo [argument], since if he wanted he could have said "I am an Israelite."
And Rambam wrote, etc. And Bach wrote: nevertheless the practice is to be lenient and believe him even for the purpose of marrying him to a Jewish woman. And it is possible that even Rambam agrees in the case of travelers who come and are presumed to be Jews — that they are believed even now when the majority are non-Jews, even for the purpose of marrying a Jewish woman, since all families are presumed to be fit, as explained in Even HaEzer siman 2. And Rambam only said [his stringency] regarding one whom we did not know as a non-Jew and who says "I converted in such-and-such court" — he is believed on the basis of a miggo. There, Rambam says that even so, a heightened standard was set in matters of lineage, so that he is not married to a Jewish woman on the basis of a miggo until he brings proof.
