However, one who comes, etc. Meaning, these rules apply specifically to one who was established as a non-Jew and then said that he had converted. However, one who was not established as a non-Jew and says that he was a non-Jew but converted before a beis din is believed through a migo, since, had he wished, he could have said that he was Jewish.
The Rambam wrote, etc. The Bach wrote that the custom is nevertheless to be lenient and believe him even for the purpose of permitting him to marry a Jewish woman. It is possible that even the Rambam agrees regarding travelers who arrive with an established presumption that they are Jewish, that they are believed even today, when most people are non-Jews, even for the purpose of permitting them to marry Jewish women. This is because all families have a presumption of valid lineage, as explained in Even HaEzer, siman 2. The Rambam spoke only of one whom we had not known as a non-Jew, who says that he converted before a particular beis din and is believed by virtue of a migo. In that case, the Rambam says that, notwithstanding the migo, an additional stringency was imposed regarding lineage, and he may not marry a Jewish woman until he brings proof.
