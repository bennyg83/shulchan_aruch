And if we observed them, etc. Rambam writes that if we observed her consistently following Jewish practice, such as immersing for niddah, separating challah from her dough, and similar conduct, or if a male convert follows Jewish practice by immersing after a seminal emission and performing all the mitzvos, they are presumed to be converts, etc. He derived this from Yevamos 45b: “Did she not immerse,” etc. Ramban disagreed and wrote that these words are astonishing, for if so the Gemara should have said, “Did she not observe even one Shabbos?” since that would be easier to ascertain than immersion. The Yerushalmi in chapter 3 of Kiddushin teaches in the name of Bar Kappara: A convert who was circumcised but did not formally immerse is valid, because there is no convert who has not immersed after a seminal emission. The Yerushalmi asks: Can immersion from a lighter impurity serve for a more severe matter? Rabbi Yose bar Bun answered: Since both immersions are undertaken for the sanctity of Israel, the immersion is effective for him.
Nevertheless, etc. This follows from the incident related in Yevamos 47a, etc.
But one who, etc.; and Rambam wrote, etc. All of this is from Rambam. He derived it from the Yerushalmi cited there by Migdal Oz: “Beloved is Eretz Yisrael, for it validates converts. One who says in Eretz Yisrael, ‘I am a convert,’ is accepted immediately; outside Eretz Yisrael he is not accepted unless his witnesses are with him.” At first glance, this Yerushalmi contradicts our Gemara, which rules alike in Eretz Yisrael and outside it, while according to Rabbi Yehudah the distinction is reversed. Rambam therefore explained that the Yerushalmi addresses the case described here. The reason is that an additional standard was established regarding lineage, as stated at the beginning of chapter 4 of Kiddushin: “All lands are as mixed dough,” etc. The Yerushalmi follows the view there that even Bavel is as mixed dough in comparison with Eretz Yisrael. See Tosafos in Yevamos 47a, s.v. “Where he was established,” etc.
