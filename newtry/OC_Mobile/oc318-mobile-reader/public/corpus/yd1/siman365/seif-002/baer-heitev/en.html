They distance. The Levush wrote that the reason is so people not encounter it constantly and become distressed. He was not precise, for the reason is because its odor is bad. So it appears in the Gemara in Bava Batra, chapter Lo Yachpor; so Rashi and the other commentators explained, and so wrote the Rambam and the Mechaber in Choshen Mishpat 155:22. End quote from Shach. Perhaps one can say that the Levush also accepts the reason of bad odor, since he wrote it in Ir Shushan regarding neighbor damages in siman 155. Rather, he teaches that even where the bad-odor reason does not apply, such as where they dug and buried him very deep in the ground so that no bad odor emerges, or in another manner where that reason is absent, nevertheless they enacted distancing fifty amot so that people not see it constantly and become distressed, perhaps thereby arousing themselves to mourning and weeping more than the time set by the sages, and being punished as the sages said. Therefore this is included in the sages' enactment not to mourn excessively. Beit Hillel wrote that in our countries this law does not apply: in some places the cemetery is in the middle of the city, and in some places it is very far from the city, depending on efforts with the authorities.
