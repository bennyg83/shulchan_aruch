And they are still worth one hundred. For otherwise it is completely permitted and there is no subterfuge. If they were initially worth ninety and he gives them to him for one hundred, it is full ribbis. So writes the Beit Yosef in the name of the students of the Rashba. Their reasoning appears to be that although one may sell an item for more than its value, as written at the beginning of siman 174, it is nevertheless forbidden here because he first said to him, “Lend me.” One cannot say that the case here concerns a manner in which it is forbidden to sell at a higher price, as mentioned in siman 174, for if so, why did they state the prohibition here specifically in the case where he said, “Lend me one hundred”?
And there are those who say that it constitutes avak ribbis. The Tur concludes: Even according to this view, if he wishes to repay him with wheat, it is permitted. However, we do not compel him to repay with wheat in order to extract it from him. This means that even if he stipulated with him that he would repay money, he may repay him with wheat; however, we do not compel him to provide the entire one hundred in wheat. It appears obvious to me that the prohibition in this seif applies where the entire arrangement was made at one time. If, however, after some time had passed from when he took the wheat for one hundred, he then sold it back to him for ninety, it is completely permitted. This is comparable to what is written in siman 174, seif 2; see there.
