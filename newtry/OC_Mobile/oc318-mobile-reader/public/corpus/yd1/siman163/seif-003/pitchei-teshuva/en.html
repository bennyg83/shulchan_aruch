At the market price. See Responsa Chatam Sofer, siman 137. After citing Beit Yosef in Bedek HaBayit, siman 173, that one who regularly purchases non-kosher carcasses for immediate resale at a lower price violates the prohibition according to everyone, he explains that no authority disagrees. He then writes that Shulchan Aruch here, siman 163, seif 3, says "and he gave him wheat worth one maneh at the current market price" specifically to exclude a case where the current price is lower, as Taz explains. Mechaber did not state the greater novelty that even if no formal market price has yet been established, but the wheat is simply worth less, the transaction is forbidden, and even without the phrase "lend me," because Beit Yosef had not yet seen Ritva when composing the main work. Bedek HaBayit was written in his later years. Taz likewise did not have Bedek HaBayit before him. See what I wrote below in siman 173, seif 1.
