The Beit Yosef writes in the name of the Nimukei Yosef that the prohibition where he does not possess wine applies specifically because the wheat was initially acquired for him as a result of a loan. Therefore, whatever he subsequently takes from the borrower requires that the borrower actually possess it. If, however, he initially gave him money for the wheat, he may afterward set a price for wine even if the seller does not possess wine, provided that the market price has been established. This applies where he holds him to the wheat and says, “In exchange for this wheat I will give you wine,” for this is an exchange of produce for produce. If, however, he does not hold him to the wheat, but rather converts the wheat into a loan against him and then wishes to give him wine in payment of that loan, the borrower must specifically possess wine. Even if he did not formally make the wheat into a loan, but merely says, “You have such and such an amount of wheat in my possession, worth such and such an amount of money, and I will give you wine in exchange,” he must likewise possess wine, since he mentioned a monetary sum as their value. Rabbeinu Yerucham, however, writes leniently in this case, since the wheat was not made into a loan against him. Although this is not the precise wording of the Beit Yosef, this is his intent. Below, in siman 175, the Rama rules stringently in this matter. The Beit Yosef further concludes, based on Halachos Gedolos, that one may borrow on the basis of the market price only in the case of a se'ah for a se'ah. One may not lend him money and stipulate: “If you do not repay me by a certain time, you will be obligated to give me produce according to the present market price.” This is forbidden, since it results from a loan. Rashi, however, explains that even money may be lent on the basis of the market price, and we rule in this manner. End quote. The reason appears to be that until that time it bears the status of a loan against him. When does the setting of the price for the produce begin? At that later time, and at that time he is not giving him money. It appears to me that the prohibition applies specifically in this form, where there was certainly a loan first. If, however, he says: “I am giving you money for produce that you will supply to me throughout the year at the present price, but I give you the option that, if you wish, you may return my money to me by a certain time,” it is permitted. He mentioned the price arrangement for the produce at the time the money was given, and merely does him a favor by allowing him, if he wishes, to retract from the sale and return the money. We find a similar principle below, at the beginning of siman 174, and in Choshen Mishpat, siman 207, regarding ribbis, where such a condition is effective. Although there in Choshen Mishpat, siman 207, it is effective only where the sale was initially made without qualification, as stated there in seif 7, that case is different because the purchaser immediately consumes the produce, creating a concern of avak ribbis. The condition must therefore be made afterward. Here this concern does not apply. In particular, since Rashi permits lending even money in all circumstances, one may rely upon his view to permit this case where the price arrangement for the produce is mentioned, even though the borrower is given an option at the time the arrangement is made. So it appears in my humble opinion.
