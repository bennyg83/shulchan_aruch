At the prevailing market price. See Responsa Chatam Sofer, siman 137. After citing the Beit Yosef in Bedek HaBayit, siman 173, that one who regularly buys non-kosher carcasses for immediate resale at a lower price violates the prohibition according to all opinions, and explaining that no authority disagrees, he writes: The Shulchan Aruch, siman 163, seif 3, states, “He gave him wheat worth one hundred at the prevailing market price.” This wording excludes a case where the market price is lower, which is forbidden, as the Taz writes in seif katan 6. It does not state the greater novelty that the prohibition applies even before a market price has been established, provided the wheat is worth less, and even without the borrower first saying “lend me.” The reason is that the Beit Yosef had not yet seen the Ritva’s words, since he composed Bedek HaBayit in his old age. The Taz likewise lacked Bedek HaBayit. See also what I wrote below, siman 173, seif 1, s.v. when he does not have it.
