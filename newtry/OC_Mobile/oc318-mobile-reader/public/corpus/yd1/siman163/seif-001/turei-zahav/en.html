It is permitted even if the market price has not yet been established. Rashi explains: since he has produce, this buyer acquires it from now — and even though he has not drawn it [meshichah] — and if it appreciated in value, it appreciated in his possession, and this is not interest since if he wants to retract he is subject to "who paid" [mi shepara'ah]; and even if the market price has not yet been established, he is discounting it for him. And if not, it is forbidden, etc. The language of Rashi on this is as follows: however, where he does not have it — since there is nothing in the buyer's hand with which to buy it, it resembles interest. It appears from [Rashi's writing] "nothing in his hand with which to buy" that even money he does not have — and so the Rosh wrote explicitly: he has no money and no wheat; and the Tur too wrote explicitly: "and he said to him: I have no money," etc. And also from the Talmud's language it is implied: for it says "give me my money for I want to buy wheat with it, and he said: I have wheat to give you," etc. — from the phrase "I have wheat" it is implied that he was unwilling to pay money [in return], and this implies he has no money to pay; and otherwise why does he accept giving wheat at the future high price rather than holding the profit himself? — rather, certainly he now has no money; and therefore afterward it says that if he has no wheat it is forbidden, since he also has no money. And there is further proof — for in the siman preceding this the Tur wrote in the name of Raavad that one may not forward-price based on the market rate unless the borrower has money, etc. — and there too, if the lender's own money helps, why [was the case about the borrower having money mentioned]? Similarly here: that the lender's own money helps if the market price has been established. So it appears to me according to the view of the decisors cited — that where the lender has his own money it helps, if the market price has been established, like having produce. But Beit Yosef wrote that from Rashi's language it appears to him that specifically having wheat is required here, not money; and likewise it appears to him from the acronyms [Rosh and Tur], which only mentioned wheat. And what appears to me in the law I wrote — for the ruling, not to be lenient in practice since [Beit Yosef] is stricter.
