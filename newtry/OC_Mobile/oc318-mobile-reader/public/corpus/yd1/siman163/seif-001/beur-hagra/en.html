If he has wheat. Specifically wheat — Rambam and other later authorities; but Hagahot Maimoniyot and Hagahot Asheri there wrote that the same applies to money; and as stated there: "what difference does it make whether they [themselves] or their monetary equivalent" — and as Tosafot wrote on the topic of se'ah for se'ah there, 72b, s.v. "there is no, etc." But the Rosh wrote there in the name of Raavad that [the leniency applies] specifically to se'ah for se'ah, not to other loans. And even though he wrote there in seif 7: "if he has none — it is forbidden" — meaning: he has neither money, etc. — Beit Yosef wrote this is a scribal error and a gloss; and Maaseh Mordechai wrote a student in error wrote it and overlooked the words of Mordechai and Hagahot Asheri; but the words of Rambam appear correct, since it states: "and wine — if he has none" — implying specifically wine. At the measure of his money. Rambam; and in Rivash there is a dispute about this, and he wrote that the main view is that of those who disagree; and he wrote that so too wrote the Rosh.
Even if he did not, etc. Nimukei Yosef in the name of Ramban; and what is stated there, "at the current market rate" — he mentioned this as a stringency, meaning even so it is forbidden when he has none — for in this matter a creditor is different from a buyer; and what is stated in the mishnah, "and so too the market rate" — according to Rashi, this is understandable regarding wheat which is permitted; but according to Tosafot's interpretation, s.v. "he purchased, etc.," it too must be understood as a stringency — and this is forced. And in Rashi, seif 3, s.v. "upon produce, etc.," he wrote specifically where a market price has been established; see Nimukei Yosef (meaning: that is his intent, as I wrote in the commentary on Maharam Schiff). Even when a market rate has been established. Gemara there: "at the current market rate, etc."; and likewise there, 62b: "but if he has none, etc. — [comes from the same clause], etc." — and all the more so according to Tosafot's interpretation, since the difficulty bears on the wheat [case] in the mishnah as well. And the Jerusalem Talmud implies like Tosafot's interpretation, as we say there: "he said to him: give me wheat, for I wish to sell it — by what [right] has he acquired [it]?" — meaning: since he has none, Rav Nachman bar Yaakov maintained one might think he is obligated to provide [the produce] to his fellow; and see Choshen Mishpat, siman 209, seif 6. And he is trusted, etc. See what I wrote in the Supplement to siman 162, seif 2 (in seif katan 11) — but according to what the Shulchan Aruch writes here, specifically from that same species at the measure of his money — there is nothing to this view. And it appears that Rashi (in Hagahot Mordechai there) does not hold the view of Rambam and the Shulchan Aruch here.
