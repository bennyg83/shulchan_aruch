The market price. Because the buyer acquires the seller's existing wheat immediately, even without meshichah. This differs from borrowing many seah against one existing seah, where each seah is viewed as borrowed against that one. In a sale, the sale takes effect only upon the amount actually owned. Cash sufficient to buy wheat does not help according to Shach; Taz permits in theory but not as practical ruling.
Forbidden. If he gives money now for wheat to be delivered throughout the year at today's market price, it is permitted once a market price exists, even if the seller owns no wheat. He may calculate only at the market price, not more cheaply, lest it appear as interest. A loan conditioned that failure to repay by a date will require produce at today's price is forbidden because the produce obligation arises from a loan.
