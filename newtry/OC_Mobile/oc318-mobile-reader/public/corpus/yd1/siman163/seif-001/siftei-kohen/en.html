If he has wheat etc. Specifically if he has wheat; but if he has money in the amount sufficient to purchase wheat — it is forbidden. So ruled Beit Yosef, Darkei Moshe, and Bach.
According to the amount of his money. And it is not comparable to above siman 162 seif 2, where if he has a small amount of that same species he borrows on it several seahs — for there, since he borrowed a seah we view it as if he borrowed seah against seah that he has, and so on always; but in a sale the sale takes effect only on what he possesses; and see above siman 173.
Permitted. Because he acquires it from now, and even though he has not yet drawn it — when it appreciates it appreciates in his domain, and it is not ribbit; since if he wishes to retract, the curse of "one who paid" stands upon them — Rashi.
And if not, it is forbidden. But if he gives him money now for wheat to be delivered to him throughout the year at the current price — it is permitted if the price has gone out, even if he has no wheat, as will be explained — end of Tur's language; and it is below siman 175 — see there; and Perishah wrote it implies he cannot value the wheat except at the market price and not cheaper, for it would appear as ribbit — but it is not decisive — end; and it seems to me it is decisive from the saying "if the price went out" and also from saying "at the current price"; and so is proved from the poskim and the Tur below siman 173 seif 7 — see there; and it is obvious. And Beit Yosef wrote that borrowing on condition that if he does not deliver by such-and-such time he will give him produce at the current price — is forbidden since it comes on account of the loan — end; and Darkei Moshe brings it; and see above siman 175 seif 6 in the hagahah.
