Until the rain. This implies the season of rain, and it implies the time of the final rainfall, namely Rosh Chodesh Kislev, by which time the first and middle rainfalls have already passed. If rain fell during those periods, however, the first rainfall, on 17 Cheshvan, suffices. This is the Rambam’s opinion, unlike the Tur, who wrote that it means until the first rainfall even if no rain fell. “Rainfall” means rain because it lies upon the earth.
Until the rains. This implies specifically the actual falling of rain. It is also plural, and the minimum plural is two, meaning that two rains must fall, the first and second rainfalls. The Mishnah makes clear that even if he says, “until the rains will come,” he is forbidden only until the rains begin. The reason is that the time is not fixed, and it is like the harvest in seif 12. According to the Ran, this is because the beginning of the rains does not occur at an equal time. The Rosh wrote that it is because the duration of the rainfall is not fixed. The Tur did not need to state this, for he relied upon what he wrote concerning the harvest in seif 12, from which this can be learned. Even if he says, “until the rain,” whose law is that he is forbidden until the rainy season, it is nevertheless not called a fixed time, since the duration of the rain has no fixed measure. Therefore, with every form of expression he is forbidden only until the beginning arrives.
And in the Diaspora, the rainy season, etc. This means that if he made this statement in the Diaspora, etc., as written in seif 13, that we follow the place where he made the vow. The Prishah wrote that this refers to one who vowed in Eretz Yisrael, but his intention is hidden from me.
