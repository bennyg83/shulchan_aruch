He is forbidden until the rainy season, etc. The Rosh, Tur, and Raavad rule that he is forbidden only until the first rainfall. Rashi explains likewise in Nedarim at the end of page 62.
Until the rainy season, which in Eretz Yisrael is Rosh Chodesh Kislev. This is the third rainfall according to the Rambam and the Mechaber.
17 in Marcheshvan, which is the first rain; and all this is Rambam's view; but Rosh and Tur ruled like R' Yehuda that the first rain is 7 Marcheshvan and the second on 17; and if he said "until the rain" he is forbidden until 7 Marcheshvan and then permitted whether rains fell or not; and if he said "until the rains" or "until there are rains", forbidden until the second rain falls (and so R' Yerucham, part 4, chapter 6, though there is a printing error in his wording, see there); and the Mechaber and Rav omitted their view because Beit Yosef wondered how they ruled like R' Yehuda against R' Yosi, but in truth I wonder at his wonder, for it is clear in the Talmud in Nedarim (daf 63 side a), what is the difference for asking, vows, and fasting; and for asking we hold it is on 7 Marcheshvan, as Taz wrote in Orach Chaim beginning siman 117, and it is agreed by all poskim; and likewise Rashi first chapter of Taanit (daf 6 end side a): it appears to R' Hagah that R' Chasda said halacha is like R' Yehuda who said first on 7 Marcheshvan and then they begin to ask, as below, end of his words; and likewise proven there in the Talmud that R' Chasda said halacha like Rabban Gamliel who ask on 7 Marcheshvan, and so the halacha is fixed in the Talmud (there daf 10); and on the contrary one should wonder on Rambam who in chapter 2 of Hilchot Tefillah ruled we ask on 7 Marcheshvan, meaning like R' Yehuda, and here ruled the first rain is 17 Marcheshvan; and likewise on the Mechaber, though it can be resolved with difficulty, requires study. And it appears to me for this reason they omitted, because in practice we follow Rambam and Mechaber for vows and fasting, and for asking we follow Rosh and Tur, see below end of this note.
And in the diaspora, etc., and likewise Rosh and Tur wrote; and it appears according to their reasoning, for they hold the first rain is 7 Cheshvan in Eretz Yisrael and the second on 17; if so we see the second is ten days after the first; since we say in the first chapter of Taanit (daf 10 side a) in the diaspora we ask sixty days after the tekufah, if so the second rain is seventy days after the tekufah, for otherwise whence do they have that in the diaspora the second rain is seventy days after the tekufah? According to this, Rambam and Mechaber who wrote the first is 17 and the second 23, if so there are only six days between one and two; if so the second rain in the diaspora is only six days after the first rain, and if so it is difficult for the Rav who wrote Rosh and Tur's words after Rambam and Mechaber's words, requires study.
Sixty days, etc. Everything follows the place where he made the vow. So write the Rosh, Tur, and Rabbeinu Yerucham.
