Lest you go, etc. Perishah copied in the name of Maharshal: specifically in vows which are lenient we say people are not careful with conditions; but regarding gittin Rosh ruled at the beginning of HaMegaresh that any condition that is in her power to fulfill — she is permitted to marry immediately, and we are not concerned lest she transgress the condition — end quote. And see in Even HaEzer siman 143. And it appears to me it is not comparable there at all, for there whenever she transgresses she is also forbidden from today onward to her husband — therefore she trembles to transgress. Unlike here where there is no prohibition from here onward — that is, after Pesach — only in what transgressed before Pesach. And for this reason fear does not come upon her head — what transgressed, transgressed. The Tur brought here in the name of the Rambam: even though Pesach passed, etc. And he wrote on it: I do not understand, etc. And it appears according to my humble opinion to resolve the words of the Rambam with a broad explanation — that is, Rambam explains from that the vow said extra language — that is, until the festival — since after Pesach there is no prohibition, like the Tur's challenge: and why did he mention at all until the festival? And on this there are two answers. The first: he intends she should not benefit until Pesach lest she transgress afterward — and such is the law according to truth. And one may also say that what he said until the festival is a manner of retraction — that is, at first it entered his mind to forbid only until Pesach alone, and therefore he said konam she'at nehenit li until Pesach if you go to your father's house, and it was not in his mind to say anything afterward; and afterward he reconsidered and said until the festival — meaning everything shall be until the festival, both the benefit and the going. And we find this view of retraction also in siman 222. And therefore Rambam wrote that if she benefited and went before Pesach she is lashed, for in this there is certainly prohibition. But after Pesach it is nevertheless a doubt in prohibition, lest there is retraction here, and unspecified vows we follow to be stringent; but one is not lashed from doubt — so it appears correct in my humble opinion. And furthermore it appears to me the reason of the Rambam along with what Rambam wrote — Shulchan Aruch brought it siman 237 at the end of se'if 1: even though the others did not accept the oath, if they fulfilled his words — behold they are praiseworthy for not accustoming him to make a vain oath. And here too, since in any case he intended in his oath until the festival — even though there is no prohibition of benefit if she goes — nevertheless one should be careful that the entire oath be fulfilled as much as possible.
