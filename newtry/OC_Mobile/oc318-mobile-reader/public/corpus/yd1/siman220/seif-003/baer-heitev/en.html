Without — Bach ruled that for "this week" and "this month" too one needs questioning at the end of the week and month; see there. And Shach wrote: even if he stood on Shabbat and vowed "this week," he is forbidden only on that Shabbat alone and tomorrow is permitted immediately; and so for month; and below seif 6 regarding year; and so Maharal ibn Chaviv — end. And so if he said "this Shabbat," its law is like saying "this week"; and the same for "Konam wine that I taste this week" or month or year — its law is like "this week," "this month," or "this year"; and he wrote in responsum Mahari Betzalel that likewise if he said "this year" it means until Rosh Hashanah, and it is plain; and he further wrote likewise if he said "until a year" its law is like "this year," and it is not forced — end Shach. And Taz wrote: it appears if he said in Yiddish "diese woch" where Shabbat is not included in the prohibition; but if he said secular language "I will not eat meat," the five or six weekdays — then certainly Shabbat is included since he spoke by counting; but one who says "diese woch" does not intend by day count but only that the days are weekday not Shabbat — and it seems the common custom is so; and all the more if standing on Shabbat he said he forbids himself "diese woch," he certainly did not intend that day but the coming week — end. [And Nekudat HaKesef challenged: if he says "diese woch" it implies Shabbat too since the world calls the seven days "diese woch," so Shabbat is included; and what he wrote "custom seems so" is if he accepted not to eat meat on weekdays only — see there.]
