Or before it. Or afterward. Even if he was standing on Shabbos and vowed concerning 'this week,' he is forbidden only on that Shabbos, and is immediately permitted the following day. The same applies to a month, as explained below in seif 6 regarding a year. So writes Mahari ibn Chaviv there.
This week. Or 'this Shabbos,' as stated in the Gemara, since Shabbos can also denote a week.
This week. The same applies if he said, 'Konam, wine that I taste this week, this month, or this year.' It is treated as though he said 'this week,' 'this month,' or 'this year,' just as 'today' is treated as 'this day.' So writes Mahari ibn Chaviv there. See below, at the end of seif katan 15. Mabit writes in part 2, siman 104, page 50, column 3, that if he said 'this year,' it likewise means until Rosh Hashanah, which is obvious. He further writes that if he said 'until a year,' it has the same law as 'this year,' but this is not compelling.
And he is permitted afterward. Bach rules like Rabbeinu Peretz in Semak and like the Bartenura that with 'this week' and 'this month' he also requires release at the end of the week or month. See there.
He is forbidden for a full period. Meaning seven complete twenty-four-hour periods from the time of the vow.
