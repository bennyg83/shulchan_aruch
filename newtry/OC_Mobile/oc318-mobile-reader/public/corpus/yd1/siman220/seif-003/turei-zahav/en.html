In the middle of the week. Not specifically as written afterward "or before it" — it does not exclude only one standing on Shabbat, for then he is forbidden only until motzaei Shabbat, as in seif 6 on 29 Elul — so Ran wrote. And what is written "this week" in Tur, Rambam, Ran, and Rosh is written "this Shabbat" — but the author of Shulchan Aruch holds that even in the language "week" Shabbat is included, for the language "week" is language of seven — meaning all seven days of the week with Shabbat; nevertheless whoever stands in the middle of the week did not forbid himself except until completion of the seven days of the week. And it appears to me that if he said in Ashkenaz tongue "diese Woch" where Shabbat is not included in the prohibition — this language is like weekdays in the holy tongue, for when we wish to say Shabbat and weekday we say in foreign tongue Shabbat und Woch — automatically Shabbat is diminished entirely; unlike language "this week" where Shabbat is included in "week" as completion of the week; but language "weekday" is until Shabbat, for language Woch is the opposite of Shabbat. However it appears to me if he says weekday language that he will not eat meat — not five or six Wochen — then his intent is by counting, and then certainly Shabbatot are included, for since they said by counting it is as if he said five or seven days — automatically Shabbat is included; but one who says diese Woch — his intent is not to count days but all the time days are Woch and not Shabbat; and as it seems the common custom is thus; all the more if standing on Shabbat and says he will forbid himself diese Woch — certainly he did not intend that day but the coming week — so it appears to me for practical law here, after the general rule we have that in vows we follow people's language.
And permitted afterward, etc. — for the difference between "this week" and an unspecified week is a great difference and all know the difference; unlike in seif 1 between "one day" and "today" — even there the gemara says "today" with "one day" can be confused and come to permit also on "one day" when it gets dark; but "one day" with "today" does not come to be confused regarding concern that also on "today" he will come to permit in half a day; therefore it is clear in all the differences found in this siman regarding "until Pesach" or "until it is Pesach" that there we do not require questioning; and Maharach wrote from his own reasoning below that questioning is required so people not err — and it does not appear so from the poskim who did not mention it at all there.
