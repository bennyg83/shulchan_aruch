Until Rosh Chodesh of the first Adar. The halacha follows R’ Yehuda, who holds that an unspecified “Adar” means the first Adar. Nevertheless, if he says, “until the end of Adar,” he is forbidden until the end of the second Adar, because the two Adars are considered as one. So wrote the Rosh. It is obvious that if he says, “until the middle of Adar,” this means until the middle of the first Adar. We do not say that he meant until Rosh Chodesh of the second Adar, because the term “middle” does not apply to the second Rosh Chodesh. Only regarding “the end of Adar” do we consider the two Adars as one, because he intended the completion of whichever month bears the name Adar. So too wrote my revered father-in-law, of blessed memory.
Until Rosh Chodesh of the second Adar. The Rambam rules like R’ Meir, who holds that an unspecified “Adar” means the second Adar. He nevertheless holds that if the person did not know that the year was a leap year, he presumably intended the Adar adjacent to Shevat.
