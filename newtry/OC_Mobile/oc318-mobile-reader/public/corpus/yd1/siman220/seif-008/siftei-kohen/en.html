Until the end of the second Adar. Whether or not he knew that the year was intercalated, all agree that he is forbidden until the end of the second Adar, because the two months of Adar are regarded as one month for this purpose. Bach writes that if he said 'until the middle of Adar,' it has the same law as saying 'until Adar.' Only the phrase 'until the end of Adar' implies the end of everything called Adar, meaning the end of the second Adar. Accordingly, the same applies if he said until the twentieth or twenty-first of Adar.
And according to the Rambam, etc. And it requires investigation, for in Choshen Mishpat siman 43 se'if kaf chet he wrote: two documents made in an intercalated year, one is written in it Adar without specification and one is written in it Adar Bet, and the borrower has no property against both of them; they give to one in which Adar is written without specification, end quote, and he did not bring the view of the Rambam at all. And likewise in Orach Chaim at the beginning of siman 428 and in Even HaEzer siman 126 se'if zayin Rabbenu Yerucham wrote without specification that unspecified Adar is the first Adar. And in the Gemara chapter Konam they compare vows to documents explicitly. And likewise Av Bet HaGadol, for the Rambam also in documents unspecified Adar is Adar Bet, and it is simple and certain that there the witnesses knew the year was intercalated. And likewise Rosh in his commentary in Nedarim 4 page 63 at the end side 1, when he knows the year was intercalated, unspecified Adar means the second Adar, and all document writers recognize an intercalated year, etc. And for the practical law, since Bach ruled in Even HaEzer siman 126 regarding gittin that the get is invalid from doubt, if so, also in vows one should be stringent in Torah prohibition like the view of the Rambam. And furthermore the view of Tosafot at the end of chapter Konam appears also like the Rambam, Maharshal in the latter part: if he knew it was intercalated and vowed until Adar and says that unspecified Adar is the second Adar, for the intercalary month Adar Rishon is and not the second Adar, know that the first Adar is thirty days and the second twenty-nine. And furthermore Megillah and Purim are in the second, evidently the first is the intercalary month, end quote. Since he wrote "know," etc., evidently he holds for halachah that unspecified Adar is Adar Bet. And so Mahari Weil in laws and halachot siman 5: we say in Nedarim one who vows until Adar, if he did not know the year was intercalated, until the first Adar; and if he knew the year was intercalated, until the second Adar. And here too he knew, end quote. And likewise in the responsum of Maharam Mintz siman 9: when he mentions Adar and knew of the intercalated year we say he means the second Adar, since the first Adar is the intercalary month, as we explain, we say this person since he did not specify, he speaks without specification, that is, Adar Bet, end quote. He further brought there the language of Avraham Zacuto in one who rents a house to his fellow until Adar, this one says until the first Adar and this one says until the second, they divide between them, end quote. And he wrote on it: it requires investigation at the end of chapter Konam. And it appears to me Avraham Zacuto is in doubt whether the halachah follows Rabbi Meir as Rambam and his supporters wrote, or Rabbi Yehudah as Rosh and his supporters wrote, and therefore he ruled they divide. And therefore in two documents made in an intercalated year it also appears for halachah that one does not give to one in which Adar is written without specification, rather they divide, like the law of two documents whose term is one day, as was explained in Choshen Mishpat siman 104 se'if yud. Also in the responsum of Maharam Mintz siman 3 he brought the words of the Rambam and wrote: since Abaye answers thus and it is also taught thus, therefore it appears to me it is good to write explicitly Adar Alef, etc. However, certainly lekhatchilah one should write in documents explicitly the first Adar for all, and likewise in the responsum of Maharam Mintz there. And so it is in Hagahot Minhagim regarding the laws of the four parshiyot siman 44. And unlike what appears from the words of the Rav and the Levush in Orach Chaim siman 428. And according to this, when we bless the month the first Adar one should say explicitly the first Adar and not unspecified Adar as some cantors are accustomed. And so Maharil at the end of the laws of Purim, when we bless these two months in the synagogue we call the first Adar Alef and the second Adar Bet.
