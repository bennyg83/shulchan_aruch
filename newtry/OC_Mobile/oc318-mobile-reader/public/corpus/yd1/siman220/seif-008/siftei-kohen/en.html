Until the end of the second Adar. Whether he knew that the year was intercalated or not, according to everyone it is forbidden until the end of the second Adar, for the two Adars are considered one month. The Bach wrote that if he said "until half of Adar," its law is like one who says "until Adar"; only when he says "until the end of Adar" does its meaning extend until the end of everything called Adar, namely until the end of the second Adar; end quote. According to this, it appears that the same applies if he says "until the twentieth" or "twenty-first of Adar": its law is like one who says "until Adar."
And according to the Rambam, etc. This requires study, for in Choshen Mishpat siman 43 seif 28 he wrote plainly: if two documents were made in an intercalated year, one written simply "Adar" and one written "Adar II," and the borrower does not have enough property for both, payment is given to the one written simply "Adar"; end quote. He did not bring the Rambam's view at all. So too, in Orach Chayim at the beginning of siman 428 and in Even HaEzer siman 126 seif 7, the master wrote plainly that an unspecified Adar is the first Adar, and in the Talmud at the end of Perek Konam it explicitly compares vows to documents. The Atzei Zahav also wrote that according to the Rambam, even in documents an unspecified Adar is Adar II, and this is certainly obvious there, for witnesses know that the year is intercalated. The Rosh likewise wrote in his commentary to Nedarim 63a that when he knows the year is intercalated, an unspecified Adar means the second Adar, and all writers of documents recognize the intercalation of the year, etc. As a matter of law, since the Bach ruled in Even HaEzer siman 126 regarding gittin that the get is invalid out of doubt, then regarding vows too one should be stringent in a Torah prohibition in accordance with the Rambam's view. Moreover, the view of Tosafot at the end of Perek Konam also appears to follow the Rambam, for they wrote: in the latter clause, where he knew it was intercalated and vowed until Adar, it says that an unspecified Adar is the second Adar, for the intercalated month is the first Adar and not the second; know this, for the first Adar has thirty days and the second twenty-nine, and furthermore Megillah and Purim are in the second, which shows that the first is the intercalated month; end quote. Since they wrote "know this," it follows that they hold as halachah that an unspecified Adar is Adar II. Mahari Weil likewise wrote in Dinim ve-Halachot siman 5 that we say in Nedarim: one who vows until Adar, if he did not know the year was intercalated, means until the first Adar, and if he knew the year was intercalated, until the second Adar; and here too he knew; end quote. So too in Responsa Maharam Mintz, end of siman 9: when he mentions Adar and knew of the year's intercalation, we say that he meant Adar II, since the first Adar is the intercalated month as explained; we say that since this person did not specify, he spoke of the ordinary Adar, namely Adar II; end quote. He further brought there the language of the Simanei Or Zarua: regarding one who rents a house to another until Adar, if this one says until the first Adar and that one says until the second, they divide between them; end quote. He wrote about this that it requires study from the end of Perek Konam. To me it appears that the Or Zarua was in doubt whether the halachah follows Rabbi Meir, as the Rambam and his supporters wrote, or Rabbi Yehudah, as the Rosh and his supporters wrote, and therefore ruled that they divide. Accordingly, with two documents made in an intercalated year too, it appears as halachah that one does not give all to the one written simply "Adar," but they divide, like the law of two documents dated the same day, as explained in Choshen Mishpat siman 104 seif 10. Also in Responsa Maharam Mintz siman 3 he brought the Rambam's words and wrote: since Abaye answers this way, and a baraita is also taught this way, it therefore appears to me that it is good to write explicitly "Adar I," etc. In any event, certainly at the outset one should write explicitly in documents "the first Adar" according to everyone, and so wrote Responsa Maharam Mintz there; so too in Hagahot Minhagim regarding the laws of the four parshiyot, siman 44, unlike what appears from the words of the master and the Levush in Orach Chayim at the beginning of siman 428. According to this, when blessing the month of the first Adar, one should say explicitly "Adar I" and not simply "Adar," as some cantors practice. Mahari Weil likewise wrote at the end of the laws of Purim that when blessing these two months in synagogue, they call the first Adar I and the second Adar II; and so one writes in gittin, ketubot, and documents; end quote. And so is the custom.
