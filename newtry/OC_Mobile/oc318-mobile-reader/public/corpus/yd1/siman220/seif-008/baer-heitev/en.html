The end. Bach writes that 'until half of Adar,' or until the twentieth or twenty-first of Adar, is treated like 'until Adar.' In a leap year he remains prohibited until the end of the second Adar, because the two Adars are treated as one extended month for this purpose. Shach.
According to Rambam. Rambam treats unspecified Adar as the second Adar, unless the person did not know the year was intercalated and presumably intended the Adar nearest Shevat. Shach notes that elsewhere Rama treats unspecified Adar as the first, and therefore advises stringency in Torah-level vows. Contemporary documents distinguish Adar I and Adar II explicitly.
