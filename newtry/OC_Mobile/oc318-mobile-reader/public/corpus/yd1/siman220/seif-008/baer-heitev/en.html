And according to the Rambam. (Who holds that unspecified Adar is the second Adar, only he holds that whoever did not know the year was intercalated presumably intended the Adar adjacent to Shevat.) Shach wrote: it requires investigation, for in Choshen Mishpat siman 43 se'if kaf chet and in Orach Chaim siman 428 and in Even HaEzer siman 126 se'if zayin, Rabbenu Yerucham wrote without specification that unspecified Adar is the first Adar, and he did not bring the view of the Rambam at all. And for the practical law it appears one should be stringent also in vows in Torah prohibition like the view of the Rambam. And according to this, in two documents made in an intercalated year it appears for halachah that one does not give to one in which Adar is written without specification — rather they divide, like the law of two documents whose term is one day, as is written in Choshen Mishpat siman 104 se'if yud. And also when we bless the month in the synagogue we call the first Adar Alef and the second Adar Bet, and so we write in gittin, ketubot, and documents. And so Maharil wrote, and such is the custom — end quote:
