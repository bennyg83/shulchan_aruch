And if it was intercalated, etc. From the words of Semag in Choshen Mishpat siman 312 se'if kaf bet it appears explained: this is specifically when standing in the middle of the year or before; but when standing on Rosh HaShanah and said one year, he is forbidden only twelve months, for otherwise it should have said "this year." But when he does not stand on Rosh HaShanah he cannot say "this year," for then it would imply only until Rosh HaShanah. And in truth it means a complete year — see there. And it appears to me also the view of Ran is thus, for in Nedarim at the beginning of page 61 he wrote plainly that if he said one year and stands on Rosh HaShanah he is not forbidden because of it (and as is evident there in the Gemara according to his explanation, and it appears there he has no other explanation in the Gemara except this). And likewise he wrote afterward on page 63 that the main novelty of the tanna is that even if he remembered from the beginning of the year, since he said "the year" we do not say he said one year but he is forbidden in it and in its intercalation — that is, thirteen months, etc. And what he wrote at the end of his words "and I strengthen you further to say that even one who vows one year without specification is forbidden in its intercalation, for presumably 'this year' implies," etc. — deals with standing in the middle of the year, which deals above shortly in it, and to exclude the words of Rashba he brought there. So it appears clear to me in the words of Ran — see there in Ran himself, and investigate, for Beit Yosef brought it briefly. And unlike Semag there who wrote: even though in Ran it appears he does not distinguish thus, etc. And unlike Bach who wrote "and Ran already elaborated," etc. — therefore if he stood on Rosh HaShanah whether he said "this year" or "one year" or "year" without specification or "the year," he is forbidden thirteen months, etc. I again found in the responsum of Rabbi Aharon Sasson at the end of siman 452 who debated the words of Ran and left them in difficulty. And in my humble opinion the matter is clear as I wrote. However, where he said one year from this day we count thirteen months even if he stands on Rosh HaShanah, as Ran wrote (also what Bach wrote: but standing in winter and said "this year" — he is forbidden only until Tishrei and also forbidden in the intercalary month; and if he stood in Nisan it is obvious that since the intercalary month passed at the time he vowed there is no distinction between an intercalated year and a non-intercalated year, and between whether he said one year or "the year" or "year" — he is forbidden only twelve months — end quote). It is explained from his words that "the year" is like "this year." And likewise afterward. However, it certainly appears that when he stood in winter and said one year or "the year" or "year" — he is forbidden thirteen months even if he did not know the year was intercalated, etc. — end quote — his words require investigation, for certainly "the year" is like "this year," as is written in se'if tet. And likewise Rosh and Ran in Nedarim at the beginning of page 61 — in "the year" he is forbidden only until completion of the year — that is, until Rosh HaShanah. And so it is evident in the Gemara there regarding "evidently 'year' is like 'the year'" — and a day too is like "this day," etc. — see there (and so it is in Rashba and Ran on page 63 side 1 — see there).
