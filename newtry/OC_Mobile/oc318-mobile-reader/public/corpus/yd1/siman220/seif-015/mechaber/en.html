If he set a time for his son's feast and vowed "until my son's feast" — we are uncertain whether his time is considered fixed, and we follow it to be stringent. {Rama: And even if the son died before his feast arrived, he must wait until that time. And likewise if he vowed to do something until his son becomes bar mitzvah and the son died — he must wait until he would have been fit to be bar mitzvah (Terumat HaDeshen, siman 277). And see in Orach Chayim, siman 569, regarding an individual or community that accepted a fast on account of some trouble and it passed — whether they must complete their fast; and the same law applies to one who vowed to give charity or other vows (Or Zaru'a ruled that he is obligated to fulfill his vow). And it appears to me that all this is when they accepted upon themselves without specification; but if he articulated his words — for example, one who vowed and said, "If so-and-so the sick person lives, I will fast such-and-such," or "I will give such-and-such to charity" — if that person died, he is exempt from his vow, for behold he stipulated explicitly, and we do not require a double condition in vows, for speech is not less than the intention of the one who vows, as above, siman 218; and so it will be explained below, end of siman 232 (my humble opinion). And all the more so one who says, "I will give a sela to charity so that my son will live, and if he does not live I will not give" — even though it is worship with intent to receive reward, he is exempt if the son does not live (Beit Yosef, siman 259, in the name of Mahariach; and it is responsum of Maharil, siman 109; and see there).}
