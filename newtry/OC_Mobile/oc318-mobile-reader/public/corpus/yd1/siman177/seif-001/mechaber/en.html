That one may not accept “tzon barzel” from a Jew, and many laws of business dealings that are forbidden because of interest; it contains 40 se’ifim. We do not accept “tzon barzel” from a Jew — meaning, that he accepts from him 100 sheep, and the shearings and the offspring and the milk are [split] in the middle as a third or a quarter until a year or until 2 years, as they stipulated between them; and if the sheep die, the recipient pays their value — this is forbidden, for the owner of the sheep is close to profit and far from loss. Therefore, if the owner of the sheep accepted upon himself that if they became more expensive or cheaper, or were torn [by predators], they remain in his domain, this is permitted — and so too, anything similar; and the same law applies to one who accepts merchandise in such a manner (Tur). {Rama: And all this is only when the recipient has profit; but if he has no profit at all in what he accepts, there is no loan here at all, and even if he accepted upon himself all responsibility it is permitted, for it is permitted for an unpaid custodian to accept upon himself to be like a borrower (Beit Yosef and Ba’al HaTerumos in the name of the sages of Lunel, and the Ran in a responsum).}
