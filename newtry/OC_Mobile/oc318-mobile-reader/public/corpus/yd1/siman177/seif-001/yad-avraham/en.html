(Siman 177, seif 5, Rama's gloss) However, if he deviated and said, "I am acting for myself and not under the terms of the iska," he is regarded as a robber. It appears that he must say this before witnesses, as the Rama wrote in Choshen Mishpat, siman 292, seif 7, regarding one who uses a deposit. The reason is explained in the Mishneh LaMelech, chapter 12 of Hilchos Gezeilah, law 8, because a person does not render himself wicked. If so, the same applies here. This is also implied in a responsum of the Rashba cited by the Beit Yosef in Even HaEzer at the end of siman 95, that where one becomes a robber by misappropriating what is not his, he must say, "I am acting for myself," before witnesses. This resolves what the Be'er HaGolah left requiring further study in Choshen Mishpat there. The Taz wrote that if he did not say this at the time he deviated from the arrangement, he is not believed afterward to say, "I altered it for myself," because we maintain his presumption of proper conduct until that point. It appears that all the more so, if he says, "I misappropriated the funds and spent them for my own expenses," he is not believed, because a person does not render himself a robber.
