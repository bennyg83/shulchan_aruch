If he gives, etc. There 68b; and Tosafot there s.v. place. And Rashi HaChadash, etc., explained: if they were, etc. And at first glance it implies for the giver's benefit he speaks, that it is included in the principal for the giver; and likewise explained in Tosafot of the Rosh. But Tur explained for the recipient's benefit he speaks, that if the recipient's produce were placed in his possession and the giver gives him money on their account he must assess according to what he would need to give in the market with porter wages; and he wrote: and all the more so if the recipient has, etc., as written in seif 22. And the Rambam explained in another manner, see there. (Likkut) If, etc. Rashi HaChadash's and Rif's and Shulchan Arukh's version in the baraita: a place where they practiced to raise porter wages for money, they raise for an animal, they raise to raise offspring with his labor wages and his food they raise. And Tosafot there in name of Rashi HaChadash; and Tur explains for the recipient's benefit as in the latter clause. Beit Yosef. And Tur wrote "if he gives," etc., as written here; and he wrote: and even if the iska were in the recipient's house he raises them at the market rate, etc.; and these are two explanations, the first explanation that Tur wrote; and here in Shulchan Arukh it is like the Rambam's explanation; and as Beit Chadash wrote the Rambam's words that whenever the recipient carries to the market to sell the iska, if it is customary to hire, etc., he must give, etc. And the second explanation is Rashi HaChadash's explanation of Tur's words, to carry from the merchandise market when he takes and carries to his house; and the first clause is when he carries to the market; and even though in Tosafot it is written from the giver's house and Tur wrote from the market, all one matter; and Beit Chadash and D.M. explained with one explanation; and in my humble opinion (end).
