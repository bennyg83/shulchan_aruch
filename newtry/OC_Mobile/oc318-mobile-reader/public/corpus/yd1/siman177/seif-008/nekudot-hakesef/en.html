(Shach, seif katan 60) In my humble opinion, the Perishah also maintains this view. The passage was written in abbreviated form, and it should read as follows: “In my humble opinion, the Perishah also maintains this view where the item remains intact in the possession of the second recipient of the gift, who must return it without compensation, as is evident from Choshen Mishpat, siman 356. The Perishah's statement that ‘that purchaser or recipient of the gift acquired it lawfully’ refers to the second recipient, and concerns a case in which the item no longer exists. In that case, the first recipient must pay, because the second recipient acquired it lawfully.” This is the correct text. Nevertheless, even on this reading, the Perishah's words are difficult. Since the owner of the investment property did not despair of recovering it, he may collect from whichever party he chooses, even though the second recipient did not know that it belonged to the investment. This is explained in Choshen Mishpat, siman 361: If one steals from another, gives the stolen item to a third party, and that party consumes it, the owner may collect either from the thief or from the one who consumed it, even though the latter did not know that it was stolen. See there. Accordingly, the owner may collect the item's value from the second recipient. The first recipient of the gift is exempt from reimbursing the second recipient, because the transfer was a gift, and a gift carries no warranty. So it appears in my humble opinion.
