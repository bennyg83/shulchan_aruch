And when they came to make the accounting, he did not wish to give him, etc. It appears to me that if the recipient already paid the giver his principal and profit, without receiving any compensation for his labor, he has no claim against him. This is avak ribbis, and even the tzon barzel arrangement of seif 1 was only close to profit and remote from loss; avak ribbis is not recoverable through beis din. However, as long as he has not yet returned the principal, he may retain from the principal the amount due for his labor. We do not say here that every removal without payment is considered collection, for that principle is stated only regarding a mortgage, in accordance with Rabbeinu Ephraim, whose view the Rama follows in siman 166, seif 3; the Beit Yosef likewise cites it at the beginning of this siman. If, however, he already returned the principal and profit without demanding compensation for his labor, and later demanded it, while the giver still owed him a separate debt, and he wished to deduct his labor compensation from that debt, it appears that he may not do so. Since he requested nothing when paying the principal, this constitutes a complete waiver. So it appears to me. See seif 21, where I wrote concerning one who arranged an iska in a forbidden manner.
