Rose. Mordechai wrote that they give the recipient based on his trustworthiness — if it seems to him that it rose so much they do not make him swear on a doubt, but rather tell him: remember that we rely on your trustworthiness. Beit David wrote on this: the reason is simple that they do not make him swear because he does not claim with certainty. Shach wrote that even if he claims with certainty, the lender is exempt without an oath if he says "I took it with permission," as above siman 169 seif 25. Taz wrote: and from this one may learn regarding one who does an iska with his fellow with permission until a certain time, and after that time the money remains in his hand as initially without making permission anew — the recipient cannot say "I will not give you profit after the stated time," since from the outset the money came to his hand with permission and certainly continues afterward as well; and it is a fortiori from the law mentioned here that he made it explicitly a loan — end of his words.
