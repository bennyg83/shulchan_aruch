Rose. The Mordechai writes that they give to the receiver according to his trustworthiness if it seems to him that it rose so much, and they do not impose an oath on him because of uncertainty. Rather, they say to him: remember that we rely on your trustworthiness. In Bedek HaBayit he writes that the reason is simple: they do not impose an oath on him because the claim against him is not certain. The Shach writes that even if the lender claims with certainty, he is exempt without an oath if he says, "I took it permissibly," as above siman 169 seif 25. The Taz writes: from this it appears to me to learn that if one makes an iska with his fellow permissibly until a certain time, and after that time the money remains in his hand as before without making a new heter, the receiver cannot say, "I will not give you profit after that time," since the money initially came into his hand permissibly, and that permission certainly continues after the time. This is a fortiori from the law stated here, where he explicitly made it into a loan.
