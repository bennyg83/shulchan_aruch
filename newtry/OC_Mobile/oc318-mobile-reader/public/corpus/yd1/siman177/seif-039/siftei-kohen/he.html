For perhaps what he buys for himself is not as good as the partnership, and his fellow is harmed thereby. Beit Yosef and the acharonim. Where there is a safek in the law or a dispute among poskim whether it is fixed ribbis or dust of ribbis, it is written in responsum of Rabbi Aharon Sasson siman 162 in the name of Maharam of Modena that we go stringently because it is a safek of Torah prohibition; and he disagrees, for it is a safek of money and leniently regarding "lest he swear"; unless most poskim agree it is fixed ribbis, which is a safek of Torah prohibition where we follow the majority; and so it appears from other acharonim. And it seems to me the court should tell him he is committing a prohibition and threaten him; and likewise one should be stringent for himself. But if he pays no heed, the court has no power to extract from him since it is a safek in the law. And at first glance it seems to me there is proof from what we say in the Talmud regarding priestly gifts, explained above siman 61 seif 23; and likewise below regarding firstborn siman 305 seif 13; and likewise in many places he is exempt due to "lest he swear" even though it is a positive Torah commandment; and see above siman 259 seif 14 — end of Shach's words.
