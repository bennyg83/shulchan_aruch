Beit Yosef and the later authorities. Where there is a doubt of law or a dispute among the poskim whether it is fixed ribbit or avak ribbit, it is written in responsum R. Aharon Sasson siman 162 in the name of Maharshal di Modena that we follow stringently, because it is a doubt of Torah prohibition. He disagreed, saying it is a monetary doubt and therefore lenient, since the burden of proof is on the claimant, unless most poskim agree that it is fixed ribbit, in which case it is a doubt of Torah prohibition and we follow the majority. So too it appears from the words of other later authorities. It appears to me that the court should tell him he is committing a prohibition in the matter and should warn him; and it is proper for him to be stringent for himself. But if he pays no attention to all this, the court has no power to extract from him, since it is a doubt of law. At first glance I have proof from what we say in the Gemara regarding priestly gifts, and as explained above in siman 61 seif 23, and below regarding a firstborn in siman 305 seif 13, and in several places, that he is exempt because the burden of proof is on the claimant, even though it is a positive commandment of the Torah.
