Beit Yosef and the later authorities. Where there is a doubt in the law or a dispute among the poskim whether a particular payment constitutes fixed interest or merely the semblance of interest, Responsa R' Aharon Sasson, siman 162, cites Maharam di Modena as ruling stringently because it is a doubt concerning a Torah prohibition. R' Aharon Sasson disagrees and holds that it is a monetary doubt, in which we rule leniently for the possessor because the burden of proof rests upon the claimant, unless the majority of poskim agree that it constitutes fixed interest. In that case it is a doubt concerning a Torah prohibition and we follow the majority. This likewise appears from the words of other later authorities. It appears to me that the rabbinical court should tell him that he is committing a prohibition and should warn him strongly, and he himself should certainly act stringently. But if he disregards all of this, the rabbinical court has no power to extract money from him, since the matter remains a doubt in the law. At first glance there is proof from what the Gemara states concerning gifts to kohanim, as explained above in siman 61, seif 23, and below concerning a firstborn animal in siman 305, seif 13, and in many other places, where the possessor is exempt because the burden of proof rests upon the claimant, even though a Torah-level positive commandment is involved.
