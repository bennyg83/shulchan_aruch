Themselves. For perhaps what he buys for himself is not as good as the partnership and his fellow is harmed thereby. And where there is a safek in the law or a dispute among poskim whether it is fixed ribbit or dust of ribbit, it seems it is a safek of money and leniently regarding "lest he swear"; unless most poskim agree it is fixed ribbit, which is a safek of Torah prohibition where we follow the majority. And it seems to me the court should tell him he is committing a prohibition and threaten him; and likewise one should be stringent for himself. But if he pays no heed, the court has no power to extract from him since it is a safek in the law. And at first glance it seems to me there is proof from what we say regarding priestly gifts, explained above siman 61 seif 23; and likewise below regarding firstborn siman 305 seif 13; and likewise in many places he is exempt due to "lest he swear" even though it is a positive Torah commandment; and see above siman 259 seif 14; end of Shach's words. (The ribbit prohibition applies at the beginning of the loan even though the borrower will not give ribbit. Torat Emet siman 162. It is forbidden to lend at ribbit to an Israelite on condition to give to the poor or for other mitzvot; Gittin 30b. A rich borrower and poor lender; forbidden to borrow from him for the poor's benefit; there 245b. One who borrowed from his fellow on collateral and because of ribbit prohibition made for him something like an absolute sale even though they were worth more, and the lender promised that if afterward he wishes he will return and sell it to him as is customary among those who give profits; behold the lender does not wish to return it at all and claims he bought it, and this one claims it was done because of the prohibition; Tzitz Eliezer ruled siman 20: since the borrower gives him something customary from the addition of his money, this is called that he did his will and he is obligated to return his collateral; and see Maharam of Padua siman 123. Further he wrote there siman 21: one who borrowed on collateral as mentioned above, but when the days lengthened and the borrower did not come to redeem, the lender came to make an account with the borrower so he pay him the principal with profits and take his own; behold according to the account he owes him more than the collateral is worth; and this one says I will not return the collateral unless you pay me in full, for to me it is worth so much as you owe me; and this one says I will only give you the collateral's value and take it. He ruled the law is with the borrower, since initially the lender did not lend him except less than the collateral's value; he revealed his intent that he wished to be secure on the principal and also on the surplus that would come through heter iska and not through prohibition; and if he takes more it is certainly forbidden; see there; and see in Kuntres Aharon of Levush that he copied from Semag the regulations from the ledger of the lands regarding ribbit.)
