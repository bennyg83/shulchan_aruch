(Siman 177 seif 5 in the gloss) However, if he changed and said "I am doing for myself and not as an iska," he is like a robber. And it appears he must say so before witnesses, as the gloss wrote in Choshen Mishpat siman 292 seif 7 regarding one who uses a deposit; and the reason is explained in Maggid Mishneh chapter 12 of Laws of Robbery law 8, because a person does not make himself wicked — and if so, the same applies here; and likewise in the Rashba's responsum that Beit Yosef brings in Even HaEzer siman 95, where he is a robber and misappropriates what is not his, he must say "behold I am doing for myself" before witnesses, and thereby what Baer HaGolah wrote in doubt in Choshen Mishpat there is resolved. And what Taz wrote that if he did not say so at the time of the change he is not believed afterward to say "for myself I changed," for we establish him in a presumption of fitness until now — and it appears all the more so if he says he misappropriated and spent for his needs he is not believed, for a person does not make himself a robber.
