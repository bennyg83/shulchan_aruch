And it is difficult — for behold this is the Rambam's words; and the Rambam himself afterward wrote in chapter 6 of Laws of Agents: whoever the dealer's profit exceeds his loss — even in less than a sixth it is permitted even if he has no other business. Beit Yosef answered: here it deals when they did not stipulate at the beginning of the partnership any condition, but when they came to make an account he said to him if there is profit a third or a tenth shall be in your wages. And according to what Beit Yosef raised afterward that a sixth is not needed — it appears to explain Tur's words that wrote "or similar to this that he gives him a dinar all the days of the iska's duration" — it was very difficult to explain, for it implies this is the law of one who did not fix with him — and he already wrote before this that this law is for one who fixes initially; and also the Rif and Rosh wrote this enactment of two-thirds in profit and half in loss for one who fixes initially. And Mahari Weil explains Tur's words in this "he gives him a dinar" — meaning what Shmuel said in the Gemara "he fixes for him a dinar"; and Shmuel deals with one who gives an iska and did not fix labor wages — and it is not clear; for if so what did Shmuel challenge on Rav — he did not find "permitted a third" he goes home empty? For behold he already found according to this that the iska was already made. Rather simple that Shmuel deals before he gave him the iska, as Rashi and Tosafot explained; and besides, it is not Tur's way to disagree with Rashi and Tosafot on his own. And Beit Yosef explained that Tur deals in that "or similar to this" — he has another business; and it is difficult for afterward Tur divides in this; and further difficult is the language "or similar to this" that has no explanation. And in my humble opinion for the law mentioned in the mishnah — he already made the iska and at the time of division claims labor wages — he must give him as an idle worker as above; and likewise if he already accepted it even though he has not yet dealt with it. But if it is before he accepts the iska from him he may stipulate with him as he wishes even with one dinar and does not need the Sages' enactment — since there is a distinguishing mark that he takes a share in appreciation more than in loss and he descended with that intent. But in the first two divisions we mentioned here a fixed matter does not help since he already accepted the business; and all the more if he already did them — in the law mentioned in the mishnah it is like an idle worker except the Talmudic sages made an enactment for this — namely Rava's law in the Gemara: two-thirds in profit and half in loss. And Tur's opinion is not specifically in this manner but in every manner that the dealer profits in profit more than he loses in loss — and as Beit Yosef wrote explicitly in explanation of R' Yitzchak's and Rosh's words: rather like that incident of Rava — meaning to take from the dealer more profit; however even with a dinar it suffices — end of his words. And these are exactly the things Tur wrote "or similar to this that he gives him a dinar" — meaning in the style he mentioned before that he takes more share in profit; on this he said not specifically so but similar to this that he takes more in profit even a tenth share in a manner that he receives only one dinar all the days of the partnership's duration — it suffices with this provided he accepts more in profit than in loss; and he holds he will profit greatly and in that benefit he toils willingly on the deposit share. And this matter Tur wrote "he gives him a dinar" applies to all he mentioned above from it — meaning also if he gives him a third in wages — for there too it is possible he will receive only a dinar. Thus appears the clear explanation of Tur's words and everything comes out correctly; also it agrees with the Rosh who wrote the enactment of two-thirds in profit for one who fixes initially — Tur explains it that he fixes initially before he deals; but in truth he already accepted the business to his hand as we said; and the reason is that if before accepting the business a fixed amount even small would suffice even though for profit and loss they are equal — nevertheless what he fixed suffices with intent that he descended for this business; not so when he already accepted the business and does not wish to retract — and there is a difference between them in labor wages; for this the Sages' enactment was needed. Thus it appears in my humble opinion. And in Derishah he elaborated greatly in explaining Tur's words and testified on himself that according to his explanation there is great strain in Tur's language; and according to what I wrote there is no strain here in my humble opinion.
