And see siman 173, this is the correct reading. There, seif 4, this law and its reason are explained. The rule is that whenever there is a definite debt and another undertakes to discharge it for a lesser amount, it is forbidden. Therefore, the Beit Yosef cites the Rashba here concerning Reuven, who mortgaged his house to Shimon for 1,000 gold coins for five years, and then gave Levi 500 gold coins in exchange for Levi's undertaking to release the house from Shimon's lien by the end of the mortgage term. This is forbidden, and we do not say that perhaps Levi will persuade Shimon to accept less. End quote. The reason is that the debt is definite, unlike royal service, which is merely a possible coercive demand, as explained above in siman 173. The Levush distinguishes here between a king and another person, but this is imprecise.
