This too is stated there. He writes that we do not say that because, according to the claimant, he is wicked, he cannot be believed under oath.
The same is written in Choshen Mishpat, siman 82, seif 10. In my humble opinion, this does not disagree with what was written above at the end of siman 169. There, he claims that he took the money permissibly and specifies the basis of permission, namely that he took it through a non-Jew, and he is believed by analogy to the law of a prozbul. Here, however, and in siman 82, he denies everything and does not claim that he took it permissibly, so an oath is administered. As for Rama's words, "and some disagree," I did not merit to see his source in Darkei Moshe.
