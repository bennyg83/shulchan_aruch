That he give him for it etc. Bach wrote: specifically when he stipulated that he give for it that amount immediately at that time — 100 gold coins; but if the condition was that if he does not give him the merchandise by half-year he will give him 100 gold coins after the close of the year — it is full ribbit, for it is payment for waiting; and this is explained in the responsum of Rivash siman 335, and many stumble in this and have nothing to rely on, for it is obvious that this is forbidden — end of his words. And I studied Rivash there, and not only did I not find it stated there, I did not find even any implication of this; and I believe he intended what Rivash wrote there: “and at once upon passing the time-limit he becomes obligated in all the addition, and this is not called ribbit but a penalty and is permitted,” etc. — for he held the obligation to pay takes effect immediately all at once; but this is not compelling, for one can say the penalty obligation takes effect immediately; and therefore if they arranged between them that if he does not give him the merchandise by half-year he will give him 100 gold coins at the close of the year, and the lender is obligated to wait until the close of the year — in this case it is reasonable that it is full ribbit, and this ruling needs no lengthening. At the end of time, he commits at once in all the additions and is not called a rabbi, but a fine and permitted to pay immediately at once, and does not need enough to do so immediately and immediately if he does not give him half a year, he will give him the same amount as the year and the loan is committed to waiting for the entire year
And he buys according to the market rate etc. Bach wrote: but not at less than its value according to the market rate, and this is obvious; and as explained above siman 173 seif 7 s.k. 18.
