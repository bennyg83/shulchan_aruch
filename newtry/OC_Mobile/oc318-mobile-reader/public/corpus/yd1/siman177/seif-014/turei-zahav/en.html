And they fixed an amount between themselves. Meaning, the entire sum would be a loan rather than an iska, which according to some opinions in siman 166, seif 2, constitutes fixed ribbis. Nevertheless, he may take the fixed amount if it equals half the profit, since the money originally came into his possession permissibly, and he continued dealing with those funds until now without using them for his own needs. From this it appears to me that where one makes a permitted iska with his fellow until a specified time, and after that time the money remains in the recipient's hands as before without executing a new heter, the recipient cannot say, 'I will not give you profit for the period after that date.' Since the money initially came into his hands permissibly, that arrangement certainly continues afterward as well. This follows all the more so from the present law, where they explicitly converted it into a loan. See also what I wrote concerning this in the responsum cited in seif 23 of this siman.
