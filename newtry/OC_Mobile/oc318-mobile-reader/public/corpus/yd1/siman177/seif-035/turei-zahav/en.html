Two-thirds of the share that he would have earned. It seems the Rambam who is master of this law wrote thus according to what we say in the Gemara: if half in profit; two-thirds in loss; if half in loss; two-thirds in profit. Rashi explains the first clause refers to the giver and the latter to the recipient; and the Rambam explains everything regarding the recipient; meaning in the first clause if they stipulated he take half in profit and did not stipulate in loss; the law is he takes two-thirds in loss. Explanation: calculate the loss as if it were profit and from that sum he accepts two-thirds; by way of example if the loss is twenty-four gold coins we say; if this sum were profit the recipient would take twelve; now he accepts damage of eight gold coins; and likewise always according to value. For example a quarter in profit for the recipient; we say if there will be damage the recipient accepts two-thirds of it, meaning four; and if he wrote he takes three parts in profit and the giver a quarter, damage comes upon the recipient twelve. This law if it is explicit in the document which share he takes in profit and the loss share was not mentioned; but if half loss; meaning it is written in the document the recipient takes half in loss and his share if there is profit was not mentioned; we say two-thirds in profit, meaning if there is profit of twenty-four gold coins he takes two-thirds of all the profit, meaning sixteen gold coins. But one should not explain two-thirds in profit here from the sum that would accrue to him if there were damage as we explained in the first clause two-thirds in loss; for then the recipient would take only eight gold coins in profit; and this is a prohibition that specifically the recipient must take more in profit than his loss share. But if a quarter in loss is written on the recipient and the profit share was not mentioned otherwise; two-thirds in profit, meaning we say if there were loss the giver would accept loss of three parts, meaning eighteen gold coins; that sum we calculate in profit now and the recipient takes two-thirds of those eighteen gold coins, meaning twelve gold coins which is half; thus is the primary version and not versions that read "he takes a third"; for that version is according to Ramban, who holds whether for profit or loss we follow the recipient's share; and it results at the end of this seif the recipient would take only eight gold coins. But the Rambam does not hold so; rather in this it is not proper that he take only eight gold coins and the giver take sixteen gold coins; therefore we calculate here as the giver's share from which he takes two parts, meaning twelve gold coins; and this is according to his loss share and the addition of his fellow's third. And the rule in this: regarding loss we follow only the share accruing to the loan; for the recipient pays only a third of the loss accruing to his share; and regarding profit we follow the deposit share; meaning the recipient takes another third beyond what accrues to the deposit share in addition to what accrues to his share. The reason is explained in Kesef Mishneh in chapter 6 of Hilchos Sheluchin: and never does the recipient lose except a third of the loss affecting his share; and the other third the giver assists him; and in his compensation for his labor; and never does the giver take except two-thirds of the profit affecting; the other third he gives the recipient as his compensation for his labor; and likewise always according to this value whenever loss is mentioned in less than half. However if it is written that he accept three parts in loss; then one cannot say at all two-thirds in profit; rather he takes in profit according to the value above according to his loss share and the addition of a third in his fellow's share; and it results he takes twenty gold coins from the profit.
