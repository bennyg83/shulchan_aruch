And if he violates this, he shall give 100 gold coins in exchange for it. Since it does not say that he must pay at that time, namely at the end of six months, it implies that we are not concerned if he gives the 100 gold coins only later. The reason is that the additional payment is taken as a penalty, not as compensation for waiting. My father-in-law, of blessed memory, wrote that this applies only where he stipulated with the seller that if he did not supply the merchandise within six months, he must immediately pay 100 gold coins for it at that time. If, however, they stipulated that if he did not supply it within six months, he would pay 100 gold coins only at the end of one year from today, this is full ribbis, for it is compensation for waiting. He writes that this is explicit in the Rivash's responsum, that many stumble in this matter, and that they have no basis upon which to rely. It appears to me, however, that there is not even a trace of prohibition here. I do not know where he found this in the Rivash. On the contrary, the Rivash writes that since there is no ribbis at all in fixing the price of the merchandise, the additional stipulation is likewise not ribbis. Had he supplied the merchandise at the end of six months, there would have been no ribbis even if its price had risen. At the instant the deadline passes, he becomes liable all at once for the entire additional amount, and this is called a penalty, not ribbis. End quote. Thus, when the six months pass without delivery of the merchandise, the penalty becomes due. What difference is there whether he pays it then or later? There is no compensation for waiting, since the only sum being delayed is the penalty that already became due upon the expiration of six months. He does not pay more because he has not yet received even his principal. Compensation for waiting applies only where paying earlier would benefit the borrower and delaying payment causes him to pay more, so that the lender's money increases with each day of delay. Here, once six months have passed, later delivery of the merchandise no longer helps; he is liable for 100 gold coins. There is therefore no difference whether he pays those 100 gold coins one day after the six months or much later. In my opinion, there is no prohibition.
