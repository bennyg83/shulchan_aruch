(Likkut) One who gives, etc. This follows the first textual version in the Rosh. It is also our version in the Gemara, and the version of the Rif, Rambam, and the other poskim. (End.)
Each one, etc. See Rashi there, s.v. rather, etc., and, 'I heard,' etc. Rambam explains according to the latter interpretation that Rashi reports having heard. Tur rules in accordance with both interpretations, and Nimukei Yosef rules likewise.
And in a place where there is no custom, etc. This refers to a place where no custom had been established. Rambam likewise wrote this, as indicated by the statements concerning Rav Menashya, etc., and, 'another place,' etc. The baraita was not raised as an objection to him, which shows that the baraita was taught at a later time.
(Extract) And from then on, etc., and if he comes, etc., what is written "and if he comes," etc., explains what is written above "and from then on," etc., specifically when he divided before three, etc.; Taz and so Beit Yosef and so Rambam and Rashba in name of Rashi; but Tosafot disagree and write even division without appraisal stipulation he takes half-and-half, etc.; and so in baraita that teaches anonymously "from here onward," etc.; and so in Yerushalmi; and what Abaye said "who disagrees with you," etc., two matters he said: first to nullify division, second to exempt entirely. And Shulchan Aruch follows Rashi and Rambam, unlike Rosh and Bach in last commentary who forced.
He must inform him, etc. Tur, according to the interpretation of some commentators in Tosafot there, 70b, s.v. in such a case.
Or to assess, etc. Rambam; and as written "who," etc.; and as written 31b.
And if not, etc., and it is not, etc. See Rashi there s.v. who, etc.; and Nimukei Yosef wrote from his explanation it appears that for each reason alone it suffices that they divide equally; and likewise is the Rambam's and Tur's opinion. But in Tosafot they said that even without division at all and without informing, he takes half of his fellow's share; and as written "from an unspecified case from here onward he takes," etc.; and what is written "who," etc., and further, etc., two matters: "who," etc., and the division is nothing; and further "place," etc., and you may not take half of your fellow's share; and likewise we say in Yerushalmi: a person who gave a hundred dinars to his fellow and he profited twenty dinars, he took for himself ten dinars and the other became principal on him; people do not do so, they give and at the end they divide; and the Sages taught; and since the custom is so, even according to Tosafot he does not take half of his fellow's share unless he assessed, for they partnered with intent of the custom; and likewise siman 174; and likewise in the gloss like Tur's and Rashi's opinion. But in Shulchan Arukh it is written "the division is nothing" like Tosafot's opinion; and even though Rashba concludes "since they practiced," etc., this is in a place where they practiced.
