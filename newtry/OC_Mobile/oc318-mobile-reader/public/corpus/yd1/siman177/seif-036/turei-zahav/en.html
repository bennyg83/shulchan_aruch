He shall take nothing. The reason is that his compensation was initially dependent upon earning a profit, and no profit was earned. In the latter clause, however, they stipulated to divide profit and loss equally, and he must receive compensation as a worker. From the outset he relied upon that compensation. Had there been profit, his worker's compensation would first have been taken from the common funds, and they would have divided the remainder. Now that there is no profit, he nevertheless does not lose his worker's compensation and may take it from the principal. Since he must also replenish his share of the loss from the principal, he takes only one-half of his compensation from the principal and loses the remainder. This is explained in the responsum of the Ra'avad cited by the Beit Yosef. The Levush supplied another reason of his own, but it is entirely incorrect.
