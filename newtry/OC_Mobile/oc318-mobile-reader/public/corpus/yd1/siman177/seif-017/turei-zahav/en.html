The orphan is believed. I do not know how to explain this orphan's special credibility. If he is an adult orphan, how does he differ from any other person? If he is a minor orphan, he is not capable of presenting a legal claim at all, as explained in Choshen Mishpat, siman 110. The Levush explains that the case is where the borrower borrowed from the guardian and undertook at the time of the loan to support the orphan. When the orphan came to collect the debt, the borrower claimed that the support he had provided should be deducted, while the orphan claimed that he had received nothing. The orphan is believed without an oath, since the borrower's obligation to him is established and he must pay. His claim that he fed him is not a claim against this debt, for the orphan was not his opposing party regarding that matter. Perhaps he supported him for the sake of the mitzvah, perhaps the guardian repaid him, or perhaps it was merely a form of avak ribbis, and the orphan need not answer him concerning it. End quote. These words have no sound basis. First, according to this explanation, there is no relevance to describing the orphan as believed, since he does not prevail because his statement is credited over that of one who contradicts him. Further, if he stipulated this when borrowing from the guardian, we must follow that stipulation and it stands. Above, siman 160, seif 18, the Rama cites this law, and the Levush gives another explanation there that is likewise not praiseworthy, as I wrote there. I therefore say that anyone who seeks to grant an orphan greater legal strength than another person where his claim is contradicted must explain this law and bring clear proof for his interpretation, for the burden rests upon one who departs from the ordinary rule.
