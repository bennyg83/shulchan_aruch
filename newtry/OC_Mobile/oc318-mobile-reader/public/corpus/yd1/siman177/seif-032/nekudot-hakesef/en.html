(In Shach, seif katan 60) In my humble opinion, the Perishah also maintains this, etc. The wording was abbreviated. It should read: In my humble opinion, the Perishah likewise holds that where the property remains intact in the possession of the second recipient, he must return it without payment, as is evident from Choshen Mishpat, siman 356. The statement that the purchaser or recipient acquired it lawfully refers to the second recipient, and concerns a case where the property no longer exists. In that case the first recipient must pay, because the second recipient acquired it lawfully. This is the correct reading. Nevertheless, even this explanation leaves difficulty in the Perishah's words. Since the owner of the investment did not despair of recovery, he may collect from whichever party he chooses, even though the second recipient did not know that the property belonged to an investment partnership. As explained in Choshen Mishpat, siman 361, if one steals from another, gives the item to a third party, and that party consumes it, the owner may collect from either one, even though the consumer did not know it was stolen. Accordingly, he may collect the value from the second recipient. The first recipient is exempt from reimbursing the second recipient, because it was a gift and gifts carry no warranty. So it appears in my humble opinion.
