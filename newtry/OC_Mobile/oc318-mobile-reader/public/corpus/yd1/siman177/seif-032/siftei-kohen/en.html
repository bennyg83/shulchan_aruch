He gave etc. Specifically gave, for he should not have received a gift from him since it is not his; but if the agent sold — the money owner cannot extract it from them, for every iska may be sold and with permission he purchased from him — Perishah.
Obligated to pay — the first giver; and if he has nothing the recipient must pay the recipient-of-gift from him as a gift — not the buyer or recipient of gift with permission who bought from him or received gift from him, for that buyer or gift-recipient with permission bought from him not knowing it was from the issur. Perishah. And Bach in last commentary wrote: meaning even though the gift is not in the recipient-of-gift's hand but he changed and exchanged for another thing or sold or gave to others or lost — the recipient-of-gift is obligated from the recipient to pay the giver even though he did not know he gave from issur, for it is like a thief who paid a debt or credit that must be returned free (as explained Choshen Mishpat siman 356 seif 6); and all the more a thief who gave as gift; and it appears the same when the recipient gave to others cheaply to hide the issur from the giver — his law is like a thief on whom market regulation was made if buyer did not know it was from issur; if he knew his law is like buying from a known thief as above; and from Perishah's words it appears he did not understand thus and I do not know his reason — end; and in my humble opinion Perishah also holds thus, and what he wrote "that buyer or gift-recipient with permission bought," etc. — refers to the second case — examine.
