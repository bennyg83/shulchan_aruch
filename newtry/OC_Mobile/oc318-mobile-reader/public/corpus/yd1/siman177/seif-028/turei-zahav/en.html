The Tur writes that even if the merchandise was already in the recipient's house, he values it at the market price. This exceeds the principal by the amount of the porter's wage that would have been required to bring it to the recipient's house, and the recipient takes that amount first. End quote. It appears that the meaning is as follows. It goes without saying that if the recipient has not yet paid the porter's wage and the expense still lies before him, the iska may not be arranged in a manner that requires him to bear that expense. The Tur teaches that even if he already paid it before the iska was made, or personally carried the merchandise to his house, one might have thought it is permitted because he now need not spend anything from his pocket and has waived the earlier expense. A person feels more distress over an expense still before him than over money already spent. The Tur therefore teaches that this is not so; the giver must reimburse him, and the recipient may place that sum into his own pocket. If, however, the merchandise reached the recipient's house without expense or effort, and he sells it without expense, why should the giver pay him an expense gratuitously? The requirement exists only so the recipient not suffer a loss; he need not profit from it. So it appears to me. I have seen other explanations, but they are not clear to me.
