The giver, etc. It is the Mechaber's error and he erred in this; and it is neither the Rif's opinion nor Tur's opinion as above; and this is the Rambam's language there law 3: and further the Sages enacted that whoever gives money to his fellow to deal with them and they decreased or increased and he did not wish to give him his labor wages every day and they did not stipulate any condition that the dealer's wages on half the deposit shall be a third, etc.; and the Mechaber added of his own mind "and if he had a business," etc., and erred a great error as above; and the arrangement of the Rambam's language misled him that he thought the Rambam's words apply to all the above in seif 2, and it is not so, as written above.
The wages shall be, etc. Rambam; and he holds that what is written half in profit, etc., the choice is in the dealer's hand; but Rashi in chapter HaMekabel (Bava Metzia 104b) s.v. one iska wrote that in an unspecified case it is half in loss and two-thirds in profit; and likewise the Rif there and Rosh and Ramban and Rashba and Semag and Shulchan Arukh; and likewise is primary as written half loan and half deposit, meaning regarding liability whether accidents or depreciation; and in profit he takes more than his labor wages; and there are other methods, the Rambam's teachers' opinion to the contrary: half in profit and two-thirds in loss; and the Rambam already wrote that it is money; and further Raavad's opinion that the choice is in the giver's hand, and the Rashba's students already rejected that. (Likkut) The giver, etc. As written above that there are many methods here; and Rashi's method is the clear one; and likewise Semag and Rashba and Shulchan Arukh compelled from what is written in chapter HaMekabel "this iska half loan," etc., evidently in an unspecified case liability is on both equally for loss; and a fifth method, Maharam's method: if it is written explicitly in the document half in profit and in loss, see there (end).
