(Taz, seif katan 15) We do not administer an oath to him on an uncertain claim, etc. See Shach, seif katan 20, that even if the claim against him is definite, no oath is administered.
(Shach, seif katan 23) There is an error there, and the text should read as follows. The glosses of Mordechai there state that the Bedek HaBayit writes: The reason is simple, because he does not assert the claim with certainty. Nevertheless, if he wishes to impose an unspecified ban, he may do so. This applies when the lender claims against the borrower. But when the borrower claims against the lender concerning money already taken, it appears to me that even if the borrower’s claim is certain, the lender is exempt without an oath if he says, “I took it permissibly,” as explained above, siman 169, seif katan 73. This is the correct text.
