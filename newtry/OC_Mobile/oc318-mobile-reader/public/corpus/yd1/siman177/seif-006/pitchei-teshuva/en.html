See Baer Heitev; and see in Dagul MeRevavah who wrote that Bach's words here — that if the recipient wishes, etc. — contradict his words in Choshen Mishpat, etc., and he concludes therefore it seems to me that even if it were written in the Rosh's responsum that it is forbidden to take profit as Bach quoted there, nevertheless it applies only to the beginning of the Rosh's words where he combined ribbis with the principal; but where he wrote only the principal and stipulated on the iska orally, it is forbidden only to do so initially, but if he transgressed and did so, not only is it permitted to accept if the borrower gives willingly, but even the borrower is obligated to give half the profit according to the condition — and you will know, etc. — see there. And behold, by chance I met with my honored friend the sharp rabbi Rabbi Shmuel, dayan in our community, who showed me his writings challenging Dagul MeRevavah's words, for in the Rosh's responsum klal 88 din 7 it is explicit that even if he wrote only the principal and the iska condition was oral, the giver is forbidden to take any ribbis; and the Rosh did not mention there at all initially the law of combining ribbis with the principal. And he elaborated on this and concluded that it is a full prohibition to take ribbis if he wrote in the document only the principal, even if the borrower wishes to give, as explicit in the Rosh's responsum there in klal 88 and 89 — see there. [And see in responsum Chemdat Shlomo siman 27 who also challenged Dagul MeRevavah on this — see there further.]
