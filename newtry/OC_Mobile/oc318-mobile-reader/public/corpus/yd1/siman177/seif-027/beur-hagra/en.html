If they stipulated, etc. Rambam; and as written "if half in profit," etc., "if," etc.; and it results that always he takes a sixth of all the wages more than his share which is as a loan; and likewise in loss the giver pays a sixth more which is a third of one of their shares. And the Rambam's teachers wrote that that third is calculated from the giver's share which is the deposit — and it is a third of his share both in wages and in loss; therefore in such a case where it is written here in Shulchan Arukh that they stipulated the dealer shall take a quarter — it results that the deposit share is three parts in loss — the giver pays all. And Ramban and Rashba hold the opposite: that the said third is calculated from the lender's share both in wages and in loss; therefore in such a case the dealer pays a sixth. But in the latter clause where they stipulated a quarter in loss he takes a third in profit which is his share and a third of his share. But the Rambam challenged his teachers that otherwise sometimes the giver will pay more than the loss — for example if they stipulated he take one of seven in profit and the loss is seven dinars — the giver will pay eight dinars and similar; and likewise he challenges Ramban and Rashba that sometimes the dealer will take more than the profit — for example they stipulated the giver lose one of seven and they profited seven dinars — the dealer must take eight dinars; therefore the Rambam explained that if they stipulated on profit, in loss the giver adds a third from his fellow's share which is a loan; and if they stipulated on loss, the dealer takes a third from his fellow's share which is the deposit; and this is what is written "he shall lose," etc., "he shall take," etc.; and likewise is the reasoning: in profit he takes the addition from his fellow's share; and likewise in loss the giver loses the surplus in his fellow's share — therefore we estimate everything according to his fellow's share. But Ramban and Rashba answer the Rambam's difficulty that never does the giver pay more than the loss or the profit; and likewise Choshen Mishpat siman 176 seif 6; and see there siman 93 seif 13 that the Rambam disagrees there and holds he pays from his own house. (Likkut) If they stipulated, etc. And another method for Semag — and Kesef Mishneh wrote the Rambam's reason: in profit he takes a third more from the deposit share — for that surplus he takes as labor wages on the deposit share; therefore he takes from the deposit share whether much or little according to the deposit share's portion — not so in loss where he takes nothing in profit except that from his labor wages he deducts from his loss so he does not lose so much — he deducts according to the recipient's loss in his share; so much he deducts a third in his loss so he never loses except two-thirds of the recipient's share. And on Beit Yosef siman 176 that incident of half in profit and a third, etc. — he does not hold that he should have a sixth extra in wages than in loss but something small; and see there in his words that he explained the Rif's and Rosh's words like the Rambam's and Tur's words — and his words do not appear correct (end).
