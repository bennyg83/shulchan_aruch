With any form of ribbis that is rabbinic, such as an iska. Meaning, he gave him funds as an iska and stipulated that he give him a fixed amount whether or not he profits, thereby placing himself at risk that he may take from him without there having been any profit, as explained above. Since all liability rests upon the giver, it is permitted. However, with fixed produce interest, where there is no iska but merely a loan of 100 for 200, accepting liability does not help. That arrangement is rabbinically forbidden because it may be confused with full Torah-level ribbis, namely where there is no acceptance of liability.
