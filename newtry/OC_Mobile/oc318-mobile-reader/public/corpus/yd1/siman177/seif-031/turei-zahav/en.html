Question: one received an iska from his fellow for a sum of money for a time, and after the payment time the recipient withheld the money with him and dealt with them and does not wish to give profit from what accrues after the payment time, because he says after that time the money was a deposit with him since he has no heter iska with him only until the time and afterward it would be ribbis what he gives him.
Another question in Maharam Mintz: one accepted on himself three hundred gold coins principal in an iska and to give profit to the document issuer from that iska one thousand gold coins at payment time — principal and profit on Rosh Chodesh Nisan; and if payment is delayed even partially, the signatory accepted from now to deal with that sum withheld with him as that iska and to give profits according to the profit rate above; and afterward payment was delayed some time and the giver came saying he give him profits from the profits — meaning from Rosh Chodesh Nisan it became five hundred gold coins principal and he should give him profits also from the thousand gold coins if the law is with him or not.
Answer to the first question: it seems clear the law is with the giver and he is obligated to give him profits also from the business after payment time; and the permission from the outset continues afterward as well until he informs after the time that he does not wish to receive further in the iska those monies; and proof for this from chapter Eizehu Neshekh (69a): the Sages taught — one who places an animal with his fellow until when is he obligated to tend the offspring — thirty days for a small animal and fifty for a large; from here onward he takes half his own and half of half of his fellow's. Rashi: if he wishes to tend them more than this time they assess them in money and what appreciates from here onward the recipient takes half his own share and in the second half which is his fellow's he takes half of the appreciation as he takes with a mother animal half the appreciation. Beit Yosef wrote in siman 177 in name of the Rashba's students: likewise one who takes money to deal for a time and profited and dealt, etc.; and the Rambam wrote chapter 8 of Laws of Partnership: he does not take half of his fellow's share unless he assessed the offspring and stipulated before three; and if he did not assess and stipulate before three — behold he waived and the offspring are divided equally. Tur in this siman did not write "assessed and stipulated" but wrote "and if he comes to divide he must inform his fellow," etc.; and Beit Yosef wrote they do not disagree and Tur cited one and the same applies to the other. Behold before us that in any event the recipient takes from the profit that accrues after the stated time — meaning thirty days or fifty for a large animal — and we do not say he is called iska recipient from his fellow only until that time and from here onward it shall be a deposit in his hand and he shall not take from profit after the time at all, for there is no iska here; perforce all the time the business is unspecified in his hand even though the time ended — nevertheless he withholds the business in his hand with the intent from the outset; except if he informed him — then a new iska is made and he acquires even in half his fellow's share. And Tosafot wrote there that even without any condition he takes half of his fellow's share, as Beit Yosef wrote there in name of the Rashba's students — because since from the outset as an iska he descended in it, meaning for half wages with intent that he tend profit he will always take in this matter — end of his words. Behold before you that the business from the outset was not completed with the time's completion but remained unspecified and even in profit; and further proof from chapter HaUmanin: Rava said — this one who accepted an iska from his fellow and lost and toiled and filled it and did not inform — he cannot say to him "rise from this loss with me." Meaning: you accepted half the loss upon yourself and for wages take a third according to the law of iska recipient from his fellow — and it results he diminishes from the principal; for he said to him therefore you toiled and filled it lest you be called one who ruins an iska. Rashi: you were ashamed — therefore you toiled to fill the principal since you did not inform initially; so too in our case, since from the outset he accepted the money as an iska unspecified he is ashamed to say that after the time he withholds the money with him and does not return them to their owners and their owners lose that they have expense in their homes and no profit and their principal is destroyed; and it is a fortiori — there with one who ruins an iska there is no negligence upon him for Heaven withheld him that he wished to profit willingly — nevertheless he is ashamed in this matter; all the more so here who withholds his fellow's money with him through negligence not according to law after the time — certainly he is ashamed in this and worries they will not give him to others in an iska; and certainly he dealt further for the iska's benefit as initially. And on the second question if there will be on the profit there principal after the time — it is explained in Yerushalmi that Nekudot Yosef brought in Perek Eizehu Neshekh in the plain sense: behold those two partners who did business together, etc.: money — as who divides it — since he does not need assessment, for what he left is as if he took — therefore he may divide by himself and take half his own and half of half his fellow's; and do not say for another partnership that requires assessment; and this is what Yerushalmi reads: a person who gave a hundred dinars to his fellow and he profited twenty dinars — he took for himself ten dinars and the other became principal on him; people do not do so — they give and at the end they divide; even though the law is so that if he comes to divide profit he divides — they do not practice so but carry and give and do not divide profit until the end — end of his words. Behold before you that there is profit on the principal; and this is at the iska time's completion as it implies from Beit Yosef's words that he brought in siman 177 in name of the Rashba's students this custom that the recipient may not take profit from profit; and it was mentioned there that he accepted the money for a time, etc. And behold in the case before us regarding that the giver shall take profit from profit — he mentioned explicitly in the document's wording that from today he accepts upon himself to deal with what will be withheld in his hand even part of the stated payment; and payment was already mentioned there both on principal and on profit; and on this he said that what will be withheld as iska in his hand — it appears in such a case there is no Yerushalmi custom; for the Yerushalmi spoke only of a recipient who has no explicit condition when he will divide and count profit as principal — not so with the giver, since he mentioned explicitly that there shall be considered a new iska in his hand since it is written explicitly even part of the payment.
