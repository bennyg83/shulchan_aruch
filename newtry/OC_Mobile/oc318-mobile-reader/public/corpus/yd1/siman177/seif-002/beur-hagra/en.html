The giver, etc. This entire seif is the language of the Rambam and so is correct. And behold what is written "and if he had a business," etc., and likewise "if," etc., "since," etc., is as written seif 9: Rav said permitted a third, etc.; rather Shmuel said he fixes, etc.; and as the first authority there, for Rav said, etc., and he holds Shmuel agrees in this manner, for it was stated in this context; and he explains what is written "permitted a third," etc., meaning from what is permitted on the principal a third shall be yours, meaning a third of the wages is yours permitted, and he need not give him as an idle worker; and Shmuel disagrees on this lest he have no wages at all; and likewise he disagrees with Rava's words there "half in profit," etc., for Rava speaks according to the halachah that the halachah is like Rav, and as the Rif wrote that we rule like Rav in prohibitions; and "permitted a third" he explains not specifically so but less as well; and he explains that all this is when he did not fix for him from the outset but when they come to divide he gives him, and as written in seif 3 "in what case," etc.; and likewise holds the Rambam as written below; and likewise Shmuel's words that Rav agrees with him that all the more so in such a case it is permitted; and also even if he did not fix from the outset, since he has another business; and likewise one could challenge Shmuel according to his opinion, except he could say Shmuel holds like Rabbi Yehudah even if he did not dip, etc.; but to the conclusion it is even according to Rabbi Meir and Rabbi Shimon; and he explains what is written Rabbi Eliezer of Gerona, etc., he said to him until now money, etc., not like Rashi's explanation because now it is not an iska, for the Rambam wrote explicitly chapter 6 that in such a case it is also an iska. Rather because since half the animal is his he need not give him on the other half except according to the law if he has another business; but nevertheless he must give him something small; and with this since he is his sharecropper he need not give him anything at all. And this is what is written: now partners, etc., people say, etc.; and this is what is written here "and if the dealer," etc.; and he explains what is written "if half in profit," etc., when he does not wish to give him anything at all, then the court fixes for him two-thirds in profit, etc., whether he has another business or not; and all this when he did not fix with him from the outset at all; but if he fixed with him from the outset, even if he did not have another business, it is permitted in what he fixed even in less than a sixth, and as written in chapter 6 law 7; and likewise if they stipulated that if there will be wages there, etc.; and my teachers ruled there is not, etc., and it does not appear so to me; and the reason is from what is written at the beginning of the calf for fattening, even though it does not reach a sixth of the wages; and likewise for his wages as an idle worker; and he explains in the stipulation of the sharecropper, and this is what is written in chapter 8: and likewise one who places an animal on the fattener to fatten it and the wages are divided, he must give his labor as an idle worker; and if he said to him "behold the head and tail are yours for your labor" beyond half the wages, permitted. And he wrote: or for the fattener with other animals that he fattens with this appraisal with him, etc., since he deals with his own and his fellow's, even if he raised for him only something small in all the days of this partnership it suffices that he divide wages equally [meaning even if he did not stipulate from the outset as above]; and if he was his sharecropper, since he tends his own and the field owner's he need not raise anything. And Tur also explains "permitted a third" according to the Rambam's explanation; and likewise the Rif and Rosh and Shulchan Arukh explain; and "permitted a third" is not precise but something small, and as the Rif wrote; but if he has another business and does not abandon it for this he need not give him all this in his wages, as we say Rabbi said permitted a third, etc., and the first authority, etc.; and likewise Tur wrote; and "this is when he must," etc.; but, etc., such as a shepherd, etc.; and likewise he explains everything in the Rambam's manner; and he said: and the Sages' enactment is for one, etc., meaning if he does not wish to give him as an idle worker but his intent is specifically when he has no other business, this is the measure of his trouble close to his wages as an idle worker; and therefore he wrote "and the Sages' enactment," etc., first; and "this is when he must," etc.; and "this is when he must" refers to both, whether his wages as an idle worker or half in profit, etc., that his intent is his labor wages; and likewise Rif and Rosh. However, that which Rabbi Meir said, etc., and if he wishes to fix, etc.; and afterward he wrote "and this is in a shopkeeper," etc.; except that the Rif and Rosh's opinion is that half in profit, etc., does not help except when he fixed with him from the outset in such a case, and specifically half in profit, etc., behold less than this, no; and this is the opinion of the Rambam's teachers as written; but if he did not fix with him from the outset, not unless he gives him as an idle worker, and as the Rosh wrote. And that which Rabbi Meir said, etc., and he did not fix a condition with him, etc.; but if, etc., "and this is in a shopkeeper," etc.; but who, etc., meaning whether he fixed with him initially or did not fix, it suffices with all this; and likewise is Tur's opinion in this as above; but Tur disagrees with them and holds that at the end half in profit, etc.; but initially even one dinar suffices, and like the Rambam's words who disagreed with them; and what is written "one dinar suffices", his words are taken from what is written "he fixes for him a dinar"; and he holds Rav was stated alone when he has another business; but Shmuel deals with fixing from the outset even without another business; and there is a scribal error here in Tur that wrote "or similar to this that he gives him a dinar all the days of the iska's duration." From "that gives" until "the iska" should read "above after"; and even with another dinar he may give him a dinar, etc., not "a dinar every day"; and what is written here "or similar to this" means not specifically half in profit, etc., but likewise in another manner only that there should be a sixth more in profit than in loss; and for the law his method is like the Rambam's method; but the Rif's method is like the Rosh's words that if he did not fix from the outset, specifically as an idle worker; and if he fixed half in profit, etc., without another business, only the Rif's words are somewhat ambiguous from what is written; and this is what he says "whether," etc., "as they fixed," etc., that certainly to give his wages as an idle worker he need not fix from the outset; and it is possible he holds that if he did not fix from the outset it is forbidden to take entirely because of ribbis since initially it was in prohibition, and as written above seif 25; and Tur took the Rambam's method only when he has another business he holds like Rif and Rosh that half in profit, etc., is not applicable there; and his words are correct, how did they estimate half in profit, etc., for his wages as an idle worker and for his wages all the more so [and see in seif 4 that the Rambam's opinion is also like Tur]; and in Beit Yosef and Derishah they explained here distant paths, see there; and there "and now I will explain one by one."
(Likkut) The giver, etc. As written above that the Rambam's and Tur's methods are one for the law; and what is written in seif 4 "and if he had a business," etc., is the Mechaber's addition and he erred in this, only what the Rambam wrote "and if the dealer was his sharecropper," etc., Tur did not write that he holds like Rashi's explanation, and as written in seif 3 in the gloss (end).
(Likkut) The Rif wrote that he gives as an idle worker; and if he wishes to fix like Rava half in profit, etc., "and this is in a shopkeeper," etc.; and he explained in Sha'arim and Semag that with a shopkeeper he fixes with him in one of three conditions, either as an idle worker, or half in profit and a third in loss, or half in loss and two-thirds in profit; and they did not distinguish between fixing initially or not; and likewise Rashba; and he explained that when he gives him from himself and in himself, and it is possible there was great profit that reached him as an idle worker or more, the Sages were not strict in the matter; and when he has another business it suffices with "permitted a third"; and Semag explains like Rashi's explanation; and he wrote that incident of the calf and fixing a dinar without another business, only that he is an idle person and it is impossible to estimate as an idle worker; and this is what is written in the baraita "whether much or little", only that he give him something, etc., it deals with such a case. And the Rosh explained the Rif's words that without fixing he gives as an idle worker; and with fixing it suffices like Rava half, etc., "and this is," etc.; and for all, even with fixing, it does not suffice with less than Rava's amount; but the Rambam's and Tur's method is that Rava is without fixing and he does not wish to give as an idle worker; but with fixing it suffices even with a dinar, only they disagree in explaining the Gemara in the explanation of "he fixes", for the Rambam when he has another business, and for Tur without another business and fixing initially; and likewise in explaining the incident of Rabbi Eliezer, Tur omitted it (end).
