Idle. The Shach wrote: not his full wage. Rather, we assess how much an idle person with no work would want to take to engage in this business. Even if he had work that pays well and he is idle from it, we do not assess how much he would want to take to leave his work and engage in this business, for then he would take a great amount. Rather, we assess an idle person. Tur. But the Beit Yosef explains that we assess how much one worker would want to take to be idle from his work entirely; whether they give him light or heavy work, the measure is the same. Some poskim explain that we assess according to his original work as he earns during the idle season. For example, if he is a tailor who normally sews a garment for a sela near the festival when work is plentiful, but on other days sews it for less than a sela, that is what he now receives as his wage. Rashi and other poskim explain that if he originally did heavy work and earned much from it, and this work is light, we assess how much of his wage he would forgo to do this work. The Taz wrote that the Tur's explanation and the Beit Yosef's explanation are both true: the Tur speaks of a craftsman whose wage for this toil is known. But what shall we do with a completely idle person who receives an iska? How shall we assess his wage? Therefore one must assess according to what a person would want to do this business when idle. That he. Even a dinar per thousand, and even if the other business is not of the same kind as this business, it is permitted, as long as he is not neglecting his affairs because of this person's money. Shach.
His third. Meaning: we will divide the profit equally, but as payment for your effort I give you one third of all the extra profit. This is permitted since he has another business. It implies, however, that if he has no other business it is forbidden; rather, one must give the receiver two thirds of the profit, while in loss he pays only one third, since he did not stipulate with him at the outset. The Shach wrote: but according to the poskim I bring below, that if the recipient receives one third of the profit it is permitted even if he pays half the loss, then even if he has no other business it is permitted. The Bach wrote that even if he stipulated a dinar with him at the outset, it does not help unless the recipient is satisfied with what was stipulated; otherwise, what does his stipulation help?
Of unavoidable accidents. This is not comparable to above siman 173 seif 13, where it is permitted to accept responsibility for vinegar, for there it is a sale. The Shach wrote that it implies that even in an iska he must accept all responsibility relevant to appreciation and depreciation, and all the more so where he gives him money for half-profit; in that even the lenient authorities agree. Hagahot Maimoniyot wrote: although one gives an iska to his fellow on condition that the manager take three parts of the profit and the owner of the money one quarter, nevertheless it remains half loan and half deposit, and what the manager takes extra in profit is payment for his effort.
