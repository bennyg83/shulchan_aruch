He wrote in responsum Shev Yaakov siman 35: what they are accustomed to write the iska condition beneath the condition — we are not concerned that he might cut off the iska condition and the document remain without condition, for we are not concerned about wicked people. And also if it falls before an heir we are not concerned — only if the iska condition is not written on the document face we are concerned the wicked one might cut and collect not according to law. And specifically regarding an oral condition the Rosh forbids because we follow the document — see there. And the questioner wrote to refute and the author returned to uphold the matters. And it is clear from his words that if the lender and borrower sign on this — the way of heter iska is preferable, for it is like a document that uproots a document — see there at length in Taz's words siman 176. He wrote in She'elat Yaavetz part 1 siman 39: Reuven and Shimon bought merchandise together on credit for half a year; the merchant said he would deduct four per hundred if they give him money immediately — Reuven agreed and Shimon did not; Reuven went and paid the merchant and deducted for himself the four per hundred — nevertheless when he collects from Shimon he need not deduct anything from him and there is no ribbis here; specifically if the merchant took a document from Shimon — then Reuven acquired by the law of acquiring documents; but if Shimon's debt to the merchant was oral — then he has the law of paying his debt without permission; and even so there is nothing here in what Reuven acquires of the permitted amount since the lender has no kinyan orally. And even if he instructed Reuven to pay on his behalf — nevertheless it again becomes like a loan and it is forbidden to take more than he gave the merchant for the debt — for it is ribbis — see there. And it appears this is said regarding if the merchant waives for the early payment before its time; but if the time already arrived and the merchant waives to Reuven because Shimon evades him or he is in another city and similar — there is no law of ribbis here since Shimon was already obligated to pay; and since it is in a matter he is obligated to pay him and there is nothing of paying without permission — he is obligated to pay him the whole debt as it is; and still it requires investigation in this law if what he waived was because he thought Shimon does not wish to pay him and similar; and see Choshen Mishpat; and also it requires investigation — nevertheless how did Reuven acquire the debt corresponding to the waiver that Shimon should be mortgaged because of this since it is an oral loan — see Choshen Mishpat regarding the status of three in a debt from Israel to a gentile — it requires investigation. Further there siman 148: regarding a wealthy man who wishes to receive his money from a Torah scholar to feed his fruits a fixed amount of five or six per hundred — and it is known the wealthy man does not need his money but the Torah scholar's benefit: if all liability is on the giver, meaning on the Torah scholar — it is permitted; and if liability is on the recipient but this recipient does not deal with them at all and does not profit anything and gives him profit from his pocket — certainly it is also permitted, for it is a mere gift he gives him — investigate there. And he wrote there regarding the case in responsum Chavot Yair siman 189: one gave money to a wealthy man for wine barrels in harvest days to suffice for his needs and not count for him liability, trouble, and tending; and what is lacking in spillage — there is no prohibition here, for he did not benefit — only he wished to benefit the Torah scholar; and it is explained in siman 177 where the recipient has no benefit — even if all liability is on him and the giver does not lose and profits — it is permitted; all the more so here where he accepted depreciation at least — even for Raavad it is permitted. But in the case he brought there in letter chet: one who advanced payment for two barrels for free on condition he also make one barrel for his needs — this is certainly full watchman's fee — on condition thus he advanced him money for two barrels in order that he profit on the third what the other loses in his trouble and expenses — and this is full ribbis — see there. He wrote in responsum Avkat Rokhel siman 5: the custom in these lands is to sell one another flowers as a gift and they fix for them payment in silver coins as the flowers are worth in negotiation — always worth one to one in negotiation more than one who buys them for money immediately from the money changer; according to poskim that the version of Rabbenu Nissim: all who do not say "from now," etc. — it is permitted since they have no known rate — for even in money some buy so and some buy for more; and even though in most instances it is not common that they buy them for money as they are worth in negotiation — since we rule gold is collected for silver as fruit, and also increase applies only to a small matter — it is like one who sells a gold tongue and raises for him a small matter and does not say "from now," etc. — permitted; here too all that he does not raise the flowers more than they are worth in negotiation — permitted; but if he raises more it is recognizable he raised for him because of the gift of money and it is like one who specifies — forbidden. And see there that he brought many poskim who rule like Rabbenu Nissim's version. And see there that if he explains that liability for the flowers shall be on the seller until payment time of his debt — it is possible it is permitted even if he gave nothing at all as labor wages, for it is not similar to Rav Chama — see there. And see in responsum Radbaz part 3 siman 491 — he also wrote since when small coins are abundant the dinar is cheap and when they are abundant the dinar is dear — behold their assessment is not known, for gold is fruit; and he further wrote other sides of permission — see there. Further there siman 112: regarding a mortgage document where they stipulated that if the mortgagor needs to dwell with his son he may dwell in whichever house he wishes, etc. — it is not similar to lessors of oil lamps; but here now in any event he does not dwell and perhaps he will never come to dwell; and further there it is done for the money owner's benefit — not so here, on the contrary it is distress to him that if he has a tenant he must evict him, etc.; and regarding the condition at the end of four years he can claim his money — the condition is in violation and the condition is void and the mortgage stands according to Rashba — see there. Further there siman 137: Reuven who did an iska with Shimon and afterward claims he gave him ribbis; and Shimon responds with proof and presumptive evidence that he took only with heter iska — the law is with Shimon that even without proof he is believed to say he took with permission; and even though there is a witness — the witness does not testify he took ribbis from him, only that he compromised regarding ribbis; and Shimon responds that so it was only he asked a sage who rebuked him and from then he retracted from that compromise and fixed the matter with heter iska — there is no oath here to contradict the witness and he is believed without oath as the Rosh and Mordechai wrote. And there is another reason: Shimon wrote with him every year an iska document and also today the document is in Reuven's hand who admitted he received from him the flowers according to the conditions written in the document; and if it were that he had ribbis money in his hand he would have collected his own and not written a document — for in court the way is they give money and then write a document, etc. — see there. Further there siman 151: Shimon claims that even if there were ribbis he has a document of waiver for any claim Reuven has; and Reuven denies it and says he never waived — behold for Rambam and Rosh waiver helps for ribbis already taken; therefore if Shimon is in possession he swears; and since he brought a witness that he waived — he is a supporting witness who exempts. However otherwise even for Reuven there is no fixed ribbis here for Rambam and his teachers since they did not stipulate on profit at the time of giving money — he holds it is asmakhta in court; even though other poskim disagree, since they practiced like Rabbi Meir according to Rambam we have no power to extract from his hand; and even for other poskim behold he denies and says it was done with permission; therefore if Shimon's document is validated he collects his document without oath — see there; and see in responsum Beit Yosef part 1 siman 132 on this law. He wrote in responsum Har HaCarmel siman 16: one who holds animal hides buys hides from the butcher for a fixed sum worth in the market because he advances money to him — permission may be learned, for initially at the time of buying the animal he buys the hide for the holder and as his agent; and see Shakh s.k. 31. And one may also say this is the statutes of the seven good men of the city — this is the community's situation, etc.; and therefore even if the hides are in the butcher's hand he must give him, for by law one does not sell a thing not in his possession — see there and all this; and perhaps one who fears the word of God can do it with heter iska — he takes from the butcher a condition on iska that if he gives him the hides for such-and-such sum he is exempt from the iska — see there. He wrote in responsum Beit Hadin siman 16: Reuven and Shimon borrowed from lenders and there was an unceasing rumor that within about half a year they would devalue the currency; therefore they wish to pay immediately as it passes to the merchant; and Levi says he is not obligated to accept them because the devaluation is visible to the eyes. He ruled they cannot compel him to accept within the time since the time was fixed for the lender's benefit — it is a period within which the currency will return to correction; and there is no ribbis here even though it improves in ingot more than a fifth — specifically if after the loan the matters changed; but here at the time of the loan it was known the kingdom debased the currency even though he did not have certainty only a near concern that the bad currency was only because of war distress; therefore he lent for three years that in the lender's mind it was near certainty that within the time the currency would return to its former state; and the borrower agreed with his mind that he would profit doubly in trade within the time more than he would lose in the currency's rise — if so both entered in doubt and there is no ribbis here; and see Tosafot Ketubot 47, etc.; and even close to profit there is nothing here — for who knows the future and new matters according to the war's strength and its quieting by which they will add or diminish currency; and since both entered in doubt with intent thus he accepted upon himself to pay at the stated time money passing to the merchant — and there is no ribbis here — see there. And see there that if there were ribbis prohibition here — even though there is only rabbinic ribbis — even though he gave a kinyan to uphold the compromise, there is no power to compel him to pay what is forbidden rabbinically as written in Yoreh Deah siman 239; but in truth there is no ribbis here for the above reason — if so the kinyan applies to uphold the compromise and we compel him to pay according to the kinyan — see there.
