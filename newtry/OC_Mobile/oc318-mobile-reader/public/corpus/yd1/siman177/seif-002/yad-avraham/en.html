(Seif 19) And if the depositor wishes to give something of his own to the depositor, there is no ribbis violation. And Maggid Mishneh in chapter 7 of Laws of Lending wrote that even if he spent the money on his debts it is permitted since it came to his hand as a deposit. And it is difficult from the Jerusalem Talmud in Shekalim, for it implies specifically regarding orphans when he deposited it is permitted — close to profit and far from loss — it implies that for others, even if it came to his hand as a deposit, it is forbidden to give from the profit. And one must say that there it speaks regarding misappropriation and use, not regarding ribbis prohibition; and not like the commentator there. And further, that in a loan as well it is permitted when he stipulated afterward that he will give him profit and did not stipulate so initially, as Shach wrote in the name of Maharshal and as is found in Choshen Mishpat siman 81 — except that in a loan he is not obligated to fulfill the condition. Rather we learn that in the Jerusalem Talmud it speaks regarding permission to use, that he is not misappropriating a deposit in such a case, even with orphans' funds.
