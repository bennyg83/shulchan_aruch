As an idle laborer etc. Not his full wages, rather we assess: a person who is idle and has no work at all — how much would he want to accept to engage in this business; even if he had a job with good pay that he must leave — we do not assess how much this person would want to accept to leave his work and engage in this business (which he would not do for a little) — rather we assess by an idle person, as Tur explained; and so is the view of some poskim. But Beit Yosef explains we assess how much a single laborer wants to take to leave his work entirely, and whether given light or heavy work — the amount is the same; and so is the view of some poskim; and see Derishab and Bach and Maadanei Melech who elaborated on this. And Rashi and some poskim explain: if he was originally doing heavy work and earning a lot, and this is light — we assess how much he would reduce from his wages to do this work. And students of Rashba and some poskim explain: we assess by his first work as he earns during idle time — such as he is a tailor and his practice is to sew a garment for a sela at the festival season when there is much work, and during other days he sews it for less than a sela since he cannot find that wage — and that is what he takes now as his wage; and see above siman 312 and in Derishab there who elaborated.
Some other business at all. It implies that even if the other business is not of the same type as this business — it is permitted, as long as he does not abandon his businesses because of this person's money; and this is the view of most poskim.
At all etc. Even a dinar against a thousand — so Beit Yosef in the name of Rambam in the Commentary on the Mishnah.
His third etc. That is the Rambam and the Mechaber in seif 4; but according to the poskim I brought below — that if the recipient takes two-thirds of the profit even if he pays him half the loss — it is permitted; if so, even when he has no other business it is permitted — examine.
Since he has another business etc. But if he has no other business — he must give the recipient two-thirds of the profit, and in loss he pays only a third, as below seif 4; for since he did not agree with him at the outset — only that the recipient does not agree to it — therefore when he has no other business a dinar suffices; but if he agreed at the outset to take for his fee even a dinar, or the excess third, or the excess tenth (meaning whatever profit is beyond the one-third or one-tenth share will be yours; and Bach seif 3 wrote that if he says to him "a third of the wages will be yours" — it is forbidden even if he agreed at the outset, since he did not fix a known dinar; and it seems to me there is no need to distinguish in this) — end of his words in brief — see there where he elaborated; and examine. And even though his words are correct, nevertheless the matter is clear that as regards the law everything follows one principle: what the Mechaber wrote in seif 3 — if he agreed with him at the outset even for a dinar it is permitted — means the recipient agreed to what he fixed; for if not, of what use is his fixing? — and it is easily understood.
Even accident-liability. And it is not comparable to above siman 173 seif 13 where it is permitted to accept souring-liability, for there it is a sale — Bach; and this is obvious.
