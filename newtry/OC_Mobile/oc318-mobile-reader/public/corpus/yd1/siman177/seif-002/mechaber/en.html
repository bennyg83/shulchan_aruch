One who gives money to his fellow as an iska — meaning, that half the responsibility is on the giver and half on the recipient — it is forbidden, for every iska is half loan and half deposit, and it turns out that he toils with the deposit portion because of the loan portion; therefore he must give him wages for his toil — for each and every day of the days of the partnership — like an idle laborer for that labor from which he is idle. And if he had another business, any amount to engage in with this one’s money, he need not give him wages for every day; rather, even if he gave him a dinar for all the days of the partnership, it suffices; and if they diminished or increased, it is equally for the middle. And likewise, if he said to him, “All the profit will be yours; [give] its third or its tenth as your wages,” since he has another business, this is permitted; and if they lost, he will lose half. And if the one engaging was his sharecropper, and he had another business, he need not give him any other wages at all, for the sharecropper is subjugated to the owner of the field. {Rama: And the entire responsibility of the deposit-half must be on the giver, even responsibility for accidents (Tur and poskim in the name of R' Yitzchak), and not like those who are lenient and permit with partial responsibility (Rivan).}
