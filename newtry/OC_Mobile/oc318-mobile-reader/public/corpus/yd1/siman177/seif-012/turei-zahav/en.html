All loss is borne by the giver. In Tur likewise in name of R' Yeshaya; and he gave a reason because it appears like a loan at ribbis and will come to be exchanged for full ribbis; end quote. And one may wonder where R' Yeshaya learned to decree of his own what is not found in the Talmud; and further behold many leniencies are found among poskim and we do not say regarding them it will come to be exchanged for full ribbis. And in my humble opinion R' Yeshaya's words are explained in this manner: it is forbidden for the iska recipient to say to the iska giver for half wages that he buy from him a share in profit that is still in doubt what it will be; and he inserts himself in the doubt and gives him a fixed amount for this; and this matter would be fit to permit here where there is no ribbis at all, only he buys from him a matter that is doubtful and he is like any person who buys such a thing; and all the more if the giver accepts liability upon him; the borrower is removed from the recipient; nevertheless it is forbidden because it appears as ribbis. And R' Yeshaya learned this from lessors of oil lamps that Tur and Shulchan Aruch brought in siman 164; forbidden for the lender to return and lease them for a fixed amount for a year; and Beit Yosef brought there the opinions of Rambam and Ramban and Rashba that it is like circumvention of ribbis; and likewise here. And if so R' Yeshaya's words have a source from the Talmud. But the reverse; that the giver buys the doubt from the recipient for a fixed amount; is certainly permitted. But if the giver gives some sum in an iska to his fellow to deal and profit and if he profits he will give a fixed amount to the giver and if he does not profit he will give nothing; there is no prohibition in this; and such is the practice every day in all communities who make mamranut and write on them that it is an iska document for such-and-such sum principal and profit according to heter iska such-and-such sum; and no doubt or flutter of prohibition is heard on this, only that liability for loss must be on both.
