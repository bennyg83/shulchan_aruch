To write with the right hand. For we expound "and you shall bind" and "and you shall write" — just as binding is with the right, so writing is with the right — end of chapter HaKometz. And what he wrote "and not with a feather." It is true that in Mordechai chapter 1 of Gittin it is written thus in Hagahah in the name of the Rosh; but I do not know the reason for the matter; and perhaps it is as we find in the order of gittin of Maharil Margoliot who wrote one should not write with an iron reed, for it is like engraving; and even though engraved letters are valid, nevertheless as much as possible to do without any engraving is preferable, etc.; and perhaps so too with a feather that is thin; and so the reason is written in Levush, and it is written that we do not practice thus. And engraving — since it came to hand, let us say a word: for I heard people saying that printed books do not have sanctity like handwritten ones; and it appears to me there is no difference, for even if we say it is engraving, nevertheless it is like writing; for also regarding a woman's get, engraved letters are valid as written in EH siman 125, even though it is written "and he shall write" and not "and he shall engrave." And further it appears to me clear proof from chapter Eglah Arufah (Sotah 48a) — those stones of the ephod and breastplate — one does not write them with ink, as it says "engraved like a signet" implies engraving; and one does not scratch them with a knife, as it says "in their fullness" they should be whole without lacking anything; rather one writes on them with ink and shows them shamir from outside on the face of the ink in the groove of the letter and they split open by themselves; and if it were so that printing were engraving, how do we know it was by shamir which is an uncommon thing — perhaps it was by printing with ink like our press, and it would work out well "engraved like a signet"; rather it is clear that printing is called actual writing and not engraving at all. And again I saw in responsum She'elot to Mahar Benjamin z"l who also wrote that printing is sacred like writing, because engraving too is sacred, and he brought proof from the ephod; but according to my humble opinion we do not need this, for it is actual writing and there is no engraving here — what difference whether one presses the pen on the paper or the paper on the lead letters — either way it is writing. Nevertheless regarding a get certainly one should not do by press since it somewhat resembles engraving; but regarding sanctity of books, whoever is lenient will give account.
