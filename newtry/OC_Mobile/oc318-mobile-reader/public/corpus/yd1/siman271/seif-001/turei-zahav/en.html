That he should say at the beginning of the processing. This is like the Rosh's conclusion that he brought first — Rabbeinu Baruch was uncertain whether we require explicit declaration or thought suffices; for regarding pigul it is written "it shall not be reckoned" and requires speech, whereas terumah is taken without speech; and regarding misappropriation as it is written "for any matter of trespass" — one who thinks to misappropriate a deposit is liable — end quote. And it is surprising — for according to Beit Hillel he is not liable for misappropriation until he acts, as stated at the end of chapter HaMafkid; and further he challenged in Derishah that even for Beit Shammai who obligate there for thought — that is when he speaks, as Rashi and Tosafot explained; and we can resolve that Rabbeinu does not hold like Rashi and Tosafot here, and it appears to me his suggestion is: regarding pigul it is written "it shall not be reckoned" and we find it requires speech — thus we have doubt here from which law to learn; and likewise regarding misappropriation as written "for any matter of trespass" and we find there too language of thought in the Mishnah that said one who thinks to misappropriate a deposit is liable according to Beit Shammai — and there the Tanna himself relied on the verse; but in other places the doubt remains. And what he wrote "at the beginning of processing" appears he did not come to exclude at the end of processing — on the contrary we find everywhere the act is called only by the name of the finisher; if so, when he assists at the end it certainly helps; rather he came to exclude that one need not assist from beginning to end — rather it suffices even at the beginning alone. But one could say: since at the beginning the gentile alone did the processing, part of it was done not for its sake; unlike when a Jew assists first — one could say afterward he draws all the gentile's processing until the entire finish, for we rule like the Rosh that when a gentile acts with the Jew's intent it is permitted. But from the Tur's words here in the name of Rav Netronai it is written: but if they have ability to return the hide to stretched form and pass lime over them for its sake — behold you have fulfilled processing for its sake — end quote; if so, all the more when one assists at the beginning at the time there is the first lime it helps; and so it implies from Semag that Beit Yosef brings in Orach Chayyim siman 32 — one must place the hides in processing for their sake, or assist the gentile at the time of processing for their sake — end quote. Nevertheless it appears to me that assistance in processing is called only in the act of processing itself, but not in what assists to blacken the hides of tefillin, for that is not from processing; and all who practice leniency in this mislead the many.
