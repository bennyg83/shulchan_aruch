They must be, etc. They must be processed for the sake of the mitzvah. This implies that if they were processed without such intent, they are invalid even after the fact and a Torah scroll may not be written upon them. It also implies that if a Torah scroll was already written upon them, it is invalid. This appears from the Gemara in Gittin, in the chapter HaNizakin, concerning the person who came before Rabbi Abbahu, and from the halachic authorities. The Rosh and the Tur, however, cite the responsum of a Gaon stating that Rav Moshe Gaon disagrees. Therefore, if they are able to return the parchment sheets to a stretched condition and apply lime to them for the sake of the mitzvah, they thereby fulfill the requirement of processing for the sake of the mitzvah. If this cannot be done, and they have no other Torah scroll and no other parchment upon which to write a valid Torah scroll, they may write upon it despite its not having been processed for the sake of the mitzvah and may read from it publicly.
Processed, etc. The preparation of the parchment and the ruling of lines need not be performed for the sake of the mitzvah. Tur.
He should say, etc. He must state this explicitly, and thought alone is insufficient, as written in Orach Chaim, siman 32, seif 8.
For the sake of a Torah scroll. The Beis Yosef writes in Orach Chaim, siman 32, that parchment processed for the sake of a mezuzah is invalid for tefillin, because tefillin possess greater sanctity than a mezuzah. Certainly, parchment processed for tefillin is invalid for a Torah scroll, because the sanctity of a Torah scroll is greater than that of tefillin. Thus writes the Darchei Moshe.
He should assist, etc. It appears that the assistance required of the Jew is only an initial preference, as the best manner of performance. After the fact, even if he did not assist at all, but merely instructed the non-Jew to process it for the proper purpose, this is sufficient, unlike the Beis Yosef. Bach.
We are not concerned, etc. The non-Jew is afraid that the Jew will recognize the substitution through his visual familiarity with identifying signs, or because the holes in the substituted hide were made more recently than those in his own. This is stated in Hagahos Maimoniyos, and the Mechaber likewise writes so in Orach Chaim, siman 32, seif 10. This must refer to a case in which the non-Jew gains from the exchange. Otherwise, we are not concerned that he intended to cause the Jew to transgress when he derives no benefit, as stated above in siman 118, seif 10.
