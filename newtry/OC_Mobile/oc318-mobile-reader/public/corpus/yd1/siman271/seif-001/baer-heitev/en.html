Processed. Shach writes that this implies that if the parchment was processed not for the sake of the mitzvah, it is invalid even after the fact, and even a Sefer Torah already written upon it is invalid. Rosh and Tur, however, cite a responsum of a Gaon: if they can return the parchment sheets to a stretched state and apply lime to them for the sake of a Sefer Torah, that is preferable. If they cannot do so, and they have no other Sefer Torah and no other parchment, they should write upon this parchment even though it was not processed for its sake and read from it publicly. See siman 4.
That he should say. He must state it explicitly and not rely on thought alone, as stated in Orach Chayyim 32:8.
A Sefer Torah. Beit Yosef writes in Orach Chayyim siman 32 that parchment processed for the sake of a mezuzah is certainly invalid for tefillin. The same necessarily follows that parchment processed for tefillin is invalid for a Sefer Torah, because the sanctity of a Sefer Torah is greater than that of tefillin. Darkei Moshe.
At the beginning. Taz writes that certainly if the Jew assists at the end of the processing it also helps. The ruling means only that he need not assist from beginning to end. Nevertheless, it appears to me that "assistance" means participating in the actual processing itself, not merely helping blacken the hides for tefillin. Anyone who is lenient in this matter causes many people to sin. After the fact, perhaps even if the Jew did not assist at all, it is sufficient that he instructed the gentile to process it for the sake of the mitzvah. Shach. Tur writes that preparing the parchment and ruling the lines do not need to be done for the sake of the mitzvah.
