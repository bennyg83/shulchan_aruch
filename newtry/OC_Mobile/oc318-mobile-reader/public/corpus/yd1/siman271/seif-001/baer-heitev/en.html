Processed. The Shach wrote: it implies that if they processed it not for its sake, even after the fact it is invalid, and even if a Sefer Torah was written on it, it is invalid. But the Rosh and the Tur brought the responsum of the gaon, who wrote: therefore, if they are able to return the sheets of parchment to a stretched state and pass lime over them for its sake, how good; and if they are unable to do so, and they have no other Sefer Torah and no other parchments, they should write on it even though it was not processed for its sake, and read from it publicly; end quote.
That he say. Explicitly, and not merely in thought, as written in Orach Chayim siman 32 seif 8.
Sefer Torah. The Beit Yosef wrote in Orach Chayim siman 32: if it was processed for the sake of a mezuzah, it is certainly invalid for tefillin; the same is certainly true that if it was processed for tefillin, it is invalid for a Sefer Torah, for the sanctity of a Sefer Torah is more stringent than tefillin; end quote, Darkei Moshe.
At the beginning. The Taz wrote: the same applies if he assists at the completion; it certainly helps. It only comes to exclude that he need not assist from beginning to end. Nevertheless, it appears to me that it is called assistance only when it is in the act of processing itself, but not when he assists in blackening the hides of tefillin. Whoever practices leniency in this causes the public to sin; end quote. (After the fact, perhaps even if he did not assist at all, but merely tells the gentile to process it for its sake, that is sufficient. Shach. The Tur wrote that preparing the parchment and ruling the lines need not be for its sake.)
