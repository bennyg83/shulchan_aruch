Parchment, etc. The hide is split into two layers. The outer layer on the hair side is called kelaf, and one writes on its inner side, facing the flesh. Tur.
And our parchments, etc. What is scraped from the upper surface is only the amount needed to prepare and smooth it. Even if the hide were split into two layers, that amount would still need to be scraped away. On the flesh side they scrape extensively, until only the kelaf remains. Thus write the authorities.
They are of superior quality, etc. Kelaf is more beautiful, because from gevil only the hair is removed, while nothing is removed from its other side. Writing on gevil is also excessively heavy. Therefore, the custom is to write Torah scrolls on kelaf. Mahari Chaviv, there.
If he deviated and wrote on our parchments on the hair side, etc. This is the correct reading. Tosafos in the chapter HaMotzi Yayin at the end of Shabbos 79 states that sacred books may be written on unerased paper, as cited by the Beis Yosef and Darchei Moshe. From the Sefer HaTerumah and the other authorities, however, it appears that they must specifically be written on the hide of a kosher animal. This is also explicit in Tosafos to Menachos 31b, beginning with the words 'Ha de'afitzan.'
