For the sake of [writing] a Torah scroll. See the book Benei Yonah which raised objections against certain scribes who are lenient to take hides that were processed by a gentile and are still not scraped and smooth by knife and the pumice stone, and they perform the scraping for its sake — this is a complete prohibition, and even after the fact one should disqualify reading from it as long as another Torah scroll is available; but when there is no other Torah scroll available, it is possible to be lenient to read from it, and it is better to read without a blessing — see there further. And see the responsum Perach Matteh Aharon vol. 2, siman 101, who wrote regarding parchments brought from captivity where there is doubt whether they were processed for the sake of a Torah scroll — and a Torah scroll was written on those parchments — it is valid after the fact, since this is a dispute among the authorities whether we require processing for its sake at all, and here there is an additional doubt whether they were processed for its sake — this is a double doubt; and furthermore even according to those who are stringent and hold we require processing for its sake, they agree that in pressing circumstances one may write on parchment not processed for its sake, and since in pressing circumstances it is valid ab initio, after the fact it is always valid to read from it in public — see there. And see the responsum Penei Aryeh siman 52 who disagrees and wrote that such a case already came before him in the community of Rotterdam and he ordered to remove the panels that had not been processed for their sake and to store them — see there. And see the responsum Noda BiYehudah second edition, Yoreh De'ah siman 168, regarding a scribe of STaM who was processing parchment and said he was processing it for its sake, and afterward intended to write an invalid book on it, and subsequently the matter was cancelled and he wrote a Torah scroll on it — what is its ruling. And he wrote: if he thought afterward to write an invalid book, a thought is nothing; however if he explicitly said he wants to write an invalid book — a statement comes and cancels a statement; and the processing itself is not considered an act either to be lenient or to be stringent, for even without that he must process it; however if the scribe did not process it himself but gave it to a gentile and assisted him by providing the lime — then there is here a demonstrative act that he is doing it for the sake of STaM; and he wrote further that even if he processed it himself, one can say that even if he explicitly said he wants to write an invalid Torah scroll, it is valid — for he did not say he was processing it for the sake of writing an invalid Torah scroll, so he did not cancel the first statement — see there.
