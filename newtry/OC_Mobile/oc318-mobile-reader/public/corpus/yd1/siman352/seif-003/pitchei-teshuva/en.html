And they wash him. See Chatam Sofer responsa siman 328, who was asked about delaying burial of the dead for a day or two by order of the government, where most become disfigured and many people do not want to involve themselves in taharah and dressing. Is it permitted to purify and dress the body immediately, then leave it overnight afterward, changing their old custom of doing the taharah close to burial because the body tends to become soiled again from food, drink, and medicines? He answered that the core custom of anointing and washing the dead is in the Mishnah, Shabbat at the end of chapter 23: one anoints and washes; and in the Rambam and Tur/Shulchan Aruch, Orach Chayim siman 311. The implication of all of them is that this washing need not be close to burial, etc. Only when burial is not far from death is it proper to wait until the time of burial, because the body tends to become soiled again. But in the present changed circumstance due to the order, one should wash, purify, and dress the body immediately, wrap a sheet around the clothing, and then, when discharge emerges from the mouth, clean it again close to burial and remove the soiled sheet, while the clothing remains clean; see there.
