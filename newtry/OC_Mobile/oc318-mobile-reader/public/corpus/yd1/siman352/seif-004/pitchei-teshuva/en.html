[And they rinse him. See responsum Chatam Sofer siman 328, who was asked about leaving the dead overnight one or two days by government decree, most becoming disfigured, and many not wanting to engage in taharah and dressing: may one permit to purify and dress him immediately and then leave him overnight afterward, changing their longstanding custom of doing taharah close to burial, because he tends to become disfigured every hour from foods and medicinal drinks. And he answered: the essence of the custom of anointing and rinsing the dead is in the mishnah, Shabbat, end of chapter 23, "they anoint and rinse," and in Rambam and Tur and Shulchan Aruch Orach Chaim siman 311, and the implication of all of them is that this rinsing is not specifically close to burial, etc.; and only when burial is not far from death it is proper to wait until the time of burial, because he tends to become disfigured again; but in the form that has now changed by decree, one should wash and purify and dress him immediately and wrap a sheet over the garments, and later when what issued from his mouth comes out they should clean him again close to burial and remove the soiled sheet, and his garments will remain clean; see there.]
[And they smear his head. See responsum Chatam Sofer siman 327, who wrote regarding one scholar appointed gabbai of the chevrah kaddisha who wanted to change the practice of sprinkling on the limbs of the dead wine mixed with eggs, saying he found in Sefer Chochmat Adam that the matter is unseemly. Behold, I searched in that book and did not find it; but sprinkling wine is explicit in the Yerushalmi, end of chapter 2 of Shekalim, that they make a sprinkling on his bier, and eggs are written in Sefer Ma'avar Yabbok HaGadol, etc.; and who is worthy and who is important enough to abolish this, especially since it is mentioned in Rama at the end of siman 352, end of his words. And I searched in the aforementioned book and found it, in Kuntres Matzevet Moshe siman 8; but he too did not write that the matter is unseemly, only regarding the practice of beating eggs in water, each taking a bit of the water and sprinkling on the dead and saying "and I will sprinkle upon you," etc.; on that he wrote that this is from the statutes of the nations and a very unseemly custom, and it is proper to abolish it; rather they should wash his head with it, and this is not part of the taharah at all; see there. And he further wrote there in siman 9 that the custom of clenching the dead man's hands and placing in his hand a wooden rod they call (geflikh) should be abolished, for his hands must be extended; and if they wish to keep the custom they should place the geflikh beside him and not bend the fingers. And see there in the Chatam Sofer responsum, whose view is not to abolish this custom; see there.]
