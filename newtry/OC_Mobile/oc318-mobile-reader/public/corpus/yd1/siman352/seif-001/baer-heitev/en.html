Expensive. In Darkhei Moshe it is written: whoever makes handsome shrouds for the dead is recognized as one who acknowledges resurrection of the dead, and may blessing come upon him. End quote. This refers to making the linen garment handsome; therefore some are accustomed to seek fine linen. But excessively important shrouds are certainly forbidden. Shach. Nevertheless, it appears that everything depends on the person: if he is abundantly wealthy, there is no prohibition in dressing him in important linen, for Rabbi Yochanan called garments honor; therefore what is honor for the dead is certainly permitted. Especially according to what the kabbalists wrote, that the soul is clothed in free garments just as the body is clothed, with proof from the two spirits in the Gemara Berachot, and several other proofs. Therefore one should be careful not to change the clothing of the dead from the accepted custom, for we find that the dead are particular about this, as stated in Sefer Chasidim and Sefer HaChayim. Ma'avar Yabok wrote that even if the shrouds decay, the soul is forever clothed in their spiritual aspect. Some people command certain things regarding their shrouds, and it is a mitzvah to fulfill the words of the dead if possible and if it is not against the law. There was an incident where the dead in our cemetery were dug up and stripped naked, and we practiced as written in Shevut Yaakov II, question 103; see there. If they forgot to dress him in one item of the burial clothes, they should place it on the coffin; see Responsa Chacham Tzvi siman 47 and Knesset Yechezkel 64. In Sefer Chasidim siman 451 he wrote that when there is plague in a city, may the Merciful One protect us, they inspect the dead, perhaps one of them has a worn-out garment, meaning they were buried in worn-out clothing and this is dangerous, or perhaps their hands are outstretched, etc. Anything that involves saving life may be done to the dead. End quote.
