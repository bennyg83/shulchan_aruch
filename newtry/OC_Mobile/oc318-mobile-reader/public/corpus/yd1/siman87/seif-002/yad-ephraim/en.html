Permitted in cooking and benefit. Baer Heitev — and what he wrote in the name of Radbaz, and Peri Chadash challenged him; and in Sha'ar HaMitzvot of Rabbenu R' Chaim Vital, parashat Mishpatim, he wrote: I saw people stringent not to eat sucuk made from sweet reeds with meat, because craftsmen whiten it by mixing goat milk in it as is known; and I saw my teacher of blessed memory eat it with meat together — end of his words. And this is like Radbaz's words, except that Radbaz permitted even without water, for even though there is no sixty in the sucuk alone it is permitted because the milk diminishes through fire and the sucuk does not diminish; and on this Peri Chadash challenged: since at the beginning of boiling there is no sixty against the milk, etc. — and his words are astonishing, and he stood on them in Mishna Berurah. And one sage wrote to him to emend the word "but if they cooked" should read "even if they cooked," etc.; and in place of "or if there is in the water" should read "but if there is in the water," etc. — his intent is that just as in prohibition diminution does not help as below siman 90, so too in permission, since it remains in its substance it is considered significant; therefore even a new pot does not help; but where there is sixty, even in the pot the meat is permitted, for everything returned to become permitted — see there. And Mishna Berurah wrote that it is not comfortable for him, for a prohibition is different: from the moment of falling it is already forbidden, unlike if it diminished while still permitted — see there. And in my humble opinion Peri Chadash wrote well according to the sage's view, for also in permission, immediately at the beginning of boiling the sucuk absorbs taste of milk, and even though the milk itself afterward diminishes, nevertheless taste of milk in the sucuk did not cease — I shortened. And Mishna Berurah wrote that for practical law all this is unnecessary, for it is known that special boilers are used and they put water in the boilers and the milk is nullified, and one need not be stringent at all; and behold there is testimony that the holy rabbi the Ari of blessed memory ate the finest of the fine — see there.
