(There) And he derived the prohibition of eating in the language of cooking, etc. And what he wrote — the prohibition of benefit in the language of cooking — according to the Rambam's explanation that one is not lashed when it is not fit for the context — it works out well. And according to Tosafot's explanation one can say it teaches us that there is cooking after cooking in basar b'chalav, as written in siman 94. And know that Kerem Shlomo wrote in siman 92 that in cooking one is liable immediately — only in eating: if they ate before it was cooked, like food of ben drusai, he is exempt, as in the Tosefta that it is not in the manner of benefit, for he holds that in basar b'chalav too, not in the manner of benefit, one is exempt. And he relied on what is stated in Tosafot in Chullin: how much must he eat to be liable — enough that it would be eaten on account of cooking — and he did not ask how much must he cook; learn from this that in cooking one is liable immediately. And it escaped me the Tosefta of lashes that is stated there: how much must it be cooked. Nevertheless, according to his view, the difficulty of Machaneh Levi in Hilchot Yesodei HaTorah is resolved — he left the Rambam's words as requiring study, who wrote there in chapter 5 halacha 8: likewise in basar b'chalav they are forbidden even not in the manner of benefit — it implies the prohibition exists but lashes do not; if so, perforce the Rambam explains what Abaye said in chapter Kol Sha'ah (daf 24): all agree in such a case that one would receive lashes for them, for not in the manner of benefit refers to eating, for in benefit there are no lashes. And since it deals with eating, it should have said: all agree in basar b'chalav that one would receive lashes for eating them not in the manner of benefit — end of his words. And according to Kerem Shlomo's words it works out well — the view of our great rabbi follows his approach, for he omitted in chapter 9 of Ma'achalot Asurot that baraita in chapter Kol HaBasar (daf 108b) regarding cooking in basar b'chalav: in what cooking did they speak — in cooking that others eat it on account of its cooking. And he stated anonymously and did not give a measure for cooking in basar b'chalav — it implies he is liable even in cooking that is not fit for eating. And Kerem Shlomo explained his reason: he explains that the tanna of that baraita in chapter 25 who requires cooking fit for eating holds that also in basar b'chalav there is no Torah prohibition even in eating not in the manner of benefit. But according to what we rule, that in basar b'chalav one is liable for eating even not in the manner of benefit, one is also liable for eating and cooking that is not fit for eating. And according to this Abaye could not say: all agree in basar b'chalav that one would receive lashes for eating it not in the manner of benefit — for this tanna of chapter Kol HaBasar who requires a measure for cooking in basar b'chalav so that it be fit for eating — perforce he does not hold thus, as stated. And according to this it works out well what Machazit HaShekel challenged on this, that he sought to derive in chapter Ein Ma'amidin that taste is like substance in all prohibitions from basar b'chalav, and did not challenge: what of basar b'chalav, for one receives lashes for them not in the manner of benefit — for it follows the view that does not hold thus. And what Machazit HaShekel further wrote regarding Pesach too — only through eating, from what is written "you shall not eat from it raw" — there is no understanding to his words, for since it is raw it is not the manner of eating, and nevertheless the Merciful One made it liable and called it eating; and likewise for cooked and the others written in that verse one is liable not in the manner of benefit. And what is written in Yad Malkei that he did not find "all agree in basar b'chalav" — because there is one who derives in Chullin (daf 115) from the verse "you shall not eat," etc. — the proofreader already noted this in Machaneh Levi in Hilchot Yesodei HaTorah and rejected this. And what Kerem Shlomo further wrote, that one who derives basar b'chalav from "you shall not eat any abomination" — it is a negative commandment included in a generality and one does not receive lashes for it — likewise wrote the Mezrichi in Mishpatim, and so appears in Sefer HaChinuch parashat Re'eh; and see in Sefer She'eilot uTeshuvot Hilchot Yesodei HaTorah that he resolves that it should not be difficult from what the Rambam wrote in Hilchot Pesach.
