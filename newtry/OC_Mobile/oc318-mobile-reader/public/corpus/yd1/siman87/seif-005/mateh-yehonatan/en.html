(Ibid.:) "And one does not receive lashes for eating it on account of basar b'chalav." This implies that, however, on account of blood one would receive lashes, even though it is dam shevishlo (blood that was cooked). It must therefore be said that he holds to distinguish between congealed and liquid blood: if it is congealed, one is not liable for dam shevishlo; and if it is liquid, one is liable. With this, what appeared at first glance as a contradiction in his words is resolved — for above in the laws of salting he wrote and implied many times that dam shevishlo is only d'rabbanan, whereas here it implies the opposite. Rather, it is certainly as stated above. And the source he cites for the Rambam and the Mechaber to distinguish between congealed and liquid is Tosafot in tractate Zevachim. {Gloss: What the Gaon, the author, wrote — that this is found in Tosafot of Zevachim — it seems that in Zevachim this statement of Tosafot is not found, and perhaps it is a scribal error, and it should read "in Menachot" (daf 21a, s.v. "kan"), for thus it is found there in Tosafot according to Rashi there. However, the matter requires further study, for according to Rashi there is no practical halachic difference at all in this — since according to Rashi even for congealed blood one is liable, and he holds that Rava disagrees there with Ze'iri. Moreover, Ze'iri's own statement that dam shevishlo is exempt applies only to the inner sin-offerings, but for the outer sin-offerings, and all the more so for chulin, even for congealed blood one is liable. According to Rashi's opinion there, that distinction between congealed and liquid applies only to congealing in the sun, but blood cooked in fire certainly always drains out, as is explained there. But in truth, to settle the Rambam's words more satisfactorily, it appears better to say as the Beit Yosef wrote here: that the Rambam holds like Rashi that dam shevishlo is truly liable with lashes. And as for what the Beit Yosef wondered regarding the sugya in Menachot mentioned above — perhaps he holds that the sugya of Menachot does not follow the halacha — though he did not clarify from where the Rambam derived that it does not follow the halacha. But in truth, according to what Rashi wrote there, it is evident that Rava disagrees with that statement, and Rava is the later authority. A great proof for this is that the Rambam does not cite at all in his laws the ruling of Ze'iri that one does not transgress for dam shevishlo, and therefore he must hold that it was rejected from the halacha. And as for what the Tur and the Mechaber copied the language of the Rambam — even though they certainly hold that dam shevishlo is d'rabbanan, as the Tur wrote at the end of siman 76 in the name of the Rashba explicitly [and so too the Mechaber, it is explained in many places that he holds it to be d'rabbanan] — and the Drisha already pointed this out here — I say there is no difficulty: since nowadays there are no lashes, they did not bother to be precise in this; and something similar is found in many places, and it is obvious.
