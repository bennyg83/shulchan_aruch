(There, seif 5) "Eggs found in birds, etc., even though it is attached by sinews, etc." See in the Taz what he raised as a difficulty against the opinion of the Rashbam and the Mordechai. And from the words of the Shach, subsection 10, it appears that he interprets the position of the Rashbam — who wrote that it must specifically be developed to the point of having a hard shell — not as his interpretation of the definition of "fully formed eggs," but rather because he holds like Rabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov and is uncertain about the meaning of "attached by sinews." But this is not necessarily so, for one can certainly validly say that the Rashbam interprets the words of the baraita such that "fully formed eggs" means that the shell has been completed. And as for the fact that the Gemara there asks "in accordance with whom? Like Rabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov" — which implies that Rabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov is more stringent — one can validly say that he is indeed more stringent, and the interpretation of the baraita is as follows: it states, "if one found fully formed eggs in it, they are permitted to be eaten with kutach" — meaning even l'chatchila, but specifically if the shell has been completed; but if the hard shell has not been completed, it is forbidden to eat them l'chatchila with kutach. But b'dieved, it is implied from the view of the Tanna Kamma that there is no prohibition whatsoever — whether the eggs are fully formed or not fully formed — with regard to b'dieved. If so, according to the Tanna Kamma, the egg does not render garments impure at the gullet. And regarding this, the Beit Yosef states: if they were attached by sinews, they are forbidden even b'dieved. And this validly explains the Gemara's question "in accordance with whom? Like Rabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov," for according to the Rabbis, we do not distinguish at all between fully formed and not fully formed with regard to b'dieved — and if so, the egg would not render the garments impure at the gullet — therefore it must be like Rabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov. Or one can say that certainly the Rashbam also held that the Tanna Kamma's position is that once the white and the yolk have been completed, it is also permitted, but since not everyone is expert enough to assess which stage is called "completed," they gave the measure that there must be a hard shell, for then the egg has emerged from doubt. And Rabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov holds that the entire prohibition regarding the white and the yolk depends only on whether the egg is attached by sinews — and therefore, even if the white and yolk have been completed, if it is still attached by sinews, it is also forbidden. Therefore, it is valid that the Gemara there asks "in accordance with whom? Like Rabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov," for it teaches: one who eats from the cluster of eggs — meaning if they are still attached by sinews — they are still neveilah. Therefore it must be that the baraita follows Rabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov, for if it were according to the Rabbis, how could the baraita teach without qualification "one who eats from the cluster of eggs, etc." — for according to the Rabbis, sometimes a cluster of eggs is not neveilah — namely, if the white and the yolk have been completed. Therefore it must follow Rabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov, who holds that everything depends on whether the eggs are attached by sinews, and it is valid to state regarding the cluster of eggs that since they are attached by sinews, they have the status of neveilah itself. And study this carefully.
