And it appears to me therefore all the more, etc. Per Raavad's view it appears one must resolve that Taz wrote in siman 63 they are permitted in cooking and benefit — it is the arranged Mishnah in chapter 25 and agreed by all poskim — meaning permitted by law, but not because of marit ayin; and there is difference in cases where there is no concern for marit ayin such as for medical need, etc., as Maharshal wrote chapter 25 siman 102 regarding woman's milk that for medical need it is permitted even without any danger since there is no prohibition at all, only marit ayin — end of his words. Or you find it in other cases where there is no marit ayin concern, and thereby what the Rav left in the sage — Rashba's words in wonder — is resolved. Indeed poskim who wrote plainly 'permitted in cooking' imply even for marit ayin there is none — and so appear Maharshal's words chapter 25 and so appears Mechaber's view since he did not write impure animal milk itself is forbidden because of marit ayin; therefore it appears clear to me it is different there since in any case one of them is forbidden in eating — certainly in cooking alone there is no marit ayin, for one may cook for medical need or other purposes; and 'forbidden to cook them' means to eat them, as Mechaber wrote siman 95 'forbidden to cook them' — therefore must also explain 'to eat them'; and we find this in Scripture and Mishnah — they derived eating in the language of cooking — see there; and only in such a case is there marit ayin concern; and therefore per the Rav too one must say so, for even with domestic animal meat in almond milk he holds one need not place almonds except in cooking — as in the previous s.k.; and if so how did they cook together at first — great strain to say during cooking he placed almonds by the pot; rather certainly in cooking alone there is no marit ayin, therefore 'forbidden to cook meat in woman's milk' means to eat; and therefore impure animal meat in pure milk or the reverse — since they do not cook to eat, for in any case forbidden because of impure meat or milk — there is no marit ayin as above; and thereby Rashba's words the Rav left in the sage in wonder are resolved well; or with impure meat there is no marit ayin concern since recognizable as impure meat, and so impure milk as in Gemara Avodah Zarah (daf 35b) — pure milk is white, impure green; but woman's milk and almond milk are not recognizable and forbidden because of marit ayin — and thereby Rashba's words are resolved well; nevertheless the first explanation is primary.
But with fowl, which is rabbinic, there is no concern. This follows his view in seif 3; but per what he wrote in s.k. 6, with fowl too there is concern — and Rashba and Mechaber stated plainly without distinguishing domestic animal from fowl.
