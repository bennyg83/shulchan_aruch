(There, seif 4, in the hagahah) And it appears that according to this, all the more so it is forbidden to cook l'chatchila, etc. See in the Acharonim what they seek to resolve the difficulty of the Darkei Moshe, who left the words of the Rashba requiring further study. And one can say: granted, regarding non-kosher meat with kosher milk, etc. — there is reason to say that since in any case it is forbidden on account of its non-kosher status, it is an uncommon matter that anyone would come to cook it, and regarding an uncommon matter the Rabbis did not issue a decree upon it. Which is not the case with human milk, for by this very reason there is no prohibition whatsoever, and there is reason to be concerned, etc. And note carefully.
(Ibid.:) "And one does not receive lashes for eating it on account of basar b'chalav." This implies that, however, on account of blood one would receive lashes, even though it is dam shevishlo (blood that was cooked). It must therefore be said that he holds to distinguish between congealed and liquid blood: if it is congealed, one is not liable for dam shevishlo; and if it is liquid, one is liable. With this, what appeared at first glance as a contradiction in his words is resolved — for above in the laws of salting he wrote and implied many times that dam shevishlo is only d'rabbanan, whereas here it implies the opposite. Rather, it is certainly as stated above. And the source he cites for the Rambam and the Mechaber to distinguish between congealed and liquid is Tosafot in tractate Zevachim. {Gloss: What the Gaon, the author, wrote — that this is found in Tosafot of Zevachim — it seems that in Zevachim this statement of Tosafot is not found, and perhaps it is a scribal error, and it should read "in Menachot" (daf 21a, s.v. "kan"), for thus it is found there in Tosafot according to Rashi there. However, the matter requires further study, for according to Rashi there is no practical halachic difference at all in this — since according to Rashi even for congealed blood one is liable, and he holds that Rava disagrees there with Ze'iri. Moreover, Ze'iri's own statement that dam shevishlo is exempt applies only to the inner sin-offerings, but for the outer sin-offerings, and all the more so for chulin, even for congealed blood one is liable. According to Rashi's opinion there, that distinction between congealed and liquid applies only to congealing in the sun, but blood cooked in fire certainly always drains out, as is explained there. But in truth, to settle the Rambam's words more satisfactorily, it appears better to say as the Beit Yosef wrote here: that the Rambam holds like Rashi that dam shevishlo is truly liable with lashes. And as for what the Beit Yosef wondered regarding the sugya in Menachot mentioned above — perhaps he holds that the sugya of Menachot does not follow the halacha — though he did not clarify from where the Rambam derived that it does not follow the halacha. But in truth, according to what Rashi wrote there, it is evident that Rava disagrees with that statement, and Rava is the later authority. A great proof for this is that the Rambam does not cite at all in his laws the ruling of Ze'iri that one does not transgress for dam shevishlo, and therefore he must hold that it was rejected from the halacha. And as for what the Tur and the Mechaber copied the language of the Rambam — even though they certainly hold that dam shevishlo is d'rabbanan, as the Tur wrote at the end of siman 76 in the name of the Rashba explicitly [and so too the Mechaber, it is explained in many places that he holds it to be d'rabbanan] — and the Drisha already pointed this out here — I say there is no difficulty: since nowadays there are no lashes, they did not bother to be precise in this; and something similar is found in many places, and it is obvious. End of the gloss of the distinguished Gaon, etc., Moreinu HaRav Shlomo HaKohen, may his light shine, Moreh Tzedek of the holy community of Vilna.}
(There, in the hagahah) And if one curdled cheese in it, etc. — until there is sixty parts of milk against the liquid. And one can say: if the stomach is forbidden only through salting, to the measure of a peel, as the Mechaber and Rama wrote below in siman 98 that one does not need sixty against the peel that became forbidden — it must be said that this is like broth. And although through salting only the measure of a peel is forbidden, broth is more stringent than salting and forbids the entire quantity. And see Shach s.k. 88, seif 40, etc.
(Ibid.) And if the stomach was liquid and coagulated, etc. — and some are lenient in this. It appears from Tosafot that they challenged: why not establish the entire discussion before the return? And that which they do not say "because of the stomach itself" — because the main curdling is in the coagulated part, lest the Gemara holds that perhaps it was liquid at first; or the Tosafot hold like those who are lenient. However, from Tosafot's conclusion it appears the opposite, like those who forbid — as written in the previous s.k. And if they hold like those who forbid, they resolve well: since at first they do not separate the liquid, and afterward the liquid coagulates and is fit to curdle in, it is properly a curdling agent.
