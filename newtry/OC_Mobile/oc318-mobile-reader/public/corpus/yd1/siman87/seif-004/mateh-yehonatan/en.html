(Ibid.) "Exempt, and one does not receive lashes for eating them, etc." See in siman 88, where he wishes to prove from the words of Tosafot, chapter 25, daf 114, s.v. HaMevashel b'Mei Chalav, that "exempt" means exempt and it is permitted to cook l'chatchila. For Tosafot wrote: "One who cooks in milk whey is exempt, but there is indeed a prohibition, for kuttach is made from milk whey, and we said above that it is forbidden to eat meat with kuttach" — implying that only in that case is it forbidden l'chatchila, but other things where we do not find explicitly that they are forbidden, it is permitted to cook them l'chatchila. But his words have no basis whatsoever. On the contrary, from there it is proven that "exempt" here means exempt but forbidden. For one could ask: how does Tosafot bring proof from kuttach, which is forbidden to eat, to the matter of cooking — perhaps it is only forbidden to eat, but to cook is permitted even l'chatchila? It must therefore be that Tosafot holds that anything that is forbidden to eat is likewise forbidden to cook l'chatchila. If so, the same applies to all other prohibitions that our Sages forbade to eat on account of basar b'chalav — cooking is likewise forbidden.
