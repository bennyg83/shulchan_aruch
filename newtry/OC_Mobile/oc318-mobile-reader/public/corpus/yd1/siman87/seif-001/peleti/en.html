It is written in the Torah three times, etc. Hagahot Maimoniyot brings a challenge in name of Ra'em: why need "you shall not cook" — derive from "you shall not eat any abomination," etc.; and one cannot say if it were written "you shall not cook" we would not forbid cooking at all, only that eating is included in cooking language, for then it would not be "abomination" at all — this is forced, to extract cooking from its meaning onto eating; and one is not lashed for a negative that is included in a general prohibition. Still, for Rambam who holds liability on benefit, the negative "you shall not cook" referring to benefit prohibition is difficult — why need "you shall not eat any abomination" — and benefit prohibition is included in "you shall not cook" and one is certainly not lashed — investigate in Peleti at length on Hagahot Maimoniyot and Rambam chapter 9.
After cooking — and behold if roasting is also from the Torah forbidden, Peri Chadash and Acharonim followed Ran who holds roasting is not included; his proof from Sanhedrin beginning that one may not read "in milk" with bitter herbs because the Torah forbade only by way of cooking — Rashi, Tosafot, and "in milk" there is no cooking only frying. But I say the opposite: the baraita tanna brings it as proof there is a letter for the masoret — that tanna who holds there is a letter for the masoret holds the Torah forbade by way of cooking, and therefore this tanna's baraita proves even frying is included in cooking — on this Maharshal and others disagree — see Peleti.
All basar b'chalav — so Rambam and many authors; Maharshal chapter 25 siman 100 wondered: whence that all rabbinic enactments are like d'oraisa — so too chametz on Pesach — no difference between rabbinic and d'oraisa; he derives from "these are their prohibited items," etc., and challenged Tosafot: behold it teaches there — resolve perhaps the questioner deals with basar b'chalav of wild animal and fowl which is only rabbinic, and it teaches a piece fit to honor guests is not nullified even in rabbinic prohibition; and therefore one must say there is no rabbinic basar b'chalav in benefit prohibitions; and benefit rabbinic basar b'chalav is permitted — investigate; and see below siman 100 — see Peleti for further proof.
For benefit — Rambam wrote basar b'chalav forbidden for benefit is buried and even its ash is forbidden; this is correct in Mishnah Temurah; but I have doubt for one who says if impossible to squeeze out it is forbidden, and all the more for one who says if impossible it is permitted — for all agree as Ran wrote: if clearly squeezed meat taste from milk the meat would be permitted only impossible to squeeze — if Elijah came and testified it was squeezed it would be permitted; if so when burned to ash certainly milk taste departed and was clearly squeezed — how forbidden? Now in a piece existing it would be permitted, all the more when burned to dust; and I wonder on "gives detrimental taste is permitted" because of nevelah not fit for a ger — investigate Peleti on Rambam and Ran at length.
