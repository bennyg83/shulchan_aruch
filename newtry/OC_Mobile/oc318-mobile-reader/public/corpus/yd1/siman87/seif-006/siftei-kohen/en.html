One is not flogged for it. But there is nevertheless a prohibition; and so milk of a dead animal — there is a prohibition, as below siman 90 regarding udder, and as the Rav wrote in hagahah nearby; and all imply there is a prohibition even in male milk; and what the Rav wrote in hagahah — male milk is not called milk at all, etc. — will be explained in s.k. 16.
In milk of a dead animal. Meaning milk that came out after its death; and 'dead' means by itself or slaughtered, as below siman 90 regarding udder — investigate.
And one is not flogged for eating it because of basar b'chalav. This wording Rambam wrote; it appears he holds it is because of basar b'chalav that one is not flogged for eating it — whereas for blood one is flogged for eating even if cooked, for he holds one who cooks blood violates it; however we hold blood cooked or salted is only rabbinic, as above siman 69 s.k. 39 and in many places.
And male milk is not called milk, etc. It appears since it is not called milk there is not even a rabbinic prohibition forbidding the food; Orach Chaim there wrote it is no worse than woman's milk; from the Rav the Maggid chapter 9 Forbidden Foods it appears male milk is forbidden rabbinically, and he wrote so appears from Rambam whom Mechaber copied; and somewhat in Gemara chapter 25 (daf 113b) Shmuel said there: in its mother's milk and not male milk, in its mother's milk and not in milk of slaughtered animal; if so since one verse excludes them and milk of slaughtered animal we hold is rabbinically forbidden as below siman 90 — so too male milk; and the Maggid appears to discuss male milk of animal, as Rashi explained there: and not male milk that had little milk from teats such as if it changed and grew teats; so Maharshal there siman 100; and Gemara clearly implies: afterward he asked one who cooks milk of a kid that did not nurse — what is the law? He said: since Shmuel needed to say in its mother's milk and not male milk — male milk does not come under 'if'; but this one since it comes under 'if' is forbidden — learn male milk of animal; but male milk of man is obviously no worse than woman's milk; therefore Mechaber who copied Rambam implies also male milk is exempt but forbidden; and the Rav in hagahah did not disagree.
And milk-water, etc. And it is written in Orach Chaim there that even that milk-water remaining after they make cheese forbids the food — end.
And some say it is forbidden to stoke the fire, etc. And Mordechai there wrote it is forbidden to cook forbidden meat in an Israelite's house in a gentile's pot lest the Israelite stoke it — therefore one needs two gentile maidservants, one pot of meat and one of milk; however this is only a mere stringency and people are not careful about it — end.
Forbidden for benefit. Meaning: when he rinsed them in boiling water and mixed them so boiling — if not, it is not forbidden for benefit since it is not forbidden from the Torah except by way of cooking, as above seif 1 and seif 3 — end.
And habit to mix meat and milk there. And that it is not forbidden from this for head-washing — for one benefits — since it is not a matter of eating it is not considered benefit when one does not benefit from the body of the prohibition, as will be explained below siman 94 see there; and above siman 108 seif 4 in hagahah and siman 155 in hagahah — end.
And even l'chatchila, etc. See in the sage general rule 76; and it appears there that in metal pots one need not be stringent even l'chatchila, for even if something splashed it would return and become white by the fire — see there — end.
