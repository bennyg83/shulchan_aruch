(There, seif 6) Blood in milk is exempt and one does not receive lashes for eating it on account of basar b'chalav. This is the language of the Rambam, Beit Yosef, and Shach, who inferred from this that on account of basar b'chalav one does not receive lashes for eating it, but on account of blood one would receive lashes for eating it even though it was cooked, as Rashi's view in chapter Kol HaBasar (daf 109). And what they rejected from the sugya in Menachot from halacha 17 — one can say because they relied on the baraita brought in Chullin chapter HaOr veHaRotav (daf 120): he congealed the blood and they ate it, or he curdled the milk and they drank it raw — he is liable. And they hold this is congealing in fire, similar to milk, for certainly it deals when he curdled it in fire; for if in the sun it is alive and exempt, as they say in Pesachim (daf 24) that one who eats raw milk is exempt. And Tosafot there in Pesachim noted this, that they strained to say it teaches sides: congealing the blood in the sun and curdling the milk in fire. But Rashi and the Rambam are not comfortable explaining thus, but rather congealing the blood refers also to fire and he is liable. However, the principal inference of Beit Yosef and Shach, who inferred from the Rambam's words that on account of blood one would be lashed, for he holds dam shevishlo is forbidden d'oraisa — one can reject: one can say the Rambam deals when it was not cooked like food of ben drusai, for on account of basar b'chalav it is cooking; for behold he omitted that which is taught in chapter Kol HaBasar: in what cooking did they speak — in cooking that others eat it (and see what I wrote above in seif 1). But regarding blood that was cooked he holds one is not exempt except in cooking that is like food of ben drusai, as the view of responsa Beit Yaakov siman 172. But in any event the Rambam also holds that dam shevishlo is exempt.
