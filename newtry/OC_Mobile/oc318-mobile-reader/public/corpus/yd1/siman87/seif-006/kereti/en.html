Smoked, etc. — Peri Chadash wrote since Yerushalmi Shabbat says smoked is cooking for Shabbat, the same for basar b'chalav; and see Peleti one may reject that for basar b'chalav we require a way of cooking.
Because of basar b'chalav — it appears that for blood they would lash, for this is Rambam's view that one who cooked blood violates; but as we hold cooked blood is rabbinic, there is no [Torah liability] even for blood.
Forbidden to stoke, etc. — although it is nevelah meat and basar b'chalav does not apply to cooking, nevertheless it applies; and so Rambam; and see Peleti where I discussed cooking nevelah meat in milk whether forbidden for benefit, resolving challenges on Rambam and Tosafot Shevuot — see there.
To stoke — Shach wrote: the same for all vessels used for cooking — forbidden to stoke if absorption of meat and milk is cooked; and it is only a mere stringency — end; and see Peleti whether absorption in a vessel is cooking for basar b'chalav; also whether cooking after cooking applies in basar b'chalav.
Forbidden for benefit — meaning in a kli rishon, and it should be understood as cooking; and that they do not forbid because of command to a non-Jew — a rabbinic shvut in other prohibitions means it was not clarified; and even for basar b'chalav command to non-Jew is rabbinic shvut — meaning we are not certain there is not sixty against the prohibition in the water, only a Torah doubt to be stringent; but regarding shvut, rabbinic doubt is lenient.
That one may not use them — and it is difficult: since that ash has concern for mixture of basar b'chalav and you forbid its use, how do they permit putting kiln ash into the cauldron — behold it cooks basar b'chalav; and if there is no cooking concern, even benefit concern should be permitted as Peleti wrote, resolving many challenges of Taz — when is nat bar lichta permitted and when forbidden — see there.
