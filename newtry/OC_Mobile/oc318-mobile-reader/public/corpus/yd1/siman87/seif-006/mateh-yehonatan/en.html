(There, in the Hagahah:) Some say it is forbidden to stoke a fire under the pot of a non-Jew, etc. Even though it is the meat of a non-Jew and is neveilah, and [therefore the principle of] ein issur chal al issur [might be invoked] — one must say: since even according to the view of the poskim who hold that ta'am k'ikar is d'oraisa in other prohibitions — that applies [only] when a k'zayit became mixed [in a quantity consumed] within k'dei achilat pras; but in [the case of] basar b'chalav, even less than a k'zayit [consumed] within k'dei achilat pras is d'oraisa — according to this, one can say there is no [invocation of] ein issur chal al issur here. Namely, if a k'zayit of neveilah meat fell into ten k'zeitim or more [of milk]: if so, regarding the neveilah [prohibition], the milk does not become forbidden, since there is no k'zayit within k'dei achilat pras; but regarding basar b'chalav, the milk is forbidden. If so, one can say the Rama was concerned for the view of the poskim — thus [ruling] stringently.
