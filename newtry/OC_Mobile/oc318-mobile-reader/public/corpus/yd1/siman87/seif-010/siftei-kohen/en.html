L'chatchila one should not place it, etc. The reason is explained there that stomach milk is sharp and sour since they set rennet in it — end.
That it was salted in its stomach. Beit Yosef wrote: and the measure required for it to remain in salting to be considered boiling was explained in siman 69 regarding salting in a vessel that is not perforated; and the measure of its salting to be boiling Mechaber wrote in siman 91 — end; if so his view is it must remain in salting until they place it on fire and it begins to boil, as above siman 69 seif 18; and it must be salted so much that it is not eaten because of its salt, as below siman 91 seif 5; and it appears that even for us, who hold salting forbids immediately, and even if not salted except a little as they salt for roasting — nevertheless one may permit here b'dieved if they set rennet in it when not salted so much, since in any case many poskim and Mechaber among them consider it mere excrement even when clear — end.
Or it stood in it a day. Meaning: by itself, for it is like pickled — end.
Forbidden to set rennet in it. It appears there is no distinction between dried and clear — and this is l'chatchila; and although one may cook when dried even l'chatchila, as written in s.k. 25 there — that case is different since there is no prohibition except because of milk, and since dried it is like excrement; but here, although excrement, since it was salted or stood by itself in the stomach, it certainly received taste from the stomach skin and therefore l'chatchila forbidden to set rennet in it to make cheese; however b'dieved Mechaber's view in his book Beit Yosef is to permit in every case, and therefore he stated here without distinction; and the Rav in hagahah does not agree — end.
Until there are sixty against the milk, etc. Meaning: until there are sixty against the milk in which rennet was set and cheeses were made, against the milk that was in the stomach forbidden in the stomach because of basar b'chalav; and apparently it is difficult: why does sixty suffice? For the milk in the stomach is forbidden in itself and became nevelah, and we say below siman 101 that the law of a piece fit to honor guests applies only when the prohibition is because of itself such as nevelah and basar b'chalav — perforce; if so here, since it is a setting agent, even in a thousand it is not nullified, as written in seif 11; and so Mordechai chapter 25 and responsum of Maimonides siman 18 see there; responsum of R' Shimon bar Avraham in a case where they made cheese from milk in a stomach and afterward found in the sack a little of the thin milk — the cheeses are forbidden since the prohibition sets — meaning it imparts taste — end; and although Tur also wrote as the Rav wrote, one may say Tur holds a setting agent that is forbidden nullifies in sixty, as Rabbenu Tam in Mordechai and in responsum of Maimonides there; and one may say specifically a setting agent forbidden in itself such as nevelah we say it is not nullified, unlike here where the setting agent is not forbidden in itself but because of mixture of basar b'chalav — therefore it nullifies well (and even if we say actual basar b'chalav forbidden by cooking is considered the substance of the prohibition and if one set rennet afterward it forbids in any amount — nevertheless here, since forbidden only by salting and pickling which is only rabbinic, the Rav holds it is not considered substance of prohibition regarding setting in any amount — see there; however Orach Chaim general rule 47 law 8 asks whether pickling is considered like cooked for this and it appears the Rav is lenient on this); and responsum of R' Shimon bar Avraham follows his view that even setting in kosher stomach skin is not nullified, as explained in his words there; but for us, who do not hold so, as Mechaber wrote in seif 11 — if so, although we forbid the milk because it absorbed from the stomach, nevertheless sixty suffices — investigate, and it is somewhat strained — end.
And if the stomach was dried, etc. Meaning: if the milk in the stomach was dried it forbids nothing even if there were not sixty against the milk in which rennet was set, against the milk in the stomach; the reason: when dried the milk is considered mere excrement and one cannot say it received taste from the stomach skin and imparts taste to the cheeses, and it is not second taste permitting the first, for the second taste of prohibition is like onions absorbed from meat and cooked in a milk pot — Taz siman 94 seif 6 wrote one needs at least sixty against the meat in the onions; and here too we would need sixty against the milk that was in the stomach, for we do not know how much it absorbed from the stomach; but this case is different, for there is no power in taste that this excrement received from the skin to when it later joins actual cheese milk to carry meat taste with it and make basar b'chalav — worse than second taste, since it is pure excrement; and Taz wrote: although it nevertheless received meat taste and again imparts that taste to cheeses set in it, it is only second taste permitting the first, like fish that rose into a bowl permitted to eat them in kutach, as will be explained below siman 95 — it appears to me — end; and he did not aim well, for we do not hold regarding fish in a bowl that it is second taste permitting the first before it came to prohibition, for meat imparted taste to the bowl and the bowl to fish — second taste permitting the first, and therefore permitted to eat them afterward in kutach; but here it is only one second taste permitting the first — the stomach in dried milk; and all poskim agree in such a case it is not second taste permitting the first, as explained in siman 95; and in truth Beit Yosef wrote: the reason is there is no power in taste this excrement received, etc. — fish that received meat taste are permitted to join afterward with milk, and all the more here since it is excrement and a nullified matter with no power to receive meat taste and carry it to join with cheese milk to make basar b'chalav — worse than second taste since pure excrement — end; and he understood here too it is second taste permitting the first, only because excrement it is worse than second taste permitting the first — but it is not so, for here it is not second taste permitting the first at all, as I wrote — rather since pure excrement it is worse than second taste permitting the first — and this is clear — end.
And in a case of loss one may be lenient. And even per what is written in s.k. 25 that even when actually dried some are stringent — it appears here in clear and congealed one may also be lenient in great loss, since there is only a rabbinic prohibition, and one may rely in such a case on responsum of R' Shimon bar Avraham in Mordechai and responsum of Maimonides, and so is the view of Semak and ShaDa; and further that in the sage general rule 79 law 3 he wrote b'dieved one may rely on those who are lenient — it appears even without great loss; if so he equates the law of clear and congealed entirely to dried law; and if so, even per those who say where there is no loss one should be stringent even when dried — here too in clear and congealed; and further, Rif, Rambam, other poskim, and Mechaber among them permit even when clear; Bach in siman 81 ruled regarding actually dried like the Rav, and regarding clear and congealed wrote we practice to be stringent on a d'oraisa prohibition and it has the law of clear, even in great loss — not like hagahah of Shulchan Aruch siman 87 — end; and it is astonishing: what d'oraisa prohibition is here? From the Torah it is forbidden only by way of cooking, and this milk in the stomach was forbidden only by salting or pickling; perhaps he refers only to milk found in a kosher stomach that nursed from a tereifah — but his wording implies all cases; and further, if so what did he challenge against the Rav? Nevertheless it appears one may permit even in kosher stomach that nursed from tereifah when clear and congealed in great loss, for many poskim permit even when clear; if so all those poskim are fit from the outset to equate clear and congealed to dried, to rely on them in such a case; and one may also say Rashba who wrote in Torat HaBayit the short version 'congealed in the animal's lifetime' — his words are not a decisive ruling; one may say he stated the usual way to equate with all those poskim above; and in Torat HaBayit the long version he did not mention this at all; on the contrary it appears the opposite, for he wrote in Torat HaBayit the long version (section 1 side 2): and Rabbenu Tam's words appear that what we say one may set rennet in milk in the stomach means when dried, for all that congealed has the law of excrement; and the usual way is one does not set rennet in clear milk except in dried — end; it appears he came to explain what the tanna teaches without distinguishing dried and clear — because the usual way is one sets in dried; and if you say clear and congealed he holds forbidden — still the tanna need not teach anonymously; rather certainly once congealed afterward it is considered congealed in the animal's lifetime; also what Beit Yosef wrote that it appears from all poskim we require congealed in the animal's lifetime is not required at all — end.
Stomach skin sometimes, etc. And the same applies to other innards when they dry them until they become like wood; nevertheless it appears l'chatchila one should not do so — end.
