(Ibid.) And if there was sixty in the milk, everything is permitted. See Shach s.k. 37, who challenged: for us, a curdling agent is not nullified — see his words at length. And one can say: the Tosafot wrote on Avodah Zarah daf 35a, s.v. chada katani, etc., that nevertheless in our locale they curdle cheese in a stomach; R' Yitzchak, son of R' Chaim, said there is some reason to forbid it because the stomach was salted in its skin and there is a basar b'chalav prohibition through salting — for salting is like boiling, etc. — see there. And they challenge: why mention the reason of basar b'chalav, which is only d'rabbanan through salting? It should have mentioned the reason of nevelah taste absorbed in the stomach skin through salting, which would be a d'oraisa prohibition. And they resolve: if the reason were nevelah absorbed in the stomach, we would not forbid because of curdling — for the stomach itself is the curdling agent and is heter; therefore they mention the reason of basar b'chalav, for the stomach itself is also like the body of the prohibition, and they correctly say regarding it that it is a curdling agent and is not nullified. And so too Rama's words here can be explained: since through pickling and salting it is not forbidden d'oraisa through basar b'chalav but only that Chazal forbade it, therefore it is not so stringent that the milk should be considered the body of the prohibition and we do not say regarding it that a curdling agent is not nullified; therefore sixty helps, as Shach wrote s.k. 30 — see there.
