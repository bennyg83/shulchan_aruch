(Ibid., seif 9) The milk found in the stomach, etc. And some interpret that he rules it forbidden, and it is noted that this is the opinion of Rabbeinu Tam, who rules it forbidden when it is liquid, and the case deals with liquid milk. So too the Mechaber wrote above in siman 81, seif 5 and seif 11, regarding milk found in the stomach of a kosher animal that nursed from a treifa. This was the difficulty Rabbeinu Tam had with the baraita's teaching that a stomach cooked in its own milk is forbidden — for this cannot be reconciled according to the approach of the Rif, who holds that after the return it has the status of mere excretion in all cases, even when the milk within it is liquid. If so, why would a stomach cooked in its milk be forbidden? He was therefore compelled to distinguish between coagulated and liquid: when the stomach is cooked in its milk, this refers to the liquid. And at first glance it appears that Rabbeinu Tam interprets the distinction between before the return and after the return as follows: before the return, he held that the milk does not have the status of mere excretion; after the return, the established ruling is that it does have the status of mere excretion in all cases — and this applies to the coagulated milk. But it is certain that whether before the return or after the return, if the milk does not have the status of excretion, it has the status of the animal's own body and is forbidden. Therefore, liquid milk, which does not have the status of excretion, should be characterized as having the status of the animal's own body. If so, a treifa that nursed from a kosher animal should also be forbidden with respect to liquid milk. This would present a difficulty with what the Mechaber wrote — "and some rule it forbidden regarding the liquid that nursed from the treifa" — which implies that a treifa that nursed from a kosher animal is permitted even regarding the liquid. And according to what was stated above regarding Rabbeinu Tam's approach, there is no basis for this distinction. It must therefore be said according to Rabbeinu Tam that the coagulated milk — whether before or after the return — was always permitted, being counted as mere excretion. But the distinction between before the return and afterward refers to the liquid: before the return, he held that the liquid has the status of the animal's own body, and therefore a treifa that nursed from a kosher animal is also forbidden, as it is like the animal's own body. But afterward, he held that it does not have the status of the animal's own body. This vindicates the words of the Mechaber above in siman 81, where he wrote that specifically a kosher animal that nursed from a treifa is forbidden — because it does not have the status of mere excretion — whereas a treifa that nursed from a kosher animal: even though it also does not have the status of mere excretion, nevertheless it is not considered the animal's own body but rather as something gathered within it, and it is permitted. This is also evident in Tosafot in tractate Avodah Zarah, daf 35a. And it is possible to say that what the Mechaber wrote here — "and some rule it forbidden" — reflects the approach of Rashi, who holds that even after the return, even the coagulated milk is not counted as mere excretion. Rather, the distinction between before the return and afterward is: before the return, he held that the milk has the status of the animal's body; after the return, he held that it does not have the status of the animal's body. And as for the Gemara's statement — "the halacha is: one may not curdle using a non-kosher substance, but one may curdle using a kosher substance, etc." — the question remains regarding a kosher stomach that nursed from a treifa: why is this so, since it does not have the status of mere excretion? One can answer that the Sages forbade it only in its intact state, but this halachic ruling pertains to the matter of curdling — regarding the remaining substance, it is permitted. That is to say, in such a case the Sages did not extend their stringency to forbid the cheese. Reflect well on this.
