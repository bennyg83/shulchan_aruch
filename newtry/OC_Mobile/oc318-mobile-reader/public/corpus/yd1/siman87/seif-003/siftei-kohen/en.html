But pure meat in impure milk, etc. Tur's language: but pure meat in milk of an impure animal, or impure meat in pure milk, and meat of wild animal and fowl — even in pure milk — is only d'rabbanan and permitted in cooking and benefit — end. Beit Yosef wrote: the wording is imprecise when he says 'only d'rabbanan' — it can refer only to wild animal and fowl in pure milk; for pure meat in impure milk or the reverse we do not find the Sages forbade eating because of basar b'chalav, and there was no reason to forbid it rabbinically after it is already forbidden and standing from the Torah because of impure meat or impure milk, etc. — end. Therefore he corrected the wording here and aimed well; but Bach challenged him and explained Tur comes to teach us that nevertheless rabbinically it is forbidden because of basar b'chalav, and there is a difference regarding a piece becoming nevelah, for he holds (below siman 92) only with meat and milk; and likewise regarding a piece fit to honor guests — see there; and so Perishah explained. Therefore even for us, who hold noten taam noten taam in all prohibitions, there would have been practical difference regarding what the Rav wrote in hagahah siman 92 seif 4 — see there and investigate. In my humble opinion this is wrong: we certainly do not find anywhere, nor did any posek omit writing this; and from Rashi and Ran (chapter 25 daf 113a in the Mishnah) it appears explicitly there is no prohibition because of basar b'chalav at all, even rabbinically; and there is no reason the Sages would forbid it because of basar b'chalav — for with fowl meat a decree fits well: if they ate fowl in milk they would also eat domestic animal meat in milk, as Rambam wrote there; but what relevance to decree that pure domestic meat in impure milk or the reverse be forbidden because of basar b'chalav, for without this there is nothing to fear since it is already forbidden because of impure meat or impure milk — and this appears Beit Yosef's intent in what he wrote 'and there was no reason to forbid it,' not as Bach's wording that it is already forbidden in eating from the Torah and they need not decree — and he understood Beit Yosef comes to say there is no difference whether forbidden because of basar b'chalav since already forbidden from the Torah, and therefore he answered there is a difference, etc.; rather Beit Yosef's intent is as I explained, and so is primary for law, since a piece fit to honor guests itself is rabbinic as below siman 110; also Derishah wrote at the end of his words: but it is still difficult that we do not find anywhere that pure meat in impure milk or the reverse has a rabbinic prohibition and becomes nevelah rabbinically — perforce.
And meat of wild animal and fowl, etc. — and even in eating, etc. Mechaber's view: meat of wild animal and fowl is not forbidden in eating even by cooking, only rabbinically, and permitted for benefit and in cooking entirely; Maharshal and Bach relied on Mahari Weil in hagahah ShaDa siman 76 where he wrote it is difficult to be lenient against Tosafot — therefore they ruled wild animal and fowl in milk is d'oraisa; in my book I upheld those who say it is rabbinic from the Gemara, and I brought there that so is the view of many poskim, and even Mahari Weil only said it is difficult to be lenient against Tosafot not to eat cheese immediately after fowl meat, but he admits the law is fowl in milk is rabbinic, for he wrote in hagahah ShaDa siman 69 complete eggs are permitted in milk because fowl in milk is rabbinic and they were lenient (and so R' Nissim in what he wrote siman 69 that Mahari Weil holds primary like Beit HaGefen — see there); and although Tosafot tied the law of eating cheese after fowl to whether fowl in milk is d'oraisa or rabbinic, nevertheless it is not required as written there; and from Tosafot themselves there it appears it is not required to say so, only they came to reconcile world practice — see there. In truth one may say another way: even as we hold fowl in milk is rabbinic, it is forbidden to eat cheese immediately after it, as below siman 89; and the baraita 'wage of fowl and cheese are eaten at dessert' we explain as Rambam and his supporters explain, and Beit Yosef brings siman 89 — meaning one eats cheese and afterward fowl; and there is further to distinguish in several aspects; and Rambam wrote end of chapter 2 of Laws of Rebels that one who says fowl in milk is forbidden from the Torah violates bal tosif — see there.
But fish, etc. Taz wrote: nevertheless one should not eat fish in milk because of danger, as explained in Orach Chaim siman 173 — end. In truth he followed Beit Yosef who wrote so, but it is an error, for there only fish with meat is forbidden because of danger, but with milk we have not heard nor seen; every day we practice cooking fish in milk and eating; Darkei Moshe also challenged Beit Yosef, and in Be'er Sheva daf 100 and other Acharonim challenged him; and in the Gemara chapter Kol HaTzlamim (daf 76b) it appears explicitly there is not even because of danger — see there — and it is simple.
They practice making almond milk, etc. since, etc. Meaning: and one could say regarding fish blood siman 66 seif 6 that it is entirely permitted; nevertheless if placed in a vessel it is forbidden because of marit ayin unless there are scales recognizable as fish blood — for there the blood itself is d'oraisa forbidden, therefore the viewer will say he eats animal blood forbidden d'oraisa; but fowl meat in milk itself is rabbinic and there is no concern for marit ayin; therefore domestic animal meat in milk, which is d'oraisa forbidden, one should place almonds near it so the viewer not think it is real milk — and so in the sage there end of general rule 62; therefore it must speak of cooking, for otherwise even domestic animal meat has no d'oraisa prohibition and need not place almonds — and so is clear in the sage there explicitly — see there and in signs of the sage. But Maharshal chapter 25 siman 52 learned from fish blood on Purim and similar when they regularly eat chicken in almond milk — forbidden unless he places almonds inside and beside for recognition, especially with fowl meat where there is more concern lest they say fowl in milk is permitted even rabbinically like R' Yosi HaGelili — end; and Acharonim agreed, and so it appears to me (again Be'er Sheva came to hand and I found he ruled so): for even in rabbinic matters we are concerned for marit ayin in many places, including Gemara chapter Bamah Beheimah (Shabbat daf 54) 'and not a pair' even though plugged — lest it appear one goes to the market — Rashi: to the market to sell, and this is marit ayin, as in chapter Bamah Ishah (end Shabbat daf 64) and agreed by all poskim, as explained in Orach Chaim siman 305 although commerce on Shabbat is only rabbinic, as in Orach Chaim and Choshen Mishpat siman 235; and so is clear in Tosafot Ketubot chapter Afelu (daf 60a) s.v. mima'akan, etc. — that even in rabbinic matters we are concerned for marit ayin — and so appears in many places.
