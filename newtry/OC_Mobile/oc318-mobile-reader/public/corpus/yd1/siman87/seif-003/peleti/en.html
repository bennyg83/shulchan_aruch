Permitted in cooking — see Bach that his view is rabbinically forbidden with difference for chein nafsho; I said practical difference: if an olive-size of slaughtered meat and olive-size impure meat fell into sixty olives of milk — it would be permitted, for milk does not receive from slaughtered meat, only sixty receives from impure meat; and conversely they nullify each other in noten taam — no milk prohibition taste, only less than sixty from impure and slaughtered; but now rabbinically forbidden, behold two meat olives forbidding in milk and no sixty — one may say the Sages decreed lest sometimes two pure olives fall and permit — investigate; see Peleti.
And wild animal and fowl — Rambam's view; understand the Gemara: therefore R' Akiva holds so because he holds prohibition applies upon prohibition — therefore no need "kid" to exclude milk of dead animal, and extra to exclude wild animal and fowl; but Rambam rules no prohibition upon prohibition and basar b'chalav does not apply to milk of dead animal — whence to exclude wild animal and fowl? One must say Rambam holds like Tosafot Chullin 101 that the entire sugya is Shmuel according to R' Akiva, but truly R' Akiva holds no prohibition upon prohibition — perforce better to exclude in "kid" wild animal and fowl than to say milk and dead animal in eating, unnecessary without prohibition upon prohibition — see Peleti on Rambam and Tosafot.
And they place fowl meat in it, etc. — Acharonim wondered: we find marit ayin itself in rabbinic prohibition without lengthy discussion — all this law is from blood in a vessel and cooked blood is rabbinic — see Chullin 111 Tosafot s.v. blood d'oraisa; Peri Toledot rejected comparing rabbinic decrees; I add: until here they forbade fish blood which is real blood only the Torah permitted; Rashbam on woman's milk which is real milk like animal milk only permitted — marit ayin applies; almond milk is not milk but fruit juice, only resembles milk — see Peleti on Rama and Maharshal.
