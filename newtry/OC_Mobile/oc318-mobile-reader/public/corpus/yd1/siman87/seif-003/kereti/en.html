Pure animal — see Peleti on settling Rambam's words.
And impure domestic meat — Bach's view: forbidden rabbinically because of basar b'chalav with practical difference for chein nafsho; and further difference. Later authorities wondered why Chazal decreed thus since it is already forbidden because of impure meat — where is the hint in Chazal's words? Perhaps pure meat in impure milk permitted in cooking and benefit — the extra 'permitted in cooking and benefit' is superfluous; it should say only 'permitted,' and it is obvious it does not refer to eating, for when impure food is eaten the precision is there — forbidden in eating because of basar b'chalav.
Since it is only rabbinic — Shach and Taz disagree; and many rabbinic prohibitions were decreed because of marit ayin without need to search, for this law is learned from blood placed in a vessel, etc., and blood that was cooked is rabbinic; nevertheless Peri Chadash agreed to permit, for one may not compare rabbinic decrees to one another. And I add it is not milk but fruit juice — almond milk; because it resembles milk in appearance it is forbidden; red wine did not change though it resembles blood — so Chazal forbade woman's milk and fish blood which is real blood and milk; and see Peleti.
