Permitted in cooking and benefit. Tur wrote: but pure meat in impure milk or the reverse, and wild animal meat, etc., is only rabbinic and permitted in cooking, etc. Beit Yosef challenged: if one impure species, behold it is forbidden in eating from the Torah — why decree basar b'chalav for eating? Derishah and Maharal resolve there is practical difference in this decree that the piece itself becomes nevelah, as Rav Ephraim holds in siman 92 only in basar b'chalav — and the main reason of Rav Ephraim who is stringent in basar b'chalav is that each alone is permitted and only mixtures are forbidden becoming one body, as Beit Yosef brings from Tosafot in siman 92 — and this does not apply here with one impure species, for without this there is Torah prohibition — and one may say the Sages decreed here that the piece itself becomes nevelah unlike other d'oraisa basar b'chalav — if so they should also decree in cooking and benefit since eating is already forbidden from the Torah and they were stringent on the mixture — they should be stringent here too; and from these words Maharal also challenged what he wrote regarding a piece fit to honor guests in siman 101 — investigate.
But fish, etc. Beit Yosef wrote nevertheless one should not eat fish in milk because of danger — and it is an error; it should say with meat as in Orach Chaim siman 173; and so Darkei Moshe — Beit Yosef confused fish with meat here in basar b'chalav, and Leket Yosher copied as in Beit Yosef and it is not correct.
As explained above siman 66 regarding blood. Darkei Moshe and Beit Yosef general rule 62 wrote Maharshal learned from fish blood to forbid eating fowl in almond milk unless almonds are placed because of marit ayin; and he rejected his words — no proof from blood where we find karet prohibition, unlike fowl in milk which is rabbinic; and even domestic animal is forbidden only by way of cooking — therefore no marit ayin concern — end; and although Rama's words are correct and custom spread thus, nevertheless l'chatchila one should place almonds even beside fowl meat; but where he has no almonds one should not forbid eating because whatever can be fixed we fix; and it is no worse than woman's milk nearby where they were concerned for marit ayin — but if no almonds one does not forbid eating as with woman's milk nearby; and Rama's wording 'one should place' implies b'dieved or when impossible to place does not block eating.
