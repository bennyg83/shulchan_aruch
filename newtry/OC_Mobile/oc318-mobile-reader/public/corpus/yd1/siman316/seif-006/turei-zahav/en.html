Until the seller informs him. This is Rabbi Yochanan’s view, which I copied above in seif 3. The “some say” hold like Shmuel above, that the doubt is lest he slaughter it immediately. The Derishah challenged this from what is written in seif 16 regarding oto ve-et beno, that if its mother was slaughtered today, the seller must inform him, and whenever he did not inform him we are not concerned, which follows Rabbi Yochanan here. He answered that the ordinary person who buys an animal buys it in order to slaughter it immediately, and therefore the seller certainly informs him; for the same reason, here too, even if it had not already given birth, he need not inform him that it had not previously birthed, for presumably he will slaughter it immediately; end quote. These are astonishing words, for in the first chapter of Chullin he wrote regarding one who slaughtered a healthy animal with the knife of a gentile, that there is no prohibition because it is damaging, since a living animal stands to produce offspring, etc. Furthermore, if so, the reason of Rav and Rabbi Yochanan would be difficult, for against each one we would ask: does it not presumably stand for slaughter? It appears that the essential question can be answered as follows: certainly there we say properly that if there were a prohibition, he would inform him, because there is no counter-argument against this; therefore we certainly follow that reasoning. This is not so here, where there are two conflicting reasons: if it had already birthed, he would have praised it; and opposite that, if it had not yet birthed, he should have informed him to separate him from prohibition. Therefore according to Shmuel the matter remains in doubt. This is clear and simple in my humble opinion.
