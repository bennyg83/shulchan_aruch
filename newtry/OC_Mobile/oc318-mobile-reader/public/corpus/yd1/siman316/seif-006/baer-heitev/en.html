Doubt. The reason is that perhaps the seller thought the buyer needed it for slaughter, and therefore did not inform him. With this, the law above in siman 16 seif 7 regarding oto ve-et beno is understood: there we say that presumably it is permitted, for if not he would have informed him, because the seller must inform him; there too, even if we say the buyer wants it for slaughter, it would be forbidden because of oto ve-et beno. End quote from Shach. The Taz answers differently: there we properly say that if there were a prohibition he would have informed him, for there is no contrary reasoning. Here, however, there are two opposing arguments: if it had already given birth, he would have praised his merchandise by saying so; against that stands the argument that if it had not yet given birth, he should have informed him to separate him from prohibition. Therefore the matter remains in doubt. End quote.
