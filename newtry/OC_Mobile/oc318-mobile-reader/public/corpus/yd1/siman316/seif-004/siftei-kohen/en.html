And we know that it did not give birth. Meaning that it gives milk although it did not previously give birth. Only when it nurses an offspring do we presume that it gave birth to that offspring, so the next offspring is exempt. Otherwise, according to the Mechaber, the next offspring is exempt in any event; according to our practice, ordinary milk does not exempt unless we saw it nursing. We then say that the nursing offspring may be the one it has just borne because it follows her, and the next offspring is exempt either way.
It is presumed to have borne it. This is not certainty, but a presumption sufficient to render a later offspring born in Jewish ownership a doubtful firstborn. It parallels siman 16:5, where an offspring following an animal is presumed to be its young for the prohibition of slaughtering mother and young, but not for lashes. Here too the offspring is a doubtful firstborn and may be eaten by the owner after it develops a blemish. The next offspring is exempt either way, because even if this was not hers she would not show maternal affection unless she had given birth.
