(Likkut) And according to this the rule is: although we do not suspect a minority like Rabbi Meir, as stated in Chullin 11b, “and Rabbi Meir, how,” etc., nevertheless where there is a presumption together with it, we are concerned, as in the case here. This is specifically for stringency, but not for leniency, for the sages disagree with Rabbi Meir in the case in Kiddushin chapter 1 regarding most children handling and a minority, etc. A minority that is uncommon does not join even for stringency, as stated regarding most who are found near slaughter are experts, even though an animal in its lifetime stands under the presumption of prohibition. Only regarding a married woman do we act stringently lechatchilah, like water with no end, and “they accept gifts and then betroth.” Tosafot there; see there. What he wrote about a minority with a presumption is according to the view that milk does not exempt; but according to the first view, no, as Tosafot wrote there: and now there is no proof for Rabbenu Tam, etc. All this follows Tosafot's approach, that the sugya at the beginning of Yevamot chapter 16 follows Rabbi Meir; see the Rosh there. But according to the Ramban, Rashba, and Nimukei Yosef, who explain that it follows even the sages and that in such a case they agree, then this law is according to all. According to them, the case in Kiddushin is also half and half, as I wrote above in the Likkutim to Even HaEzer 156:13. A very common minority is considered according to all, as in checking the lung, even without a presumption and even against an opposing presumption, as with the lung; this requires study.
