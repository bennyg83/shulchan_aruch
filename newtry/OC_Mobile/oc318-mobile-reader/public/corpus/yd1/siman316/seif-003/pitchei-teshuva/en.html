Or a non-Jew speaking innocently. See Responsa Chavat Yair, siman 37. He writes that even hearsay from one non-Jew speaking innocently in the name of another non-Jew speaking innocently is effective here. But if the statement is not genuinely made innocently, it does not help, unlike an authority who wished to be lenient where such a statement was combined with the fact that the animal was producing milk. That authority relied on Terumat HaDeshen, siman 271, which discusses a case where it was doubtful whether the non-Jew spoke merely to praise his merchandise. Chavat Yair rejects this. One may combine the fact of lactation only with a statement certainly made innocently and not to improve the sale, as the Rama's unqualified wording implies. As to whether the non-Jew must initiate the subject himself, as required in testimony concerning a married woman's husband's death in Even HaEzer, siman 17, it appears that for other prohibitions, even Torah prohibitions, this requirement does not apply. It is unique to testimony concerning a woman. He brings proof from reliance on a non-Jew's tasting. See there. Responsa Chatam Sofer, siman 64 and end of siman 308, indicates that he disagrees and holds that even for other prohibitions a statement is not considered innocent conversation unless made in the manner that would be effective for testimony concerning a woman. See there.
