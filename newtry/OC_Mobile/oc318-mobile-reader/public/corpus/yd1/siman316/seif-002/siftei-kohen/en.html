He bought a nursing animal, etc. This means we knew that it had been giving milk even though it had not previously given birth. Otherwise, since it gives milk and milk exempts from bekhorah according to the Mechaber, for most animals do not produce milk unless they have given birth, as the Mechaber wrote in seif 3, it would in any case be exempt from bekhorah; and this animal that it nurses would have been exempt even if born in Jewish possession. Rather, it must be as we said. So it is implied in Tosafot and the Rosh. The Rav did not need to write this, since it is already explained below in seif 4, but he mentioned it because of the latter clause, even if it is like a pig. Examine carefully.
Rather, this one is presumed to have given birth, etc. This does not mean it is presumed to have given birth to this offspring, as the Atteret Zahav wrote, for in the Talmud, Bekhorot chapter 3, 24a, the question remains unresolved whether we say one that gives birth shows compassion, or whether it shows compassion only if it gave birth to it. Therefore the Gemara says there that something like a pig is forbidden to eat, for perhaps an animal that gave birth shows compassion even though it did not give birth to this one. So wrote the poskim, and the Tur and Shulchan Aruch above in siman 79, end of seif 24, regarding a non-kosher species nursing and following a kosher one: it is forbidden, perhaps it was born from the non-kosher one and follows it. Rather, the meaning is that even if she did not give birth to this one, she certainly gave birth to some offspring, and therefore the one after it is exempt from bekhorah. Nevertheless, the Rambam, Tur, and Shulchan Aruch require study in writing, "even if this one that it nurses is like a pig," etc., implying that with a pig it would be more reasonable to obligate in bekhorah. In the Talmud there we say the opposite: on the contrary, with a pig it is more reasonable that it should even be permitted to eat, because one could say that since it is not of its species, it would not show compassion unless it gave birth to it. It seems to me that nevertheless they held that with a pig, since it is not of its species, it would not show compassion, and if it does show compassion, one might conclude that it shows compassion even though it did not give birth at all, and therefore it would seem to be obligated in bekhorah. The teaching informs us that even so it is exempt, because on the contrary, the opposite is more reasonable: since it is not of its species, it is more likely to say that she gave birth to it, as the Talmud says. Examine carefully.
