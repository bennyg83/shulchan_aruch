Even so, it is forbidden. The reason is in Piskei Maharai siman 130, and this is his language: as stated in Shaarei Dura regarding a wolf that came and attacked one animal, where Ri wrote that one should not permit by virtue of a sefek sefeka, perhaps it attacked with its tooth, and if you say it attacked with its claw, perhaps it is not this one; since the first doubt is Torah-level, it is not nullified even in a majority, all the more so one against one; end quote. I have room to examine this from what the Beit Yosef wrote above in siman 57: I found written, does Ri disagree with the Talmud, which says sefek sefeka in a Torah matter? He answered there that it speaks where the first doubt became known first. Although I challenged this in siman 110 seif 9 and answered differently, based on the Semag and Semak, that the essential permission of sefek sefeka depends on whether it was possible for the matter to become known in between, in which case it is forbidden, or whether the two doubts are equal, such as in two mixtures; see there at length. All this does not apply here, regarding two kids that were born, one male and one female, where there is a sefek sefeka: perhaps she had already given birth before these were born, and if you say she had not given birth, perhaps the male was born last. Why should we not say sefek sefeka regarding this male and say there is no bekhor here at all? So it is explained above in Shulchan Aruch siman 110 seif 9 in the text, that only where the first doubt is in its body, etc., do we not say sefek sefeka, and in the gloss there he wrote that the matter depends on whether it became known. None of this applies here. Therefore it requires study why we should not follow sefek sefeka here. Certainly, if she now gave birth to two males, and there is a doubt regarding each of them which one was born first, then there is certainly one bekhor among them and it is proper to be stringent, since they belong to one person. This is like the two paths in the first chapter of Pesachim, where if they come to ask at the same time we are stringent; here it is impossible to ask except at one time, because the entire permission is by virtue of sefek sefeka. But with one male and one female, we should have been lenient by virtue of sefek sefeka. Perhaps for this reason the Rama used this formulation in the case of sefek sefeka: “such as where she gave birth to two, and one can say regarding each one that it is not the bekhor,” changing from the formulation used in Piskei Maharai there, namely that she gave birth to a male and female. This is for the reason I wrote; so it appears to me, and it requires study in practice.
