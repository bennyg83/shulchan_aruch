He is like a living person for every matter. It is forbidden to do anything that hastens his death. The Ran wrote: "He is like a living person" means for giving a get and a gift, and a kohen may enter the house in which he stands, even though most who are goses die. The Bach brings this anonymously, but below in siman 370 it is explained that it is forbidden to enter a house containing a goses; see there.
One does not bind. So that his mouth not open.
And one does not anoint. Nor wash. It is the custom performed for every dead person, to wash the filth from his flesh.
And one does not remove the pillow from beneath him. Although one does not lay the dead upon anything warm, such as a pillow or cushion, but immediately lowers him and places him on the ground, because warm things cause odor and swelling, he says that one may not do this for him while he is goses. This is what he writes afterward, that one may not place him on sand. The reason is because this moves him from his place; so wrote the Perishah.
Anyone who closes the eyes at the departure of the soul is a spiller of blood. Rather, he should wait a little, lest he merely fainted. This is the Rambam's language. In tractate Semachot it concludes: anyone who touches him is a spiller of blood. To what is this comparable? To a flickering lamp; as soon as a person touches it, it is extinguished. So wrote the Rif and Rambam.
And some say that one may not, etc. The Bach wrote that by law it is permitted whenever the sick person does not feel it. However, one must be careful on Erev Shabbat close to Shabbat, lest they not have time to bury him except by desecrating Shabbat. If they do not bury him, they will have to leave the grave open. Therefore we do not permit it unless we estimate that if he dies very close to Shabbat and it is impossible to bury him, they will at least have enough time to refill the pit comfortably before Shabbat enters. See Orach Chayim siman 547 seif 11.
It is forbidden to remove the pillow and cushion, etc. The Atteret Zahav wrote: I am astonished, how is this different from removing a knocking sound and salt? Here too he does nothing but remove the impediment, and this requires study. There is no difficulty, for here the prohibition is not because of the feathers; the prohibition is because the goses is moved. So wrote the Derishah and Bach; see there.
