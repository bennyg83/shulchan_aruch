To spill all the water. Birkei Yosef wrote: I heard that someone ruled not to spill the water when a child dies within thirty days of birth, but in our regions the custom is to spill it, and this is primary. He further wrote that there is some room to doubt whether the same applies when a non-Jewish neighbor dies in a house in a Jewish courtyard; it is reasonable to be stringent, for danger is more severe. He further wrote that if they baked bread with this water, the bread is forbidden. This is Beit Yehudah responsa I, Yoreh De'ah siman 46. There he wrote that something dangerous is not nullified in sixty, as the Taz wrote above in siman 116; see what I wrote there. If they cooked a dish with this water, the dish is forbidden. So wrote Rabbi Beit David siman 161. If some of this water fell into an already cooked dish, according to what he wrote above in the name of the Taz and Beit Yehudah responsa, that a dangerous matter is not nullified in sixty, one should be stringent and forbid everything; these are his words. It appears that if they cooked for Shabbat with this water, one should not forbid after the fact, for one engaged in a mitzvah will know no evil, as stated in Orach Chayim siman 455, and I heard that several great authorities ruled this way. Shemesh Tzedakah responsa, Yoreh De'ah siman 36, wrote that the custom is not to spill on Shabbat. First, the Shach's first reason for spilling the water is primary, and for that reason on Shabbat they should not spill, so that those who hear will not become distressed and be prevented from oneg Shabbat. Even according to the second reason, he received from his teachers that on Shabbat the Angel of Death does not wipe his knife in water; see there. I heard that Tosefet Shabbat wrote likewise in the name of a Midrash. It appears that after Shabbat one should spill because of the Shach's first reason. See Adnei Paz responsa siman 26, who wrote that even washing one's face, hands, and feet and laundering one's clothes is forbidden according to the second reason, and he brings proof from uncovered water; see there. This can be rejected, for there even spilling it in the public domain is forbidden, as the Tur wrote above in siman 116, because there is danger in touching something containing snake venom, as stated in Chullin 94 regarding a sandal; see there. But here one may say that the danger is only to one who drinks.
