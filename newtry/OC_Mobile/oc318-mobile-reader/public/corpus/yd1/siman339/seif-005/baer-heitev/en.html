To pour out. The reason is that the Angel of Death casts a drop of the blood of death into the water. Tashbetz relates an incident in which a person drank water that had been in a house at the time of a death. A scholar rebuked him, and shortly afterward the drinker died. The scholar said that he had seen the Angel of Death wipe his knife in that water. See Orach Chaim, siman 455, where it is stated that water drawn for a mitzvah need not be poured out, because one who performs a mitzvah will know no harm. The measure of a neighborhood is not defined here. Tosafot in Ketubot write that it means three houses, as stated at the end of the first chapter of Avodah Zarah. Nekudot HaKesef suggests that here it may mean only an immediate neighbor, and such is the practice. Recanati writes that at the time of death one must ensure that no limb extends beyond the bed, and strongly condemns neglect of this matter. See siman 455.
