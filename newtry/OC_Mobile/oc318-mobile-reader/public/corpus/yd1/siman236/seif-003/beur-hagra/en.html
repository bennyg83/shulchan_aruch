When does this apply, etc. See Be'er HaGolah. Mishneh LaMelech brought proof for Rambam's ruling from Niddah 46b: “since we find,” etc., “with regard to the intentional violation of an oath.” The Gemara explains that case as one in which he consecrated the item and others ate it.
But if, etc. This is the Tur's text. It means that he swore that his fellow would not enter, because it is certainly impossible for him to impose an oath upon his fellow. This is also the reading in our text. The Tur likewise explains Rambam in this manner, unlike Lechem Mishneh.
Must, etc. Meaning Shimon. In our text it states that he is liable. Lechem Mishneh already challenged this, because even regarding vows Rambam writes in chapter 5 of the Laws of Vows that in such a case he is not liable. See Ran to Nedarim 15a, s.v. Halachah, etc.
