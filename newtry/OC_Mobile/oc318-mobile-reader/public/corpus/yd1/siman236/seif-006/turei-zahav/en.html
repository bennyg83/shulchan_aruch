Beit Yosef likewise in the name of Rabbenu Yerucham and Sar Shalom; and he wrote Rabbenu Yerucham brought proof from chapter 1 of Sotah; and it appears the proof is from what we say at the end of chapter 1 of Sotah — Moses said to Joseph: the time has arrived of the oath the Holy One swore that I will redeem you; and the time has arrived of the oath you made Israel — if you show yourself, fine; if not, behold we are clear from your oath, etc.; and it appears from this proof to introduce a general rule in this law — I wondered why Moses extended to say the time arrived of the oath God swore to redeem us; it would suffice to say the time arrived of the oath you made us; rather it is clear he preceded this because he wishes to conclude: if not, we are clear from your oath forever; and if not for this introduction, Joseph would have had to go there another time after his bones — for although Joseph said at the time of the oath "and you shall bring up my bones from here with you," who says "with you" specifically? Perhaps he meant: if it is possible to take you, take you; if not, come another time; but since Joseph said "pakod yifkod Elohim etc. and you shall bring up" — which is seemingly superfluous language — certainly the essence of the oath depends on this visitation; therefore Moses preceded that the time of visitation arrived that they be redeemed — therefore we are entirely clear if you do not show yourself; and from this we learn for every similar case that if there is a condition in the oath and it depends on him, the condition must exist; and if it does not come about, he is exempt from the oath entirely; unlike one who swears he will not come to such-and-such place unless his fellow comes with him simultaneously — when he knows his fellow comes he is not exempt unless the condition is not an evil thing impossible at the time of the act; and one who swears he will not take possession of such-and-such field unless his fellow comes with him simultaneously — when he knows his fellow comes, he is not exempt unless the condition is not such an evil thing that he cannot be killed at the time of this act; and one is not killed at the time of the act unless he comes simultaneously — if the condition is not such an evil thing, he is not exempt; and from all this one may learn regarding one who swears regarding a matter that depends on his fellow's coming simultaneously — when he knows he comes, he is not exempt unless the condition is not impossible at the time of the act; and the essence of the oath depends on the condition's fulfillment — end quote.
