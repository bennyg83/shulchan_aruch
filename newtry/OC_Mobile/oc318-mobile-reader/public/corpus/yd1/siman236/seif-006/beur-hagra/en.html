Two, etc.; therefore, etc. As stated in Sotah 10a. See Rashi there, s.v. Huchal, and Bereishit Rabbah at the end of parashat Vayetzei.
And even, etc. He is not regarded even as a single witness, since he has a personal interest in the matter.
(Likkut) And even if he claimed, etc. He is not regarded even as a single witness, because he has a personal interest in his testimony, and it is not within his power to establish the matter, even regarding a prohibition. See Tosafot to Kiddushin 43b, s.v. VeChen, beginning “and one may say,” etc. (End of selection.)
Nevertheless, etc. As Rashi writes on 43a, s.v. VeYotzi, etc. Even Tosafot challenged only, “Do we institute a remedy for wicked people?” That objection does not apply in a case such as this.
And all this, etc. See Ran to Nedarim 27a, at the end of s.v. LeRava, beginning “specifically through release by a sage,” etc., “but,” etc. All the more so does this apply in the present case. Proof may be brought from Nazir 20b: “I am hereby,” etc., where his own vow remains in force even though they vowed together. This is true even according to the Gemara's final explanation, and certainly according to the initial understanding that the words “and I” create a completely independent vow. The Gemara objects only because the second person made his vow dependent upon the first. Had he not made it dependent, the ruling would be entirely clear. There.
