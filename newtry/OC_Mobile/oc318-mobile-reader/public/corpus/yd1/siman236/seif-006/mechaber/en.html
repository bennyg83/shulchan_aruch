If two people swore to do one thing and one of them transgressed the oath, the second is exempt and does not require annulment. Therefore, a man and woman who were betrothed to one another and accepted a cherem to marry at a fixed time—whoever delays and passes the date is forbidden to marry another, and the other is permitted and does not require annulment; and also the delayer himself is permitted without annulment after the other has married. {Rama: And even if this one who passed the date claims he was under compulsion, he is not believed that his counterpart is still bound by his oath. Nevertheless, if this one wishes to be released from his oath, he should go to a sage and have his oath annulled, lest his counterpart bring witnesses that he was compelled in the matter in which he passed the date, and it be found that this one transgressed his oath if he married without annulment. (Responsum of Mahariv siman 337.) And all this applies only when two swore to one another; but when two swore together to do a matter, such as not to speak with so-and-so, and one of them transgressed—the second is obligated to fulfill his oath. And even if they swore to one another and they are three or more—for even if one transgresses the oath, the two remaining must fulfill their oath. (Maharam of Padua siman 26 and Rivash siman 453.) And it appears that if two swore together and did not swear to one another, and one transgressed, the second is obligated to fulfill his oath—this applies specifically when they are not dependent on one another; but if they are dependent on one another and one cannot fulfill his oath without his fellow, such as if they swore to go together to Eretz Yisrael and one transgresses and it is difficult for the second to go alone—he is also exempt, as explained above siman 228 seif 44. (Responsum of Rosh kelal 8.)}
