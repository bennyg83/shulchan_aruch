If he swore that he saw a camel flying in the air—this is a shevuat shav, even if he says he saw a large bird flying and he calls it a camel. (One who swears about a matter that is somewhat exaggerated in order to uphold his words is not a shevuat shav.) (Mordechai chapter Shevuot Shetayim.) If he said, "An oath that I will not eat matzah"—it is forbidden to eat matzah on the night of Pesach. "An oath that I will not eat matzah on the night of Pesach"—he is flogged and eats matzah on the night of Pesach. "An oath that I will not sit in the shade"—it is forbidden to sit in the shade of a sukkah. "An oath that I will not sit in the shade of the sukkah"—he is flogged and sits in the shade of the sukkah. (That oaths take effect on a matter of mitzvah in a general formulation applies specifically to positive commandments, but not to negative commandments.) (Maharam of Padua siman 74, and Beit Yosef in the name of Rivash, Rif, and Tosafot chapter 3 of Shevuot 24a in the name of R' Yitzchak.)
