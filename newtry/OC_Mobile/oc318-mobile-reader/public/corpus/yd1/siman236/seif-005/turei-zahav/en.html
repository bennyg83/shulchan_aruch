That it is somewhat exaggeration, etc. These words have no understanding — how can one swear to uphold it? And in Levush he wrote on this: because the way of the world is to uphold their words with some exaggeration — also this is not intended, for in any case how can he swear on this since it is not true? And the source of this law is from Mordechai, chapter Shevuot Shetayim; and so it states there in Yerushalmi: Chizkiyah said, the law that one who swears on two that are two is lashed for a false oath. And Rashbam challenged: the mishnah said specifically when he swore on a matter that is not true; but on a true matter there is no false oath here. And he answered: where there is some exaggeration to uphold his words, this is not a false oath; but one who swears on two that are two from truth that is not needed — behold this is a false oath — end quote. Behold he did not come to permit except one who swore on a matter that is true, only in people's eyes it is somewhat exaggeration and they do not believe him — in this there is no false oath, for the oath is needed for him. And all the more so if it is more exaggeration that he can swear it is true — this oath is not in vain, unless he swore on what is plain in everyone's eyes that it is true — then the oath is in vain. And if Rama also intended this, he did not need to write it, for Mechaber already wrote in seif 4 on a matter known to all, and Mordechai's words are included. And further, Rama should not have written this in this seif, for it speaks of matters that are lies in the world's eyes, and therefore one cannot learn any leniency from this gloss to swear according to his mind that he calls a name on some matter not in the world's way. And explicitly we conclude in Rif, second chapter of Shevuot: we do not follow his mind — he calls this name; for since everyone does not call it a camel, his mind is voided before every person, and it is a false oath — end quote. Behold what he thinks in his mind does not help, except when the whole world or the majority call that name for that thing he calls. But if some call it thus, his mind is also voided — for only regarding monetary matters do we say in the beginning of chapter HaMenich that the majority call each as it is, etc., that we do not follow the majority in monetary matters; but not in a grave oath — so it seems to me plainly. And one who wants to learn a leniency from this gloss has no root or essence; and it seems to me Rama did not examine Mordechai's words themselves, only the simanim of Mordechai — therefore he wrote thus.
We must not eat food. A soldier on him who included every mitzvah in his oath, even though he is not of command:
Specifically in a positive mitzvah. Meaning: there is a positive mitzvah to do some matter, and he swore on it inclusively not to do also the mitzvah; but if he swore on a negative commandment that he will do it, and included it in other things he will do — it does not help, for we do not tell a person "stand and sin" so that your fellow be rewarded.
