Shach — examine: Rivash shows this law speaks only of shevuat bitui; if she does not want to marry him, he is released from his oath immediately, for such was his oath — he will marry when she wishes; and if she does not wish, there is no oath here; but if she wishes afterward, he did not mention — one may say he agrees with Mahari"k; and even regarding one who swore to give something to his fellow and the fellow does not wish to accept — one may understand from Rivash's words that he is released forever from his oath, for he can say "as if I accepted"; unlike one who swore to marry her — examine; and Taz wrote: if he said, "Shevuah that I will not marry any woman except so-and-so," and afterward she does not wish to marry him — seemingly he may marry another without release, for there is a mitzvah to marry a woman, and this is swearing to nullify a mitzvah, which does not take effect; but from Ran's words I learned that release is needed, for the oath took effect because of the generalization that whoever she wishes to marry, another is forbidden upon him; therefore even now that she does not want, nevertheless the oath took effect and release is needed — end quote.
