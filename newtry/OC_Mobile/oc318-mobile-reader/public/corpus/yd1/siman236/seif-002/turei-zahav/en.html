This is fine. It is not usual to take a oath to the cell:
In any case, when she wants, etc. And if she does not want, he is obligated on account of a false oath — for at the time he swore he would marry her, he swore falsely, for it is not in his power to compel her. And it seems he concedes this view regarding "I will give a maneh to so-and-so" — there is no false oath, for giving is in his power in any case; and if his fellow does not want to accept, behold he did not swear on the acceptance. Not so an oath that he will marry her — for it is impossible for him at all to fulfill the marriage as long as she withholds. And the first opinion holds: also an oath "I will marry her" is not false — it is similar to "I will give"; only he is obligated to claim that she marry him, and if she does not want, his oath is not voided at all, and there is no punishment upon him, for he only obligated himself for the benefit of the one who urged him — that he give him a maneh or marry this woman; and if they do not want, he is clean. Such is the substance of Rivash siman 387, who is the holder of the "some say" here. And if he said "oath that I will not marry any woman except so-and-so," and afterward she did not want to marry him — ostensibly he can marry another without release, for there is a mitzvah to marry a woman, and he swore to nullify the mitzvah — it does not take effect. But from Ran's words that I brought in siman 237 seif 6, I learned that release is needed, for the oath takes effect on account of inclusion — for whoever she wants to marry, prohibition of another applies to him; therefore also now that she does not want, nevertheless the oath took effect and release is needed. So it seems to me. And even though otherwise there is inclusion, since it applies to women he cannot fulfill the mitzvah of procreation, as Rama wrote siman 239 seif 8 — nevertheless a difference emerges if he swore explicitly on every woman who is childbearing.
