And even though in Taanit 11a we rule like R' Elazar — Taanit is different, for Shmuel himself establishes there with a woman fasting. And Ran holds: even though in Bava Kamma there we say it is a dispute — this is from what is written in Shevuot, "father swore," etc., that permission is in his hand implies fully permitted; but it takes effect according to one opinion, and it is difficult [and Ran in his chiddushim; and see siman 239 seif 6].
And he swore, etc. Ran s.v. leitei, etc.; but Rambam holds it is dependent — if his fellow fulfills it, his oath is fulfilled; if not, it is void — and this is shevuat shav; and it should not be shevuat shav, for shav is only regarding the past as written 2b, but shevuat sheker as written 25b — it is not recognizable, etc.; Rivash siman 387.
But regarding a transgression, etc. In those above he is lashed for shav as written in the mishnah, except in the end — to swear regarding his fellow; Rambam wrote he is not lashed — see there; and his words are very puzzling, and Kesef Mishneh and Lechem Mishneh already questioned this; but the essence is he is not lashed, since it is dependent until his fellow fulfills — it is a lav without action, like "I will eat this loaf today," etc., per R' Yochanan there 3b. The correction of Rambam's words is thus: it is a doubtful warning — meaning, if it were known his fellow would not do it, it would be a false oath or shav and he would be lashed; and afterward it is a lav without action and he is not lashed; and so in chapter 5 halachah 2; and it is found that when they warned him at the time he swore, it is a doubtful warning, and afterward it is a lav shein bo maaseh and one is not lashed, as written in Laws of Sanhedrin; and it became confused there, and likewise Rashbat shein bo maaseh became confused; and Shulchan Aruch wrote shein bo maaseh; and it is written explicitly in Torah — and this is a famous error; and Lechem Mishneh's words are very forced; and what Rama wrote "and also there is no," etc. — meaning: even though he is not lashed, also it is possible it will be fulfilled; nevertheless perhaps it will not be fulfilled, and the Torah prohibition nevertheless exists.
