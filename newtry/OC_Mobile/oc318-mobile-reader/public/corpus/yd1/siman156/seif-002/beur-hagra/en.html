It is forbidden, etc. See the Yerushalmi, chapter 2 of Avodah Zarah, there; the Tosefta, chapter 3; and Tosafot in the passage beginning as cited above.
Unless, etc. Tosafot there explain that whenever the act is not done for adornment, it is not included in the prohibition, ‘A man shall not wear,’ etc., as stated in Nazir 59a: ‘He asked him,’ etc. See Tosafot there, in the passages beginning ‘He asked him,’ and ‘A boundary,’ etc.; and likewise, ‘One who looks,’ etc.
Or if he is having his hair cut, etc. See the Gemara in Avodah Zarah there.
And some say, etc. The Ran likewise writes that the passages in chapter 2 of Avodah Zarah and chapter 23 of Shabbat which imply that use of a mirror is permitted refer to a place where men customarily look in mirrors, so that the prohibition, ‘A man shall not wear,’ etc., does not apply. The Yerushalmi and Tosefta, however, refer to a place where only women have that custom, in which case the prohibition properly applies. In the same manner he resolves the passage in Nazir concerning shaving the pubic area and the armpits, as stated above in siman 182; see there. Nevertheless, he writes that scholars refrain from doing so everywhere. For this reason, a special dispensation was required for Rabban Gamliel in the Yerushalmi and Tosefta, and likewise regarding shaving the armpits, as stated there in Nazir; see siman 182, seif 1, in the gloss, beginning ‘Only,’ etc. His words require study. Why did he not establish that case as one where only women had the custom? The source states explicitly, ‘But for a man it is forbidden even on a weekday,’ which shows that it is intrinsically forbidden. It is concerning this that the source says, ‘They permitted it for the household of Rabbi,’ etc., as Tosafot cite in Avodah Zarah there. His distinction based upon local custom also does not appear correct, for Shabbat 50b states, ‘A person may scrape,’ etc., and, ‘If it is for the sake of,’ etc. Even the one who disagrees there does so only because of the honor of his Creator, but in a case such as this he certainly would not disagree. Likewise, Shabbat 94b and Makkot 20b discuss removing white hairs from among black hairs, etc.
And even, etc. This is stated by the Sefer Mitzvot Gadol, Hagahot Maimoniyot, and Hagahot Ashri. It is likewise stated in the Tosafot to Avodah Zarah cited above: when done because of an eye ailment, it is permitted, etc. The same ruling appears in the Sefer Mitzvot Gadol; see there.
Or when he does so, etc. Darkei Moshe, in the name of Rabbi Zelkel, derives this from Shabbat 50b. See Tosafot there, in the passage beginning ‘For the sake of,’ etc.
