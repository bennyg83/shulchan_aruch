It is forbidden for a man to look in a mirror due to “a man shall not wear [that which is for a woman],” unless it is for medical purposes — for example, if he is concerned about his eyes — or if he is cutting his own hair, or if he is getting a haircut from a non-Jew in private, and in order to appear as an important person it is permitted to look in a mirror. {Rama: And some say that that which is forbidden to look in a mirror is specifically in a place where the practice is to look in a mirror only for women, and it has the concern of “a man shall not wear”; but in a place where it is also the practice of men to look in a mirror, it is permitted (Ran, perek Ein Ma’amidin). And even in a place where they are accustomed to be stringent, if he does so for medical purposes, to brighten his eyes, or to remove spots from his face, or feathers from his head, it is permitted (Semag in the name of R' Ze’alkil), and so is the practice; and see below siman 182.}
