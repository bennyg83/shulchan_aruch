With a razor. For harm is common; but with scissors where harm is uncommon it is permitted if another is with him — Tosafot there; and the Bach explained that if another is not with him it is in any case forbidden to seclude with a gentile (as above siman 153 seif 2) — end; and so is explained explicitly in Tosafot there in the previous heading: but between him alone it is forbidden to have one's hair cut — meaning in a place where people are not often found; but actual seclusion does not need to be stated — for it is derivable that even without the haircut it is forbidden to seclude with them on account of bloodshed — end.
And the practice is to be lenient, etc. This is Derishah's wording: Rama wrote, "and there are those who are stringent," etc., until "and the practice is to be lenient," end quote. It appears that the reason they practiced leniency is because we pay them wages, and Hagahot Asheri wrote that for wages it is permitted, end quote. This is difficult: if so, what does it mean that the practice is to be lenient like the first view? Rather, it means that the first view permits in a place where people are present even without a mirror, and on this they relied to be lenient. So too is the consensus of the poskim, as Beit Yosef wrote. Those people who have their hair cut by a non-Jew when alone with him do not act properly, for in any case it is forbidden to be secluded with them, all the more so while getting a haircut.
