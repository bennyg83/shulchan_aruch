With a razor. Because danger is common. With scissors, where danger is not common, it is permitted if another person is with him. Tosafos there. Bach explains that if no other person is with him, seclusion with the non-Jew is in any event forbidden, as stated above in siman 153, seif 2. This is explicit in the preceding Tosafos there: "But between him and the non-Jew alone it is forbidden to take a haircut," meaning in a place where people do not commonly pass. Complete seclusion need not be discussed, because even without taking a haircut it is forbidden to be secluded with them because of the danger of bloodshed.
"And the custom is to be lenient," etc. Derishah writes: Rama wrote, "Some are stringent," etc., through "and the custom is to be lenient." It appears that the custom to be lenient is because we pay them wages, and Hagahos Ashri writes that for wages it is permitted. This is difficult: if so, why does Rama say that the custom is to be lenient "like the first opinion"? Rather, he means that the first opinion permits in a place where people are commonly present even when one does not use a mirror, and people rely on this view to be lenient. This is also the consensus of the poskim, as Beit Yosef writes. Those who take haircuts from a non-Jew while alone with him do not act properly, because even apart from the haircut it is forbidden to be secluded with them; all the more so while receiving a haircut.
