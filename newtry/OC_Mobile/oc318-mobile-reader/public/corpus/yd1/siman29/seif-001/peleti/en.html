Eight, etc. ; in the Gemara, beginning of chapter Elu Treifot, they say: eight types of treifot ; DaN ChaNeK NeFeSH ; excluding laktah, for Rachi said: laktah in the milt ; treifah. And Lechem Mishneh challenged Rambam; and likewise Shulchan Aruch ruled like Rachi, as they say in the Gemara: halacha like Rachi. If so, it should count laktah too, and they would be nine. And nothing is explained ; it appears to me to settle it: either in truth it is difficult for them how they said excluding Rachi, and below we rule halacha like Rachi; therefore they hold in the Gemara that Rachi said laktah in the milt ; treifah ; sons of the West say; and that is when laktah reaches the place of the charitz (groove). And Rambam holds sons of the West dispute Rachi, that for Rachi it need not reach the place of the charitz at all. And behold Rambam, and likewise Shulchan Aruch, ruled that laktah in a bird's milt does not disqualify; if so, one may say: that which it did not count laktah among these eight treifot ; what Ulla counted are by tradition, both in animal and bird; therefore it did not count laktah, which does not exist in a bird. But Rashba in Torat Ha'olah (daf 50) gave a reason that therefore it does not disqualify in a bird: the essence of treifot is in the place of the charitz and lungs; and this does not exist in a bird; if so, it is possible for sons of the West that they are not treifah until it reaches the place of the charitz; if so, it does not apply in a bird. And one may say: therefore Ulla did not count laktah ; for in both bird and animal it is required; but for Rachi, who holds laktah need not reach the place of the charitz ; if so, there is no reason to distinguish between animal and bird; if so, in a bird too it is treifah ; the difficulty is well: it should count laktah. And one must say it does not hold this of Rachi. If so, one may say the Gemara says excluding Rachi ; for he holds we do not require reaching the place of the charitz; if so, in a bird too treifah. And if Ulla held like me, it should count laktah; rather it does not hold only like sons of the West, who require reaching the place of the charitz; if so, in a bird it is kosher, and it did not count it as explained; if so, there is no difficulty for Rambam and Shulchan Aruch, who ruled like sons of the West ; and a bird is truly kosher. And it settles better for the view of the Agur, who gave a reason as Pri Chadash wrote in siman 44: all treifot of laktah in the milt are because the intestines were struck and they depend on the place of the charitz; therefore in a bird it is kosher, for it does not depend on intestines. And it seems difficult at first: if the essence of treifot is because of intestines, how does Rachi count laktah among the eighteen treifot in the Gemara, and likewise it should count among the eight types of treifot ; behold there is treifah only from perforation of intestines, and it taught them? And likewise Tosafot there: therefore it did not count lacking of skull, since the cause of treifot is perforation of membrane, and it taught perforation of membrane; if so, the same here. And one must say: Rachi holds we do not require laktah for the charitz; perforce treifah from itself ; before the charitz the intestines are not connected; if so, one may say that which the Gemara says Ulla does not hold Rachi ; excluding Rachi, who holds it is treifah from itself; if so, it should count it; but for us, who hold like sons of the West, who require laktah for the place of the charitz ; if so, there is no difficulty to count it, for they say the reason is because of intestines, and it already taught them. And in s.k. after this you will find more answer.
Types ; in the Gemara they say: eight types of treifot were said to Moses at Sinai; and so Rambam wrote. And one should understand according to what Rambam wrote in the introduction to Seder Zeraim: everything Chazal learned from breadth of mind and depth of understanding ; in them forgetfulness and dispute fell; but what they received halacha leMoshe miSinai ; in it no dispute and forgetfulness at all fall; and they are always as they are ; see there. And if these treifot were halacha leMoshe miSinai ; behold we find dispute in them: lacking in spinal column ; Beit Shammai and Beit Hillel; perforated gallbladder ; R' Yehudah and Beit Yosef permit; geludah ; R' Meir and Sages dispute; junction of gidim; broken legs ; they dispute, etc. Therefore one must say, as I wrote in Kereti: only general principles were transmitted to Moses at Sinai ; there are eight types of treifot and tradition, etc.; and Chazal brought out the details of treifot ; how and what, and all good manners of treifot according to the order of their study and wisdom. If so, lacking was halacha leMoshe miSinai and includes lacking of spinal column, or one or two vertebrae; nekuvah was halacha leMoshe miSinai and includes many perforations ; perforated heart, and similar, where there is no doubt; and Chazal disputed and were uncertain whether nekuvah applies also to gallbladder, and likewise all. And this applies to all eight types mentioned; but laktah ; if this were halacha leMoshe miSinai ; behold we find no treifah except laktah in the milt; if so, how do we find dispute in it, that there are amoraim who do not hold this of Rachi? If so, dispute falls on halacha leMoshe miSinai ; for the tradition was on something else that does not exist. And one must say: in truth, even though laktah in the milt is treifah ; that is, Chazal judged them according to the strength of their mind and power in Torah; but halacha leMoshe miSinai is not in it (see in authors the difference between what Chazal extracted from their mind and what came halacha leMoshe miSinai). And see Nekudot HaKesef, who also holds this reasoning to distinguish between halacha leMoshe miSinai and treifah from Chazal's words ; only he did not descend to analyze and clarify the matter as I wrote. If so, one may say excluding laktah ; for it is not halacha leMoshe miSinai; but nevertheless it is forbidden. Therefore Rambam wrote regarding these eight treifot ; halacha leMoshe miSinai. And in Mechaber halacha leMoshe miSinai was omitted ; and this is not correct.
Treifot ; seventy, as I wrote in Kereti: Taz and more authors wondered on Tur and Rambam, who derived from the verse "to a dog you shall cast it" that treifah is not alive; and in the Gemara R' Levi said as written: this is the animal ; a living animal eat, and what is not a living animal do not eat; and why did they change to bring other proof? What appears: certainly even according to the one who says treifah is alive ; nevertheless, if the simanim of shechitah were perforated, it is certainly not alive, as we hold; in truth it is neveilah and dead. If so, one may say thus is the continuation of the verse: and flesh in the field, treifah you shall not eat ; the verse taught us prohibition of the negative commandment of treifah; and the verse says: even though the treifot are so severe that "to a dog you shall cast it" ; that it is treifah in the simanim where certainly one casts to a dog ; nevertheless shechitah purifies, only because of and flesh in the field, treifah you shall not eat. And so is the continuation of the verse: treifah whose end is to die ; this is neveilah; it teaches nevertheless shechitah purifies and one is not lashed only because of treifah. This is only if the simanim of shechitah were perforated ; shechitah purifies from neveilah; but if in truth it does not purify ; if so, why need this? And in chapter HaShochet (daf 32) R' Levi holds regarding slaughter not at the place of the cut, where initially his shechitah was defective ; only treifah; and R' Levi holds nevertheless it is neveilah, and so we hold. If so, one may say: certainly Rambam grasped this derashah of "to a dog you shall cast it," which is the simple derashah, and Rambam's way is always the simple and short way. If not, one may say the verse comes even when it is in simanim that stand and certainly for a dog ; nevertheless it is treifah; for in truth regarding disqualification in shechitah, shechitah does not purify; but R' Levi goes according to his view that shechitah purifies even with perforated simanim; if so, well one may say the verse teaches this ; that even in such a case shechitah avails; therefore R' Levi needed to bring another derashah, "this is the animal," etc. And further one may say: in the Gemara the one who says treifah is alive derives from the verse to distinguish between an animal that may be eaten and an animal that may not be eaten ; that is treifah; and they called the verse "animal" (chayah). If so, one may say: that which the verse "this is the animal" was needed ; treifah is not alive, and one does not derive from "to a dog," etc. ; for this verse of the dog is not superfluous, for Chazal expounded it in Pesachim chapter 2: that you cast it, and you do not cast other forbidden or non-sacred meat in the Temple courtyard. Only Rambam derives from the verse's meaning even though it is not superfluous ; this is correct only if there is no other derashah to occupy it; but since here is a verse to distinguish as mentioned, to teach treifah is alive ; if so, from the outset one might say it is not alive, since the verse for a dog is not dedicated to this derashah and is not superfluous; therefore we expound from "this is the animal," for the word "alive" (chayah) is superfluous ; it should have written only "this that you shall eat," to teach treifah is not alive; and so is the end, and to distinguish from other derashot. And behold it seems there is to challenge: whence do we interpret the verse and distinguish regarding treifah ; if so, treifah is alive; this is strained ; the Torah would call treifah "alive," for even though it lives, nevertheless "alive" implies complete health, unlike treifah, which all agree is in complete weakness. And likewise Tosafot, Chullin (daf 100), s.v. treifot, etc.: it should interpret simply according to Beit HaGedolah and Tosafot and poskim, that the essence of simanim is not treifah and it lives ; its milk is permitted; only shechitah does not avail and it is forbidden in eating; if so, this is the intent of the verse between the animal that you shall not eat, which is alive and truly healthy until its milk is permitted, and nevertheless you shall not eat as the verse implies. But whence do we bring proof on treifah? Only the verse deals with animal and bird, and there is no essence of simanim in a bird according to one opinion ; if so, there is no way to explain the verse thus. And in Chullin (daf 20b) Rashi, s.v. ha hu deR' Yehudah, wrote: whether there is essence or no essence in a bird depends whether there is shechitah for a bird from the Torah or not ; see there. And we say in the Gemara in Nazir in the mishnah: a man may vow his son a nazir ; R' Yehudah holds it is halacha in Nazir; and R' Levi said to train him in mitzvot; and the Gemara challenges: behold he pinches a bird chatat; and answers: R' Levi holds there is no shechitah for a bird from the Torah; if so, one may say R' Levi properly was forced to learn treifah is not alive from "this is the animal," which is permitted ; for it is not superfluous from "to a dog," etc., since it is not superfluous ; if not, one might say from here to distinguish that treifah is alive; and if you say: perhaps this verse deals with essence ; this is not so, for R' Levi holds there is no shechitah for a bird from the Torah; if so, there is also no essence in a bird, and the verse to distinguish applies also to a bird. But for us, who hold there is shechitah for a bird and there is essence for a bird from the Torah ; if so, well one may say to distinguish regarding essence as mentioned, and there is no place to err; and Rambam and Tur grasped the short and simple way, for the simple meaning of the verse is "to a dog you shall cast it." And since I speak of this, one should arouse Rashi in the Torah, who explains on the verse to distinguish between whether it has signs of purity or signs of treifah ; that is, a living animal that may not be eaten; and see Mizrachi ; and so he holds treifah is alive. And in chapter Metzora, "birds that are alive" ; Rashi explained to exclude treifah; and it is explained in the Gemara, Chullin (daf 100), that this is according to the one who says treifah is not alive; and likewise Ramban there on Rashi ; if so, Rashi's words contradict each other. And in Mizrachi I did not find he addressed this; and to examine other Torah commentators ; I lack leisure; and it requires study at first glance. And what appears to me in settling it: in any case one may analyze that the Gemara says this tanna of the house of R' Yishmael, who holds treifah is alive, derives from the verse and distinguishes, etc.; and the way of the Talmud is to challenge as he challenged earlier on "this is the animal," etc.; and the other ; and here he did not challenge ; and a reason is needed; and one must say Rashba and Maharshal expanded: that whether treifah is alive or not alive is only for doubt of treifot; but definite treifah, even if it lives many days ; nevertheless treifah; and Maharshal added in reason: most treifot do not live, but a minority live; therefore in doubt of treifah we follow the majority and reject "learn from it" that it is not treifah, for most treifot die; but in definite treifah we say from the minority it is treifah; if so, one may say this is the explanation of the verse "from the animal" ; that is, definite treifah that was alive ; nevertheless you shall not eat, to remove from the heart of one who asks in Rashba's responsum who thought: since it lives, it is permitted; and the Torah taught us: even though it was alive, nevertheless do not eat, since it is treifah; and this is R' Levi's reason, who holds treifah is not alive. However R' Yehudah derives nevertheless from the verse to distinguish, etc.; for all of R' Yehudah's intent ; whether treifah is alive or not ; is for doubt of treifah; for if treifah is alive, one may not permit doubt of treifah even though it lives many days; and R' Yehudah properly challenged on this verse that there is treifah that lives; and it is true, only a minority ; nevertheless one may not permit in doubt of treifot, as the tanna of the house of R' Yishmael and R' Yehudah are one (see Kiddushin daf 40); and R' Yishmael holds in Bekhorot (daf 20) that we are concerned for the minority; if so, even in doubt of treifah one may say from the minority it is treifah; if so, one may say there is no contradiction in Rashi's words, and his words are correct ; for us, who follow the majority, well one may say the verse deals with definite treifot, and there is no difficulty; examine well. I wrote at length in siman 30 and 31.
