Permitted. The reason is found in Rashba — that here there are two doubts: a doubt whether there was swarming or not; and if you say there was — perhaps it dissolved and was nullified; therefore we follow it to be lenient. And Or Zarua wrote: and this is when they were not presumed — meaning stam; but in a region where they were presumed in this, it is certain and not a doubt.
Fruit. The Shach wrote: it appears that even in small fruits such as cherries and the like there is sixty against the taste of the worm; and nevertheless he wrote that he accepted that specifically in coarse apples or other coarse fruits we say the worm is nullified in sixty — but not in small ones. And Rashal wrote that one should be stringent so; and in a small fruit it becomes nevelah and requires sixty against all of it if there is concern it became wormy while attached even if not perforated outward, and when detached if perforated outward. And in Tur Choshen Mishpat he wrote: if there are also other things in the cooked dish besides the fruit — it is permitted to all if there is sixty against the worm; and he also wants to be lenient from the reason that the worm does not forbid by its taste because it is defective — and he compares it to flies and mosquitoes, which are nosen taam lifgam; and the Taz also wrote this reasoning, and the Shach's view does not lean to this at all — for Rashba himself permits regarding a fly, and nevertheless forbids here regarding a worm; therefore there is a difference between them — see there.
Three. The Shach wrote: since so many were found there, this cooked dish is presumed to have more — for what is there, perhaps it dissolved and was nullified? Because of one doubt we do not permit it; and this is specifically within twelve months — for then, even if it is inside the fruit, it is forbidden; but after twelve months, when there is no prohibition except when they separated, there is a double doubt — a doubt whether it separated alive or separated dead, which is permitted; and if you say it separated alive — perhaps it dissolved; and especially since there are poskim who hold that worms die immediately at the time of cooking before they separated; therefore they wrote that if many worms are found in the cooked dish, all is permitted — even the worms themselves — if their way is not to become wormy while attached; therefore one should be lenient here, since there is no definite prohibition but only concern lest there are more worms. However, all this is regarding worms inside; but fruit whose way is to become wormy — even if their way is to become wormy when detached, and it is usual for worms to be among them outside — then they are forbidden immediately; obviously when cooked without checking and afterward three or four are found in the cooked dish — all is forbidden, for here there is only one doubt of dissolution. And the Taz wrote: and it is a wonder why I need four — since with three it is forbidden; and it appears the Ran wrote so as an extension — that even with four there is permission in the sauce through straining; and Shulchan Aruch follows him in language "even," though he did not conclude so — end of his words.
That was presumed. Rashal wrote: and therefore my own practice was not to eat the dried greens, for they were presumed wormy — because after checking and sorting I found several worms in the cooked dish at the bottom of the pot. And the Taz wrote: an incident on Shavuot when they made in almost the entire city breads and pastries with raisins — and one woman checked her raisins and mealworms were found in them; and all bought from one place, and at the seller's too they checked afterward and found mealworms there as well. It appears to me that those bought before it became known there were mealworms are permitted — for this is similar to what Rashba wrote: if one transgressed and cooked within twelve months, it is a double doubt; here too there are two doubts — the first, perhaps at the time they bought, they were not yet mealworms until an hour afterward; and if you say they were — perhaps they dissolved in the oven from the heat of the fire. And one can say this is against the reasoning — to say that in an hour mealworms would form; and it turns out there is only one doubt. This is not so — for there too, in that case of Rashba, since their way is to become wormy, the worminess is like certain — for a regular doubt is not called a doubt, as written in Terumat HaDeshen siman 141 regarding wormy wheat; and nevertheless Rashba considered it a double doubt. But those bought afterward — one must forbid all the breads and pastries, for here there is only one doubt, perhaps dissolved; and certainly the mealworms separated from the raisins at the time of arranging and kneading, and perhaps one will eat the substance of the prohibition — and one should be concerned for a Torah prohibition — end of his words. And in Nekudot HaKesef he attacked him, and his view is to forbid even those bought before the prohibition became known — see there. [And Peri Chadash disagrees with the Taz and wrote that all the pastries are forbidden — see there. Various laws if many people bought flour from a shop and made bread from it and worms were afterward found — what is the law. In Darkei Moshe siman 286 he instructs to be lenient regarding oil made from olives that became wormy while attached — see there. In responsum Maharam of Lublin he permitted under duress in a year when all the kishut became wormy and a multitude cooked with it afterward beer without straining — that it is better to sell to a non-Jew; and if one cannot sell it all to a non-Jew, when he comes to strain the beer through a basket he should also place a garment on the basket; and what was already cooked without straining — if he can strain at the time of drinking, that is good; and after the fact, since it is a small sum that he cannot fix, and financial loss and time of duress — permitted — see there. And Tzitz Eliezer siman 51 wrote: this is specifically where there is concern for prohibition from the substance of the worms themselves that were crushed and cooked in the beer; but regarding spiced wine, where there is no concern for prohibition except from taste and moisture exuding from fruit and from worms — certainly permitted even without financial loss — end of his words. And from here we can learn for those people who are accustomed to forbid eating new grain — nevertheless it appears to me they are permitted to drink mead, even according to the Rosh who forbids drinking beer; for one can distinguish — mead is different, for it is mere moisture, unlike beer which is taste like the essence (Hagahah Lemberg press). And he wrote further that it is permitted to fatten poultry with flour that has worms in it — specifically that one make immediately from the flour small pieces and have a recognizable sign so he will not come to eat it — see there.]
Permitted. The Shach wrote: even if it is known that many worms were there before cooking — nevertheless permitted. And the Taz wrote in the name of Maharshal: also within twelve months proper checking helps — meaning to check each fruit by itself. And what the Tur wrote — that within twelve months it has no remedy — means the remedy of boiling water; and Rema in Tur Choshen Mishpat wrote that the custom is to sort chickpeas within twelve months on the table and sort out the perforated ones from them. And Rashal wrote: since proper checking is required and there is exertion — one should not rely on women in this checking. And Tur Choshen Mishpat wrote that the custom is to be lenient in this, for there are many sides to be lenient in this; and see above seif 5 whether legumes are accustomed to become wormy while attached or not.
