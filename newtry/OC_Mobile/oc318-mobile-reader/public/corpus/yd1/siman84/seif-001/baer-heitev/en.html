"In vessels." And the same applies to other liquids in vessels.
"Its mouth." Shach wrote: it appears that even for the prohibition of "you shall not make yourselves detestable" they do not apply; and although above siman 13 Rabbeinu Yerucham wrote that even clean fish are forbidden to eat alive because of "you shall not make yourselves detestable" — it is different here since his intention is only to drink the water and they are also not visible; and Taz wrote in the name of Or Zarua that only when one is not repulsed by them; but if repulsed or there is danger concern, it is forbidden because of "you shall not make yourselves detestable". [Rambam end of Hilchos Rotzeach, and so Maharshal. Peri Chadash wrote: know that whatever people are not accustomed to eat and refrain from is called repulsive and may not be eaten; and if it is repulsive to him even though not repulsive to everyone, all the more so it is forbidden to him because of "you shall not make yourselves detestable" — and all this is only d'rabbanan and one is not flogged for it; see there.]
"In a vessel." Shach wrote: and the same — to take water by hand is forbidden; and in a vessel — the reason is that we are concerned lest they separated on the sides of the vessel; for only on the rim of the vessel they do not commonly separate, but on the inside walls of the vessel it is common that they separate and they are forbidden; for only when they separated along with the liquid from vessel to vessel is it permitted in sixty even if they afterward separated on the walls of the second vessel — that is their majority; therefore a second vessel has the law of a first vessel. Not so here, where they were initially in the cistern; and accordingly what was written "but forbidden to draw" etc. refers only to pits, springs, and caves; but it is permitted to draw with a small vessel from a large vessel, for even if they later separate in the second vessel they are permitted; but from Taz's words it appears that even to draw with a small vessel from a large vessel is also forbidden, and it is not permitted unless when pouring specifically from vessel to vessel; but when drawing from vessel to vessel we are concerned lest they separate on the vessel before the liquid enters there.
"Behind." Shach wrote: the law is not corrected; and in Terumat HaDeshen for Rashba he wrote behind the vessel or on the rim of the cistern; nevertheless it appears that sometimes one finds a vessel whose rim is wide.
"Permitted." Shach wrote: and some say to strain in sixty — that we are concerned lest they separated; for there it is different since they commonly separate on the spices and remain; and the same if they separated on the inner wall of the cistern — also permitted.
