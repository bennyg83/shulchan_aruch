But it is forbidden to draw with a vessel, etc.—know that the Rav Beit Yosef wrote as follows: and how did he pour liquids that have sheratzim in them from vessel to another vessel—it appears the second vessel has the law of the first vessel; and when they separated onto the interior walls from inside, since they did not separate there except with the liquid, it is "their majority," end quote. And even though from the words of the Rash regarding pits it appears that if one drew water in a vessel and they separated onto the interior walls of the vessel they are forbidden—perhaps there it is different, for initially it was in a pit and now in a vessel; but here, where initially it was a vessel and now a vessel, it is fitting to say "their majority," end quote. And the Shach and Pri Chadash followed him; and therefore they explained that which the Rama wrote—that it is forbidden to draw with a vessel—its meaning is from a pit; but from vessel to vessel it is permitted. And Pri Chadash wondered at the Rama that he ruled anonymously—see there. I did not understand the difficulty—for do we not know the difference between pit and vessel and between vessel and vessel? Only this: there is a difference between pouring from vessel to vessel and drawing from within a small vessel to a large one—for certainly on the interior walls of a vessel, in a place where there is liquid, it is called "their majority," even though sometimes the worm stirs above the liquid; nevertheless, such is "their majority," and nature is from above to below—and this is "their majority." But if they separated onto the exterior walls of the vessel, in a place where there is no liquid at all—in this "their majority" does not apply, and it is forbidden. Therefore, when pouring from vessel to vessel—if so, the stirring inside the vessel will stir on the wall above the liquid—this is "their majority"; and to stir outward to the other wall—we are not concerned at all. But when drawing—if so, the exterior wall comes into the liquid and sticks, and the stirrers stirred on it; and when he takes the vessel outside, behold the stirring is on the exterior wall of the vessel without liquid—and therefore we are concerned lest it stir from outside to inside; but not from inside to outside, for it is not its way to distance from liquid—and this is clear. Therefore the Rama ruled anonymously that it is forbidden to draw—even from vessel to vessel pouring is permitted, but drawing is forbidden—and this is clear and correct in my humble opinion; and so it appears somewhat in the Taz, see there, s.k. 5—see there.
