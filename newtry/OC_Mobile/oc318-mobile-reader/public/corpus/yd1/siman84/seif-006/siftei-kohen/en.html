We do not apply to them "the sheretz that crawls on the ground" as long as they did not stir—and likewise the Maharshal. However, it appears that as long as the fruit is not cooked, one should remove the worm from it, as written in Tur Choshen Mishpat there, dalet bet—for the Rashba, Ran, and R' Yonah wrote to forbid even in a narrow place that did not stir; and also in Sha'arim it appears so; and likewise the Rav HaMaggid, chapter 2 of Ma'achalot Asurot, for so is the view of the Rambam and Ramban, and so is his view. And also what the poskim wrote that Rashi's view is to permit—I do not know where Rashi's view appears thus; and likewise in Orchot Chaim there, dalet bet. If so, certainly one should be stringent in a Torah prohibition as long as it is not cooked; but certainly if it was cooked, even a small fruit is permitted—for otherwise some say that every fruit, even if small, is sixty against the worm, as will be explained in seif 9.
And sometimes it is found in fruit, etc.—there is difficulty in what the Mechaber wrote, that regarding worms found in beans and peas under the shell, and the shell closes over them from outside—they are permitted since they are placed in a narrow place and did not stir; and the Rav ruled like him. And likewise in the responsum of the Rashba there it appears explicitly that he did not write this law except according to his view that we do not require stirring. However, one can resolve that the Rav also wrote thus according to what is written in Tur Choshen Mishpat—that even in beans one should be stringent to remove them from there as long as it is not cooked. And what he ruled anonymously like the words of the Mechaber—that is regarding the law; and the practical difference is that he does not forbid the stew. And in Eitan Tzvi he brought the words of the Mechaber anonymously and afterwards the words of the Rav anonymously—and it appears he was not precise, as I wrote.
