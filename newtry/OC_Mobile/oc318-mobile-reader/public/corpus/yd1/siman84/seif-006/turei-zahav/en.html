But worms found in beans, etc. The Rosh permits worms found beneath the skin where they have no room to crawl, while the Rashba prohibits them because anything that develops in produce while attached to the ground is treated as land-creeping, even if it never crawled upon the ground. The Ran and Rabbeinu Yechiel agree with the Rashba, and the Rama's authorities rule accordingly. Ideally one should therefore remove and discard the affected item, even after cooking, whenever there is concern that the worm developed while attached. The taste alone does not prohibit the entire dish, but one must avoid consuming the worm itself. The Rama's later ruling concerning a black point in fruit follows the Rashba's view. In practical halacha, we should be stringent even for worms beneath the skin, though the dish is not prohibited merely because of their imparted taste.
