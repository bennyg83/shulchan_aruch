We do not apply to it "crawling on the ground"—the Rav in Shach, s.k. 19: his view is to be stringent regarding the worm, for most poskim are stringent—and he is correct with him. But what he wrote that Rashi is not mentioned to permit—I did not understand his words; for at first glance Rashi's words—for Rashi wrote explicitly they are forbidden, for it walks inside the rind and it is attached to the ground—it is like walking on the ground; behold he gave a reason: since it walks. But in such a case where it is in a narrow place and cannot stir at all and is fixed in its place—there is no "crawling on the ground" here; and see Nekudat HaKesef who sensed this. But with all this, in my humble opinion there is no compulsion from Rashi to infer that certainly in the body of the law it is obvious Rashi holds that as long as it does not stir it is not forbidden; and therefore Rashi explained "walking inside the rind." But for the law—what of it that the shell is narrow and cannot move? Behold this worm is formed from mold, as the Rambam wrote—and see his hasagah on Shoresh 9 of the Rambam's count of mitzvot. If so, from the beginning it forms in utmost smallness until literally no eye controls it; and afterwards multiplies and grows by nature like all creatures that come into being—and this is straightforward. If so, from the beginning of its existence the worm had a place where it could stir somewhat and move whatever amount; and afterwards when it grew it filled all the space of the fruit and a narrow place—it cannot be that as it is now, so it was from the beginning of its creation, for such is not the nature of things that come into being, as is known. If so, obviously we should forbid—perhaps then it stirred somewhat and was already forbidden, and it does not return to permission; and therefore in the body of the explanation, and particularly this explanation partially afterwards was not spread out—Rashi had to explain it walks inside the rind. But according to what we hold, explained partially—it is forbidden; if so, obviously we should be concerned before it filled the entire quantity of the fruit it stirred partially even whatever amount—and it is forbidden; and this is clear and correct. Therefore for the law one should be stringent and forbid—examine. And it is not difficult for Rashi why the gemara answers on those two baraitot that teach one to include and one to exclude—one with enmity and one without enmity; he equates everything to enmity—only one where there is place to stir and one where there is no place to stir, as taught: protrusions in lentils that are narrow and cannot stir—and therefore they are permitted. One can say: that which it did not stir—it is permitted—is because we require crawling, and this is not crawling. But from "on the ground" it was not excluded—for it is with enmity and it is on the ground. And in the baraita it was excluded from "on the ground"—therefore he establishes it in detached produce and not on the ground; and properly it was excluded from "on the ground." And the gemara rejects: also with enmity it is not called attached except in vine roots—and properly it was excluded from "on the ground."
