Swarmed. The Shach wrote: however it appears that as long as the fruit has not been cooked, one should remove the worm from it — since the Rashba, Ran, and R' Yitzchak forbid even in a narrow place; if so, certainly one should be stringent regarding a Torah prohibition. But certainly if it was cooked, even a small fruit is permitted — since in any case some say that every fruit, even if it is small, has sixty against the worm. And the Taz wrote: and regarding practical halachah, certainly we hold to be stringent even when found within the shell; but we do not forbid the cooked dish because of the taste of these yabchushin; however, whenever there is concern that within the cooked dish one will eat the yabchushin themselves — certainly one should be stringent — end of his words.
Itself. The Shach wrote: it requires study — for the Mechaber wrote that worms found in beans under the shell are permitted, since they lie in a narrow place and did not swarm; and the Rav stam follows him, yet here he forbids. It can be resolved: the Rav wrote so according to what is written in Tur Choshen Mishpat — that even in beans one should be stringent to remove it from there as long as it has not been cooked; and that he wrote stam like the Mechaber's words is for the law and practical difference that he does not forbid the cooked dish (and see what is written in s.k. 14 in the name of the Taz — that if there is concern one will eat the yabchushin themselves, one should be stringent).
