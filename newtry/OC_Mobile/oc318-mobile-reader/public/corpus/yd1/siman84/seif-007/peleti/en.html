Fruit that became wormy, etc.—the view of the Acharonim: even if it is not its way to become wormy while attached, as the Shach and Pri Chadash wrote; and this law is mentioned in the Tur, and according to his method he goes above in siman 81 regarding cheeses from an animal where treifah was found—for he holds like the Rashba that whatever you cannot say "at this moment the treifah occurred" we forbid retroactively forever; and if so, here too at the moment the worminess did not occur—and if so, we forbid retroactively. But according to what the Rama wrote above, that even in a wound on the hoof we do not forbid retroactively until after three years—for certainly we say establish the animal in its presumption, and now it deteriorated—even here let us say establish the fruit in its presumption that it is not its way to become wormy, and now it became wormy; and particularly here, where if now it became wormy behold it is permitted—why should we not say establish it in its presumption, presumption of heter? And perhaps one can say: certainly to forbid the fruit because of the worm it is obvious that according to the Rama above it is permitted; only we judge here regarding the worm whether it is permitted—and the worm has no presumption of kashrut ever until we say now it came into being; and we establish retroactively it has no presumption of kashrut at all. But this also depends on the dispute: if we say while attached it is immediately forbidden and does not require separating at all—it is obvious it has no presumption of kashrut. But according to Rashi and his supporters—for even for Rashi stirring is required; and if so, at the time of its creation it was not stirring and was permitted—and if so, it had a clear presumption for one moment at the hour it came into being for heter; and we say establish it in its presumption. However, this is difficult—for perhaps we say it immediately stirs (and therefore I wrote below siman 100 that it is called forbidden from the beginning of its creation—for it immediately stirs; and see s.k. 5 before). But to forbid the fruit—for it has presumption of kashrut—certainly there is to permit; and we say establish it in its presumption: now that it deteriorated, unless it is common to become wormy while attached—we depend on the common thing.
