And they do not flow—some forbid and some permit; the dispute is explained in the gemara, since it is written "in water, in water": one tanna holds this is not klal u'frat but ribuy u'miut; therefore it included charitzin and neitzin even though they do not flow, for prohibition. And one tanna holds like the tanna of the house of Rav: wherever you find two adjacent klalot, cast the prat between them and judge them as klal u'frat. And even so, what prat—flowing water; all flowing water; but those that do not flow are permitted. In this the poskim disagreed whether the halachah is like the tanna of the house of Rav or like the two tannaim of Rabbi Yishmael; and the Rambam ruled like the tanna of the house of Rabbi Yishmael, stringently to forbid even those that do not flow. And Pri Chadash challenged: behold in Bechorot, perek Yesh Bekhor, we say in the mishnah that one does not redeem with land and slaves and not with documents; and Rabbi disagrees that everything is redeemed except documents—and we establish the dispute: Rabbi holds like the tanna of the house of R' Yosi that two adjacent klalot are judged by ribuy u'miut; Rabbi—all; and what did he exclude—documents; and the tanna of the mishnah holds like the tanna of the house of Rav—cast prat between them and excludes land, slaves, and documents—see there. If so, the Rambam and all poskim who ruled one does not redeem with land and slaves—it implies they hold we judge by klal u'frat and not ribuy u'miut; if so, also regarding charitzin and neitzin we should also require flowing, for from "in water, in water" we derive the above regarding bechor—and the difficulty is fitting for him. And one can answer in this: either the Rambam does not decide for us the halachah like whom—in such a case do we expound klal u'frat or ribuy u'miut—and therefore he ruled everywhere stringently regarding pidyon haben he ruled stringently that one does not redeem with land, for perhaps we expound klal u'frat; and regarding permitting water sheretz we go stringently, perhaps we expound ribuy u'miut—even charitzin that do not flow are forbidden; and we go on every side stringently. And in my chiddushim I said: it already appears from Tosafot, perek 1 of Shevuot 4b, d"h yatz'u shetarot—that which appears here that sale applies to documents, since it was necessary to exclude documents explicitly in Ma'achalot Asurot—behold they hold like Tosafot that if not from the Torah sale does not apply to documents, how is it possible regarding pidyon that the default is that he gave the kohen a document that has another as counterparty in redemption money—and if Torah acquisition does not apply, obviously there is no redemption in it (and see my chiddushim to Choshen Mishpat siman 60, where I wrote ways that there are documents where Torah sale is possible; but nevertheless the plain meaning of documents implies ordinary documents, and if Torah sale is not in them, the verse was not needed to exclude—and as Tosafot wrote). And behold Tosafot in Bava Batra 56b, d"h ketani, etc., wrote: and if you ask why he does not acquire from the Torah also the lien of land written in it—for land is acquired by money, document, and chazakah, etc.—and one can answer: just as he cannot dedicate it as proved in perek 26, since it is not in his possession, so he cannot transfer Torah acquisition, etc.—see there. If so, it works out for those who hold a person does not sell or dedicate what is not in his possession; but for R' Tam and his supporters who hold a person dedicates and sells something that has not yet come into the world—and all the more so what is not in his possession—obviously he can sell Torah documents; and we say in Yevamot 93a that Rabbi is like R' Meir, that a person acquires what is not in his possession; if so, for Rabbi Torah sale of documents is possible—as Tosafot's difficulty—and properly the verse was needed to exclude documents; and likewise for the tanna of the mishnah it was necessary to say he expounded klal u'frat and we do not say he holds sale of documents is not from the Torah and the verse was not needed to exclude documents—for the anonymous mishnah is R' Meir, and R' Meir holds a person acquires what is not in his possession; therefore we say they disagree in klal u'frat and ribuy u'miut. But for us, who hold a person does not sell what is not in his possession, and sale of documents is not Torah law and the verse was not needed to exclude documents—it comes to exclude land and slaves that are equated to land; and therefore even though we rule to expound ribuy u'miut, nevertheless one does not redeem with land—and examine and understand.
