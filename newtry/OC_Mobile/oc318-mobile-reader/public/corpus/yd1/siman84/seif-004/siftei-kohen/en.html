And some say even if it died inside the fruit, etc.—and in Tur Choshen Mishpat, klal 46, dalet heh, he wrote as follows: and the Maggid Mishneh wrote in chapter 2 of Ma'achalot Asurot in the name of the Halachot (that is, the Rif) and R' Chananel according to the words of the Rash (who holds that explained as dead is permitted), and so agreed the Rashba; and Orchot Chaim wrote that so we hold, end quote. And also in klal 47, dalet bet, he wrote: and it was already explained above that the Rambam forbids them even if they separated as dead—but we do not hold thus, end quote. And a mistake presented itself before him—for on the contrary, the Maggid Mishneh wrote there in the name of the Halachot and R' Chananel and the Rashba according to the words of the Rambam; and so it is in the Rif: explained as dead—what of it, end quote. And likewise in the Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe. Nevertheless, there is difficulty in the law—for even though in Knesses HaGedolah there he also wrote in the name of R' Tam like the Rambam, nevertheless the view of the Semak, Mordechai, Semag, lavin 332 (folio 44a), Hagahot Maimoniyot, Semak siman 212, Sha'arim, and other poskim is according to the Rash; and R' Yerucham also wrote that the great poskim agreed with the Rash; and so Orchot Chaim and Maharshal, Peri Etz Chaim, siman 112; and likewise is the view of the Mechaber—that this is primary. Therefore he brought first the view of the Rash anonymously, and afterwards the view of the Rambam in the name of "some say"; and likewise below, seif 8 and seif 16, anonymously as the view of the Rash, as will be explained there. And even though in his book the Beit Yosef brought only the words of the Rash and afterwards wrote "but the Rambam," etc., and the Rav HaMaggid wrote that such is the girsa of the Halachot, etc.—nevertheless, from what he wrote on this in his book Bedek HaBayit, it appears his view is that the view of the Rash is primary—see there.
And some forbid if its hole is pierced outward, for we are concerned lest they separated and returned. Some say regarding sheratzim in a vessel in SA we are not concerned for this—for here, since its hole is pierced outward, there is somewhat of proof that they separated and returned. Alternatively, it is the way of worms to extend outside the fruit and return, and not in a vessel.
Worms, etc., up to fruit-to-fruit—all these are problems in the gemara, and they were resolved in "Tiku," and we hold stringently.
