And some forbid even if it died inside the fruit—and the Shach's view is to permit; and so inclines the view of Pri Chadash; and so it appears clear—even those who forbid forbid only rabbinically, for how does "crawling on the ground" apply after death, and while alive it did not crawl at all; and it is clear it is forbidden rabbinically lest it be confused with separating while alive. If so, in a rabbinic matter we listen to be lenient. And know that in the gemara they also asked: if it separated to the air—what is the law? And Rashi explained: it separated from detached fruit to the air, and before it reached the ground he caught it in his mouth and swallowed it, etc. And one must understand why this strange case was needed—he did not explain simply that part of the worm separated through the hole while part is still inside the fruit and it is alive: if we judge it like separating onto the ground it is forbidden, or he did not intend onto the ground even partially but only in the air. It appears Rashi holds here we certainly require all of it and part does not suffice—for such is the way of worms; and this is an extension—and this is not separating except partially on the ground we are in doubt; but not in the air. Therefore Rashi had to explain that it separated entirely and before it fell he caught it in his mouth and ate it—and with this the words of the Rashba are correct, who permits in a hole—for it is not the way of a worm to return. Pri Chadash wondered: perhaps it took part out to the air and returned (and this is according to those who say safeik of the gemara does not enter the category of safeik safeika—for otherwise it is safeik safeika: perhaps it did not exit at all; and if you say it exited partially, perhaps in the air it is not called "crawling on the ground," for it was resolved in Tiku) that it slipped in the air—certainly it must exit entirely—and examine; and likewise Pri Chadash, except he did not bring proof for his words; and per what was said, it is compelled from Rashi—examine.
