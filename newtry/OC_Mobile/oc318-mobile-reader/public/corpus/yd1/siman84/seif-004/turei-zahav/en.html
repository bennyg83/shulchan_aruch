Even if its hole is pierced outward we are not concerned, etc. In Terumat HaDeshen, the long version, he explained his reason—that it is not common for them to return to their holes after they exit, as is implied in Eilu Treifot, that we are not concerned lest they separated on the outside wall of the vessel and returned to the vessel; thus we are not concerned lest they separated from the place of their majority and returned inside. And those who forbid—it appears they distinguish: here, with any separation there is prohibition when they separate only from the hole outward; unlike above, regarding separation from the liquid inside the vessel to the outside wall. And from the words of the Rosh whom Beit Yosef brings, s.v. the Rosh concluded—and below in our sugya it implies that wherever if it separated it is forbidden, we are concerned lest it separated—it appears like these forbidders. And if so, the Tur's view is puzzling, that he brought here only the view of the Rashba, which is for leniency, and did not bring his father's view for prohibition; and also above from it he brought the Tur explicitly not according to his father's view, who wrote "but stam one need not be concerned," etc. And further, it appears the Tur rules like the first language of the Rosh, that even to draw in a vessel is permitted from pits, ditches, and caves, since he is not concerned lest they separated—he should have brought that even with a drawing vessel; and why did he write only that one ducks and drinks? And it appears all is consistent: everywhere we do not establish presumption to say they separated, since there is no presumption before us; and we are concerned only specifically in a place where through human action there is this concern—such as in a strainer, where it is reasonable that through his filtering the worms came onto the strainer; unlike in that case where it separated through a hole pierced outward, where no action was done here that we should suspect separation—therefore we do not establish presumption. Therefore the Rosh wrote properly regarding drawing in a vessel from pits, etc.—there is also reason that at the time of insertion there, worms came onto its exterior; and even if they came to the inner side there is prohibition, since they came from the water of the pit—for heter is only if they came inside from the water in the vessel. And therefore the Rosh said properly; and below in our sugya regarding filtering we say that wherever if it separated it is forbidden, we are concerned lest it separated—and this is in a place where there is reason for this from human action. And therefore the Tur ruled: specifically one ducks and drinks from a pit, but not in a vessel. And therefore the Tur said properly "but stam one need not be concerned"—that is, if no action was done that brings about concern lest they separated. And one cannot say from what they said in the Gemara "if so, in a barrel too"—lest it separated to the walls of the vessel and fell back—there is no action here that brings about concern lest it separated; this is not difficult—for the questioner too knew it is customary to separate from the liquid inward on the wall, except he thought there is prohibition here; therefore he challenged lest it fell; and it answers: on the contrary—this very thing that brings about the concern you mentioned itself brings the heter—that is, their majority, for they are accustomed thus.
