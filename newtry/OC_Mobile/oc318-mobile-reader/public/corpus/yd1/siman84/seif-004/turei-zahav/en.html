Even if its hole opens outward, we are not concerned, etc. The Rashba explains that it is uncommon for a worm to emerge and then return to its hole. The stringent opinion distinguishes this case because even a slight emergence from the hole may constitute prohibited separation. The Rosh's language supports the stringent view wherever separation would prohibit. The Tur nevertheless distinguishes between a situation containing an actual basis for concern, such as filtering or inserting a vessel into a cistern, and a case with no human action suggesting separation. We do not create a defect without evidence. Thus, where the hole merely opens outward, no concern is raised unless some action made separation likely. This also explains why creatures that move naturally from the liquid onto the inner wall remain permitted: that movement itself is their ordinary mode of growth.
