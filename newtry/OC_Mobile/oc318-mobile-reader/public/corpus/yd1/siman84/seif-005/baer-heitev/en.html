The ground. The Shach wrote: likewise, if the flour is placed in a vessel — forbidden, for we are concerned lest they separated onto the inner walls of the vessel; for specifically regarding a liquid item we say in seif 1 that they are permitted when they separated on the inner walls of the vessel — "from all that is in water" — unlike a non-liquid item. [And this is what the Rosh wrote in Hagahah: or other non-liquid items; and see Peri Chadash who disagrees.] And specifically regarding small worms called mealworms — for one cannot sift the flour with a sieve and strainer; but if large worms are found in flour or in other items, which can be clarified with a sieve and the like — certainly the food is permitted. And the Taz wrote that there is wonder at the Mechaber: above in seif 4 he ruled that we are not concerned lest they separated even if its hole is open outward, for there is a contradiction before us — there is a hole made by a worm and nevertheless we are not concerned lest they separated even partially; yet here he ruled stringently. And he wrote further that the Bach distinguishes between somewhat large and small ones, and wants to permit the small mealworms in flour that one cannot sift with a sieve and strainer so that the worms remain on top (but certainly if large worms are found in flour or in other items that can be clarified with a sieve, the food is certainly permitted). And this is a wonder — for according to us, who hold that we are concerned lest they separated, there is no difference between large and small — end of his words.
In salt. Meaning: worms came into it from outside.
To an Israelite. The Shach explained: one must be concerned that he will sell it afterward to an Israelite in a manner whereby the prohibition is not recognizable — such as flour from which one made bread, and likewise salt that one puts in bread or in other things, as is customary to buy from non-Jews; but an item whose prohibition is recognizable is permitted to sell to a non-Jew, as the Rosh wrote in Hagahah siman 57 seif 21 — and not like the Bach, who permits in salt, and his words are not plausible. However, specifically all together is forbidden to sell to a non-Jew; but little by little is permitted to sell in a manner where there is no concern lest he return and sell it to an Israelite, as written in Orach Chaim siman 469 [and the Shach's son in Nechmad VeNaim challenged the Shach's four words that permitted selling to a non-Jew little by little — that they do not intend halachah at all; rather the main permission in this is to make it into bread and divide it into pieces and afterward sell it to a non-Jew; and so Peri Chadash]. And Maharshal wrote: likewise it is permitted to feed it to his slave and maidservant even though their sustenance is upon him; however it appears one should not leave it with him a long time lest a mishap occur, as above siman 57 seif 20. And the Taz disagrees on this — that it does not appear right to distinguish between a long time and a short time, for even in a short time there is concern for mishap; and even though in siman 86 it is explained that in a short time we are not concerned for mishap — there it is different, for the owners refrain from this matter and we are not concerned for stumbling; unlike here, where he will make bread from it for his slave and perhaps this bread will become mixed with other bread since it is eaten within his house. And he wrote further that the Bach permits selling wormy salt to a non-Jew, and likewise Yere'im and grapefruits or kinds of fruit such as plums and raisins — for there is no concern that he will sell to an Israelite, since their way is to become wormy and forbidden to eat them until one checks first; and he attacks him — for behold, all the aforementioned things we certainly buy every day from non-Jews and need not check as long as we see no contradiction — end quote; and certainly forbidden to sell to non-Jews except bit by bit, in my humble opinion — and it is obvious — end of his words. And the Shach wrote: and even though the Rosh wrote in end of siman 57 regarding doubtful tereifot and some declare it tereifah — even though we hold to forbid it — nevertheless it is permitted to sell it to a non-Jew, for there are two doubts: one, perhaps the halachah follows those who declare it permitted; and if you say it follows those who forbid — perhaps he will not sell it to an Israelite; and if so, here too there are two doubts — perhaps it did not separate, and if you say it separated, perhaps he will not sell it to an Israelite. But here it is different, since it is forbidden to all; and further, as I already wrote in siman 57, we do not say so except specifically regarding doubtful drusah and the like — since in any case there are several sides to permit — end of his words.
