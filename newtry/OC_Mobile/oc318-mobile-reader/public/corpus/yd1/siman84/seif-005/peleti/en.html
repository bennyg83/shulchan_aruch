Worms found in flour, etc.—Pri Chadash: his view is to permit if the flour is placed inside the vessel—that is, "their majority" like in liquids; and his words are incomprehensible. And properly he said in the hagahah of Shad that one should not compare—for granted regarding sheratzim that have no scales and fins, we forbid them only because the Torah permitted "all that is in the water" to eat them, and we say specifically "that is in the water"; but if they separated from the water they are forbidden—for this we say: since it is "their majority" to separate onto the interior walls of the vessel and return, it is called "that is in the water," for they are inside water; and if they separated, this is not called separating from the water, and the heter of "all that is in the water" applies to them. Unlike worms in detached fruits—is it written a heter? Only the verse "on the ground" excludes its protrusions, etc.—it is not in the category of "crawling on the ground," for they did not crawl; but if they separated, behold they are crawling—and what of it that they return, and such is "their majority"—nevertheless they are crawling, and behold they are in the category of "crawling on the ground"—and who will permit them? On the contrary, such is "their majority"—to crawl and return—and behold they are in the category of "crawling on the ground." Unlike stirring in water—true, they are crawling, but the Torah permitted them "all in water you may eat," even if they have no signs of pure fish; and since their arrangement is thus, they are included in "all that is in the water," and the Torah permitted. But here we have no heter to permit a sheretz whether "their majority" or not "their majority"—ultimately it is a sheretz, and this is clear; therefore Heaven forbid to be lenient. But if the worms are not recognized, I already wrote below in siman 100 that one can say it is not called clear and it is nullified—see there; and if so, obviously it is permitted to sell them to a gentile, for when a Jew sees it he will separate to buy, as the Shach wrote; and where he does not see, nullification is in majority—and see what I wrote below; see above siman 57, for the view of Tur Choshen Mishpat where there is "their majority" that permits—it is permitted to sell to a gentile; and here behold those extensions that hold it is nullified in a thousand—certainly it is permitted; and if so, obviously one should be lenient and sell to a gentile—and see Shach, s.k. 8, what he wrote on this. And what the Taz wrote—that according to those who permit in a hole in fruit we are not concerned they separated, here too he permits—it does not apply, for the Rash wrote explicitly that it is proven from the gemara: where there is concern they separated, we say perhaps they separated only next to the taste-hole—for it is not the way of a worm exiting from the hole and a hole that returns inside the hole and inside the fruit; and since it separated, separation—we are not concerned. But here in flour—is not the nature of stirring to stir here and there? Therefore it is forbidden—and it is straightforward.
