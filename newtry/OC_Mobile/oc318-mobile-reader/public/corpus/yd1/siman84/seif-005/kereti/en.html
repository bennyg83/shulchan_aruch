With regard to flour, Pri Chadash maintains that if the flour is contained in a vessel and is not resting directly upon the ground, it is permitted even if the worm separated. See Pleiti, where I wrote that Heaven forbid one should be lenient. There is no substance at all to Rabbeinu Tam's words on this point, and it is clear that the matter is forbidden.
Lest it return, etc. See Pleiti, where I wrote that in a case of necessity one may permit selling it to a non-Jew; see there. The Taz writes that according to the view that permits a worm found in a hole in a fruit, without concern that it may emerge through the hole and return, the same should apply to flour, and we should not be concerned that the creeping creature may separate and then return to its place. See Pleiti, where I explained that the comparison is not sound, and here it should be forbidden. The Taz also forbids producing spirits from such flour and the like, lest one later come to eat the flour itself. Pri Chadash has already challenged him, for we may not devise new decrees on our own, and his objection is correct.
