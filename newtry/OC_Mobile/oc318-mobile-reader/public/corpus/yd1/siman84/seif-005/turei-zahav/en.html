Worms found in flour, etc. Beit Yosef cited in the name of the gloss of Shad that some permit even if the worms separated to the walls of the jar and returned to the flour, like liquids above—and this is an error; for there the answer is from "all that are in the water" to include pits, etc.—that one ducks and drinks from them. But yabchushin in a vessel—even if some of them separated to the air of the world—it is a question and was not resolved; and therefore it is a complete prohibition. And I heard that the Av Beit Medrash did such a deed with wheat that became wormy—to throw them in the river and not sell to a gentile, lest he bake bread and sell it to a Jew, end quote. And Maharshal wrote on this, perek Eilu Treifot, that even so it is permitted to feed his slave and maidservant—only that he should not leave them a long time, lest stumbling come, like with eggs of safeik treifah, end quote. And it is not clear in my humble opinion to distinguish here between a long time and a short time—for even in a short time there is concern of stumbling; and even though in siman 87 it was explained that in a short time we are not concerned for stumbling—that is different there, for the owners abandon this matter and have no involvement in it, and we are not concerned for stumbling; unlike here, where he will make bread from it, and perhaps this bread will mix with other bread—since it is eaten in his house, it is impossible to be careful that it not mix with other bread—in my humble opinion.
Lest they separated. It appears that those who hold in seif 4 that we are not concerned lest they separated—even if its hole is pierced outward—all the more they were not concerned here; for there there is presumption before us that a hole was made by the worm, and nevertheless they were not concerned lest they separated even partially. Therefore there is wonder on the Mechaber in the Shulchan Aruch, who ruled above leniently and here stringently; and the Rama already ruled above also to be stringent. And in the gloss of Shad he wrote in this language: worms found in flour are permitted, and if they stirred in a sieve they are forbidden, end quote—Rokeach, end quote. It appears straightforward that he holds like those who permit above, that we are not concerned lest they separated; but for us, certainly we are concerned lest they separated. And I wrote this because Maharshal z"l brought proof from this to permit small milvein in flour, and distinguished between somewhat large and small—and it is very puzzling in my eyes, for one who permits worms in flour is found only in Agudah in the name of Rokeach, who wrote he permits, and his reason is that he is not concerned lest they separated; and we hold that we are concerned lest they separated, as written in Terumat HaDeshen, siman 171—for so we hold regarding fruits; and therefore there is no distinction between milvein in this.
And it is forbidden to sell them to a gentile, etc. Maharshal z"l wrote on this that the Rama was not precise—for there is no prohibition in selling to a gentile except regarding flour, lest he bake bread and sell it to a Jew; but not regarding salt, and likewise legumes such as peas and beans, or fruits such as plums and raisins—for one need not be concerned lest he sell to a Jew, since their way is to become wormy, and it is forbidden to eat them until one inspects first. And he brings proof from the responsum of the Ran, siman 57, seif 8, who concluded: but such as this, whose mark is evident and publicized in their city, it is permitted to sell to a gentile, end quote. What he brings as proof for leniency from the Ran—one who examines there will see the opposite, for there it deals with wheat that sprouted for Pesach, which is visible to the eye that it sprouted, and also publicized in the city—therefore he permitted selling to a gentile; unlike here with salt, peas, and beans—we certainly buy every day from the gentile without any inspection; and also dry plums and raisins need no inspection—all this because they saw no presumption, as I proved in seif 8. Therefore certainly it is forbidden to sell this to a gentile except kav by kav, as they said in Orach Chayyim, siman 464, seif 1, regarding wheat upon which water fell—in my humble opinion, straightforward. I was asked regarding flour that became wormy and one wants to make from it resin wine—that is, they extract moisture from it and that moisture becomes resin wine; here there is only taste alone, not any substance. And I answered that here there is no concern of "one does not nullify prohibition," etc.—for he did not intend only to extract moisture from the flour, as written above in this siman in the name of Terumat HaDeshen. However, I forbade for another reason—for we see that even to a gentile it is forbidden except kav by kav, lest he sell it to a Jew; so too here, if a Jew has any heter, it will come to be eaten as is, as stated.
