Worms found in flour—the Rash says they are called milvein; and this is specifically milvein that are small, so that the flour cannot be sifted in a sieve and sifter so that the worms remain on top. But certainly if large worms are found in flour or in other things, in a matter where they can be clarified by sieve and sifter or other means—certainly the food is permitted, as below seif 9 in the hagah.
Lest they separated and crawled on the ground—not specifically; for likewise if the flour is placed in a vessel it is forbidden, for we are concerned lest they spread to the interior walls of the vessel from inside, as above seif 1—for specifically regarding a drink we say in SA that they are permitted when they separated on the interior walls of the vessel, having multiplied from all that is in the water; not so regarding something that is not a drink—and so it is stated in the hagahah of Shad. And this is what the Rav wrote in the hagah: "or other things that are not of drink."
And likewise in salt, etc., and it is forbidden to sell it, etc.—meaning there is concern lest he later sell it to a Jew in a matter where the prohibition is not recognizable—for example flour: they will make bread from it and sell it to a Jew; and likewise salt—they will put it in bread or other things and sell it to a Jew. But something where the prohibition is evident—it is permitted to sell it to a gentile, as the Rav wrote in hagah, siman 57, seif 21: for behold a Jew sees it and it is straightforward. Only that in Beit Chadash it is written that the Rav was not precise—that there is no prohibition in selling to a gentile only regarding flour, lest they bake and sell to a Jew; but not regarding salt, for behold they will inspect it first—end quote. I did not arrive at the end of his view, for certainly the Rav was very precise: for even in salt and other things there is concern lest they put it in bread or salted fish or herring or lambs that it is permitted to take from them, as below siman 114, and many such cases. (Also what he wrote "for behold they will inspect first"—it does not apply, for certainly salt is not usually wormy at all, as is also stated in perek 3 of Bechorot regarding the sages of Bei Atuna: every thing that is not usually wormy needs no inspection. And here we deal with worms found in salt that came from elsewhere, and due to moisture we treat them together with the others—but for another reason we are not concerned lest he sell it to a Jew with the eye—for presumably if there are worms in it, a Jew will see them.)
And it is forbidden to sell it to a gentile, etc.—meaning all together; but it is permitted to sell little by little in a manner that the gentile will not later sell it to a Jew, as the Tur and Mechaber wrote in Orach Chayyim, beginning of siman 466, regarding grain that was drowned in a river. And Orchot Chaim wrote there, dalet vav: likewise it is permitted to feed it to his slave and maidservant, even though their sustenance is upon him; however it appears he should not keep it a long time, lest mishap occur—as above siman 57, s.k. 20. And likewise wrote the Maharshal, Peri Etz Chaim, siman 125.
Lest he return, etc.—and that which the Rav wrote in hagah, end of siman 57, that regarding safeik treifot there are those who permit the treifah—even though we hold to forbid it—nevertheless it is permitted to sell it to a gentile, for there are two safeikot: one, perhaps the halachah is according to those who permit; and if you say according to those who forbid, perhaps he will not sell it to a Jew. If so, here too there are two safeikot: perhaps they did not separate; and if you say they separated, perhaps he will not sell it to a Jew. Here it is different, since it is forbidden to all, as the Tur Choshen Mishpat distinguished that I brought at end of siman 57. And even though in the hagahah of Shad he wrote in the name of the Rivam to permit, and the Beit Yosef brings it, and likewise in the Agudah in the name of the Rokeach, and it is brought in Tur Choshen Mishpat at the end of klal 46—nevertheless those are nullified against all the poskim. And moreover, in the hagahah of Shad there he wrote that this is an oversight—see there. We do not say they rely on those who permit except where there is no decision in the matter, and also not all poskim disagree with those who permit—unlike here. Alternatively, here it is different, since the Rambam himself forbade selling them to a gentile, and likewise other poskim—we do not say it is permitted to sell to a gentile except where even those who forbid did not mention in their words that it is forbidden to sell to a gentile; for perhaps they themselves relied on those who permit selling to a gentile—unlike here. And moreover, I already wrote at end of siman 57 that we do not say thus except specifically regarding safeik derusah and the like, since otherwise there are several sides to permit—see there; and see end of siman 23 and siman 112, and in what is written there and in siman 142, s.k. 8.
