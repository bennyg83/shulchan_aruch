Taz wrote: for there is a double doubt — a doubt whether there are any, and even if there are — a doubt whether they dissolved at baking time; and there is no nullifying prohibition l'chatchila here since our intention is only to bake the bread and not to nullify the worms. And Shach wrote: this permission is only when they are wormy in a way that one can sift them before grinding; but not when worms are found in the majority in a way that cannot be sifted. And in Hagahat Shmuel David he wrote that he had an episode with wheat that became wormy — he cast it in the river and did not wish to permit selling it to a non-Jew lest he bake bread and sell it to a Jew; and in Terumat HaDeshen he wrote the reason they permitted grinding — that the worms squirm and flee outward through the sides of the hopper due to the noise and shaking of the mill; and accordingly with a mill that has no hopper, like hand mills, it would be forbidden; except that afterward he wrote another reason to permit — that even if ground the worms are nullified in sixty within the flour; and if so in every mill it is permitted; but it appears that where one can grind in a large mill with a hopper, one should not grind in another mill.
