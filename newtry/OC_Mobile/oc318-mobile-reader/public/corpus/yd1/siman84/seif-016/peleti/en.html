Because there are those who permit them in every matter—it is what he wrote in the name of Rabeiah: separating is forbidden only in something of the great earth, like fruits and the like; but in meat, fish, cheese there is no rule of sheretz at all, even if separated. And Pri Chadash extended to attack. And in truth, in the body of the matter it is true with him that many authors disagree and did not distinguish at all between fruits and meat; but he exceeded measure to speak great things against Hagahot Ashri and Orchot Chaim as if they erred in the words of Rabeiah—for the entire tendency of Rabeiah is to distinguish thus: if they separated from inside the meat to on top of the meat—in fruit it is forbidden, for there the way of the worm is to stick to the pit; unlike in meat and cheese, the way is to separate from inside onto on top. And apart from Heaven forbid to speak against great investigators of the heart—and particularly Orchot Chaim, from whose mouth we live literally in practical instruction of Orach Chaim—that he writes and rules things not clear to him, and he examines and is precise. And in the words of Rabeiah—even so, according to Pri Chadash there is no distinction between meat and fruit except if it separated from on top into inside, and likewise the reverse—that is, "their majority"; and otherwise not. If so, in that case if it separated to the air one cannot depict a distinction between fruit and meat. However, the Mordechai wrote explicitly in perek Elu Treifot in the name of Rabeiah: this question—separated to the air—in fruits it was stated, and not in meat and fish. Let Pri Chadash say: what difference is there regarding separating to the air between fruits and meat and fish? And the matter is clear that he holds in something that is not of the great earth—even if separated it is permitted by law; and Pri Chadash did not speak correctly. And I found that Rabeiah had proof—for he explains like Rashi: kokaini, meaning worms in an animal, as the language of the gemara implies through "beit hara'i ibai"—and this language suits an animal; but in fish the language would be "in the gut if he wishes to find"—and if so, the difficulty of Tosafot is hard for him: how could he permit—behold it is explicit in the baraita for the majority, worms in an animal. Therefore he explained in the gemara: the tanna "swallow it for me"—Rashi: swallow inside the fish; "give them that I not see and be disgusted by them"—and it is hard how he can put worms inside fish; for if he takes them and puts them elsewhere or the same place—you have no greater separating than taking a worm by hand from fruit or fish and putting it in a special place; and it is unnecessary "he left them"—only the language "swallow it for me" does not bear this, and it must be as Rashi wrote: "give them," etc. And if it is difficult—behold this is separating—there is no difficulty, for in fish it makes no difference whether it separated, since it is not of the great earth. And in the Ran he indeed did not explain thus on "swallow it for me"—see there. If so, Tosafot's difficulty is resolved—it is not difficult from that baraita, for one can say we deal with separating; and if it is difficult—if so, what does it teach us? It teaches us much—that not like Rabeiah, for even in something that is not of the earth separating prohibition applies. But after the gemara says "swallow it for me," etc.—it is proven the truth is like Rabeiah, that separating does not apply in fish; if so, he properly challenges from that baraita—for according to his explanation, since it is not of the great earth. And the gemara answers: therefore it is forbidden—for it is limb from the living—and this is clear; and the words of Rabeiah are almost compelled. Therefore certainly a baal nefesh should be stringent, for many great rabbis are stringent; but one should not protest against those who are lenient, for they have whom to rely on—Rabeiah and his supporters and Yeshuot Yaakov—and they are fit to rely on them even not in time of distress.
