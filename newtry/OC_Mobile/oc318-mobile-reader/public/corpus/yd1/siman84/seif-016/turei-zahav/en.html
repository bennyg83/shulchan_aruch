Those found in their intestines are forbidden. They came from outside and entered through the fish's nostrils while it slept.
Those that grow in meat. An animal becomes permitted through slaughter, but worms possess independent life and are not included in the slaughter, so worms already alive before slaughter remain prohibited. Fish require no slaughter, and worms that develop within them are therefore permitted unless they entered from outside. Worms that develop in animal flesh after slaughter are likewise permitted.
Lest they separated while alive. This means while the worms themselves were alive; those found during washing may have detached. The Rosh writes that one might also have been concerned that when the meat was placed in cold water they moved onto the pot's wall, but the custom is lenient. The basis is the opinion that the prohibition of creeping creatures applies only to organisms growing from the ground, so worms in meat or cheese remain permitted even after complete separation. The Rama nevertheless prohibits worms in cheese once they have fully separated, at least because of appearance, and the same applies to meat. Maharshal adds that worms produced by decay in meat are repulsive and prohibited under “Do not make yourselves repulsive,” even if they do not technically have the status of a creeping creature.
