"In an animal." The reason is in Shach: because regarding an animal the matter depends on shechitah, and these that grew inside it before it was slaughtered came from the prohibition of ever min hachai; and the animal's shechitah does not help them since they have independent life; and although a fetus is permitted through its mother's shechitah — there it is derived from "from every animal you may eat" — but here their prohibition remains.
"In their stomach." For they came from outside into them; but between skin and flesh is permitted, for they grew from them and there is no issue of ever min hachai, for fish need only gathering.
"They separated." Shach wrote: and this means they did not fully separate; but if they fully separated they are forbidden — for certainly they are no better than worms in cheese.
"Those who permit." Meaning — according to those who permit in meat after shechitah; and what was written "if it separated dead" — and above seif 4 brought, some forbid even if it separated dead — that is because the view of those who permit is primary in his view.
"They fell." Tur wrote: one should have been concerned when putting the meat in the pot in cold water lest they separated on the walls of the pot; but they are accustomed to permit — end of his words; and the reason is because there are those who permit in every case.
"The cheese." Shach wrote: not specifically — but the same in a bowl they are permitted; but if they separated on the table they are forbidden because of marit ayin.
