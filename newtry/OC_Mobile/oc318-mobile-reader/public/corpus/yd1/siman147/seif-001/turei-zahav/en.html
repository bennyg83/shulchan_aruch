And it is forbidden to mention it, and so forth. Tur writes: "It is forbidden even to mention it by name gratuitously, without need." The word "even" teaches two points. First, one should not think that only making a vow in the name of idolatry is prohibited; even merely mentioning its name is prohibited. Second, the Gemara says one may not tell another person, "Wait for me beside such-and-such idolatry." Rosh writes that one should not say the prohibition applies only in such a case, where he has a need for the reference. It applies equally when there is no need, and Scripture merely speaks in the ordinary manner. Alternatively, one could reason in the opposite direction: perhaps where he has a real need, Chazal were not stringent about mentioning it because everyone understands that the name is being used only as a landmark. The teaching therefore tells us that even for a need it is prohibited. Tur then states that in truth even gratuitous mention is forbidden, and all the more so where he has a need, because the expression may sound as though he attributes real significance to the idolatry. This second novelty is more straightforward than the first, and it resolves Beit Yosef's question on Rosh and Tur.
