May your god assist you. In the course of his discussion, he was not sufficiently precise in this ruling, for he derived it from the Rambam at the end of chapter 5 of Hilchot Gittin. I examined the passage and, Heaven forbid, the Rambam did not intend to permit such a statement. There he discusses encouraging those who violate the laws of Shemittah by working during the sabbatical year, and nevertheless one may say to them, 'May God assist you,' or, 'May your work prosper,' referring to Hashem. In old editions of the Rambam the text reads, 'May your god assist you,' but this is unquestionably a scribal error, for that wording has no connection to the subject under discussion. The newer editions state explicitly as I have written. To say, 'May your god assist you,' appears to be entirely forbidden, because it strengthens the hands of idolaters. The Levush explains the permission on the ground that the speaker is mocking him, but this is insufficient, since the non-Jew assumes that he is speaking sincerely, and the statement therefore encourages his idolatry. Accordingly, there is no basis for permitting it. I presented this ruling before my revered father-in-law, of blessed memory, and he established it as halachah in my name. See 40.
