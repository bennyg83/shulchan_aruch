"May your god help you." In the course of his discussion he was not sufficiently precise in this ruling. He derived it from Rambam at the end of chapter 5 of Hilchot Gittin. I examined Rambam there, and Heaven forbid that Rambam intended to permit such a statement. Rambam is discussing the rule that one may strengthen the hands of sinners during Sheviit, meaning people performing prohibited labor during the Sabbatical year, and nevertheless one may say to them, "May God help you," or "May He prosper your work," referring to the Holy One, blessed is He. In old printings of Rambam the text reads "your god," but there is no doubt this is a scribal error, because that phrase has no connection to the subject under discussion. New printings explicitly have the correct wording, as I have written. Saying "your god" to an idolater appears to me to be a complete prohibition because it strengthens the hands of idolaters. Levush explains the supposed leniency by saying the Jew is mocking him, but this is no answer. The gentile believes the Jew is speaking sincerely, and the Jew thereby strengthens his idolatrous practice. There is therefore no permission at all. I presented this to my father-in-law, of blessed memory, and he adopted it as practical halachah in my name.
