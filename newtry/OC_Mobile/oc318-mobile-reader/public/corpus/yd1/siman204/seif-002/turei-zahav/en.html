As Jerusalem. P.R. was rejected by the ministers of the office. And the Rebbe interpreted as sacrifices in Jerusalem:
If he vowed "like the table in the Temple," etc. I say — may the honorable sages inherit, but here the Rav did not intend, on the side of truth, the words of Mordekhai from whom this hagahah is taken. For this is the language of Hagahot Mordekhai in the chapter Shevuot HaEdut — they asked Maharam regarding one who swore in the sacred table or in one of the sacred vessels or in "table" without specification, whether it is an oath and he is obligated to fulfill it; and he answered: if he said "this loaf upon me like the table of the Temple" or "like one of the sacred vessels," this is a complete vow — he vowed with something vowed as stated in Nedarim "like an ewe, like enclosures," etc. If regarding one of those who serve at the altar, this is a vow. But in such a case where he vowed in "table" he said nothing — like one who vows in a Torah scroll, he said nothing, for it lies on the ground. But when he holds it in his hand, his mind is on the remembrances in it — thus even though this parchment on which the Torah is written is sacred, nevertheless he said nothing, except where his mind is on the remembrances, which is like a vow in the Name; and in a table there are no remembrances, therefore he said nothing. And nevertheless, for embarrassment one should penalize him, end quote. The Rav understood what Mordekhai said — but when he vowed in "table" he said nothing, this means when he vowed in "table" without specification and did not say "the sacred," and this is the distinction between the first part and the last part. And therefore this hagahah too distinguishes between the sacred table and "table" without specification. But this is not so — for why would "table" without specification be lighter than "ewe, enclosures" in this seif, where we do not say he means an ordinary ewe but unspecified vows are to be stringent? So too with "table" without specification. And in Levush it is written on this that a table is different because it is regularly a secular table — and there is no opening for these matters; are ordinary ewes and enclosures not common? And he did not examine the source of the law at all in this. And the simple matter is that the questioner of Maharam did not ask the law of "table" without specification — what is its law, whether its law is like one who says "the sacred" or not — for behold he wrote his question also regarding one who explicitly swore in the sacred table. Rather, if he said in the language of oath — meaning "I vow in the sacred table" which is like swearing in the sacred table — what is its law in this? And likewise he could ask if one says "I vow or swear in ewe and enclosures of the sacred." But such language of swearing or vowing in the sacred table is common; therefore he used this matter. And therefore he also used in the question "or in table without specification," for it is obvious that if the sacred table is an oath, likewise "table" without specification is like the ewe and enclosures we mentioned — rather in both he is in doubt; and Maharam answered that specifically when he says language of attachment, such as "this loaf upon me like the sacred table," then it is forbidden for he attaches to something vowed — and it turns out that also in attaching in "table" without specification, saying "this loaf upon me like table" without specification, it is likewise forbidden like ewe and enclosures. But in vowing in "table" — meaning he says language of oath "I vow or swear in table," whether sacred or unspecified anywhere — it is not an oath in any case. For also in vowing in a sefer Torah — which is also language of oath as the commentators explicitly explained, and Beit Yosef brought them in siman 212 — he did not forbid, because it lies on the ground, etc.; and in a table, even in the sacred, there are no remembrances; therefore it is not stricter than a Torah scroll parchment and it is not an oath at all here. It turns out there is no distinction at all between sacred table and unspecified — in both it is forbidden with language of attachment in a vow, and in both it is permitted if he says language "I vow or swear." And as written below siman 237 seif 6 — one who swears in heaven and earth or in the Temple table, etc., it is not an oath at all. And the Rav Rama's words here are like an error issuing from before the ruler; and these matters I arranged before the Maggid Mishneh z"l after he printed his work, and he agreed with my words and said explicitly that Rama erred in this.
