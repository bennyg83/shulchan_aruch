As Jerusalem. P.R. was rejected by the ministers of the office. And the Rebbe interpreted as sacrifices in Jerusalem:
If one vowed by the Table that is in the Temple, etc. I say: honor to the wise shall be inherited — but here the distinguished author did not correctly intend the words of Mordechai, from which this gloss was taken. This is the language of Hagahot Mordechai, chapter Shevuot HaEdut: they asked Maharam: one who swore by the holy table, or by one of the holy vessels, or by the table in general — is this an oath and is he obligated to fulfill it? He answered: if he said "this loaf is upon me like the table of the Temple or like one of the Temple vessels," that is a complete vow — for he has vowed by a vowed item, as it says in Nedarim: "like an offering, like enclosures, etc."; if by one of the accessories of the altar, it is a vow; but in this case of one who vows by the table — he said nothing, like one who vows by the Torah scroll — he said nothing when it rests on the ground, but when he holds it in his hand, his intent is upon the divine names written in it — implying that even though the parchment upon which the Torah is written is sacred, he said nothing unless his intent is upon the divine names, for that is like vowing by the Name; and with the table there are no divine names, therefore he said nothing; and in any case, an ignorant person should be penalized — end of quote. The distinguished author understood that when Mordechai said "one who vows by the table said nothing" this refers to vowing by the table in general without saying "the holy table" — and this is the distinction between the first clause and the last; and therefore he wrote this gloss distinguishing between the table of the Temple and the table in general. But this is incorrect — for why should a plain "table" be lighter than "offering" or "pens" in this seif, where we do not say he refers to secular offerings but all general vows are interpreted strictly? Likewise here with a plain table. And in Levush he wrote that a table is different since one is used to a secular table and there is no opening for such language — but this does not hold, for are secular offerings and pens not common? And he did not examine the source at all. The simple truth is that the questioner to Maharam did not ask what the law is for vowing by the table in general versus saying "the holy table"; for he already wrote in his question about one who explicitly swore "by the holy table" — if this formula has the status of an oath, what is the law? — and the same could have been asked about "the holy offering" or "pens." But the formula of swearing or vowing "by the holy table" is common, and therefore he took this example; and therefore he also wrote "or by the table in general" — since it is obvious that if the holy table constitutes an oath, the plain table does equally, as with "offering" and "pens." But he had doubt about both, and Maharam answered: only with the language of affixing — such as "this loaf upon me like the holy table" — is it forbidden, for one is affixing to a vowed item; and similarly affixing to a plain table is also forbidden, like "offering" and "pens." But when one vows by the table — meaning uses the language of swearing: "I vow or swear by the table," whether holy or plain — in every case it is not an oath, for even when one vows by the Torah scroll — which is also language of swearing, as the commentators explicitly explained, cited by Beit Yosef in siman 212 — he said nothing "when it rests on the ground, etc."; and with the table even when holy there are no divine names, hence it is not more serious than the parchment of the Torah scroll — and there is no oath here at all. Thus there is no distinction at all between the holy table and the plain table: in both, using the language of affixing to a vow is forbidden, and in both, using the language of "I vow" is permitted — and as written below in siman 237 seif 6: one who swore by the heavens and earth or by the Table of the Sanctuary, etc., it is not an oath at all. And the words of Rema here are like an error going forth from a ruler; and I presented these matters before my father-in-law of blessed memory after he had already published his work, and he agreed and said explicitly that Rema erred in this.
