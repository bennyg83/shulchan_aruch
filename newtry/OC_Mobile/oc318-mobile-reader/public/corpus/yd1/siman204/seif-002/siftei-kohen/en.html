If he vowed in the Temple table, etc. The Rav's words are not precise, and what emerges from Hagahot Mordekhai is that if he said "this loaf upon me like the table in the Temple" or "like one of the sacred vessels," it is a vow for he attached it to something vowed; but if he vowed or swore in the sacred table or in one of the sacred vessels, it is nothing (as below siman 237 seif 6). But one who attaches or vows in "table" without specification, and his intention is not like the table in the Temple — that he would require annulment in an unspecified manner — we have nothing on this. And in vows we follow the language of people, as below siman 217. And perhaps it seems to the Rav from that Hagahot Mordekhai concludes that in an unspecified manner one requires annulment, as stated in chapter 2 of Nedarim regarding a vow in cherem, etc. (as below siman 208 seif 2) — and he did not compare it to a vow in a Torah scroll (below siman 213), where he brings proof that annulment is not required. And we rule that in an unspecified manner annulment is required — thus he fully equates it to a vow in cherem in Torah, which has two meanings in its utterance and requires annulment in an unspecified manner — likewise here; but it is strained and requires investigation.
