"Like an imra." Meaning like the lamb of a korban. "Like pens" means the pen of sacrificial animals. "Like Jerusalem" refers, according to the Rosh, to the city's walls and towers, which came from the surplus of the chamber. Ran explains "like Jerusalem" to mean like the korbanos offered in Jerusalem.
"If he vowed by theirs," etc. The Rama's words are not precise. The conclusion of Hagahos Mordechai is that if he says, "This loaf is forbidden to me like the table in the Mikdash," or like one of the sacred vessels, it is a valid vow because he associated the permitted object with something whose sanctity resulted from a vow. But if he vows or swears "by the sacred table" or "by one of the sacred vessels," it is nothing, as below in siman 237, seif 6. If one associates his vow with an ordinary table, or simply vows "by the table" without intending the table of the Mikdash, a layman should not require release, because with vows we follow ordinary human language, as below in siman 217. Perhaps the Rama inferred from the conclusion of Hagahos Mordechai, which says that a layman requires release, as stated in chapter 2 of Nedarim concerning one who vows by cherem, see below siman 208, seif 2, and does not compare it to one who vows by the Torah, below siman 213, which Hagahos Mordechai cites there as proof that release is unnecessary. Since we rule that a layman does require release in the former case, perhaps the Rama understood that the table case is entirely analogous to a vow by cherem or by the Torah, expressions that can bear two meanings, and therefore a layman requires release here as well. This is strained and requires study.
