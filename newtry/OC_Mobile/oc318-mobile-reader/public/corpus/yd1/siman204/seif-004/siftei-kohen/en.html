A person forbids, etc. And Hagahot Asheri wrote in Ketubot chapter "Even though" that something that has not yet come into the world one may forbid upon himself by konam, just as one may forbid the fruits of his fellow; but in full hekdesh, no. And upon his fellow he cannot forbid something that has not yet come into the world even by konam — Mahariach, end quote. And this is explained from the sugya in chapter "Even though" (Ketubot page 59). And see there siman 227 s.k. 4 what he wrote in the name of the Ran; and below siman 234 seif 71; and in Hagahot Maharshal; and in Derishah siman 215. And in Beit Chadash siman 216 it is written in the name of teshuvah of Maharam Matz: regarding one who vowed to give a tithe from the profit that would come from his loan, and afterward he retracted and regretted — it appears he may retract because the profit had not yet come into the world. However, if he had said "this money — whatever profit comes from it I will give a tithe from it," the hekdesh takes effect since the money has come into the world. And likewise if he had lent a gentile two gold coins for three years and accrued interest with him immediately and gave him a document — it is possible that from this he cannot retract, etc. And it is different there regarding a vow to give a tithe, for it is hekdesh and it is also permitted for him. And see there siman 258 seifim 7 and 8.
