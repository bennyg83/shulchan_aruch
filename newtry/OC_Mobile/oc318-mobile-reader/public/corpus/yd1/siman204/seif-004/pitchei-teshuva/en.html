And see in Sha'ar HaMelekh chapter 3 of Hilchot Nedarim law 10, who brought from there the teshuvah of Bnei Yaakov regarding a slaughterer who forbade his slaughtering upon all of Israel; and the opinion of Rabbi Elazar Alfandari is that it is something that has not yet come into the world. And the mentioned rav disagrees with him, that it is not considered something that has not yet come into the world except when one says "the fruits of the palm shall be forbidden to you" — for the fruits have not yet come into the world and he did not forbid the tree itself upon him. And likewise regarding hekdesh something not yet in the world, etc. But here he is not forbidding upon him anything that he will do in the future — rather slaughter itself he is forbidding upon him, and that has come into the world. And he brought proofs for this; and the rav, author of Sha'ar HaMelekh there, disagrees with him and rejected his proofs, and raised that one may distinguish: where he forbade all his benefit upon Israel or upon so-and-so, whether benefit of his person or benefit of his property — then his prohibition falls on his person itself, which is in the world. But when he forbids a particular benefit, like one who says "the benefit of my use-objects is upon you" but did not forbid other benefits upon him — then the prohibition does not fall on his person and it is something that has not yet come into the world. Therefore he wrote that specifically when one says "the fruits of the palm shall be forbidden to you" he is not forbidden in them; but if he forbade upon his fellow the benefit of this palm, he is forbidden in the fruits that emerge later — for since he forbade upon him all benefit of the palm, the prohibition falls on the palm, and it is in the world. And if so, in the case of the mentioned rav too, where he did not forbid upon Israel only the benefit of his slaughter alone and not other benefits, it is something that has not yet come into the world, as the opinion of Rabbi Elazar Alfandari z"l — see there at length.
