The law of one who associates in a vow; it contains 4 se’ifim.
The essence of a vow stated in the Torah is that one associates with the vowed object — such as when one says, “This loaf is upon me like a korban,” or when he said, “This loaf is forbidden upon me,” or when he said, “This loaf is upon me as a prohibition.” And likewise, if he forbade upon himself one loaf, and hung it on the vowed object and said regarding another, “Let it be like this,” it is forbidden — and even up to 100, all are forbidden. Or if he vowed regarding one day to fast on it, or not to eat meat on it, and said regarding another day, “Let it be like this,” it is forbidden. (There are disputes regarding formulations in the matter of vows, and the Rav did not write them; see inside.)
