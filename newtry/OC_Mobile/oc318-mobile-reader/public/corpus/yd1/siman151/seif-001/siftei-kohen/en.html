Forbidden to sell etc. Unless he disqualifies them — as below; and all the more is it from below that it is forbidden.
To sell. Always, even not on their festival day and even nowadays — so the later authorities wrote, and it is simple; and the Bach further wrote: it is forbidden to every idolater even if not a priest, since they dedicate it to idolatry and he certainly needs it for idolatry.
For commerce it is permitted. We are not concerned that he may later sell it to others, because we are commanded concerning lifnei iver, but not lifnei d'lifnei, placing a stumbling block before one who may place a stumbling block before another. This is the Gemara's rule. See also Even HaEzer at the end of siman 5 in the Rama's gloss.
White frankincense — this includes black frankincense. And the Tur wrote even frankincense that is not white; and the Bach wrote because nowadays idolaters are not particular about this — therefore even if it is not white it is forbidden; however if it is apparent that he buys it for trade, it is plainly permitted even frankincense, as above — end.
To an ordinary idolater it is permitted. And we are not concerned he will give it to the priest — for lifnei de-lifnei we are not commanded.
Some say, etc. Their view is that just as the Gemara's prohibition on handing a cup of wine to a nazir applies only when the parties stand on opposite sides of a river and the nazir cannot obtain it himself, so too here. Others are stringent that a rabbinic prohibition remains even when the transgressor could obtain it independently. In my humble opinion these authorities do not disagree. Everyone agrees with the Mordechai and Tosafos in the first chapter of Avodah Zarah that with a non-Jew or an apostate it is permitted when he can obtain it independently. Hagahos Mordechai, Tosafos and the Rosh in the first chapter of Shabbos, and the Ran in Avodah Zarah concern an observant Jew whom one is obligated to separate from prohibition. The Rosh explicitly compares this to the duty to prevent a Jewish minor from eating prohibited food. No such duty applies to a non-Jew or an apostate. This also resolves the apparent contradictions among Tosafos, the Rosh, and Rabbeinu Yerucham.
And they practiced leniency. The Darkei Moshe’s language: however nowadays there is reason to be lenient because they are lenient to partner with them — end; meaning as he wrote in Orach Chaim end of siman 157 that nowadays it is permitted to partner with them, for their intent is to the Maker of heaven and earth, only they associate God’s name with something else; and we did not find that this constitutes lifnei iver, since Bnei Noach are not warned about partnership; and the same applies here.
