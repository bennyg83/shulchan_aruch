Things, etc. Rambam's and Tur's wording.
(Likkut) Things, etc., were. He [Rambam] omitted that which we say there: a rooster — for whom is it permitted, etc.; and also from there that it is permitted to sell a bundle, etc.; and also the question of Rav Ashi — and they gave him a black one and he took, etc.; Beit Yosef and Lechem Mishneh already questioned this — end.
And if he buys, etc. Tur and the other decisors. The Rambam omitted it, and this requires study.
And so too if, etc. These are the Mechaber's words, and it appears he derived them from what is written there: even if he said "this rooster [belongs to] a non-Jew who did such and such," etc.
(Likkut) Unless he invalidated them, etc. Mishnah there — end.
It is forbidden to sell, etc. This is included in their explanation; and see Tosafot there s.v. "chatzav." And in the conclusion, etc.
And specifically to a priest of idolatry. In the aforementioned Tosafot: if so it is forbidden forever, etc.; and also, etc. — it implies in the first part specifically to a priest.
Or to a non-Jew, etc. Ri"o.
Some say, etc. As written above seif 6 aleph-bet; and Tosafot there s.v. minayin. And it deals, etc.; but the Ran there wrote that all stated there is a Torah prohibition; but nonetheless it is forbidden rabbinically — for he is obligated to separate him from transgression, and how can he assist those who transgress? So too Tosafot in Shabbat 3a s.v. ba. The Rosh there. Darkei Moshe — and he wrote in the gloss: and there are those who are stringent, etc. From the Gemara in Avodah Zarah there it implies that even rabbinically it is permitted with a non-Jew — for otherwise what difference would the challenge make? Behold the Mishnah is only rabbinic; and the Ran's and Shabbat Tosafot's difficulty can be answered: specifically with a Jew it is forbidden to assist; but the Mordechai's law does not appear to deal specifically with one who "has" — they said there he does not transgress — and not with one who "finds" — for otherwise the case in Nedarim 62b would be difficult: "were there no wood in the world?" And it can be answered as the Ran wrote — rabbinically it is forbidden; and nonetheless there is a practical difference for before their festivals, since it is not known that he buys for idolatry; but the Sages only decreed in a case where one clearly knows — in such a case they did not decree. Ultimately, that case in Nedarim decides like the second view; and so is a great difficulty throughout the Mishnah, and so there 15b "Rabbah sold," etc. — he should have bought a donkey elsewhere; and so weapons there.
(Likkut) Some say, etc. At first glance it appears he learned from what is written there seif 6: for what practical difference does it make — he has an animal, etc.; but that is not comparable to there, for there he already "has" it; rather from the fact that it challenges "and does he have it," etc., and derives "here it is the case," etc. — so too Tosafot in Chagigah 13a s.v. ein, etc.; but this requires study from Nedarim 62: Rav Ashi had, etc. — could he not have bought wood elsewhere? Furthermore, in any case there is some prohibition; and Tosafot in Shabbat 3a s.v. ba, etc.: in any case there is a prohibition, etc. — and even if it deals with a non-Jew, etc. — meaning he is not commanded regarding Shabbat; but if "before the blind" applies to a non-Jew, it is forbidden even with a non-Jew even in such a case where he could have taken it — and see further — end.
