For commerce. Baer Heitev states that one is not commanded regarding 'before the one who is before.' Chavot Yair discusses bribing a non-Jewish judge and suggests a violation of lifnei iver because Noahides are commanded to establish justice. Giving the gift through the judge's servant may be only indirect assistance, provided no theft is involved, though Chavot Yair elsewhere treats agency by a non-Jew stringently.
They can purchase elsewhere. Emunat Shmuel questions this leniency. The Gemara permits where the non-Jew already has another animal, not merely where he can buy one. Otherwise the Mishnah's prohibition against selling would nearly disappear. A possible answer is that the alternative seller must himself be a non-Jew, so the transgression would occur without any Jewish assistance.
To sell. Chavot Yair analyzes a Jewish meat partner who supplied hindquarters and treifah meat to a non-Jew whose wife was a Jewish apostate. Sale on credit may be forbidden because she cannot readily obtain the same goods elsewhere, and delivery through a servant may count as direct agency. He later considers leniency for cash sales, indirect feeding by the non-Jewish husband, and concern for enmity, but notes that selling shaatnez clothing to a Jewish apostate may remain forbidden even where other sellers exist because a suitable garment may not be readily available.
Some are stringent. Baer Heitev cites Shach that the opinions may not truly disagree. Pitchei Teshuva finds support from the Mishnah in Demai concerning one who gives produce to his mother-in-law.
