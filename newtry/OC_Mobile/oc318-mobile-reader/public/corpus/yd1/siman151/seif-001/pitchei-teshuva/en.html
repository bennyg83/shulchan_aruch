For commerce. See Be'er Heitev, that we are not commanded regarding "before the before." See Chavot Yair responsa siman 136, concerning what some light-minded people do when they have a case with someone before a non-Jewish judge: they give, or promise to give, the judge a gift. It appears that this involves the prohibition of lifnei iver, since bnei Noach are commanded regarding laws of justice; see there. In my humble opinion it would appear at first glance that if he does not give the gift directly to the official judge himself, but to his servant to give to his master or to advocate for him, it is certainly permitted, provided there is no concern of theft, for it is "before the before." And if one would say that the servant is his agent and it is as though he gave it himself, that is not so, for there is no agency for a non-Jew; see below siman 169 seif 9 in the gloss. I later found that the author of Chavot Yair himself, in siman 185, noted this in another matter, but he treats it as obvious like the view that there is agency for a non-Jew as a stringency; I will bring it below on the words of the gloss.
