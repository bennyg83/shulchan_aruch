To sell. See responsum Chavat Yair siman 185 — asked: a Jew was partner of a gentile in the meat business, and this Jew gives all hindquarter meat even if it happened to be treifah to his partner on account of their business dealings, and this continued several years; recently the gentile married an apostate to idolatry — if permitted to give him as before treifah meat and hindquarters, since she eats from them and she is commanded regarding treifah, milk, and blood — behold he transgresses lifnei iver; he wrote that seemingly there is no prohibition concern because he can buy elsewhere, as the Rama writes here. Indeed a document and its receipt are with him — there are those who are stringent. And although they were lenient in practice, Shach wrote in the name of Darkei Moshe: the reason is that these gentiles are not idolaters because they were not warned about partnership (as written above siman 148 s.k. 2) — and this applies only to a plain gentile and not to an apostate in all transgressions; and moreover in our case the Jew gives the gentile on credit on account — certainly in such a case he cannot buy from another, and it is forbidden even according to the first view; therefore it appears for everyone forbidden to sell to an apostate to idolatry a garment fixed with shaatnez, although there are several gentile clothing sellers — it does not happen to fit his measure and price as required; also there is no permission in what he does not give the meat in the gentile's hand but only to his maidservant — and it is lifnei de-lifnei, for he sends her and it is as giving to his hand, for we rule stringently that there is agency for a gentile (see below siman 169 seif 9); and again he sided to be lenient and wrote that if henceforth he will not sell on credit but only for cash — certainly permitted, for Shach wrote that even those who are stringent deals specifically with a Jew and not with an apostate to idolatry; and even on credit there is another side — that every man is sovereign in his house, and especially between them the money is his; therefore although the gentile who buys feeds the apostate woman — lifnei de-lifnei we do not command; and on this side if he were an apostate to idolatry it is forbidden, and even if certainly he does not eat from it but feeds his apostate wife — also forbidden, since the apostate is also commanded regarding lifnei iver — this one who gives him also transgresses lifnei iver. And there is further to be lenient that there is concern for enmity; see there further what he wondered on the aforementioned Shach; and see in Dagul MeRevavah what he wrote to reconcile; see there in the omissions to page 172 and on page 201 further on the aforementioned Shach.
