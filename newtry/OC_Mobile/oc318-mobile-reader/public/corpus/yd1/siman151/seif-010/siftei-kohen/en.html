But not etc. Beit Yosef wrote: it appears that selling even for dwelling is permitted, since the gentile acquired it and Israel has no right in it; even if the gentile brings idols into it, we have no objection — end; and the Bach wrote it is simple that this is proven from the mishnah's language — end; and so appears from the poskim; and so is explained in the Rosh's words in the next s.k.
Since they are not accustomed etc. This is difficult, for we see that they bring idolatry into their homes even permanently; it is forced to say that since nowadays they are not idolaters it is not called their idol as idolatry; and it is possible that in the Rav’s time they were not accustomed to bring idolatry into their homes permanently, as the Rosh and Tur wrote in their times. But in my humble opinion we rely on the other answer the Rosh wrote: even if they bring idolatry into their homes it is permitted, since those Israelites pay a land-tax to the gentile — the house is not specially designated for the Israelite; and Bach brings it; and so in Tosafot at the end of the first chapter of Avodah Zarah s.v. "af bemakom" — it is proven explicitly according to R. Yitzchak and R. Chaim’s approach, and according to R. Elchanan’s approach that Tosafot brings in the conclusion — that even if he brings idolatry into it, it is permitted to rent in the diaspora; see there (and so the Mordechai and the Agudah brought R. Yitzchak and R. Chaim’s approach as the plain position). And so it appears explicitly in the Ran at the end of the first chapter of Avodah Zarah.
