For residence. Beit Yosef writes that sale even for residence is permitted, because once the non-Jew acquires the property the Jew has no right in it. If the non-Jew later brings idolatry inside, that is not the Jew's concern.
The practice. Shach asks how this can be reconciled with the observable fact that they bring idolatry into their homes. Perhaps in Rama's time they did not keep it there permanently. Alternatively, as Raavan writes, even if they do bring it in, renting remains permitted because Jews pay land taxes to the non-Jewish ruler, so the house is not exclusively designated to the Jew.
