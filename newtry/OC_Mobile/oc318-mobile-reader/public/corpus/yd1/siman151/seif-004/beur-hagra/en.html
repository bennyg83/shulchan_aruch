Or he knows, etc. There: “Rav Huna sold,” etc. See Tosafot there, s.v. “Say,” etc., who explain the passage with difficulty. The Rosh, however, writes there in the name of Rav Hai Gaon that it concerns a case in which it appears to him that the non-Jew purchases it only for slaughter. The Ramban explains that he sold it to a butcher, as stated in the Jerusalem Talmud on the Mishnah in Sheviit cited there in the Gemara: Beit Shammai say that one may not sell, etc. If he sold it to a butcher, all agree that it is for slaughter; if he sold it to a sharecropper, all agree that it is for plowing. Rather, the case concerns selling it to a broker.
