Animal. Such as in a place where they are suspected of bestiality — so in the Talmud. And for large livestock the reason is lest he lend it to a non-Jew or rent it and the non-Jew works with it on Shabbat and transgresses the negative commandment — for a Jew is commanded regarding resting his animal; or sometimes he sells it to him close to sunset on erev Shabbat and says to him "come, try it out" and it hears its voice and goes because of him, making it as though he drives his animal on Shabbat. The Tur wrote in the name of the Ramban: when he sells it to a butcher — certainly he buys for slaughter and it is permitted; and to a landowner — certainly for work and it is forbidden; for we always follow him and not the majority of the world; but one who is neither a butcher nor a landowner — then we follow the majority for what purpose they buy. So the Turei Zahav.
Broker. Meaning a Jewish broker, with whom those reasons do not apply.
Permitted. The reason given in the Semag: the "trying out" concern no longer applies in our times, as we are not so expert in [discerning] its voice; and also it is not our custom to lend or rent to a non-Jew. The Ran wrote: that is why they are lenient even to purchase in order to profit from its sale; and nonetheless, a conscientious person should be concerned and minimize it. But regarding lending and renting one should protest against those who are lenient. And see in Orach Chaim siman 246. All this is with an unclean animal; but with a clean animal the Sefer HaTerumot and the decisors wrote that none of this is required, since one can say he takes it for slaughter, as Rashi wrote.
