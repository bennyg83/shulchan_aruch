An animal. Such as in a place where they are suspected of bestiality. Regarding large livestock, the concern is that the Jew may lend or rent it to a non-Jew who works it on Shabbat, violating the Jew's obligation that his animal rest. Alternatively, he may sell it shortly before sunset on Friday and tell the non-Jew to try it. Hearing its owner's voice, the animal may walk because of him, making the Jew one who drives his animal on Shabbat. Ramban rules that sale to a butcher is certainly for slaughter and permitted, while sale to a landowner is certainly for labor and forbidden. If the purchaser is neither, we follow the usual purpose for which most people buy such animals. Taz.
A broker. Meaning a Jewish broker, to whom these concerns do not apply.
Permission. Semag explains that nowadays the concern that the animal will respond to the Jew's voice is uncommon, because we are not so expert in controlling animals by voice, and we do not ordinarily lend or rent them to non-Jews. Ran therefore permits even purchasing them for resale profit. A conscientious person should nevertheless minimize this. Lending or renting should still be protested. See Orach Chaim 246. This concerns non-kosher animals. Regarding kosher animals, Sefer HaTerumot and the authorities write that these restrictions are unnecessary because one may assume they are purchased for slaughter.
