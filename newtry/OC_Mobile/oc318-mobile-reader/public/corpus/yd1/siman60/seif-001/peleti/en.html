Animal that was fattened, etc. The source of this law is tractate Temurah: we taught Rabbi Chanina ben Antignos says: kosher is one that nursed from a treifah; forbidden. And we precede that it ate hot milk morning and evening — it was fattened on prohibition. And Tosafot wrote: the same if fattened on idolatrous beans; and at first glance it implies from their wording that for a layman too it is forbidden; but in Pesakim Tosafot they wrote: for the Most High forbidden and for a layman permitted — and so there would be no contradiction in the wording; Shach and Peri Chadash distinguished between milk and idolatrous beans, which are prohibitions of benefit, and challenged Rama who forbids a layman even on prohibitions of eating — we require specifically prohibitions of benefit. And it appears to me to add: even if fattened on chametz — nevertheless permitted, for what of it that benefit reaches from prohibitions of benefit — this is not in the manner of benefit, and one is flogged only for benefit in the usual manner; and is this the manner of benefit — that the prohibition divides to abundant food for the animal? In the animal's eating one benefits from prohibition — there is no "not in the usual manner" more than this; therefore everyone spoke of idolatrous beans, which for a gentile is forbidden even not in the usual manner, as written above siman 10 — see there. And one may say: but regarding other prohibitions, all agree it is permitted. And see Kesef HaGadol, who wrote that Moro's view too, Mahari"t, tends specifically to idolatrous beans and did not give a reason for his words. And behold, at first glance all these matters were only for Rabbi Chanina, as appears from Rambam's explanation of the mishnah and Rav Ra'avad; but the Sages disagree and the halacha is like them; and we precede on Tosafot's addition that they did not mention at all, for the tanna kamma disagrees — strained to say this is their intent in what they wrote; and at first glance it appears that for a layman too it is forbidden, and they did not write from what it appears; and it must be said: since the Gemara discusses this "kosher is one that nursed," etc., it implies these are matters for halacha; and it is difficult — behold the tanna kamma disagrees; therefore he holds Rabbi Chanina said even for a layman, and in this the tanna kamma argues; but for the Most High they agree — and with this the Gemara's discussion comes; and this is strained; and the Rav in Kesef HaGadol tends to be stringent and wrote from the implication of most poskim that since they did not mention the tanna kamma who argues on Rabbi Chanina — we learn there is no view that argues on Rabbi Chanina, and we do not find this at all. And I wonder how it could enter the mind that there is no view arguing on Rabbi Chanina, as implied from the poskim's words — if so, why did the Gemara toil in chapter HaTa'aruvot to find a way for this treifah that became mixed, and they say: offspring of treifah, and we reject that the rabbis argue; and it is difficult to establish in such a case an animal that nursed from treifah milk — and for the Most High certainly forbidden; and behold here there is wide room; and thus: since the rabbis argue on Rabbi Chanina, the Gemara did not want to establish such a case, for we do not rule like Rabbi Chanina — examine well.
