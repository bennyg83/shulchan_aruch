Seized by blood. — That blood seized it and it became ill — Rashi and Ran. And Rambam in his explanation of the mishnah explained that blood overcame it until it choked.
And smoked. — That smoke entered its body — Rashi and Ran. And Rambam explained that black bile overcame it.
And chilled. — Illness from cold — Rashi and Ran. And Rambam explained that white phlegm overcame it until its senses were nullified; and he wrote that they did not mention overcoming of red bile, because it is very rare in animals, as explained in books of animal medicine, end of his words.
Bad water. — In Rashi and Ran and Rabbenu Yerucham: exposed water; and see below siman 116.
An animal that was fattened, etc. — It is written in Torat Chatat general rule 65 din 10 in the name of Or Zarua: l'chatchila it is forbidden to buy an animal from a gentile that nursed from impure milk; and it implies there that even if it did not nurse all its days, it is forbidden to buy l'chatchila — see there in simanim; and therefore all the more it is forbidden l'chatchila to feed it forbidden things; and so appears from what we rule below siman 142 seif 11 regarding a cow fattened on idolatrous beans — permitted only b'dieved for this reason and that reason together; but l'chatchila forbidden. But in Mordechai chapter HaAlmanah leKohen Gadol it implies that even l'chatchila it is permitted to feed it forbidden things — see there; and it appears Mordechai holds that even if it was fattened all its days it is permitted, for he disagrees with the cow fattened on idolatrous beans that is in the Gemara Avodah Zarah daf 49a, that it is permitted specifically because this and that together; for there idolatrous beans are forbidden in benefit, unlike prohibitions of eating — for he does not eat the prohibition itself; and so appears in Tosafot Avodah Zarah there s.v. that if planted, etc., to distinguish thus; and so Or Zarua end of general rule 47. And that which the Rav wrote in the name of Tosafot: if it was fattened — it is not precise that the same applies if it ate and was not fattened, as proven in Tosafot Temurah; and also obvious that "all its days" is not precise, but means that its main growth was from them; and the same if it ate forbidden things all its days from morning to morning, even if in between it ate permitted things — the law is thus, as in the Gemara and Rashi and Tosafot there; and so appears at first glance in Tur HaShalem there, that so is Mordechai's and Or Zarua's view; and also Tosafot there appear as doubting the matter — see there; and one may answer that Tosafot refer specifically to idolatrous beans that they mentioned there, that in this even Mordechai concedes, as written above; and that which they wrote "and according to the flow," etc., and did not write plainly that it is forbidden, as proven in the Gemara Avodah Zarah (daf 49) — that if it is one cause it is forbidden; lest one say specifically if it ate all its days idolatrous beans, but from morning to morning eating other things in between — no; and so appears in Agur end of chapter Kol HaAsurim, who wrote: nursed hot milk from morning to morning — since all its growth was from treifot, forbidden; and Tosafot explained: the same for a layman — forbidden is an animal whose entire growth is from prohibition, such as from idolatrous beans and the like, end of his words — since it opened with treifot and concluded with idolatrous beans, it implies that for a layman it is forbidden only when it grew from idolatrous beans, which are forbidden in benefit; but for the altar it is forbidden even if not prohibitions of benefit, as in the Gemara Avodah Zarah (end of daf 46) and Rashi there; and one may answer that Tosafot wrote thus for Rabbi Chanina ben Antignos, and Rambam in his explanation of the mishnayot and Bartenura there wrote that the halacha is not like Rabbi Chanina — and requires study.
