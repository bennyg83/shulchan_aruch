All its days. Be'er Heitev wrote in the name of Beit Yosef, and it is explained there: specifically if it ate them in the measure that it can stand from time to time without other eating; but due to a small amount one need not forbid l'chatchila to slaughter it; and therefore they are not careful to slaughter chickens that peck in the garbage and eat worms and forbidden things — to wait for them me'eit le'eit — see there. [See in Sefer Levushei Serad ot 81, who wrote: it appears to me that even one who forbids if it was fattened all its days on prohibition — even b'dieved this is if it was slaughtered thus immediately; but if two or three days afterward it eats permitted food, again there is no reason to forbid it; for what difference to me if it ate all its days permitted and forbidden that it is permitted — what difference to me in such a case that it first ate all its days forbidden things and now ate permitted — do not remember the first; and even if we say forbidden to buy it l'chatchila intending thus to feed it afterward permitted — nevertheless b'dieved it appears reasonable it is permitted; and nevertheless he does not decide this matter — see there.]
