One who buys wool utensils from a gentile must examine them well, lest they be sewn with linen (and one who buys garments from a gentile must undo their sewing and sew them with cotton (Tur)). And even if the gentile speaks offhand that he sewed them with cotton, he is not believed. And in places where linen is more expensive than cotton, one may rely to permit. {Rama: And it is forbidden to tell a gentile to sew garments for him with cotton threads even though linen is more expensive, for it is easy to sew with linen than with cotton (Rashba siman 561). But if he gives him cotton threads, it is permitted, and we are not concerned for substitution, since one can ascertain the matter—that linen threads when lit extinguish quickly and cotton threads burn (as implied in Maggid Mishneh chapter 8 of Laws of Forbidden Mixtures). And so is the custom, even though some are stringent (the aforementioned Rashba). And an Israelite suspected of sewing kilayim—his law is like a gentile. Some are stringent that he should not sew a wool garment with white cotton lest they suspect it is linen, and therefore they dye the cotton (Maharil).}
