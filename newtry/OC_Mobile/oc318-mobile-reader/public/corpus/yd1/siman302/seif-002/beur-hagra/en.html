One who purchases, etc.; and one who buys, etc. Mishnah 7.
Even, etc. Although a non-Jew speaking incidentally is believed in rabbinic matters, here he makes the statement to enhance his merchandise, since it is known that Jews seek hemp. See the Rosh.
In places, etc. The Rash writes that in our places one may rely, etc. He apparently maintains that either reason alone suffices, but this is incorrect. Both reasons are needed, since the seller derives no benefit at all. See siman 118, seif 10. This is why the gloss says that it is forbidden to tell him. Nevertheless, the Rash permits reliance after the fact, as the Shulchan Aruch states, but this too is not correct.
But if, etc. As in Chullin 63, where he says that it is from a particular bird, and in chapter Gid HaNasheh 97 and chapter Kol HaBasar, where we rely on a professional cook's tasting. There it appears that he must be an expert, as stated at the beginning of siman 98, and the Maggid Mishneh writes likewise here. See the Shach. Similarly, Menachos 43a states that tzitzis may be bought from a merchant. See the Ran at the beginning of Chullin.
And a Jew, etc. The Ran writes that it appears permitted, as in Chullin 4a concerning their chametz. The Rashba is uncertain because sewing with linen is easy, as the Gemara says there that he would not trouble himself, etc.
