One may rely upon this to permit it. The Beit Yosef, in the name of the Rash, writes that in a place where linen is expensive and it is preferable to sew with hemp because hemp thread is stronger for sewing, one may permit it. My father-in-law of blessed memory asked why the Shulchan Aruch rules here that it is permitted on the basis of a single condition, namely that linen is expensive, whereas the Rash required a second condition as well, that it is preferable to sew with hemp because of its strength. He was compelled to answer that the Shulchan Aruch is speaking after the fact. It appears to me, however, that the Rash’s statement that hemp is stronger is not an additional condition at all, because hemp is certainly stronger in every place. Rather, the decisive condition is that linen be expensive. The Rash mentions the strength of hemp only to answer an objection: what difference does it make that linen is expensive, since there is a countervailing reason to prefer linen for sewing, namely that it is smoother than hemp, as the Beit Yosef writes in the name of the Ran and as the Rama writes below? To this the Rash answers that hemp is stronger for sewing and does not tear during the sewing, whereas linen may tear and require the craftsman to repair his work anew. These considerations therefore balance one another. Linen has an advantage because of its smoothness and a disadvantage because of its weakness. The matter consequently depends upon whether linen is expensive, for that factor tips the balance toward permission, and the price varies from place to place. The greater strength of hemp thread, however, is certainly the same everywhere. This appears obvious to me.
But if he gives him, etc. The Rama rules that one may permit specifically in this case, following the view of the Maggid Mishneh, because we are concerned that the craftsman might substitute one material for another when the matter can later be discovered. If the Jew did not give him hemp, the craftsman can excuse and repair his misconduct by saying, “I made a mistake and thought that this was hemp.” But when the Jew gave him hemp and it is later discovered that he used linen, he cannot repair his conduct with such an excuse, for he should have been careful with the material given to him and should have set it aside in a designated place. This appears to me to be the Rama’s reasoning. He does not accept the Rash’s reason that hemp is stronger. The Rama did not formulate his ruling as a disagreement with the Shulchan Aruch because it is possible that the Shulchan Aruch too is speaking of a case of this kind.
