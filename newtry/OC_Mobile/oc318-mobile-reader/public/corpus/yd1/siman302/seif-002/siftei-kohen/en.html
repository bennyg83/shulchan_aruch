And even if the gentile speaks offhandedly, etc. — for it is known to all gentiles that Jewish tailors go around the villages to buy hemp thread from them for sewing, because it is not as available as flax; therefore one can say this gentile says so to enhance his merchandise; so wrote Rosh and Taz. And one should not challenge: for what purpose did they need this — for we hold a gentile is not believed in casual speech even regarding a rabbinic prohibition, except regarding a woman's testimony, as above end of siman 137, where I already proved that this applies only where the prohibition was established; but here the prohibition was not established and he would be believed, if not that it is known to all gentiles that to enhance his merchandise he says so.
And in places where flax, etc. — meaning only after the fact may one rely to permit on this reason alone, that flax is more expensive than hemp, even though it is easier to sew with flax than hemp and his profit is his loss; but ab initio it is forbidden, etc., as the Rav concludes; and likewise in Rashba's responsum and Bach; and see above siman 118 seif 7 and 10.
And we are not concerned for substitution, etc. — for since he is a craftsman he does not ruin his craft, since he can verify the matter; so is stated in Rav HaMaggid, Ran, Rabbenu Yerucham and other authorities — the craftsman's reason; and the Rav abbreviated what is understood.
When lighting it quickly extinguishes, etc. — and in Sefer Ma'adan Ma'aseh end of page 292 he wrote: I heard from Jewish tailors that they examine hemp in this manner — they pull the thread until hairs are drawn out; and if those hairs are very long it is known to be hemp — end quote.
