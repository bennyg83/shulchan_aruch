In the Tur he wrote "or let him tear out the thread," etc., as I copied nearby, and it is from the Gemara in Niddah (daf 61): this one who casts a linen thread into a woolen cloak and tore it out, and it is not known whether it was torn out or not torn out — it is well. What is the reason? From the Torah "shaatnez" is written, until it be shua, tavui, and nuz, and it is the Rabbis who decreed concerning it; and since it is not known whether he tore it out, it is permitted. And the Beit Yosef asked that from this language it implies that it is not permitted l'chatchila to tear it out, and the Tur wrote "or let him tear it out," which implies that even l'chatchila it is permitted to tear it out, end of his words. And I will add to wonder: what is the difference from that of siman 110 regarding a mixture of issur that is not batel because of its importance, that it is permitted if one of them fell into the sea, for we say the issur fell, and specifically if it fell of itself, but if a person caused it to fall, even b'shogeg, it is forbidden; and here the threads of kilayim are also important, as is written in siman 299 in the Beit Yosef in the name of the Sefer Mitzvot Gadol, and furthermore I have proven that all hold that it is not batel, see there; and they permitted here even if he removed one thread b'mezid. And there too it is dealing with a d'rabbanan issur, for from the Torah one in two is batel even in an important item — and from where did the Tur have it to distinguish between them? And because of this wonder it appears to settle the view of the Tur, that he proved that even l'chatchila it is permitted: for if there is an issur in one who does it b'mezid, why did they permit in this if a person tore it out — why did we not decree because of mezd even in this as well? And further it is difficult, the extra language in the Gemara that said "and he tore it out, and it is not known if he tore it out" — it should have said "and it is not known if he tore it out or not"; why does it need to say "and he tore it out"? And one cannot say that it is dealing with a case where it is known that the issur was torn, except that it is not known whether all of it was torn or not — that is not so, for what difference is there between a safek whether he did not tear it out at all, or that not all of it was torn, for in this a part is like the whole. Rather, certainly this is its meaning: after the mixture became known, he went and removed one thread from them intentionally in order to hang upon it, and it is not known whether he tore out the issur or not, and even so it is permitted, for here it is dealing with kilayim d'rabbanan, for the Tur wrote "a garment in which he wove one thread," etc., and so it is in the Talmud in this matter. And since here there is a great loss, for even to sell it to a non-Jew is forbidden, therefore the Rabbis were lenient to rely on this, that the issur was torn out. Which is not so in that of siman 110, where it is permitted to sell it to a non-Jew, so it is a small loss; therefore they decreed not to rely on this if he does it b'mezid. And so we find in many places that they were concerned specifically for a great loss. So it appears to me is the view of the Tur. And for this reason the Rambam did not bring this matter of "he tore it out," for he holds that here there is a d'oraisa issur, as is written in siman 299 seif 1.
In what case are these words said — regarding kilayim d'rabbanan, etc. In the Rambam it is written, and this is his language: "And we have already explained that all issur of sefeikot is from the words of the Scribes, and therefore they were lenient in a safek." And the Raavad wrote upon him: it is true that all sefeikot are not of the Torah; however, we hold that every safek of a Torah issur is l'chumra, and d'rabbanan l'kula. And the author of Kesef Mishneh wrote: that which the Raavad asked, that a safek d'oraisa is l'chumra — one can say that the Sages, who said so, they themselves said that this safek should be l'kula because it is a matter that is not common. And his words are astonishing: why is it a matter that is not common, a mixture of flax among wool? And furthermore, according to his understanding of the Raavad's words, why did he ask on the Rambam in this and not on the Talmud, for this din is in the Gemara in a beraita? And it appears that the Rambam found it difficult regarding what the Gemara said, "and since it is not known, say it slipped off, we permit," which implies that this is not a certainty that we permit, but a safek; and similarly the language of the poskim, "perhaps it slipped off" — and why should we be lenient in it in a safek d'oraisa? Therefore he answered that every safek of issur is from their words, and there is no safek d'oraisa l'chumra here, since he already checked after this, and checking is effective to nullify a d'oraisa issur, except that one must be concerned lest he not have checked well, and this is a safek d'rabbanan. And the Raavad was not comfortable with this, and therefore he asked that nevertheless we hold that a safek of a d'oraisa issur is l'chumra. And the view of the Raavad is that this issur is a certainty as well, as we find in issur tumah regarding a safek of derusah, "say he made it whole," and the Ran wrote that all these "say" that we say here we consider them a certainty, etc. And from all this it is proven in any case that here it is dealing with kilayim d'oraisa that was lost in the garment. And so it is proven in the Gemara in Niddah (daf 61), that it compares this checking to that Mishnah, "he checks until he reaches rock," and there it is dealing with a kezayit of a corpse that is in a pile, as Rashi explained from the Mishnah there, and even so the checking is effective; so too here, checking of dyeing the garment regarding kilayim. Hence even in a d'oraisa matter the checking is effective. But it is difficult on the Tur, who wrote after this din, "in what case are these words said — regarding kilayim d'rabbanan, but regarding kilayim d'oraisa we do not hang upon a safek to be lenient," end of his words — and it is difficult from the Gemara that I brought. Therefore it appears clearly that what he wrote, "in what case are these words said — regarding kilayim d'rabbanan," etc., refers only to the second clause that he wrote, "or let him tear out one thread from it, and it is permitted, for we hang to say that he removed the thread of kilayim," etc., but not to the first clause, namely the dyeing. And so it is clear from the language of the Tur: "but regarding kilayim d'oraisa we do not hang upon a safek to be lenient" — this language "we do not hang" belongs to the second clause, in which he wrote "we hang," etc.; but in the first clause the language "we hang" does not belong, for the language "we hang" applies to an act that was done in a matter of safek that it was done in heter, and that is only in the latter clause. And with this is also answered why the Tur needed to lengthen and say "we do not hang upon a safek to be lenient" — he should have said "but regarding kilayim d'oraisa, no." Therefore it appears clearly as I have written, and there is no dispute in this between the Tur and the Rambam and the Raavad, for all of them admit that dyeing is effective even regarding kilayim d'oraisa. And the Beit Yosef wrote that this "in what case" refers also to dyeing, and according to him the Tur would dispute the Rambam and the Raavad, and after him the Rama followed here, who wrote "in what case," etc. And it appears to my humble opinion that they did not fulfill the obligation of the inquiry as fully as needed, with apology from their honor, for in the Gemara it is proven explicitly that even in d'oraisa checking of dyeing is effective, like checking of tumat met d'oraisa, as I have brought.
