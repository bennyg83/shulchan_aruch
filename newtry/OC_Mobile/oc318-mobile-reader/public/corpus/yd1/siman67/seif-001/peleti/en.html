And was absorbed in another place; and Tur concludes that one receives lashes on it; and Pri Chadash wondered: behold regarding one who breaks the neck-bone of a domestic animal we say it causes blood to be absorbed in the limbs — we must ask whether we permit or not; if so this [case of] absorbed blood should not have been resolved, and a doubt is for stringency; but what is the nature of lashes for a doubt? And the essence of his challenge is to Tur's views who explain like Rif and Rosh that the question is to eat rare — according to Rashi's view the question is whether salting helps; for us the question arises only according to their view whether one may eat rare. He wondered: if blood that was absorbed from place to place — is it not obvious it is forbidden to eat rare? And it appears Tur does not explain like Ran that the doubt is whether to forbid blood absorbed from place to place or not, but only the doubt of blood in the slaughter-house through breaking the neck-bone being absorbed in all the animal's limbs; and if so it is clear the blood is little against the animal, and in every limb whose blood is limb-blood — if slaughter-blood was absorbed and divides to the limbs it is distressing and nullified one in two, and therefore permitted; or nevertheless forbidden, for it is like nullifying a prohibition; and also it is possible it does not divide to the limbs equally — perhaps for this one little and for that one much until there is no majority — and it requires further study:
