(Siman 67 in the Mechaber) Or that it separated from its place and was aroused, etc., and was absorbed in another place. Thus Rosh chapter 1 of Chullin: his colleagues carried [the matter] to say it is R' Yehuda's [view], etc.; and you might ask: but one cannot salt it on Shabbos; and one may answer that he eats it rare without salting, and even though there is a negative commandment for blood of the limbs, etc. — this is when it separated from the meat; but as long as it did not separate from the meat there is no prohibition, etc.; and therefore Rabbeinu Tam would say that meat for roasting need not be salted, etc., for according to one view if it roasted well — but even if it did not roast well and not all the blood was expelled outside — permitted, for what went outside is absorbed worse than what remains is limb-blood that did not separate, etc. And one may ask: behold Rosh wrote below chapter 25: meat salted in a vessel that is not perforated is forbidden only what is inside the brine — and not as Nimukei HaRav Peretz wrote: one who salts in a vessel that is not perforated is forbidden even what is outside the brine even without fat, etc.; and who is the prophet who told him it separated from place to place — see there in Rosh. If so it appears from his words that if we knew certainly it separated from place to place — forbidden; if so it is proven he holds limb-blood that separated from place to place is forbidden. And it appears to me Rosh holds that even after the measure of roasting and salting it should be considered like exudate that departs from it as blood; but since the Sages' enactment was to roast or salt meat, and since he did as the Sages enacted, again the blood that departs from it outside is permitted, for they said and they said that blood that was salted and blood that was cooked is forbidden only d'rabbanan; if so, since he did the enactment of half-roasting, even though blood remained inside and also separated from its place — permitted; not so for meat salted in a vessel that is not perforated, for he did it not as the Sages enacted — certainly forbidden because the blood separated from place to place. Therefore it is possible why Rosh did not raise Tosafos' question in chapter 1 s.v. venasvin, etc., and we say in chapter 25 regarding R' Shimon who salted piece by piece — what are two [reasons]? Not because this absorbs and that expels; one also expels from this side and absorbs from the other side; if so for one piece too there is blood that separated, etc.; and according to what I said in the name of the Maggid from there, for according to R' Shimon's words it is proven the Sages were concerned to salt two pieces together even though he said the Sages' enactment was thus and whatever one did as the Sages enacted there is no concern for blood that was absorbed — nevertheless it must be the Sages were concerned for blood absorbed through this salting; if so for one too there is concern it separated from place to place from this place to another place.
