One who breaks the neck-bone of a domestic animal. Specifically before the soul departs, as stated in the Gemara and poskim:
It is forbidden to eat from its flesh raw. Because the blood is aroused to depart at the time of slaughter, and when one breaks its neck-bone it cannot leap and [the blood] returns and is absorbed in its limbs. Tur:
And through salting it is permitted. For through salting it departs, and it is not comparable to meat salted in a vessel that is not perforated, for salting does not help for it afterward, as written in siman 69 s.k. 68 — see there:
And for roasting it is permitted, etc. Ran wrote: and for roasting too until it is roasted to its full need, for if it was not roasted to its full need it is like raw meat, end of his words; and see below siman 76 and what is written there:
And the custom is to be stringent, etc. This is according to the text of Rashi and Baal HaItur that salting does not help neither for a pot nor for roasting, as Ran wrote; and Rabbenu Yerucham wrote netiv 4 that Rav Acha also wrote thus and it is proper to be stringent; and the Rav holds that cutting and salting help in any case even for a pot, for it is no worse than blood coagulated due to a blow in seif 4; and so Rambam chapter 6 of Laws of Forbidden Foods law 9: one who breaks the neck-bone of a domestic animal before the soul departs — behold the blood is absorbed in the limbs and it is forbidden to eat from its flesh raw; rather what should he do? He cuts the piece and salts it very well, and afterward he cooks or roasts it, end of his words; and Bach wrote that thus we hold, and not like the Eit Tzvi who understood that they were stringent in general:
And the same applies if one cuts, etc. The Eit Tzvi omitted this and I do not know why; and it is possible he holds this is the same as that in seif 4 in the hagahah; but in truth it is not relevant here, for here it speaks of one who cuts an olive's bulk of meat before the soul departs, that he requires cutting and salting even for roasting; and the Rav derived thus from Ran's words in Perek HaShochet and he erred, for Ran wrote that although for roasting in general salting is not required, for one who cuts an olive's bulk of meat from the slaughter-house salting is required because through slaughter the blood is aroused and separates from place to place, end of his words; and it appears to the Rav that since he gave the reason as separating from place to place — if so it is comparable to breaking the neck-bone, whose prohibition is because it separates from place to place, that we hold cutting and salting are required even for roasting; but for cutting after the soul departs — cutting and salting for roasting are not required at all, rather its law is like other meat for roasting below siman 76; only for a pot cutting is required before salting, and this is that in seif 4 in the hagahah:
If one cuts, etc. And it appears from Ran's words in Perek HaShochet and Perek Gid HaNasheh that for one who cuts an olive's bulk of meat from the slaughter-house salting is required by law for roasting, and even b'dieved it is forbidden if he did not salt it; not so for breaking the neck-bone, for salting is not mentioned in the Gemara, and on the contrary most poskim do not require salting for roasting; and so it appears from Tosafos Perek HaShochet beginning (Chullin daf 33), that for one who cuts an olive's bulk of meat from the slaughter-house before the soul departs — forbidden by law for roasting, even b'dieved without salting:
