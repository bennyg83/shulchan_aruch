Even for roasting — Pri Chadash wrote not like Shach who gave a reason for cutting to be concerned for Rashi's view who holds that if salting helps we are in doubt, but for cutting it certainly helps; and Pri Chadash challenged him: for Rashi, according to the one who forbids — he has no remedy at all; and he wrote that we are concerned for Rambam's words who wrote in chapter 6 of Laws of Forbidden Foods: what should he do? He cuts the piece and salts it or roasts it — meaning he compares it to flesh that constricted. And he wrote well; but he wrote that for roasting cutting is not required as with flesh that constricted; and Rambam's wording "or he roasts it" at the end implies this "he cuts" also applies to roasting, and it is not comparable to flesh that constricted where the blood stands at the opening of the cut as is recognizable and heat from fire easily penetrates and dominates; but here where no mark is recognizable and the blood is hidden in the meat — one should be concerned heat of fire will not penetrate and cutting is required — and this is simple. However I did not understand: whether through salting or roasting the blood is absorbed in all limbs; and if so all limbs are included as absorbing blood and the whole animal is one piece; and if so when one cuts the animal into portions we have cut — consider yourself how many cuts are needed in a piece and how large the piece should be — is it not enough when he cuts and the place of blood to flow? And here where all limbs all received as one piece they are considered, and in cutting limbs we have cut; and it must be this is what I wrote in s.k. 1, for it is not clear to us that it was absorbed in all limbs, and it is possible there are limbs that did not absorb — therefore cutting limbs does not help, for if it is possible they did not absorb the blood will not flow — see there; and it requires further study: therefore if he cut one limb he need not cut more — and this is simple and clear:
