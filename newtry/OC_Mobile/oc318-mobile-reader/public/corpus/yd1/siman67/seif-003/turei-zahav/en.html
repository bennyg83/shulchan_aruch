Forbidden to eat from its flesh raw. Because the blood is aroused to depart at the time of slaughter, and when one breaks its neck-bone it cannot leap and extract it, and [the blood] returns and is absorbed in the limbs:
In order to hasten its death, etc. This is difficult against what Tur wrote siman 23: when the bird tarries to die after slaughter it is better to strike it on the head to kill it, etc.; and many resolve that there it speaks only to inform us not to delay, and he said it is better to strike than to slaughter again — but in truth even striking is forbidden because of causing blood to be absorbed in the limbs, and this is not correct at all. And it appears Rashi holds that specifically for breaking the neck-bone they were concerned thus, as written in Hagahot Ashiri in the name of Rabbenu Yehuda: specifically a breaker — because due to the pain of breaking it has no strength to groan and extract blood — but a cutter did not forbid, end of his words. And for this reason one should not forbid here other hastenings of death such as striking on the head, and Rama does not hold as he wrote here, and this is from Shibolei HaLeket who forbids thrusting a knife in its heart; if so Rama should not have ruled in siman 23 that one may strike on the head and here forbid all hastenings of death. And it appears he holds that above it deals when he delayed a long time and it is still alive and the blood that should depart in a leap has already departed, and what remains a little will be absorbed in the limbs; and here it deals when he wants to hasten death immediately after slaughter while it is still leaping, and thereby blood is absorbed in the limbs. In Beit Yosef in the section beginning "blood coagulated," etc., he wrote in the name of Rashba: it appears to me they permitted dripping only when they removed them from inside the meat, etc.; it appears to me this is a scribal error and it should read that they come out from inside the meat; and so I found in Tosefta daf 69, and these are its words: it appears to me because they are stretched out and their faces stand toward the fire, etc. — meaning such is their nature. In the hagahah "and the custom is to be stringent," etc. — I was astonished: here is one [difficulty] from the language "and the custom," which implies it refers to breaking the neck-bone, which is something not common at all — how can he write "and the custom" on this? And further, who mentioned cutting in the law of breaking the neck-bone? And if he came to innovate this matter from custom he should have written "and the custom is to be stringent" with a vav even for roasting, since he came to teach two new stringencies from custom. And further, what relevance does the law of cutting from the slaughter-house have to the law of breaking the neck-bone, and why did he write on this "and the same applies," etc.? And further, below immediately it is written in the hagahah "and some say that for this reason," etc., which implies he disagrees with what he wrote before, but in truth this was not mentioned there; and at minimum he should not have written "and some say" but "some say" without vav, as is his way — aside from what is difficult from what we will mention below. And it appears to clarify: this "and the custom is to be stringent" until "soul" belongs in its place in seif 4 after the law of blood coagulated due to a blow, where Shulchan Aruch ruled that for roasting cutting and salting are not required; on this he wrote "and the custom is to be stringent" with cutting and salting even for roasting — and this is truly the daily custom among us for blood coagulated due to a blow; and on this he wrote well "and the same applies if one cuts meat," etc., for this too has the reason of blood coagulated due to a blow, as Ran stated explicitly in Perek HaShochet: the reason is since he cuts from the slaughter-house and before the soul departs the blood is uprooted from its place and exudes through there. And on this he wrote well afterward "and some say that for this reason," etc. — he means because of this stringency of the slaughter-house one should be stringent even for one who cuts in the slaughter-house after the soul departs. And now it is also settled what was difficult: since Rama wrote here that in every place one should be stringent in the slaughter-house, why did he write above specifically for cutting before the soul departs?
