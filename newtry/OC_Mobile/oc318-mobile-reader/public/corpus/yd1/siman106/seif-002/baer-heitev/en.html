According to us, since we rule that chatichah na'asis neveilah applies to all prohibitions, and likewise regarding a prohibition attached to a piece, sixty is required against the entire piece. The same applies here: sixty is required against the entire piece to which the forbidden fat is attached, and nevertheless that piece itself remains forbidden. Shach.
Until it cools. Meaning, if he removes the piece while it is hot, the piece will become forbidden because of the fat within it, since the piece itself does not contain sixty against the fat. But when he leaves it in the pot, the absorbed taste spreads evenly throughout the entire contents. One should not object that the piece must necessarily have been salted beforehand, for otherwise it would be forbidden because of blood and the cooked food would require sixty against the entire piece; if so, it should already have become forbidden when salted together with the fat and could never again become permitted. The answer is that the case concerns fat forbidden only d'rabbanan, or the fat of a lean animal, which through salting forbids only the thickness of a peel. Alternatively, the piece originally contained sixty against the fat, and afterward, before cooking, part of the piece was cut away. End of the Shach's words.
