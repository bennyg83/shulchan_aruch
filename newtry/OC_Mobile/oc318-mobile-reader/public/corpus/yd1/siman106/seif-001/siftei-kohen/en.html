It does not forbid except according to calculation, etc. — this is according to the Mechaber's view, who holds we do not say chadichah na'aseh nevelah in other issurim, unlike for us, as the Rav explains; and see above siman 99 seifim 3 and 5:
But the piece itself is forbidden, etc. So Rashba in Torat HaBayit daf 98 — even according to Rabeinu Efrayim and his supporters who do not say chadichah na'aseh nevelah in other issurim, "if it is possible to squeeze it out, forbidden," and the piece itself is forever forbidden; and so many poskim, and so agreed the Acharonim; and there are poskim who hold likewise "if it is possible to squeeze it out" is also permitted — we do not hold so; however for us who hold chadichah na'aseh nevelah in all issurim, plainly for all the piece is forever forbidden:
And according to what we practice saying in all issurim chadichah na'aseh nevelah, etc. Explanation: also when moist issur was mixed in moist permitted we say chadichah na'aseh nevelah in other issurim where there is no great loss, as in hagahah siman 92 seif 4 — there is no distinction, etc.:
If it is not a piece worthy of honor. At first glance it implies this refers to other issurim that he is dealing with — and it is difficult, for if so even if it were a piece worthy of honor it would be nullified, since its prohibition is not on account of itself, as explained at the beginning of siman 101; and one must say the Rav's continuation is thus: and according to what we practice saying in all issurim chadichah na'aseh nevelah — thus all issurim have the same law as basar bechalav, and there is never distinction between them regarding moist and dry — only regarding: if what became forbidden was already a dry thing and there is sixty against it — then whether in basar bechalav or other issurim, the piece first forbidden remains in its prohibition and must be removed if recognized; and if not recognized, it is nullified — even in basar bechalav if it is not worthy of honor; for if worthy of honor in basar bechalav it is not nullified, for the issur from its body is considered significant since each alone is permitted, as explained at the beginning of siman 101; and if it is a moist thing — whether in basar bechalav or other issurim — everything is permitted if there is sixty against what was first forbidden; however, that we require sixty against all that was first forbidden in a moist thing — this is where there is no great loss; but where there is great loss we do not say in such cases chadichah na'aseh nevelah in other issurim, only in basar bechalav, as the Rav wrote in siman 92 seif 4:
