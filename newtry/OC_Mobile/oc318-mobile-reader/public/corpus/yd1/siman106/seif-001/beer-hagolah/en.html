In the responsums to the Ramban, it is noted that this question was asked of the Ramban and he answered yes:
Beit Yosef gives the reason and writes that he later found the same ruling in a responsum. Even though the original cause of prohibition is no longer present, since it became prohibited it remains prohibited forever until all of the absorbed prohibition is completely expelled. This is unlike a substance that blends and dissolves, where because everything mixes evenly it is regarded as though all of it has been expelled. Here the Mechaber retracted from what he wrote above in siman 92. In my opinion, however, the correct explanation is what I wrote there: by Torah law the pieces become permitted again once the prohibition no longer imparts flavor, whereas here they remain prohibited rabbinically. A practical difference follows, as explained above in several places and in siman 110, seif 1, in the gloss.
