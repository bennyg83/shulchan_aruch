The Tur in the name of the Rashba.
Responsa attributed to the Ramban, siman 151, where the Rashba was asked this question and answered accordingly.
The Beit Yosef explains the reason and writes that he later found it stated there in a responsum: Although the forbidden taste is no longer present, once the item became forbidden it remains forbidden permanently until it emits all of what it absorbed. It is unlike a substance that blends and dissolves, where everything mixes uniformly and is considered entirely emitted. Here the Mechaber retracts what he wrote above in siman 92. In my humble opinion, the correct explanation is that above they become permitted by Torah law because the forbidden substance no longer imparts taste, whereas here the prohibition remains rabbinic. This distinction has practical consequences, as explained above in several places, including siman 110, seif 1, in the gloss. See siman 92, siman 110, and siman 151.
