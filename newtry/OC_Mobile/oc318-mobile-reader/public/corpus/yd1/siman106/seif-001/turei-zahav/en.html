The Tur challenged this — "I do not understand," etc. Beit Yosef challenged this: behold, even if it were clear to us that everything does not spread and mix throughout, we require sixty against all of it, as we say regarding lung meat, etc.; and I say the whole proof of our rabbi is from the sugya of lung meat — that very challenge our rabbi raises, the Talmud raises there: "rather, if so, if it fell into another pot it should not forbid"; and Tosafot wrote: since we estimate in all of it, if so it leaves, etc.; and if you say: perhaps what we estimate is because we are uncertain whether it leaves, etc., and therefore if it fell into another pot it forbids lest perhaps not all left — and they answered in the conclusion that thus it challenges: since you say lung meat counts from the number, evidently we do not consider it a piece of issur and it is forbidden only on account of milk enclosed in the crevices inside it; and if so, if it fell into another pot it should not forbid, for all that will eventually leave from the milk left in the first pot — end of their words; and the Gemara answers: since R' Yitzchak said lung meat itself is forbidden, the Sages equated it to a piece of nevelah; and Tosafot wrote there earlier from it: and if you say — what is the reason lung meat itself is forbidden? Behold all milk enclosed inside it in crevices left outside and left equally to all pieces, as it says shortly — "rather, if so" implies it is obvious to him that all leaves, as I will explain shortly; and one may say: because from the beginning of its cooking, before the milk fully left, the milk in the lung was forbidden on account of taste of meat in it — therefore even after cooking finished and all already left, it is no longer permitted, for "if it is possible to squeeze it out, forbidden" — end of their words. Behold our rabbi's words are correct accordingly — here too is the questioner's challenge, for everything joins, even the piece itself, to sixty — conclusion: there is no name of nevelah upon it, as he challenges regarding lung meat itself; except here the answer does not apply that the Gemara answers: since R' Yitzchak said lung meat itself is forbidden, etc. — its explanation according to Tosafot is on account of "if it is possible to squeeze it out, forbidden," as we mentioned; and behold our rabbi already wrote in siman 92 we do not say "if it is possible to squeeze it out, forbidden" except in basar bechalav — thus also in lung meat one must say so, for the Gemara's issur there is on account of basar bechalav, and even though d'rabbanan, since slaughtered milk — nevertheless all d'rabbanan enactments like Torah enactments — thus here in this siman dealing with other issurim and "if it is possible to squeeze it out, permitted," the matter remains as the questioner's challenge — even the piece is permitted. And Rashba's view in explaining the Gemara of lung meat — "if it fell into another pot it forbids" — is for another reason, as he wrote in Torat HaBayit daf 107: we are concerned it did not emit all its milk in the first pot, as Rashba wrote in siman 90 seif 1; thus there is no distinction between basar bechalav and other issurim — the piece itself forbids — everything is satisfactory, with Heaven's help. Also what Tur wrote afterward "nevertheless there is to distinguish," etc. — Beit Yosef wrote these are simple matters not given to writing. And in my humble opinion, our rabbi's words are before our eyes for benefit — to resolve one challenge: because Tosafot wrote that I brought shortly — the essence of the challenge "rather, if so, if it fell into the pot," etc. — is because all that will eventually leave from the milk left in the first pot; and behold the milk of lung meat is in existence in the lung, and thus remains in it even after cooking finished — otherwise how did they say in the Mishnah "they did not tear after its cooking it is permitted" — according to the first answer in Yoreh Deah, because the Sages did not decree on this since it was not separated, as Rashi explained there — thus to say the issur in lung meat milk goes from it at the end of cooking, i.e., taste of meat that entered from the pot; for if on account of lung meat meat there is no issur, as he wrote there — thus we say: even though the milk there in lung was forbidden, nevertheless all issur in it goes entirely in the first pot cooking; and so we may say here that all that is fit to leave from the milk to that piece attached to it already left in the pot's cooking; and when one removes it — although it is like another pot, nevertheless what was fit to leave already left; and if the milk fell elsewhere it returns and forbids, for then it makes new emission — unlike here in this piece that already received from this milk and is satiated from it — what will it do afterward? Certainly certainly it is a challenge — except our rabbi dismissed it that one must distinguish: since issur is in existence, it returns to new emission in another pot even to that piece attached to it; and in Darkei Moshe he strained to reconcile that here is different with a drop that emitted on the piece that can permit all of it through stirring and covering — and with what we mentioned everything is settled; and it is simple that also for Rashba the piece is permitted here, since at the time the milk absorbs to that piece it has support from other pieces like that above:
