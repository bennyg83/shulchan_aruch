And one may challenge: that which we say in Chullin 108b — "and if it is possible to squeeze it out" — is a condition; and he brings the dispute of R' Yehuda and the Sages regarding a drop of milk that fell on a piece that forbids the whole pot — R' Yehuda's words, and the Sages say until it imparts taste in the gravy, foam, and pieces, etc.; and the Gemara dismisses: perhaps they disagree whether in its kind it is not nullified. And it is difficult — he should have challenged briefly: perhaps all hold "if it is possible to squeeze it out, forbidden," and they only disagree whether chadichah na'aseh nevelah — for according to Rashba it does not depend, and even where we do not say "becomes nevelah," nevertheless "if it is possible to squeeze it out, forbidden" — thus from the Torah one should say they only disagree in "becomes nevelah," and R' Yehuda holds "becomes nevelah" and therefore forbids the whole pot, for in its kind it is not nullified, and the Sages hold there is no "becomes nevelah," and thus sixty against the absorbed suffices; and that which they say "until it imparts taste on the pot" — refers to the pot, but the first piece they agree is tereifah, for "if it is possible to squeeze it out, forbidden"; and so one must say in the Gemara's challenge: perhaps they disagree in its kind. And one need not challenge strainedly "perhaps they disagree in its kind" — what does this dispute have to do here? It should have disputed plainly whether in its kind it is nullified or not — unlike this challenge. And one cannot say: how does it teach the words of the Sages in stirring and covering — for according to the above the Sages agree that piece is forbidden, only the rest are permitted, whereas in stirring and covering even that piece is permitted, for the whole pot joins and is nullified in sixty — this challenge is also difficult for the Shulchan Aruch's view, whether they disagree in nullification. Also difficult: how does it appear that for the Sages who permit on account of mivtza bilti — that piece is forbidden, for all hold "if it is possible to squeeze it out, forbidden," and in stirring and covering everything is permitted — thus the questioner should have challenged the above challenge. And what appears in its resolution is: behold R' Efrayim wrote in Beit Yosef that the law should be chadichah na'aseh nevelah, because this alone is permitted and that alone is permitted — only joining in its kind causes prohibition; thus "if it is possible to squeeze it out" does not apply, for even by itself each is permitted — and this is very reasonable; thus one must say the reason of the one who says even in basar bechalav there is no chadichah na'aseh nevelah: because "if it is possible to squeeze it out, permitted" — how can the name nevelah apply to a piece that stands every moment to become permitted? And accordingly, Shulchan Aruch deals with other issurim, and in this certainly it does not depend on one another, as R' Efrayim well explained — there is no chadichah na'aseh nevelah, and nevertheless "if it is possible to squeeze it out, forbidden"; but in basar bechalav, if one cannot say there is no chadichah na'aseh nevelah — for R' Efrayim's words are reasonable — unless we say "if it is possible to squeeze it out, permitted" and this depends on one another — there is no "if it is possible to squeeze it out, forbidden" and no chadichah na'aseh nevelah regarding basar bechalav, as above:
To say in all issurim chadichah na'aseh nevelah, etc. Shach wrote: this is where there is no great loss — for where there is loss we do not say moist in moist chadichah na'aseh nevelah. And at first glance it is difficult: since Rama did not emend anything on Shulchan Aruch's words that in a thing that was mixed "if it is possible to squeeze it out, forbidden" does not apply, and he did not emend to distinguish between place of loss and not loss — we never say moist in moist "if it is possible to squeeze it out, forbidden" — how then can there be "becomes nevelah"? For the Gemara challenges: what does he hold? If "if it is possible to squeeze it out, permitted," why "becomes nevelah"? Rather he holds "if it is possible to squeeze it out, forbidden" — thus one cannot say "if it is possible to squeeze it out, permitted" and chadichah na'aseh nevelah; and how can Rama think moist and moist "becomes nevelah" when we hold "if it is possible to squeeze it out, permitted"? And one may resolve this challenge: certainly moist in moist — "if it is possible to squeeze it out" does not apply — because the whole reason of "if it is possible to squeeze it out, forbidden" is as Shach wrote, that absorbed issur does not fully leave, and this does not apply to moist in moist, for it mixes well; this applies only to cold wine in cold wine, where absorption is through mixture, and when permitted wine is added it mixes more and is nullified. Unlike forbidden wine libation that was hot mixed with permitted wine hot — it absorbs taste of forbidden wine hot and absorbs like from a dry piece; thus when there is law of meat absorbing from libation, even if he increased permitted wine on it, nevertheless the absorbed does not leave — "if it is possible to squeeze it out, forbidden." But since it is moist in moist, we do not say regarding it chadichah na'aseh nevelah, because it is not recognizable and stands alone, as Mordechai wrote in chapter Kol HaSha'ah — see there. However, Rama's words here are difficult to reconcile, for he says for us who hold chadichah na'aseh nevelah there is no distinction between moist and dry. And it is difficult: in which moist and dry does he deal? If in cold where both apply "if it is possible to squeeze it out, permitted" — we also do not say chadichah na'aseh nevelah, as written; and if in hot where chadichah na'aseh nevelah — we also have "if it is possible to squeeze it out, forbidden" — what difference regarding "if it is possible to squeeze it out" whether recognizable and standing alone or not? And the matters depend on one another, as written: if "if it is possible to squeeze it out, permitted," the piece also does not "become nevelah" — requires study at first glance. And the Acharonim did not explain this. And it appears to say: Rama holds like Ran who raised chadichah na'aseh nevelah in other issurim d'rabbanan on account of taste of basar bechalav — see there; and thus even though we do not say "if it is possible to squeeze it out, forbidden," nevertheless "becomes nevelah" on account of taste of basar bechalav, and there "if it is possible to squeeze it out, forbidden" and "becomes nevelah." And if challenged: even "if it is possible to squeeze it out" should forbid on account of taste of basar bechalav — one may say: since chadichah na'aseh nevelah, otherwise there is no difference in "if it is possible to squeeze it out," and one need not decree that if you do not find a difference, as Rama wrote — see there: "if it is possible to squeeze it out, permitted" and nevertheless "becomes nevelah" — this is in other issurim; but in basar bechalav, where there is no decree only Torah law, plainly this depends on one another, and the Gemara asks about basar bechalav itself — and properly asks: if "if it is possible to squeeze it out, permitted," why chadichah na'aseh nevelah? — investigate:
