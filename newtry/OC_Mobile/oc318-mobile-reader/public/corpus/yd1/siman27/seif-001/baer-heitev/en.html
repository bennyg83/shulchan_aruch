The animal. And Taz wrote: but after it died, permitted to eat from it even what was cut before it died; and permitted to feed it to a non-Jew — even though for a non-Jew shechitah does not permit except death, and this flesh was cut before it died — one might have thought death afterward would not avail him, since it was cut before its permissibility; it teaches there is nothing permitted for Israel and forbidden for a non-Jew. And Shach wrote: but if he cut from a tamei animal before its soul departed — since shechitah does not apply to it — forbidden to give from it to sons of Noah. And Maharshal wrote: in an animal of a non-Jew, even if Israel slaughtered it — since he was not commanded regarding shechitah — in his animal shechitah did not permit it for him; its law is as if a non-Jew slaughtered it; and if he cut from it while convulsing — forbidden to give to sons of Noah. And Taz wrote: in Tur it is written here: the spinal column broke and majority of flesh with it — neveilah; and for us too there is practical difference regarding doubt of egg of treifah, as written siman 86: in egg of treifah we follow doubt stringently — see there; and he wrote on this in Taz: it appears to me to be lenient even in broken spinal column and majority of flesh with it when the cord is intact — kosher; and what Tur wrote: broken spinal column and majority of flesh — neveilah — that is specifically when the cord was severed — see there at length. And so Peri Chadash: everything depends on the cord. And in Beit Yosef, siman 162, he tends to be stringent even without majority of flesh. And in She'eilot Ya'avetz, she'eilah 3, he also refutes Taz's proof from Tosafot, Chullin daf 32, s.v. velichashov — see there; also proven from mishnah of VeElu Kesherot, etc., that lists broken spinal column and the cord was not severed, and does not teach broken spinal column and majority of flesh — rather certainly in such a case treifah even if the cord was not severed — see there. And Tur wrote: also for intestines it is permitted to invite a non-Jew, and we do not say: since he slaughtered the simanim, it is as if he removed the intestines and placed them in a basket like water — it teaches: no.
