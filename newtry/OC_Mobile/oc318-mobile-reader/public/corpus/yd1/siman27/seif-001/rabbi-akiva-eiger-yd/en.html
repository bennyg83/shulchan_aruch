(Siman 27, Shach s.k. 2) But where it was not slaughtered properly. I was asked regarding a fattened goose whose esophagus was perforated into its cavity: must we throw away the intestines and all the losses and all that depend on esophagus and trachea, or is it permitted to sell them to a non-Jew? Answer: behold in the sugya itself in Chullin (daf 33): R' Levi said: he slaughtered the trachea, etc.; R' Eliezer bar Yose said: learn from R' Levi — we invite Israel for intestines and do not invite a non-Jew, etc.; it teaches not like R' Avahu — clear: immediately when he slaughtered trachea and esophagus, they and all that depend on them are like detached while convulsing. And that which permitted for a son of Noah — that is because of "is there anything," since for Israel the matter depends on shechitah and convulsing is permitted — so too for a son of Noah. Unlike where he did not slaughter but only strangled — the law of convulsing is forbidden because of eiver min hachai, as stated explicitly in Chullin (daf 121b): Israel who slaughtered a tamei animal for a non-Jew, etc. — limb separating from it as separating from life — behold where there is no shechitah, limb separating from convulsing as separating from life; and so explicitly Shach in the name of Rashba: where it was not slaughtered properly — forbidden to cut a limb while convulsing and give to a non-Jew; if so, automatically intestines are forbidden, as detached and placed in a basket like water; and so explicitly Torat Chayim in Be'er (chapter 1 of Chullin, daf 20), and he wrote to reconcile Tur's words (siman 26) that wrote: whatever was invalidated in shechitah — behold this is neveilah; and whatever was slaughtered properly and another matter caused it to be invalidated — R' Zeira — treifah; what practical difference for us, since both are forbidden? And as Beit Yosef wondered: one may say practical difference regarding eiver min hachai — initially Tur wrote there: one cuts from the animal after proper slaughter while convulsing, waits until it dies, permitted in eating and to feed a non-Jew, and permitted to feed intestines to a non-Jew — that is because of "is there anything." On this Tur wrote: whatever was invalidated in shechitah is neveilah — practical difference: since shechitah was invalid and does not avail to purify from neveilah — in this forbidden to feed intestines to a non-Jew, for they did not leave eiver min hachai prohibition; but whatever was slaughtered properly and another matter caused invalidation — R' Zeira — treifah; here, since the name shechitah applied and avails to purify from neveilah — there is no eiver min hachai prohibition — "is there anything," etc. — end; behold his lips clearly stated: in invalidated shechitah forbidden to give intestines to a non-Jew because of eiver min hachai prohibition; and where it became treifah through another matter — no eiver min hachai concern. And although Peri Chadash challenged from that we hold: they are trachea and afterward lung was perforated — forbidden; if so we do not hold like one who places in a basket. Turei Zahav nicely rejected him: for in the reason of the matter, since we do not rule like R' Levi, Ramban wrote in Milchamot two reasons: first, he explains the sugya as Tosafot and R' Zeira — they are trachea and afterward lung perforated — forbidden; and therefore that which we rule like R' Zeira against R' Levi is stringently; but regarding giving intestines to a non-Jew we hold stringently like Rava, as like placed in a basket — and likewise for the second reason: since the Talmud challenges R' Levi against R' Levi — automatically R' Levi retracted, and one of them was stated last, and we do not know which — see there; automatically there is doubt, and everywhere we go stringently — automatically forbidden to give intestines to a non-Jew. Also for what Ramban concluded, who challenged: behold R' Yochanan, who answers here "before it returned," etc. — and therefore he holds even not at the place of the cut it is neveilah, as placed in a basket; and he answered: in the trachea itself life certainly ceased in the majority and slaughter in another place does not help; but the lung, although it depends on the trachea, its life also depends on the esophagus — they depend on each other — see there. From this language it implies: if both were esophagus and trachea — according to all like placed in a basket; if so, where it became neveilah through shechitah — forbidden to sell intestines to a non-Jew; and so is the intent of latter poskim. Now, according to agreement of latter scholars Turei Zahav and Turei Zahav and latter poskim: in invalidated shechitah forbidden to give intestines to a non-Jew — automatically where perforated esophagus is found, which we hold in Shulchan Aruch (siman 33), and Rambam ruled it is neveilah and imparts by carrying and shechitah does not quickly avail from neveilah — forbidden to give intestines to a non-Jew. It is true I stood many days on this confusion and did not understand whence for Rambam perforated esophagus is invalid in shechitah — in the sugya it implies when not at the place of the cut it does not help — because like placed in a basket; and as I wrote in Ramban's name that in the simanim themselves we say according to all like placed in a basket; and this applies to crop severance in its majority that life departs; whence do we have it is called invalid in shechitah? And how I struggled on this until approximately printed Sefer Pri Megadim for Yoreh Deah and Sefer Teshuvat Pnei Aryeh — I saw they wondered on this and found no rest; nevertheless who would dare approach the sacred against Rambam and Shulchan Aruch rulings that perforated esophagus is neveilah — automatically we return to the law: intestines forbidden to give to a non-Jew; and I leaned to be lenient that in a bird it is like end of siman doubt — halakhah like R' Zeira that not like placed in a basket; and even if he slaughtered two simanim — not like Ramban; and doubt like Rambam's view (chapter 9, law 11 of Hilchot Melachim), who wrote: but a bird — it appears to me a son of Noah is not executed for eiver min hachai from it; even though for eiver min hachai there is presumption of prohibition — nevertheless Kenesset HaGedolah already wrote: in doubt of law, do not establish on presumption of prohibition — presumption does not decide the law; and I was lengthy on this elsewhere, with God's help. However, for truth of the matter, this too is not so — if we explain Rambam's words literally, they are astounding from an arranged sugya (Chullin daf 102): its shechitah does not purify it — for a son of Noah; behold a son of Noah is commanded on a bird; and it is almost proof with no answer; and necessarily either as Ra'avad emends — it should say "but sheretz" — or as Kesef Mishneh wrote: a son of Noah is forbidden but not punished; and automatically there is no doubt, since for Rambam too forbidden at least for a son of Noah. And I saw latter poskim (siman 63) wrote plainly according to Rambam: bird eiver min hachai is not forbidden to a son of Noah, for whatever is forbidden to a son of Noah — he is executed for it, for their warning is their death; and I do not know what he answers to the arranged sugya mentioned; and latter poskim raised one proof for Rambam's view — first we will explain some introduction, and from this it will be clarified there is no proof. And behold in the ancient dispute in that which we say: an animal in its lifetime stands in presumption of prohibition — Rashi explained: presumption of eiver min hachai prohibition; and Tosafot, chapter 3 of Beitzah (daf 25), challenged: behold it died before you, and certainly eiver min hachai prohibition departed — why establish on its presumption? Therefore they explained: presumption of prohibition means presumption of prohibition that it is not slaughtered — for whatever was not slaughtered is forbidden from the verse the Merciful said: you shall slaughter and eat — slaughtered eat, and what is not — do not eat; and this matter is what Tosafot disputed in Shevuot (daf 24a), s.v. haochel neveilah: Tosafot hold it is a negative commandment derived from a positive commandment — slaughtered eat, what is not do not eat; and they wrote there Ritzb"a disputes, that "you shall slaughter" does not come to forbid — for otherwise it is forbidden in its lifetime because of eiver min hachai, and after death because of neveilah; and the verse came only to inform of the remedy — shechitah, not strangling — see there; for Ritzb"a necessarily it must be as Rashi explained: presumption of prohibition is eiver min hachai — from prohibition to prohibition. And I saw the gaon Peri Megadim at the end of his introduction to Hilchot Shechitah challenged Ritzb"a from the sugya in Chullin (daf 86a): why specifically majority — even minority too, for R' Meir is concerned for minority and relies on minority near presumption; and R' Meir, according to his view that eiver min hachai applies only in an animal — in chayah and bird there is no prohibition in life and no presumption of prohibition. And in my humble opinion it appears the reverse: for Ritzb"a nothing is difficult — only for the aforementioned Tosafot's view is the difficulty: Peri Megadim was in doubt for practical halakhah according to the aforementioned Tosafot's view whether in a bird there is prohibition that it is not slaughtered — one may say the negative commandment derived from a positive commandment of "you shall slaughter" applies only to cattle and flock from its juxtaposition — but not chayah and bird. And it appears to me to prove from the sugya in Chullin (daf 12a): R' Nachman said: one saw someone slaughter, etc. — did we not teach: if he found a chicken? Behold Ra'osh challenged the poskim, that we also rely on minority near presumption stringently — why do we rely on majority commonly found with expert slaughterers? Let us say rely on minority near presumption! And he answered: it is minority of a minority; and accordingly, if it were that in a bird there is no prohibition that it is not slaughtered — what does the Talmud challenge "did we not teach"? From there, dealing with a chicken, we would hear only majority of experts — but it is not proven it is minority of a minority; and in a chicken, where there is no presumption it is not slaughtered — majority would avail. But R' Nachman may say it deals with an animal — therefore forbidden, for we say rely on minority near presumption; in any case it is proven there is also in a bird prohibition it is not slaughtered. From your cattle and flock is not precise — the same chayah and bird; however it appears this matter is reasonable for R' Meir, who holds eiver min hachai does not apply in chayah and bird, and derives from "you shall slaughter" from your cattle and flock — he holds specifically cattle and flock; automatically in chayah and bird also the positive commandment "what is not slaughtered" does not exist. And behold in that R' Meir said eiver min hachai applies only in an animal — if we say specifically eiver min hachai, but flesh from life applies — at first glance it depends on dispute of R' Yehudah and R' Levi: R' Yehudah derives flesh from life from "and flesh in the field, treifah," and it applies to everything; and R' Levi derives flesh from life also from "you shall not eat the soul with the flesh," juxtaposed to "and you shall slaughter from your cattle" — as eiver min hachai applies only in cattle and flock, so flesh from life — all from that verse. Now for Ritzb"a's view nothing is difficult with the aforementioned challenge, since according to his view it is proven we establish from prohibition to prohibition; and so the Talmud properly challenges R' Yehudah according to his view that flesh from life applies in chayah and bird; and so properly there is presumption of prohibition from flesh from life — let us say rely on minority near presumption — and this is very correct, with God's help. But for Tosafot's view, that we do not establish from prohibition to prohibition — since it died before us, with this it is properly difficult: why does the Talmud challenge R' Meir from relying on minority near presumption? For R' Meir, from your cattle and flock — there is no prohibition it is not slaughtered in chayah and bird; if so, no relying on minority near presumption regarding flesh from life prohibition — behold it died before you in presumption of prohibition it is not slaughtered — there is none for R' Meir in chayah and bird; and this is a great wonder at first glance. Therefore, because of the greatness of this difficulty, we must seemingly compel: we do establish from prohibition to prohibition; and still need to explain: nevertheless it is difficult for R' Levi, that eiver min hachai and flesh from life come from one verse — for R' Meir both do not apply in chayah and bird; and as explained, if so it is difficult like Tosafot's difficulty: why does R' Meir exempt from covering? Behold R' Meir is concerned for minority; and since there is no presumption, no saying like Tosafot's answer where there is presumption with majority it becomes minority of a minority. And it appears this too is not difficult for Tosafot's view in the sugya: R' Meir is concerned for minority only rabbinically; and one may say this is only for concern he will encounter prohibition, but the Sages did not obligate him in a positive commandment because of minority — great difference between active violation and sit and do not do; and one may further mitigate according to what is written in Sefer Teivat Gome, parashat Vayera, to lean: in a positive commandment, do not say d'oraisa doubt stringently — only in a negative commandment, derived from that the verse was needed regarding mamzer; one may say specifically in negative commandment its doubt is forbidden from the Torah — the Sages were stringent to be concerned for minority; but in obligations of positive commandments, where even from doubt one is not obligated d'oraisa — they did not be stringent in majority to permit. And that R' Meir permits slaughtering after them — that is because oto ve'et beno prohibition applies only in an animal — presumption of eiver min hachai applies well; and even for R' Levi, who holds an animal in its lifetime is not for limbs — nevertheless in an animal one may say presumption stands it is not slaughtered; or R' Meir holds it is for limbs — that is a dispute of tannaim later; and from our words this too is concealed — Tosafot's view (daf 102b), s.v. she'ein bo, in their second answer: according to all, not for flesh from life; if so, it is difficult as above: why does the Talmud challenge R' Meir? For R' Meir there is no presumption of eiver min hachai and prohibition it is not slaughtered in chayah and bird, and also no presumption of flesh from life — for in its lifetime it is not for flesh from life. Nevertheless there is no contradiction from this to poskim that we do not establish from prohibition to prohibition — according to what we say above (daf 9): he did not check, etc.; one view: it stands in presumption of prohibition and now it died; and see in Rashba there, who wrote to explain: the reason of the view that imparts is that until now it was in presumption of life, and any live thing that died without prior correction is impure; only it comes from the act of correction — shechitah; and since there is doubt whether he did the correction properly — do not remove it from doubt; if so, for this tanna automatically any doubt in shechitah, even without presumption of eiver min hachai and flesh from life and prohibition it is not slaughtered — nevertheless there is presumption of prohibition, since any live thing that died is forbidden because of neveilah; and since it died before us, it is forbidden because of neveilah without prior correction, and from doubt of correction do not remove it from its prohibition. And what Tosafot pilpulized — presumption of prohibition is that it is not slaughtered — that is for the other tanna, who holds it stands in presumption of prohibition, not presumption of impurity, and does not hold Ritzeb"a's reasoning mentioned; and accordingly one may say they properly challenge: why specifically majority — even minority too, rely on minority near presumption — that is presumption: any live thing that died without correction is forbidden because of neveilah; necessarily R' Meir holds like this tanna — he did not check imparts by carrying; and because of Ritzeb"a's reasoning mentioned, since he holds exempt from covering even in chayah and bird where there is no presumption of prohibition — as explained; and we are concerned for minority; and therefore nevertheless there is presumption — as explained; and automatically there is majority and presumption — and they properly challenge — examine well. Also I have to say something new, with God's help: that "animal in presumption of prohibition" is eiver min hachai prohibition; and what Tosafot challenged — behold it died before you — one may say according to Shach (siman 86): in doubt of shechitah the egg is permitted, for it is rabbinic doubt; and Minchat Yaakov disputes, since the chicken has presumption of prohibition — for the egg it is rabbinic doubt where presumption became prohibition — see there; and it appears to me Minchat Yaakov's words are correct in reason, as we say: for one who permits the mother permits the daughter, since it is one body — the same from chicken to egg; and now one may say: if we hold like R' Levi that it is like placed in a basket, or slaughtered two simanim — according to all like placed in a basket; and slaughtered one siman in a bird — at least that siman itself is like placed in a basket — if so, regarding the simanim themselves there is presumption of eiver min hachai that shechitah was invalid and there is no shechitah and they are still forbidden because of eiver min hachai; and since in part of the animal there is presumption of prohibition, there is presumption on the whole; and one may say, as the first way above: for R' Yehudah according to R' Meir there is presumption on the simanim because of flesh from life that applies for R' Meir also in chayah and bird; and R' Yehudah, according to his view, who answers here "before it returned," etc. — and therefore he holds at least the siman itself is like placed in a basket — if so, why specifically majority — even minority too — examine well. And now we come to latter poskim's proof, who proved eiver min hachai does not apply to a bird according to Tosafot, Chullin (daf 33), who wrote: for R' Meir it is not difficult "is there anything," etc. — permitted for Israel and forbidden for a non-Jew — for also for Israel forbidden in chayah and bird because shechitah is required; and Rambam holds this is prohibition it is not slaughtered; and it was difficult for Rambam for the view there is no Torah shechitah for a bird — "and you shall slaughter" does not apply to a bird; if so, there is no prohibition it is not slaughtered; and if R' Meir holds there is Torah shechitah for a bird — if so, it is difficult here that they challenge daf 28: let the knife avail — the challenge is only for R' Meir specifically, as Tosafot wrote there; and for R' Meir it is proven he holds there is Torah shechitah for a bird; for this Rambam proved eiver min hachai does not apply to a bird for a son of Noah — end; although Rambam, according to his view, ruled like R' Avahu — "is there anything" — that is regarding convulsing, and because a non-Jew is different — not subject to shechitah; but regarding chayah and bird, "is there anything" applies, as appears to me. And behold in plain meaning according to our words there is no beginning to our proofs — for in truth it is proven for R' Meir forbidden to eat a bird from life, since they challenge: let us say rely on minority near presumption; and even according to the second way, that R' Meir holds there is presumption like this tanna who did not check imparts by carrying from Ritzeb"a's reasoning mentioned — and especially Rambam, who ruled imparts by carrying — nevertheless according to our words, for R' Meir certainly there is no prohibition it is not slaughtered in chayah and bird, since he holds "from your cattle and flock" is written specifically; if so, still difficult in chayah: "is there anything," etc.; and also this is in truth forbidden from life in chayah and bird because of flesh from life, derived from "flesh in the field, treifah," and applies for R' Meir also in chayah and bird — as explained; and latter poskim's proof is concealed; and nevertheless initially latter poskim's proof for the law does not exist in my humble opinion — perhaps for R' Meir in truth eiver min hachai does not apply to a bird for a son of Noah; and since his proof is concealed, and conversely we find strong proofs to the contrary: first, from the arranged sugya that its shechitah does not purify it; second, why is eiver min hachai practiced for a son of Noah — it was stated and repeated — certainly for its matter it was repeated to forbid eiver min hachai in a bird — according to plain sugya and Ra'avad and Kesef Mishneh views, that for Rambam too eiver min hachai applies to a bird for a son of Noah; if so, in our case for practical halakhah forbidden to give intestines to a non-Jew. And I heard from a certain scholar a proof that perforated esophagus is neveilah, from that they challenge Ulla in "thorn sits in esophagus": what is different from one who slaughters with a knife and it is found defective — and he does not distinguish that there it is presumption it is not slaughtered, that it is neveilah; if so, perforated esophagus too is neveilah — in my humble opinion this is no proof; and it is Tiferet Shmuel, who wrote proof to the contrary: perforated esophagus is not neveilah, from what Ra'osh wrote, chapter 2 of Chullin, siman 6, in the name of Rabbenu Tam: the question regarding pausing in minority of simanim deals with first minority of esophagus — for although it becomes treifah through perforated esophagus, perhaps it did not invalidate through pausing except in something that makes neveilah, such as after majority of siman — see there; behold explicitly perforated esophagus is not neveilah, only treifah. And behold in Ra'osh's words I have room for doubt in his intent: if his intent is neveilah does not occur through pausing in first minority of esophagus — even if we say parts do not combine, nevertheless shechitah of first minority would be only like thorn prick, which is treifah; and when he slaughters afterward, it purifies from neveilah and is like half defective trachea where completion avails — the same one may say in half defective esophagus completion avails to purify from neveilah; and the essence of the question is only that perhaps even treifah prohibition does not apply, for pausing invalidates only in something that makes neveilah — between siman and siman, that if you do not combine them there is no shechitah at all and it is neveilah; but in first minority of esophagus, even if you do not combine them, nevertheless the name shechitah applies to purify from neveilah — therefore pausing did not invalidate at all, and shechitah combines and it is valid shechitah to permit in eating. Or Ra'osh's intent is the question applies even regarding neveilah — the examiner will see Ra'osh's intent is even regarding neveilah it is required, for it is like any pausing in the world; and the reason that specifically in half defective trachea there is no matter of significant act, since it is not made treifah through it — therefore it is not in the category of shechitah; but in esophagus, even the minority anyway (therefore) ruins shechitah, for every act of preparation must be through shechitah; and in half defective esophagus completion does not avail to purify from neveilah except through pausing — it is required whether they are lenient in it with pausing and parts do not combine — automatically first minority is like merely defective, and it is even neveilah; or they are not lenient with pausing here and shechitah combines and permitted in eating — as appears correct to me, with God's help: in such manner, in half defective esophagus, if he slaughtered below it — certainly purifies from neveilah and is only treifah; but if he slaughtered at the place of the cut and completed there — certainly neveilah, for majority of simanim must be through shechitah specifically; and the essence of the question is only through pausing. And now: just as in half defective esophagus, if he completed at the place of the cut — neveilah; the same if he slaughtered below it on the other side — when he reaches the half, already majority is with the half defective and life departs through majority by combining the defective, for it is not called shechitah unless he slaughtered below on that same side. And now one may say the Talmud's challenge on Ulla: "what is different from one who slaughters with a knife and found defective" — Ulla stated generally, even if he found the thorn stuck on the other side of the place of shechitah; in this, if it healed, it is not shechitah — as explained; and it properly challenges — examine well.
