See Ba'er Heitev; and he wrote in Shevut Yaakov, part 1, siman 56, to prove not like Taz and his brother, may their memory be blessed, at end of siman 27, regarding broken spinal column before us with majority of flesh, even though the cord is intact; and nevertheless in a chicken where the spinal bone is found crooked and bent and thick, and it appears and is recognizable that the spinal column broke with the flesh and returned and connected well together — one may be lenient in case of loss, since if the spinal column and majority of flesh had broken, it would not have returned to fitness for life, as his brother Taz wrote in responsum — this reasoning too; and therefore the spinal column and majority of flesh with it did not break, only majority of flesh was still scraped off, even though somewhat of the spinal column broke with a little flesh — it returned and connected and became so thick that the examiner thinks and supposes it broke entirely with majority of flesh — we do not establish it as treifah so much; and perhaps see there. And from what he wrote at the end "so much and perhaps" — it appears he still leans to permit in case of loss; but in the index of responsa he wrote to permit — see there. And see in Shevut Yaakov, part 3, siman 63, where he brought Chacham Tzvi's responsum siman 28 on this, that he too forbade in broken spinal column and majority of flesh when the cord is intact — but he doubts because of Tosafot's words; and he wrote he already wrote in part 1 resolving Tosafot's words — see there.
