Captives are not redeemed, etc. This implies that even a relative may not be redeemed for more than his assessed value, because of the welfare of society. This is also implied by the conclusion, "but one may redeem himself," which indicates that this dispensation applies specifically to himself. The Bach, however, rules that one may redeem another for more than the person's value, whether his daughter, his relatives, or anyone else, and no one may object, since this does not impose pressure on the community. Certainly no one may object when he comes to redeem his wife, although he cannot be compelled to redeem his wife for more than her value. This is the accepted practice.
So that the enemies will not, etc. Meaning, so that they will not be encouraged to capture additional people in the future.
