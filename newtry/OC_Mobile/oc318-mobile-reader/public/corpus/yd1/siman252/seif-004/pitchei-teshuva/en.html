More. Authorities dispute whether a captive facing execution may be redeemed for more than his value. Maharam Lublin limits the legal obligation to the ordinary value but allows greater expenditure to prevent desecration of Hashem's name. Knesset Yechezkel argues that where the danger is genuine and the concern of encouraging further kidnappings is absent, the community may be required to spend heavily, with relatives first contributing according to assessment.
More than their value. Maharam Lublin defines value by the market price of a slave, even in lands without a slave market. Radbaz instead compares the ransom paid for non-Jewish captives and defends the custom of paying more, calling extensive involvement in redemption highly praiseworthy.
