And payment is collected for this even from the assets of minor orphans — and even though we rule that we do not administer the estates of minor orphans, as stated in Choshen Mishpat siman 110, there the reason is that testimony is not accepted except in the presence of both parties, and minor orphans are considered as if not present; but here testimony is not required, since by Torah law one is obligated to save [a captive]. Moreover, if one would have to wait until the orphan comes of age, no one would ever redeem any orphan with his money — so wrote Mahariv at the end of siman 148. (He further wrote that a woman is not obligated to redeem her husband from her dowry assets.) One who owes money to a non-Jew, or head-tax silver — this falls under the category of redeeming captives, and the community is obligated to pay on his behalf; only it must be known with certainty that he has no means. But if he is a swindler or is accustomed to borrowing from non-Jews so that others will redeem him, one does not redeem him. Radbaz, part 2, siman 163. (Maharam of Lublin, siman 15, wrote regarding one who was imprisoned on a claim that he had relations with a prostitute: even though strictly the community need not redeem him, since it would cause desecration of Hashem's Name, one should redeem him — specifically if they did not seek to kill him; but if they sought to kill him, one is strictly obligated to redeem him.
