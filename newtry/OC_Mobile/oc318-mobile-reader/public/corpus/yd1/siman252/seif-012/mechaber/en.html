The father is obligated to redeem the son if the father has means and the son does not: {Rama: And likewise relative after relative — the closer relative takes precedence, so that not all of them enrich themselves and cast their relatives upon the community (Mordechai, chapter HaShokher). One who redeems his fellow from captivity is obligated to pay him if he has what to pay — and we do not say that he is smuggling a lion from his fellow's property (Mordechai, chapters HaKones and Dayanei Gezeirot, and Teshuvat Mahariv simanim 148–149, and Beit Yosef in the name of Mahari Cohen); and he must pay him immediately and cannot say, "I will obey your judgment," and if he has a claim afterward he sues him in court — for without this a person would not redeem his fellow (Mahariv siman 149; and so wrote Beit Yosef).}
