And some say, etc. This is the view of the Tosafot cited above, as indicated by the statement there: 'The Sages say: She must examine herself.' The first opinion understands 'she must examine herself' as referring to the actual time of the fixed period. The passage concerning the dispute of Rav and Shmuel, which concludes that she later examined herself, was stated according to Shmuel. Their basis is the statement on 15a of Rabbah bar bar Chanah: 'Even if the time arrived,' etc. This implies that the rule applies even immediately after the fixed period, provided that the onah has passed, unlike Tosafot there, s.v. 'Even.' Rabbi Yochanan, who says, 'She calculates the days of her fixed period,' etc., maintains that fixed periods are a Torah law, and we do not rule in accordance with him. See the Ran in Shevuot.
Or it is, etc. For a woman's ordinary onah when she has no fixed period has the status of a fixed period, as stated there by Reish Lakish in the name of Rabbi Yehudah Nesiah, etc. See Rashi there, s.v. 'Within,' etc., and the passage on 9b.
