See Beit Yosef who brought Rambam and Rif's opinion: veset passed she does not need bedikah at all, and her husband has relations with her even if asleep, and need not ask if she is impure — for we are not concerned at all for veset; and Ran wrote on this: behold this is a great leniency, and therefore he did not bring it in Shulchan Arukh at all. And one must understand according to them Tosafot's challenge in Niddah 15a: behold near her veset she is forbidden to have relations lest she see at veset time — presumption regular time comes; and if so when she had veset how does he have relations with her without asking, lest she actually saw? And if he asks, her mouth will tell him if she sensed and saw or not. And one must say: otherwise the commentators wrote regarding one who sees three times due to relations — we attribute this woman always sees due to relations and do not attribute to other occurrences, because nature of desire and heat of relations cause blood to flow, as we say "they demanded of the woman," etc., "perhaps due to desire," etc.; and if so we hold regarding a woman in general she is not prone to blood, and veset derabbanan — even veset passed we do not establish her as seeing; unlike if she has relations near veset due to desire of relations — and this too is otherwise limited time for sighting she will see without sixty. And therefore there is warning to separate; and with this one can say: that which we rule even for taharot they are stringent in "she has veset" — her time, and we are not concerned at all even for one moment she advanced — for we say presumably she saw at limited time; and behold she must separate onah beforehand from concern lest she see earlier — behold we are concerned for advancement; and according to what is written it works well: specifically through relations we are concerned for this, not without relations we are not concerned for advancement at all; and so is Rambam's opinion that even if she has veset she always needs after relations bedikah — meaning relations cause blood; and so Rashi explained there. And with this Kesef Mishneh's challenge is settled: how on her veset day she is forbidden and day after permitted — lest she saw that day, etc.; and according to what is written it works well: we are concerned if she has relations at veset time it causes sighting, unlike if she lay down veset passed. And one can say. But behold the challenge: since Rambam rules a woman without veset always needs bedikah before relations — what difference whether she has no veset at all or has veset and veset time passed — behold she returns in law from having veset to not having veset — her impairment of regular time comes ruined, as we say plainly she saw at her time; and how does Rambam permit relations afterward while she is asleep without knowledge whether she checked herself and did not see or not? And one must say: he holds therefore in a woman without veset she needs bedikah before relations — not because we are concerned perhaps she is impure, only since she has no veset behold she is in doubt lest she see due to relations, being she has no veset and blood for her is like occurrence and does not depend on time at all; if so when desire and heat of relations join her she will see — and therefore Rabbi Meir forbids her to her husband; but Rabbah permits in this that she check herself, and if she finds blood three times after relations she leaves her husband; and therefore bedikah is required before relations — for otherwise still impossible to forbid her, lest she already saw earlier and this is not due to relations; and therefore the Sages required bedikah before relations so she know certainly it is due to relations if she sees then before after relations. And so they said relations at night onah — although she does not see at night in it, nevertheless tomorrow proves standing; and Rashi explained: and she know to bring atonement — and this is the reason bedikah is required before relations: so if she sees at time of relations she be established as seeing due to relations and be forbidden to her husband. And in this Rabbah was precise: "whether she prepared and whether they prepared it" — he did not say briefly "she has relations with two witnesses," for according to Rambam she always needs two witnesses; rather to instruct us therefore she has relations beforehand in order to prepare her and fix her to know if she sees due to relations. And one can say Ran and Maggid Mishneh's words in Rambam's explanation (Avraham Karelitz). And with this what Darkei Moshe challenged on the Tur is settled — he copied Rashba's words: if she has no fixed veset and it is less than intermediate onah which is twenty-fifth to twenty-fifth, and veset passed and she did not check — pure without bedikah; behold the Tur rules for practical law a woman without veset always needs bedikah before every relations? And Taz seif kaf gimmel explained it deals she has another veset, only she began changing from twenty-fifth to twenty-fifth and did not establish it three times — therefore no bedikah needed; and his words do not appear — for if so why was Rashba and Tur precise she is less than intermediate onah — what relevance of intermediate onah here since she has fixed veset and did not uproot it yet, she is not concerned at all for intermediate onah as is known? And Darkei Moshe explained it deals she passed that twenty-fifth and did not check, and afterward washed lower face — and if so even if she checks herself afterward before relations as law of woman without veset, one would say she is impure from concern of sighting that twenty-fifth that was unfixed veset and she did not check, and either way she washed herself — he teaches us no. And in truth one need not this — one can say then it was before pregnancy was recognized and afterward pregnancy recognized; and if so she has relations without bedikah and woman without veset is not relevant since cleared of blood — only one would say she needs bedikah from concern lest she saw at that time twenty-fifth passed — he teaches us no. But this itself is difficult: why are we not concerned for what is written we are concerned now before relations lest she saw and needs bedikah — why not concerned lest she saw at earlier time? For every time she is in concern of seeing; and if the husband wanted to have relations immediately he would need bedikah like all women without veset; and because he waited time — why not concern lest she saw at that time? And if so what of it that now she is cleared of blood according to what is written, or washed face according to Darkei Moshe — concern from where did it go? And according to Darkei Moshe one can say: since she already washed face and cannot fix, she is not forbidden after the fact, for Rashba and Tur's language does not imply so that it depends on after the fact; and according to what is written it works well: there is no concern of seeing before relations, only every bedikah before relations is to clarify sighting at time of relations in order to know due to whom the seeing and this bad thing as above "whether she prepared," etc. And one can say Darkei Moshe's explanation, or according to what is written without difficulty — investigate. And from Rambam's words it is proven as above: he holds in all women even those with veset bedikah is needed after relations; and if so why did Rabbah say "whether she prepared and whether they prepared it" — perforce before relations; and one must understand what preparation is in it — granted fixing is if he does not see, permitted to have relations unlike Rabbi Meir who forbids; but what is preparation? And if blood is found on him, preparation is not relevant; and according to what is written it works well: the preparation is that perforce if she finds blood three times she is forbidden to her husband — and this is through bedikah before relations; and one can say.

And that opinion holds: that which Rabbi Zeira said everyone veset deoraita — the intent is not entirely deoraita, only they were stringent with it like Torah; and according to him the challenge of Rabbi Yochanan in first chapter of Niddah: pregnant woman and her veset time arrived and she did not check — it asks for one who holds veset Torah, not according to halakhah it asks, only he holds here regarding bedikah they fixed it like Torah; and one must say specifically here alone he holds they fixed it like Torah — for here her husband counts her veset days and has relations with her mentioned in seif 11 — we depend in Gemara too whether veset Torah or derabbanan; and in this generally poskim permit; and one must say only for this matter they fixed it like Torah. And the reason: since it is Sages' enactment to check when veset arrives and be concerned for it — if you say no bedikah is needed right at it, you nullify Sages' enactment; and what did the Sages accomplish in their enactment? And afterward one returns to investigate how much rabbi is strained in this; and I do not know what Bach saw to be so stringent — after all everyone admits it is derabbanan; and how shall we be stringent in rabbinic matter where Rif and Rambam do not require bedikah at all, and Tosafot, Rashi, Rosh, Rashba — all do not hold like Ra'em Mordekhai? Behold their opinion is nullified against these great ones — and therefore one should not be stringent.
