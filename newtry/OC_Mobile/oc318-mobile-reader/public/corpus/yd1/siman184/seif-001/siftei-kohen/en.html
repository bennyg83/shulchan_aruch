Or from thirty etc.
And the standard veset is thirty days — Tur; and so it is in the Talmud and poskim, and later end of seif 9 and beginning of siman 189; and that is: if she did not establish a veset, then her veset is presumably every thirty days, and within thirty is called not at the time of her veset.
And she need not, etc. This follows Rambam, whose view the Mechaber cites at the beginning of siman 186. According to the majority of poskim cited there, however, and according to the Rama there and here, she should not be stringent to examine herself at all: not before marital relations, lest her husband's heart become apprehensive when he sees her examining herself and he think that had she not sensed something she would not have examined herself, causing him to separate from her; and not after marital relations, lest his heart become apprehensive and he separate from her in the future. The statement of the Gemara, and below in siman 196, seif 9, that any woman who frequently examines herself is praiseworthy, refers to examination at a time other than marital relations. So wrote the Tur, Rashba, and the other poskim.
