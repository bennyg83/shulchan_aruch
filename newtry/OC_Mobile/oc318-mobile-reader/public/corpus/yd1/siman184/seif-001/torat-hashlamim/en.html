He had relations with her outside the time of her veset. The Tur writes that he may have relations with her as he wishes, meaning whether she is awake or asleep. This refers to one who is not fully asleep, for if she were, it would be forbidden to have relations with her, as stated in Nedarim. Rather, she is not sufficiently awake to answer whether she is pure or not. Tosafot write this in Niddah 12a. These are the words of the Taz, and the Bach writes likewise. The Tur and Beit Yosef similarly write explicitly in this siman regarding a woman who remained after the veset long enough to count and immerse, which the Mechaber cites below in seif 11. Since the Mechaber writes without qualification both here and in seif 11, it appears that he accepts as primary the reasoning that the Gemara in Niddah 12 distinguishes between an awake woman and a sleeping woman because a sleeping woman was not fit to handle taharot, whereas an awake woman was fit to handle taharot and therefore required a more thorough examination. Rashi explains this there according to one interpretation. Accordingly, nowadays, when taharot are not practiced, there is no longer reason to distinguish between whether she is awake or asleep. This is readily understood.
She should also not act stringently by examining herself. The Rama disputes the Mechaber's view in two respects. The Mechaber's language implies that she does not require an examination, but that she may be stringent if she wishes; after relations, however, she necessarily requires an examination. This is Rambam's view, which the Mechaber cites at the beginning of siman 187, and the Shach writes likewise. The Rama's view is that under all circumstances she should not act stringently by examining herself at all, as the Rama also concludes below at the beginning of siman 187. If so, he should have written, “Some say,” in his usual manner. This requires study.
