He had relations with her, etc. The Shach's opinion in seif kaf alef: if she has no veset, presumably her veset is every thirty days, and within thirty is called not at her veset time and it is permitted to have relations with her without bedikah — and this has no basis. For that which Shulchan Arukh wrote "a woman who has a fixed veset" — because in a woman without veset later in siman 186 he rules like Rabbenu Chananel and Rambam that even her husband needs bedikah — if so, what help is it that she has intermediate onah? And if so, that which we learned throughout Talmud and poskim that a woman without veset needs bedikah — how do we find this? For behold there is intermediate onah here. And it is strained to say it deals with one who uprooted intermediate onah after three times passed and she did not see at intermediate onah — one, this is strained, for all this negotiation in a woman without veset would be specifically in such a remote case; and further for the Shach who equates intermediate onah to veset — if so perforce, if she uprooted intermediate onah for three other equal sightings, behold she established veset for that sighting and has no veset — how do you call her without veset? And if she uprooted for three unequal sightings, intermediate onah is still in its place like fixed veset as explained later siman 189. But I add that we do not find anywhere uprooting of intermediate onah as long as she did not establish veset — for we say: this woman, since she has no fixity of sighting, we say the way of women for her is like most women from onah to onah; and therefore it is clear that when we say intermediate onah, we mean to make its law like fixed veset law, but not to be lenient — for onah does not fix time like veset; and as Ran wrote: for veset its time suffices, but for intermediate onah we do not say so. And therefore here to be lenient and permit in a woman without veset within thirty days without bedikah — there is none. And therefore the Mechaber was precise and did not write here that intermediate onah has the law of fixed veset as he wrote in several places — and it is correct (Avraham Karelitz).
