Veset of the body, etc., and similar. These matters are according to what Beit Yosef wrote; but in truth his words are lame — for certainly if she has a veset for days and veset of bodily veset, she is always concerned for her veset, and it is not relevant to say "perhaps she did not see," and one need not be concerned for the intermediate onah since she has a veset; and so it implies later siman 189 seif kaf tet, and so in Darkei Moshe siman 189 seif 29: if she has a veset for days and veset of the body, she is no longer concerned at all except for her veset itself; and all the more according to what he wrote there in seif kaf that intermediate onah means from month to month — if so, behold the Tur wrote explicitly there that if she has a veset for days and veset of the body she need not again be concerned for the monthly veset; and so the plain meaning of the poskim in siman 189: if she has veset of the body for known days, her law is exactly like one who has a veset for days without veset of the body. So it appears in Rashba and the Tur itself, who did not write this law except regarding jumping — and thus: since her veset depends on action, say she did not jump and did not see due to jumping — then I am concerned perhaps the regular time came at the intermediate onah, end of his words. Hence specifically regarding jumping, since it depends on her action — if you want she jumps and if you want she does not — we say thus; but not regarding veset of the body. And so it appears in Terumat HaDeshen and Tur siman 189, who wrote first veset of jumping and afterward veset of the body; and afterward they wrote stringency of veset of jumping over other veset — that for veset of jumping, whether closer than intermediate onah or farther, she is always concerned for intermediate onah, end of his words. Hence specifically for veset of jumping we say thus, and not for veset of the body. And further, since Rashba wrote in Terumat HaDeshen and Beit Yosef brings it: likewise if veset of jumping is swallowed within the days of the onah and she did not jump and did not see — behold she is concerned for the day of the onah, end of his words — it implies explicitly: if she jumped and saw, she is not concerned for the day of the onah since she already saw; and so Bach wrote explicitly in siman 189 seif 35 — and it is simple. And if so, when she has veset for veset of the body and for days too — since she has a veset she certainly saw — why should she be concerned for intermediate onah? And so is proven from Ramban in Hagahot Maimoniyot chapter 4 of Hilchot Issurei Biah: a woman who has a veset — she counts her veset days; if her time arrived, behold she is in presumption of impurity. If she had veset of the body and not a fixed day veset, behold she is pure until the onah like one who has no veset, end of his words — hence if she had veset of the body for a fixed day, her law is like one who has veset for days alone. And Beit Yosef wrote: and it implies that Ramban means: if she had veset of the body such as jumping and similar, although it is on a known day, since she has no fixed day veset that there not be with it veset of the body at all, behold she is pure until the intermediate onah, as Rashba's words; and although Rashba did not write so except regarding jumping, and Ramban wrote so regarding veset of the body — and every one of veset of the body is included; it appears Rashba's jumping that he mentions is not specifically but one veset of the body he mentions, and likewise for any other veset of the body, end of his words. And it is difficult: if so, for what purpose did Ramban write "and has no fixed day veset" — it is obvious if she has fixed day veset for days alone she need not be concerned for intermediate onah; and what relevance is here — do we not know this until now? And further, if she has veset for days alone — behold they wrote at the beginning "a woman who has a veset," etc. And further, at the beginning he uses language "if she had a veset," and here he uses "and no fixed day veset" — it implies veset of the body, that it is not for a fixed day. And further, where is jumping mentioned in Ramban's words? And further, Rashba specifically mentions jumping, as written above; and perhaps Beit Yosef also admits regarding veset of the body: if she fixed it for days, its law is like fixing for days alone, and it deals only with veset of the body that comes without her action, similar to jumping — and as he mentions: since veset depends on action, say she did not jump and did not see, etc.; but not for other veset of the body such as yawning and sneezing and similar. And it is strained that the plain meaning of his words implies he deals with all veset of the body; and further we do not find in Talmud and poskim any veset of the body through action except jumping; and also in the language of poskim and Tur, veset of the body is yawning and sneezing and similar, as later siman 189 — investigate.
