Regarding bodily vesatot, etc. These words follow the Beit Yosef, but in truth require considerable study. If a woman has a fixed combination of days and bodily signs, she should be concerned only for that fixed veset and not for the ordinary intermediate onah, because she has a veset. This is implied later in siman 189 and by the Derisha. All the more so according to the view that the intermediate onah means the monthly date: the Tur and Shulchan Aruch explicitly say that a woman with a fixed day combined with a bodily sign need not separately observe the monthly veset. Rashba and the Tur state the extra concern for the intermediate onah specifically with jumping, because jumping depends upon an act she may or may not perform. Thus if she did not jump, one can say perhaps she did not see because the trigger did not occur and must still consider the ordinary onah. This reasoning does not apply to involuntary bodily signs. Toras HaBayis and the Tur likewise first discuss jumping, then other bodily vesatot, and later single out jumping for the stringency of always observing the intermediate onah. Ramban also writes that a woman with a bodily veset but no fixed day is presumed pure until the ordinary onah, implying that if the bodily veset is tied to a fixed day her law is like any other fixed veset. The Beit Yosef reads Ramban more broadly, but that interpretation is difficult.
Say she did not jump etc. And he need not ask her, even if she did not wait the duration for counting and immersing.
But she is concerned etc. And it is forbidden to come to her after the intermediate onah until he asks her, or until she has waited the duration to be able to count and immerse.
