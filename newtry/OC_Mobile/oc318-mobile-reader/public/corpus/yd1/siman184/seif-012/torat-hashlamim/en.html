Such as jumping or the like. This includes eating garlic or other hot foods, or another act that she performs and that causes the sighting through an external trigger, which is comparable to jumping. See above, siman 189, seif 23, in the gloss. This is straightforward. A veset established through a bodily occurrence, however, such as yawning or sneezing, does not depend on her own action. One must therefore be concerned both for the days and for the bodily veset, as explained below in siman 189. The Shach understood Beit Yosef's words as referring even to yawning and sneezing. He therefore argues at length and concludes: Perhaps Beit Yosef also concedes regarding bodily vesatot, and speaks only of a bodily veset that comes through an act, like jumping, but not of other vesatot such as yawning and sneezing. This is difficult, since the plain meaning of his words indicates that he discusses every bodily veset. Moreover, we do not find in the Gemara or poskim any bodily veset brought about by an act other than jumping. These are his words. His statement that no bodily veset brought about by an act other than jumping is found is certainly incorrect, for Beit Yosef follows Rashba, and Rashba also includes eating garlic and similar matters together with jumping, as Beit Yosef writes below in siman 189. The Shach clearly overlooked Beit Yosef's words there. His statement that the plain meaning of Beit Yosef's words indicates otherwise is also incorrect. On the contrary, since Beit Yosef writes that the veset depends upon an act and perhaps she did not jump, his words explicitly imply the reverse. Beit Yosef's words are correct. Examine this carefully.
