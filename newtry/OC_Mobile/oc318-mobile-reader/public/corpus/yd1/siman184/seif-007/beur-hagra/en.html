From when it became recognizable, etc. Mishnah, Niddah 7b.
For they are, etc.; even, etc. This follows the Sages, Rabbi Yehudah, and Rabbi Shimon there, and the Gemara on 9a.
And even, etc. This follows from the discussion there: 'What is meant by since,' etc.; and from the alternative version, etc. It must refer to the time of the fixed period. Otherwise, why does the Gemara not entertain the possibility that an examination is ineffective, as stated on 16a? It should then have said: Since, etc., is she impure, or perhaps, etc., is she pure, rather than, etc. This is the language of the Rosh, chapter 1, seif 4: 'From here it appears,' etc., 'that even at the time of their fixed period,' etc. Torat HaBayit writes likewise.
