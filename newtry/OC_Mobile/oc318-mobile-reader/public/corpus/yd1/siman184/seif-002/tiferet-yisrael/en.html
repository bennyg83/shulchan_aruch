Seif 2. See in Maharshal siman 3: he brought Beit Yosef's challenge on the Rosh who ruled in Niddah that veset is derabbanan, and in Yevamot he wrote it is forbidden to be intimate with one's wife near her veset like Rabbeinu Tam. And it is possible to say the Rosh holds: that which we rule veset derabbanan and the presumption of regular time — is only rabbinic, although we see most women have a fixed veset and certainly from the Torah we follow the majority; only the reason is nevertheless veset derabbanan, since nevertheless the regular time does not come with sensation, and from the Torah she is not forbidden except with sensation — if so he ruled well that it is forbidden to have relations near her veset, lest she see at the time of relations; and even if it will be with sensation, she will attribute it to the sensation of relations — and for this it is forbidden, possibly even from the Torah, for then certainly the majority also assists the prohibition. And see what I wrote siman 4. Or one can say he holds like the approach of Maharshal in several places, that the nature of relations causes blood to flow — and if so, although the presumption of regular time is not a Torah presumption, nevertheless she is forbidden from the Torah to have relations at the time near relations, for relations cause blood to flow. But in truth all this is strained in the Rosh's words; but were I not afraid of speaking against Beit Yosef, I would say: although veset is derabbanan, nevertheless the Rosh holds it is forbidden even from the Torah because of the negative commandment "you shall not turn aside" — and it requires investigation.
