(Seif 2) At her veset time he must separate from her, etc. In Binat Adam he forbids immersing a woman whose husband was not in the city, for she does not put her mind at ease and perhaps she will become aroused at her veset time without her awareness. And it appears that according to one who holds veset is derabbanan this is fine, for in rabbinic matters one does not say a matter that does not put one's mind at ease without awareness, as written in Magen Avraham siman 190. And so is the opinion of Shulchan Arukh and Shach in siman 194. And see there in Binat Adam who forbids immersing and having relations on the night of the onah, because she is also warned about the prohibition and about the mitzvah of peru urevu she is not warned. But I have doubt, for perhaps it is not comparable to what Tosafot wrote in Gittin (41a) regarding a slave who marries a maidservant — for here she causes him that he cannot fulfill peru urevu, and if so we say to her: sin on account of your fellow's benefit because of the mitzvah of peru urevu, as we say there that they compel the master to free him even though he transgresses a positive commandment in order to fulfill the mitzvah of peru urevu, which is a great mitzvah as Tosafot wrote in Pesachim (88b). And it appears the reason of a master is weaker than a family member, because he causes it. And further, regarding a master there is actually the mitzvah of peru urevu, unlike if he married a maidservant — he does not fulfill the mitzvah of peru urevu, only Shabbos in general; and if so, here too it is comparable to a master whom she causes to nullify peru urevu, and we say to her: sin on account of your fellow's benefit in the mitzvah of peru urevu.
