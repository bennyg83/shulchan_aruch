He must separate from her. If they transgressed and had relations, both the man and the woman require repentance. The Shach writes this in seif katan 7. Although his words there imply that he holds that veset observance is required by Torah law, whereas we rule that veset observance is rabbinic, as explained in Beit Yosef in this siman, they nevertheless require repentance as transgressors of a rabbinic prohibition. If she actually found blood, it was certainly a Torah transgression, as stated in the second chapter of Shevuot. The Levush writes likewise at the end of siman 165. Although the Rama rules at the end of siman 185 that the woman does not require atonement, that case is different because she was compelled by circumstances and did not transgress at all, unlike here. This is also the implication of the Levush there.

If the veset occurs by day, he must separate from her. This means even in a darkened house, or in the case of a Torah scholar who covers himself with his garment to darken the area. Otherwise, relations by day are forbidden even outside the time of her veset, as stated in Orach Chaim siman 240 and Even HaEzer siman 25.
He must separate from her. He must separate for that entire day, but no longer. There is no distinction between the days of Nisan and Tishrei and the days of Tammuz and Tevet. Beit Yosef writes this and states that it is the implication of the Gemara and the poskim. He disputes Or Zarua, who writes that a full twenty-four-hour period is required, and Avi'asaf, who writes that in Tammuz and Tevet one must separate for half a day and half a night. Ma'adanei Melech at the beginning of Niddah and the Bach defend Avi'asaf's words, and the Shach in seif katan 7 follows them. The Taz in seif katan 2 rejects their words, and his position is correct for one who examines the Gemara and the poskim. Although the Shach defends his position in Nekudot HaKesef, his words are not compelling at all. In particular, his attempt to uphold Or Zarua's view from the Gemara is unclear and runs contrary to the implication of the Gemara and all the poskim, who maintain that she is forbidden only during that single onah. Even when the veset continues for three days, most poskim hold that she is forbidden only during that onah, and according to all opinions she is not forbidden beforehand, as explained below. Raavan, siman 318, upon whose words the Shach built his foundation, clearly had a different text and approach in the Gemara. That is the approach of Or Zarua and Avi'asaf. According to the text before us in Niddah 63b, however, there is certainly no basis for stringency. This is the approach of Rif, Rambam, Rosh, Rashba, Raavad, Tur, Rabbeinu Yerucham, Semag, Semak, and the other great poskim. One should certainly not depart from their words or from the Mechaber's ruling, especially since we rule that veset observance is rabbinic.
