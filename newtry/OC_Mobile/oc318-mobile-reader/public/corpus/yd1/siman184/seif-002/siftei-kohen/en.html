He must separate etc. That is by rabbinic law — so is the consensus of most poskim and Acharonim, that vestot are rabbinic.
And not from other forms of closeness. This implies that even embracing and kissing are permitted, as the Beit Yosef rules from the implication of the poskim, unlike Terumas HaDeshen. This also appears to be the Rama's view in seif 10, and the Bach rules likewise, although he concludes that one who is stringent in these matters is praiseworthy.
That day, etc. Hagahos Maimoniyos cites Avi Asaf that an onah is measured as a day or night in Nisan and Tishrei, but as half a day plus half a night in Tammuz and Teves. The Beit Yosef rejects this because the other poskim do not mention it and the Gemara speaks simply of a day or a night. Maadanei Melech and the Bach defend Avi Asaf, and I likewise found his ruling in Raavan. The omission of other poskim is no proof, since they speak of an ordinary day and night; during Tammuz and Teves seasonal hours must be calculated, as in several other areas of halacha. Or Zarua writes that a woman separates from her husband for twenty-four hours around her expected time: if she regularly sees by day, she separates for that entire day and the preceding night, and vice versa. It appears Raavan distinguishes between a fixed hour within the day, where only that day is the expected onah, and a woman who regularly sees by day without a fixed hour, whose entire day is effectively her veset and who therefore separates also during the preceding night. This reconciles Or Zarua with the Gemara. The Bach likewise writes that one who fears Hashem should follow Or Zarua, and Hagahos Shaarei Dura and Maharil cite this practice.
But not in a veset dependent on bodily change. Apart from this, he need not separate except at the time of the veset if it does not come at a known time, as below siman 189 seif 24 and 25.
