That day, etc. See Shach seif kaf zayin who brought Aviassaf and Raavan's opinion that the measure of separation of onah is twelve hours; and Bach and so Levush supported him from the onah of virgin blood that is explained as twelve hours. And in truth the Gemara sugya in Niddah 63 is clear not like Raavan and Aviassaf — for we say: if she was accustomed to see by day, she is permitted the night before, for the entire night is hers; and in the Gemara the presumption was that separation of onah is another onah, and it answers: no, that onah. And Shach seif kaf zayin explained the Gemara deals with one who has veset to see always at its beginning or always in its middle, etc. — she need only separate that day and not before; and Or Zarua deals if there is no fixity that day, sometimes at beginning, etc. — investigate. And these matters have no basis — apply yourself: a woman whose veset is to see immediately at sunrise — she would be permitted the night before; and if sometimes she delays seeing from sunrise onward, the entire night would be forbidden according to my view; and still nothing returns to Aviassaf's approach who holds onah is twelve hours — and behold Shach admits if she fixed to see at the beginning of the day she is permitted the night before — where then are twelve hours? And Taz in seif kaf bet struggled to distinguish between this onah of virgin blood and onah of separation — and one need not strain, for the distinction is clear: there it is the measure that blood flows from the wound of virginity compression, and what does blood care to flow at changed times day and night — behold twelve hours from the wound blood flows; and shall we say when the day is long in winter fifteen hours it flows fifteen hours from the wound, and in summer when day is short nine hours it flows nine hours — this is impossible; therefore we always say twelve hours. But niddah veset is already known to depend on the course of sun and moon until they established monthly veset for the moon's course, saying this day of the month causes sighting; and likewise weekly veset that this day of the week causes sighting; and likewise sometimes in a woman the sun above the horizon causes sighting and sometimes the reverse when sun is below the earth — according to the woman's constitution and qualities the occurrence changes, sometimes absence of sun and sometimes presence of sun fixes and causes veset — and this is simple. And if so, what difference if day and night are long or short — in the end the sun shines or sets and sighting from the woman depends on this, not on duration long or short — and this is simple and correct. And I made support for the above distinction in what we say in the Gemara Shabbat chapter Rabbi Meir: according to Rabbi Akiva who said a woman emits seed on day five of onah — they separate in the morning, and behold they are tevulei yom; and it answers: the Torah was given to tevulei yom. And it asks: was it given or fit — and one must understand: what difference if given or fit? And also what it answered "fit" is only prophetic words — from where did he know to say sixty myriads of women of Israel, not one emitted seed and immersed? This requires kabbalah halakhah leMoshe miSinai that was then at Sinai; and for what purpose is this transmitted where there is no difference? However the matter is clear: there is doubt whether we say onah is twelve hours like Aviassaf's approach and it does not depend if day is long or short, or everything depends on day and there is no difference long or short day — and accordingly if onah depends on hours, when Israel separated on day four, behold here is day four and night twenty-four hours; day five and night likewise twenty-four hours — behold four onot; and on day six twelve hours — if so on day of Sivan which is a long day, before the sun set all five onot ended, and even though they still emitted on the long day to immerse before sunset — behold they are tevulei yom. But if we say it depends on days — day six would be so long like the day the sun stood for Joshua — nevertheless if she emits with sunset she is impure, and if so they have immersion only at night and are tevulei yom — it is not difficult, for if so why does the Gemara challenge "behold tevulei yom"? And it answers: the Torah was given to tevulei yom — from where do we know this approach who holds twelve hours is onah? One can say: otherwise it is difficult from where does a woman emit seed on day five of onah — four onot suffice; and that they separated on day four in the morning — because if they had relations on day four until eve, behold it is night five and day five two onot, night six and day six two onot — behold four onot; and if they emit on day six at sunset, behold tevulei yom — how will they receive the Torah? Therefore Moses had to advance one onah for them; and since he advanced, he gave them in the morning — for in the morning it rose, etc.; and on this it answers well: the Torah was given to tevulei yom. And if so there was no need to nullify peru urevu from Israel every day four; and therefore we require five onot. But now that we rule five onot are required and on four they separate — it works well for them; and on this it answered: fit to give it — this is truly so, for twelve hours it depends and not days; and if so then the day was long and they immersed before evening and were tevulei yom — and one need not prophetic words that no woman was careful about emission, only we hold as above — and this is clear in my humble opinion. Nevertheless we must resolve Aviassaf and Raavan's words that the Gemara's words should not challenge them; and it appears they certainly hold according to Rabbi Yosi who holds for her veset time: if she saw at midday she is permitted to have relations until midday until the hour she sees — and Rabbi Yehuda should wonder: will he not expound this warning that they separate from their wives near their veset? And if so Rabbi Yosi's warning to Israel to separate, etc., is like tannaim — for Rabbi Yosi we do not expound — and this is very strained. And one need not establish this warning on veset time itself besides that the Gemara language does not imply so; and also one need not a verse — "and you shall be careful" is obvious at veset time one must separate without warning at all, lest at time of relations she see — how can one think a) at onah and at that time have relations? One must say there are two matters of warnings: veset is at the time fixed for seeing — this is called veset, and this is certainly forbidden to have relations for one who holds veset deoraita from Torah and for one who holds derabbanan from the rabbis; but this has no relevance to "and you shall be careful," for this is a full obligation and prohibition even for one going on a journey and similar. But afterward near it — this is called near veset; and this is included in "and you shall be careful" — that they should separate near veset specifically, for that time when she sees is called her veset; however the earlier time is called near her veset, and this is included in general carefulness; and this one the Torah permitted for one going on a journey and similar. And if so, the mishnah never mentioned this warning near her veset, for it is only a general stringency and fence — the mishnah sages did not speak of it, only Talmud sages afterward were stringent in this warning to say even near her veset it is forbidden to have relations; and therefore we do not find separation near veset in the mishnah. Rabbi Yosi and Rabbi Yehuda divided in the mishnah the law of separating veset itself, for this is included in prohibition either of Torah or derabbanan; and therefore Rabbi Yehuda permits one who sees by day on the night before, for this is not included in veset; and Rabbi Yosi permits even by day until the boundary fixed in hours of her day of seeing. But near veset is not dealt with in the mishnah — and with this it is settled that the Torah permitted having relations near veset for one going on a journey: for one who holds her veset deoraita, how did Torah permit — Tosafot and Rosh's challenge; but according to what is written it works well: this is not included in veset deoraita, and the Torah did not permit for one going on a journey except the adjacent onah; but Talmud sages were stringent to separate from onah near veset, and it is included in rabbinic warning — and this is specifically to separate near veset, for on the day itself it is called veset but before it is called near her veset. And accordingly it is justified what the Gemara challenges on that which Rava said halakhah like Rabbi Yehuda, and we learned, etc., and Rava said: how much onah — what, not another onah? It is difficult: one, how can one think to say another onah, and why did Rava understand thus and challenge from him further questions; two, according to this if onah is another onah, then Rava is not like anyone — not Rabbi Yehuda who holds only that onah as the Gemara's challenge, and all the more he is not like Rabbi Yosi — and if so he should have challenged Rava is not like anyone; three, why specifically that he said halakhah like Rabbi Yehuda. But according to Aviassaf it works well: we say a person must separate from his wife near veset — how much onah? The Gemara understood this "near" is the measure of onah; and the Gemara knew daily practice would prove; and so halakhah is taught: one separates from a woman only twelve hours measure of onah from time of sighting — if she saw midday, he separates from her half the night before that aligns to twelve hours; and if so, granted halakhah like Rabbi Yosi that veset is called at the hour she sees midday — he says well that near twelve hours from it one must separate, and this agrees with daily practice. But if halakhah like Rabbi Yehuda — according to his words even one seeing at midday, the entire day is considered veset; and if you fix him to separate onah beforehand, he is also forbidden the entire night — and this is against daily practice; and the Gemara's challenge works well. And the Gemara answers: not that onah — the intention of "how much onah" is not on near veset alone that this would be onah, but the intention is one must separate so that between all from time of separation to time of sighting there are twelve hours that is measure of onah; and thus Rava said onah. And if so the matter agrees with Rabbi Yehuda and daily practice. And one can say: on this the Gemara challenges "why two" — certainly this explanation; and one need not Rava's explanation of measure of onah to know halakhah like Rabbi Yosi; and the Gemara answers: I would think like Rabbi Yosi except for that veset time, but that which it challenged requires the reverse — let us say halakhah like Rabbi Yosi and he should not say onah — this is difficult at first glance for Aviassaf's approach, for from this we do not know he separates further outside veset — and according to Rabbi Yehuda we only heard the entire day is included in prohibition. However according to the above one can also say: a reason is needed why the Sages made him separate twelve hours beforehand — what difference twelve hours, why not less or more? For granted for us everything depends on sunset and sunrise as above; but for his approach it is difficult. And one must say: the entire essence of the stringency of separating before her veset is: if you say only according to the stringency mentioned in the mishnah for Rabbi Yehuda — if she sees at dawn she is permitted to have relations the night before — the woman will not notice the difference between sunset and sunrise and will think before time of sighting she is permitted to have relations like Rabbi Yosi; and even if she sees near sunset at day's end she will have relations beforehand and this is forbidden, for the entire day is included in prohibition; and therefore the Sages made separation of twelve hours — and if so it is always impossible to come to prohibition of having relations at her veset time. And one can say: even though Rava did not say "how much onah," only he teaches halakhah like Rabbi Yehuda — this Rabbi Yosi statement alone suffices: separate from one's wife near veset — we would know measure twelve hours, for the entire essence of separation is because of the above error; and therefore the measure is twelve hours, for otherwise what did the Sages accomplish in their enactment? And more than twelve hours is unnecessary — for what purpose shall one say so? Granted if halakhah like Rabbi Yosi it works well that separation near is that entire day or night, and what for Rabbi Yehuda is forbidden by law for Rabbi Yosi is because of reason of separation near veset; and Rava needed to inform us it is not so, only measure of onah which is twelve hours. But now that he said halakhah like Rabbi Yehuda — separation reason is because of the above error — if so it is obvious it is twelve hours. And if so Rava's word onah is unnecessary — and one can say; and therefore he says: I would think for her husband halakhah like Rabbi Yosi — and one can say; investigate well this explanation for Aviassaf's approach in my humble opinion as a key that flies — and it has proof from the above Gemara challenges. And we wonder at the Shach for his words: what does the Gemara challenge "why two" — behold from Rabbi Yehuda's statement we do not hear that if she has no fixed hour that day, only earlier and later, she must separate also the night before; and therefore Rava needed to inform us she separates onah twelve hours beforehand — and the Gemara's challenge does not apply — it requires investigation.
