And even, etc. Rashi there, s.v. 'And this applies,' etc., and the other authorities. Tosafot there, s.v. 'He is obligated,' disagree, as does the Rosh there. The other authorities disagree with Tosafot and write that since fixed periods are rabbinic, the Sages supported this rule with a Scriptural verse only as an asmachta; the verse 'You shall separate,' etc., is merely an asmachta. Semag writes that one who acts stringently is worthy of blessing.
And if he is going, etc. The Gemara there.
And some say, etc. This follows the textual version, 'to his wife who is a niddah,' cited by Tosafot in the passage mentioned above and by the Rosh. Tosafot object that relations are certainly impossible in such a case. Nimukei Yosef resolves that the passage concerns a case of this kind.
