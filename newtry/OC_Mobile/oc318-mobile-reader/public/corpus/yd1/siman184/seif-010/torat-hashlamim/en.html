He must attend to his wife. Rambam omits this law, and the Maggid Mishneh questions him in chapter 4 of Hilchot Issurei Biah. In my humble opinion, Rambam follows his own position, for he writes in chapter 5 of Hilchot Deot that one should not have relations on the day he departs on a journey or returns from a journey because of danger. See there. The Tur and Mechaber, who cite these words of Rambam in Orach Chaim siman 241 and Even HaEzer siman 24, likewise follow their own approach, for they accept Rabbeinu Tam's interpretation that this attendance does not mean relations, but words of affection. This appears straightforward to me. The Levush in Orach Chaim and the Rav in responsum 132 distinguish the cases differently, and the Bach in Even HaEzer writes likewise. See the Perishah there. In my humble opinion, my explanation is preferable, for it also resolves the Maggid Mishneh's difficulty regarding Rambam's omission of this law.
A blessing shall come upon him. Semag writes in siman 111 that one who is stringent is praiseworthy. Maharshal comments there: I find this difficult. How can it be said that one who is stringent is praiseworthy, since the Gemara states that he is obligated to attend to his wife? On the contrary, one who is exacting to fulfill the mitzvah properly is not merely called stringent. Had the Gemara said that he is permitted to attend to her, it would be appropriate to say that one who is stringent is praiseworthy. Maharshal offers several resolutions. See there. He ultimately concludes that the meaning may be that one who acts stringently with himself and refrains from departing on a journey close to her veset, so that he will not place himself in doubt, is praiseworthy. This differs from the Rama's view here, who explains that the stringency is to attend to her with words of affection. It appears correct to me, where possible, to follow Maharshal, for one thereby satisfies all opinions.
It has already been explained that all forms of closeness and affection are permitted. This means that they are permitted even when he is not departing on a journey. Accordingly, when he is departing, he is obligated to act in this manner. Beit Yosef. The Shach writes that even according to the Bach, who states that one who is stringent regarding embracing and kissing is praiseworthy, as I wrote above in seif katan 4, departure on a journey is different, and one should not be stringent at all.
He must wait. This too applies specifically when traveling for an optional purpose. If he is traveling for a mitzvah, he need not wait. Even when traveling for an optional purpose, if there is a great need, it appears that he need not wait. Shach.
