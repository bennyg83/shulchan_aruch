He must command his wife. The Rambam omitted this law, and Kesef Mishneh wondered on him in chapter 4 of Hilchot Issurei Biah. And in my opinion it appears the Rambam follows his approach that he wrote in Hilchot Deot not to have relations on the day of departure on a journey or arrival from a journey because of danger — investigate. And the Tur and Mechaber who copied these Rambam words in Orach Chaim siman 281 and Even HaEzer siman 24 follow their approach that they grasp the main thing according to Rabbeinu Tam's explanation that this commanding is not relations but words of appeasement as above — simple. However Levush in Orach Chaim and the Rav in responsum siman 332 distinguish in another matter; and so Bach in Even HaEzer — investigate in the separation. And in my opinion it appears as written, in order to resolve here too Kesef Mishneh's wonder on the Rambam who omitted this law.
Blessing shall come upon him. So Semag in siman 111: one who is stringent loses out. And Maharshal wrote there in his explanations: and it is simple — how is it relevant to say "and one who is stringent loses out," since he said he is obligated to command his wife — on the contrary, this is called stringency, one who is careful to fulfill the mitzvah properly. Granted if he said it is permitted to command, it is relevant to say "and one who is stringent loses out." And he wrote there several answers to this — investigate. And in conclusion he concludes: or alternatively this is what is meant — one who is stringent on himself and refrains from going on a journey near her veset so as not to put himself in doubt loses out; and this is not like the Rav here who wrote that the stringency is to command her with words of appeasement. And the correct approach in my humble opinion is to practice where possible like Maharshal, for then one exits to all opinions.
