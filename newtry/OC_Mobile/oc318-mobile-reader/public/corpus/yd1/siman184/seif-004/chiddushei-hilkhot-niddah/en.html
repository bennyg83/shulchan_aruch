She is forbidden only by day. See Taz the reason that we attribute impairment to the impaired; and in my humble opinion otherwise one can say: at night she need not separate, since it is rabbinic matter and rabbinic doubt is resolved leniently — unlike by day, since we see the night passed and she did not see, and there is presumption regular time comes — if so necessarily her veset is not at night, for presumably she would see then, rather her veset is by day; and therefore she did not see at night for it is not her time — and if so now without seeing at night this doubt is clarified for us; and therefore day doubt is stringent, and therefore she must separate by day. And one can say: however for practical law the later authorities already wrote to be stringent; and possibly this doubt is doubt of lack of knowledge whether she can distinguish before sunrise or after; and likewise husband Magen Avraham brought in Sefer Baal Nefesh to Raavad before us is also stringent — and therefore one should be stringent.
