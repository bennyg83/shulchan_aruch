She is forbidden only by day. The reason is that since veset observance is rabbinic, we rule leniently and say that her previous sighting certainly occurred during her days of niddah. The Rosh and other poskim write this in the name of Raavad's Baal HaNefesh. However, in the edition of Baal HaNefesh before us, printed in Venice, in Shaar Tikkun HaVesatot, he writes as follows: If she is accustomed to seeing precisely at sunrise, or if she is not certain of the matter and we do not know whether it was before sunrise or after sunrise, we certainly treat her stringently for the entire night and the entire day. Thus, Raavad explicitly rules without qualification that one must be stringent. His words also imply that sunrise is treated as a distinct uncertainty. See the Bach, who likewise writes that one should be stringent.
