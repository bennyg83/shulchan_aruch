Seif 4. See there siman 5: what Maharshal wrote is a correct answer to Tosafot 15a according to the Rambam and Rif who hold that regarding a pregnant woman at her veset her husband has relations with her without asking her mouth. And in my opinion it is possible to say thus: I already wrote in the previous seif kaf that after her veset she is permitted, for close is the minority who do not see at their time; and if they see, they do not see with sensation — for presumption, presumption of purity; and this is a Torah majority, for certainly there is a minority who do not see with sensation — and if so, this presumption of regular time does not exist, for there is against it the above Torah majority; and otherwise majority is preferable to presumption — and therefore one need not even ask her mouth, for we rely on Torah majority. But all this is after veset; for even if she saw, perhaps it was without sensation; but near her veset she is forbidden to have relations, lest at the time of relations she see with sensation and she will think it is the sensation of relations. And one can say: this is explained in Shulchan Arukh in seif 7 in the previous seif that a pregnant woman is permitted to have relations at her veset time — and it is difficult: let us be concerned lest she see due to relations and attribute as above. One can say: this is only the Rosh's opinion, but not the Rambam. And further the Shach that her veset time is forbidden — and it is possible to say they disagree in the Gemara whether veset is deoraita: although derabbanan, although we see most see at their fixed times, nevertheless they do not always see with sensation; and although this too is a majority who see with sensation, nevertheless there are two minorities — a minority who do not see at all, and a minority who do not see with sensation — and close are two minorities and it becomes a majority; and this one holds veset derabbanan, and this one holds we do not say close two minorities and holds deoraita. And more one can say: Tosafot's challenge there, and also Kesef Mishneh's challenge: how on her veset day she is forbidden and after it permitted — for behold from the Torah she is permitted by presumption, meaning perhaps she did not see at all, for from the Torah there is no presumption of regular time here; and if you say she saw, perhaps not with sensation (and majority with sensation is not here, since from the Torah she has presumption of purity of veset — a minority without sensation exists for majority, and only doubt remains) — and by presumption she is permitted and one need not here reverse that she begin immediately lest she did not see; and also one coercion is not considered — for this doubt that perhaps she did not see permits entirely. But seeing without sensation defiles at least evening tumah, as proven in Rambam chapter 3 of Hilchot Metamei Mishkav uMoshav, and see general rules of Shach that we say presumption as above. And one can say: behold there is to clarify by asking her mouth — the Rif and Rambam hold no clarification is needed, for this is not agreed among poskim that clarification is needed; and already I cast my lot on Peri Chadash, and see what I wrote in general rules of Shach on this. And if so, since from the Torah she is permitted and only rabbinic doubt, the Rambam and Rif ruled leniently. However near her veset and plainly at her actual veset time we are concerned lest she see at the time of relations — and it is a Torah doubt — and it requires investigation. And one can say: behold the Rif and Rambam require bedikah initially at veset times — and if so, presumably she checked, and it is also doubt of Torah sensation — say the sensation was slight. One can say: they transferred this law onto one coming from the road; and see in the Gemara 15a that if her husband is not in the city she does not put her mind to check — investigate. And in siman 185 I proved the above reasoning of close minority, etc.
