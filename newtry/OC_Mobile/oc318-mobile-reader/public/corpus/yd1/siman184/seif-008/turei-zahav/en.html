She need not be concerned for it. So wrote the Beit Yosef in the name of the Rashba, that even l'chatchila she need not be concerned. This case is therefore like that of a pregnant woman in seif 7. The Tur, however, wrote here that she must be concerned for her veset, except that if the day passed, etc., as the Rama subsequently states in the name of some authorities. The Beit Yosef challenged the Tur, for in the Gemara R' Yochanan derives the law of a pregnant woman from the law of a woman who was hiding, who does not require an examination. How, then, can we distinguish between them? He discussed this at length. My father-in-law, of blessed memory, wrote that the Tur and the Rosh had a different textual version in the Gemara. In my humble opinion, however, the matter is straightforward. When blood withdraws because of fear, there is reason to be concerned that at the time she joins her husband, the fear may leave her heart. Since it is the time of her veset, she may then become impure, because the accustomed time arrives at its proper time. The Sages said that fright causes the blood to withdraw only when she remained frightened throughout the entire time of the veset and the fright did not cease. This is clear: if she was afraid at the beginning of the time of her veset, but the fear left her during that period, she is then like other women, for her natural state has returned to its former condition. Therefore, she may not have relations until the entire period of the veset has passed. This is unlike a pregnant woman, whose blood has already withdrawn and whom there is no reason to forbid even during the time of the veset. Thus, the similarity between a pregnant woman and one who was hiding applies only after the fright is complete, meaning after the period of the veset has passed, when the withdrawal of blood is certain. Each case follows its own circumstances: in the frightened woman, only after the fright has passed; in the pregnant woman, even immediately. Therefore, the Gemara properly derives that a pregnant woman is permitted l'chatchila from the case of one who was hiding, who, after the entire time of the veset has passed, does not require an examination. This is correct and clear. I am astonished that the commentators did not note this point, for it is a sound rationale and it is proper to establish the halacha accordingly.
