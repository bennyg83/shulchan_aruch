If she was, etc. This applies even at the time of her fixed period, since the Gemara there compares her to a pregnant woman, as explained above.
And some say, etc. The Tur writes this. His basis is the statement there cited by the Rama: 'If,' etc., 'and she did not examine,' etc., which implies that the fixed period passed and she did not examine. The statement there, 'This proves,' etc., 'and not,' etc., means after the time of the fixed period. The further statement, 'The reason is that there is,' etc., 'this proves,' etc., but where the indication comes from her own body, does not establish a Torah prohibition. Thus, even according to the view that fixed periods are rabbinic, this ruling applies specifically when the period passed and she did not examine herself.
