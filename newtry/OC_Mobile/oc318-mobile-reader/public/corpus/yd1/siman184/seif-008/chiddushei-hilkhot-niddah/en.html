Ab initio she needs bedikah. Beit Yosef wondered on the Tur: in the Gemara we derive from pregnant woman the law of hiding — why regarding pregnant woman she does not need bedikah initially but in hiding requires bedikah? And Beit Yosef's distinction is strained — for from where does the Tur have this? And Taz seif kaf alef wrote: regarding fear there is concern at that time she needs her husband fear departs from her heart and she will see — and therefore she needs bedikah; and his words are not understood in Rama's words that imply without relations she need not bedikah at all like pregnant woman — and this is not mentioned in Rama's words at all. And it is simple Tur's language does not imply it deals with forbidden to have relations; and therefore his reasoning appears simple: ab initio she needs bedikah all veset time, perhaps through some thought or distraction in other matters her fear departs from her almost a moment — and if so she will see then; and therefore ab initio she needs bedikah all veset duration. But if veset passed and she did not check — since it is derabbanan we are not concerned and she is permitted. But still: from where do we have this Tur reasoning? And it appears he holds in the Gemara "she requires in her veset days" regarding pregnant woman — since her veset is deoraita she requires bedikah, since it says language "requires bedikah" — if so this too was doubtful to him whether requires ab initio bedikah; and so Ran in his chiddushim. And it is difficult: if so why does it require for one who holds veset deoraita whom we do not rule — let it require even for one who holds veset derabbanan ab initio bedikah? And it ends: this knew for one who holds veset derabbanan in pregnant woman's days she does not need bedikah at all — only asks for one who holds veset deoraita; and he holds this depends on that: if her veset time passed and she is pure — learn pregnant woman's days push off that presumption of regular time even though that presumption is complete and Torah matter; and if so even ab initio she does not need bedikah. Unlike if veset passed and she did not check — impure, simple she needs bedikah. And it is difficult: if so that which is simple from hiding she checked — pure, why does it mention veset passed and she did not check — behold we say this depends on that, even ab initio she does not need bedikah? And further why did the Gemara need to bring proof veset deoraita from precision "there is fright," etc., and did not prove from simple that ab initio bedikah is required — learn veset deoraita; for if derabbanan in derabbanan it is not logical at all that bedikah is required? And it ends: there is distinction — for matter of initial bedikah in hiding there is another reason not relevant to pregnant woman; and as above, perhaps distraction from fear and confusion — and Tur's words are compelled from Gemara (Avraham Karelitz).
