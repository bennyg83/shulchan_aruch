Noda BiYehudah argues that the statement that menstrual cycles are rabbinic means only that after the expected moment we do not presume she certainly saw blood, because her presumption of purity remains. But the obligation to abstain near the expected time is Torah law, out of concern that she may see in the future. Chatam Sofer notes that the conceptual distinction is already found in Ra'ah, though the practical scope differs: before the precise expected moment the abstention may be rabbinic, while afterward the concern that relations may induce bleeding can be Torah-level.
