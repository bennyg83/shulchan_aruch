Seif 3. Maharshal in siman 4 brought Beit Yosef's challenge on the Tur who ruled one in hiding needs ab initio a bedikah, and in the Gemara we derive from a pregnant woman the law of hiding; and regarding a pregnant woman he ruled she does not need one. And in my humble opinion it is possible to say thus: in truth one must understand how she is permitted after veset, although the presumption that fright removes blood — nevertheless there is against it the presumption of regular time. But one can say: even if there is a presumption against it, the presumption remains that a woman stands in presumption of purity. Or one can say: although there is the presumption of regular time, nevertheless there is at least a minority who do not see at their time, and a minority is close to the presumption that blood is removed and it exists for the majority — and majority and presumption, the majority is preferable. However there is still a question: as we do not rely on presumption where there is to clarify — it is obvious we do not rely on majority where presumption is strong regarding Torah matter over majority, as explained in general rules of Shach — and if so here there is to clarify through bedikah, and therefore she needs bedikah. But all this is in Torah prohibition; but in rabbinic prohibition we certainly rely on majority or the above presumption and need not check and clarify. And according to what I said regarding a pregnant woman, she is well permitted without bedikah, since there is no sensation here and it is only rabbinic doubt — we rely on majority or presumption. But regarding one in a pan, there is concern for Torah prohibition, lest she actually saw with sensation and due to confusion and fear was unaware she sensed — and in Torah doubt we do not rely on majority where there is to clarify; and therefore the Tur requires bedikah. Unlike a pregnant woman — for if there were sensation she would certainly check herself. And the Gemara that compares hiding to pregnant woman actually refers to the one who holds veset is deoraita — and I have one law.
