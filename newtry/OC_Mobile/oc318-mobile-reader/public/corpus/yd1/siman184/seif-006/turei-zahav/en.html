Strictly speaking, however, it would have been proper to forbid all three days, just as in seif 5 she is forbidden during the portion of the day for as long as the flow continued. Indeed, this is the Ra'avad's opinion, as cited by the Tur, in both cases. The author of the Shulchan Aruch, however, ruled leniently here after the first onah passed. His reason is that when the flow continues only briefly into the day, that continuation is regarded as part of the veset itself. This is not so here, where it is impossible for the entire three-day flow to be one continuous sighting; rather, it is additional blood. Once the principal sighting has ceased, the additional blood is also deemed to have ceased. Many explain the words of the Shulchan Aruch this way, but it requires further study from where he derived this distinction.
