And she did not see — permitted. See Shach seif kaf yod dalet what he wrote to distinguish between prolonged sighting for several days and one sighting from before sunrise until after sunrise. However Rav Taz in seif kaf tet challenged from where to distinguish; and it is clear his intent is not Shulchan Arukh, for Shulchan Arukh did not innovate from himself but only as Rosh wrote; and his challenge is on Rosh himself — from where to distinguish with such reasoning without clear proof? And perhaps Raavad and Rosh were precise from that which is taught: she was accustomed to see with sunrise — Rabbi Yosi holds she is forbidden only with sunrise, and Rabbi Yehuda says the entire day is hers. And it is difficult: behold the main dispute of Rabbi Yosi and Rabbi Yehuda is at night — Rabbi Yosi permits and Rabbi Yehuda forbids — why does Rabbi Yosi say the entire day is hers? And for precision it comes: behold at night it is forbidden — Rabbi Yosi should have said the entire night is forbidden. And why does he use language of what is permitted — for every day even Rabbi Yosi admits? Rather certainly Rabbi Yosi permits only beginning of veset; and therefore he says she was seeing with sunrise — meaning it begins with sunrise, but continues into sunrise. And as Rosh and Raavad wrote: she does not escape — if sighting lasted so long, she is forbidden only with sunrise — meaning also near sunrise is forbidden, for "with" means near; but at sunrise itself permitted. And Rabbi Yehuda argues and says also at sunrise forbidden — only the entire day is hers, meaning after sunrise; but at sunrise forbidden — and this is the entire day's intent after sunrise, and all sunrise is forbidden since she saw then too; and the dispute is stated well regarding "with sunrise." And Rabbi Yosi says the entire day — to exclude sunrise itself. And so Rashi: the entire day is hers from after sunrise — investigate. And this cannot be said perhaps also for one who sees for days — for then still Rosh's challenge remains: why during niddah days she need not bedikah, lest sighting continued and you know to separate all duration of sighting — and one can say. And later authorities' distinction is compelled in Gemara — again proof in Raavad to Sefer Baal HaNefesh; and he actually has version "the entire night is hers" and explains "hers" to forbid; and both challenged Razah in his hasagot and said "hers" means to permit. But for Raavad otherwise it requires investigation: Raavad's opinion also in continuation of onah for days she is forbidden all onah time for days; and as Rosh wrote explicitly in the name of Raavad — unlike Rosh who must be precise that he has version "the entire day is hers" as above; and it appears Raavad has the reverse version and also explains in it "hers" to forbid from the above precision.
