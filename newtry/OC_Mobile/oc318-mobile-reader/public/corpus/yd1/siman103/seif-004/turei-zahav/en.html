And so for honey. Beit Yosef wondered: the world cooks meat in honey and it is very praiseworthy; answered: through spices and onions it imparts beneficial taste; but meat and honey alone would spoil; Darkei Moshe challenged from Mordechai second chapter of Beitzah: nevelah piece in honey would not be for spoilage — he wrote: therefore if nevelah piece found in honey, all honey forbidden, etc.; Maharach answered: Mordechai did not forbid honey because of beneficial taste of nevelah, but when issur and heter equal, forbidden even in noten ta'am lifgam per Rashba — when equal, forbidden even in noten ta'am lifgam — end; wonder, for there they recognized the nevelah piece and issur was only from taste; above seif 1 and Rashba: if issur from taste not substance, even if issur greater than heter, permitted in noten ta'am lifgam — therefore we need Rama's distinction brought afterward — Maharil responsum distinguishes honey itself — Mordechai deals with — from liquid made from honey; this is primary, Eshel Tzvi's conclusion; I do not know why lenient here with great loss even in honey itself — apparently no leniency against Mordechai.
