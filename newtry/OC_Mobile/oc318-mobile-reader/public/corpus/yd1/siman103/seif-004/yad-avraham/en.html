(There in hagahah) Even though issur noten ta'am lifgam and food permitted — pot forbidden. Lest issur impart beneficial taste in dish to be cooked. I wrote above seif 1: specifically when issur b'ein praiseworthy or slightly spoiled; if completely spoiled or no taste at all — even pot permitted as I proved there with clear proofs in section 4; Machatzit HaShekel general rule 80: in all earthenware cannot be kashered — one may l'chatchila use after twenty-four hours with great loss as b'dieved; so I brought siman 10 in the name of Radbaz; not like Ran and Ritva chapter Kol HaBasar regarding pan — it seems permitted to cook a dish known the first issur will impart spoiling taste — no concern lest cook something beneficial; as siman below — see there; proof from pot not ben yomo — we forbid l'chatchila cooking decree lest ben yomo as Gemara end Avodah Zarah 76 and all poskim — not decree lest cook sharp thing imparting beneficial; we learn they do not fear this; nor b'dieved nullifying issur l'chatchila — noten ta'am bar noten ta'am of permitted since absorbed in heter pot; now noten ta'am lifgam in heter — all noten ta'am lifgam the Torah permitted; see siman 95 similarly; I wrote there seif 2 noten ta'am bar noten ta'am of permitted and noten ta'am lifgam l'chatchila permitted — no decree for sharp thing — proven in many places; b'dieved if cooked and unknown whether first issur spoiling or beneficial — we do not forbid; if beneficial, perhaps sixty against taste absorbed in pot; hagahah wording seems extra — what does it teach except we forbid only if cooked dish known first issur imparts beneficial and certainly no sixty — so Shach wrote no need sixty against all absorbed since spoiling, piece not nevelah — challenge lest issur impart beneficial in dish cooked before cooking issur within twenty-four hours and all absorbed becomes nevelah within twenty-four hours — see siman 98 seif 5 and siman 92 seif 6; as we fear it imparts beneficial in dish to be cooked later; say: as I wrote, from safek not forbidden b'dieved — requires study.
