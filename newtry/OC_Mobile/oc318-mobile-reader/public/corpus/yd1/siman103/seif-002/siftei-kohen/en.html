Even spoils somewhat. But a thing that is not noten ta'am — neither beneficial nor spoiling — is forbidden, as Beit Yosef wrote — see there Hagahot Mordechai chapter 2 of Avodah Zarah; and Beit David general rule 80 law 22 in the name of Maharai — see there; and it seems proof from what we hold there is no taste in gidim, nevertheless dissolved gid body needs sixty against it, as above siman 65 seif 9 and s.k. 62: even though only like wood, Torah forbade it; and so for every issur — even if not noten ta'am, needs sixty against it; so in Yerushalmi chapter 10 of Terumot halachah 6: you said — neither beneficial nor spoiling — forbidden.
Minor issur, etc. Beit David end general rule 80 wrote: do not distinguish dissolved and not — taste is like essence d'oraisa; spoiling taste is not considered taste and is nullified; if so issur body too nullified in majority, etc.
But much issur. And so issur gravy — Tur in the name of Rashba.
Only its taste, etc. Rashba wrote: it seems even if it fell into little heter and they recognized it, throw it away and the gravy is permitted, for there is only its taste, not substance; and even though we estimate in all of it because of what exudes, we do not know now that there is no benefit in its taste — it does not forbid the mixture at all; like heter cooked in pot not ben yomo; the reason in all these: we certainly know its emission is not in all of it; only whatever imparts beneficial taste we are stringent, fearing perhaps it emitted until it imparts taste in its mixture; therefore whatever a sample shows no taste — we permit; when issur spoils we do not estimate in all, only in taste, and taste is as if none since it spoils — end.
When stated, etc. This is a matter by itself as in Gemara and poskim — clear; and see siman 134 further on noten ta'am lifgam laws.
Or spoils and finally beneficial — forbidden. Meaning: after it imparted beneficial taste; but before beneficial taste, permitted even though it might impart beneficial taste later, since now it spoils — like the pot of salt below s.k. 12; so Or Zarua general rule 32 law 5.
Second dish forbidden. For it is like something that initially spoiled and finally imparted beneficial taste; see siman 134 s.k. 29.
Sixty against the first issur. But one does not need sixty against all absorbed in the pot, for since it was spoiling, piece did not become nevelah, as above siman 98 s.k. 17.
Pot not forbidden. For it is noten ta'am bar noten ta'am — issur in spoon, spoon in dish, dish in pot — still all heter since it spoils; so Beit David general rule 80 law 23 in the name of Or Zarua.
