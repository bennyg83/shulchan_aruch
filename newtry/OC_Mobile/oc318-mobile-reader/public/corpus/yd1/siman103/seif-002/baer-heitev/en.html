Somewhat. But a thing that is not noten ta'am — neither beneficial nor spoiling — is forbidden; proof from gid hanasheh siman 65 seif 9 — Shach.
Minor. Beit David wrote: one should not distinguish between dissolved and not dissolved, for issur substance is also nullified in majority.
Major. And so issur gravy.
When does this apply. This is a matter by itself, and refers to the beginning of the siman.
It imparted beneficial taste. Shach wrote: specifically after it imparted beneficial taste it is forbidden; but before it imparted beneficial taste it is permitted even though it might impart beneficial taste later, since now it spoils; so too Or Zarua (and so Gra and Peri Chadash disagreed; they wrote even before beneficial taste it is forbidden; and if the matter is safek whether beneficial or spoiling, we are stringent and estimate with sixty. Peri Chadash).
The issur. Shach wrote: but one does not need sixty against all absorbed in the pot, for since it was spoiling, piece does not become nevelah, as above siman 98.
The pot. For it is noten ta'am bar noten ta'am — issur in the spoon, spoon in the dish, dish in the pot — and still all is heter since it spoils; Taz challenged: why stir the spoon twice for noten ta'am lifgam — even if first dipped in beneficial taste and then in spoiling taste and then cooked in the pot, it is permitted, for noten ta'am bar noten ta'am; he concludes for the halachah even one noten ta'am lifgam is permitted.
