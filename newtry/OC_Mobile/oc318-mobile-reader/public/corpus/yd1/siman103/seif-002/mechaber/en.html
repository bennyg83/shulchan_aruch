This spoil need not spoil completely until it is inedible; rather, even if it spoils somewhat it does not forbid its mixture. And some say this is specifically when a small issur mixed with much heter; but much issur into little heter, and even half and half — we do not say noten ta'am lifgam is permitted until it spoils completely so it is unfit for human food. And if there is no issur substance, only its taste alone — even much issur and little heter is permitted if it spoils somewhat. And some are inclined (meaning: a basis to be stringent and forbid) to say: if the issur enlarged beyond the heter's measure until it imparts more beneficial taste when eaten in its full quantity than it spoils by impairing taste — forbidden until unfit for human food. When is this stated — that it spoils from beginning to end; but if it imparted beneficial taste and finally spoils, or spoils and finally imparts beneficial taste — forbidden: {Rama: some say: even though the issur is noten ta'am lifgam and the food is permitted, nevertheless the pot is forbidden; and if they cooked in it within twenty-four hours food in which the first issur imparts beneficial taste, the later food is forbidden if there was not sixty against the first issur; but if they stirred the initial food with a spoon and then inserted the spoon into later food that also spoils, the pot is not forbidden; and so for a thing that has no taste at all, such as a cauldron in which they melt honey — even though bee legs are there, the cauldron is not forbidden, and all similar cases (there in Arukh).}
