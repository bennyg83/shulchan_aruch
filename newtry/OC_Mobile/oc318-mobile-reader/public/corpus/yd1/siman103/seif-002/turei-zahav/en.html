Even a large amount of prohibition, etc. For example, where a large prohibited item fell into a little sauce and he threw the prohibited item out of the sauce. It is explained in the Tur that brine of a prohibited item is considered like the item itself; therefore, if visible brine of a prohibition mixed into permitted food, it must either impair completely or there must be a majority of permitted food. So too wrote Issur VeHeter HaArukh.
And there is one who is hesitant, etc. Meaning, he disagrees with what was written initially, that if the body of the prohibition is there and is nullified by majority, slight impairment helps. This opinion holds that even so it is forbidden, for he benefits from the body of the prohibition by having more to eat, and the benefit from the increased food caused by the prohibition is greater than the slight damage it causes in taste. It is as though there were no majority, and it must impair completely like neveilah.
When is this said, that it impairs, etc. This refers to the beginning of the siman. The Tur explained: where it initially improved and afterward impaired, such as vinegar of yayin nesech that fell into cold grits and improved them, then he heated them and it impaired them; end quote. Consequently, the explanation of where it initially impaired and afterward improved is the reverse: it fell into boiling food and he cooled it. This is forbidden all the more so than where it improved and then impaired. What the Tur wrote at the beginning of the siman, that it impairs its mixture more, such as vinegar of yayin nesech that fell into grits, and it is permitted, means where it fell into boiling grits and he eats them while they are hot. So too in the Levush. But the Perishah wrote that if it fell into boiling grits and he cooled them it is permitted based on what the Tur wrote at the beginning of the siman, and this is very astonishing.
It too impairs; the pot was not forbidden. Since the dish is permitted, the pot is also permitted, for it is nat bar nat of permitted taste: here too the spoon gives impaired taste to the dish, and the dish to the pot, and the taste still impairs. This is the reason given in Darkei Moshe in the name of Issur VeHeter, klal 32. I wonder why the Rama states here that the spoon was first forbidden through an impaired prohibition, namely from the initial food that had an impaired prohibition in it. Even if there had initially been a full prohibition in the spoon, and he inserted it into a pot containing a dish that this prohibition in the spoon impairs, the pot would be nat bar nat and would not forbid what is cooked in it afterward, even if that prohibition in the spoon gives beneficial taste to what is cooked afterward. The spoon gave impaired taste to the dish, the dish gave impaired taste to the pot, and the pot that will later give beneficial taste to what is cooked afterward is still nat bar nat of permitted taste, since the taste of the prohibition initially came by way of impairment. This is explicit in Issur VeHeter, klal 32: if a utensil absorbed from that prohibition which gives detrimental taste fell into a pot, such as a spoon forbidden from milk that fell into boiling wine, the pot is permitted even if the wine does not contain sixty against the utensil, for since the food is permitted, the dish has no name of prohibition and nat bar nat of permitted taste applies properly; end quote. From this this gloss was copied. Wine and food are one matter, for the food there was wine, and milk impairs wine, as explained earlier there, and so the Rama wrote nearby. This requires study: why did the Rama change it unnecessarily and write twice "to impairment"? In Levush it is written: but if he stirred the initial food with a prohibited spoon and afterward inserted the spoon into a second food which it also impairs, the second pot is not forbidden, because there are many transfers of taste; end quote. This is even more difficult than the Rama's language here. Regarding the law, certainly it is permitted even with one detrimental transfer of taste, as I wrote explicitly in the name of Issur VeHeter, and the words of the copyists require further resolution. It is further written in Issur VeHeter there: if a dish into which an impaired prohibition fell then fell into another pot, and that dish contained the visible prohibition in a beneficial way, the second pot requires only sixty against the prohibition in the pot, for we do not say the first pot became neveilah, since it was permitted; end quote of Darkei Moshe.
