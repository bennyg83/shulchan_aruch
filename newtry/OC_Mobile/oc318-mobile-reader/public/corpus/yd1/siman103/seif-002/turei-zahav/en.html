Even much issur, etc. Such as much issur fell into little gravy and they threw issur from the gravy — clear in Tur that issur gravy is like its body; therefore if issur gravy b'ein mixed into issur, must spoil completely or majority heter; so in Or Zarua.
And some are inclined, etc. Meaning: he disagrees with what was written initially: if issur body present and nullified in majority, slight spoilage helps; this view forbids even so, for one benefits from issur body in having more to eat — one enjoys more from issur quantity than from slight taste impairment — forbidden as if no majority until completely unfit like nevelah.
When stated that it spoils, etc. Refers to beginning of siman; Tur wrote explanation: spoiled and finally beneficial — like libation wine that fell on cold beans, imparted beneficial taste, heated and spoiled them — end; automatically explanation of spoiled and finally beneficial is the reverse — fell into boiling and cooled — forbidden in any case, all the more spoiled and finally beneficial.) What Tur wrote at siman beginning that spoiling the mixture more — like libation wine on beans — permitted, meaning fell on boiling and they eat while boiling; so Levush; except in explanation he wrote boiling and cooling permitted from what Tur wrote at beginning — very surprising.
Also spoils — pot not forbidden. Since the dish is permitted, the pot too is permitted — noten ta'am bar noten ta'am of the permitted; here too the spoon imparts spoiling taste to the dish and dish to pot — taste still spoils; so Beit David in the name of Or Zarua general rule 32. I wonder why Rama wrote here the spoon was first forbidden from spoiling issur dish — even if full issur in spoon first and inserted into pot where that issur in spoon spoils it — pot is noten ta'am bar noten ta'am and does not forbid what is cooked afterward, even if that issur in spoon imparts beneficial taste — spoon gave spoiling taste to dish, dish spoiling to pot, pot will give beneficial in what cooks later — noten ta'am bar noten ta'am of permitted since taste of issur came initially through spoilage — clear in Or Zarua general rule 32. From this the hagahah was copied in wine and food — one case, food had wine and milk spoiling in wine as explained above; Rama nearby similarly. Why Rama changed unnecessarily — wrote "spoiling" — Levush wrote: but if they stirred first dish with issur spoon and then inserted into second spoiling dish — second pot not forbidden — many noten ta'am — more confused than Rama here; for halachah certainly permitted even one noten ta'am lifgam as I wrote in Or Zarua explicitly; translators' words need study; also Or Zarua: dish fell spoiling issur, fell into another pot, in that dish issur was b'ein [beneficial] — only sixty in second pot against issur in first pot, not "first pot became nevelah" since it permits — end; Beit David.
