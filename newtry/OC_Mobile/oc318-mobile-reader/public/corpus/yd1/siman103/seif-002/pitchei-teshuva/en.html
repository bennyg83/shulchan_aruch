**Even though.** See responsum Kenesset Yechezkel siman 21 — and from his words we learn that his view is: if they cooked in an issur pot a dish to which the issur imparts ta'am l'shevach, and afterward they cooked in it a dish to which that first issur is noten ta'am lifgam, the second dish is forbidden — for since the first dish was already forbidden and became nevelah, therefore now when he cooked the second dish in it, the first dish imparted ta'am to the second, since the first dish does not spoil the second; and see responsum Givat Shaul siman 18, who challenged him and concluded that the second dish is permitted, for that which was forbidden is not more severe than that which forbids — see there at length. Also in my manuscript responsa I likewise challenged the aforementioned Kenesset Yechezkel and likewise concluded to permit; and see further responsum Teshu'at Chen siman 23, what he wrote on this.
**The pot is forbidden.** See responsum Noda BiYehudah, Yoreh De'ah volume, siman 31, regarding wine cooked in a ben-yomo meat vessel and stirred with a ben-yomo dairy spoon; and he wrote that the wine should be permitted — even though what is written in the hagahah below in seif 4, that meat or milk in wine is lifgam, meaning milk in that form — nevertheless, since the milk itself does not forbid the wine except by reason that meat mixed with it, and it is basar b'chalav, and if so the meat is pogem; but the vessels one should forbid according to us, for we are stringent in siman 95 seif 3 in the hagahah that we do not reckon in such a case noten ta'am bar noten ta'am, for we are concerned lest the dairy and meat infusions mixed without the medium of water — and if so the vessels are forbidden, as written here in the hagahah — see there; and see Siftei Da'at who challenged him for forbidding the vessels [and see Sefer Beit Yehudah, what he wrote on this at length].
**[Against the first issur.** See Baer Heitev; and see Sefer Levushei Serad siman 47, who wrote: it appears to me that one need not [have] sixty against the entire issur, but only according to calculation — for even in the first food some of the issur remains, as Taz wrote in siman 92 s.k. 24, for here too it did not become nevelah since it is pogem, etc. And he further wrote: but if the issur is noten ta'am l'shevach in the first food, afterward one needs sixty against the entire pot (see Shach at the beginning of siman 93). However, if there is some safek — for example it spilled and we do not know whether there was sixty against the pot, and it is known at any rate that there was sixty against the issur, even if not against all of it but according to calculation; or that that issur was a safek issur and was not known until after it fell into the first dish, and afterward in the second dish there is sixty against the issur according to calculation — it is permitted; and it is possible that this requires specifically that it not become known until the second dish was cooked — and the matter requires settling — see there for the reason.]
