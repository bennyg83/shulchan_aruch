Although. See Responsa Kenesset Yechezkel, siman 21. From his words we learn that his view is that if one cooked in a prohibited pot a dish in which the prohibition gives a beneficial taste, and afterward cooked in it a dish in which the first prohibition gives a detrimental taste, the second dish is forbidden. Since the initial food had already become forbidden and became like neveilah, when he now cooked the second dish in it, the initial food gave taste into the second dish, because the initial food does not impair the second. See the responsum of my relative Givat Shaul, siman 18, who challenged him and concluded that the second dish is permitted, for that which became forbidden is not more severe than the forbidding item; see there at length. In my own manuscript responsa I also challenged the above Responsa Kenesset Yechezkel and likewise concluded to permit. See also Responsa Teshuat Chen, siman 23, what he wrote on this.
