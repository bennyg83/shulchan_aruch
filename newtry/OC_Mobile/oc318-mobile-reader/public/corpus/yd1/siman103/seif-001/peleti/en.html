Peri Chadash challenged Mishneh Lemelech who holds even beriah spoiled in themselves if rotted unfit for human food — exempt; Peri Chadash: nevertheless forbidden from being spoiled from the outset. We see in Shevuot 24: oath not to eat nevelot and tereifot — he ate and is liable; Gemara challenges: issur cannot come on issur; R' Yochanan answers: in a inclusive prohibition of permitted things; meaning half measure; Gemara challenges for R' Yochanan where do we find "yes and no"; answers: with putrid nevelah — thus was Rif's text there s.v. "but," etc.; we too: oath "my wife shall not benefit if I ate today" — he ate nevelot, tereifot, sheratzim, crawlers — his wife is forbidden; Tosafot explained: some sheratzim are less fit than putrid nevelah, nevertheless oath applies — learn even such is included in eating. Challenge: in inclusive sheratzim and crawlers putrid ones are included — how challenge R' Yochanan why not say like R' Elazar half measure — answer: if he swore to eat, not applicable; only with oath not to eat. Challenge for R' Yochanan in inclusive sheratzim and crawlers that spoil in themselves — what "yes" is there — all forbidden from outset; nevertheless Torah forbade putrid too — clear per Mishneh Lemelech that even putrid is "spoiled" and nevertheless forbidden l'chatchila; where need, be lenient. One answer in Tosafot: even oath to annul a mitzvah without specifying the issur name — inclusive oath applies; some sheratzim not spoiled in themselves, permitted in their spoilage; oath "I will eat" sheratzim applies even if spoiled in themselves since he did not specify issur name — if applies to one, applies to other; if pure fowl not beriah at all, why stricter on embryos — end. With this Tosafot's question on Gemara is resolved: R' Yochanan should say like R' Elazar half measure — forbidden d'oraisa; challenge: in sheratzim and crawlers that spoil in themselves what "yes" — all forbidden from outset; Gemara intends to challenge R' Yochanan to gain like R' Elazar — not to profit "yes and no" in inclusive; if oath not to eat, more reasonable he swore on heter — if he swore to eat issur — but per above oath "sheratzim" applies even spoiled in themselves since no specific issur name — if applies to one applies to other; if pure fowl not beriah, why stricter on embryos — end. Truly per Rif and Ran agreeing with Rambam no proof for all above; nevertheless from Tosafot we learn on the clear.
