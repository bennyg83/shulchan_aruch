On the bed that a Torah scroll is upon it. In Rama's responsum siman 34 he learns from this that one may not sit on a chest that contains sacred books; and afterward he wrote: however one must analyze — perhaps since they are placed in the chest, behold they are in their own domain and are considered separated; nevertheless it seems one should not distinguish here, for we say in chapter Mi SheMeto regarding tefillin: even though he placed them vessel within vessel — forbidden to place them under his head, and we do not say the vessel interposes; and the same regarding sitting on them. And afterward he left this requires study. And it seems to me this should be learned from that which is in chapter B'chol Ma'aravin (Eruvin 30): taught — one who enters the land of the nations, etc., that he becomes impure in a tent in a sedan-chair, chest, and tower — Rabbi impure and R' Yosi b'R' Yehudah pure; what do they dispute? One holds a portable tent is called a tent (Rashi: a portable tent interposes before tumah); and one holds it is not called a tent and does not interpose. And Rashi explained: we deal with a large one that holds forty se'ah liquid which are kors dry; but if small it is a vessel and receives tumah and does not interpose before tumah — behold before us that in our arks that do not hold kors dry, plainly it does not interpose and is not called by itself; and even in a large one that left the category of vessel — behold tannaim dispute it and the halacha is like Rabbi, as the tanna of the mishnah there taught plainly that one may not place the eruv in a cemetery for a kohen, since he cannot go there even via sedan-chair, chest, and tower — and this is like Rabbi. And it seems further that here it does not help even if one wants to place a tallit on the ark and sit on it — even though we say nearby seif 8 regarding an implement it helps — nevertheless here plainly it does not help, since even a large chest is not called a tent and is like a movable item, certainly it does not help with a movable item, even if he placed the book in many movables one upon another and sits on them — for they said in seif 3 one may not place it in a sack and ride on it — implies even many sacks one upon another, for it has no remedy except to place it against his heart. And the reason is that sitting on them is great disgrace; but if attached to the wall with nails — permitted to sit there. So it seems according to my humble opinion clearly.
