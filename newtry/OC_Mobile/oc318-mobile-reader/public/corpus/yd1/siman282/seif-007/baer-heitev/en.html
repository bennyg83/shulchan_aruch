And it is proper to be stringent that the place of the sefer Torah be higher than him by ten handbreadths, and not less than three handbreadths — for anything less than that is not considered a height at all — Beit Yosef; and it appears that he speaks of a measure of piety, but by strict law a height of one handbreadth suffices for all opinions. Books. And R' Menachem wrote: specifically not during the time of the study hall; but during the time of the study hall when the space is cramped for the students, it is permitted to sit level with the books and commentaries; and so R' Meshullam of Béziers practiced; but when not in the place of the study hall, he was careful that the book be placed upon something else, however small.
And the author of Sefer Orchot Chayyim wrote: it appears one should not be lenient in this matter so as not to appear to belittle the honor of the books — end quote, Beit Yosef.
