That he should stand. If he stands merely to rest, his status is like one who is walking, since he is not standing in a settled manner. Kesef Mishnah writes this. See above, siman 242, seif 18.
"One who hears." Shach writes that this reading appears in all the books, but the custom is not so. Beit Yosef likewise writes that the custom is not to stand before them, and no one has ever protested that people must stand before a Chumash. It therefore appears that the text should read: "Some are stringent and say that one who hears the sound of the person carrying," etc.; this refers to what follows. Levush has this reading. Taz writes that Beit Yosef cites R' Manoach that for this reason the custom is to place bells on the Sefer Torah. In my humble opinion this is not correct, because if so such a Sefer Torah could not be taken out on Shabbos, since the bells produce a sound. It therefore appears that such bells should not be made, and this is why Rama did not cite the practice. We have already seen a synagogue where this was done, and people objected and removed them.
