He shall stand. If he stands to rest, his law is as one walking since he is not standing permanently — so writes Kesef Mishneh; and see above siman 242 seif 18.
Who hears. The Shach writes that this is the reading in all books, but the practice is not so. The Beit Yosef likewise writes that the custom is not to stand before them, and no one has ever been heard opening his mouth to challenge that people should have to stand before a Chumash. It appears that the text should read: some are stringent and say that one who hears the sound of the carrier, etc.; and this refers below. So the Levush reads. The Taz writes that in the Beit Yosef he brings in the name of R. Manoach that for this reason they were accustomed to make bells, but in my humble opinion this does not appear correct, for if so one may not take out such a Torah scroll on Shabbat because it makes sound. Therefore it appears to me that one should not make those bells, and for this reason the Rama did not bring it. We have already seen such a case in a synagogue, and they protested and removed them. Thus his language.
