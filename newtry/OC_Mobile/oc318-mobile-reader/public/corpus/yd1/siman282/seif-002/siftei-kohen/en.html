And if he stands to rest, his law is as one walking, since he is not standing permanently — so writes Kesef Mishneh, and Bach cites it.
(One who hears, etc. So it is in all the books, but we do not practice thus — and so Beit Yosef wrote: they practiced not to stand before them, and never was heard one opening his mouth to protest; and it is brought in Darkei Moshe. And it seems that the correct text is: some are stringent and say "one who hears the sound of one carrying" etc., and below it refers to this; and so is the version in the Ateret Zekenim.
[The voice of one carrying, etc. And likewise the voice of the bells on a sefer Torah — Ateret Zekenim; and this is from the words of R' Menachem cited in Beit Yosef.
