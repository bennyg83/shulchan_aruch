It seems to me that those people who, when studying from a book and wishing to raise the book [to a convenient height], reach out and take another book and place it underneath the one being studied — there is a prohibition in this of disrespect for sacred books, since these books are placed solely to serve as a stand, not for their intended purpose; and one may not use sacred objects for one's own benefit in a disrespectful manner. And do not say this resembles what is written in Orach Chaim siman 315 regarding using three books as a tent-support structure, one on each end and one on top — for there it concerns sukkah, which is itself a mitzvah, and the prohibition of using sacred objects does not apply when used for another mitzvah; also, in that case all three books are used as walls of the structure and are not merely a stand for one of them; but here [in our case] the lower book is used solely as an inanimate support for the upper one, which is a clear disrespect — and this should be prohibited.
In the responsum of Maharil siman 114 it is brought from Agudah, chapter HaKometz: one should not make Torah scroll accessories [tashmishot kedushah] from cloth or material previously used for ordinary mundane purposes; and he distinguishes between accessories of holiness themselves [tashmishot kedushah] — such as a Torah scroll covering directly — and accessories of accessories [tashmishot d'tashmishot] — such as the case that holds the covering — for accessories of accessories have no inherent holiness from Torah law. Once a garment has been made into an accessory of kedushah, one may not demote it to a lesser level of holiness; one can only elevate, never lower. And a garment used for mundane purposes may not be used as an accessory of kedushah without first being purified and set apart for that purpose. Therefore one must be careful in this matter.
