It appears to me that there is a prohibition in the practice of those who, while studying from one sefer and wishing to raise it higher, reach for another sefer and place it beneath the sefer from which they are learning. This is disrespectful, and worse than resting a sefer on one's lap, discussed in seif 7, because here he degrades the lower sefer by using it for a function that could be performed by a piece of wood or stone. At first glance one might bring proof to permit this from Tur and Shulchan Aruch in Orach Chayyim, siman 315, which state that on Shabbos one may place one sefer on one side, another sefer on the other side, and a third sefer on top of them, and this is not considered making a tent because he has no need for the space beneath. It appears to me that there the case is where he needs all three sefarim for study and wants all of them prepared before him; the novelty is only that there is no prohibition of making a tent. Here, however, he uses the sefer merely as one would use a wooden object, and this is a serious degradation whenever he brings it from one place to another for that purpose, even on the same table. If the lower sefer is already lying there, however, it is certainly permitted to place the second one upon it.
Responsa Maharil, siman 114, cites Sefer Agudah in chapter HaKometz that one may not make accessories for a Sefer Torah from a garment previously used by an ordinary person. This is derived from the exposition: just as the altar had not been used by an ordinary person, so too the wood, etc. Those who act otherwise should be stopped. It nevertheless appears that this applies specifically to an item that itself directly serves holiness, such as those listed in Orach Chayyim, siman 154. An accessory of an accessory, such as those listed there, may be made from an ordinary garment. If, however, the garment has already been made into a direct accessory of holiness, it is forbidden afterward to reduce it to an accessory of an accessory, because we do not descend in sanctity. Although we are stringent initially not to make a direct sacred accessory from an ordinary garment, we do not therefore say leniently that the sanctity never took hold after it was made. This appears correct.
