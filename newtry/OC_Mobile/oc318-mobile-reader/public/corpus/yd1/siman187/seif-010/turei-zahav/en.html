There, the concern depends specifically on that night, and afterward she is permitted. Therefore one must say that the case is one in which there were intervening permitted acts of relations. Such intervening permitted relations are effective in the ruling discussed below, but not here, because there they serve only to establish that the first blood after childbirth resulted from the childbirth, as explained there. That reasoning does not apply to the night of immersion. See what I write in seif 11.
"Or she saw after every childbirth three times," etc. Rama's words cannot be reconciled unless we assume there is a textual error. First let us set out the view of Maharar Yochanan in Hagahos Shaarei Dura, from whom this ruling is taken. He discusses a woman who saw blood once after childbirth and then had relations without seeing blood, and this repeated after three childbirths. He rules that because she established a pattern of permitted relations in between, we may say that the single bleeding after each childbirth resulted from the sides having been injured by childbirth, and it is like a wound, as above in seif 5. This is the essence of his words. It follows that if no pattern of permitted relations was established in between, but there were only three acts of relations in total, namely one act after each childbirth at which she saw blood, and she then had no relations at all until the next childbirth, it is obvious that she becomes forbidden. We do not say that each bleeding was caused by an injury to the sides merely because each occurred soon after childbirth. All the more so, if after one childbirth she had relations and saw blood three consecutive times, she is forbidden until she is examined with a tube, because the second and third acts of relations are even more severe, as they were not immediately after childbirth. It further follows that if this occurred three times after childbirth, meaning that after each childbirth she saw blood three consecutive times and thereby became forbidden as explained, and she then transgressed and had relations after those three sightings following each childbirth, such later relations do not help her. Certainly an examination with a tube would not help to undo the established pattern for future childbirths. A tube examination, or an act of relations without seeing blood, is effective only where there is uncertainty whether that particular act of relations is prohibited; then, once examination shows that the blood came from the sides, she is permitted, as above. Here, however, even if we say that she was repaired and permitted afterward, that would be only until the fourth childbirth. Once she established three times that after every childbirth she sees blood three consecutive times, we cannot attribute the blood to injury of the sides, and even if after each set of three she transgressed and had relations without seeing blood, this does not permit her after the fourth childbirth. She has established a prohibited pattern three times after childbirth and will presumably again see three times after the fourth childbirth. There is no room to say that permitted relations intervened, because after each childbirth she saw three consecutive times, and now she is again after childbirth, analogous to the night of immersion discussed above. Only if after a childbirth she transgressed and had relations when prohibited and did not see blood does that help, for it removes the presumption that she sees after childbirth. All this appears clear and simple, and Rama's rulings themselves point in this direction. The difficulty is his phrase, "or she saw after every childbirth three times," which implies three consecutive times. Otherwise, why does he afterward say, "all this is if she already transgressed and had relations"? Why call it a transgression if her relations were permitted because she had not seen three consecutive times after any one childbirth? Necessarily he means that she saw three consecutive times after each childbirth, and his statement "if she transgressed," etc., refers to that. If so, why does he then conclude, "but if she saw three consecutive times after childbirth, we do not attribute it to the childbirth"? The first clause also dealt with consecutive sightings. Levush inserted a word and wrote, "but initially, if she saw three consecutive times," etc. He means that Rama teaches that we only find intervening relations without blood when she transgressed and did so, but in truth it was not permitted. This gloss is incorrect. If that were the point, Rama should simply have written, "but in truth she is forbidden." Why mention here that we do not attribute the blood to childbirth? Why repeat "if she saw three consecutive times" when the preceding clause likewise dealt with that? And why did he not write the word "consecutive" in the principal ruling? Most difficult of all, according to this interpretation it would imply that if she saw three consecutive times after each childbirth, then because she later transgressed and had relations without seeing blood between one childbirth and the next, she becomes permitted even after the fourth childbirth. Heaven forbid saying this. She established three sets of prohibited relations after childbirth without any intervening established pattern of permitted relations before the prohibition took effect. Maharar Yochanan permitted only where she saw once after each childbirth, so the permitted relations between those separate episodes interrupted the acts one seeks to combine into a prohibited pattern. Here, by contrast, after every childbirth there are already three consecutive prohibited acts of relations, and she is forbidden even before the next childbirth. The theory that the sides were injured does not help, because the prohibited pattern was already established. Her condition after childbirth is then like the night of immersion described above. Therefore, after the fourth childbirth she is forbidden. What Rama writes at the end, that where examination is required, if she transgressed and had relations it can help, refers to transgressing and having relations after the fourth childbirth, as he explicitly writes in Darkei Moshe. Relations without blood before the fourth childbirth do not help, as explained. This is clear to anyone who understands the matter. It therefore appears that there is a textual error and the words should read, "and all this is if she already had relations," without the word "transgressed." Rama's words then correspond exactly to Maharar Yochanan. His phrase, "or she saw after every childbirth three times," would mean three nonconsecutive times, with permitted acts of relations between them. He says that if she already had relations, meaning permitted relations, we do not say that she established a pattern of seeing after childbirth, but attribute the blood to injury of the sides. He can then properly conclude, "but if she saw three consecutive times, even after one childbirth, she requires a tube examination." If she later transgresses and has relations without seeing blood, that helps only for future relations until the fourth childbirth, meaning after the third childbirth she may continue until the fourth. Once the fourth childbirth occurs, however, she is forbidden even though she had relations in between without seeing blood, because this woman's pattern is specifically to see after childbirth, and it is a veset. My father-in-law, of blessed memory, also wrote that Rama's gloss in the first clause concerns nonconsecutive sightings, but I do not know why he did not point out that the phrase "if she transgressed" is then inappropriate, since there was no transgression. Thus the text must be emended as I have stated, and the principal law is now fully explained, with God's help. Rama's further statement, "Nevertheless, wherever," etc., means as follows. Here too, if she saw three times, namely once after each childbirth, but no intervening pattern of permitted relations was established, she is like any woman who saw blood because of relations three consecutive times. If she later transgressed and had relations without seeing blood, that helps so that she need no longer be concerned. If, however, permitted relations had been established between the childbirths, there is no concern in the first place. Likewise, if after each of three childbirths she saw blood three times and after the fourth childbirth she transgressed and had relations without seeing blood, that helps. But relations without blood between the earlier childbirths do not help, because she has established a veset tied to childbirth and requires a corrective test specifically after the fourth childbirth. Similarly, for any woman who saw blood because of relations three times and therefore requires a tube examination, as written in siman 186, all the more so if she transgressed and had relations without seeing blood it helps her. This is Rama's reasoning in Darkei Moshe, and it is correct.
