Of lead. Responsa Mishnat Binyamin, at the end of siman 49, writes that the same applies to other kinds of metal, which are also proper, and that when they said “of lead” this was only to exclude wood; end quote. The Shach writes: “It appears to me that the same applies to iron, where it does not scratch”; end quote. I do not know whence he derived this, for in Responsa Mishnat Binyamin it is explicit that all kinds of metal are proper, except for wood, which scratches. Even iron can be made smooth in a way that does not scratch. Wood is different, because even if it is made smooth, its opening still cannot be continuous with its interior and it scratches, unlike metal, iron, and the like, whose opening can be continuous with the inside. This is also implied in Maadanei Melekh, p. 298b; see there. Nevertheless, it appears to me that where it is possible to use lead, it is better to perform the bedika specifically with a tube of lead, for the Talmud and the poskim did not mention lead specifically for nothing. The reason appears to me to be that metal draws and cuts, as stated in Shabbat 134a; perhaps there it does not refer to lead, but to other kinds of metal, like an ordinary knife, and it is clear. And she inserts it into that place. The Rambam writes in chapter 4 of Hilkhot Issurei Bi’ah, law 22, that at first she inserts the tube as far as she can, and afterward she inserts the applicator inside the tube and presses it until etc. It appears to me that he says this precisely, and so it is implied in the Beit Yosef. The reason is that if she were to insert the applicator immediately without the tube, or even together with the tube at one time, then if it emerged soiled with blood we would not know whether that blood came from the source, for even if it came from the sides, the head of the applicator would certainly become soiled as it was inserted. Therefore she first inserts the tube alone and afterward inserts the applicator; if blood is found on it, it is certainly from the source and not from the sides, since the tube separates. See Responsa Emunat Shmuel siman 37, who writes in the name of one sage that at first he checked without a tube, using a wad and a stick of the thickness and length of an organ, perhaps the blood would be from the sides, in which case she would no longer need to be checked with a tube; and if it was found on the head of the wad, the examination would not help as a stringency, and he then checked again with a tube. He writes that this seemed upright and proper to him. But in my humble opinion this is an unnecessary examination, for if it is from the sides, the head of the applicator will certainly become soiled, and why trouble her for nothing, as I wrote. Moreover, one should not multiply examinations so much, lest through this the woman be physically harmed by the handling and examination. For this reason they permit her to a second and third husband without an examination, and do not require her to examine herself to leave the doubt, for whenever it is possible to permit without examining her, we do so; and the Beit Yosef writes similarly in siman 196. Also, in the Talmud and all the poskim this examination is not mentioned. It is also possible that the wad will fall from it as it is inserted into that narrow and cramped place, unlike when it is inserted within the tube. Therefore it appears to me that one should not add to the examination. And if not. This means whether blood was found on the sides of the tube or no blood was found at all, nevertheless she is permitted even to the third husband. Shach.
And even nowadays one may rely on the examination. Responsa Mishnat Binyamin siman 40 writes that she need insert it only as far as she can, estimating for herself according to her judgment that she has reached the place where the organ reaches. The woman herself is certainly trusted regarding this, and there is no need at all to force the tube into a narrow and cramped place. We need not be concerned that perhaps she did not insert it to the measure of intercourse, for the Torah was not given to ministering angels. He further writes that the tube should be made according to the measure of an average organ. Responsa Emunat Shmuel siman 57 writes likewise: “according to the measure of an average organ, for the Torah was not given to ministering angels, even if it is thicker or thinner than the organ”; end quote.
