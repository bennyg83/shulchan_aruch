(Taz s.k. 10) He elaborated. He elaborated in challenging the Rama, and his language is exceedingly confused. In truth, there is no difficulty at all. One who studies the rulings of Mahara'i will see clearly that, at first, in the case of a woman who examines the folds and crevices, he permits her even though she does not know that her wound discharges blood. His reason is that, when she touches that particular place during the examination, she feels some pain, whereas in the other folds and crevices she feels no pain at all. This proves that the blood came from the wound. According to this reason, even a woman who has no fixed veset is pure. Therefore, in se'if 7, the Shulchan Aruch states without qualification that the blood may be attributed to the wound on that side, etc., and it does not say that this applies only to a woman who has a fixed veset when she sees outside the time of her veset. Mahara'i then added another reason for permitting her. Even if one were to say that the pain is not proof that the blood came from the wound, since she has a veset and she sees outside the time of her veset, she may be permitted in accordance with the Mordechai. He explains that, although Rashi disagrees with the Mordechai, the Mordechai's view may nevertheless be joined to the other grounds for leniency. That is to say, when she has a wound, even if she does not know whether it discharges blood, she is pure. Accordingly, the words of the Rama are correct. Even without this, there is no difficulty. Even were one to maintain that Mahara'i does not allow blood to be attributed to such a wound, that would be only with respect to establishing her as pure, for in that respect he did not wish to rely on the Mordechai alone. However, we do not establish her as one who sees blood because of intercourse when she sees outside the time of her veset and has a wound, even though she does not know whether the wound discharges blood. I distinguished between these matters in the Shach, s.k. 20 and 24; see there. This resolves everything that the Taz challenged against the Rama, including his challenge from siman 191. In any event, there is no difficulty from siman 191, in accordance with what I wrote in the Shach there, s.k. 7; see there and examine it carefully. It further appears that there is no difficulty at all from siman 191. Certainly, where examination is impossible, Mahari Segal did not say that she is impure. He required an examination only where it is possible to clarify the matter. There, an examination is possible, as Mahari Weil wrote in a responsum, cited by the Taz himself in siman 191, letter 3, concerning the instruction that she insert clean absorbent material, etc. A woman has two openings. One, through which urine exits, is lower and close to the exterior. The other, through which menstrual blood exits, is higher and extends deeply into the body. It is therefore possible for her to insert clean absorbent material into the deeper opening, urinate, and then determine whether blood is found on the material. In that case, the wound is where she experiences pain during urination. Here, however, the wound is in the source. Whether the blood comes from the source or from the wound, blood will always be found on the material. Examination with a tube is likewise inapplicable here, since she has a wound in the source. Mahari Segal's refusal to be lenient without examination was also for the reason he himself gave, namely, that she had no clearly established wound. Although Mahari Weil held that pain during urination is stronger evidence than the presence of a wound, Mahari Segal did not agree. This also removes the Taz's earlier challenge against the Rama from siman 191. There the Rama first cited the view that it appears permissible in every circumstance, even for a woman who has no fixed veset. That is the view of Mahari Weil, who held that pain during urination is stronger evidence than the presence of a wound. He then cited the view of Mahari Segal, who held that it is weaker than a wound; see there. The Taz also wrote: “It is furthermore difficult, for in the Darchei Moshe, etc., and this is contrary to what is evident to anyone who studies the glosses to Shaarei Dura, etc.” This is incorrect in light of what I have written. Mahari Segal required examination only for the reason he stated, that in the present case she had no clearly established wound. When she does have a clearly established wound, even if she does not know whether it discharges blood, she may attribute the blood to it when she sees outside the time of her veset, as the Darchei Moshe wrote in his name. The Taz's assumption that the Mordechai disagrees with the Rashba is also incorrect, as I wrote in the Shach, s.k. 24.
