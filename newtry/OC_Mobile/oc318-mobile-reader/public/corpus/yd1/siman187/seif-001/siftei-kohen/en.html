Immediately, within the time required, etc. That is, if she saw blood immediately within this interval, although one could say that she saw it only after relations, nevertheless, since her husband would have been liable to a provisional guilt-offering, she is considered one who sees blood because of relations. After this interval, however, she is pure. Although one could say that she had seen earlier, she is no longer included among those who see blood because of relations. This is implied by the authorities. But if she examined herself immediately after relations and found nothing, and afterward saw blood, even within this interval, she is not considered one who sees blood because of relations. All this is straightforward. My revered teacher, the Gaon, of blessed memory, likewise explained it at length in a responsum; see there. I add that this is also explicitly implied by the words of the Raavad in Baalei HaNefesh, cited by Beis Yosef: “A woman who sees blood because of relations, etc., is forbidden to have relations, provided that she examined herself within the time needed to extend her hand beneath the cushion,” that is, within the interval in which her husband would be liable to a provisional guilt-offering. “Afterward, however, it is like the twenty-four-hour period of a menstruant, when she does not render the man who had relations with her impure and she is not established as one who sees blood because of relations,” end quote. This explicitly shows that although she had not yet examined herself at all, once the interval needed to extend her hand had elapsed, she is not established as one who sees blood because of relations. Furthermore, he says that it is like the twenty-four-hour period of a menstruant, and there the case is one in which she had not yet examined herself, for if she had examined herself she would not convey impurity retroactively for twenty-four hours, but only from one examination to the next. Accordingly, for us, who rule that whenever the blood is found close to relations she is considered one who sees blood because of relations, as the Rama wrote, this applies only when it is definitely known that she saw blood close to relations. But if she examined herself a substantial time after relations and found blood, although it is possible that she saw during relations, she is permitted. All the more so, a woman who on three occasions, in the morning after the night of her immersion, arose from bed and found a large quantity of blood on her garment, is permitted. My revered teacher, the Gaon, of blessed memory, further ruled in that responsum that even if this woman has relations once or twice more and then finds blood immediately at the time of relations, she remains permitted with respect to the earlier occasions. We do not say that it has now been revealed retroactively that the earlier blood also appeared during relations. Rather, the earlier days fall away, and the reckoning begins from this point onward. All this is clear. I wrote it only to dispel the reader's possible impression that the Rama disagreed with the responsum of my revered teacher, the Gaon, of blessed memory, on this matter. It is obvious that wherever she is not considered one who sees blood because of relations, she remains permitted even if this occurred many times. This is also the view of the later authorities. Maharam of Lublin likewise wrote in responsum 22 that there is no difference whether she had relations three times or forty or fifty times; see there.
