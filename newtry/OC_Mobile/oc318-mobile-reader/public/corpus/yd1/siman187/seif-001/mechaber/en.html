The laws of a woman who sees blood because of tashmish; it contains 14 se’ifim. A woman who saw blood because of tashmish immediately — within the time it takes for her to extend her hand beneath the pillow or beneath the cushion, take an ed to check with, and wipe herself — if she had tashmish 3 times, and in all 3 consecutive times she saw blood, (and all the more so if she found blood 3 times on his ed) (Mordechai; and so wrote Beit Yosef in the name of Terumas HaDeshen and Sefer Mitzvos Gadol; and so is proven in the Talmud), she is forbidden to have tashmish with this husband; rather, she should be divorced and marry another. If she married another and saw blood because of tashmish 3 consecutive times, she is forbidden to have tashmish also with that husband; rather, she should be divorced and marry a third. And if also with the third she saw blood because of tashmish 3 consecutive times, she should not marry another; rather, she is forbidden to all, until she checks.
{Rama: And some say that we are not expert in what is called “because of tashmish,” for we are not expert in the measure mentioned; therefore, anyone who sees 3 times close to tashmish is, for us, called “because of tashmish,” and she becomes forbidden to her husband (Beit Yosef in the name of Raavad, who wrote so in “some say”). And these 3 times must be consecutive; but if they were not consecutive, she is not forbidden to her husband (Beit Yosef in the name of Rambam; and so wrote Rashba, siman 1239; and Mahari”v 53; and so wrote the Mechaber below). And there is no difference between whether she saw 3 times immediately when she married, or whether she became impaired afterward and saw 3 times (Mordechai at the beginning of this perek; and in a responsum of Rashba, siman 1239; and in Tosafos). And all this is only when she saw close to tashmish; but if she did not see close to tashmish, she is not forbidden to her husband, and she is permitted to him after her taharah always (Beit Yosef in the name of Sefer HaTerumah and Sefer Mitzvos Gadol; and it is obvious in the Talmud and poskim), and her law is like one who has no veset.}
