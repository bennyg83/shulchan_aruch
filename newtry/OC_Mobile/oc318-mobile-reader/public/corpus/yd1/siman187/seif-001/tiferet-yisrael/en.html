And behold, at first I could not understand his holy words, where he wrote that the Minchat Yaakov's question refers to the Gemara's question, "let us establish it near her veset," and that it concerns entry. This necessarily cannot be like the Mahartz, for the Gemara in Niddah later remains difficult: why is she exempt from a korban? Yet the Gemara answers that she felt, and perhaps it was the sensation of the organ; however one explains it, it still speaks of sensation, like the Mahartz. Moreover, the Gemara wants to establish the mishnah as referring to withdrawal, and Rashi and Tosafot explain that they told him, "she became impure," which is like the Mahartz. It must indeed be so, for otherwise how would he know at all? Although the Gemara rejects this, it does not reject it for that reason, and at first glance this requires study. It is even more difficult because he himself sensed this and wrote "understand this," because the case is near her veset, whereas during her veset she is pure; this implies it refers back above, and the Gemara should have explained it. I do not know why this is forced, for he himself later rejected the Shev Yaakov on this basis, that near her veset the Mordechai agrees. What he wrote that the Gemara should have explained it is also difficult, for the Gemara holds that if it is within her veset it really refers to withdrawal, and necessarily where she told him, for otherwise how would he know, as Rashi explains. However, the explanation of his words appears to be as follows. First one must consider the Mahartz's distinction. At first glance it is already stated in Mahari Isserlein, brought by the Beit Yosef in seif 7, that if she felt the chamber open she is certainly impure, but with a woman who sees because of relations we say perhaps it was the sensation of the organ, even though at other times she does not see without sensation, and it is counted a Torah doubt. This seems like the Mahartz, that there is a distinction whether she feels the chamber opening. But in truth the distinction is difficult to understand. What difference is there, during relations, whether she feels the makor open or not? Even if she says she felt that, it may still be attributed to the sensation of intercourse, and if she sees blood after relations without sensation there is also a doubt that she may have felt during relations and thought it was the sensation of the organ. This is the Gemara's answer in Niddah: she felt, but perhaps it was the sensation of the organ. According to the explanation of my grandfather in siman 183, the doubt in the Gemara can be understood both ways. Therefore even if she told him she became impure, there is still a Torah doubt: perhaps the sensation was from intercourse and she mistakenly thought it was a blood sensation, while the blood was from the sides because of pressure of intercourse, according to the Mordechai, who gives strength to the presumption that the period comes at its time. This is also the meaning of the Terumat HaDeshen. When he writes that if she feels her makor open she is certainly impure, that means where it cannot be attributed to another cause; but during relations we attribute her sensation to intercourse. This is why he writes that we say it is the sensation of the organ. If not so, then when the Gemara wanted to establish the case as not near her veset and as referring to withdrawal, meaning where they told him, it would be difficult: even if no blood was found, according to the Terumat HaDeshen, since she felt the opening of the makor she is always impure and would even be liable to a korban. Rather, since there is doubt that perhaps the sensation came from intercourse, there is only a Torah doubt. Therefore I do not understand the Mahartz's distinction; after careful analysis there is no distinction, for there is always a Torah-level doubtful sensation. This also must be how to understand the words of my grandfather here, despite the pressure in his language and the Minchat Yaakov's answer to this question. Thus this still requires study.
And now it appears to me that the explanation of my grandfather the gaon, z"l, is this: he understood that the Minchat Yaakov's question refers to the Gemara's statement, "let us establish it near her veset," and that it concerns entry. The Gemara therefore held that when it is not near her veset, he is not liable for entry because he was an ones. This is in fact the answer: even if it is not near her veset, it refers to withdrawal, meaning that for entry he was an ones, as Rashi and Tosafot explain there. On this the Minchat Yaakov properly asked: why call him an ones? She should be pure. One cannot answer like the Mahartz that the case is where they told him she became definitely impure, that her makor opened and she became niddah, because then why afterward is she exempt from a korban, as Tosafot indeed asked and left difficult? My grandfather already answered in siman 183 that here she said she felt the makor open, but it remains only a Torah doubt, and there is no proof that she is certainly niddah; therefore she is exempt from a korban, and Tosafot's question is not difficult. However, one cannot explain like the Mahartz that the whole liability is because of her sensation, since she says, "I feel that I am becoming niddah," and he holds that if she says she certainly feels her makor opening then she is certainly believed without doubt, to the point that even the Mordechai agrees we do not attribute it to the sides. For then Tosafot's question remains in place. Necessarily, even according to the Mahartz, the case here cannot be that. This, in my humble opinion, is the meaning of my grandfather's words. When he wrote "understand this," he meant that the Gemara holds that even though not at the time of her veset she may in truth be completely pure, the Gemara did not explicitly mention whether he is an ones or completely pure; this is not so, because it refers back to what was said explicitly, that he is an ones. Also, the Gemara should have explained if she were entirely pure, so one not err against the Mordechai's view and think that the blood is still attributed to the makor, only that he is an ones. This is how to explain his holy words.
And in truth, were I not afraid to speak against the great later authorities, I would answer simply. The Mordechai's entire permission rests on the presumption that the period comes at its time. But if we saw that she changed her veset and did not see at her fixed time, the Mordechai would certainly agree that one can no longer rely on that presumption to attribute the blood caused by pressure of intercourse against the majority that blood comes from the makor. Therefore one may say that the Gemara speaks of "not at the time of her veset" and yet he was an ones: this woman always had a fixed veset, but once, before the intercourse, she had uprooted it and did not see at her fixed time, and had not yet seen at all. Regarding entry he was still an ones, for she is like a woman who has a veset, and a veset is uprooted only after three times. Nevertheless, even the Mordechai agrees that since she had already uprooted it once and is prone to see, we attribute this blood during relations to blood from the makor; perhaps today the veset will be fixed. He cannot say it is certainly blood from the sides because the period comes at its time, since her veset time already passed and she did not see, and that presumption was weakened. This also answers why the husband is liable for withdrawal with a living organ even though she says she feels the opening of the makor. If it were simply not her time to see, the husband might think the presumption that the period comes at its time shows she is mistaken and that it is only the sensation of the organ, as the Gemara says above. But if she already uprooted it once and had not yet seen, he is certainly obligated to withdraw, for she says she feels, she is prone to see, intercourse causes blood to flow, and where else should the blood go? It is obvious he must separate; this is worse than near her veset, and the Mordechai agrees she is impure. One may further say that she then saw again two or three times on that day, so it became revealed retroactively that she fixed a veset on that day and it was entirely makor blood, and the Mordechai agrees. Or one may say that she normally sees at the beginning of the day and he had relations the preceding night; then the Mordechai agrees that we attribute the mishap to the already impaired status, as the Perishah explains the Shulchan Aruch in this siman, seif 4, that if she had relations near her veset we attribute it to her veset. Then she is permitted to have relations, and it is properly called not near her veset, and it is clear.
