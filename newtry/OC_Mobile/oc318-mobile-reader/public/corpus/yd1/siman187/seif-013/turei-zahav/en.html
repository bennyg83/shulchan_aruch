One who has relations with a virgin. The Beit Yosef derived this law from the Tosefta, which states: All of them require examination with a tube, except for a virgin, whose blood is pure. This requires analysis, for the Tosefta specifically says “a virgin.” Perhaps it means only as long as she retains the halachic status of a virgin. This would follow what is taught at the beginning of the chapter Tinokes, that each woman is governed according to her own law. If she has not reached the age at which she is expected to see menstrual blood, her blood is attributed to the hymenal wound until the wound heals. According to Shmuel, this means as long as saliva remains present in the mouth, meaning as long as she continues to see because of relations. The Rambam likewise rules in accordance with Shmuel in chapter 5 of Hilchos Issurei Biah. If she has reached the age at which she is expected to see, the allowance extends for four nights. If she had already seen menstrual blood while in her father's home before marriage, it extends only through the first act of marital relations, and no longer. After that she no longer has the halachic status of a virgin for this purpose. Why, then, is the Beit Yosef lenient regarding all virgins? The Beit Yosef further brings proof from the teaching in the chapter Tinokes that she is permitted until the wound heals. He writes that although according to our practice all of these women are permitted only for the first act of marital relations, nevertheless, regarding the question whether to establish her as one who sees blood because of relations, we presume that the blood is hymenal blood, since most women do not see blood because of relations. He concludes, however: “Yet I am afraid to permit her unless there are indications demonstrating that it is hymenal blood.” It appears that his expression of fear is based upon the difficulty I mentioned, and he therefore requires indications demonstrating that it is hymenal blood, namely, that she experiences at least some pain during relations. Maharam of Padua likewise wrote that if she experiences no pain at all during relations, she is like all other women. The Rama follows his ruling here. My father-in-law, of blessed memory, wrote that one should not accept this stringency, since it has no proof. The Gemara says that the allowance continues until the wound heals, which according to Shmuel means as long as she sees because of relations. We have already explained, however, that this is no proof regarding a woman who has reached the age at which she is expected to see menstrual blood. All the more so, if she had already seen blood in her father's home before marriage, her allowance even under the law of the Mishnah lasts only for the first act of marital relations. Thereafter her hymen is considered spent. From where, then, do we know that she should still be treated as a virgin when there are no indications supporting this? The Rambam too, in chapter 5 of Hilchos Issurei Biah, writes that where a woman sees blood because of relations we attribute it to her virginity specifically in the case where her law is that the allowance continues until the wound heals.
