I have not arrived at the intent of Rema's ruling and toward which decisors he is following. In the Talmud, chapter Tinokot, it says: "a woman is believed to say I have a wound in my womb from which blood comes" — and this is what the Shulchan Arukh wrote in seif 6. Beit Yosef cited a dispute in interpretation: Rashba explains that even if she does not actually feel the blood dripping from the wound — since her eyes cannot see between her wounds and she can only know by examination with a tube, etc. — rather the Talmud means: she herself feels the wound, and is believed to say "I have a wound there from which blood comes, which I attribute to my wound" — etc. But Mordechai and Hagahot Maimonit hold that we require her to know with certainty that she has a wound producing blood — and then it is permitted even not at the time of her period. And in Responsa of Maharai siman 46, cited by Beit Yosef at the end of this siman, he wrote permissively for a woman who examines in creases and folds and only produces blood in one spot on the sides — and this is what the Shulchan Arukh transcribed in seif 7: when she has a fixed period, she has this leniency as long as her period time has not arrived; but in any other case, he did not permit even not at the time of her period, since according to Mordechai we require certainty that her wound produces blood — and that woman did not know this with certainty. And in Hagahot Shaarei Dura, Maharai of Segal's responsum about a woman who saw blood after urinating is transcribed, cited by Rema in siman 191 — and in the conclusion Maharai of Segal wrote: "but in our case she does not have a clearly identified wound — nevertheless, between period and period it seems one can rely on the above leniences" — in that responsum he was lenient even not at the time of her period when she does not feel that blood comes from the wound, attributing it to makkah blood for those who hold periods are rabbinic — end of quote. See that he was lenient only through the specific leniences he mentioned with the examination noted there — and it seems he did not wish to be lenient otherwise. And I have two difficulties with Rema's ruling. First: since the Shulchan Arukh wrote in seif 7 "if every time she examines," etc. — which is Maharai's ruling — implying that unless clearly identified in one of the sides we are stringent unless she knows with certainty that her wound produces blood, and Maharai spoke of a case with a fixed period: Rema should have written regarding this that we do not rule accordingly per his ruling — but instead he just wrote a blanket leniency for all cases where she has a wound and it is not the time of her period. And a second difficulty: since Rema saw the view of Maharai, Beit Yosef, Maharai Weil in his responsum siman 25, and Maharai of Segal — all requiring specifically examination — from where did he derive leniency against them? And furthermore, in Darkei Moshe he first transcribed Maharai's ruling, then wrote: "and so wrote Hagahot Shaarei Dura in the name of Maharai of Segal: where she has a period we attribute to the wound not at the time of her period even if she does not know her wound produces blood" — but this contradicts the text of Hagahot Shaarei Dura we transcribed, which required specifically the above-mentioned leniences with examination. Therefore, since we hold periods are rabbinic as stated in siman 184 and in many places, we can also be lenient here without any examination with a clean cloth even when she does not feel blood coming from her wound — and this is what Rema wrote here: to be lenient where she has a fixed period and not at her period time. And this is contrary to Maharai's view. But there is a further contradiction: in siman 191 Rema himself wrote in accordance with Maharai and Maharai of Segal — to be stringent for a woman who sees blood after urinating, requiring specifically examination with a clean cloth; but not without it; whereas here he is lenient not at the time of her period in all cases even if she does not know her wound produces blood. And one cannot say the law of a woman with a wound is superior to the law of one who finds blood after urinating — for Maharai Weil explicitly wrote in his responsum 25: "it seems to me this case is superior to a woman who has a wound in that place, since when she is in pain during urinating it demonstrates the matter that blood comes with the urine" — meaning the case of blood in urine is more lenient than a wound; and if so, Rema's words contradict each other — in the stricter case he is lenient without any examination, and in the lighter case he requires specifically examination. And my father-in-law of blessed memory wrote that one should rule according to this gloss even though it is contrary to Maharai's view, since Rashba is lenient in all cases. But I am puzzled — what do we do with the words of Maharai, Hagahot Shaarei Dura, Maharai Weil, Beit Yosef in seif 7, and Rema's own words in siman 191 — for all these reasons one should be stringent not to permit a woman who has a wound and does not know her wound produces blood, except in the manner the Shulchan Arukh wrote in seif 7. And it further seems to me: if at the time of the pain she finds blood, the matter demonstrates that it comes from there — and therefore it is permitted; and also after the pain ceases, if she finds blood, it seems the blood that was present during the pain has oozed out, as Rema wrote in siman 191. And furthermore, it seems to me: if she has a wound such that other women typically see blood from such a wound — even if she herself does not know — one may attribute it to the wound. So it seems to me.
