(Seif 14) A woman who has wounds and sores that prevent immersion must leave her husband. Beit Hillel writes that this applied only before the enactment of Rabbeinu Gershom. Now that his ban against divorcing a woman against her will has spread, she may not be divorced against her will even in this case. Beit Hillel follows his own view in Even HaEzer 79 regarding a wife who suffers a prolonged illness, where the husband otherwise could say, “Here is your get and ketubah.” He maintains that today he may not do so against her will. According to the Beit Shmuel there, however, even today he may divorce her against her will, and the Chelkat Mechokek agrees. The same should apply here. A woman whose wounds prevent immersion is no better than one with a prolonged illness. Moreover, because he cannot fulfill procreation and would be forced to remain without a wife, perhaps all agree that Rabbeinu Gershom never enacted his ban for such a case involving a mitzvah. See Even HaEzer simanim 1 and 119.
