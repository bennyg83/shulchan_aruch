A woman who has wounds and sores that prevent her from immersing must leave her husband. The Beit Hillel writes that this applied specifically before the enactment of Rabbeinu Gershom. Now that Rabbeinu Gershom's ban against divorcing a woman against her will has become accepted, she may not be divorced against her will even in this case. It appears that the Beit Hillel follows his own view, for he writes similarly in Even HaEzer, siman 79, regarding a wife who became chronically ill, where the husband may say, “Here is your bill of divorce and your ketubah.” He maintains that according to Rabbeinu Gershom's ban, the husband may not say this and divorce her against her will. According to what the Beit Shmuel writes there, however, that even nowadays he may divorce her against her will, and as the Chelkat Mechokek likewise writes there, the same applies here. Where she has wounds and cannot immerse, he may divorce her against her will, for her case is no less serious than chronic illness in Even HaEzer. Moreover, since she cannot immerse, he will be prevented from fulfilling the mitzvah of procreation and will remain without a wife. It is therefore possible that in such a matter of mitzvah everyone agrees that Rabbeinu Gershom did not enact his ban. See Even HaEzer, simanim 1 and 119.
