In that place, etc. The Bach wrote that this does not mean merely anywhere in that region. Rather, it means that she has a wound in the very place from which the blood emerges. Therefore, if the blood emerges from the source, she must know that she has a wound in the source. But if she merely says, “I have a wound,” without specifying further, we do not attribute the blood to that wound. This concludes his words.
We attribute it to the blood of her wound, etc. My revered teacher, the Gaon, of blessed memory, wrote in a responsum that even if she examined herself with a tube and blood was found on the wad, we attribute it to the wound. He brought proofs for this; see there. The Bach likewise wrote this at the end of se'if 7.
And if the blood of her wound, etc. The Maggid Mishneh, Ran, and Rashba wrote that this applies specifically when it has been established that the blood of the wound is different in appearance. Otherwise, we attribute the blood to the wound, because the woman retains a presumptive status of purity. This is also the view of the Rosh, and the later authorities ruled accordingly, unlike the Ramban. It is implied by the words of the Rosh that she need not show the blood to a sage to determine whether it is different, and Beis Yosef and the later authorities wrote likewise.
And all this, etc. See my work, where I proved that the Mordechai, and Maharam's responsa, Prague edition, siman 655, maintain as follows: if a woman has a fixed time at which she sees blood, then outside that time she attributes the blood to her fixed cycle even if she has no wound at all. We say that this blood is pure blood from the sides, produced by the pressure of relations, since the time of her cycle has not yet arrived. I also proved there that Terumas HaDeshen understood the Mordechai this way in his rulings, siman 47. He inferred from the Mordechai by an a fortiori argument that, since the Mordechai maintains that by virtue of the established presumption we say that the menstrual flow comes only at its proper time, and this enables us to attribute the blood to a pure source where we would not otherwise do so, all the more so he maintains that where there is a wound, even if it does not discharge blood, she is pure. In such a case it is possible that even the authorities who disagree with the Mordechai concede, since there is an additional ground for leniency, namely, that she has a wound. The Rama's words are based on this understanding. The Bach understood the plain meaning of the Mordechai differently, namely, that the phrase “she attributes it to her wound” is connected to “she attributes it to her fixed cycle.” He raised many questions and offered many resolutions, but I already refuted all his words at length there; see there. Rather, it is clear that the Mordechai is citing the baraisa, “she attributes it to her wound,” etc. This is also evident from Beis Yosef, Darkei Moshe, the responsa of Maharam of Padua, simanim 9 and 10, and the other later authorities. It is straightforward. Accordingly, it appears that this leniency applies only with respect to remaining with her husband, because in many places the Sages were concerned not to remove a woman from her husband and were lenient due to the risk that she remain unable to remarry. Therefore, we do not establish her as one who sees blood because of relations. Nevertheless, she must count seven clean days, for in this respect one may not rely on the Mordechai. Rashi appears to disagree with him, and the Ramban, Rashba, Rambam, Tur, and all the other authorities interpret “if she has a fixed cycle” to mean that if she had relations close to her cycle, she attributes the blood to her cycle. This implies that if she did not have relations close to her cycle, even if she has a fixed cycle we do not attribute the blood to a pure source. Maharam of Padua likewise wrote in responsum 10: “Regarding your honor's uncertainty whether one may be lenient in accordance with the Mordechai's interpretation of ‘if she has a fixed cycle, she attributes it to her cycle,’ this is a great leniency. Who is strong enough to be lenient against all the other authorities?” end quote. The Rama too was lenient only not to establish her as one who sees blood because of relations, and even this only when she has a wound, although it does not discharge blood. The Bach's words imply that he permits her in every respect, but in my humble opinion the correct ruling is as I have written.
Even though she does not know her wound discharges blood. Meaning even if she does not know at all that this wound customarily discharges blood; and see above s.k. 26.
Likewise, if she does not, etc. The Rama derived this ruling in Darkei Moshe from the words of Shaarei Dura, siman 6: “A woman who has a wound in that place and does not know whether the wound discharges blood, if she senses that the blood comes from the womb, she is impure and cannot attribute it to the wound. If she knows that it came from the wound, she is pure, for she attributes it to the wound. But at the time of her fixed cycle, even if she knows that it came from the wound, she is impure, for otherwise a woman with a wound in that place would never become impure. However, all stains found on her garments are pure, because they are attributed to the wound, even if she does not know whether the wound discharges blood, since we rule leniently regarding stains,” end quote. Darkei Moshe wrote that this is implied by his words. He then wrote that this is likewise implied by Hagahos Maimoniyos, chapter 11 of the Laws of Forbidden Relations, in the name of Sefer HaTerumah, and by the Mordechai cited above, whose words Beis Yosef brought in this siman under the heading beginning “However, Hagahos Maimoniyos wrote,” end quote. In my humble opinion, the Rama understood Shaarei Dura this way because he wrote, “if she senses that the blood comes from the womb, she is impure,” which appears to mean that she is impure only when she knows that it came from the womb, that is, from the source. Otherwise she is pure, even if she does not know that the wound discharges blood. He also apparently understood that the unqualified wording includes both a woman with a fixed cycle and one without a fixed cycle. On this basis he wrote that the same is implied by Hagahos Maimoniyos and the Mordechai cited above, whose words accord with Shaarei Dura. In truth, however, nothing of the kind is implied. Shaarei Dura said, “if she senses that the blood comes from the womb,” only to contrast it with the concluding clause that all her stains are attributed to the wound. Proof of this is that Hagahos Maimoniyos wrote in the name of Sefer HaTerumah: “A woman who has a wound in that place and does not know whether her wound discharges blood, if she examines herself in that place and finds blood, or if she senses that the blood is falling from the womb,” etc., “nevertheless, all her stains are attributed leniently,” end quote. The same wording appears in Sefer HaTerumah, siman 92. Thus, Hagahos Maimoniyos and Sefer HaTerumah explicitly prove the opposite: even if she does not sense the blood falling from the womb, but examined herself in that place and found blood, she is impure if she does not know that her wound discharges blood. This is clear, and it is also the plain implication of the authorities, that even in an unspecified case she must know that her wound discharges blood. Furthermore, the Rama's ruling is difficult for me, for what double doubt exists here? One can immediately say that perhaps it is menstrual blood. A case of this kind is not considered a double doubt at all, as I wrote in the laws of double doubt, rules 11 and 13; see there. I later found that Maharam of Lublin, at the end of responsum 111, wrote regarding the Rama's gloss that his distinctions require examination. Perhaps he intended the objections I have written.
If she has no etc., unless she knows her wound discharges blood. For then even though she has no fixed veset she is not established as seeing blood due to relations — for we attribute to the wound; and also she is pure and need not sit seven clean days since it is within thirty days — so appears the view of the Rabbi.
Unless she knows with certainty her wound discharges blood. Even if she does not now feel that this blood comes from her wound (and likewise a woman who has a veset and found blood not at her veset time — even though for the matter of seeing due to relations it suffices even if she does not know her wound discharges blood when she had relations not at her veset time — for we rely there on Mordechai as explained — nevertheless not at time of relations, if she examined herself and found blood, or when she feels blood falling from the womb — then we require she know her wound discharges blood; it suffices if she knows her wound customarily discharges blood even though she did not now feel this blood comes from the wound): so I proved in my book from words of Tosafot chapter Kol HaYad (Niddah 16a) and from Agur chapter Kol HaYad and chapter Tinokot, and from Hagahot Maimoniyot chapter 11 of Laws of Forbidden Relations in name of Shach and Mordechai, brought by Beit Yosef; and from Shach siman 6; and from responsum Maharil siman 203 copied in Hagahah Shach siman 7; and likewise Derishah; and I proved there that also Rashba in Torat HaBayit agrees — for he only says she need not know she now feels this blood comes from her wound; but she must know her wound discharges blood; and Beit Yosef, Darkei Moshe, and Bach understood that Rashba disagrees.
Nevertheless, at the time of her fixed cycle, etc. The Rama copied this from the authorities cited above, but their discussion is not relevant here. They are discussing blood seen outside the context of relations, with respect to whether she must count seven clean days, as is clear from their words and as I wrote in my work. Regarding a woman who sees blood because of relations, however, it is obvious that blood seen at the time of her fixed cycle does not establish her as one who sees blood because of relations. Although we do not attribute it to the wound, we do attribute it to her fixed cycle. One must therefore say that here too the Rama refers to the general law, namely, that she is impure and must count seven clean days.
She does not attribute it to her wound. This is so even if she knows that her wound discharges blood, for otherwise she would never become impure. But if she presently senses that this blood came from the wound, she is pure even at the time of her fixed cycle. In such a case one cannot argue that otherwise she would never become impure, because she will be impure whenever she does not have that sensation. Furthermore, if this were not so, the same proof would present a difficulty for Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel in the chapter Kol HaYad, Niddah 15a, and likewise according to the Gemara's conclusion there that all agree that fixed cycles are rabbinic. If she is pure even at the time of her fixed cycle, when could she ever be impure? Rather, the Gemara must be discussing a case in which she presently senses that the blood is emerging from the wound. Tosafos, the Agudah there, and all the other authorities wrote likewise, as I explained in my work. Beis Yosef, Darkei Moshe, and the glosses to the Derishah wrote that the Rambam and Rashba disagree. This is incorrect. Although the Rashba explicitly disagrees and permits her even at the time of her fixed cycle, despite her not sensing that the wound is presently discharging blood, the Rambam merely copied the Gemara in the chapter Kol HaYad. Just as Tosafos and the other authorities interpreted the Gemara as referring to a case in which she senses that this blood is presently coming from the wound, the Rambam's words can be interpreted the same way. Only the Rashba explicitly disagrees, and he stands alone against all the authorities cited above.
And stains she attributes to her wound in every case. Even at her veset time; even if she does not know her wound customarily discharges blood — so implies from words of poskim; and see below siman 190 end of siman 11 and siman 196 seif 10 and what is written there.
