If she has no etc., unless she knows her wound discharges blood. For then even though she has no fixed veset she is not established as seeing blood due to relations — for we attribute to the wound; and also she is pure and need not sit seven clean days since it is within thirty days — so appears the view of the Rabbi.
Unless she knows with certainty her wound discharges blood. Even if she does not now feel that this blood comes from her wound (and likewise a woman who has a veset and found blood not at her veset time — even though for the matter of seeing due to relations it suffices even if she does not know her wound discharges blood when she had relations not at her veset time — for we rely there on Mordechai as explained — nevertheless not at time of relations, if she examined herself and found blood, or when she feels blood falling from the womb — then we require she know her wound discharges blood; it suffices if she knows her wound customarily discharges blood even though she did not now feel this blood comes from the wound): so I proved in my book from words of Tosafot chapter Kol HaYad (Niddah 16a) and from Agur chapter Kol HaYad and chapter Tinokot, and from Hagahot Maimoniyot chapter 11 of Laws of Forbidden Relations in name of Shach and Mordechai, brought by Beit Yosef; and from Shach siman 6; and from responsum Maharil siman 203 copied in Hagahah Shach siman 7; and likewise Derishah; and I proved there that also Rashba in Torat HaBayit agrees — for he only says she need not know she now feels this blood comes from her wound; but she must know her wound discharges blood; and Beit Yosef, Darkei Moshe, and Bach understood that Rashba disagrees.
