And it is puzzling — he explained according to the general explanation; nevertheless most poskim agree to Tosafot's view. However, Shach wrote that even not at veset time specifically — where it is known the wound discharges blood, even though she does not feel that from it this blood discharges — on this basis the Rabbi decided here. And all this, etc. — and Taz and Shach: (Extract) And all this, etc. — thus Mordechai: a woman is believed to say, etc. — specifically when she knows blood comes from the wound she attributes to it; but from doubt she does not attribute; but at veset time even if she knows certainly blood comes from the wound she does not attribute to it — for otherwise she would never be tamei; however it is puzzling that she finds on garments pure and attributes to the wound even when she does not know the wound discharges blood — for on stains chazal were lenient because stains are rabbinic; and it implies from his words even if known this blood comes from the wound — tamei at veset time; and so implies from the Rabbi's words; and it is questionable for it is against the Gemara; and further implies from Mordechai's words that even not at veset time she must know this blood comes from the wound; but language of Sefer HaTerumah siman 92: a woman who has a wound in that place and does not know if it discharges blood or not, etc. — tamei and does not attribute to the wound; for it is taught: a woman is believed to say I have a wound in that place from which blood discharges — implies specifically when she knows blood comes from the wound she attributes; but if she doubts she does not attribute; but at veset time even if she knows certainly blood comes from the wound she does not attribute — for otherwise she would never be tamei; however all stains she finds on her garments are pure and she attributes to the wound even when she does not know the wound discharges blood — for on stains chazal were lenient; and an ordinary wound sometimes discharges blood; and Nekudot HaMefaresh copied P.Y.A. briefly — and implies from his words all distinctions are regarding an ordinary wound that discharges, not this specific blood; and it appears there is a scribal error in Mordechai, for all his words are words of Sefer HaTerumah; and so is the Rabbi's language. And what he wrote regarding a woman who has a veset, etc. — even though, etc. — even though it does not imply so from poskim' words — he relied on Mordechai's words who explained what is stated in the Gemara "and if she has a veset she attributes to her veset" — if she has a fixed veset she attributes not at veset time to the wound even if she has no wound at all; and even though halachah is not like his words — nevertheless there is room to join Rashba's view who does not require knowing at all that the wound discharges blood, even at veset time; so too one can say the Rabbi's view; but the Rabbi's reason is that he understood Mordechai's words thus: not at veset time she need not know the wound discharges blood; and thus Mordechai: "and if she has a veset of fixed time that she sees blood — she attributes to her veset that she can say this blood she sees is pure for her veset has not yet arrived; and she attributes to her wound that she can say it is from her wound and is pure if her wound's blood is distinct," etc.; and he explains "and attributes to her wound" refers to the above from it; and so Mahari ibn Ezra understood in siman 47 who further wrote in Mordechai, etc. "and if she has a veset she attributes to her veset" — meaning her veset has not yet arrived; and she attributes to her wound, etc. — evidently he holds habit does not come except in its time — it helps that we attribute to the wound what we would not attribute without this, even though it is not known the wound discharges blood; and as stated.
