The Shach, s.k. 26, writes that if she feels that this blood comes from the wound, she may attribute it to the wound, for the concern that she would never become impure does not apply: if she feels otherwise she will become impure. He further argues that this proof would also be difficult according to Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, and also according to the conclusion there that the dispute concerns whether vesetot are rabbinic. Therefore he decides that if she feels this blood is from the wound, she is pure. He also writes that what the Beit Yosef, Darkei Moshe, and Hagahat Perishah wrote, that the Rambam in chapter 1 of Hilchot Issurei Biah disagrees, is not so, for the Rambam merely copied the language of the Shas. In truth, although his interpretation is forced, for if the Rambam spoke specifically where she now feels it comes from the wound, he should have explained that if she does not feel so she is impure, nevertheless one may say that the Rambam holds there is no distinction at all between whether she says she feels it comes from the wound or not. The Rambam wrote regarding Issurei Biah that even if her whole body trembled she may attribute it to urine; likewise here he may hold that even if she says it certainly came from the makor, we do not heed this, but say she erred, as the Rashba writes: are there eyes in that place to know whether her sensation came from the wound? Therefore he may purify her forever by attributing it to the wound. Still, this is no proof. As to the law itself, although I am unworthy to raise my head against the light of Israel, the gaon Shach, and Heaven forbid that I should wish to overturn his words, especially since the Shach's words are the words of Tosafot on Niddah 16, nevertheless I come only to discuss humbly. In my weak opinion there is room to question this permission. What compelled me, besides the fact that the Rama here rules simply without distinction, is that in siman 188 regarding forms like tiny creatures he rules otherwise; the matter therefore requires explanation.
