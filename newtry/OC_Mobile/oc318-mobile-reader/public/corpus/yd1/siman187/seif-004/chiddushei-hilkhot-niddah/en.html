Near the time of her veset. See the Shach, who writes that this can occur when the husband is departing on a journey. According to Rabbeinu Tam, who forbids relations then, it can occur with a minor who has not yet reached the age at which she normally sees blood. This explanation is unnecessary. We find a comparable case with a progressively shifting veset, for which we rule that she need not be concerned until it has been established three times. Thus, she may have relations near what later proves to have been her veset, and when the third progressively shifted sighting occurs it becomes clear retroactively that the earlier relations took place near her veset. The same applies to an older woman who did not see blood for three intervals and was therefore presumed to have ceased menstruating. She was permitted to have relations, but then saw twice more after intervals of three onot, revealing that her flow had not ceased and that this was instead an established pattern. Nevertheless, I am not satisfied with these explanations. What novelty is taught by saying that relations near her veset do not establish her as one who sees blood due to relations? The entire reason relations are forbidden near a veset is that she is then likely to see because of the veset. Indeed, if blood is found, they are liable to bring an offering and he is not considered compelled by circumstances, because he should have separated when she was likely to see. It is therefore obvious that seeing then does not establish a pattern caused by relations. The explanation of the Perishah is more persuasive. Her veset occurs during the day and they had relations near that day, but not within the actual onah during which separation is required. Although she was not then considered likely to see for purposes of the prohibition on relations, the sighting is nevertheless attributed to her veset so that she is not classified as one who sees blood due to relations. The Minchat Yaakov likewise agrees with the Perishah, though he does not explain the reason. According to what I have written, this follows necessarily from the Gemara. I remain uncertain regarding a woman who has no fixed veset and must be concerned for the average interval. If she had relations near the average interval, is this likewise included in the rule that a woman with a veset may attribute the sighting to her veset? She must separate near the average interval because we treat her as likely to see then. Alternatively, perhaps concern for the average interval is imposed only stringently, but not leniently so as to permit a woman by attributing the blood to it. I have not found the later authorities addressing this. According to the Perishah's approach, even where her established sighting is by day and relations occurred in the adjacent night onah, she may attribute the blood to the veset. It may therefore be that this leniency applies only to a fully established veset, whose presumption is stronger.
