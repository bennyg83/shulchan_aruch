If she engaged in relations close to her expected period, etc. The Beit Yosef cited Rashi: If there is a fixed pattern for this impairment, meaning that she does not always see blood because of relations but only at intervals, she attributes it to her expected period and may engage in relations without examination between one expected period and the next. He further cited the Mordechai as follows: A woman who has a fixed period at which she would see menstrual blood may attribute the blood to her expected period, for she can say that the blood she was seeing was pure, since her expected period had not yet arrived. End quote. These words have no explanation at all. In the Mordechai itself, however, it is written immediately afterward, “and she attributes it to her wound,” etc. The Beit Yosef understood this as an independent statement. In truth, it is all one statement. It means that if she has a wound, then even though the circumstances would not sufficiently justify attributing the blood to it were she not to have a fixed period, nevertheless, now that she has a fixed period we attribute the blood to the wound. This is also stated explicitly in the ruling of R' Yisrael Isserlein cited by the Beit Yosef at the end of this siman; see there.
