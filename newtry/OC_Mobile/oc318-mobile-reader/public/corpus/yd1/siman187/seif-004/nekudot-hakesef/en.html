(Taz s.k. 16) He follows. He follows his preceding position, but it has already been explained that his words are untenable. Gloss. In the Beis Yosef, beginning “And what he said, that this measure, etc.”: Since, when blood is found, we do not render them definitely impure, it appears that the reason is that we may say that the blood came only after intercourse, etc. This is difficult, for even when the blood is found within the time required for her to descend from the bed, we do not render her definitely impure. It is only a doubt, except when it is found immediately, as is evident from the Mishnah and Gemara. In truth, however, their view is that since one is liable to bring an asham talui, this is called seeing blood because of intercourse, as is explicit in the Semag, Raavad, and other poskim. In the Beis Yosef. “Know that the Semag wrote in the following language, etc.,” until the end of the paragraph. It appears to me that, because in the chapter HaBa Al Yevimto the Gemara rules with respect to marriage and lashes in accordance with Rabbi, that two occurrences establish a chazakah, whereas with respect to vesetos and an ox becoming forewarned it rules in accordance with Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, that three occurrences are required, the Semag therefore ruled with respect to marriage that she is permitted only to a second husband. Once she saw blood because of intercourse with the second husband, she is established with respect to marriage. However, with respect to vesetos, meaning with the first husband or the second husband himself, she is not established until three occurrences. This is comparable to a woman presumed to cause the death of her husbands, regarding whom the poskim ruled that she is forbidden to marry a third husband, yet she is entitled to a kesubah from the second, as explained in Even HaEzer, siman 9. Evidently, with respect to monetary matters we do not establish a chazakah until three occurrences, like an ox becoming forewarned, whereas with respect to marriage we establish it after two. This is what Tosafos and the Rosh wrote in the chapter HaBa Al Yevimto, and the Rosh wrote the same at the beginning of the chapter Na'arah SheNispatsah; see there. The other poskim, however, treated these marriages like vesetos, since the matter arises because of a veset. This concludes the gloss.
