"If he transgressed and circumcised," etc., "and they circumcised within eight days," etc. This follows Darkei Moshe's suggestion that Hagahos Maimoniyos and the Rosh need not disagree, for perhaps circumcision within eight days is better than circumcision at night. In my opinion this cannot be said, as is explicit in Menachos at the end of chapter R' Yishmael, 72a, where R' Elazar son of R' Shimon is said to follow the approach of his teacher R' Akiva, etc., and the Gemara argues: if one assumes that harvesting the omer at an improper time is valid, why should it override Shabbos? See the Gemara and Rashi. This also challenges Maharshal, Bach, and Perishah, who write that a circumcision performed at night is valid after the fact, and the Rosh in chapter R' Eliezer D'Milah, citing R' Shimshon, who says that a circumcision within eight days is valid and requires no drawing of covenantal blood. The Gemara there indicates that the mitzvah was not fulfilled: if such circumcision were valid, why should circumcision override Shabbos? Let it be performed on Friday. There may be a strained resolution. R' Shimshon's proof from chapter R' Eliezer D'Milah, Shabbos 137a, that where one mistakenly circumcised on Friday the child whose circumcision was due on Shabbos, Shabbos was not given to be overridden, and therefore no drawing of covenantal blood is required, is not a proof in my humble opinion. One can say that although the mitzvah was not fulfilled, Shabbos was nevertheless not given to be overridden merely for drawing covenantal blood, but only for actual circumcision. In truth, circumcision at night does not fulfill the mitzvah even after the fact, as the Gemara indicates: the very fact that circumcision overrides Shabbos proves that an improper-time circumcision is invalid even after the fact. Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe also say that a responsum of Rashba cited below in siman 264 implies the Rosh's view, but this is not evident there; see there, and see below siman 264, seif katan 6. Rambam's ruling in chapter 7 of Hilchos Temidin UMusafin, halacha 6, concerning the omer, that its proper mitzvah is to be harvested on the night of the sixteenth whether weekday or Shabbos, the entire night is valid, and if harvested by day it is valid, is itself astonishing, because Menachos there explicitly says that since harvesting overrides Shabbos, harvesting by day at the improper time must be invalid. Rebbi, who holds that harvesting by day is valid, holds that it does not override Shabbos. This is explicit throughout the sugya and requires study. Practically, it appears from all that I have written that whether a child was circumcised at night or by day before the eighth day, the mitzvah was not fulfilled and one must again draw covenantal blood. Bach likewise rules that one should do so, though he frames it as a stringency. According to what I have written, this is the primary law.
