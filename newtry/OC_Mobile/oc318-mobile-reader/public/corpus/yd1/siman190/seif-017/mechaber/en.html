She found a ketem above the belt and a ketem below it, and she knows that she did not stretch out — she is tehorah, for I say: just as the upper one came from “elsewhere,” so the lower one came. In what case are these matters said? When the upper one has a gris and more, or more, for then it certainly came from elsewhere, since we cannot attribute it to a louse. But if it does not have a gris and more, we do not attribute it to elsewhere, for perhaps it is the blood of a louse; and if the lower one has a gris and more, that we cannot attribute to a louse, she is tamei.
