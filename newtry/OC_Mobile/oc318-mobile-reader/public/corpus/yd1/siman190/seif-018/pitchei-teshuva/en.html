See responsum Noda BiYehudah YD 52 reason for this; see Chokhmat David who challenged (see from this responsum Chatam Sofer 182); see Noda BiYehudah tinyana 105 who wrote our paper receives stains, not inferior to linen that receives impurity — see there; see responsum Shivat Tzion 39 where a great one challenged Noda BiYehudah from Rambam ch. 2 Laws of Vessels that paper does not receive impurity — reason: new surface comes; he answered distinction between our paper from worn rags or beaten grass which is certainly linen work and brought great proof; but paper they had in earlier generations and still make in India from tree bark and vegetables or tree peel smoothed and prepared — this does not receive impurity and Rambam refers to this; he challenged that one on what he wrote not comparable to linen since new surface comes — that is we do not judge it receives impurity because it was once woven but from what is now linen work — see there. See Siftei Taz who wrote if found on paper not through examination and wiping but she passed over it or sat on it — Beit Yosef ch. 2 Kelim wrote impurity does not apply to paper though he deals with Gemara-era paper from grass unlike our paper from linen; nevertheless no distinction appears since beating and new surface — end quote; likewise Chokhmat Adam klal 113 halachah 8; if they saw Noda BiYehudah and his son the Gaon's responsum they would not write thus (now printed Chatam Sofer vol. 6 s. 81 wrote even our paper does not receive impurity nor stains since once thoroughly beaten and dissolved in water becomes other surface — wool and flax departed; further I say only standing for garment, weaving, ropes and linen; but these papers designated for other uses — if we need wool and flax we say wood work as explained Chullin 129a, etc. — concludes on stains found we go lenient; but for examination of unexamined ed if paper even though for lentil measure has stain law, for this we do not say lenient on paper — see there). See Siftei Taz below seif 15 s.k. 36 who wrote doubt where she used her bed and afterward found stain on ed that does not receive impurity or on dyed garment generally we hold tehorah even though certainly from her body since without sensation she sees; but here rabbinic doubt — say she felt and thought sensation was from bed use — tamei — see there. If she found this stain near urination there is also this doubt — see above siman 183 (see responsum Chemdat Shlomo 20 who brought proof tehorah even in such case — see there). See also Siftei Taz whether on imbecile we attribute if found on thing not receiving impurity or on dyed garment — see there.
