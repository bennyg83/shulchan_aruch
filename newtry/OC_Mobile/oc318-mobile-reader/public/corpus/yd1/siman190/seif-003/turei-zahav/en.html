If she flowed continuously, etc. The Gemara in Niddah states: A young girl who has not yet reached the age at which she is expected to see blood, and who flows throughout all seven days, has only one sighting; if she stops, it is regarded as two sightings. Rav Shimi said that dripping is not regarded as seeing. The Gemara asks: But she is in fact seeing. Rather, say that dripping is not like flowing continuously, but like stopping. The Gemara asks: Does this imply that “flowing” means flowing like a river? Rashi explains that this is a question, for if dripping is not considered flowing, it would follow that the earlier statement referred to flowing like a river; yet it is impossible for her to flow like a spring for seven days and live. The Gemara therefore concludes: Rather, say that dripping is like flowing continuously. According to the conclusion, the original case of flowing throughout all seven days must mean dripping, since flowing like a river for seven days is impossible. It would therefore seem difficult that the Tur and Shulchan Aruch write, “If she flowed continuously or dripped,” since flowing must itself mean dripping. My father-in-law explained that “flowing” means a discharge with interruptions between one flow and another, yet it is nevertheless regarded as one sighting, whereas “dripping” means drop after drop without interruption. He further explained that the later phrase, “But if she stopped briefly,” refers only to dripping; with flowing, even if she stopped briefly and then saw again, repeatedly, it remains one sighting because each discharge was a flow. This requires further study. It is difficult, for the Gemara explicitly says that flowing is permitted and stopping is forbidden, proving that stopping refers to flowing. Moreover, it is difficult to distinguish between an interruption among drops and an interruption among flows. The words of the Tur and Shulchan Aruch are correct. The flowing they mention means actual continuous flowing like a river without interruption. Although the Gemara says that this is impossible, it means that it is impossible throughout all seven days, as Rashi deliberately wrote “all seven days.” The Tur and Shulchan Aruch, however, write “several days,” meaning two or three days, and it is certainly possible for her to survive that. It follows that whenever there is an interruption between sightings, whether between flows or between drops, they are regarded as two sightings. This appears obvious to me.
The blood of three fixed periods. This means three times, each time constituting a separate fixed period.
