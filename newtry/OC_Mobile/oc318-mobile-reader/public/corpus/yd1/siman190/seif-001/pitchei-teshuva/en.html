That she senses. There are three Torah-level sensations. Sidrei Taharah debates whether blood appearing together with abundant white mucus is treated as without sensation because the sensation is attributed to the mucus.
On her clothing. Panim Meirot says the stain decree was stated for a woman's clothing, not a man's garment, unless he used it to wipe immediately after relations. A Tosefta appears to extend impurity to a stain found on another person's garment while lying beside her, raising a serious difficulty.
She sensed. Shev Yaakov permits a stain smaller than a gris found hours after forceful sneezing that caused wetness, attributing the sensation to urine. Later authorities distinguish where only a small amount was felt and where white mucus is habitual.
Her uterus opened. Chokhmat Adam writes that mere bodily trembling without any finding can be attributed to other causes and does not create impurity.
And she examined afterward. Chavot Daat is stringent even when she examined within the onset interval and found nothing. Kreti U'Pleiti and Sidrei Taharah are lenient when the examination was prompt. Chatam Sofer remains uncertain.
And found nothing. If she found only pure-colored discharge, she may attribute the sensation to it. Chavot Daat is stringent unless she knows clearly that no blood was present. Chatam Sofer distinguishes full uterine sensations from merely feeling moisture, and between a soiled unchecked cloth and a garment on which pure discharge is unusual.
She is impure. Torat HaShelamim and Sidrei Taharah exempt pregnant and nursing women who sensed uterine opening but found nothing, attributing it to white or green discharge. Yaavetz goes further and questions the underlying stringency generally. Panim Meirot and Noda BiYehudah discuss cases where a tiny blood point was found.
