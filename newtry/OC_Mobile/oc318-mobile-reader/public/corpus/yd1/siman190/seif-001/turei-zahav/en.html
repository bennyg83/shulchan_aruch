Until she senses that blood has emerged from her flesh. Since the verse states, “in her flesh,” she is not impure until she senses it in her flesh. This means that even if she actually sees blood, she is pure when she did not sense it. So wrote the Maggid Mishneh; I copied his words in siman 192, seif 1.
And the Sages decreed, etc. One might ask: This should be a case of a double doubt, perhaps it emerged from her and perhaps it came from elsewhere; and even if it emerged from her, perhaps it did not come from the source, as we find in siman 187, seif 5. One can answer that since most blood that emerges from a woman who has no wound comes from the source, this is not regarded as a doubt at all. Tosafos wrote similarly in the first chapter of Kesubos, 9b, in explaining the double doubt there. One should not challenge this from the Rama's ruling of a double doubt in siman 187, seif 5, for there she has a wound and it is possible that the blood came from the sides; the double doubt is based upon that possibility. The Kesef Mishneh offered other resolutions.
That she is impure. For I say that a mustard-seed-sized drop of blood emerged and was wiped away or dissolved. The principle that sensation is required by Torah law applies where there is nothing else to which the blood can be attributed. The Terumas HaDeshen states this, as cited by the Beit Yosef.
