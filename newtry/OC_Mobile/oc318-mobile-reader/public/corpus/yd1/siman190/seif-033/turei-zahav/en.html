A crushed louse, etc. The reason is that this area is presumed checked against lice. Therefore, an examination cloth is treated more stringently than a garment. On a garment, we attribute a stain to a louse when it is no larger than a bean plus a little. But this cloth had been checked before she wiped with it, and the Sages knew that there was no louse on it. Therefore the blood certainly came from her body, while the louse came from elsewhere and was crushed while the cloth was against her body.
And the same law applies if she examined herself and placed it in a box, etc. These are the Rashba's words. He does not mean to exclude a case where she left it exposed and blood was later found on it, for the Mishnah plainly rules that blood found on her examination cloth after a delay renders her impure because of doubt. Rather, he contrasts this case with the end of the seif, where she placed it beneath a pillow or cushion, or pressed it against her thigh. His meaning is that if blood is found immediately on a checked cloth she is obviously impure; and even if it is not found until the following day, she is likewise impure when the cloth was meanwhile kept in a box and was not pressed against her thigh. The wording of the Tur, however, is difficult. It says, “and the following day she found blood on it, even the smallest amount, or she placed it in a box,” without explaining where the cloth had been kept in the first case. Even with the Beit Yosef's correction, that it had been left exposed and that the Tur proceeds from the lesser to the greater novelty, the wording remains unsettled, since the first clause does not identify its location and the second cannot naturally say “or she placed it in a box.” The Perishah writes that “or even if she placed it in a box” refers to an unexamined cloth, but this cannot be written, for the Gemara explicitly states that even Rebbe, who is most stringent regarding an unexamined cloth and renders her impure as a niddah, agrees that a bean plus a little is required. Were I not afraid to emend the text, I would say that “the following day” in the first clause is a scribal error, and it should read: “She found blood on it, even the smallest amount; or she placed it in a box and the following day...” This would conform precisely to the Rashba. It would also resolve why the Tur, when discussing an unexamined cloth, writes that blood was found on it the following day but does not mention that it had been kept in a box. The Tur relied on the first clause: with a checked cloth, if blood was found the next day after it had meanwhile been in a box she is impure, whereas if the same happened with an unexamined cloth the Rashba declares her pure.
