The woman who is not presently fit to see blood attributes it to the woman who is fit. This does not include a case where one woman has a fixed period that has not yet arrived, while the other woman's fixed period has arrived. So wrote the Beit Yosef.
If all three were awake, etc. Toras HaBayis HaAroch cites this ruling in the name of the Ra'avad: “The Mishnah's ruling that when blood is found beneath one woman, she and the woman next to her are impure, applies specifically when they were sleeping. In their sleep they press against one another and one enters the other's place. The same applies when they are awake but move from their positions and ordinarily enter within one another's area, such as women grinding. But when they sit or lie awake on a bed, there is no concern that they shifted, for each can know her place; and if she says, ‘I am certain that I did not enter here,’ she is pure.” End quote. I have not understood his reasoning. His words imply that she is pure only when she states with certainty that she did not enter the inner woman's place. Yet such a statement should not be effective. Choshen Mishpat, siman 79, seif 11, rules that if one says, “I lent you a maneh beside a certain pillar,” and the defendant answers, “I never passed that pillar,” but witnesses testify that he did pass it, though they did not see the loan, he is not established as a liar, because a person may make an inaccurate statement about a matter that was not important to him. Here too, perhaps the woman entered the inner area without noticing, since she had no reason to pay attention. One cannot answer that an awake woman does not ordinarily enter her companion's place, for then why must she say, “I am certain that I did not enter”? She should be permitted even if she does not know. It follows that her statement is the basis for permission, and the difficulty remains. It appears that this case is different because the blood was found shortly after they lay down. Within such a brief interval she can reliably know whether she entered her companion's place, especially since doing so is somewhat unusual. But if a long time elapsed between their lying down and the discovery of the blood, it appears to me that the outer woman is also impure for the reason stated. This appears correct.
