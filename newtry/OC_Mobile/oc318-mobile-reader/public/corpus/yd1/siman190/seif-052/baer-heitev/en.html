How. Only in the particular pairings stated. A woman with a fixed veset may not attribute her stain to one without a veset, nor may one whose veset has not arrived attribute to one whose veset has arrived. Hagahot Maimoniyot.
Blood. Even if she is married. Rashi. Shach cites Perishah that between a young woman and an elderly woman, the stain may be attributed to the young woman even if she has a fixed veset and the stain was found outside its time.
One. The same applies to all other pairings where neither woman is presently fit to see blood: neither may attribute to the other, and both are impure. Shach.
Certain. Taz limits the outer woman's credibility to where the blood was found soon after they lay down, so she can reliably know whether she entered the other's place. If much time elapsed, he would also declare the outer woman impure because people may make statements about matters they were not attentive to. Nekudot HaKesef disagrees. Taz further discusses women with chronic sores and wounds, generally permitting attribution where the stain can reasonably come from those wounds, while distinguishing cases in which red blood appears as part of one continuous discharge from the uterus.
