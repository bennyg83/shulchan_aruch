(Ibid., seif katan 41) He objected. He objected to Rama's responsum, and all of his proofs can be rejected. Nevertheless, his ruling is reasonable. Examine this carefully. Rabbeinu Yerucham wrote in Netiv 15, part 2: 'It is obvious that we are never concerned for a stain above the genital area, etc., until: If the stain was found on her garment and on her body, and she did not pass through the butchers' market, then from the belt downward she is impure, while from the belt upward she is pure.' End quote. His concluding words, 'from the belt upward,' must refer to a case where she bent backward, as discussed at the outset, and even so she is pure, in accordance with Rashba's opinion. Otherwise, even if the stain were on her body she would be pure, as is implied by the beginning of his words and as is proven in the Gemara at the beginning of the chapter HaRo'eh. The Gemara asks: If the stain was above the belt, why is she impure when it is on her body? Did we not learn that if she saw blood on her body away from the genital area, she is pure? This necessarily refers to a case where she did not pass through the butchers' market, as does the Mishnah that discusses seeing blood, etc. Furthermore, at that stage the Gemara had not yet considered the answer that she had passed through the butchers' market. Moreover, were the other interpretation correct, why did the Gemara ask its question? It could have answered that the case always concerns a stain above the belt where she did not pass through the butchers' market, so that a stain on her body renders her impure while one on her garment leaves her pure, and that the Mishnah concerns a case where she did pass through the market. Rather, it is certain that above the belt, where she did not bend backward, even if she did not pass through the butchers' market, she is pure even when the stain is on her body. This is also the view of all the authorities. The passage must therefore concern a case where she bent backward. This appears correct to me.
