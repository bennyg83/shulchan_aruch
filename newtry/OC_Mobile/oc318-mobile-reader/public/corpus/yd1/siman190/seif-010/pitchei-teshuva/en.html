The lentil. See responsum Chavot Tzvi 67 who wrote it is the species called beans; of them two kinds: one called Turkish beans which are not grown attached; and second kind called large beans in which worms are common — this species' lentil, meaning half of it after splitting in two as its growing way two parts each alone where attached to its fellow resembles square — in it one estimates for practical law — end quote see there; in every case he wrote on it if tradition we accept. See She'elat Ya'avetz vol. 1 s. 69 who brought great support for his father's view — see there; see responsum Me'ir Netivim s. 9 who wrote what Chavot Tzvi wrote resembles square is a sign not a requirement; reason of his ruling to estimate in those large beans is not because he holds chazal intended this lentil, rather as Shulchan Aruch states if a larger lentil occurs we estimate with it; therefore wrote now found even larger beans than these from the first kind Chavot Tzvi mentioned — those without worms and not square called pasuli — estimate with them — see there at length. See Chokhmat Adam there halachah 6: custom among us is approximately like new Dutch peas. See also Siftei Taz on this (who brought in Kitzur Sofer answered on Ma'il Tzedek's measure that it is large; see responsum Chatam Sofer 150 and 182 who wrote correct stain measure as he established in responsum Me'ir Tzaddik where they mark measure with precision; much effort until I checked after him and found his measure reliable; I removed all complaints; even though large much more than food-blood found in our time — reason: beginning of stain decree was stringency of taharot and rabbis did not distinguish and declared her tamei also for her husband so woman not tamei niddah for taharot and tehorah for husband; since forbidden by count even though taharot reason nullified decree did not cease and still forbidden to husband; since herself not forbidden to husband except by enactment of early sages we do not declare tamei except stain measure of their time when food-blood was common even though not found now; he wrote further all this in stain found, etc. — see below siman 36 s.v. kegris).
