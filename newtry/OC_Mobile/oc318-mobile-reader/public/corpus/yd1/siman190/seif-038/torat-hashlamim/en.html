Seif katan 38: "unless it had been examined for her." Even an ordinary examination cloth described above in seif 36 is considered examined with respect to a stain larger than a gris. "Unexamined" means not examined at all, such as one taken from the marketplace and found in a state presumed to be soiled. Taz, unlike Bach.
"She examined her garment." This is the Tur's language. One cannot say that the word "examined" was needed because otherwise it would be obvious that she is pure even without having lent the garment to her friend. The difficulty remains: why say that she examined it? Even if she did not actually examine it, if she took it from the market when it was not presumed soiled, it likewise has the status of examined with respect to a stain larger than a gris, as I wrote above in seif katan 37. Bach certainly agrees with this, for he would not dispute the Tur and Rashba, who write it explicitly. Bach's view is only that even where the cloth is actually presumed soiled, nevertheless for wiping or examination she becomes impure with more than a gris; in truth that is incorrect, as Taz writes in seif katan 29. Rather, the Baraisa specifically says that she examined the garment to teach that only if she examined it a second time when she removed it is she pure. If she did not examine it a second time, even though she examined it when she put it on, or all the more so where she did not literally examine it but it was presumed clean, for otherwise both women would be pure, both women are impure. This appears to me straightforward. This is the basis of the Perishah's and Bach's question on the Tur, and this is Bach's intended resolution of the Tur, as I have explained. The answers of Taz and Shach, and their surprise at Bach, therefore fall away. According to this, however, Bach's words in seif 38 still require some study.
