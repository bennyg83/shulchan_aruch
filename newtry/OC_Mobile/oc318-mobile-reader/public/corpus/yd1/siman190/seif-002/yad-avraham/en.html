(Shulchan Aruch, se'if 25) Two women handled one bird containing only a sela's amount of blood, and a sela-sized stain was found on each woman: both are impure. Turei Zahav distinguishes between stains and chametz on the grounds that, in the latter case, when it is uncertain whether chametz entered one house, we rule leniently. This does not accord with Rambam, who rules stringently in the case of one house. Rambam must hold as follows: he rules leniently concerning two houses, although regarding two paths in a private domain everyone agrees that even if the two people traversed them one after the other, both are impure. The cases differ because, with two paths, each side constitutes an uncertainty concerning impurity in a private domain, which is impure according to the Torah. Consequently, we must declare both people impure in the case of two paths and two people. Regarding chametz, however, one side of the uncertainty, whether it entered one house, is only rabbinic, because the obligation to search for chametz is rabbinic in a case of uncertainty. We therefore do not rule stringently concerning two houses. The same applies to every matter in which the Sages ruled stringently concerning one uncertain case: we rule leniently concerning two such cases when they arise one after the other. One may further explain Rambam's reason for leniency concerning two houses as Maggid Mishneh wrote in chapter 2, law 12, of the Laws of Chametz: one of the houses was certainly searched. This must also be the reason that we rule leniently regarding one hiding place in the case of a captive woman, although we otherwise rule stringently when her status is uncertain. Accordingly, Turei Zahav's reason for ruling stringently regarding stains, namely, that without attributing the stain to another source she is forbidden because of the uncertainty, is incorrect, for this is no worse than chametz or a captive woman. Rather, as Derishah wrote, it is possible that both women are impure. This must be said according to Rambam, who rules leniently concerning chametz even with two people and one house. If stains were equivalent to chametz, as Nekudot HaKesef wrote, we should not declare both women impure even in the case of one house. We must therefore conclude that stains are treated more stringently, contrary to Nekudot HaKesef. See Sederi Taharah.
