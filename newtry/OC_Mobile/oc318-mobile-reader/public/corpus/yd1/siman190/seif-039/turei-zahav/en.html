But if it was not checked, etc. The Rashba and the Tur phrase this as: “But if she took a garment from the marketplace and wore it without checking it.” This is also the Shulchan Aruch's intention here. The phrase “not checked” here does not have the same meaning as in seif 36, where a stain larger than a bean renders her impure. There the cloth had been stored with a presumption of cleanliness, as explained there in the Rashba's name; the Gemara calls that an unexamined cloth, and the poskim follow the Gemara's terminology. Here the Shulchan Aruch uses ordinary language: she has no knowledge of the garment at all. The opening and closing clauses might seem contradictory. The opening implies that only an actually checked garment renders her impure, while an intermediate cloth of the kind mentioned in seif 36 would leave her pure. The closing implies that only a garment taken from the marketplace leaves her pure. Therefore the opening deliberately says, “unless it had the status of checked for her,” rather than, “unless she checked it.” This teaches that even where she did not personally inspect it, but it had a presumption of having been checked, namely the intermediate category described by the Rashba and called “unexamined” in seif 36, she is impure. Thus this seif accords exactly with seif 36. My father-in-law understood the phrase here as referring to the same unexamined cloth as in seif 36 and therefore found a contradiction with the Rosh. He answered that seif 36 is more stringent because she wiped herself, whereas here she merely found a stain. This is difficult. The present seif follows the Rashba, who declares her pure even when she wiped with the unexamined cloth of seif 36. Although the Rosh is stringent there, he certainly agrees that a garment taken from the marketplace is more lenient, since no dispute is recorded. There is also no basis to distinguish between wiping without sensing blood and merely finding a stain, for by Torah law a woman is not impure until she senses the blood leave her flesh. Blood found afterward on an unexamined cloth is therefore only a rabbinic stain. The Tur explicitly writes in the Rosh's name that an unexamined cloth has the same law as any other stain. It follows that wiping with an unexamined cloth and finding a stain without wiping are equal: both are pure when the garment came from the marketplace, while the intermediate cloth of seif 36 renders her impure. The end of this siman, seif 54, also proves that an unexamined cloth is treated only as a stain. This appears clear and obvious to me.
