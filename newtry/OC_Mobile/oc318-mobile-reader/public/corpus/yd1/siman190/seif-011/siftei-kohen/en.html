And all the more on her feet themselves. Bach wrote specifically the top of the foot that faces opposite the foot; but on the rest of the top of the foot toward the outside, and all the more when found on the other four toenails — she is pure, etc.; but his words are not compelled; and likewise Derishah wrote it requires investigation regarding the other toenails, etc. — see there.
And likewise if found on her hands, etc. — perhaps they touched, etc. It is clearly implied from Taz's words that even presumptively we are concerned that they touched; and so is the view of Hagahot HaRambam.
But, etc., she is pure. Even if she did not pass through the butchers' market where there is nothing to attribute to — so proven from the Gemara and poskim, and it is obvious; and we are not concerned that perhaps she brought it there with her hands, for we do not propagate tumah from place to place, as stated in the Gemara and poskim.
That she flipped upside down. The Tur's language: "like a kid"; meaning specifically when her head was turned downward and feet upward — as Rambam, Rashi, Rashbam, and other poskim wrote.
From the girdle. It is an apron that women gird for modesty; Rashi and Bartenura wrote at the beginning of chapter HaRoeh Ketam that the girdle is opposite the vulnerable place.
In what case is this said, etc. That is, in what case is it said she does not attribute when found on her flesh alone, such as below the girdle — that is when she has nothing to attribute to except the matter of stains or the butchers' market; that is when we do not attribute; but if there is a wound, etc.
But if there is a wound, etc. And within the first three days of counting the seven clean days we do not attribute even to a wound, as below siman 196 s.k. 12.
