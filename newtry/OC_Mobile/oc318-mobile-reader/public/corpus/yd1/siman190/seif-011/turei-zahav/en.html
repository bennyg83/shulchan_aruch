If it was found on her heel, she is impure. Perhaps, while sitting, her heel touched that place. The Perishah explains that this refers to sitting in the manner of the Ishmaelites, who place their feet beneath their buttocks.
And all the more so if it was found on her actual foot. The Beit Yosef wrote this because he understood it from the phrase, “even on the tip of the big toe.” This is not conclusive, for “even” may refer to the big toe itself, concerning which it is obvious that she is impure, while the top of the foot may be no worse than the outer side of her legs and feet. My father-in-law also explained it this way. Nevertheless, one should not be lenient in practice, since the elder authority, the Beit Yosef, has already ruled, and the Rama cited his ruling. It does appear permissible, however, when the stain is found on the other toes, apart from the big toe. It is possible that the Beit Yosef also agrees, since they are distant from the inner side.
Above that place, etc. We do not say that she touched the source with her hand and afterward transferred the blood to this place by touching it, for we do not establish impurity from one place to another. Similarly, at the end of the seif, where it is impossible for the blood to have come from the wound, we do not establish purity from one place to another.
On her flesh and also on her garment. The Tur writes in the name of the Ramban that when blood is found on both her flesh and her garment, she may attribute it to whatever cause is available. The Beit Yosef cites the Rashba that if it had come from her body, it should have been found on her flesh alone. At first glance this is difficult, since immediately above it is stated that had it come from the source it would also have been found on her flesh. There is no difficulty. The Beit Yosef earlier cited the Rashba's phrase that it should have been found on her flesh alone, but he did not present this as proof that something coming from her body cannot also reach her garment. Rather, his meaning is that there is no proof that it came from her body, since it was not found on her flesh alone.
But if she has a wound on her body, etc. Likewise, if she actually handled staining substances with her hands, she may attribute the stain to them even when it is found on her hands. The Tur states this in the name of the Rashba.
And if the wound is on her shoulder, etc. Concerning a wound on her neck, the Gemara states that sometimes she may attribute the stain to it, such as when it is on the side opposite the genital area, for she sometimes bends her neck and blood from the wound falls there.
