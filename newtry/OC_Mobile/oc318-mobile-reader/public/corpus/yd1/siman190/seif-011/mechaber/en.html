Not everywhere that a ketem is found is she tamei; rather, only in a place where it is possible that it came there from the makor. How so? If it was found on her heel, she is tamei. And likewise if it was found on the entire length of her shin and the soles of her feet on the inside — and these are the places that cling to one another when she stands, and foot clings to foot and shin to shin. And likewise if it was found on the head of the big toe of her feet (and all the more so on her feet themselves) (Beit Yosef in the name of Rashba). And likewise, if it was found on her hands, even on the joints of her fingers, for the hands are busy, and perhaps they touched that place. But if it was found on her shins and on the soles of her feet on the outside, or even on the sides — and needless to say above that place — she is tehorah. And if she knows that she stretched out and lifted her legs upward, she is tamei wherever she finds it, even above the belt, whether in front or in back, even if she passed through the butchers’ market or dealt with ketamim — and this is specifically when it was found on her flesh alone. But if it was found on her flesh and also on her garment: if she passed through the butchers’ market or dealt with ketamim, she attributes to it, whether it was found below the belt or she lifted her legs and it was found above the belt (Ramban and Rashba). In what case are these matters said — that when the ketem is found on her flesh alone she does not attribute — when she has nothing to attribute to except dealing with ketamim or the butchers’ market. But if she has a wound on her body that she can attribute to, such that it is possible that blood came from it, she attributes to it and she is tehorah. But if the wound is on her shoulder and the ketem is on her thigh in a place that it is impossible for it to come from the wound, she is tamei.
