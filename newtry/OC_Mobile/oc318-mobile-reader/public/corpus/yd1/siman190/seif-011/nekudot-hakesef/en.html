(Taz s.k. 17) He challenged Bach; and in his humble opinion Bach's words are correct as I will explain. What Bach wrote — and so it appears in Tosafot — does not refer to coming to ask together they are tamei; rather one who examines Bach will see explicitly it refers even if they were occupied one after another they are tamei; Bach: two women who were occupied, etc., and it appears to teach even where they did not occupy with the bird together but one after another — and if the first were asked we would declare her tehorah since she occupied; nevertheless since they came to ask together both are tamei — and so Tosafot implies — end quote. What he brought as proof from Rashba in Teshuvos HaRashba 178 is no proof at all, for Rashba goes per his reasoning; but per what we hold to be lenient one after another as written there in Siftei Kohen seif 19 — if so likewise here; and this is the view of our French rabbis and Ramban whom Beit Yosef brings, and Rashba's words, etc. What he distinguished between chametz and paths from here is not clear as I will explain; also what he challenged Derishah who wrote specifically in rabbinic matters we say thus — he was not precise, for even in Torah matters we say so in Pesachim regarding two paths, and in ch. 2 of Ketubot regarding permitting a woman, etc. He was not precise — how could one say in Torah prohibition thus; we do not permit except from doubt leniently, and Torah doubt is stringent; rather the entire sugya in Pesachim proves specifically in rabbinic matters we say thus — and so is clear from Rashi there who explained on "there is one group of chametz," etc., two paths, etc. — Rashi: if each asked alone tehor; Beit Din cannot declare tamei as we hold doubt of impurity in reshut harabim is tehor, etc.; and regarding houses too if each asked alone we tell each you need not check since bedikat chametz is rabbinic we attribute leniently — end quote; thus only in rabbinic matters we attribute leniently because rabbinic doubt is lenient; and likewise paths whose doubt in reshut harabim is tehor; but not in Torah prohibition; and what we attribute leniently in ch. 2 of Ketubot is specifically regarding a captive where they were lenient greatly as explained there in the Gemara; per this it is proven here too we attribute leniently, for just as in chametz we attribute leniently because bedikat chametz is rabbinic — meaning from Torah annulment suffices and rabbis required examination — if a stain is found on her body she is tehorah until she feels blood left her flesh, and rabbis decreed on stain where there is nothing to attribute; therefore where they came to ask one after another they are tehorot.
