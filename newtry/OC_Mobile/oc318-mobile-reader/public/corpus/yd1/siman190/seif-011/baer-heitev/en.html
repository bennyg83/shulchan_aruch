Her heel. Perhaps she touched that area while sitting. Perishah explains that she sat in the manner of the Ishmaelites, with her feet beneath her buttocks. Taz.
Her feet. Bach limits the prohibition to the upper part of the foot facing inward. On the outer upper foot, and all the more on the tips of the other toes, he is lenient. Shach finds this unproven. Taz writes that one should not be lenient regarding the upper foot, following Beit Yosef and Rama, but may be lenient on the toes other than the big toe because they are far from the inner side.
Perhaps. The Tur and Mechaber clearly imply that even without specific evidence we are concerned that her feet may have touched. Shach.
Pure. Even if she did not pass through a butcher's market and has no known source to which to attribute the stain, we do not assume that her hand touched the uterus and then transferred blood elsewhere. We do not establish impurity from one place to another. Shach.
From the belt. Meaning an apron worn by women for modesty. Rashba.
Her garment. There is no proof that the blood came from her body, because it was not found on the flesh alone. Taz.
A wound. During the first three days of the seven clean days one does not attribute a stain even to a wound, as stated in siman 196. If blood was found directly on her hands and she had handled blood with those hands, she may attribute it to that source. Tur in the name of Rashba.
On her shoulder. Taz writes that blood on the neck may sometimes be attributed to a wound, such as on the side aligned with the genital area, because she may bend and blood from the wound may fall there.
