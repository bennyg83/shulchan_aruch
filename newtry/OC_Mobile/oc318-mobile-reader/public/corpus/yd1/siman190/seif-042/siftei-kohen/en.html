She may attribute it to them. This is because attributing the stain to any of these women does not impair her status, whereas attributing it to the first woman would impair her status. This is analogous to what they said regarding two baskets, one of unconsecrated produce and one of terumah: I say that the unconsecrated produce fell into the unconsecrated produce, and the terumah fell into the terumah. This was explained above in siman 111. Rashba in Torat HaBayit.
Even nowadays. Nowadays a virgin who has relations is granted only the first marital act, as stated below in siman 193. Nevertheless, another woman may attribute a stain to her, because blood is commonly found with her. Likewise, she may attribute it to a woman who is observing the days of pure blood after childbirth. Although nowadays such a woman has the same status as all other women, as stated below in siman 194, nevertheless, another woman may attribute the stain to her, because according to the strict law she is pure, and attributing it to her causes no impairment. The stringency not to have relations during the days of pure blood was adopted for themselves, but they did not extend the stringency so that she could not protect another woman from impurity.
