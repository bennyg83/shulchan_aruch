The measure of a gris. Yereim writes that we are not expert in the measure and should assess whether the stain reasonably comes from an insect. Besamim Rosh and Sidrei Taharah debate whether thickness and concentration alter the measured area; practical judgment remains with the decisor.
The gris. Chacham Tzvi identifies a particular large bean as the measure. Yaavetz and later authorities support him, while Meir Netivim says the square shape is only an identifying sign and that an even larger commonly available bean may be used. Chatam Sofer defends the larger historic measure because the original rabbinic decree followed the insects common in their era.
On the human body. Meir Netivim says the comparison is to body hair, not scalp hair, and describes measuring six hairs by six hairs on the forearm with surrounding space.
It is louse blood. Meil Tzedakah suggests a black stain smaller than a gris should be impure because ordinary lice produce red blood. Sidrei Taharah cites Raavad that only a red stain is attributed to lice, but permits black where fleas commonly leave black spots.
