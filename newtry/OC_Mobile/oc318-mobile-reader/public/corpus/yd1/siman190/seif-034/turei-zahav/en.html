And there is something to which it can be attributed. This implies that even where the doubt arose through an internal examination, attribution to another cause is effective. Therefore, a woman who regularly discharges blood from the rectum because of some condition, and who examined herself even with a checked cloth while the cloth also touched that area, may attribute the blood to the rectal condition when it is not her fixed period. There is no Torah-level doubt, since she did not sense blood emerging from the source. The Beit Yosef writes here that where there is concern that the blood reached the cloth because she handled blood near the place where the cloth lay, it may be attributed to that cause even when it exceeds a bean plus a little; the fact that she wiped herself does not prevent attribution to a possible cause, since she did not sense blood leaving her body, provided the stain is not elongated. The same applies exactly to the case described here. Indeed, even an elongated mark is pure, since it was produced by wiping a pure area. This is all the more evident because it resembles a wound in that place, discussed in siman 187, seif 5. Even the stringent opinion in seif 35 agrees here, for that case is different, as will be explained there. See also what I wrote in seif 39. I record this because I saw one decisor rule stringently, and it appears to me that he erred in a simple matter.
