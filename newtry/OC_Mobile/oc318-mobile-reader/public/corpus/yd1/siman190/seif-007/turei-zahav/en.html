Or she smelled its odor, etc. But otherwise we do not attribute to lice; Rosh Beit Yosef brought: from default we do not attribute because not common — implies in common places we attribute like food; Rashba explicitly Beit Yosef brought — then attribute to lice until lupin; but Rav Beit Yosef cut off his hands and wrote Rosh should be explained: not common means not close to body so much and does not smell odor; afterward wrote can reject saying so; brought Rav HaMagid who brought Rashba's words that Ramban and Rambam did not distinguish common or not — possible they hold even if common we do not attribute since it smells and we do not say perhaps she smelled unawares, etc.; for practical law appears since Rashba explicitly explains leniently and Rosh's words clear per plain meaning — likewise in Darkhei Moshe in Rav Yerocham's name: where lice abundant we attribute; in stains we listen to be lenient — appears where lice very common one may be lenient and attribute stains until lupin as stated.
