See Torat HaShlamim who wrote regarding those women presumed blood ceased, such as pregnant and nursing — even if her makor opened she is tehorah if she found nothing — see there; Siftei Taz agreed for practical law and wrote the reason: we attribute what opened the makor to expel white or green discharge which also come from the makor, since they are presumed blood ceased it is more reasonable to attribute to them as is the way even of pregnant and nursing from what is said that blood left, etc. — see there; they also brought Chokhmat Adam there halachah 2 (and in responsum Chatam Sofer 168 his view appears to be stringent — but he did not see Siftei Taz's words who also leaned to his reasoning; nevertheless he is lenient for the above reason). See She'elat Ya'avetz vol. 2 s. 5 asked there whether to rely on Torat HaShlamim since at end of Beit Levi he challenged him; he extended to challenge the fundamental ruling of Torat HaDeshen; therefore he concluded not only for one presumed blood ceased do we not concern ourselves, but even for one presumed bleeding the lenient is rewarded and there is no concern — see there. Responsum Radbaz chadashot 149 also disagrees with Torat HaDeshen's ruling — see there; nevertheless Siftei Taz wrote to answer his challenge and brought proofs for Torat HaDeshen's view — see there. See responsum Ateret Zekenim Panim Me'irot vol. 3 s. 13 who wrote regarding a woman who felt her makor open, examined herself with an unexamined ed and found a drop of blood like a mustard seed — tamei even if we are lenient when she found nothing; nevertheless here since she found a drop of blood it is worse and we do not attribute leniently — see there. It appears if she also found tehorah appearances she may attribute her sensation to that and the blood to tehorah food per the lenient view when she found nothing; even Chokhmat David above s.k. 6 did not write tamei in this ruling except per the stringent view when she found nothing — as written there you will know, etc.; requires study per Pri Megadim's view that this is worse than when she found nothing; perhaps even pregnant and nursing are tamei in such a case per the stringent view when she found nothing. See responsum Noda BiYehudah tinyana YD 119 regarding a woman who felt her makor open, examined immediately within her veset interval, left the ed until morning and in morning found nothing — declare tehorah and attribute that there was tehorah appearance that dried on the ed and is not recognizable — closer than saying it was red and dissolved before she checked immediately (and explained there if she did not leave the ed until morning but saw immediately and found nothing — tamei even though she checked within veset interval — per Chokhmat David above s.k. 5). But if she did not check until after veset interval we are concerned perhaps it dissolved or was lost; and even if she checked within veset interval specifically if tomorrow she found nothing; but if she found any blood even less than a lentil even though the ed is unexamined — tamei. See there the son of the Mechaber's Hagahah who wrote what Shulchan Aruch means "if she felt her makor open to expel blood" — she felt makor open and also felt moist discharge — both specifically required; with this resolved what puzzles regarding this ruling — see there he extended. See prior responsa there regarding a woman who dreamed in sleep as if she felt makor open, woke and immediately checked and found nothing and is in doubt whether dream or makor truly opened; he extended to refute Torat HaDeshen (source of this Shulchan Aruch ruling); concluded even Torat HaDeshen means not Torah sensation that is certain but doubt of Torah and it is stringent; now where the woman doubts the sensation itself it is double doubt; all this if she is in days of taharah; but in days of counting whether double doubt helps is explained in my Noda BiYehudah — end quote; and see above siman 187 s.k. 23; all this.
