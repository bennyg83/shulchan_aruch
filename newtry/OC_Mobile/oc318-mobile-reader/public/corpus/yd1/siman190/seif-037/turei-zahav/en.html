And she pressed it against her thigh. There are two doubts, and we rule leniently: perhaps the blood was not transferred from the cloth to her thigh; and even if it was transferred from the cloth, since the cloth had not been examined beforehand, perhaps the blood was already on it. Rashi.
And all the more so if she placed it, etc. This means that she did not press it against her thigh. Likewise, the later phrase, “But if she did not place it,” also refers to a case where she did not press it against her thigh. If she did press it against her thigh, she is pure in every case because of the double doubt explained above.
