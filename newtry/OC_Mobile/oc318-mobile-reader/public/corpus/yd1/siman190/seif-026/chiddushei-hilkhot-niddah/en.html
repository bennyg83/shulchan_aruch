She attributes a lentil-sized portion to the blood with which she handled, and another lentil-sized portion to a louse, etc. According to this, half a sela must be larger than a lentil-sized area. Otherwise, in the case of two women and bird's blood discussed in the preceding seif, we could attribute half a sela to the bird's blood and half a sela to a louse. It follows that half a sela is larger than a lentil-sized area. A sela is one third of a handbreadth, so half a sela is half of one third of a handbreadth. It is well known that a handbreadth contains four thumbbreadths, and from this one may estimate the much smaller measure of a lentil-sized area. I saw that the saintly and accomplished scholar, Rabbi Yonah of Prague, of blessed memory, calculated in his treatise, on the basis of geometry and the principles of Euclid, that the area of nine lentils corresponds to this measure of width and length. From here there is a refutation, for the lentil-sized measure is no larger in length and width than approximately half a thumbbreadth.
