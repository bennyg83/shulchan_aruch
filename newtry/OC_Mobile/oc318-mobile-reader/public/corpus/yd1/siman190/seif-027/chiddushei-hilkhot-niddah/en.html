If she handled less than a lentil-sized quantity and two lentil-sized areas were found. The Beit Yosef and the later authorities were greatly perplexed concerning the reason for this ruling, since the Gemara concludes that we do not say, 'Take the bird's blood and cast it into the middle.' It appears to me as follows. The added amount in the expression 'a lentil-sized area and more' has no fixed minimum. Even the smallest possible point, like the geometrical point which most authors describe as the final tip of a needle, suffices, for in any event the total is more than a lentil-sized area. All measures fixed by the Sages operate in this manner. Accordingly, if she handled a lentil-sized quantity and two lentil-sized areas plus an additional amount were found upon her, there is only one uncertainty, namely, perhaps the lentil-sized quantity she handled should be placed in the middle. There is no further uncertainty that perhaps two lice were crushed adjacent to one another, despite the statement that no sheet is without several drops of blood, because here there are two lentil-sized areas plus an additional amount. Had two lice been crushed, only two ordinary lentil-sized areas should have been found. By contrast, if she handled less than a lentil-sized quantity and exactly two lentil-sized areas were found upon her, there are two uncertainties. Perhaps the blood she handled should be placed in the middle, and perhaps two lice were crushed immediately adjacent to one another, producing exactly two lentil-sized areas. Since there are two uncertainties, although each possibility is uncommon and individually remote, together they create a single meaningful doubt, and in a rabbinic matter a doubt is resolved leniently. This is the explanation that appears correct to me. Although it is strained, it is preferable to the other explanations offered by the authorities.
