If she handled less than, etc. These are the Rashba's words as cited by the Tur. He rules leniently concerning the Gemara's unresolved question. The later statement, “and likewise if she handled less than a bean,” follows the Beit Yosef's version of the Tur in the name of the Ramban. The Beit Yosef explains that we place the amount smaller than a bean in the middle, leaving less than the required measure on either side. Although the Gemara rejected that reasoning where she handled a bean and found two beans plus a little more, the Beit Yosef applies it here. This does not appear correct, for what difference is there between handling a bean and finding two beans plus a little, and handling less than a bean and finding two beans? Nor do the Rashba's words imply the Beit Yosef's ruling, for the Rashba declared her pure only when she handled less than a bean and found a bean plus a little. The correct version, found in precise texts, is that the Ramban declared her pure when she handled a bean and found two beans. The Tur needed to state this even after citing the Rashba's ruling, because one might have thought that according to the Rashba, if she handled a full bean and found two beans she is impure. To declare her pure, one must assume that the entire substance she handled adhered to her garment and none remained elsewhere, leaving only exactly one bean of unexplained blood. This assumption is not especially compelling, since perhaps some remained at the place where she handled it and slightly less than a bean adhered to her, leaving more than a bean unexplained. The Ramban nevertheless rules leniently, assuming that the entire bean adhered to her. But if she handled less than a bean and found two beans, she is certainly impure; we do not apply the theory of placing the handled amount in the middle. This appears correct to me.
In the Tur it is written in the name of the Ramban that one should rely on examination of the identifying signs only for matters of ritual purity, but not to permit her to her husband. This is difficult in light of the beginning of siman 187, where ritual purity is treated more stringently than permission to her husband. There is no difficulty according to what the Beit Yosef writes in siman 191, s.v. “The Hagahos Maimoniyos wrote”; see there.
