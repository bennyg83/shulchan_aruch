Before us. Shach understands that the garment itself is also absent. If present, one should inspect whether the stain has penetrated or hardened; if no expert is available, one must be stringent. Where the garment was lost, one need not be stringent.
Washing. The presumption was that Jewish women examined their own and their friends' garments at laundering and reported any stain. Shach writes that this is not observed nowadays and one should be stringent.
Even. This implies that even if the launderer is present and says she examined it and there was no stain, we do not rely upon her statement either to forbid or permit; we judge as though she were absent. Bach and Shach.
To be stringent. This is not an ordinary rabbinic doubt, because uncertainty resulting from lack of expertise is not treated as doubt; another expert could know. Rashba. See siman 99:3.
