(Shulchan Aruch seif 25) Two women who occupied themselves with one bird that has no blood except a sela, and a sela was found on each — both are tamei. What Taz wrote to distinguish between stains and chametz that there in doubt of entry to one house we are lenient — does not apply per Rambam who is stringent in one house; perforce Rambam's reason for leniency in two houses even though regarding two paths in reshut hayachid all agree even one after another both are tamei, for there is different — in two paths one side is doubt of impurity in reshut harabim which is Torah tamei; therefore one must declare tamei also in two paths and two houses; but regarding chametz one side of doubt of entry to one house is only rabbinic because of doubt in bedikat chametz which is rabbinic; therefore they are not stringent in two houses. And so in all matters where they were stringent in its doubt in one from rabbinic law they are lenient in two one after another. Further Rambam's reason for leniency in two houses is as Hagahot Maimoniyot ch. 2 Laws of Chametz halachah 12 — certainly one of them was checked; and likewise regarding captive — therefore lenient in one hiding place even though otherwise stringent in its doubt. If so Taz's reason to be stringent in stains since without attribution it is forbidden does not apply — it is no worse than chametz and captive; rather as Derishah wrote — perhaps both are tamei; perforce must say so per Rambam who is lenient in chametz even in one house; and if you say stains equal chametz — as Nekudot HaKesef wrote — we should not declare both tamei even in one house; perforce stains are inferior unlike Nekudot HaKesef; see Siftei Taz.
