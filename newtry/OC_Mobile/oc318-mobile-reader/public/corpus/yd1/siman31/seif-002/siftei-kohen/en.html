[The brain itself that decayed a little of it, etc. This is the wording of Rambam, and Tur wrote: if a little is missing from the brain, such as if it decayed, etc.; and in my books I proved that all poskim agree in this: even if it decayed or was crushed so that a little is missing — in a case where if the same occurred in the spinal cord one would hold it and leave the empty part above his hand and it would stand and not bend — kosher; but if much is missing so that if the same occurred in the spinal cord, if much protruded above his hand it would bend — treifah. And likewise regarding the spinal cord the law is the same. Therefore Tur wrote in siman 32 seif 4: if a little of the brain is missing and it became hollow — kosher — to exclude if much is missing, for then treifah, since if one placed it in his hand the empty part outside his hand would sag. But when it melted like wax they did not distinguish — neither here nor in siman 32 — between a little and much, because its way is to melt greatly; and the distinction is only whether it was smoothed well or not: when smoothed well like wax then it sags and is treifah, and if not — kosher. And so is the opinion of R' Yerucham, who wrote: "it became mushy" — meaning it became hollow and not so much as "it melted" — kosher — end. And therefore he means: its way is to melt greatly, and if much became hollow too it could not stand and would be treifah; therefore it is not kosher except when a little became hollow and not so much as melted. And this is Rashi's view, who explained on that which the Gemara says "it melted" — treifah because it cannot stand: meaning it melted but not so much that it would pour like a pitcher of water, but rather when one holds it and leaves a little above the hand it bends and falls; and on "it became mushy" — kosher — he explained: a little of the brain became hollow by itself. And so is Rashba's view in Tur HaDeshen the long one (daf 39a). And now — what Rambam and Mechaber wrote, "decayed or was crushed," etc. — means through deficiency, for presumably a little is missing when it decays or is crushed; and as Mechaber wrote in siman 32 explicitly; and as "and the membrane remains intact" implies: wherever the membrane remains, even if the brain itself is deficient through decay — kosher. And all this is clear in my eyes, not like Bach who was lengthy and multiplied dispute among poskim and thought to breach a rampart against Tur — but in my humble opinion, as I wrote, now all poskim are struck with one blow and there is no dispute here — examine well.]
