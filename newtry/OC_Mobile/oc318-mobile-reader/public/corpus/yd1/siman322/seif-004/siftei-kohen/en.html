All the Land, etc. Until Keziv, which is full Eretz Yisrael, and it is eaten by kohanim in purity.
Which is from Keziv until Amanah. It is near the land of the nations, and they cannot keep themselves and their produce in purity. Their challot are tamei and require burning like tamei terumah. Therefore they separate two challot. The first, which is by Torah law, must be separated with the required measure; since it is tamei, it is burned. The second was enacted by the Rabbis so that the law of challah not be forgotten. Therefore, since it exists only so that the law of challah not be forgotten, it has no measure, for they established it according to the Torah baseline.
And all the land from Amanah outward, etc. This does not belong to Eretz Yisrael at all, and there is not even one challah there by Torah law. They decreed regarding those places because of Eretz Yisrael and also enacted that two challot be separated. The first, which is burned, has no measure, since it is not Torah law; the second is one forty-eighth, since it is eaten by kohanim and is permitted to the tamei, because it is entirely rabbinic.
And they burn it, etc. Mahari Weil wrote in the laws of Pesach that one must burn it in the fire of a kohen or kohenet according to Rabbeinu Tam's explanation in chapter Kol Sha'ah, for so Maharam wrote, etc. It is proper to make for it a separate fire in a place where they do not cook, or in the oven where they bake matzot before the matzot are placed inside, so that he knows no Jew will derive benefit before its prohibition has been consumed, etc. Mahari Weil likewise wrote in the laws of Pesach, siman 153, in the name of Tosafot in chapter Kol Sha'ah, that one should do so with other mitzvah items, making a separate fire for it; the Rav brings this at the end of this siman. It appears to me that only benefit from burning it is forbidden to a non-kohen, but other benefit is permitted to a non-kohen, as we say regarding terumah below in siman 331 seif 19 in the gloss.
