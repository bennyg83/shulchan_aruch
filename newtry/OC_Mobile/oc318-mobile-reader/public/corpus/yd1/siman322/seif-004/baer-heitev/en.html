Amanah. It is near the lands outside Eretz Yisrael, where people cannot keep themselves and their produce in purity. Their challah is impure and requires burning like impure terumah. They therefore separate two portions of challah. The first is required by Torah law and must be separated in the prescribed amount; because it is impure, it is burned. The second has no prescribed amount, for it was instituted only so that the law of challah not be forgotten, and the Sages left it at the Torah minimum. This is the Shach.
And beyond. This refers to territory wholly unrelated to Eretz Yisrael, where not even the first challah is required by Torah law. The Sages decreed there because of Eretz Yisrael and likewise required two portions the first, which is burned, has no prescribed amount because it is not required by Torah law; the second is one forty-eighth, since it is eaten by kohanim and is permitted even to impure kohanim because it is entirely rabbinic. See the Levush.
And they burn it. Maharil writes in the laws of Pesach that it should be burned in the fire of a kohen or koheness. It is proper to make a separate fire for it in a place where food is not cooked, or in the oven used for matzos before the matzos are placed inside, so that it certainly will not provide benefit to a non-kohen before the prohibition is consumed. It appears that only benefit from the burning is forbidden to a non-kohen, while other benefit is permitted, as stated regarding terumah and in siman 331, seif 19, in the gloss. This is the Shach. See siman 9 and siman 331.
