There are three laws for challah, etc. There are two approaches here. The first is the approach of Tosafot and the Rosh in Perek Kol HaBasar, in brief. We learned: there are three lands for challah. From the Land of Israel until Keziv, one challah. From Keziv until the river and until Amnon, two challot, one for the fire and one for the kohen; that of the fire has a measure, and that of the kohen has no measure. From the river and Amnon inward, two challot, one for the fire and one for the kohen; that of the fire has no measure, and that of the kohen has a measure, and a tevul yom may eat it. Rabbi Yose says it does not require immersion, but it is forbidden to zavim, zavot, niddot, and women after childbirth. The Rosh explained that the reason for the two challot mentioned in the middle clause of this Mishnah is that that place is near the land of the nations and is a place of tumah, and people cannot guard themselves and their fruit in purity. Their challot are tamei and require burning like tamei challah; therefore one challah is for the fire. They enacted that another be separated for the kohen to eat, so that the law of challah not be forgotten. But the one for the fire is Torah-level, so it has a measure. The two challot mentioned in the latter clause of the Mishnah are rabbinic; therefore the eaten challah is more important and has a measure. A tevul yom may eat the second challah even though the sun has not set for him, since it is only so that the law of challah not be forgotten. It appears that in the middle clause too we say this regarding the challah for the kohen. What it says, “and it is forbidden to zavim, etc.,” means without immersion, teaching that although according to Rabbi Yose those who had seminal emissions do not need immersion, nevertheless these require immersion. According to the Sages, with tumah that does not emerge from the body, such as contact-tumah from a corpse, since it does not emerge from the body it also does not require immersion. There is also a fourth law, namely what we mentioned in Perek Ad Kama: Ravina said the halachah is that a niddah separates challah, and a minor kohen eats it; if there is no minor kohen, she takes it on the tip of a shovel and throws it into the oven, and afterward separates another challah so that the law of challah not be forgotten, and even an adult kohen may eat it. It follows that where there is a minor kohen, one need not separate another challah. Although in the law of the latter clause of the Mishnah the challah for the fire is not eaten at all, like the challah for the fire in the first clause, and two challot are always required, the law of the latter clause is different, because those places are near the Land of Israel and their challah resembles the challah of the Land of Israel. Therefore they decreed tumah upon it so that even a completely pure kohen may not eat it. But the fourth law speaks of distant places, such as Babylonia, and it does not resemble the challah of the Land of Israel, so they did not decree prohibition because of its own tumah, and it is eaten by a minor kohen. They did not require separation of another challah. The same applies to an adult kohen who immersed from his emission, even though the sun has not set for him. Just as they were lenient in the fourth law, where there is a minor kohen, that one need not separate another challah and burn this one as in the third law, so too they were lenient that the eaten challah is permitted to zavim and zavot, unlike the eaten challah in the third law. The Rambam has another approach: he makes the third law and the fourth law all one, and considers all of it outside the Land, making no distinction between near the Land of Israel and far. Even nearby, the law is like what is mentioned in the fourth law. The essential distinction between the middle clause and the latter clause is this: in the first clause it is actual Land of Israel for every matter, meaning that this border was conquered by those who came up from Egypt and also by those who came up from Babylonia; therefore there is only one challah there. In the middle clause it is a place conquered by those who came up from Egypt but not by those who came up from Babylonia; therefore two challot are separated, because the first is tamei, since it was not sanctified in the days of Ezra and the first sanctity ceased when they were exiled. Since it is the Land of Israel, they separate challah with a measure and burn it, and separate a second challah for the kohen’s eating, so that people should not say pure terumah is burned. In the third and fourth laws above, we say in both that he separates two challot, one for the fire so that people should not say, “We saw tamei terumah being eaten,” and it is better to increase the eaten one so that it has a measure. In these two laws, a pure kohen is effective so that they need separate only one challah. This is explained in the Rambam’s Commentary to the Mishnah, where there is no distinction between near and far. However, the Tur’s words have no explanation, for he follows no view. He first wrote, “and in places near it,” implying that he holds like the Rosh, who distinguishes between places near the Land of Israel and far. If so, what he wrote afterward, “when are these words said? When there is no pure kohen there, etc.,” refers to those distant places, as I wrote in the name of the Rosh, and therefore he wrote that it is permitted to zavim, etc.; but in nearby places it is forbidden according to the Rosh. He then concluded, “and from the words of the Rambam it appears that specifically outside the Land, etc.,” implying that according to the first view, even in the Land of Israel a minor kohen would help. This is not so according to anyone, for in the Land of Israel it is obvious that it is not eaten, and even in nearby places according to the Rosh, as we mentioned. With difficulty one may say that what he wrote, “and from the words of the Rambam, etc.,” means: even according to the Rambam, who does not distinguish between near and far, nevertheless he distinguishes between the Land of Israel and outside the Land. Although he already wrote that nowadays there is no purity in the Land of Israel, meaning that we do not have ashes of the red heifer to sprinkle on one who is tamei met, and therefore challah cannot be eaten, he repeated it here as the essential law because of the tumah of the challah itself, which is tamei by contact, and for this the purity of the person does not help. Here the Shulchan Aruch copied the Rambam’s words, and it is astonishing that in the Beit Yosef he did not notice that there is a dispute here between the Rambam and the Rosh.
