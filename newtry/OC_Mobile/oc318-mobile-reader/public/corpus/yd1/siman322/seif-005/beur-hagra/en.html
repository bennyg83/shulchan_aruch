Even tamei persons. One must say this means one tamei from a corpse; so it is in the Rambam and as stated in Bekhorot 27a-b: what is the law of one tamei from a corpse, etc.? Meaning, whether he requires immersion. What is said there, “and the halakhah is not like him,” means not like the view requiring immersion; rather, he does not need immersion. So wrote Tosafot there s.v. “and the halakhah is not,” unlike Rashi there, who explains it regarding sunset.

And some say, etc. For there it did not mention a minor kohen. See Ran there. It appears that he holds like the Rash wrote, that “one who immersed that day” refers to those tamei by contact, and the sugya in Bekhorot follows Rabbi Yose; this requires study. The source of the Ran's words is the Ramban in Milchamot there, who follows his approach at the end of Hilkhot Challah, explaining the dispute of the sages and Rabbi Yose regarding those tamei by contact, specifically with the second challah of the first conquest and Syria; see there. The sugya in Bekhorot then follows all views. But the Rama brought this view alongside the Rambam and Shulchan Aruch, who ruled that even in Syria it is forbidden only to those whose tumah emerges from their body. Therefore one must say as our master wrote, that the Bekhorot sugya follows Rabbi Yose.
