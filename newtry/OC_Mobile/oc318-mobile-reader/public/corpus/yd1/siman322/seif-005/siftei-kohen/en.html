But where there is no minor boy or girl, and no adult who immersed for keri, one separates one for the fire, with no measure, and one for the kohen, with a measure; and zavim, zavot, niddot, and women after childbirth may eat it in tumah, as explained. The "some say" in the gloss hold that in every case nowadays we separate only one challah. Even if there is no pure kohen, but only someone whose tumah emerges from his own body, one still separates only one challah. The poskim wrote the reason: it must be guarded from non-kohanim and non-Jews, and sometimes they place it in a bowl with hot broth, and now a non-kohen would be forbidden to eat from the bowl. It implies that the "some say" disagree even where there is a minor kohen or a pure adult kohen, holding that one should not give it to them to eat but should burn it. So wrote Maharshal in chapter Kol HaBasar siman 60, that the practice is not to give challah at all to a minor, because we do not maintain a definite kohen nowadays. So too Mahari Weil wrote in the laws of Pesach that his practice was to teach publicly not to feed it to a minor kohen, so that people not say that challah outside Eretz Yisrael is eaten. So too Mahari Weil wrote in responsa siman 153 in the laws of Pesach that the practice is not to give challah to a minor kohen. The reason seems to me to be that we are concerned lest he crumble it and come to a mishap, or because we do not maintain a definite kohen nowadays. These are his words. So too the Rav wrote in Orach Chayim at the end of siman 457, even though there he first stated anonymously like the Tur's view that one feeds it to a minor kohen. This is our practice nowadays with challot of matzah; but with challot of the rest of the year our practice is not to give it to a minor kohen. One must distinguish between Pesach and the rest of the year.
