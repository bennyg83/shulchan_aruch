It has no measure. See Noda BiYehudah Tinyana, Yoreh De'ah siman 201, who developed a deep point: the mitzvah of separating challah contains two elements. One is to remove the prohibition of tevel from the dough, and the second is to give it to a kohen. These two elements differ. To exempt the dough, there is no Torah measure at all; one grain of wheat exempts the entire pile. But to fulfill the mitzvah of giving, there is a Torah measure, since it says "you shall give," requiring something fit to be given. This measure, whether one twenty-fourth or one forty-eighth, is Torah law in one respect and rabbinic in another. For example, taking one twenty-fourth is a significant measure to fulfill the mitzvah of giving; but that one twenty-fourth is measured against an issaron, which is the measure requiring challah. From the Torah's perspective, there is then no difference whether the dough is one issaron or one hundred se'ah. The rabbinic element is one twenty-fourth of the dough as it stands; if it is twenty-four issaronim, he must give a whole issaron to the kohen. See there, where he discusses this at length and fixes it firmly.
