The mitzvah of separating challah contains two elements: removing the prohibition of tevel from the dough, and giving challah to a kohen. These are legally distinct. To remove tevel from the dough, Torah law sets no minimum at all, and one grain can exempt an entire heap. But to fulfill the mitzvah of giving, there must by Torah law be an amount that qualifies as a meaningful gift, because the verse says "you shall give." The familiar measure, one twenty-fourth or one forty-eighth, is therefore Torah-based in one respect and rabbinic in another. Taking one twenty-fourth as an example, this is a meaningful gift measured against one issaron, the minimum dough quantity liable in challah. Once that basic Torah measure is met, Torah law makes no difference whether the dough is one issaron or one hundred seah. Rabbinically, however, one must give one twenty-fourth of the actual dough. Thus if the dough contains twenty-four issaron, one must give an entire issaron to the kohen. See there at length.
