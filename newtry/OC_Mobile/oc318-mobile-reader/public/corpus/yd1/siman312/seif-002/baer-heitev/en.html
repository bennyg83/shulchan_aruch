For himself. The Taz writes that the reason is suspicion. This is difficult in light of the Rama in siman 18, seif 18, who says that in some places the custom is that a person does not slaughter and inspect for himself, implying that it is permitted by strict law. Rabbeinu Shimshon raises this difficulty in the second chapter of Negaim and distinguishes a case where a prohibition had already been established. This also explains why one may inspect his own slaughtered animal, since once it was slaughtered it attained a presumption of permissibility. One might ask that he should not be believed regarding the slaughter itself, since an animal during its lifetime is presumed forbidden. We rely, however, on the rule that most people who perform shechitah are proficient. Concerning examination of blemishes on Yom Tov, see Orach Chaim, siman 498, seif 9. See simanim 18 and 498.
