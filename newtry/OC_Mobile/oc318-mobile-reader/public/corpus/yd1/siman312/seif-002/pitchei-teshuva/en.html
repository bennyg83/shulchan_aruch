The sage may not inspect. See Bechorot 31a, which establishes the mishnah as referring to an individual expert, for three judges are not suspected. Tosafot Yom Tov writes likewise at the end of chapter 2 of Negaim, as does Maadanei Melech, who explains that this is why the Rosh omitted the rule: nowadays, when we have no individual expert and always inspect with three, it is inapplicable. Nevertheless, further study is needed if all three judges are partners in the animal, since an animal of partners is also subject to firstborn sanctity. Rashi explains there that we do not suspect all three of acting improperly for one person's sake. It could therefore be argued that when they all own a share, they are suspect. Yet several passages imply that even in such a case they are not suspected. See Responsa Chavat Yair, siman 121, who was asked whether nowadays one may inspect a firstborn belonging to his brother. He writes that since nowadays it is permitted only by a panel of three, even the owner himself may join the panel, as is explicit in the Gemara. Certainly one may inspect his brother's firstborn. Even in the era when an individual expert could permit it, the mishnah at the end of chapter 2 of Yevamot teaches that all such relatives are permitted with respect to their sons and brothers, even where some suspicion exists.
