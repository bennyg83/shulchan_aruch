The sage does not inspect. See the Talmud in Bekhorot at the end of chapter 4, 31a, which establishes this with an individual expert, for if there are three they are not suspected. So wrote Tosafot Yom Tov at the end of chapter 2 of Nega'im, and so wrote Ma'adanei Melekh, that for this reason the Rosh omitted this law, since for us, who have no individual expert and always inspect with three, it does not apply; see there. However, this requires some study even for us if all three have a partnership in it, for an animal owned by partners is also obligated in bekhorah, as below in siman 320 seif 2. Can they inspect it? Rashi explains there that all of them are not suspected for his sake; see there. If they have a partnership in it, perhaps they are suspected. Nevertheless, it is implied in several places that even in such a case they are not suspected. See Chavot Yair responsa siman 121, who was asked whether one may inspect a bekhor for his brother in this time. He wrote that nowadays, when it is permitted only by three, even the owner of the bekhor may join, as is explicit in the Talmud; all the more so a man may inspect for his brother, which was permitted even when it could be permitted by a single expert, as taught at the end of chapter 2 of Yevamot: all of them are permitted for their sons and brothers, even where there is suspicion. See there.
