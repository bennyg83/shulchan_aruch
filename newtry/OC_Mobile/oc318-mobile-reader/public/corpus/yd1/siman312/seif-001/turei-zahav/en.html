And they fixed compensation for him. Tur says that where the expert's loss of wages is evident, he receives the amount a worker engaged in that occupation would accept to sit idle. Elsewhere, regarding returning lost property and judging, Tur uses somewhat different formulations. Tosafot explain that with quasi-interest the Sages were lenient and require only the wage of complete idleness, whereas returning lost property allows compensation for shifting from strenuous work to this lighter mitzvah. Inspecting a firstborn is not itself ordinary labor, so Tur allows only compensation for sitting idle. Travel expenses for going to inspect are clearly payable.
