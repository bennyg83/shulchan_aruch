And so is the custom, etc. His words are puzzling, for even the first reasoning concedes this, as Rif and Rosh wrote at the end of chapter 19 of Shabbat.
And some say they are obligated, etc. As written there: invalid.
An apostate, etc. Since he violated the covenant; and as written in Torat Kohanim, beginning of Vayikra: "from among you" — to exclude apostates, etc. — apostates are excluded, etc.
Or, etc. And even according to R. Yochanan, and therefore it says specifically "when his brothers died," etc. — Ba'al HaIttur. But Tosafot there s.v. alef-bet wrote the opposite.
An infant who needed, etc. Not as written in siman 262 seif 1 in the gloss; and so Rashba explicitly — that it is like mere flesh-cutting; and even through a Jew his law is like circumcision performed by a non-Jew; and Rosh's proof from what is written in Shabbat that Shabbat is not given [for this], etc. — is not conclusive, for since he is already circumcised what can they do with him; and as written here. However if remnants remained, etc.; and for drawing covenant blood we do not desecrate Shabbat — for even one who holds regarding one born already circumcised that we desecrate Shabbat, the reason is because the foreskin is certainly hidden; however from Rashba's words it appears that even drawing blood is not needed, like the words of Rambam and Shulchan Aruch regarding a non-Jew — their reason being that he is like one born already circumcised; and even for one who requires it there, it is lest the foreskin be hidden, which is not so here.

And it is of no effect, etc. For it is an acquisition in words alone, as written at the beginning of Bava Batra.
But if, etc. See Choshen Mishpat siman 207 seif 19 and siman 209 seif 4 in the gloss.
And if, etc. certainly, etc. As above — certainly he did not transgress regarding the remnant of Israel, etc., and did not send [it] to another except when the first [portion] ran out.
