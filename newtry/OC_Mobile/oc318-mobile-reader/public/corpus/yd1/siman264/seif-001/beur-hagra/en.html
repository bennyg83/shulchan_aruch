Even a slave. According to all opinions, for they disagreed only regarding a woman.
A woman, etc. Since the dispute is between Rav and Rabbi Yehudah, the halachah follows Rabbi Yehudah, as stated in the first chapter of Yom Tov (4a).
And a minor. Because intent for the sake of the mitzvah is not required. This is stated there in the Gemara.
And an uncircumcised man, etc. This is stated in the Gemara there according to all opinions.
And some say, etc. See Tosafot there, s.v. ishah, etc.
And this is the custom, etc. His words are puzzling, for even the first opinion agrees to this, as stated by the Rif and the Rosh at the end of the nineteenth chapter of Shabbos.
And some say that they are obligated, etc. As stated there, it is invalid.
An apostate, etc. Since he has violated the covenant, as stated in Torat Kohanim at the beginning of Parashat Vayikra: "from among you," to exclude apostates, etc.; apostates are excluded, etc.
Or, etc. This applies even according to Rabbi Yochanan, and therefore it specifically states that his brothers died, etc. This is the view of the Baal HaIttur. Tosafot there, s.v. i ba’it eima, however, wrote the opposite.
An infant for whom it was necessary, etc. This is unlike what is stated in siman 262, seif 1, in the gloss. The Rashba likewise wrote explicitly that this is merely like cutting flesh, and even when performed by a Jew its law is the same as circumcision performed by a gentile. The Rosh's proof from the statement in Shabbos that Shabbos was not given, etc., is not decisive, for once he has already been circumcised, what can now be done to him? This accords with what is stated here: however, if remnants remain, etc. Shabbos is not desecrated merely to draw blood of the covenant. Even according to the opinion that Shabbos is desecrated for an infant born circumcised, the reason is that he certainly has a compressed foreskin. From the Rashba's words, however, it appears that even drawing blood is unnecessary, in accordance with the Rambam and the Shulchan Aruch regarding circumcision by a gentile. Their rationale is that he is like one born circumcised. Even the opinion that requires drawing blood there does so only because there may be a compressed foreskin, which does not apply here.
And a person has, etc. See Shulchan Aruch, siman 265, seif 11.
And if he gave it, etc.; however, etc. As stated in Bava Metzia 49a: here too, this is reasonable, etc.; he gave it to a Levite, etc. See Rashi there.
And it is ineffective, etc. For this is merely an acquisition of words, as stated at the beginning of Bava Batra.
But if, etc. See Choshen Mishpat, siman 207, seif 19, and siman 209, seif 4, in the gloss.
And if, etc., for certainly, etc. As explained above, he certainly did not transgress "the remnant of Israel," etc., and he sent for another only because the first person was unavailable.
A woman, etc. As explained above in siman 261. See Tosafot to Avodah Zarah there, s.v. ishah, etc.
