Whose brothers died, etc. This applies only when he does not intend to repudiate the covenant.
And this is the custom, etc. I do not understand his meaning. Even according to the Mechaber, an adult Jewish man takes precedence over everyone else, and for that reason the custom is to seek out a man. If he means that even where no adult Jewish man is available one does not allow a woman to circumcise, and instead seeks an adult man because a woman is unfit to circumcise, as the Bach writes in explaining the view of the Semak and the Hagahot Mordechai, namely that even after the fact, where no man is available, a woman may not circumcise, then the expression “and this is the custom” is inappropriate, because no custom can be established regarding something uncommon, as I wrote above in siman 190, seif katan 3. Furthermore, even if this was the practice, it may simply have been because women did not know how to circumcise. This requires study.
And some say, etc. The Beit Yosef writes that the first opinion also agrees in this case. See there.
His status is like that of a non-Jew. Meaning, regarding circumcision his status is like that of a non-Jew. For other matters, however, one who rejects circumcision is not treated as a non-Jew, because one who is an apostate regarding a single matter is not thereby an apostate regarding the entire Torah, as explained above in simanim 90 and 119.
There is no distinction, etc. Meaning, even a non-Jew may circumcise him before the eighth day.
However, if strands remained, etc. Meaning, if strands of foreskin remained, he must complete the circumcision according to halachah, either by removing the strands or by performing periah. But if nothing remained, one must draw covenantal blood, because a circumcision performed before the eighth day, even by a Jew, is not considered a circumcision at all. The statement does not mean that when no strands remain there is no need even to draw covenantal blood, because circumcision before the eighth day is not called circumcision. I also concluded above in siman 262, seif katan 2, that covenantal blood must be drawn when the child was circumcised before the eighth day. Proof of this is that the present ruling also addresses circumcision performed by a non-Jew, and even according to the Rama covenantal blood must then be drawn. Clearly, therefore, the intent is not to exempt him from drawing covenantal blood. Examine this carefully.
A kinyan is ineffective for this. This is because it is merely a kinyan concerning words, as stated in a responsum of the Rosh. It is possible that the Rosh meant only that beit din cannot compel him, while nevertheless agreeing with Maharam that the verse “The remnant of Israel shall not commit injustice, nor shall they speak falsehood” applies, and that one may call him wicked. Although the Rosh wrote there that Maharam had ruled that one should follow the custom, but that this did not appear correct to him, his objection concerned only the claim that custom itself creates a binding kinyan, as implied by the Mordechai above in the name of Maharam, who compared it to situmta, a customary commercial act that effects acquisition. The Rosh may still agree with Maharam that one may not retract initially. This is also implied by his wording in the responsum, as cited by the Darkei Moshe.
