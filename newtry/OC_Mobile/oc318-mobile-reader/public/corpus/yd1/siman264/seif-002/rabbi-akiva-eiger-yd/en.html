(There in the gloss) An apostate regarding foreskin — his law is like a gentile. It is astounding to me, for from Tosafot's words, from what they asked why he did not say that an apostate regarding foreskin is not included in keeping the covenant, as we say in Gittin that an apostate is not fit for ritual slaughter; and according to the one who says "the circumcised shall circumcise," it is resolved that an apostate regarding foreskin is like one who is circumcised and is fit according to R. Yehuda; and since we hold that a woman is fit to circumcise, as Rif, Rambam, Rosh, and Tur wrote, because we hold that when no man is present she is fit — and in any case if she circumcised, one need not let blood of the covenant flow — the same applies to an apostate regarding foreskin. To say Tosafot deals with not for provocation and Rama with not for provocation, in which case he is not like one circumcised — this is forced, for Tosafot too must deal with not for provocation; when for provocation he is called fit for slaughter as he is called fit for slaughter. To say Tosafot is when not for provocation and not to provoke but he is not careful to circumcise, and Rama when to provoke — though Rama's wording is difficult to explain — nevertheless from Shach's wording (s.k. 1) who wrote "but if he intends to break the covenant" it does not imply that only when intending to break the covenant is he unfit; but in my view one cannot say so, for if Rama deals with provoking, what is the relevance of "apostate regarding foreskin even in one matter" — behold to provoke he is a heretic and apostate to the entire Torah, as in Shulchan Aruch above (siman 2 seif 4); and especially that even regarding apostate to entire Torah it appears Tosafot's view is to permit, for according to their answer "you bound them" is in a place of binding (and one must say that for provocation he is called one who upholds binding and slaughter). Requires further study.
