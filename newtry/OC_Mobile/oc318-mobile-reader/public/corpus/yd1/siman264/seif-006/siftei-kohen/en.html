He must correct the excess flesh, etc. At first glance this implies that he corrects it by cutting away the excess flesh. From the Rambam, however, in chapter 2 of Hilchot Milah, it appears that nothing need be cut, since he looks circumcised when the organ is erect. Rather, one should correct it by working with the child, pushing the skin backward, and tying it there around the organ above the corona, if it can remain there without returning downward. If this cannot be done, nothing need be cut. The Terumat HaDeshen writes likewise in siman 264, and the Bach rules accordingly.
Since he was once circumcised properly. This means that the present case differs from seif 5, where most of the corona must be exposed, because there the circumcision had never been completed according to halachah. Here, however, since he was circumcised properly, on a Torah level he would not again be considered uncircumcised even if he did not look circumcised at all when erect; the issue is only rabbinic. Since the head of the corona and a little below it appear circumcised, he is regarded as circumcised. The Terumat HaDeshen writes this in siman 264. The Bach adds that if a child was born partially circumcised, but skin still covered most of the height or circumference of the corona, then even if covenantal blood was drawn on the eighth day, he must later be circumcised fully if, when erect, only part of the corona appears exposed. Flesh must be removed from both sides until he appears fully circumcised when erect. The exemption from cutting applies only where the original circumcision was properly performed. One who was never properly circumcised has the same status as a child whose circumcision left strands covering most of the height of the corona, which prevent completion of the mitzvah. The Beit Yosef cites a Spanish sage who is even more stringent, maintaining that examination during erection applies only to those who were initially circumcised properly but later became fleshy or the skin became loose. In every other case, the corona must be exposed even when the organ is not erect. The Bach states that he ruled accordingly in practice. This view requires study, because the Yerushalmi states regarding the strands that prevent circumcision that one examines him when the organ is erect, which indicates that this examination also applies where the circumcision was not initially completed properly.
