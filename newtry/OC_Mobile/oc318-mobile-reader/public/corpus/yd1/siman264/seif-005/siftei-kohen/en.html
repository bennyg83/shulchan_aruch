Even in one place, etc. This wording appears in all editions of the Tur and is also used by the Mechaber below in siman 266, seif 2, but it is not precise. It appears that the meaning is: even when the remaining flesh covers most of the height of the corona in one place, and certainly when it covers most of its height in several places. More likely, however, the word “even” has been displaced, and the text should read: if skin remains that covers even most of its height in one place, meaning that all the more so it is disqualifying when it covers most of its circumference. Rashi likewise writes that the Mishnah should not be understood as requiring most of the circumference, because even most of the height in one place is sufficient. The same wording appears in the Remezim: flesh covering most of the height of the corona prevents completion of the circumcision. The Mechaber likewise writes in Orach Chaim, beginning of siman 331, that flesh covering even most of the height of the corona in one place prevents completion. The Beit Yosef cites a Spanish sage who explains that the corona for purposes of circumcision is the entire flesh at the head of the organ, and the primary mitzvah is to expose the whole corona together with its surrounding ridge, which is its principal part. There are two independent forms of obstruction: flesh covering most of the circumference even without covering most of the height, and flesh covering most of the height even without covering most of the circumference. Thus, whether strands cover most of the height of the head in one place, or flesh or strands cover most of the circumference of the ridge that forms the principal corona, even without covering most of the height of the head, he remains uncircumcised as before and must be circumcised again. The Darkei Moshe cites this, and the Bach rules likewise.
