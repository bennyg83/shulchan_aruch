Chatam Sofer, citing Maharit, distinguishes discretionary obligations, which may sometimes be delayed, from a mitzvah such as marriage or buying a home in Eretz Yisrael, which must be fulfilled promptly when a suitable opportunity arises. In a matchmaking agreement secured by reciprocal monetary penalties, if the money was only security for the marriage, the marriage is primary and delay beyond the customary preparation time activates the penalty and releases the other party.
