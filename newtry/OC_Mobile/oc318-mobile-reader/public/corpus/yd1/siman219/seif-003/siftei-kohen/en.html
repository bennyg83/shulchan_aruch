It is forbidden to him forever, etc. Darkei Moshe writes that Hagahot Mordechai to Shevuos appears to hold that this applies specifically to a vow, since the person prohibited objects to himself. An oath, in which he prohibits himself from an object, resembles nezirus, whose unspecified duration is thirty days. He nevertheless concludes there in the name of Rosh at the end of the chapter Shevuos Shetayim that an unspecified oath too is forbidden forever. This is also explicit in Rosh there. We can now understand the difficulty in Bach's statement that Rosh wrote this at the end of Shevuos Shetayim based upon reasoning. In my humble opinion, this law is explained in the first chapter of Nedarim, 4, etc. The Gemara, however, explicitly discusses only vows, while Rosh discusses oaths. Rama likewise writes below in siman 237, seif 4, concerning an oath, that if one swore without specifying a duration, the prohibition lasts forever.
