One who purchases the shearing of his flock, etc. Explanation: the view of the author is that specifically when one purchases the shearing of a non-Jew's flock after the non-Jew has already shorn them — since it belonged to the non-Jew after the shearing — he is exempt. But if he purchased the sheep for their shearing, meaning he purchased the shearing while it was still attached, even though he did not purchase the sheep themselves but only the shearing, since he purchased it while attached before the non-Jew shorn them, he is obligated — even though the shearing grew in the possession of the non-Jew, and even though the sheep itself belongs to the non-Jew. And those who disagree hold that even if he purchased it while attached before shearing, he is exempt when the body of the sheep belongs to the non-Jew, and he is not obligated unless he also purchased the sheep for their shearing, meaning that they should be his at the time of shearing. Examine this carefully.
