His fellow. While it is still attached.
Began. For there is a presumption that a person does not sell gifts of priesthood, and the kohen's gift remains with the seller.
The purchaser is obligated. For the seller says to him: I did not sell you the kohen's gift, and the money I took from you I took only for my wool, apart from the kohen's portion.
Specified. The Shach wrote that this means: if after shearing he sold it, the seller is obligated, for from the time of shearing the obligation to give took effect upon him, so why did he sell it? The purchaser is exempt, for it is not "the shearing of your flock." If he sold it to him while it was still attached and specified, "I am selling you everything," both are exempt: the seller is exempt because while it was attached the obligation of the first shearing had not yet taken effect upon him, and at the time of shearing it was not "the shearing of your flock," for the shearing was not his; and the purchaser is also exempt, for the flock is not his. Nevertheless, at the outset it is forbidden to do this, to sell the wool while attached and remove one's money from a mitzvah; end quote.
