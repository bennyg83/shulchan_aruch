They are permitted if they have no intention of returning. It is surprising, for here he wrote according to the words of the Rivash siman 99, who derived this from the Gemara in the first chapter of Chullin (page 18) regarding that which Rabbi Zeira answered: if he has no intention of returning, it applies — and this is according to the method of Tosafot and the Rosh in the chapter "Makom she-Nahagu," and likewise the opinion of the Ran there and the Tur in Orach Chaim siman 568 and likewise Turei Zahav in Orach Chaim siman 574 regarding one who travels from a place where they fast to a place where they do not fast, and his intention is to return — he must fast, etc.; thus if he has no intention of returning he need not fast. But in Orach Chaim siman 568 seif 4 the author cited the words of the Rambam at the end of Hilchot Yom Tov, and it appears from his words that even if he has no intention of returning, they impose upon him the stringencies of the place he left from there — and this is according to the sugya in the chapter "Makom she-Nahagu" — and likewise it appears from the words of the Rav HaMagid there that such is the opinion of the Rambam, see there; and the author did not bring there the opinion of the Rosh and his supporters at all (and in Beit Yosef there he brought only the opinion of the Rosh and his supporters and did not mention the opinion of the Rambam at all). And it is possible that the author's opinion is to distinguish between one who has no intention of returning but nevertheless has no intention of settling in the city he went to, only in another place — and in that manner it deals in Orach Chaim siman 568 — and between one whose intention is to settle in the place he went to — and in that manner it deals there siman 574, and here — and it requires investigation. And regarding the law, it appears according to the opinion of the Rosh and his supporters that he must always act according to the custom of the place where his intention is to settle, whether for leniency or stringency (only in a place where there is concern for dispute he should not change from the place where he is, even to be lenient). And likewise wrote Rabbeinu Yerucham netiv 5 chapter 5, and likewise the Levush in Orach Chaim siman 568 did not write the words of the author but the words of the Tur. And one must force an explanation as Tosafot wrote in the chapter "Makom she-Nahagu" and in the first chapter of Chullin that they teach distinct matters: rather the Mishnah always deals with one whose intention is to return, as is explained in Chullin; but when he has no intention of returning he is not obligated in the stringencies of the place he left from, only in the stringencies of the place he went to — for just as he is obligated in the stringencies of the place he left when his intention is to return, because he is like a resident of the city he left since his intention is to settle there, so too when he has no intention of returning they impose upon him the stringencies of the place he went to since his intention is to settle there in the place he went, and he is like a resident of that city — for it is one reason. And if so, the challenge in the chapter "Makom she-Nahagu" is well posed, and the answer is: where he has no intention of returning, he heard from the Mishnah that he practices according to the place he went to by law; but Rabbah in Beit Chafetz — if his intention is to return it applies, and if so he is not obligated in the stringencies of the place he went to, only because he should not change due to the dispute — and therefore he concealed it. And likewise the Ran in the chapter "Makom she-Nahagu" — and this is the main point.
