According to their practice. However, this applies only to an important custom established on the authority of Torah scholars, but for an inferior custom he is not obligated to follow their practice when not in their presence or in the presence of Torah scholars — only in the presence of unlearned people is he obligated to follow it so as not to show disrespect. So wrote Tosafot. However, Rosh wrote that if there is concern of controversy, it is forbidden in every case, for one must not deviate from the local custom on account of controversy. And see in Orach Chayyim end of siman 496, where it is explained that [a traveler] who has gone beyond the Shabbat boundary is not subject to the stringencies of the place he has gone to. And see above end of siman 83. (And see Knesset HaGedolah who brought distinctions in the laws of customs: a custom adopted to honor the Torah overrides even a rabbinic prohibition — Maharik root 9; and see in Masa Melech in the laws of customs. And see in Chayei Adam siman 126, where it is explained whether a son is obligated to follow the piety and separation practiced by his father. And there is explained the law of a community that was destroyed and its members scattered — whether they are still bound by all the stringencies of the place that was destroyed; see Leket Kemach siman 103, see there.):
To return. Sh"akh wrote that there is reason to wonder, for in Orach Chayyim siman 468 seif 4 it appears from the author's words that even if he does not intend to return, the stringencies of the place he left are imposed upon him. It is possible that the author's intent is to distinguish between: having no intention to return yet also having no intention to settle in the city he went to — only somewhere else — and this case is discussed in Orach Chayyim 468; but here and in Orach Chayyim 574 it discusses where his intention is to settle in the place he went to. This requires further study. And regarding the practical ruling, it appears to follow the view of Rosh and his school: he should always follow the custom of the place where he intends to settle, whether for leniency or for stringency — except in a case where controversy is likely, in which case he should not deviate from where he currently is, even toward leniency:
