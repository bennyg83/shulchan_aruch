But. Proof comes from the first chapter of Chullin 6b, where Rabbi permitted Beit She'an. The Yerushalmi, cited by Rosh in Pesachim there, states: R. Yosi bar Bun said that anything a person does not know to be permitted, but mistakenly practices as forbidden, he may ask and they permit it for him, meaning without formal annulment by a sage. Anything he knows to be permitted but nevertheless practices as forbidden, he must ask, meaning through formal annulment by a sage, and they do not simply permit it without annulment.
And there is one who says. This authority explains "they permit him" and "they do not permit him" as referring in both cases to formal annulment by a sage, which appears at first glance to be the simple reading of the Yerushalmi. However, the Bavli's derivation from the verse, "He shall not profane his word," indicates that the custom has the force of a vow, supporting the first explanation. Ran.
