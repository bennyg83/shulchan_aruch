To transgress a custom requires release; it contains two se’ifim. Permitted matters, and they know that they are permitted — if they practiced prohibition regarding them, it is as if they accepted them upon themselves by vow, and it is forbidden to permit them without release. Therefore, one who is accustomed to fast the fasts before Rosh Hashanah and those between Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur, and one who is accustomed not to eat meat and not to drink wine from Rosh Chodesh Av or from the 17th of Tammuz, and wants to retract because he is not healthy — needs three to release him. If at the time he began to practice his intention was to practice so forever, and he practiced so even once — he needs release, and they open for him with regret that he regrets having practiced so as a vow. Therefore, one who wants to practice in some permitted matters for a fence and separation should say at the beginning of his practice that he does not accept it upon himself as a vow, and also that he does not intend to practice so except that time, or the times he wishes, and not forever. But those who practice prohibition in permitted matters because they think they are forbidden — it is not as if they accepted them by vow. And there is one who says that if he erred and practiced prohibition in a permitted matter, he is questioned and they release him with three, in the manner of vow release. And if he knows it is permitted and practiced prohibition regarding it — they do not release him, even in the manner of vow release, for it is as if he accepted upon himself like prohibitions that the Torah forbade, which have no permit forever. (And the custom is according to the first view.)
