They practiced. Because of a fence, safeguard, and abstinence.
Healthy. The Shach wrote: I saw someone ask that here the Rav wrote plainly like the Mechaber, that if he is not healthy he needs release; but in Orach Chayim siman 562 at the end of seif 2, he wrote in the gloss that in the Ten Days of Repentance, if a brit milah occurs, it is a mitzvah to eat and one does not need release, for they did not practice fasting in such a case, etc. If so, here too let us say that they did not practice fasting when one is not healthy. This is not a challenge: there, they did not practice fasting when a brit milah occurred, and they practiced eating, so everyone who fasts does so with that in mind. But here, he did not bring to mind that when he is not healthy he will not fast, and therefore he needs release.
With regret. Maharshal wrote in a responsum that release through regret applies only to what some of the common people practiced, but not all of them, as the language implies, "and others practiced it as forbidden." But something practiced and accepted by all Israel cannot be released in any manner, and this is simple; end quote, Shach.
Who think. (And now it became clear that it is permitted and their custom was mistaken, it is not an acceptance.)
