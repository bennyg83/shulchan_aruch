Driving. Farming a fence and simplicity:
Healthy. Shach writes that someone asked why Rama here follows the Mechaber that if a person is not healthy he requires hatarat neder, whereas in Orach Chayyim siman 562, seif 2, Rama writes that during the Ten Days of Repentance, if a brit milah occurs, it is a mitzvah to eat and no annulment is required because the custom to fast was never accepted in such a case. The question is unfounded. There, people never adopted the fast when a brit milah occurs and customarily eat, so anyone who accepted the fast did so with that understanding. Here, however, he did not have in mind when accepting the practice that if he later became unwell he would not fast. He therefore requires annulment. See Orach Chayyim siman 562.
With regret. Maharshal writes in a responsum that annulment through regret applies only to a practice adopted by some of the populace, as implied by the phrase, "and others practiced it as prohibited." A practice adopted by all of Israel cannot be annulled in this manner under any circumstances. Shach writes that this is obvious.
Who believed. If it has now become clear that the matter is permitted and their custom was based on error, it is not considered a binding acceptance. Shach writes that the same applies if they thought it involved a severe prohibition and afterward it became clear that the prohibition was not so severe. It is not considered a neder, as stated below in siman 232, seif 10. See siman 232.
