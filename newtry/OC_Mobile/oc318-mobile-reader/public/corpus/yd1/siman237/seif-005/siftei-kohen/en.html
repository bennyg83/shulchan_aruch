One who says twice, etc. For Scripture states, “Never again shall all flesh be cut off by the waters of the Flood,” and, “Never again shall the waters become a flood,” and it further states, “For this is like the waters of Noach to Me, as I have sworn.” Since saying “no, no” constitutes an oath, saying “yes, yes” likewise constitutes an oath.
Provided that he intends, etc. This implies that the rule applies even if he did not previously mention an oath. I am therefore puzzled by what Rabbeinu Peretz wrote in the glosses to Tashbetz, siman 401, that this applies only if he mentioned an oath before saying “no, no” or “yes, yes.” If so, even saying “no” or “yes” once should constitute an oath, for it is no less than an incomplete expression. Perhaps he refers to a case where the person mentioned an oath earlier, but then interrupted with another matter, so that the later words do not constitute an incomplete expression of that oath.
