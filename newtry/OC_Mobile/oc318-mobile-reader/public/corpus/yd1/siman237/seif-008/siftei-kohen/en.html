Provided that he intends, etc. Meaning, that he intends to swear by the right and left of the Holy One, blessed be He. This is also implied by the language of the Raavad cited by the Tur: “If he intended the right and left of the Creator, it constitutes an oath.” End quote. The Ateres Zahav writes likewise: “Provided that he intends the right and left mentioned by the Holy One, blessed be He, meaning His right and His left.” End quote. This is also implied by the Perishah. The Bach, however, appears not to have understood it this way, and therefore wrote: “This is puzzling. What difference does it make if he intended the right mentioned by the Creator, since other things are also called right and left? How is this different from one who swears by heaven and earth? Furthermore, it is no stronger than explicitly saying ‘an oath,’ and according to the Rambam he must explicitly mention the Divine Name or an appellation in order to incur a korban. It therefore appears to me that even when swearing by right and left, he must mention the Divine Name or an appellation. Nevertheless, he must also intend the right and left mentioned by the Holy One, blessed be He, for otherwise it is not an oath with respect to a korban or lashes, just as with saying ‘no, no.’” End quote. I also do not understand his statement that one must mention the Divine Name or an appellation and nevertheless also intend the right and left. Once he mentions the Divine Name or an appellation, why are right and left needed? There is no greater oath than this. Rather, he certainly does not mention either the Divine Name or an appellation, and nevertheless it constitutes an oath. This is not comparable to one who swears by heaven and earth, because heaven and earth are not appellations of God, whereas right and left are appellations. It is therefore as though he swore by the Holy One, blessed be He, Himself, since the Holy One, blessed be He, swore by His right hand and by His mighty arm. Therefore, when he intends the right and left of the Creator, it is a complete oath.
