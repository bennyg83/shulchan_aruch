And it is that he intend, etc. — meaning his intention is to swear by the right and left of the Holy One; and so implied in Raavad's language in Tur — end quote: if he intended the right and left of the Creator, it is an oath — end. And so Atzei Levanon; and it is that he intend the right and left that the Holy One said — meaning His right and His left, blessed be He — end. And so implied in Perishah. But Bach seems not to have understood thus; therefore he wrote wonder: if he intended the right that the Creator said — what of it? Since there is something else called right and left. And what is different from one who swears on heaven and earth? And furthermore, not better than if he said explicitly "oath" — for according to Rambam he must remember the name or epithet explicitly for the matter of sacrifice; therefore it seems the essential view: even with right and left he must remember the name and epithet; and nevertheless must intend the right and left that the Holy One said, for otherwise it is not an oath for sacrifice and lashes, and as the law of negative-negative — end quote. Also what he wrote that even with right and left he must mention name and epithet and nevertheless must intend right and left, etc. — I did not grasp the end of his view. For since he mentions name and epithet, why right and left — you have no greater oath than this? Rather certainly he does not mention neither name nor epithet, and nevertheless it is an oath. And one can say like one who swears on heaven and earth — heaven and earth are not epithets; but right and left are epithets, and it is as if he swore on the Holy One Himself, for the Holy One swore by His right and by the arm of His might; therefore when he intends the right and left of the Creator, it is a complete oath.
