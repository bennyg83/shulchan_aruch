One who says, etc. Even though we say there, 35b, that a Name is required, and the Sages disagree only in that a Name is not required — but a cognomen is required, as stated in the mishnah there; and there, 38b: even if you say the Sages [disagree] — that is to obligate a sacrifice, but the prohibition of the Name always applies. Rambam, chapter 2. Proof from the beginning of Nedarim: cognomens for oaths, etc. — there, 10a: "shvuta, shpika, etc." and there, hence without a Name; as stated there: cognomens, etc. — "language, etc." — perhaps he said "korban to the Lord"; and in Shevuot 36a: "not, not" is an oath; and if with a Name, even once, as written at the end of Yoma (84a): "to the God of Israel I will not reveal"; and at the beginning of chapter 3 of Nedarim: "Mari kola lo ta'amna"; see Ran there, s.v. "and if you vowed, etc." — it follows that the Name and cognomen are mentioned only for sacrifice and lashing. Ran, Nedarim 2a, s.v. "and oaths"; Rosh, chapter Shevuat HaEidut, seif 24. The clearest approach of all is Rambam's; and so wrote Ran in Shevuot there.
And there is no distinction, etc. As written in Nedarim there: "shvuta, etc."; "he vowed by mohi, etc." — and we say there: R. Yishmael said, "These are foreign [non-oath] expressions." And see above at the end of siman 207; and there: "Mari kola lo ta'amna."
