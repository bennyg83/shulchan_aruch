One who says, etc. Although we say there, 35b, that the Divine Name is required, and even the Sages disagree only to say that the actual Name is unnecessary, while a substitute Name is still required, as stated there in the Mishnah; and likewise there, 38b, even according to the Sages, that is only regarding liability for an offering, but a prohibition always applies even without the Name. This is the Rambam's view in chapter 2. A proof is the statement at the beginning of Nedarim concerning substitutes for oaths, and there, 10a, forms such as "shevusah" and "shekukasah" are effective. That case must be without the Divine Name, as indicated there by the discussion whether substitutes are forms of speech and whether one might have said, "an offering to Hashem." Likewise, Shevuos 36a states that saying "no, no" constitutes an oath; if the Divine Name had been used, even one utterance would suffice, as in Yoma 84a, "By the God of Israel, I will not reveal it," and at the beginning of the third chapter of Nedarim, "Master of all, I will not taste." See Ran there, s.v. Ve'i nadarta. Rather, mention of the Divine Name or a substitute Name is required only for liability to an offering or lashes. See Ran to Nedarim 2a, s.v. U'shevuos, and Rosh, chapter Shevuas HaEdus, siman 24. The clearest approach among all the views is that of the Rambam, and the Ran in Shevuos likewise writes so.
And there is no distinction, etc. As stated there in Nedarim regarding expressions such as "shevusah" and "a vow by Mohi." The Gemara says there that, according to Rabbi Yochanan, these are foreign-language expressions. See also the end of siman 207, and the expression there, "Master of all, I will not taste."
