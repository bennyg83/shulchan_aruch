The same applies to one who swears by a prophet, etc. Even though his intent is only toward the One Who sent that prophet, or toward the One Who commanded what is written in that sacred text.
Or by another book of the sacred writings. But one who swears by the Torah requires release in every case, as the Rama wrote above in siman 212.
Or by a table, etc. See above, siman 204, seif 2, in the gloss.
But if he took it in his hand, etc. The responsa of Maharam Mintz, siman 76, states that if he placed his hand upon them, his law is the same as if he took them in his hand. Lifting them is unnecessary; merely holding them is sufficient. See there, where he discusses this at length.
And he said, “By what is written in them.” The responsa of Maharam Mintz there states that if he opened them and swore by them, it is as though he said, “By what is written in them.” Although the responsum discusses the Ten Commandments, the same appears to apply to all sacred books, since the reasoning is identical.
