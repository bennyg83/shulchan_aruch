Or in the temple. “Look at what I have written in the letter of Hashem’s words, ‘And I will not be able to say to you, and I will not give you a little bit of trouble, but it is a mistake for you to do so.’
And what is written "like the vows of the wicked" — since wicked people are accustomed to swear, [accepting something "like the vow of the wicked"] is considered as if he uttered the oath from his mouth in what he accepted upon himself as [being] like the vow of the wicked.
And what is written "that I am not eating from you [she-i ochel lecha]" — this is also the text in some Tur manuscripts; but it is a scribal error, for if so it would be obvious [that a vow takes effect]; rather it should read "that I cannot eat from you [she-i ochel lecha]" — for so we say explicitly in the Gemara in Rav Ashi's resolution, and [the Gemara] challenges "it is obvious"; to which the answer is: you might have thought it is a lapse in language — that he meant to say "that I am eating from you [she-ochel lecha]" and tripped over his words and said "she-i ochel" — [therefore] it teaches us: since he uttered "she-i ochel" with his lips, we do not believe him when he says he was merely stumbling in speech. So wrote the Rosh, chapter 2 of Nedarim.
