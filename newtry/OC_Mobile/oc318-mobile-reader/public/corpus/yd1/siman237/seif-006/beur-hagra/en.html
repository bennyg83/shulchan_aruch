One who swore, etc. This is the Rambam's ruling. The Raavad, however, writes that the Mishnah's statement that such persons are exempt means only that they are exempt from an offering and lashes.
Although, etc. This parallels the opening clause, "I adjure you, etc., by an oath, etc.," and the concluding clause, "by Alef-Dalet, etc." Although Rava said there in the Gemara, 35b, "If so, etc.," and answered, "The cases are not comparable, etc.," that distinction concerns only what is expressed by his lips.
The same applies to one who swore, etc. As stated in Nedarim 14b; see Ran and Rosh there, s.v. HaNoder. This is the meaning of the gloss, "and all this, etc.," and the same applies if he did not, etc., as stated there. See also the gloss at the end of siman 212, where the Rama is stringent regarding one who swears by the Torah itself. Therefore, the gloss here says, "or by other sacred writings, etc.," but does not include the Torah itself.
Or by the table, etc. For even swearing by the Torah is ineffective, as explained above. Hagahos Mordechai there.
Nevertheless, they frighten him, etc. As stated there in Nedarim 1a, and there, 20a, concerning one who vows by cherem, etc.
Even, etc., or an alphabet book, etc. See Rosh, chapter 3 of Nedarim, siman 2, in his response beginning, "You asked concerning one who took a book of supplications, etc.," and further there. The Mordechai rules likewise. If so, the same applies to a book of secular wisdom. Beis Yosef.
