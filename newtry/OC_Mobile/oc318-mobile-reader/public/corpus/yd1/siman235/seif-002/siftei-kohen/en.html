And she provides for herself, etc. Otherwise it is obvious that the vow does not take effect, since he is obligated to provide for her.
And she does not provide, etc. This implies that if she provides even for the minor necessities, the vow takes effect and he need not divorce her. The Hagahos Ashiri at the beginning of the chapter HaMadir writes that this requires study.
Nevertheless, since he prohibited her, etc. Meaning, since he prohibited her and she does not eat with him, he becomes obligated in these expenses immediately. Yet he may not support her directly, because he prohibited her from receiving benefit from him. He must therefore appoint an intermediary to provide for her. See Even HaEzer, siman 70.
Whoever provides for her will not lose. The Perishah writes that afterward he must reimburse the provider. Likewise, above in siman 221, seif 8, when he says, “Whoever provides will not lose,” he must afterward reimburse him. Only where she goes to a shopkeeper with whom he regularly deals is he not obligated to reimburse the shopkeeper unless he wishes, for the shopkeeper appears to have acted as his agent. This is contrary to the Ateres Zahav, who writes here as well that he is not obligated to reimburse him. The Bach also appears to hold that he must reimburse him; see there. See also above, siman 221, seif 8.
After thirty days he must divorce her, etc. This applies when he prohibited her without specifying a period, or for more than thirty days. If he explicitly prohibited her for thirty days, he must remain married to her.
He must divorce her, etc. During the first thirty days she does not object to being supported through an intermediary. Beyond that point the matter is increasingly degrading to her before other people. He must therefore divorce her and pay her kesubah.
