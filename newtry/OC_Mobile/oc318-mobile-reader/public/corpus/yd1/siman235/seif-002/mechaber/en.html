If he forbade her not to derive benefit from him, it does not take effect, for behold he is obligated to her. And if he said to her, "Go—your handiwork is for your sustenance," and she provides from them for large matters but does not provide for small matters—if her entire family is accustomed to those small matters, it does not take effect, for behold he is obligated to her regarding them as well. And if not all her family is accustomed to them but only the wealthy, and her father is wealthy and accustomed to them, and her husband is not wealthy and not accustomed to them—the vow takes effect, for behold he is not obligated to her for them. Nevertheless, since he forbade her benefit from him and she returned to her father's house, he is now obligated to her for them; therefore, for up to thirty days he provides her support for those small matters through a supporter who says, "Whoever feeds does not lose," and he feeds her and collects afterward from the husband. It makes no difference whether he forbade her for thirty days or more, or forbade her without specification—after thirty days he divorces her and gives the ketubah.
