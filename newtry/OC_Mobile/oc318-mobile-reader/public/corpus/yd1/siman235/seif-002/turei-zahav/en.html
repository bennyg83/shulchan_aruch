For he is obligated to her. Tosafos at the beginning of the chapter “One who imposes a vow” asks that we find a case where he is not obligated to support her even without saying, “Use your handiwork,” namely, where he makes support dependent upon relations, saying, “Your relations shall be forbidden if I support you.” Tosafos answers that if so, the Mishnah should not say that after thirty days he must divorce. Rather, after thirty days she would receive support and relations would become forbidden, and he would not need to divorce until seven more days passed. Nor can the case be that he says, “Your relations are forbidden to me from now if you benefit from me,” for then she may not benefit, like one who says, “My eyes are forbidden in sleep today if I sleep tomorrow,” since people are not careful with conditions. The seven additional days mentioned by Tosafos after thirty days apply specifically here. During the first thirty days the vow against benefit can exist without depending on relations, because a third party can support her. After thirty days that is impossible. The vow then no longer takes effect on its own, but because it was made dependent on relations, the prohibition of relations begins and he waits seven more days. This differs from the later law in this seif, “therefore, until thirty days,” etc., which implies that the vow continues afterward without being made dependent on relations and he must divorce immediately. There the vow fully takes effect regarding minor benefits for which he has no present obligation while she remains with him. Once the vow causes her to go to her father’s house, those obligations arise. The Sages therefore allowed thirty days during which those minor needs may be supplied through a third party; after thirty days he must divorce immediately. Here, however, he is presently obligated to support her himself, not through a third party. She merely waives this for thirty days. The vow therefore cannot take effect afterward unless it was made dependent upon relations, and she then receives another seven days like one prohibited only from relations.
Until thirty days, etc. Beyond thirty days people hear about it and the matter becomes degrading to her. Up to thirty days it can remain private. Therefore, she waits, perhaps within that period he will find an opening for his vow. Whenever delay is possible in this siman, we delay for this reason.
And he may collect from the husband. This means that if the husband wishes, he may reimburse him. It must not be an enforceable obligation. I already wrote about this in siman 221, seif 8, unlike what is written here and in the Perishah there.
