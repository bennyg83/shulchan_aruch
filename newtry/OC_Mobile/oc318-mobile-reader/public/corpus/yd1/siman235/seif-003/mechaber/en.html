If he forbade her not to adorn herself, or not to eat one of the kinds—even if it is bad and she never tasted it—and attached it to marital relations, having said, "Konam, your marital relations are forbidden to me if you adorn yourself or eat such-and-such a kind;" or if he said, "Konam, the benefit of your marital relations is forbidden to me if you go to your father's house" more than once a month if he lives with her in the city, or more than twice a year if he lives in another city—he upholds it for seven days and then divorces her and gives the ketubah. And if she vowed, having said, "Konam is such-and-such a kind upon me," or "Konam that I not adorn myself with such-and-such adornment," or she said, "Your marital relations shall be forbidden to me if I eat such-and-such a kind or adorn myself with such-and-such adornment," or "if I go to my father's house," and he heard and did not annul for her—he divorces her immediately and gives the ketubah. And according to the Rambam, the vow is hers and he upholds it for her: if he wishes to remain and she does not eat of that kind, she sits; and if he says, "I do not want a woman who vows," he divorces her and gives the ketubah. The same law applies if she vowed not to go to her father's house, having said, "Konam, your marital relations are forbidden to me if I go to my father's house," and the husband heard and did not annul. And similarly if she said, "Konam, your marital relations are forbidden to me if I go to a house of mourning or to a house of feasting," and he heard and did not annul for her. The same law applies if she said, "Konam, your marital relations are forbidden to me if I do any work," and he heard and did not annul for her. The same law applies if she said, "Konam, your marital relations are forbidden to me if I borrow anything from a neighbor or if I lend a utensil," and he heard and did not annul.
