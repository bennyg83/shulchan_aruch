And he made it dependent upon marital relations. For otherwise he cannot impose the prohibition upon her.
Or for more than one festival. This refers to a woman who is eager to visit her father. But regarding a woman who is not eager to go to her father’s home, if he prohibited her for three festivals he must divorce her, while for two festivals he must remain married to her. This is stated by the Tur. See also the Bedek HaBayis.
And if she made the vow, etc. Where she prohibited fruit and adornments to herself and did not make the vow dependent upon marital relations, it must be that he explicitly prohibited them for as long as she remains married to him. Therefore, specifically in this case, he must divorce her immediately, because after he divorces her she will again be permitted fruit and adornments. The reason he must divorce her immediately is not that his failure to annul shows that he hates her, since that reason applies only when she made her vow dependent upon marital relations. Rather, the reason is that she cannot remain even one day without fruit and adornments, and by failing to annul her vow he placed a finger between her teeth. The law therefore requires him to divorce her immediately and pay her kesubah. But where she prohibited fruit and adornments to herself and made the prohibition dependent upon marital relations, even if she did not specify that the vow applies for as long as she remains married to him, he must nevertheless annul it immediately, because otherwise his failure indicates that he hates her. This is from the Perishah; see there, where he discusses the matter at length. These laws are explained in Even HaEzer, siman 74, for that is their proper place.
If I go to my father’s house. This is in accordance with the period explained there: one month when her father is in the same city, and one festival when he is in another city.
Your marital relations are forbidden to me if I go to a house of mourning, etc. This is from the words of the Tur. It implies that where she did not make the vow dependent upon marital relations, he need not divorce her and pay her kesubah, because he has no power to annul it, since refraining from going to a house of mourning or a house of feasting is not a vow of personal affliction. The Bach writes likewise. This requires study, however, because above in siman 234, seif 62, the Mechaber rules that it is personal affliction. We must say that here the language of the Tur was cited incidentally. The same explanation is required in Even HaEzer, siman 74, seif 5.
