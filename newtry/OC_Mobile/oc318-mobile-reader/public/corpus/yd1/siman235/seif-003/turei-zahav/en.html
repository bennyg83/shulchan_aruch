More than a month. If he specified explicitly one month, there is no obligation to divorce at all, like one who forbade seven in relations, seif 1; but here he forbade explicitly more than a month, and this is impossible for her; and since he tied it in relations, its law is like one who forbade relations alone, as above. And this matter is clear: also in a general vow not to go to her father's house, and he tied it in relations — its law is thus, since all general is like specified forever — the essence of the vow is prohibition of relations forbidden upon him, since he tied it in an impossible matter. And Tur wrote explicitly nearby: konam your relations upon me if you go to a house of mourning — divorce after seven; and in any matter he forbids and it is impossible to forbid without tying prohibition of relations, it is like one who forbade relations alone. And if he tied the vow of going to her father in prohibition of enjoyment from him, it is as if he vowed enjoyment alone, as its law is explained in seif 2 — this is the essence of the vow. And thereby resolved: not like what is said in seif 2 in the name of Tosafos, forbidding enjoyment from him and tying in relations — they give thirty-seven days; since different there, he can make a vow of enjoyment until thirty days without tying, since possible through a provider; therefore the relations vow does not begin until the time possible through provider, then she enjoys from him and is forbidden in relations seven days. There also no distinction between saying generally not to enjoy and tying, or specifying more than thirty and tying — in both they give thirty-seven days. This seems clear after examination. And Maharach wrote on this law: konam your relations upon me if you go to your father's house more than a month — nevertheless, certainly where he only forbade one month, he waits one month, and afterward forbidden seven days from relations, and divorces and gives ketubah, as Tosafos wrote, s.v. Hachi Nami — end quote; these are the Tosafos I mentioned in seif 2, and an error crept into his pen here. For if he forbade one month, certainly a complete vow is upheld, and she will not go one month to her father, nor be forbidden in relations ever, nor divorce at all; and Tosafos' words are not relevant — only there a general vow not to enjoy, or specified more than thirty, and the law in thirty-seven for the reason I wrote. And in Perishah also explained as I divided, except he wrote therefore the vow is effective in not enjoying even without relations, since in his power to forbid his on every person even though enslaved to her, nevertheless the vow takes effect — called; and against the Gemara entirely. And it is a wonder, for behold a pleasant reason is here, since thirty days are permitted through a provider, and it is obvious the vow is effective on thirty days; only afterward not effective except due to tying in relations, and therefore he waits seven days afterward.
And if she vowed, etc. The reason in this in the Gemara (71a): where the husband vowed on her, she reasons boiling anger at him, and now he calms — therefore give him time, perhaps he finds an opening for his vow. But she vowed and he was silent — she reasons: from his silence and not annulling, he hates her, and she cannot dwell with him at all, even until forbidden. And this reason applies to all that is written here, whether he tied in prohibition of relations [whether she did not tie in relations] — found forbidden that matter forever, even after she divorces — what benefit in divorce? For the second husband cannot annul for her what she already vowed. One can say: nevertheless she can say, "I do not want a man who upholds my vows" — and so Ran explicitly here. And even though Tosafos, chapter HaMedir, wrote here that she did not tie in relations her vow not to taste one of the fruits — it must be while under him; if she vowed forever, what benefit in leaving, etc. — this is before the conclusion resolved from "hates me" that I copied nearby. But after the conclusion one can say so even without tying in relations. Perishah extended here to say this law without tying also speaks when she said while under him specifically; and wrote further this reason "hates me" in the Gemara only applies to her vow of relations, and proof from Tosafos not mentioning this reason hates me — all this is not settled for me; there is no proof from Tosafos, since Tosafos spoke before the conclusion, as I wrote, and the words are plain.

If I go to my father's house. Since she said generally, it means forever, and is like specified forever; and if he did so, they would give seven days as above. But since she vowed and he was silent, she reasons hates me, as above — therefore divorce immediately. And it is simple: if she vowed explicitly not to go one month, the vow is upheld, permitted in relations, and she will not go, as above; and no need to write it. And Tur wrote: and truly regarding her who vowed one month, he upholds two or general — divorce immediately, etc. And Maharach wrote difficulty: what is different from vowed not to taste one species and tied in relations — we do not say when he did not annul he waits; and here vowed not to go we say he waits? And he answered: there a general vow forever is implied, and the same for general not to go; but here she forbade explicitly one month, etc. And accordingly: regarding she said "enjoyment of your relations upon me if I eat fruits of so-and-so" or "if I adorn" — if he upheld, if she specified forbidding enjoyment of his relations one Shabbat, he does not divorce immediately, even if he upheld; rather he waits seven days, then divorces. And so Hagahot Ashri, chapter HaMedir — end quote; his honor rests in his place, and there is error in his words. Rather, certainly here if she forbade one month explicitly, there is no relevance of a get, since it is possible to uphold the vow and she is not forbidden, as written; only Tur when she forbade more than a month or general — then divorce immediately. And we do not find "she vowed and he was silent" any manner that he waits some measure then divorces, except if possible to uphold and she is not forbidden; and if impossible to uphold, divorce immediately and give ketubah. And so there in Hagahot Ashri — end quote: if she specified relations of the bed one Shabbat, he does not divorce even if he upheld. But from one species in fruits she cannot bear, even if she specified less than one Shabbat and he upheld — divorce. Behold, regarding relations one Shabbat he does not divorce at all; and so the intention of Tur and Shulchan Aruch in what they wrote "one month he upholds" — he does not divorce her at all; why divorce, since it is possible to uphold without any prohibition on him or on her from what is impossible — not as Maharach thought, that this "he upholds" means and afterward divorce.
