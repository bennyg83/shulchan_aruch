And he made it dependent upon relations. Without such a condition he has no power to prohibit her in this matter, and he should seemingly divorce immediately, because a woman cannot remain prohibited in this manner, just as she cannot remain without adornment even for one hour, as Tosafos writes at the beginning of the chapter “One who imposes a vow.” Since he made it dependent upon relations, however, its law is like a vow concerning relations alone, where she can remain seven days without relations, as stated in seif 1.
For more than one month. If he explicitly mentioned one month, there is no obligation to divorce at all, just as one who explicitly vows from relations for seven days need not divorce. Here he explicitly vowed for more than a month, which she cannot endure. Since he made it dependent upon relations, the principal prohibition becomes relations, as above. The same law clearly applies if he said without qualification that she may not go to her father’s house and made it dependent upon relations, for an unspecified vow is like one expressly made forever. Since the condition is something she cannot keep, the true effect is a prohibition of relations. The Tur writes explicitly nearby: If he says, “Your relations are a konam if you go to a mourner’s house,” he must divorce after seven days. Whenever he cannot impose the underlying prohibition without making it dependent upon relations, the law is like a vow from relations alone. Likewise, if he made going to her father’s house dependent upon a prohibition of benefit from him, it is as though he vowed only against her benefit, whose law is in seif 2, because that is the essential vow. This resolves why it differs from what Tosafos wrote in seif 2 regarding one who forbids her benefit and makes it dependent upon relations, where he receives thirty-seven days. There he can impose the prohibition of benefit for thirty days without making it dependent upon relations, because a third party can support her. The prohibition of relations therefore begins only after the period during which third-party support is possible. She then benefits from him and becomes prohibited in relations for seven days. There too, it makes no difference whether he said without qualification that she may not benefit and made it dependent upon relations, or explicitly said for more than thirty days and made it dependent upon relations. In either case he receives thirty-seven days. This is clear after careful study. My revered father-in-law wrote regarding, “Your relations are a konam if you go to your father’s house for more than a month,” that if he prohibited only one month, he should wait one month and then seven days of relations before divorcing, based upon Tosafos. This is an error. A vow for one month is fully valid and fulfilled. She simply refrains from going to her father’s house for that month, relations never become prohibited, and he never divorces. Tosafos concerns an unspecified vow from benefit or one exceeding thirty days, and it allows thirty-seven days for the reason explained. The Perishah also distinguishes as I did, but says the vow against benefit takes effect without relations because one may forbid his property even to one to whom he is obligated. That contradicts the Gemara. The sound explanation is that the first thirty days are permitted through a third party. The vow therefore certainly takes effect for those thirty days, and afterward it can continue only because of its dependence upon relations.
And if she vowed, etc. The Gemara on 71a explains the distinction. When the husband imposed the vow, she thinks he became angry with her but will calm down. Therefore, he is given time to find an opening. When she vowed and he remained silent, she thinks that his silence and failure to annul show that he hates her. She cannot live with him at all, even until the prohibition begins. This reasoning applies throughout this seif, whether or not she made the vow dependent upon relations. One may ask what she gains from divorce if the prohibited item remains forbidden forever and a second husband cannot annul an earlier vow. She may nevertheless say, “I do not want a husband who confirms my vows.” The Ran states this explicitly. Tosafos in Kesubos 71a says regarding a woman who vowed not to taste one fruit without making it dependent upon relations that the case must mean only while she remains married, because if forever, divorce would not help. That statement preceded the Gemara’s final answer that she thinks he hates her. After the conclusion, the same reasoning applies even without a dependence upon relations. The Perishah wrote at length that without a relation condition the vow must be limited to her marriage and that the reasoning of hatred applies only to vows involving relations. This is not satisfactory, for Tosafos spoke before the final answer. The law is as its simple meaning indicates.
And he did not annul it for her, he must divorce immediately, etc. The reason is that he placed the finger between her teeth to cause the bite. He is therefore responsible, and no time is granted, not even seven days, because she thinks he hates her. The Rambam does not follow the Tanna who says that he placed the finger between her teeth, but the Tanna who says that she did so, and therefore imposes no immediate liability upon him.
If I go to my father’s house. Since she spoke without qualification, it means forever and is equivalent to saying so explicitly. If he had made such a vow, he would receive seven days as above. Since she vowed and he remained silent, she thinks he hates her, so he must divorce immediately. It is obvious that if she explicitly vowed not to go for one month, the vow stands, she remains permitted in relations, and she simply does not go during that month. The Tur did not need to state this, though it actually writes that if she vowed for one month he keeps her, while for two months or without qualification he divorces immediately. My revered father-in-law asked why this differs from a woman who vowed not to taste a fruit and made the vow dependent upon relations, where failure to annul requires immediate divorce, whereas here regarding going to her father’s house the Tur says he waits. He answered that there the vow was unspecified, meaning forever, and the same would apply here if unspecified; here she expressly vowed for one month. According to this, if she said, “The benefit of your relations is forbidden to me if I eat a certain fruit or adorn myself,” but expressly limited the relation prohibition to one week, he would not divorce immediately even if he confirmed it, but would wait seven days. Hagahos Ashri says similarly. With all due respect, this is an error. If she expressly vows for one month, divorce is irrelevant, because she can fulfill the vow and never become prohibited. The Tur’s case concerns more than one month or no limit, and then he divorces immediately. We never find a case where she vowed and he remained silent in which he first waits and then divorces. If the vow can be fulfilled, they remain married and she does not become prohibited. If it cannot be fulfilled, he immediately divorces and pays the kesubah. Hagahos Ashri itself says: If she specified one week regarding relations, he need not divorce even if he confirmed it. Regarding a single fruit, however, she cannot endure even less than one week, and if he confirmed it, he must divorce. Thus, “he shall maintain her” means that he never divorces, not that he later divorces. Why should he divorce if the matter can be fulfilled without any prohibition upon either spouse?
And the husband heard and did not annul. Since she made it dependent upon relations, he could have annulled. If she did not make it dependent upon relations, he could not annul. It is obvious that everything written in this seif regarding her vow and his failure to annul, requiring divorce, follows the opinion that disagrees with the Rambam cited here by the Shulchan Aruch.
If I go to a mourner’s house. The husband can annul this vow even if she did not make it dependent upon relations, as stated in siman 234, seif 62. The law mentions dependence upon relations to teach that even then he must divorce immediately. He does not receive seven days as in a vow involving relations, because she vowed and he confirmed it, and she therefore thinks he hates her. If he himself vowed that she not go to a mourner’s or wedding feast and made it dependent upon relations, he divorces after seven days, as the Tur writes. The reason is that he locks the door before her. If she later needs others for such an occasion, no woman will come to her.
Any work. Idleness leads to mental instability. Therefore, even a minimal amount of work suffices to prevent it.
If I borrow from my neighbors. This vow is harmful to her, and he did not annul it. Even though she made it dependent upon relations, he must therefore divorce immediately. Likewise, if he imposed such a vow and made it dependent upon relations, he waits seven days and then divorces.
