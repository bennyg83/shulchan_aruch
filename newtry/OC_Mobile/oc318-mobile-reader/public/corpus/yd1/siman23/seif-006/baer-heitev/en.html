And Shach wrote: it implies if he knows clearly that it was not perforated, such as where he grasped the trachea alone in his hand — valid even without examination; and it is not comparable to seif 1, that if the slaughterer says "It is clear to me I only cut the skin" we do not rely on him: there he did not intend from the outset, and it is a matter not dependent on a person; not so here, if he intended to grasp the trachea alone — valid even without examination.
Of the trachea. And Shach wrote that one should permit even for us where he slaughtered the esophagus and left it and slaughtered the windpipe and afterward slaughtered and finished the esophagus, if he did not pause at all before he began to slaughter the windpipe, since he is occupied in shechitah — valid; and so in Sefer Ohel Mo'ed in the name of Razah: an animal that has a pit in its esophagus — he slaughtered and struck the pit, raises the knife and slaughters immediately before pausing above or below the pit; and so Peri Chadash; and see Shach. And in Zivchei Riv he challenged this not according to law — end of his words.
As such. And Shach wrote: one should not challenge — for we rule in siman 57 regarding treifah that is unknown, and some validate that treifah, that nevertheless it is permitted to sell to a gentile because of safek safeka; and here too, even though we rule to declare treifah, nevertheless since it is only concern that perhaps he will sell it — safek safeka: perhaps if we were expert in examination we would see it was not perforated; and if you say it was perforated — perhaps the gentile will not sell to an Israelite. Maharai answered: a doubt in a dispute is not like a doubt in the act itself; and it requires study in many places in the Gemara on this. And Shach answered: in any case there is no difficulty: since the doubt is because of our lack of knowledge — we are not expert in examination — it is not called a doubt at all, as the poskim wrote in several places. And he wrote in Hagahot: and that which it says "kill it," etc. — I say: rather before death sell it to a gentile, and afterward the gentile kills it before us; for it is forbidden to cause treifot in something permitted by Torah to eat. And Peri Chadash in kaf alef disagrees with him.
Esophagus. And Shach wrote in the name of Mordechai: it is forbidden to leave it in his house lest mishap come; but if he need not leave it, only one day until it becomes pregnant and gives birth — a treifah does not give birth — it is permitted, as below siman 57, seif 18, and siman 86, seif 9.
Esophagus. And Shach wrote in the name of Darkei Moshe: even though we know that through plucking feathers the esophagus is not perforated, nevertheless we decree lest the skin tear from the neck; according to this there is no distinction between plucking at the time of shechitah or before — always, if blood came out from them, declare treifah because of the decree; and it is great stringency in my eyes. Also Maharshal validated this and wrote it is stringency without reason; therefore it appears as Bach wrote, that in great loss one should be lenient. And Taz wrote the reason for this stringency: lest one come to permit also a bar goose whose blood spurts, as in siman 33; therefore one forbids only specifically when blood came out, for since blood came out we are concerned perhaps a very fine hole was perforated and touched the esophagus, and it is not visible to us, or the blood blocked it; for otherwise from where did this blood come, since in the skin there is no blood (and I omitted the sugya in Yoma, daf 48, that there is blood in the skin — responsum Shai, question 28). Therefore one may not be lenient in a cut, even part of the skin, when blood is seen; and in plucking feathers, when blood is seen one should be stringent specifically if a little blood came out — end. But if only redness is seen, not blood — do not be stringent at all. And he wrote he proposed this before Maharam Yafeh and he agreed with his words.
The cut. And Taz wrote: it appears simple — if on the fowl's skin there is dry scratch on its outer side, and he slaughtered elsewhere and saw on the inner side that there is no defect on the skin — do not be stringent according to all views. And if the upper membrane on the simanim peeled even somewhat — treifah; and examination does not help, since we are not expert in examining the esophagus. However, in mere swelling around the simanim, when there is no other defect — we are not concerned for perforation of the esophagus; rather we attribute it to illness — see Kenesset HaGedolah.
