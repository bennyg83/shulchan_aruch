[And he does not know, etc. It implies: if he knows clearly that the esophagus was not perforated — his shechitah is valid even without examination, such as where he grasped the trachea alone in his hand, as Beit Yosef wrote in this siman and in seif 21, and Beit Yosef there; and see there and siman 34 on what is written in seif 1, that even if the slaughterer says "It is clear to me that I only cut the skin" — we do not rely on him: there, since he did not intend from the outset for this, it is a matter that does not depend on a person and was not in his mind — similar to above siman 18, seif 14, that where he knew there is masuchseches he is believed to say "It is clear to me I only did holacha"; and when he did not know before shechitah he is not believed, because a matter that does not depend on a person was not in his mind — see there.]
[In another place, etc. The reason: because there is concern — perhaps since he already began to slaughter in that place it will be easier to strike the esophagus, and he will come to say "now it was slaughtered" and "before I did not touch it"; and perhaps before he touched it, and he validates the prohibition improperly. And it deals with one who slaughtered the trachea alone; or perhaps before he did not touch it, and now since he is likely to strike it he will strike it — it turns out he invalidates improperly. Good advice the Mechaber teaches — end of Beit Yosef and Perishah.]
[Even if he did not pause, etc. And Rokeach, siman 381, wrote: he slaughtered the esophagus and left it and slaughtered the windpipe and afterward slaughtered and finished the esophagus — since he is occupied in shechitah, it is valid — end of his words. And in my humble opinion, the same applies for us where he did not pause at all before he began to slaughter the windpipe; and so in Shechitot of R' Tzvi; and Zivchei Riv challenged him not according to law.]
[And it is forbidden to sell it to a gentile, etc. One should not challenge: for Beit Yosef wrote in seif 27 of treifot that are unknown, and some validate that treifah — even though we rule to forbid it, nevertheless it is permitted to sell it to a gentile because of safek safeka; and here too, even though we rule to declare treifah, nevertheless since it is only a concern that perhaps he will sell it — this is safek safeka: perhaps the esophagus was not perforated; and if we were expert in examination we would see it was not perforated; and if you say it was perforated — perhaps the gentile will not sell it to a Jew. These are two "perhaps"s here. They decreed on a Torah doubt, or even on something whose prohibition is only rabbinic from the root, such as bread of an Israelite that a gentile baked (below siman 112, seif 11) — the decree not to sell lest he sell it to an Israelite; but regarding something in which there is a dispute of amoraim or rabbis, we always say regarding selling to a gentile that it is rabbinic — follow the lenient side; for a doubt in a dispute is not like a doubt in the act itself — and it is simple. And it requires study in many places in the Gemara on this matter — end of Maharai in Terumat HaDeshen, seif 178. And in my humble opinion, in any case there is no difficulty: for here, since the doubt is because of our lack of knowledge — we are not expert in examination — it is not called a doubt at all, as the poskim wrote in several places; and as written in siman 53, seif kaf 14, and siman 98, seif kaf 9; and so Maharai himself in Hagahot Sha'arei Dura, siman 87, regarding a broken bone — see there; and even safek safeka does not help in such a case, as written in siman 55, seif kaf 4.]
[Rather kill it and afterward sell it, etc. And Mordechai wrote in the name of Rif: it is forbidden to leave it in his house, lest a mishap come — therefore; and Darkei Moshe brings it. But if he need not leave it, only one day until it becomes pregnant and gives birth — a treifah does not give birth — it is permitted, as below siman 57, seif 18, and siman 86, seif 9.]
[If he plucked the feathers, etc. In Darkei Moshe he wrote in the name of Maharam of Padua the reason in this: for even though we know that through plucking feathers the esophagus is not perforated, nevertheless we decree lest the skin tear from the neck; and he wrote: according to this there is no distinction between plucking feathers at the time of shechitah or before — always, if blood came out from them, one should declare it treifah because of the decree; and it is great stringency in my eyes — end of his words. Also Maharshal, chapter HaShochet, siman 14, validated this and wrote it is stringency without reason; and so Chayei Adam in the name of Rashba that Beit Yosef brought, and latter authorities, that there is no concern here for perforation of the esophagus; and therefore it appears as Bach wrote, that in great loss and the like one should be lenient.]
[But if no blood came out, etc. Behold: if blood came out, even though he did not cut all the skin; or he cut all the skin even though no blood came out — treifah. And that which it says at the beginning "or he cut the skin and blood came out" — this means part of the skin; and so Darkei Moshe wrote explicitly. But Maharshal there validated both where he cut part of the skin and blood came out, and where he cut it all and no blood came out; and he only declares treifah where he cut it all and blood came out. And in what he permits where he cut it all and no blood came out — I already wrote in seif kaf 4 that we do not rule thus. But in what he validates where he cut part of it even if blood came out — it appears so at first glance: for what concern is there, since he did not cut all the skin? And so the Taz brought two views on this; therefore it appears one should be lenient in great loss and the like; but in any case one should not be lenient, lest it be only plucking of a feather that they practice to declare treifah when blood comes out; and so Bach declares treifah when part of the skin was cut and blood came out. However, all this is when the slaughterer did not say "It is clear to me I did not touch the esophagus"; but when he says "It is clear to me," even if he cut all the skin it is valid when no blood came out, as implied by the poskim and the Mechaber in seif 1; and as written in seif kaf 4; and where he intended from the outset not to touch the esophagus, such as where he grasped the trachea alone in his hand — even if he cut all the skin and blood also came out, it is valid; and as written in seif kaf 15 — examine this.]
[He slaughtered fowl, etc. It implies specifically in fowl, whose fitness is in one siman — this remedy applies to it; but not in an animal; and so is simple among the poskim.]
