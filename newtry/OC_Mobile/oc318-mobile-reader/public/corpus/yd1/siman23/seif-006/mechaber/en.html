He slaughtered fowl and paused in it and does not know whether the esophagus was perforated — he returns and slaughters the trachea alone in another place and leaves it until it dies, and inverts the esophagus and checks it from inside; if no drop of blood is found in it, it is known that it was not perforated and it is kosher. {Rama: And the custom is to declare treifah everything, even if he only paused in the first minority of the trachea (Mahari Weil and Mordechai). And it is forbidden to sell it to a gentile as such; rather kill it and afterward sell it to a gentile (Hagahot Semak and Mordechai), because we are not expert in examining the esophagus and we are concerned for perforation of the esophagus (Agur). And for this reason, if one plucked the feathers from the fowl and blood came out, or cut the skin in an animal and blood came out from it — one should declare treifah, for we are concerned for perforation of the esophagus (Mahari Weil and Maharam of Padua, siman 63, and so in latter shechitah books). But if no blood came out and he did not cut all the skin — one may declare it kosher by slaughtering above or below and checking opposite the cut (Beit Yosef, not like Mahari Weil, shoresh 34). And therefore one should be careful not to pluck the feathers if he can slaughter without this (Or Zarua).}
