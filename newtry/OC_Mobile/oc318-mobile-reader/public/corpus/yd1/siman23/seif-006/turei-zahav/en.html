And he slaughters the trachea alone in another place. That he needs another place — Beit Yosef explained: perhaps since he already began to slaughter there it will be easier to strike the esophagus, and he will come to say "now it was slaughtered" and "before I did not touch it"; and perhaps before he touched it, and he validates the prohibition improperly. Nekudat HaKesef: and he was not precise — for certainly any drop of blood found he will not attribute to leniency and forbid from doubt; for we rule: any drop of blood found inside is forbidden everywhere it is; if so, there is no stumbling block here. And it appears to me the reason: when he wishes to grasp the trachea alone he must grasp it forcefully; and if he does so in the place he already cut, there is concern that thereby it will detach and the cut widen until it is invalid — therefore he grasps it in a place that is whole. And further it appears to me: if he slaughters in the first place, there is concern he will ruin it for himself — perhaps he already slaughtered the majority in the trachea, and then there is no invalidation in what perforated the esophagus; and it is impossible to see well at the time of shechitah whether the majority was slaughtered, since it is soiled with blood — therefore he slaughters in another place, and afterward he can see in the first place that if the majority was slaughtered, perforation of the esophagus will not harm him.
And we are concerned for perforation of the esophagus. This is a thing impossible to say — that through plucking a feather the esophagus would be perforated. And in the version of the Hagahah that Maharam of Padua brought, siman 83, the reason is written: one who sees would think to permit also a bar goose that blood spurts, as stated in siman 33. And it is possible that Rama too here, who wrote the reason because of perforation of the esophagus, wrote regarding the second division "he cut the skin," etc.; but in the first division the reason is because of a decree, as I explained; and since the main reason is because of a decree, it is difficult to add a decree that was not mentioned in the Talmud; nevertheless, since it is practiced stringently, it is practiced — only one should not be stringent except if a few drops of blood came out in a matter that resembles spurting blood; but if they did not come out, only redness is seen — one should not be stringent at all. And I proposed this matter before Maharam Yafeh, z"l, and he agreed with my words. I again saw in Maharshal, who wrote in the name of Chayei Adam in the name of Rashba in plucking a feather that Beit Yosef brought, that there is no concern for perforation of the esophagus; and he wrote on this: and not only that — it appears in my eyes that even with a knife that we are stringent and concerned for perforation of the esophagus, whether in fowl or in animal, it appears specifically where all the skin was cut; but if the skin of the animal was cut only in part of its thickness and not entirely — there is no concern; and even if blood came out there is no room for concern — end of his words. And this is unlike Rama's words here, who wrote "but if no blood came out and he did not cut all the skin" — implying if blood came out, even though he did not cut all the skin, it is forbidden. But it appears the main point is like Rama's words: since blood came out we are concerned perhaps a very fine hole was perforated and touched the esophagus, and it is not visible to us, or the blood blocked it; for otherwise from where did this blood come, since in the skin there is no blood? Therefore one may not be lenient in a cut, even part of the skin, when blood is seen; and in plucking feathers, when blood is seen one should be stringent specifically if a little blood came out, as we mentioned above.
And he did not cut all the skin. This refers to animal specifically; but in fowl it is not relevant to say so; and so explicitly in responsum of Mahari Weil there. But nevertheless it appears simple: if on the fowl's skin there is some dry scratch on its outer side, and he slaughtered elsewhere and saw on the inner side that there is no defect on the skin — one should not be stringent in this according to all views.
