Gomi. Ba'er Heitev, seif kaf heh [he wrote in the name of Shach: and I did not know why Bach was lenient, etc.; and see responsum Mishkenot Yaakov, siman 12, who reconciles Bach's words — see there]. And that which he wrote in the name of Kenesset HaGedolah regarding esophagus with bran — see responsum Batei Kehunah, siman 10, asked similarly on a chicken after shechitah: pit in esophagus; he elaborated: specifically when a soft thing like gomi is found forbid; but if a hard thing like thorn, wheat, barley, or pit lying in esophagus was cut — kosher; this is Bach's reason permitting grass; and even Shach who disagreed — only regarding grass, for though not soft like the siman, nevertheless unlike the siman in hardness and firmness after stretching; but in other hard things that are like stretched and stand until they lean the siman to stretching — even Shach admits not to forbid; nevertheless he fears to rely practically after this distinction was not mentioned in any latter authority; but in safek whether it was cut — plainly certainly permit and rely on Kenesset HaGedolah, who permits even gomi in such case — see there, where he elaborated greatly. And that which he wrote in the name of Or Zarua regarding cutting his finger — see responsum Avnei Nezer, part 2, siman 42.
