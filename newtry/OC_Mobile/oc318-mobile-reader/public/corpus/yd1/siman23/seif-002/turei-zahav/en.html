In order to lift a large animal and lay it down. This is Rambam's view; and in Tur it is written so too in the name of Rif and Ra'osh. The author of HaDrishah already wondered — a settled wonder — for in Ashiri it is explained that it is not so; rather we estimate lifting and laying down: small for small and large for large; only for shechitah we estimate even small according to large. And he answered with difficulty: Tur means we estimate in slaughtering large and its lifting and laying down of itself; and the wording does not imply this, for "its lifting" refers to what he mentioned first. And in my humble opinion there is a textual error here, and it should read: "rather, that he wrote: even though we estimate the time of shechitah of an animal and its lifting," etc.; and our Rabbi's intent: Ra'osh agreed with Rif in this, that for shechitah we estimate even from large to fowl — not as he mentioned before; rather for lifting and laying down he disagrees; and therefore he said "rather he wrote: even though we estimate the time of shechitah of an animal and laying down," each according to its law; nevertheless this is specifically for animal, but for fowl there is no measure of laying down at all.

Nevertheless this includes only if he raised the knife even somewhat, since he removed it from the neck — one does not distinguish whether he paused a lot or a little; but if he did not raise at all, only the knife was pushed from place to place — it is valid even for us, as we find regarding one who slaughters in two or three places in siman 21; and so I found in Maharshal; and he wrote: nevertheless it appears specifically where he ceased from slaughtering or raised the knife; but if his hand was pushed downward there is no shehiyah on him and it is valid.
