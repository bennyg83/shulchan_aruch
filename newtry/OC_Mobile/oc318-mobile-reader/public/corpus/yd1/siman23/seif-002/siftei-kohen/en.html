[And slaughter until the majority, etc. It appears to me that for this reason the Beit Yosef added the word "until," because Rambam wrote (chapter 3 of Hilchot Shechitah, law 4): if he paused the time to lift it, lay it down, and the time to slaughter a minority of simanim — not the time to complete shechitah — this is doubt of neveilah; and Beit Yosef explained, and so it appears, that this means he paused the time of shechitah by a hair's breadth more than he would have paused the measure of shechitah; and therefore, since the matter is estimated by judgment, it is possible he will think he only paused the time to slaughter a minority of simanim, and perhaps he paused the time to slaughter the majority of simanim — end of his words; therefore the Beit Yosef emended the word "until," meaning he paused the time of shechitah until he reached the majority of simanim by a hair's breadth — the majority (and after it the Taz continues). And even though in such a case it is doubt of neveilah, the Beit Yosef was not precise about this, and so in several places. However, from Tosafot there (s.v. im yesh bah), and Hagahot Ashiri in the name of R' Yechiel and Mordechai and Agudah there, it appears clearly that we say "the piece becomes neveilah" in such a case; and so too is the view of R' Yerucham there; and it appears from these poskim that even in salting we say "the piece becomes neveilah" — see there: the Mechaber and Beit Yosef and other poskim were not precise about this.]
[Until the blood comes out, etc. Thus wrote Beit Yosef; and Mahari Weil, shoresh 34, wrote that even though it was cut, he does not forbid until blood comes out, and he was precise about this from that which it says "until the blood comes out"; and it does not appear to me that even if blood did not come out we are concerned perhaps he touched the esophagus; and that which it says "until the blood comes out" — he mentioned it as the ordinary way of speaking; however, where the slaughterer says "It is clear to me that I only cut the skin" — we do not rely on him, namely when blood came out, as the wording implies "we do not rely on him since blood came out" — end of his words. But Maharshal, chapter HaShochet, siman 14, wrote: and even though Beit Yosef questioned Mahari Weil, nevertheless I say he wrote well, etc.; and the Rav's view in Darkei Moshe and in Hagahot at the end of the siman is like Beit Yosef, as will be explained there; and so the latter authorities in their shechitah books.]
[And even if he said "It is clear to me," etc. See seif kaf 15.]
[And afterward invert the esophagus and check, etc. And for us, who are not expert in examination as below siman 33, seif 8 — treifah; and so too Beit Yosef at the end of the siman; and so in latter shechitah books; and Maharshal there wrote that in great loss or for the need of a mitzvah one should look to be lenient and rely on the latter great ones who practiced and ruled practical halakhah for permission, as is found in Hagahot in part of Semak and Mahari Weil copied it in responsum siman 34, etc. — see there.]
[And the settled custom, etc. It is simple that this refers after he relied on it, that even in great loss and the like the custom is to declare treifah; and so it appears in Darkei Moshe and other latter authorities; and so Bach wrote explicitly; and so is practiced — not like the Taz.]
[And if after shechitah gomi is found, etc. In Terumat HaDeshen he compared this law to seif 4, that when he paused on something that has no aspect of shechitah — it is shehiyah; and all the more so here. And afterward he wrote: and you may ask — who tells us that he paused when cutting the gomi? Perhaps he actually cut the esophagus and the gomi at once, equal in time, and if so the knife did not cease even a moment from the siman. It appears that he must say thus: for behold the siman is stretched at the time of shechitah, and the gomi is not stretched because it is not attached to the jaw and body; and it is known that any soft thing the knife's edge strikes — if it is stretched it is cut faster than if it were not stretched; and therefore, since the gomi too is soft and not stretched, the knife's edge paused longer to cut it than when cutting the siman; and it turns out he did not cut them exactly equal — end of his words; and Darkei Moshe and Maharshal, chapter HaShochet, siman 10, and Mahari Weil bring it. And I did not know why Bach was lenient to permit long thin grass lying along the esophagus without reason and proof; and also from Beit Yosef's words, who wrote "gomi or the like," it implies as I said; and so it appears in the Taz — see there.]
[And he raised his hand, etc. The same applies if he did not raise [his hand] and only paused — he mentioned it as the ordinary way of speaking, and it is simple.]
