Willingly. And Shach wrote: that which the Mechaber wrote "and he raised his hand" is not precise; rather the same applies if he did not raise and only paused — he mentioned it as the ordinary way of speaking.
Comes out. And Shach wrote: the same applies when no blood comes out — he mentioned it as the ordinary way of speaking; however, if the slaughterer says "It is clear to me that I only cut the skin" — we do not rely on him, namely where blood came out, as the wording implies "we do not rely on him since blood came out." And Maharshal ruled that it is forbidden only specifically when blood came out.
It. And Shach wrote in the name of Maharshal: for us, who are not expert, one should declare treifah unless in great loss.
Any amount. And Shach wrote: and even in great loss. And Taz wrote: nevertheless this includes only if he raises the knife even somewhat, since he removed it from the neck — one does not distinguish whether he paused a lot or a little; but if he did not raise at all, only the knife was pushed from place to place — it is valid even for us, as we find regarding one who slaughters in two or three places above in siman 21; and so Maharshal; and he wrote: what should we be concerned for? Even if the esophagus was perforated — it is one shechitah — end of his words.
Treifah. And Shach wrote: and I did not know why Bach was lenient to permit long thin grass lying along the esophagus without reason and proof. He wrote: an incident came to him where one slaughtered fowl and at the time of shechitah cut his finger and declared treifah because he panicked from the cut on his finger and raised the knife somewhat — and it is shehiyah; and several geonim agreed with him that it is treifah. And in responsum of Or Zarua, siman 86, he distinguishes: if he cut his finger not at the time of cutting the siman, such as after he passed the entire knife in holacha or hava'ah outside the neck, and then cut his finger, and afterward returned and did holacha or hava'ah with the knife and finished the shechitah — certainly it is shehiyah and invalid. But if he cut the simanim and his finger at once — there is no shehiyah here, and it is not comparable to gomi found within the simanim, for there the gomi is soft and through the knife's sharpness he pauses longer to cut it than when cutting the siman. And regarding kashering utensils or transferring the slaughterer — lean to leniency. And if the slaughterer is in doubt in what manner the cut on his finger was — be lenient — see there. And if he cut the finger of the one holding the simanim — it is kosher, for the slaughterer is not startled. See responsum Chinuch, Beit Yosef, question 24 — end. And I say: not only gomi, but even a hair found either in the esophagus or trachea is forbidden — see there. He wrote to Kenesset HaGedolah: if the esophagus is full of bran feed and our eyes see the knife full of bran and going down with spurting of blood — forbid by law — see there. If in doubt whether the gomi was cut with the shechitah or not — do not declare treifah, since it is mere stringency that they practice to forbid shehiyah in any amount; one may be stringent in certain cutting but not in doubt. And so I ruled practical halakhah several times — Kenesset HaGedolah, this siman.
