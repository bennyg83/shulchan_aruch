Even any amount. Ba'er Heitev, seif kaf heh. And see Sefer Beit Lechem Yehudah, who wrote: if he slaughtered and at the time of shechitah the knife head touched the wall or ground — latter shechitah books write this is complete shehiyah — see there; and Teshuvat Beit Shmuel testified this is slaughterers' practice. And see responsum Makom Shmuel, siman 29, who raised that one should not be stringent at all; and it is obvious where he does not know when the knife touched wall or ground, whether before finishing shechitah or after — certainly kosher; and even Beit Lechem Yehudah concedes — see there. However, in responsum Zichron Yosef, part 4, siman 1, he is stringent: if the knife head was pushed at shechitah and touched the special wooden slaughter chair called "shraagin" — shechitah invalid; even if he does not know when it touched, before or after finishing — the custom spread among slaughterers to declare treifah unless clarified clearly he did not slaughter after the push; and they do not distinguish strong push from light push and touch; and it should not be like permitted matters, etc. (however according to this, specifically if slaughterers' custom is fence and safeguard; but if they practice prohibition because they think the law is so — permitted matters do not apply, as above siman 214, seif 1 — see there; also difficult: for in an uncommon matter custom does not apply, as Shach wrote below siman 190, seif kaf gimmel). And he further wrote: if the knife head was not pushed, only the butcher passed the knife point in holacha or hava'ah on the animal's neck and on the shraagin wood at once — even though there is concern he paused on the wood more than on the siman — nevertheless validate, since there is no shehiyah in any amount, neither Torah nor rabbinic prohibition, only from custom; therefore we say safek for leniency (and one must distinguish this from gomi found, where there is no safek at all, as Rama wrote "certainly he had to," etc.). And all the more when there is safek when it touched, before finishing shechitah or after; and especially if the slaughterer says "It is clear to me I did not pause at all" — see there at length.
