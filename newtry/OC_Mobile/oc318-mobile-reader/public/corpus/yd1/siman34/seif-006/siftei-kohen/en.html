Some say that if punctured with a puncture open through, etc. From Rif and Rambam's words it appears we deal with a bird, as explained in Beit Yosef; nevertheless it appears the law is likewise in an animal according to them — that if punctured open through from both sides we estimate as the thickness of an issur; except they came to explain we should not say specifically in an animal, but in a bird its measure is by if it covers, which is smaller than two thicknesses of an issur, as Kesef Mishneh wrote in chapter 3 of Hilchot Shechitah: if it covers less than the measure of two thicknesses of an issur — nevertheless in a bird we do not say "if it covers" but only wide enough for the thickness of an issur to enter through the two punctures; and the same in an animal — if indented we estimate wide enough that an issur can enter in its thickness through the two open punctures; and so it is proven from Tur's words that for Rif and Rambam the law is likewise; and Kesef Mishneh challenged Tur and his intent: since Rif and Rambam wrote this law regarding a bird — their view is not in an animal; and so in Darkei Moshe; and what he wrote is clear.
