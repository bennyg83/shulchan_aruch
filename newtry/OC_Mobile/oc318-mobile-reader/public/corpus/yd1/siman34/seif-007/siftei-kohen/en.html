**And if something remained,** etc.Bach challenged the Mechaber that this law depends on variant texts, etc. — see there; and to my humble opinion — even according to the text "some say," etc. — it may be explained: one authority — R' Yochanan — did not say "anything above and below" but R' Yonatan said so; and they said thus before R' Yochanan in name of R' Yosi; and R' Yochanan said: they know, etc., and they praised R' Yonatan — see there; and nevertheless Rif, Rambam, Rashi, Rashba, Rosh, Rabbeinu Yerucham, and all hold like the Mechaber; and Maharshal in chapter 1 siman 10 upholds the Mechaber's method.
**Above and below in the place fit for slaughter,** etc.Beit Yosef wrote: specifically to exclude a place not fit for slaughter below; but a remainder to the side above — even in a place not fit for slaughter — helps, for in that place it is not disqualified except in a majority — end; and so Peri To'ar and Bach wrote: but Rambam in chapter 3 of Hilchot Shechitah wrote: if split along its length — even if nothing remained of the place fit for slaughter in it except something above and something below — kosher, etc. — until also in Shulchan Aruch he retracted and wrote like Rambam's language — end; and I do not know what retraction is here — for on the contrary he intended and was precise and wrote opposite Rambam's words: Rambam wrote "even if nothing remained of the place fit for slaughter except something above," etc. — implies explicitly he also refers to something above near the place fit for slaughter; and the Mechaber wrote the opposite — and it implies thus: if something remained above in whatever place it remained — for there his puncture is not in any amount; and something below in the place fit for slaughter — kosher; nevertheless regarding the law, Maharshal's view there is also that even above the large ring it does not help; and see siman 20 s.k. 1.
