A puncture that has deficiency — Shach, Taz, and Peri Chadash wondered: behold the majority of the circumference of the kaneh is only a slight thing that has length and no width of a mere line encircling it; but an issur has length and width; and if you cut it to straps the circumference from a small coin in great length results, as the diagram printed in Shach — go read in it; and if so, how is without deficiency more stringent than with deficiency when its measure is smaller? See Shach and Taz who divide regarding the position of punctures — if on the circumference or along the length: along the length it is only as split and kosher, unlike with deficiency — whether on circumference or along length, with an issur — treifah. And one must understand according to their explanation the gemara that challenges: on "if punctured knafah" — why do they not combine to an issur? And Tosafot on "it was omitted" explained according to one view we deal with punctures with deficiency like knafah; and nevertheless what is the difficulty — perhaps we deal standing on the circumference and not along the length — and then it is obvious they combine to a majority; and the language knafah implies circumference as Rashi wrote. Also we say in the gemara: "a strap was removed from it" — they combine to an issur — Tur explains along its length; and it is difficult — behold the mishnah teaches deficiency as an issur; therefore necessarily along its length — for in its width an issur is not needed according to their words; and Tur's language "on the circumference" is difficult to fit them, as Shach himself. And it appears simply: certainly what is kosher in slaughter disqualifies in severance or removal; and see above siman 21 — if slaughtered in the kaneh in one place and finished in a second place — if not on one side like this and it is a majority — kosher; and if so likewise if severed like this — if it is a majority — treifah; but if slaughtered on one side like this — slaughter does not help and we do not say if combined along the length it is a majority — for the majority of the kaneh remains; and what is it to us if in that place it was cut once or many times one after another? And if so likewise in severance in such a case they do not combine to a majority — for the majority of the kaneh was not severed; but this is only in severance without deficiency — treifah not from severance of the kaneh but from deficiency; therefore there is no difference in whatever manner — treifah. And if so — "if punctured knafah" teaches: punctures on the circumference of the kaneh one below another like this — certainly if you take the amount of punctures and cut to straps and combine — behold here is the majority of the circumference; but you cannot combine — for the puncture like this is full of cracks like this and only the upper line combines, for the other lines are one below another and do not render treifah, as above; and the upper line alone is not a majority; therefore the issur Shach drew on the issur page — you have only an upper line, for the other lines and stretched straps are one under another and do not combine to the circumference as stated; and therefore kosher until punctures stand like this — that if you take only the upper line alone, nevertheless the majority of the circumference results — treifah. Unlike if the punctures amount to an issur — we say all punctures combine, not the upper line alone — for we do not render treifah from severance but from deficiency; and behold before you what I wrote — if side by side or one below another — and to my humble opinion. All is correct and clear; length here is unnecessary — only set your eyes on what I wrote and matters will clarify.
And according to what will be explained shortly, etc. — the acharonim did not understand Rama's intent until the great rabbi son of Shach in Nekudat HaKesef explained Rama's words well — for his intent is on the Rosh who explains in the bird's question: "if punctured knafah" — if the intact portion between them combines, and also proportionate to an issur in an animal its measure is very small; and on this the gemara says "one folds it," etc. — hence this measure is greater than that measure of an issur; and therefore Rama challenged well that he wrote to be lenient — see there. And regarding Rashba who brought this law in chiddushim — no difficulty, for he refers to Rif's words who brought this question on the bird regarding the mishnah teaching deficiency of kaneh as an issur, and therefore asked on a bird; and he answers "one folds it," etc. — and certainly we estimate according to proportion as the Rosh wrote; and then the proportion of an issur in a bird is like an issur in an animal; and nevertheless it suffices by folding and placing on the opening of the kaneh — hence both are one measure; and all the more if we come to answer the Rosh's difficulty on Rif — why not ask on all measures regarding a bird? — for it is obvious we estimate according to the proportion of a large ox; if so, likewise for puncturing the kaneh — only say: in all types of treifot there is no essential difference between animal and bird except one is large and one small, and it is obvious we estimate according to the animal's own proportion — ox, kid, and tender lamb, large and small, all by proportion; but in the kaneh of an animal — if the kaneh alone was slaughtered — slaughter helps, for life does not depend on it alone; but in a bird — if the kaneh alone was slaughtered — slaughter helps, for life goes out; and hence in the kaneh alone life depends; how compare animal treifot regarding kaneh to bird treifot where life goes out with severance of the kaneh much more than in an animal? And the gemara challenged and answered well: in truth in a bird they gave the measure of "one folds it," etc.; and if so it is proven it is a small measure proportionate to an issur — for otherwise on the contrary, what reason to be stringent in a bird you would be lenient. And therefore Ba'al HaItur's words that Rashba brought in his name are correct. But Rama's difficulty on the Mechaber who cites in Shulchan Aruch like the Rosh that the bird's question refers to knafah puncture — if so he should not have concealed like Rashba; and it may be said the Mechaber certainly holds even the Rosh agrees both are one measure — for otherwise the Rosh should have challenged Rif briefly: how lenient in a bird to give a measure greater than the proportion of an issur? And if so it was tradition from the Sages that the measure of "if it covers," etc., was transmitted to a bird — it is obvious there is no place for the Rosh's wonder why he asked on a bird regarding puncturing the kaneh and not other treifot where he did not know there is reason to distinguish; for certainly from the outset we estimate everything by proportion as the Rosh wrote; but regarding knafah he knew there is tradition that a bird has another measure — therefore he asked what the measure is; and he answered "one folds it," etc.; and to my humble opinion — nevertheless one may learn from the Rosh's words who explains like Rashi that the question on a bird refers to knafah puncture — meaning he knew that in a bird we estimate by animal proportion a very small measure of deficiency; we are not stringent at such a small measure to combine the intact portion with the very small deficiency; and he answers: no — the Sages gave another measure, etc. And it is asked: if the law of the judgment is to estimate by proportion and the intact portion between them combines — destined to be deficient — what of it if very small or large? So is God's perfect Torah to render treifah as it is, small or large; and a small amount renders treifah; and more wondrous that he answers they gave another measure — do they have mercy in judgment because its measure is small and easily treifah? They gave another measure to be lenient against the law of judgment — we never heard such in treifot; and it is forced to say combining the intact portion is only a rabbinic stringency and "they said, they said" to be lenient — this is forced; and if so its stringency is its leniency; therefore it appears certainly if the measure came according to the law of deficiency — and a matter rendered treifah in utmost smallness and minority we do not heed — what of it if it is small? So the Torah-giver gave in His straight and righteous statutes — only to estimate in a bird by animal proportion in these measures of sela, issur, olive, and the like, to make an almost precise proportion; in almost all of these I found no one who knows these measures on earth — only since impossible to aim precisely, we are stringent to minimize the measure utterly until doubt leaves our heart that this is certainly the proportionate measure relative to an animal — for such are our obligations and such is good for us; and therefore he did not ask on any treifot of sela, olive, and the like; but regarding deficiency in gargeret — where anyway we are stringent to combine the intact portion between them, destined to rot and congeal — one need not combine two stringencies; and to congeal one need not combine two stringencies of the intact portion mentioned to be stringent in the proportionate measure from lack of knowledge — if so it would be very heavy until a small puncture renders treifah; therefore they answered: what can be done with combining the intact portion — only from proportionate deficiency, and do not combine to be more stringent; and here there is another measure — "one folds it," etc.; and with this no estimation and proportionate measure is needed until you must be stringent; and if there is stringency from the intact portion between them — we do not heed, since the law of judgment is so. And this is clear in the Rosh's words; and if so it is proven the measure of "one folds it" is one measure with an issur — for otherwise how would they transgress the law to give another measure to be lenient against Torah law? Or one measure; and the leniency is only in knafah puncture so as not to need to be stringent in the proportion of the issur, as stated — for in this "one folds it," etc., its measure is understood. And therefore Rashba's words are stated for all; but with all this my heart wavers — for we do not know how much an issur is; and we say simply it is small; and in Berakhot until one sees an issur opposite his heart — meaning a small coin placed on the ground — if it does not sink much the sight will not fall on it; nevertheless there is to reject — who knows, perhaps relative to another coin it is small, and nevertheless essentially it is large — only this is to be precise in the measure of sela, which is more than an issur as known, and the measure of sela is one-third tefach as above end of siman 21 for one view — and it is proportionate to a finger and a half; and the eye saw and testified in the animal in its kaneh — one can insert several thumb-widths in the width; therefore examine the law. And more it appears Rashba holds what we see in a bird — "one folds it," etc. — and the sense testifies it is larger than an issur, as stated — that both are in tradition from the Sages: the cavity, majority of width of the kaneh we require without doubt, for the essence of treifah depends on majority in severance of the gargeret — this on the circumference and this on the width; only sometimes in a small animal the kaneh is small and the measure of covering the majority of the width of the kaneh is small — they gave the measure of an issur: if there is not in it an issur, even though it covers the majority of the width — nevertheless kosher; and both are required; and therefore in an animal we estimate as an issur — even though sometimes it does not cover the majority of the kaneh; nevertheless they were stringent and gave the measure of an issur; but in a bird, difficult to estimate by proportion — the measure of covering the kaneh remains; and understand; and examine the law — for Rashba himself was in doubt and did not decide for practical halachah. And from the Rosh's words we learn: if in the punctures and intact portion there is not enough to cover half the majority of the width of the cavity of the kaneh — certainly kosher; for the Rosh wrote: all the stringency is since the intact portion between them combines; and if the punctures and intact portion cover the majority of the width of the kaneh — the punctures alone cover half the majority of the width of the kaneh; for the intact portion between them is only like a full puncture; and if half the majority of the width of the kaneh is more than the proportion of an issur — still difficult is what is stated here regarding a bird that we do not estimate by proportion; and if here we are stringent to combine the intact portion — without the intact portion from here the punctures alone do not cover more than half the majority of the width of the kaneh, and it is more than an issur — and still you permit the forbidden at the measure of an issur; therefore it must be said this half is certainly less than an issur and permitted for all; only the intact portion between them that combines causes another measure — and understand, for this is clear.
