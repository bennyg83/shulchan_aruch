BiYah ShMo. These letters are intended always to begin at the head of a column: the bet of Bereishit, the yud of "Yoducha achecha," the heh of "haba'im bayabashah," the shin of "Shemor lecha," the mem of "Mah tovu," and the vav of "Ve'a'idah bam." So write Hagahot Maimoniyot, chapter 7 of Hilchot Tefillin. They note that there is some defect in forcing the layout this way, and regarding the practice of beginning columns with vav, Mordechai and Semak write that if they had the authority they would abolish it. Hagahot Maimoniyot calls the scribes who insist upon it ignorant. I have seen another serious practice of ignorant scribes. When a final kaf occurs at the end of a line and a little space remains, but not enough for another word, they extend the final kaf horizontally like this:
Dalet so that it resembles a dalet. This is a complete invalidity, because the letter is then a dalet rather than a final kaf. Responsa Maharil, siman 98, likewise warns against this and calls such scribes ignorant people who invalidate Sifrei Torah. A final kaf must have a vertical leg twice the length of its roof. Hagahot Maimoniyot, chapter 1 of Hilchot Tefillin, cites Re'em that bent kaf and final kaf have the same proportions, meaning that if one straightened the bent kaf it would become the final kaf. Since the bent kaf has three proportional parts, the final kaf's leg must be twice the length of its roof. This mistake is found in many Sifrei Torah, and every halachic authority should warn scribes about it and repair existing errors. A Sefer Torah containing such a form is invalid for public reading. When correcting such a final kaf, the entire letter must be erased and rewritten correctly. Merely erasing part of the roof would create chak tochot, as we explained in Orach Chayyim siman 32, seif 17, regarding a letter originally written in a single stroke.
