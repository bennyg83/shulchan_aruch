These mnemonic letters identify words that should begin a column: the bet of Bereishit, yod of “Yehudah, your brothers shall praise you,” heh of “those who came on dry land,” shin of “take heed,” mem of “how goodly,” and vav of “and I shall call them to witness.” Hagahot Maimoniyot records some invalidity when these are misplaced. By contrast, Mordechai and Semak wished to abolish the broader custom of beginning every column with vav. Ignorant scribes also sometimes stretch a final kaf at the end of a line until it resembles a dalet, which invalidates the Torah scroll. A final kaf's leg must be twice the roof's length, corresponding to the proportions of a bent kaf. Rabbis should warn scribes and correct existing scrolls. When repairing such a kaf the entire letter must be erased and rewritten, lest the repair constitute engraving the interior of the letter rather than writing. [Citation 32.] [Citation 98.]
