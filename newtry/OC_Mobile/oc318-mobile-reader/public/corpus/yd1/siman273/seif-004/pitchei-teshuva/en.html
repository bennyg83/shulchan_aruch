Baer Heitev of Rabbi Maharit z"l wrote in name of Shev Yaakov; and see in responsum Chinuch Beit Yosef siman 73 who wrote in name of Gaon Maharshashach regarding a Torah scroll that has an extra row in a sheet beyond its fellow — there is no disqualification and permitted to read from it per law; and see in responsum Brit Avraham Orach Chayyim siman 11 who wrote Chavot Yair permitted only in such a case where there is an extra row; but if one row is missing on one page it is disqualified for this is a sign of an open parashah and thereby made a parashah where there is no parashah; and he wrote he saw in responsum Shev Yaakov that he permitted only if the remaining line's part was not ruled and also did not rely on this alone unless the change was in the entire sheet, etc. — see there who raised that if possible to erase the last row and extend the letters it is fine; but if there are names that cannot be erased then erase at the head of the following page two upper lines and write one below and one above (and extend letters); however if also at the head of the page above in upper rows there are names that cannot be erased — it is disqualified. And see in responsum Teshuvah Me'ahavah part 1 siman 105 asked about a similar matter in a Torah scroll written properly only the scribe did not carefully sew the sheets aligned so pages are equal top and bottom but rise and fall so that even though row count in all pages is equal, nevertheless because one is higher and one lower it appears as if a whole empty line is here — a parashah where there should not be a parashah; and he extended to disqualify the book until corrected — for even though written properly nevertheless the stringency found a place to rest after eyes see rows are not aligned; but he was uncertain after all pages in that sheet are equal whether it is unlike leaving an empty line (and as stated in name of Shev Yaakov); and he raised that the law requires permitting the stitches to make them aligned — but if found thus on Shabbat one should not take out another — see there his reason; and the Gaon author of Noda B'Yehuda agreed that initially must correct; however if they already took out this book or on Shabbat and have no other book they may read from it for one can say the ruling will prove (and so I wrote above in name of Shev Yaakov); and the author returned and answered disputing this permission (however I saw in Bnei Yonah who wrote if he wrote the row count he has but made rows cramped so they do not reach the bottom like rows in other sheets and it appears as if a row is missing — there is to validate for nevertheless the row count appears — see there; if so all the more here; and see in responsum A.A.Z. Pnei Me'eirot part 2 end siman 179 whose view plainly is only if on one page one line is missing it is disqualified but for the entire sheet there is no concern; and he wrote that sometimes it is impossible to compress and they sew sheets not aligned so all is equal and it appears as if one row is higher than its fellow — and this did not occur to anyone to disqualify and this matter is common — go out and see — therefore it appears to me complete permission to read in this Torah scroll without any doubt — end quote — see there). And he further wrote there it is possible if it happens to a scribe on three pages three open parashot at the heads of pages or at their ends in a way not recognizable from both sides — the scribe must train his hands not to make them all open-form of a whole line but another open form; and even so a God-fearing person should act per Rambam's view and per Rosh's view as below siman 275 — and an open whole line is closed per Rosh's view — see there.
