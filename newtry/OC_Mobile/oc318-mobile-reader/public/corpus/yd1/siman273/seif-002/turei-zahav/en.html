He should not reduce the script. This means that if he ruled lines across the entire sheet and did not leave enough space above the uppermost line so that, after writing in his usual script, four fingerbreadths of blank margin would remain above it, and he cannot spoil all the ruling he has already made, one might think that he should write the top line in smaller letters so that the proper upper margin remains. He should not do so, because the writing would look mottled. Rather, after the fact he need not be concerned that the upper margin is smaller than prescribed. This is also implied by the Nimukei Yosef, who writes that although the space between lines should equal the height of a line, one should nevertheless not reduce the script for that reason. This indicates that after the fact the matter does not invalidate the scroll, as the conclusion of this siman states that these measurements are only the ideal manner of performing the mitzvah.
The space between one section and another. The Tur explains that, for example, where a closed section must begin in the middle of a line and have writing on both sides, one should not reduce the letters in order to begin it at the end of the line. This requires study, for it implies that he should not be concerned and should write letters as large as elsewhere in the Torah scroll, yet the scroll will be invalid if the required closed section is absent, as explained in the next siman. The Nimukei Yosef writes instead that the scribe should take care not to reach such a situation. This implies that if he has already completed the preceding section on this line and no other solution is possible, he should reduce the script slightly rather than create an invalid section break, as the Rama writes in siman 275, seif 1, provided that he does not impair a Divine Name. Accordingly, the latter case is not comparable to the former, which remains valid after the fact. See 50, 81, 275, 350, and 509.
