Between one parashah and another. Tur explains: for example, where a closed parashah must remain open in the middle, with writing on both sides, one should not reduce the size of the writing merely in order to begin at the end of the line. This requires study. Tur seems to imply that one should not be particular about this and should write in the same large script as elsewhere in the Sefer Torah, yet if that causes the required closed-parashah spacing to be absent, the Sefer Torah will be invalid, as explained in the next siman. Nimukei Yosef writes instead that the scribe should take care beforehand not to come to such a situation. This implies that if he has already reached the end of the line and cannot otherwise preserve the parashah structure, he should slightly reduce the size of the writing rather than create an invalid parashah, as Rama writes in siman 275, seif 1, that one may make such a correction provided he does not damage a Divine Name. If so, the latter clause here is not truly parallel to the earlier clause mentioned above, which remains valid after the fact.
