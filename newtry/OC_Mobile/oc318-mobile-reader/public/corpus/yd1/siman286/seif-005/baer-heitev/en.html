To cover. Taz wrote: I learned from this that those people who do not make a mezuzah on the grounds that thieves steal it from there and treat it with disgrace — this does not save them, for they can place the mezuzah there in a manner that it will not be exposed at all from the outside, even against the Name of Sha-dai — for that is at any rate better than nullifying the mitzvah entirely. And Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Semag: even at the house entrance in a place where the beginning of the upper third is above ten handbreadths, one may place it there, and even though pigs are found there — end quote.
Visible. The Taz writes that it appears to him that one should not leave it without any covering at all, for this leads to transgression: through touching it each time, the Name Shaddai is erased, as we see in most houses. Rather, one should cover it with a piece of glass, and then the Name will still be visible. This also removes the claim of those who are lenient about making a mezuzah because there is filth there or women wash there, for this is considered a covering even though it is visible. Only in a room where husband and wife have relations is a glass covering insufficient, and it must be covered with wax. Thus his language.
