Such as where children are found. Beit Yosef, in the name of Semag, wrote: even at the house entrance in a place where the beginning of the upper third is above ten handbreadths, one may place it, and even though pigs are found there — end quote; and from what he wrote "it is good to cover it" — we derive that the essential mitzvah is not for it to be visible. From this I derived that those people who do not make a mezuzah on account of saying that thieves steal it from there and treat it with contempt — this does not save them, for they can place the mezuzah there in a manner that it will not be exposed at all from the outside, even against the name of Sha-dai — for that is at any rate better than nullifying the mitzvah entirely.
It is good that [the Name] be visible. It seems to me that one should not set it up so that [the Name] is visible with no covering at all — for this leads to transgression: every time [the mezuzah] is handled on entry and exit, a little of the Name Shaddai is erased until it is entirely gone after a long time, as we see in most homes. Rather, one should insert a piece of glass there, and the Name will be visible through it. This also refutes the argument of those who are lenient about placing a mezuzah uncovered in places where waste matter is sometimes found nearby, or where women bathe occasionally — for with [glass] covering it is considered covered even though visible; and the proof is from what is stated in Orach Chayyim siman 76: feces inside a glass vessel — it is permitted to recite Shema facing it even though one sees it through the walls, since the Torah makes covering determinative and it is covered. The same applies here in reverse: since the Name is covered, there is no prohibition — for the matter does not depend on visibility. And all the more so where [the mezuzah] is above ten handbreadths, which constitutes a separate domain, as Semag wrote, cited by Beit Yosef — in which case one would not even need the glass covering; but this is the better approach. And in a room where a man and his wife sleep, the mezuzah should be covered with wax, as Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Semag in the name of Riva — regarding a case where waste matter is common there; and the same should be done in the room mentioned above, for there a glass covering is not sufficient since marital relations occur there, as it is written: "there shall not be seen among you a shameful thing" — just as we say that nakedness in a glass vessel makes it forbidden to recite Shema facing it. So it seems to me. And see what I wrote in siman 287 seif 2.
