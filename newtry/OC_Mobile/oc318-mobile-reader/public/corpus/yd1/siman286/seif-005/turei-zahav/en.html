Beit Yosef, citing Semag, writes: even at the doorway of the house, where the beginning of the upper third is higher than ten tefachim, he may place the mezuzah there even though pigs are commonly present. Since Semag writes that it is good to cover it, we learn that the essential mitzvah does not require the mezuzah to be visible. From here I learned that people who omit a mezuzah because they say it will be stolen and treated disrespectfully have no excuse. They can place the mezuzah in a manner in which it is not exposed at all from the outside, even opposite the name Shaddai. This is certainly better than completely neglecting the mitzvah.
It is good for it to be visible. It appears to me that one should not leave the mezuzah visible without any covering. Doing so leads to a transgression: every time people touch it while entering and leaving, a little of the name Shaddai is rubbed away until, after many years, it is completely erased, as we see in many houses. Rather, one should place a piece of glass over it; then the Name remains visible. This also removes the argument of those who are lenient about not placing a mezuzah where filth is sometimes present around the location, or where women occasionally wash there, because under glass it is covered even though visible. A proof is Orach Chayyim, siman 76: feces inside a glass container may be opposite a person reciting Shema even though he sees it through the walls, because the Torah made the matter depend on covering and it is covered. The same applies here in reverse: once the Name is covered there is no prohibition, because the matter does not depend on visibility. All the more so where the mezuzah is above ten tefachim, which is like a different domain, as Semag writes and Beit Yosef cites; technically it would not even require glass, though glass is the preferable practice. In a room used by husband and wife, one should cover the mezuzah with wax, as Beit Yosef writes in the name of Semag citing Riva regarding a place where filth is common. The same should be done in such a bedroom, because glass alone is insufficient where marital relations occur, for the verse says, "No nakedness shall be seen among you." Just as one may not recite Shema facing nakedness enclosed in glass, so too here. This appears correct. See what I wrote in siman 287, seif 2.
