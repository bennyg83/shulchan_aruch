Exempt. “Your house” — just as a house is designated for dwelling, so too whatever is designated for dwelling — to exclude these that are not for dwelling. And as for what is written “gatehouse” — it is a small house at the courtyard gate where the watchman sits. And as for “some say a gatehouse, etc.” — it is the view of Rabbi Yitzchak in the Tur, who found two baraitot contradicting each other: in Yoma we learned — in those: exedra, gatehouse, and porch — they are exempt; and in Hakomeitz it is taught that they are obligated. Rabbi Yitzchak resolved this: Hakomeitz speaks of rabbinically. If so, those “some say” also apply to porch and exedra; and Tur and Shulchan Aruch mentioned only one of them. But according to this, also in a garden there would be rabbinic obligation, and even though a garden is not mentioned in the Talmud alongside these — nevertheless Tur holds they are as one since they have no dwelling and are not comparable to a house, yet the rabbis obligated them.
