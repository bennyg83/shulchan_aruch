[All are obligated. See in Sema, Choshen Mishpat siman 427 note 2.
[But the house, etc., is exempt. Since part of it belongs to non-Jews, and also because of danger lest the non-Jew say the Israelite is performing sorcery. And likewise the gates of courtyards and gates of towns are exempt for this reason — due to danger — as stated in the Talmud; and unlike the Ateret Zekenim who writes the reason is because it is written "in order that your days may be multiplied," etc., and non-Jews do not desire this since they do not fulfill the Torah, etc.
[And likewise courtyards or towns, etc. Where some non-Jews live there — even if most of them are Israelites — they are exempt from danger. And for this reason one does not make a mezuzah at the gates of streets such as in the holy community of Kraków and the holy community of Prague, which established gates on the Jewish streets that close at night — and they never established mezuzot there. And this reason is due to the danger of the ruler's false accusations, which were always ready against the enemies of Israel. And there is another reason not to make mezuzot there: they will surely take them and disgrace them, and thereby cause disgrace to sacred writings. From this we learned regarding an Israelite who leases a concession from the lord to sell spirits or beer or mead, and the lord's servants fill the house every day and are accustomed to taking mezuzot and treating them with contempt — one should not make a mezuzah at those entrances unless the entrance is in a place protected from non-Jews who would not take the mezuzah. So wrote Bach.
