A doorway behind the door, etc. Rashi explains that there may be a fixed doorpost in the corner of a house serving two openings, one facing north and one west. If the mezuzah was affixed to that post within the northern doorway, then when the door is open the mezuzah is behind the door. If the thickness of the post creates a separation of one handbreadth between the two openings, another mezuzah is required for the western opening, since the first mezuzah is not within its doorway. If not, no second mezuzah is required, because the first is considered to be within the western doorway as well. See Bach. It appears from this that where two openings are divided by a pillar in the middle, one mezuzah suffices even if the pillar is one handbreadth thick. Two mezuzot are required because of a handbreadth-thick divider only when one doorway faces north and the other west. If both openings face the same direction, they are considered one doorway even when the divider is one handbreadth thick. This is true even according to Rashi's second explanation, concerning a small doorway beside a large gate, where the large gate is not usually opened and the small doorway is used instead. There, if a divider one handbreadth wide separates them, another mezuzah is required. A divided doorway is different, however, because in Rashi's case the small doorway is entirely independent of the large gate, and each opens and closes without the other. Here the two sections are not independent; when one opens, the other opens, and they close together. The central pillar is merely decorative, so the two sections are certainly regarded as one doorway. This is the language of the Tur. See Bach and Derishah regarding their discussion of this matter.
