Doorway behind the door, etc. Rashi explained: such as in the corner of the house there are fixed pegs for two doorways cut in it — one to the north and one to the west; and he fixed the mezuzah in the pegs in the hollow of the northern doorway; and when the door is open it is behind the door — if in the thickness of the pegs there is a separation of a handbreadth between doorway and doorway, another mezuzah is needed for the western doorway since the mezuzah is not in its hollow; and if not, no other mezuzah is needed for it is as placed in its own hollow — end quote. And see Bach. It appears from this that those doorways that are divided with a pillar in the middle suffice with one mezuzah even if the pillar's thickness is a handbreadth — two are not obligated when there is a handbreadth thickness in the pillar except when one is north and one west; but when both doorways are in one direction, even if the peg thickness is a handbreadth, all is considered one doorway — even according to the second explanation that Rashi explained: like the way to make a small doorway next to a large gate that is not always opened, only the small one is opened — and if there is peg thickness of a handbreadth between them, another mezuzah is needed. Even according to this, it seems a divided doorway does not need one — for there the small doorway is completely separate from the large gate, being opened and closed each independently; but here where they are not separated from each other — when this one opens the other also opens and closes together — the pillar is made only for decoration, they are certainly considered as one.
