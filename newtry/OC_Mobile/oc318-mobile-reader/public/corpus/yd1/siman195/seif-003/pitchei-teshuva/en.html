And he shall not receive it from her hand. Yad Eliyahu forbids a niddah from holding a candle for her husband to use. Tashbetz permits transferring a child from her arms to his because a living person carries himself, but not where the child is very small, ill, or restrained. He also advises avoiding touching her garments while she wears them, though the garments themselves are permitted when not on her.
In a separate bowl. Masat Binyamin permits eating from one bowl where other household members eat with them, since their presence is a strong separation. Pitchei Teshuva distinguishes a shared table from a shared bowl, because the husband may inevitably eat her leftovers; therefore a single bowl should be forbidden.
