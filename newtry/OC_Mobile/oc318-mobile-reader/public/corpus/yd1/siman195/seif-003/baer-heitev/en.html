With a cloth. Alternatively, she may uncover part of the table and place her bowl there. Taz writes that bread or a pitcher serves as a distinguishing sign only if they are not eating from that bread or drinking from that pitcher, like the law of meat and milk.
By herself. Bach limits the leniency to where other household members eat with them at the same table. If the couple eat alone, each from a separate bowl but on the same table, it is forbidden. Shach finds this unproven. Taz condemns taking small pieces alternately from one common bowl, but permits where large portions are transferred from a common serving bowl to separate personal dishes before eating.
Some say. Shach writes that Rama cited this as 'some say' only because it came from a found booklet, but it follows all the more from the prohibition against drinking from her remaining cup. Taz rejects Bach's extension forbidding her to bring any food, because ordinary household service is permitted; mixing a cup is forbidden because it is an act of affection.
