Any distinguishing sign. Analogous to above siman 88 seif 2 regarding meat and milk.
Or each one shall eat, etc. Or she should uncover a small part of the table and place her bowl on it — so R. Yonah in Sefer Derashot HaNashim.
And some say, etc. Bach wrote that even per this view it applies only when also the household members eat with them at the same table together — there it is permitted when she eats from her own bowl even though they eat on the same tablecloth without any distinguishing sign; but when the household members eat at another table and the husband and wife eat alone at one table each from his own bowl — it is forbidden even if he was accustomed to eat with her from one bowl; and his words imply that when there are no household members present at all — forbidden; but his words are not compelling.
Some say that it is forbidden, etc. The Maharshal writes that this is specifically when another person has not yet eaten from it, but if another person has eaten from it in between, it is permitted, and one should not be stricter than with her cup. However, in my opinion it is forbidden even if another person ate from it in between. In Hagahot Maimoniyot, in the name of Raam, and in the Mordechai, from whom this law derives, they wrote simply that one may not eat from the leftovers of her food, and they did not distinguish as they did regarding her cup. Moreover, this matter is evident from their proof there from Eliyahu and from Shemuel; see there. Likewise, when it is written above that it is forbidden to drink the leftovers of her cup, that is not specifically drinking, for eating the leftovers of her food is the same, and food is forbidden even if someone else ate from it in between. See what I wrote above, siman 89 s.k. 9.
