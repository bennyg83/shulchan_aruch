(Addendum) Unless, etc. See what I wrote above in siman 88, seif 2. According to the second explanation in Tosafot and the Mordechai, it is forbidden regarding meat and milk. The Raavyah and the glosses to the Mordechai in the first chapter of Shabbos ruled likewise, and all the more so here. The glosses to the Mordechai there also state that the sages of Narbonne forbade a niddah to eat at her husband's table. They expounded the verse, 'Your wife shall be like a fruitful vine in the inner chambers of your house.' The She'iltot of Rav Achai likewise states that a woman who is a niddah may not eat at her husband's table, as we learned: He may not eat, etc. See also above, siman 88, addendum 2. (End of addendum.)
Some say, etc. Hagahot Ashri to Shabbos there states that this itself constitutes a distinguishing sign.
Some say that it is forbidden, etc. This is derived from the statement in Shabbos 13b: 'He ate with me and drank with me.' The Gemara did not mention these facts unnecessarily. Rather, regarding all of them Eliyahu, of blessed memory, said, 'Blessed is the Omnipresent,' etc. 'He drank with me' means that he drank after her, and the same applies to eating. This is why the Mishnah there, 11a, states, 'He may not eat with a zavah,' but does not state the reverse, because she may eat after him, as written in seif 4 in the gloss.
