Bread or a pitcher. It appears that this applies specifically when they do not eat from that bread and do not drink from that pitcher, similar to siman 88. So too wrote the Perishah.
From one bowl. The Perishah and my father-in-law, of blessed memory, copied this wording: "I further saw a great folly, that they cut small pieces into one bowl, he takes one and she takes one until the meal is finished. This is a great error, for it is obvious that this is considered eating together." End quote. This was emended from the work of the Maharshal. Nevertheless, it is implied that when they place a bowl containing large pieces, as they place them in a pot, and each one takes from the bowl, places it before himself on a separate utensil, and eats from there, there is no prohibition. This is because the eating does not follow immediately upon taking from the bowl from which she also takes; rather, he uses another utensil in between. So it appears in my humble opinion.
From the remnants of food, etc. Since food is compared to drink, it would appear at first glance that she may not even bring him food. So too wrote my father-in-law, of blessed memory, in the name of R' Shmuel of Austria. This is difficult to me, for why did they say that she may perform all tasks for him except pouring his cup, without also listing preparing food before him? Therefore, it appears that the Sages assessed that there is no intimacy in this, but only an act of service involving exertion, unlike pouring the cup, as the distinction is made in seif 11 regarding making the bed.
Her food. If another person interrupted between them, it is permitted, as with the remnants of a cup discussed below. Likewise, all the leniencies stated there apply here.
