So that she should not become repulsive to her husband. The Gemara states, “Until Rabbi Akiva came and taught: If so, you cause her to become repulsive to her husband, and her husband will divorce her.” The wording suggests that Rabbi Akiva follows his own opinion at the end of Gittin, where he permits a man to divorce his wife even if he found another woman more attractive. He therefore permits her to adorn herself here. According to us, however, since the halachah in matters of divorce does not follow Rabbi Akiva, perhaps she should be forbidden to adorn herself. We must say that the Rambam, the Tur, and those who follow them hold that although Rabbi Akiva's formulation accords with his own view, even without the concern that he will divorce her, she may adorn herself so that she not become repulsive to him. Similar formulations occur many times in the Gemara. See seif katan 10. The principal reason that a niddah is separated from her husband for seven days is so that he not become overly accustomed to her and grow weary of her, as written above in siman 183; see there. I later found that the Lechem Chamudot, at the end of the chapter Tinoket, page 303, column 3, also noticed this difficulty. He answers that although we rule that one may not divorce his wife merely because he found another woman more attractive, there is still concern that if she becomes repulsive to him he may invent accusations against her until he divorces her.
