They remember always she is niddah. And so is Tur's language — and I do not know from where they derived this reason; behold the reason is explicit in Talmud end chapter "although" — so she not be repulsive to her husband; and Rashi explained in days of purity if she wears clothes she wore in niddah days — end quote; and great wonder on all Acharonim who did not notice this on Tur; and there are several practical differences between the two reasons — requires study. (And this remembrance is why distinguishing sign not needed unless there is some habit and affection and closeness of mind.) And it appears simple — for all reasons if she has special clothes even fine and adorned — she is permitted to wear them; and although there in Talmud it says permitted with worn-out clothes (meaning old worn clothes) for woman who covers herself in them during niddah days — meaning husband is not obligated to give her important clothes to cover in niddah days; but if she has — plainly permitted to wear them; and this is included in what is stated seif 9 — they permitted her to adorn in niddah days; and plainly not like Maharshal wrote in his commentary to Semag negative precepts page 80a — halachah is not like R. Akiva who permits adorning; and we find in words of our rabbis — woman shall not adorn in niddah days; and she has special clothes for her niddah as above in laws of marriage — end quote; and in truth all this is not — special clothes are not because of "she shall not adorn"; and see Shach who ruled also woman shall not adorn; and see s.k. 11.
