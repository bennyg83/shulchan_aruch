So that they will always remember that she is a niddah. This is also the wording of the Tur, but I do not know the source of this reason. The Gemara at the end of the chapter Af Al Pi explicitly gives the reason that she should not become repulsive to her husband. Rashi explains that during her days of purity she might wear the garments she wore during her days of niddah. It is very surprising that all the later authorities did not note this difficulty in the Tur, for there are several practical differences between the two reasons, and the matter requires study. The need for this reminder is understandable only where there is some habitual affection and closeness, for otherwise no distinguishing sign is required. It appears obvious that according to either reason, if she has garments specially designated for her days of niddah, even if they are fine and decorative, she may wear them. Although the Gemara says that a woman may wear worn garments during her days of niddah, this means only that the husband is not obligated to provide her with expensive clothing for those days. If she already owns such clothing, she may plainly wear it. This is included in the ruling in seif 9 that she may adorn herself during her days of niddah. This is contrary to Maharshal's commentary to the negative commandments in the Semag, page 80a, where he writes that the halachah does not follow Rabbi Akiva, who permits adornment, and that our Sages taught that a woman should not adorn herself during her days of niddah and should have special garments for those days, as stated above in Hilchot Ishut. In truth, this is incorrect, because special garments were not instituted in order to prevent adornment. See also the Shaarei Dura, which rules that a woman should not adorn herself, and see seif katan 11.
