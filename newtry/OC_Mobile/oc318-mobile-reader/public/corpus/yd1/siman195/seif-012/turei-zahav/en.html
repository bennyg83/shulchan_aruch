Forbidden to pour water for him. Apparently from this language forbidden to give water in vessel and he washes afterward; but in truth not so — for this is Rashba's language — even when he washes and she pours — for if to wash with her hands even without washing forbidden — for there is flesh closeness; forbidden to touch even small finger — end quote. It implies permitted to prepare water in vessel and he washes from there — for since it writes "he washes and she pours" — implies during his washing she pours and not that she prepares for him first before his washing; and further if you explain this too is forbidden — difficult from where to forbid this — for he wrote afterward proof one should not explain washing with her hand from flesh closeness — we infer deals here with matter not flesh closeness — namely she pours during washing; and one can say also this matter that she pours while he washes — also forbidden without affection of washing but because she touches him through water flowing from her hand to his hand — one can say this is touch through something else — she touches vessel and vessel in water and water in his hand — would your mind say permitted? He informs forbidden because of washing that brings to affection; and so appears to me from Talmud language that said in this law — washing his face hands and feet forbidden; and this matter of giving water to vessel first without pouring on him — not called washing at all; and he should have said giving water for washing forbidden as stated there regarding mixing cup — so too should have said; and giving water — not so if she pours on his hands — properly called washing; but certainly as Perishah explained — no prohibition in giving water alone; and so in Perishah; but nevertheless plain to me also this is in category of prohibition — if he washes from vessel that has hole below and during his washing she pours water into vessel — also this is called washing as above.
