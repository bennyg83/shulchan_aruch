She is forbidden to pour water for him. At first glance, this wording implies that she is forbidden to place water in a vessel for him to wash afterward. In truth, however, this is not so, for the Rashba writes: "Even if he washes and she pours. For if she were to wash him with her hands, even without the act of washing it would be forbidden, since there is physical closeness, and it is forbidden to touch even his little finger." End quote. This implies that she may prepare water for him in a vessel and he may wash from it. Since he wrote, "he washes and she pours," it implies that she pours while he is washing, not that she prepares it for him beforehand, before he washes. Furthermore, if one interprets that even preparing the water is forbidden, it is difficult to understand the source for such a prohibition. He afterward brings proof that the passage cannot mean that she washes him with her hands, because of physical closeness. We therefore infer that the case here concerns something that is not physical closeness, namely, that she pours while he washes. One might ask that pouring while he washes should also be forbidden even apart from the affection associated with washing, because she touches him through the water flowing from her hand to his. One can answer that this is contact through another medium: she touches the vessel, the vessel touches the water, and the water touches his hand. One might have thought this permitted; therefore, it teaches us that it is forbidden because washing leads to affection. So too it appears from the wording of the Gemara, which says regarding this law that washing his face, hands, and feet is forbidden. Placing water into a vessel beforehand, without pouring it upon him, is not called washing at all. It should then have said that providing water for washing is forbidden, just as it says there regarding pouring the cup. Here too, it should have said "providing water." By contrast, when she pours upon his hands, it is properly called washing. Rather, it is certainly as I explained, that merely placing water in the vessel is not forbidden. So too wrote the Derishah. Nevertheless, it is obvious to me that the following is also included in the prohibition: if he washes from a vessel with an opening at the bottom, and while he washes she pours water into the vessel, this too is called washing. So it appears to me.
