Even at her heel. The Gemara in the second chapter of Nedarim states: “Whoever gazes at a woman's heel will have children who are unworthy.” Rabbi Yosef said that this refers to one's wife while she is a niddah. Rabbi Shimon ben Lakish said that the “heel” mentioned there means the private area aligned opposite the heel. This appears difficult for the Tur, whose wording is followed by the Mechaber, for he writes that the same applies to all normally covered areas, whereas the Gemara appears to mention specifically the heel. The Beit Yosef and other later authorities explain that the Tur understands all covered areas to be included in the private area, and the Rashba likewise writes in Torat HaBayit that from the heel we derive the rule for every covered area. Yet this too is difficult, since the Gemara carefully specifies the private area aligned opposite the heel. Moreover, the Tur's wording, “even at her heel,” does not appear precise. It therefore seems clear that, according to the Rashba and the Tur, Reish Lakish's statement does not refer to Rabbi Yosef's explanation at all. Rather, they disagree in their interpretation of the baraita. Rabbi Yosef establishes the baraita as referring to the actual heel of one's wife while she is a niddah, whereas Reish Lakish explains that it refers to any woman, even when she is not a niddah, and that “heel” means the private area. According to Rabbi Yosef, the baraita indeed means the actual heel, and gazing at it is nevertheless forbidden because it is normally covered. The same rule therefore applies to every covered area. I found this very interpretation in Baal HaNefesh of the Raavad, page 49a. He concludes: “We follow both opinions stringently. It is forbidden to gaze at the heel of one's wife while she is a niddah, and from the heel we derive the rule for every covered area. It is also forbidden to gaze at the private area even when the woman is not a niddah.” This also appears to be the view of the Rashba and the Tur. Their words are therefore correct and clear, and we need not resort to the strained explanations of the Beit Yosef, Bach, and Derishah. See there.
