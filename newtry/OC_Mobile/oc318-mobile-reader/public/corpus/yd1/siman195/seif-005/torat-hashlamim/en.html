He should not sit on the bed designated for her. Certainly he may not sleep on it. From the words of the poskim, however, it appears that she may sleep on his bed, because she is not thereby likely to become accustomed to intimacy, as explained above regarding drinking. There is also some support from the account in the first chapter of Shabbos concerning Eliyahu, where she said, “He ate and drank with me,” but did not say, “I ate with him and drank with him.” See Maharshal's commentary to the negative commandments in the Semag. The passage there also states, “and he slept with me.” See seif katan 8. The Taz, however, wrote that she too is forbidden to sleep on the bed designated for him, but his words are not compelling. The Taz further wrote that there is no prohibition against touching a sheet soiled with blood, although some people are careful about this, and that their practice is mistaken. The Derishah likewise wrote this at the end of siman 193. I see some support for this from Tosafot at the end of the second chapter of Niddah, which states that a man may inspect his wife's blood. It is also clear from the Gemara and from the Rambam, Hilchot Issurei Biah, chapter 5, that he may take the cloth in his hand and shade it with his hand. Thus, touching it is permitted. See the Derishah, who explains how this mistaken custom arose.
