He shall not sit on the bed, etc. This implies even mere sitting without lying down, for sitting also gives rise to improper thoughts, and through such thoughts he may come to habitual transgression. So wrote the Beit Yosef. In some texts, the Tur reads here, "he shall not lie down." My father-in-law, of blessed memory, wrote that the reading "he shall not sit" is a scribal error, since above, at the end of siman 193, the Tur and Shulchan Aruch wrote, "he shall not sleep." This does not appear correct, for there the teaching is that with a bride, even sleeping there is permitted. Here, however, sitting certainly also involves improper thoughts, for the Mordechai wrote, and the Rama cites it below, that it is forbidden to sit together on a long bench, etc. All the more so is sitting on her bed forbidden, for there is greater cause for improper thoughts. It appears that, all the more so, she may not sleep in his bed, because there is greater cause for improper thoughts in her lying down and rising. Mere sitting on his bed, however, is permitted for her, for she does not entice him. So it appears to me. There is no prohibition in touching a sheet soiled with blood, even though some are careful about this; that practice is an error.
