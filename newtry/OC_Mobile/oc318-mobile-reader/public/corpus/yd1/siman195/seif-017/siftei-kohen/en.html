It is forbidden to feel the pulse, etc. However if the patient is in danger and there are no doctors, it is implied somewhat from Ramban’s responsum siman 127 that it is permitted because of pikuach nefesh; but one may say he follows his own view that touching a niddah is only rabbinic; but for Rambam who holds touching an ervah is forbidden by Torah law — here perhaps even though there is pikuach nefesh it is forbidden as an ancillary to sexual immorality — requires investigation — end of Beit Yosef. And so Taz. And it does not appear correct, for certainly even for Rambam there is no Torah prohibition except when done with desire and affection for relations, as I wrote above siman 157 s.k. 10; which is not the case here; and so the widespread custom is that Jewish physicians feel the pulse of a woman even a married woman or a gentile woman, even though there are other gentile physicians; and they also perform other kinds of medical touching according to medical practice; rather the matter is plain as I wrote; and this seems to be Rav’s view for above in siman 157 his words imply like Rambam, as I wrote there in s.k. 10; and here he permitted feeling the pulse — nonetheless when there is no danger it is forbidden for her husband to feel her pulse when she is a niddah, as in Ramban’s responsum and Rav’s words.
