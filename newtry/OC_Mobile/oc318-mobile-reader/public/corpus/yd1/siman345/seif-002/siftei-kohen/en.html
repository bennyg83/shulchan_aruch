Such as where he said, "I am going up," etc. The Ramban wrote that even if they did not see him throw himself down, since he said, "I am going up to the roof, and I will throw myself down and die," there is a presumption that he acted knowingly. The Bach wrote in the name of Maharshal that even if he said, "See that I am going up," etc., but they did not see him go up, we do not rely on his speech alone, and he does not have the presumption of one who destroyed himself knowingly. The Bach wrote that his words are correct.
Or killed, etc. See above siman 364 seif 4 regarding the law of those killed.
One who stole, etc. It implies that the Rav means: one might have thought that since he stole and robbed and exposed himself to death under their laws, one should not mourn for him, because he is like one who destroyed himself knowingly. Therefore he teaches that this is not so, because he thought he would be able to escape. But because of the transgression of theft and robbery, one does mourn for him, for he is not considered one who separated from the ways of the community below in seif 5. So it appears from the Rav's words. So wrote the Rashba in a responsum at the end of siman 763, that ordinary sinners such as robbers and the like are not included among those who separate from the ways of the community. According to this, if a thief or robber dies in bed, one mourns for him, and the Bach wrote that this is the practice. Although Mahari Weil responsa siman 114 wrote regarding an incident of a thief executed under their laws, "What is written in books, that the thief accepted, etc., I do not know its explanation. Certainly he has atonement and a share in the World to Come, for even those executed by court have a share in the World to Come, even non-Jews, as stated in chapter Nigmar HaDin: they tell him to confess, for anyone who confesses has a share in the World to Come; here too presumably he confessed," etc., he does not mean that specifically his execution is an atonement, as the Bach understood. Rather, the same applies if he died in bed: it is an atonement when he confesses. It is just that the incident there was one of execution. However, above in siman 340 seif 5, the Rav wrote that for one accustomed to transgress one does not mourn. It appears this is where he did not confess before death.
