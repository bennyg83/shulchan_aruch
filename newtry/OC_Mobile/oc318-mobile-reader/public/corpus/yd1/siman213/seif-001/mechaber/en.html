That vows do not take effect on a matter that has no substance; it contains three se’ifim. Vows do not take effect on a matter that has no substance. How so? If he said, “Konam that I speak with you,” “that I act for you,” “that I walk to you,” “that I sleep with you” — it is not a vow. And likewise if he said, “My speech and my action and my walking are forbidden to you,” and “sleep is forbidden to me” — it is not a vow. However, d’rabbanan he needs questioning (meaning: to ask about his vow and find a breach to release him). Therefore, Reuven who forbids upon himself hearing Shimon’s prayer needs questioning d’rabbanan. But if he said, “Konam my mouth speaking with you,” “konam my hands acting for you,” “konam my feet walking to you,” “konam my eyes in sleep” — it is a full vow from the Torah. And all the more so if he said, “My mouth shall be forbidden to you for my speech,” and “my hands for my action,” and “my feet for my walking.”
{Rama: One who says, “Speech of my mouth upon you,” or “lifting a stone upon me” — some say it is a vow, since he mentioned the mouth and the stone (Taz, siman 114; and so it appears in Ran in the name of Tosafot that are in Beit Yosef, end of siman 239).}
