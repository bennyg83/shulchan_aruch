Konam she'ani, etc. — Ran's wording: in all these we read "she'ani" without yod, because in vows the object is forbidden to the person, so it is phrased as "she'ani," etc.; and Kesef Mishneh brought it in chapter 3 of Nedarim, concluding: so one should emend in all these "she'ani" without yod — end. And above in siman 206, where he said "modarani mimcha" or "mufrashetni mimcha" — it also fits the language of "she'eini ochel," for there the person is the object being forbidden and he says "modarani" or "mufrashetni" upon the person, that he shall be separated from him so he shall not eat with him — investigate — end of Hagahot Perishah; and so Tosafot distinguish at the beginning of Nedarim.
Nevertheless rabbinically, etc. — And the practical difference between a biblical vow and a rabbinic one is regarding whether one must observe prohibition for the days that passed, as above siman 208 seif 2 — Bach. Alternatively as above siman 205 s.k. 2.
Requires a query. — even for a matter that has no substance, and we open an opening for him from another place and annul his vow — Rambam; and see above siman 208 s.k. 9.
His mouth, etc. — for his mouth and his hands, etc., have substance; and see above siman 234 seif 71.
Konam my eyes in sleep. — that is, when he specified one or two days; but if he vowed unspecified "in sleep" it is like vowing never to sleep and is a vain vow; and likewise a vow not to sleep three days is a vain vow, as below siman 236 seif 4.
Some say it is a biblical vow. — and even though speech and taking have no substance, nevertheless since he mentioned the object as well the vow takes effect.
