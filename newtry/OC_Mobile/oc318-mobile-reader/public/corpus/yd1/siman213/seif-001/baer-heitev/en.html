She'ani. Ran writes that in all these cases the text reads she'ani without a yod. Since in the law of vows a person prohibits an object to himself, the Mishnah uses the formulation she'ani, etc. Above in siman 206, where one says, "I am forbidden from you," or, "I am separated from you," the formulation she'eini okhel is also appropriate, because there his own body is the object that he prohibits. These are the words of the Perishah's gloss.
Release. Even when the vow concerns something intangible, an opening is suggested to him from another source and his vow is released. Thus writes Rambam. Siftei Kohen writes that the practical difference between a vow governed by Torah law and one governed by rabbinic law concerns the requirement to observe the prohibition for a number of days corresponding to those on which he violated it. See above, siman 208, se'if 2.
Concerning sleep. This applies when he specified one or two days. An unspecified vow concerning sleep is equivalent to a vow never to sleep and is therefore a vain vow. A vow not to sleep for three days is likewise a vain vow, as stated below in siman 236, se'if 4. Thus writes Siftei Kohen.
A vow according to the Torah. Although speaking and taking are not tangible acts, since he also mentioned the object, the vow takes effect. Thus writes Siftei Kohen.
