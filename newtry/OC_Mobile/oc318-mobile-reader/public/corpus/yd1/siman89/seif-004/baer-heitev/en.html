Sticking. Taz wrote that this refers to what was written that one wants to cut cheese with a meat knife; but what was written that it is forbidden to cut bread, etc. — one need only wipe the knife, as Beit Yosef wrote, for it is noten taam bar noten taam; and for us one need not this, for it is explained in siman 96 that specifically with a sharp thing they say it absorbs because of pressure of the knife, end of his words. And Shach wrote that Maharshal forbids cutting bread with it to eat with cheese even if they stuck it in the ground; and he challenged him, for the halachah is that if one cut bread with a meat knife its taste was nullified in the bread and it is permitted to cut with it even a radish to eat it in kutach as below end of seif 96. (And I say one may resolve that one who investigates Maharshal's words appears that certainly it is permitted to cut bread with a meat knife through sticking to eat with cheese; but Maharshal deals with a meat knife that is forbidden l'chatchila to stick in the ground to eat cheese itself with it — investigate.)
Knives. Shach wrote at a time of pressure when he has no other knife we establish the law for it; and Taz wrote that according to the custom it is forbidden to cut cheese with a meat knife even through sticking in the ground; but nevertheless to cut bread with it to eat cheese — in this we may well rely on wiping it first, end of his words. (And Bach wrote specifically at a time of pressure, such as when he is on the road and has no other knife — then it is permitted to cut bread with a meat knife and eat with cheese — and so Shach.)
