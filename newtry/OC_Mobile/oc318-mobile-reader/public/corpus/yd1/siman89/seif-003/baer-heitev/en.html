And fat. Taz wrote in name of a sage that even with goose fat one should be stringent so; and Beit Yosef in Orach Chaim siman 173 wrote that meat broth has the law of a meat dish since it is clear, but if it is thick like vegetables it has the law of meat, end of his words.
In a pot. Shach wrote below siman 95 it will be explained that even to eat it with cheese is permitted, for it is noten taam bar noten taam; and it appears that what it teaches us here that it is permitted to eat cheese afterward is even if it was cooked in a pot that was not rinsed well, for there is a bit of substance of prohibition — in such a case it is forbidden to eat it with cheese as explained there — and here it is permitted, end of his words. (And I do not understand what he wrote that even to eat it with cheese is permitted as noten taam bar noten taam — for Rama there forbids, see there.)
Between them. Shach wrote that even by day, according to all, cheese sticks more to the hands than cheese itself.
Waiter. Shach disagrees on this, for Rashi in chapter Kol HaBasar does not appear so, but only regarding netilat yadayim at the meal for eating, as written in Orach Chaim siman 163; but intermediate waters between a dish and cheese or the reverse — the reason is that it sticks to the hands — it is plain there is no difference between eater and one who touches. (And Peri Chadash wrote it is reasonable that if one carries and brings the food placed in the bowl, he need not rinsing, see there.)
