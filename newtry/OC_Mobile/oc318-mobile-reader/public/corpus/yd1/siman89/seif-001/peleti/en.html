Even of wild animal and fowl — Rosh wrote so they practice; but by law it appears Tosafot and Rosh do not hold so, for they challenged lest the Gemara not be established for aggra that the master said fowl and cheese are eaten at an apikores meal — he ate fowl meat first and afterward cheese — and resolved: if so it implies domestic animal meat requires wiping — even waiting six hours requires, see there — and they did not resolve briefly how one eats at an apikores meal — he needs six hours — and how is apikores meal relevant — only certainly for fowl Tosafot hold need not; therefore they brought all proofs from precision of domestic animal.
If meat remains between his teeth: Tur implies that according to Rambam, the reason for waiting six hours is because of meat remaining between the teeth. After that interval, it is considered digested. Pri Chadash asks that the Gemara itself asks what the law is regarding meat between the teeth and answers from the verse, "while the meat was still between their teeth." In my view, this very passage provides support for the six-hour interval. The ordinary interval between one meal and the next is six hours, and the reason is that during that time a person is not yet hungry to eat again, whereas after six hours he again desires food, as explained in Orach Chayyim regarding the measure of digestion. This was the Gemara's question: how long does meat between the teeth retain its status before it is considered digested? The answer is from the verse, "while the meat was still between their teeth." Tehillim likewise says, "They had not yet turned from their craving; their food was still in their mouths." This shows that the food had not yet been digested, and Midrash likewise says it was before the food had been digested. Thus the period during which meat between the teeth is still considered meat is the same period before digestion is complete. Once digestion is complete, it no longer has that status. This is clear.
After eating meat one hour — Peri Chadash's view to give reason for this custom from Zohar that says do not eat one time — this is not so, for language of one time means one period and is not measured in hours of the world but a time — and from afternoon until evening is also called one time; but it appears this is whether to wait one hour or six hours they disagree about digestion — see Orach Chaim siman 184 Magen Avraham s.k. 9: beginning of digestion is about an hour and a fifth and end of digestion six hours; the lenient counted this as measure of beginning of digestion for Birkat HaMazon so no longer need Birkat HaMazon — if so also for this it is already digested and gone and therefore permitted to eat — and almost their reasons are sensible; the stringent follow end of digestion which is six hours as Magen Avraham wrote there — therefore six hours waiting is required — and this is clear. And here we learn we require full six hours and not like Peri Chadash to say in short winter days one eats at noon and can eat cheese at evening — for that is between meal and meal, for per my words we require measure of digestion. And also what did he tell us that in winter if one eats at noon the meal time is immediately evening — certainly they eat when many hours passed at night — can digestion or meat taste pass in winter and shortest days more than long days? There is nothing to this; and in truth on the contrary there is room to be stringent, for four hours is meal time for all — nevertheless they eat only at evening as is known — and if they want to be precise they should add more than six hours; therefore the essence is six hours without adding or subtracting.
But they are not scrupulous in this — and Taz picked a quarrel; and I did not understand the reason of those who forbid, for the whole matter of not eating meat and cheese together is because of heker lest they eat meat and milk together — what of it if he intends to bless in order to eat cheese afterward — in the end there is heker; and on the contrary if his intent is only to bless in order to eat cheese there is greater heker — how would he eat together; and also he cannot eat if not with a break of Birkat HaMazon — therefore I did not know what stringency is here.
