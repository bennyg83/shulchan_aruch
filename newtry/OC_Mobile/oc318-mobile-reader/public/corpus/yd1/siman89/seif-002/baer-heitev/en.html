That he examine. Shach wrote that the same applies at night if he has a good lamp like a torch — he need not rinsing; however Rif wrote [R' Peretz] that one should wash them even by day, because sometimes the cheese is fatty and sticks to the moisture of the hands without his knowledge; and Atzei Etz Chaim wrote that so they practice. (And Peri Chadash wrote that one who eats with a fork, whether by day or night, need not rinsing.)
To wash them. Specifically in water rinsing helps, but not in other liquids — Shach.
And to rinse it. One need not be precise whether mouth-wiping or rinsing comes first — whichever he wishes he may do first.
Fowl. Shach wrote that even though meat of wild animal too is only d'rabbanan, nevertheless since its meat resembles that of domestic animal they were stringent with it.
To be stringent. Shach wrote that Maharshal picked a quarrel with those who are stringent that it is like heresy, and his words are not compelling. And Taz wrote in name of Orach Chaim that the measure of hard cheese that Rama mentioned means that six months passed on it or that it is wormy; and specifically with wormy cheese one should be stringent; but if it is not wormy and was not made from milk that was set in the stomach but from mere milk and dried out, or one eats butter — one should not be stringent in this more than wiping, cleaning teeth, rinsing, and rinsing hands, except for one who practices abstinence and scrupulousness, end of his words. And Shach wrote in name of Mitzaref LeChochmah that according to what they practice to wait one hour, it is possible the Zohar too agrees that it is forbidden only in one meal [and in one time], and the language of the Zohar in parashat Mishpatim so appears.
