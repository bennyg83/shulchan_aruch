And this applies, etc. In the Tosefta at the beginning of chapter 4: A person may rent his money to a money changer so that he may display it, learn from it, and adorn himself with it. If it was stolen or lost, he is responsible. If it was taken from before him through unavoidable circumstances, he has the law of a paid custodian, meaning that he is exempt. If it was entrusted to him on condition of full responsibility, it is prohibited because of interest. Meaning, it was entrusted on the original condition that he would be liable even for unavoidable circumstances. This is what the gloss means by, “specifically when he did not,” etc.
And if he accepted, etc.; nevertheless, etc. Terumat HaDeshen writes similarly. He cites the gloss to Tosafot, 70a, concerning the statement there that the money of orphans is permitted, etc. The gloss states: “It appears that where all responsibility remains upon the giver, it is permitted even to stipulate a fixed payment, for this is not a loan but a deposit, and he receives payment for the use of his money. The Tosefta permits renting money to a money changer because the coins are returned intact. The same should apply even if he rented them with permission to spend them, and it should be permitted,” etc. Tosafot there, 69b, s.v. “He rented,” writes that he accepted responsibility, etc. Terumat HaDeshen strained to explain that Tosafot may be speaking where he accepted liability only for unavoidable circumstances, but not for theft or loss. His words are extremely forced and should not be accepted. Certainly, when Tosafot wrote “unavoidable circumstances,” that wording was not precise, as Rivash wrote, because had he not accepted all responsibility, Rav Hama would not have erred in this matter. The gloss plainly disagrees. It maintains that there is no distinction between renting money for use while retaining the identical coins and renting it with permission to spend them. This follows Rashi's explanation, as Tosafot states in s.v. “The spade” in Rashi's name. The Tosefta posed a difficulty for them, and they answered that if the recipient accepts responsibility it is indeed permitted. Tosafot, however, distinguished between use of the identical object and permission to spend it, and all the authorities made the same distinction.
