It is permitted to charge more for the rental of land. The reason is that rental payment is not due until the end. Consequently, the reduction he grants him now is considered merely a discount. The Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Hagahos Maimoniyos, citing the Rashba, author of the Tosafos, that this leniency applies only if the renter immediately enters the property; otherwise, it is payment for waiting. The Darkei Moshe wrote, however, that the Maggid Mishneh cited the Rashba as ruling that even if he does not immediately reside in the courtyard, it is permitted, since through the rental of land he acquires rights in it immediately. This is unlike the hiring of a worker, because a worker does not become obligated from now. End quote.
And all the more so if he has already become obligated, etc. It appears obvious that this constitutes fixed interest. Even in the first case, where he had already taken possession, etc., the Beit Yosef was uncertain whether it constitutes fixed interest; but in this case it appears that he concedes that it constitutes fixed interest.
It is permitted to increase a groom's dowry, etc. The reason is that it is considered as though he is increasing the dowry itself, as stated afterward. The same applies whenever a person now obligates himself to give his fellow a gift, stipulating that if he does not pay by a specified date, he will add 4 peshitim per month for every litra. So wrote the Beit Yosef at the end of siman 161, citing the Rivash in the name of the Rashba. It appears to me that what is written here, that the condition must be made before the marriage, means at the first time that he binds himself toward his son-in-law to give him a dowry, namely, when the marriage conditions are written for the groom. Since until that time he has no obligation whatsoever, and if he wishes he need not give him anything, the additional payment is included within that gift. However, if the conditions record only the obligation of the dowry, and at the wedding, when he is settling the dowry, he writes that he will give him profits, this leniency is ineffective. At that point he is already obligated to give him the amount of the dowry, and if he does not give it, he must pay the penalty written in the conditions. It is therefore like an ordinary debt, and a valid arrangement permitting the profits must be made. This is also implied by the words of the Rivash there in the Beit Yosef: Reuven betrothed his daughter to Shimon and stipulated with him that he would give 400 dinarim as a dowry in promissory notes between kiddushin and the seven blessings, etc. End quote. Regarding this he wrote the present leniency, implying that they stipulated this at the first time they wrote the binding agreement. This is clear in my humble opinion. The Levush likewise wrote at the end of siman 161 that if they reached such an arrangement after the chuppah, it constitutes outright ribbis, because immediately after the chuppah the obligation becomes like a loan upon him. Even before the chuppah, it is preferable to combine everything into principal and write that the bride's father is obligated to give the groom such-and-such an amount in the first year, and such-and-such an amount in the second year, etc.
