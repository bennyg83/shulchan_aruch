And likewise beside the roots, etc. The Ateret Zekenim wrote that one must be careful not to sow near the roots of a vine or tree, because the roots draw nourishment and the sowing is considered close to the vine. The Ran's words imply that the prohibition depends primarily on what is visible above ground and on the place of sowing, not on hidden roots. Nevertheless, lechatchilah one should be stringent where the roots are exposed or where the sowing is directly beside them in a way that appears connected to the vine. But where the roots are hidden underground and the required distance from the trunk or vine is kept, there is no need to prohibit bedieved. The practical rule follows the visible measure, while taking care lechatchilah not to create an appearance of kilayim near exposed roots.
