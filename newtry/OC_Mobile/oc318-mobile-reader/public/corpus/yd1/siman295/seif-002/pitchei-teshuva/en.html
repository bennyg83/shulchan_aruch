The apple with the hawthorn. See Levushei Serad in Chiddushei Dinim siman 106 — he was asked regarding apple trees grafted onto wild apples called in foreign tongue "quince" — whether permitted to maintain them. When he investigated it became known that in those wild apples there are two kinds — for found among them a few apple trees that are good and larger than quince, and a refined person eats them without pickling; and most are the common quince that cannot be eaten without pickling. He answered in any case the grafted trees are forbidden and must be uprooted, for the inferior quince are barren trees as explained from Taz's words in Orach Chaim siman 203; and we hold food tree on barren tree is kilayim; and the good kind among them — although not called barren trees — nevertheless it is not the same kind as good apples, as we learned "apple and hawthorn are kilayim one with the other"; and Ra'avad explained hawthorn is wild apple; and one cannot say this means the inferior kind, for if so, etc.; rather certainly hawthorn is the superior kind among them — nevertheless because of distance of their tastes it is kilayim, for they are two food trees. And he wrote the great ones of the generation agreed to his words — see there. And see responsum of Mishkenot Yaakov siman 66 who challenged him and elaborated to uphold the custom the world practiced to permit in this even ab initio — see there at length.
