And specifically, etc. This is the view of the Rosh. He proves it from the law of a single vine, whose required working space is six tefachim, even though its roots spread beyond six tefachim. We also say that where a fence stands between them, one may plant adjacent to the fence on one side and the other may plant adjacent to it on the other side. The Ran, however, at the end of the first chapter of Kiddushin, as cited by the Beis Yosef and the Behag, writes that the roots of a vine spread below three tefachim. It is therefore permitted to plant at its side. The Rash likewise writes there that planting at the side is permitted even next to a vine, because roots do not travel laterally. See Tosafos to Kiddushin 39a, at the end of s.v. “No.” Their concluding explanation requires study.
And even where he bent a branch into the ground, etc. This is stated in the Yerushalmi, according to the Rash's explanation there of the question, “With what case are we dealing,” etc. The concern is seeds planted directly above the vine, meaning specifically above the vine itself, as the Rash concludes there. Tosafos to Bava Basra likewise writes this there, s.v. “One who bends a branch,” etc.
