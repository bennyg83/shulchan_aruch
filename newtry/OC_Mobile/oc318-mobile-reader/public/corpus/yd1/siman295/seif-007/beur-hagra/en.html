It is forbidden, etc. This is the Mishnah at the beginning of the eighth chapter.
But the fruit, etc. This is stated there, and the same applies to the fruit of a grafted tree. Thus, Chullin 115a asks, “Why should mixed seeds not be forbidden,” etc., and answers that the prohibitions are juxtaposed, etc. The verse under discussion concerns grafting trees, as explained above.
And even to that person, etc. This appears from the Yerushalmi in the fifth chapter, which states: If a landowner maintained field vegetables in a vineyard, they are forbidden both to him and to others. If a worker maintained field vegetables in a vineyard, they are forbidden to him but permitted to others. The Yerushalmi asks: If they are forbidden to him, they should be forbidden to everyone; and if they are permitted to everyone, they should also be permitted to him. It answers that the ruling follows Rabbi Shimon, who holds that a person cannot consecrate property that is not his. Although Rabbi Shimon says that a person cannot consecrate property that is not his, he agrees here that it is forbidden to the worker himself. This implies that the special rule applies specifically to a vineyard. The same is evident from the question, “If it is permitted to everyone,” etc., and from the answer that Rabbi Shimon agrees here, meaning that with respect to the worker himself he concedes to the Sages that the produce becomes forbidden.
And it is permitted, etc. This is stated in the Yerushalmi in the first chapter and cited by the Rash there at the end of the fourth Mishnah.
