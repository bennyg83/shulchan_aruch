(Collected note) But, etc. At the end of the second chapter of Pesachim (39a), Rabbi Zugi said, etc. The Yerushalmi at the beginning of Kilayim states: Rabbi Yosi said in the name of Rabbi Yochanan that all of them are paired species. Was this statement said regarding the entire chapter, or only regarding this halachah? It may be inferred from Rav's ruling that all five vegetables with which one fulfills the obligation of maror on Pesach may be sown together in a single garden bed. It was said that Rav's ruling disputes Rabbi Yochanan, which indicates that Rabbi Yochanan's statement concerned the entire chapter. Rava said in Rav's name that all of them are paired species. The Yerushalmi asks whether Rav's position is inconsistent, since there he says that all are one species, while here he says otherwise. It answers that when Rav said they are all one species, he meant only that they are all species of vegetables and may therefore all be sown together in a single garden bed, as explained there in Pesachim. This concludes the collected note.
And it is likewise permitted, etc. This is stated in the Yerushalmi in the first chapter and cited by the Rash in his commentary to the first Mishnah: “You shall not sow your field with kilayim.” One might have thought that this includes even two varieties of the same species, etc. The Yerushalmi states further at the seventh Mishnah: If so, it should be forbidden to graft a black fig onto a white fig. Rabbi Avin said: Did you not learn this from the prohibition of mixed fabrics? Just as the mixed fabrics that I prohibited to you consist of two distinct species, neither being a variety of the other, so too every form of kilayim that I prohibited consists of two distinct species, neither being a variety of the other.
Even though, etc. As stated there in the Mishnah regarding cultivated endive and wild endive, etc.; Egyptian mustard and ordinary mustard; and the varieties of gourds, etc.
And likewise a non-fruit-bearing tree, etc. As explained above in seif 3 from the Yerushalmi and Toras Kohanim, only grafting a non-fruit-bearing tree onto a fruit tree is forbidden.
Nevertheless, etc. This is the ruling of the Semak. The Mechaber lists those species mentioned in the Mishnah.
