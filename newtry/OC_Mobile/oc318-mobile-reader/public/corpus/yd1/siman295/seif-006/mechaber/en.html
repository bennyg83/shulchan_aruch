There are among trees some similar to one another in leaves or in fruit, and nevertheless since they are two species, these are kilayim with one another. How so? Apple with quince (meaning: a forest apple species); peach (meaning: persica in the vernacular) with oleaster (meaning: he grafted olives on pomegranate, which are fruit similar to olive, and oleaster comes out from them) with pomegranate—even though they are similar to one another, they are kilayim with one another. But pears (in the vernacular) with crustumenian pears (a species of pears), and quince with medlars (sorbele in the vernacular) are not kilayim with one another. {Rama: And likewise it is permitted to graft one species even black on white and thin on thick, even though it has an accompanying name (Kol Bo and Bach in the name of Orach Chaim). And likewise wood on wood is permitted even if not its species (so it appears from the Tur). Nevertheless, since most of the world does not recognize most species, it is good to be careful with all of them (Beit Yosef in the name of Semak).}
