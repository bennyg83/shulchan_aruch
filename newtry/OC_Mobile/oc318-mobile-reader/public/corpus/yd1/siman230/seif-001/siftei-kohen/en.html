They permit, etc. Responsa Maharam Mintz, siman 73, writes: “Nevertheless, whenever a case comes before me to permit a vow for members of the public who are lax regarding vows, I impose upon them a fast on Monday, Thursday, and Monday, or something similar, depending upon the person and the vow. I also require them to give money for a mitzvah, one gold coin or a similar amount according to their wealth, so that they will not treat vows lightly and say, ‘Why should I care if I swear as much as I wish? I will go to a Torah scholar and he will release me.’ I heard this from my teachers”; this concludes his words. It is proper to act accordingly.

Some say, etc. In Choshen Mishpat, siman 208, Rama rules without qualification that his sale is valid. Sma there rules in accordance with the view cited here as “some say,” explaining at length that this case is different. Bach there, however, rules that it is an unresolved legal doubt, and the burden of proof rests upon the person who seeks to extract property. This is the primary view. Responsa Rosh, rule 7, siman 4, also appears to disagree with that opinion. Rosh writes that Rava's statement at the beginning of Temurah, that if one performed the act it is ineffective, applies to commandments whose violation cannot be annulled, like all the cases cited there, such as a rapist who divorced his wife and one who separated inferior produce as terumah for superior produce, etc. Regarding an enactment, a cherem, and the like, which can be annulled, Rava concedes that the act is effective, etc.
