"You shall not wear, etc." Bach wrote that there is permission in two matters: First, that there is no prohibition even in something that is adornment and ornamentation except when a woman wears men's garments to resemble a man, and a man wears women's garments to resemble a woman for adornment and ornamentation; but if they wear in order to protect from sun or cold and the like, it is permitted. Second, that even for resemblance there is prohibition only in things made for adornment and ornamentation, etc. — see there that he elaborated, and his words are not so compelling; nevertheless it appears this is specifically in women's repairs, but if he actually wears women's garments until it is not recognizable that he is a man, and likewise the reverse in every matter — forbidden; and likewise the Rambam in Sefer Yereim, that it is forbidden to wear even casually and in jest, for the verse did not distinguish; and because I saw people wearing women's garments and also women in men's garments casually at wedding feasts of groom and bride, and also in many matters I wrote thus — end of his words; and it implies he speaks in all garments, and deals with the case of Perishah; and Bach strained in his words — see there. And regarding the prohibition of a man wearing a woman's garment on Purim, Bach elaborated here; and see in Orach Chaim siman 696 and what is written there.
