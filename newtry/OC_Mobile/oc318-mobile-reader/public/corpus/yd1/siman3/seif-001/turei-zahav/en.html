**Does not require intention.** For the Merciful One revealed regarding meitatek in kodshim that it is invalid—meaning one who slaughters without intention to slaughter, as it is written “and he shall slaughter the young bull”—until the shechitah is for the sake of the young bull; learn from this that in chullin it is kosher. Nevertheless if it fell without human force we say below it is invalid because slaughtering is at least like “and you shall do” that requires intention for the act, for we require human force. And in the gloss he challenged: why do we not say likewise regarding “and he shall slaughter” that we derive from it the beginning of slaughter that he not make it a handle {gistera}; here too say from what is revealed in kodshim, learn chullin is permitted as a handle, and enter there the strains and tzarich iyun—for Rashi explained on gistera that he not cut the entire neck joint in two, and this is that he need not cut the entire neck joint as will be explained in siman 24; if so it is a leniency that Scripture teaches in kodshim a fortiori in chullin. And although Rabbenu Yitzchak explained it is forbidden to cut the entire neck joint in two, and Rama already wrote the custom is to be stringent—it will be explained there it is only a general stringency and not the truth; and also for Rabbenu Yitzchak there is no difficulty at all—for this is obvious that we require in chullin shechitah like kodshim, and whatever is diminished in kodshim from “and he shall slaughter”—automatically it is not shechitah at all; perforce he disqualifies also in chullin, for in any case shechitah we require; not so what is diminished from “young bull” or other wording of the verse—we may say specifically in kodshim the diminution, but in chullin those wordings are not written and we do not require them at all, for in chullin we require only what is in the scope of “and he shall slaughter”—and this is obvious to every understanding person.
