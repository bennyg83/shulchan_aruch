(Siman 342, Taz s.k. 1) He elaborated greatly with proofs, but all his proofs are uncertain and can be rejected. In particular, what he wrote, “and more difficult than this is what the Ran writes in the first chapter of Ketubot, that although, etc.,” is not difficult at all, for we rule like the Sages, as below in siman 399 seif 13 in the gloss. But what he labored over, he labored in vain, for these laws are explained in Rabbeinu Yerucham, Chelek Chavah, Netiv 22, part 2, as follows: “If he already married but had not yet had relations, and a relative of the groom died on that day after the wedding blessings had already been recited, not his father, there are some explainers who wrote that he observes the days of celebration and afterward seven days of mourning, like one whose dead relative died on a festival, for since the marriage blessing was already recited, the days of celebration have already taken effect upon him. Although when his father died we say that he first has the mitzvah-intercourse, etc., from which it appears that the joy does not take effect until he has relations, there it is different, for the mourning took effect before the marriage, except that everything had been prepared, and this joy is not strong enough to obligate him in the seven days of celebration until he has relations. Therefore he has relations before the seven days of celebration take effect upon him. But here, where he had already married, the joy has already taken effect upon him even without intercourse, and therefore he does not have relations until the seven days of celebration and the seven days of mourning have passed. So wrote the Ri Giat and Rabbi David Kohen in a responsum. But the Rosh wrote and ruled in practice in this case that he should first observe mourning, for joy does not take effect upon him until he has relations, since the deceased is not his father but another relative.” End quote from Rabbeinu Yerucham. Where there is no loss, or there is someone else to trouble himself with the preparations, it is obvious that the law of his father and mother is like that of other relatives, as implied by the Gemara and poskim. Rabbeinu Yerucham further wrote there that regarding his father and mother, when they are not fit to trouble themselves, he observes mourning first, even where there is loss; end quote. It appears that in our times, even if his father or mother died, they have the law of other relatives, for it is common that other relatives trouble themselves and prepare the needs of the meal and the bride’s adornments. Therefore in every case he observes mourning first, and does not have relations until the seven days of mourning pass, and afterward he observes the seven days of celebration. So it appears to me.
