Inclined. Bach writes that this applies specifically where the sick person is approaching death. If he is not approaching death, we do not tell him to confess. Although we tell him that many who walk in the marketplace have confessed and recovered, if we also say that this is the practice of all those condemned to die, he will understand that he is dangerously ill, for otherwise they would not mention death before him, and his heart may break. Shach notes that Bach appears to understand that the phrase about all who are dying confessing is actually said to the patient. Perishah understands it merely as Tur's explanation and not part of what is said. Accordingly, even where he is approaching death one does not say that phrase to him. Nevertheless, where he is not approaching death we do not tell him to confess, because he may infer that he is dangerously ill. Moreover, there is still time for him to confess later. When he is approaching death, however, we fear he may die suddenly without confession.
