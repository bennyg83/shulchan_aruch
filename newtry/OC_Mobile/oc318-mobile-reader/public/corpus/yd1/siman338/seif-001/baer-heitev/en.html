Inclined. The Bach wrote: it implies specifically when he inclined toward death, but if he did not incline toward death, we do not tell him to confess. Although they tell him, "many walk in the marketplace," etc., nevertheless when they tell him that such is the way of all who are executed, that they confess, he will know that he is in danger, for otherwise they would not mention his death before him, and his heart will break; end quote. From his words it appears that this language is said to the sick person, "for such is the way," etc. But the Perishah wrote that "for such is the way of all who are executed, that they confess" is the language of the Tur, not what they say to the sick person. If so, even when he inclined toward death they do not say this to him. Nevertheless, it appears that if he did not incline toward death, one cannot tell him to confess, for he will think he is in danger. Furthermore, he still has time in which he can confess; but when he inclined toward death, one must be concerned that he may die suddenly without confession; end quote, Shach.
