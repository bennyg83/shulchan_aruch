They tell him: confess, for such is the way of all who are put to death, that they confess. This is the Tur and the Talmud, Shabbat 32a. The Bach wrote that this implies specifically where he is near death; but if he is not near death, they do not tell him to confess, so that his heart not be broken. Although they say to him that many walk in the marketplace and confess, nevertheless when they tell a person, "confess, for such is the way of all who are put to death, that they confess," he will know that he is very dangerously ill, for otherwise they would not mention his death in front of him, and his heart will be broken. Therefore they say this only when he is near death, etc. From his words it appears that they say to him, "confess, for such is the way of all who are put to death, that they confess," and for that reason they say it only when he is near death. But the Perishah wrote that the phrase "for such is the way of all who are put to death, that they confess" is the Tur's wording, not what they say to him. If so, even when he is near death they do not say this to him. Although the Bach then brought Rashi's language, that those standing there say, "for such is the way of all who are put to death, that they confess," as we learned in Sanhedrin, etc., Rashi does not mean that those standing there say "all who are put to death confess." If so, how could he write "as we learned in Sanhedrin," etc.? Rather, the phrase "for such is the way of all who are put to death," etc., is a separate comment. At first he explained who the "they say" are, namely those standing there; afterward he explained "for such is the way of all who are put to death," etc., from the language "for such is the way of all who are put to death," as we learned in Sanhedrin, etc. Nevertheless, it appears that specifically when he is near death they tell him to confess, for otherwise he will think he is dangerously ill and his heart will break. Alternatively, when he is not near death they do not say this because he still has time to confess; but when he is near death, there is concern he may die suddenly without confession.
