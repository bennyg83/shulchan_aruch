His teacher's lost object. Meaning, both lost objects are before him and he can return only one. See Choshen Mishpat, beginning of siman 264.
His primary teacher. If the teacher is not primary, his father's lost object takes precedence even if his father is not a Torah scholar at all. Beit Yosef; this is obvious.
Equal, etc. But if his father is not equal in wisdom to his teacher, even though his father is also a Torah scholar, his teacher's lost object takes precedence.
He redeems his father, etc. See above, siman 251, end of seif 9, and siman 252, seif 9.
Likewise, he unloads, etc. The Beit Yosef is uncertain regarding unloading even where his father equals his teacher, since there is no danger to life or monetary loss. This implies that where his father is not equal, it is obvious to him that the teacher takes precedence. The Rosh's words do not decisively resolve the matter.
But if his father hires a teacher for him, etc. Sefer Chasidim writes that a teacher's lost object precedes the father's only where the father did not hire the teacher. If the father paid the teacher, and the teacher would not have taught without payment, the father's lost object takes precedence. If another Jew or group of Jews paid the teacher to teach him, the lost object of the payer takes precedence.
