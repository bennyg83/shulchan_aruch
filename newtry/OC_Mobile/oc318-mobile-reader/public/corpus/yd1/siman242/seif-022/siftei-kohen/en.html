He says to him. Respectfully: 'Our teacher taught me such and such.'
He must protest. This is because he is certain that his teacher is about to violate a prohibition. Terumat HaDeshen suggests that even where he is uncertain he should protest. The rule that regarding a rabbinic prohibition one first acts and afterward raises the objection applies where the teacher is issuing a practical ruling and is presumed to have considered it properly. It differs from seeing one's teacher personally about to perform a prohibited act. This appears primary, though the Rama follows Terumat HaDeshen's conclusion not to draw the distinction too sharply.
