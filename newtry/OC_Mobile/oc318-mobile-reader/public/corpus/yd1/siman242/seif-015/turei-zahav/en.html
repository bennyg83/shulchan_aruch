If it is a wondrous name. Rambam Hilchot Talmud Torah; Tur copied in this language: forbidden for a student to call his rav by name when the name is wondrous so all who hear know whom he means; even not in his presence; he shall not mention his name in his presence; even to call others whose name is like his rav's name is forbidden unless he changes their name, even after death; end. Kesef Mishneh challenged this against Rambam Hilchot Mamrim that Tur and Shulchan Aruch siman 200 copied; "he shall not call him by his name" etc.; there it implies specifically to others it is permitted when the name is not wondrous, but to his father forbidden; and what is permitted to others is specifically not in his presence, but in his presence not; here it implies even to his rav permitted in his presence, all the more to others. Further difficulty: wondrous name seems there forbidden even not in his presence, for he wrote if the name is not wondrous he calls others not in his presence; if wondrous, even not in his presence forbidden; here he wrote "he shall not mention his name in his presence"; end. What he wrote that here it implies even to his rav in his presence permitted without wondrous name is not proven at all; one may say that he required specifically wondrous follows what comes after, i.e. even not in his presence; but in his presence we do not require wondrous and it is forbidden in every manner. Also the second difficulty; wondrous name seems above forbidden even not in his presence even to others, and here "he shall not mention his name in his presence"; I do not know the difficulty; perhaps "and he shall not mention" refers to in his presence when forbidden even without wondrous. In Prishah he answered the second difficulty; Tur did not have in Rambam's words there at the end "he calls others not in his presence," for Tur did not mention it there; therefore he wrote Rambam holds above there is no prohibition with others' names except when wondrous and also in his presence; but one factor for stringency; in his presence; and one for leniency; not wondrous; or the reverse: wondrous and not in his presence permitted, and forbidden not in his presence with wondrous only regarding calling his rav; end; I wonder at the Mechaber who did not notice the core difficulty; also Kesef Mishnah; why not challenge briefly that here it implies even in calling his rav there is at least permission if not wondrous, for regarding calling his rav he wrote "when it must be wondrous"; without wondrous permitted at least not in his presence; there he wrote forbidden to call his father by name in life and after death, i.e. not in his presence, and this deals when not wondrous for he does not distinguish afterward regarding wondrous name except in calling others; and this is simple in plain halachah: a sage who expounds mishnah changes his father's name and says "my father, my teacher"; certainly absolute prohibition to call father or rav by name not in his presence even when not wondrous. Maharshal also came to answer what conflicted regarding calling others per his way, but regarding calling himself there is issur even not in his presence as he himself wrote; the difficulty I mentioned remains. Therefore in my humble opinion Rambam's words here are reversed and must be so: forbidden to call his rav by name; he shall not mention his name in his presence; even to call others whose name is like his rav's forbidden; when the name is wondrous etc., as Shulchan Aruch wrote here; so Ran first chapter Kiddushin in Rambam's name; specifically if others' name is like his rav's it is forbidden when wondrous; implying regarding his rav himself no distinction even not in his presence; see there. Rambam's words are aligned here with the earlier passage: Rambam says he shall not mention his name in his presence etc.; meaning even if he does not call him by it and even if not wondrous; even to call others forbidden if wondrous; then forbidden even not in his presence, even after father's or rav's death, as Tur wrote here even after their death. Prishah explained his intent to say of others; error; his intent is to explain what he wrote that he should not change their names, i.e. of others; but after their death certainly refers to father and rav mentioned there in Rambam; this is simple. All I wrote seems clear and correct in this.
