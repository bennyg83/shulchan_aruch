Unless the teacher accords him honor, etc. Even if the teacher does not personally accord honor to his student, if the teacher is honored when others honor that student, Rabbeinu Simchah ruled that the student may be honored in his teacher’s presence. We find that honor is accorded to the deputy because of the honor of the Kohen Gadol. So states the Mordechai at the beginning of Kiddushin. Tosafos in Shevuos 36 wrote that the positive commandment to honor Torah may perhaps not apply to the wife of a Torah scholar after his death. Maharam Mintz, however, wrote that if the wife of a Torah scholar marries an ignoramus, one is no longer obligated to honor her as before. This implies that while she remains the widow of a Torah scholar, one is obligated to honor her as before. I also found the following in the words of my revered father-in-law, of blessed memory: When a person’s teacher is mentioned before him as though he were merely his colleague, he need not object and say, “He is not my colleague but my teacher.” The proof is from the beginning of Beitzah, where they said to R’ Eliezer, “Your colleagues have already voted concerning you.” The Gemara asks, “Who are your colleagues?” and answers, “R’ Yochanan ben Zakkai,” who was his teacher. I further found that when one writes ordinarily to a great person, or speaks with him repeatedly, he need not say “Master” every single time. If he sometimes addresses him as “you,” there is no concern. A colleague-student is not required to address his teacher as “Master” while speaking with him, as stated in the chapter Tefillas HaShachar concerning R’ Yirmiyah bar Abba, who was a colleague-student of Rav. Thus, R’ Yirmiyah said to Rav, “Did you separate?” and did not say, “Did the Master separate?”
