He does not greet his rabbi nor return greeting like other people; rather bows before him and says with awe and honor, "Peace upon you, my rabbi"; and if his rabbi greeted him, he says, "Peace upon you, my teacher and rabbi": {Rama: (And so is the custom; and some say a student should not ask peace of his rabbi at all, as it says, "I saw youths and hid") (Yerushalmi, brought in Hagahot Maimoniyot chapter 5, Teshuvat Or Zaru'a netiv 2, Beit Yosef in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah).} He does not uncover his head before his rabbi, nor sit before him, but sits as one sits before a king: {Rama: (If his rabbi sat at a feast with others, he takes permission from his rabbi and then from others) (Rokeach siman 335).} He does not pray before him, nor behind him, nor at his side; and needless to say he may not walk at his side — rather distances behind his rabbi and should not be aligned behind his back but turns aside, whether praying with him or walking with him; and beyond four cubits all is permitted (and see Orach Chaim siman 94).} He does not enter the bathhouse with him unless he needs him: {Rama: (If the student was in the bathhouse before his rabbi and his rabbi came — he need not leave (Mahariv in laws); and all this only where they go naked in the bathhouse; but where they go in pants it is permitted (Agudah, chapter Makom Shenahagu); and the custom is widespread to enter with rabbi, father, father-in-law, mother's husband, and sister's husband to the bathhouse, even though the Gemara forbade all — all because now they go in pants).} He does not sit before him until he says "sit," nor stand until he says "stand" or takes permission to stand; and when taking leave he does not turn his back but backs away with his face toward his rabbi's face: {Rama: One taking leave of his rabbi who took permission and lodged in the city must return and take permission again (Gemara, first chapter of Moed Katan, Rabbeinu Yerucham); and specifically if he did not say at first he wishes to lodge in the city; but if he told him when taking permission, he need not return (Ran there).} He does not sit in his place, nor contradict his words, nor lean his words before him; and he is obligated to stand before him from when he sees him from afar while facing him until hidden from him so he not see his stature; and afterward he sits; and even if riding he must stand before him, for riding is considered walking: {Rama: Some say one is obligated to stand before his rabbi only morning and evening (Tur in the name of Rambam); and specifically in the rabbi's house; but before others who do not know he stood for his rabbi, he is obligated to stand (Beit Yosef in the name of Tosafot, Mordechai in the name of R' Yehudah, and see below).}
