And one who takes leave of his teacher and receives permission, etc. At first glance, the source of this ruling appears unclear. It is based on the Ran’s explanation of the Rif at the beginning of Moed Katan. Scripture states, “On the eighth day he sent the people away.” The Ran explains that the eighth day of the festival was the 22nd of the month, and they received Solomon’s permission because they intended to depart on the 23rd. Scripture then states that on the 23rd of the seventh month he sent the people away. From here we learn that a student who takes leave of his teacher but then remains overnight in the same city must take leave a second time. The Ran writes that because they remained there that day and lodged in the city, they needed to obtain permission again for their departure on the 24th. However, on the 23rd, although they had lodged the night after the 22nd, they did not need to obtain permission again, because when they received permission they intended to depart the next day, the 23rd. There is plainly a scribal error in the Rif, for the verse and Gemara state the 23rd, not the 24th. Nevertheless, the Ran’s words are correct. His difficulty was why they needed permission twice. The permission on the 22nd was for departure on the 23rd, since they could not depart on the 22nd, which was Yom Tov. They later changed their minds on the 23rd and decided to depart on the 24th, and therefore needed permission again on the 23rd for the journey of the 24th. Thus, the Gemara correctly derives that one who remains overnight in the city must take leave again. Yet the lodging after the 22nd did not itself require new permission, since they had originally intended to depart on the 23rd. Only when they decided on the 23rd to remain another night did they require permission again for the 24th. Everything is therefore properly resolved, with God’s help.
