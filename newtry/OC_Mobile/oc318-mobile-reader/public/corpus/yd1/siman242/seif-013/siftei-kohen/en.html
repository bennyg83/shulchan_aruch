When he has drunk wine. The Rambam writes that this refers specifically to drinking one reviit of undiluted wine. If the wine contained any water, or he slept briefly, or walked a mil, the effect has passed and he may rule. But if he drank more than a reviit, even diluted wine, brief sleep or travel increases his intoxication; he must wait according to the degree of intoxication until no trace remains. The Bach writes that there is no distinction between walking and riding, but the Gemara distinguishes riding, which dissipates intoxication only after three mil. Possibly he follows the Gemara's first answer, though even there riding three mil after more than a reviit does not increase intoxication. The Semag states this explicitly. The Bach's statement must therefore refer only to one reviit, though his omission of the Gemara's conclusion requires study. The Rashba writes that on Yom Tov or at a large feast, such as a bris, wedding, or Purim meal, where much wine is consumed, one should not rule for the rest of the day until the next morning. If he had already resolved the ruling before the meal, he may state it afterward, whether to forbid or permit. The Beit Yosef questions this and holds that once he senses the wine has passed he may rule. Yet the Rashba proves his view from the Gemara, and Rabbeinu Peretz and the Bach agree. They likely speak of the ordinary case; if he clearly knows that the wine has entirely passed, he may rule. The Rambam also writes that an intoxicated person may study Torah, even halachot and midrashim, provided he does not issue rulings. A sage regularly appointed to rule should not study, because his study itself is treated as ruling.
Or other intoxicating substances, etc. Even if he ate dates or drank milk and his mind became somewhat confused, he should not rule. Rambam there.
Unless it is a matter explicit in the verses, etc. The Rambam and Terumat HaDeshen indicate that even a matter clear among the poskim may not be ruled upon while intoxicated, unless it is explicit in Scripture and even the Sadducees acknowledge it. The Bach writes likewise. I therefore wonder at the Rama's permission regarding a matter clear among the poskim. Maharik, whom the Darkei Moshe cites, did not state this as a settled ruling, and he is a lone lenient opinion against the Rambam and Terumat HaDeshen. Keritot 13b likewise indicates that even according to Rabbi Yosi son of Rabbi Yehudah, only study is permitted, whereas ruling is forbidden unless the matter is explicit in Scripture. Possibly 'clear among the poskim' is a scribal error and should read 'clear in the verses.' The Bach further writes that one who is distressed and unsettled should not rule while distressed. As a pious practice, every halachic authority should avoid ruling in the conditions mentioned in Eruvin, such as while angry, immediately after travel on foot, or in a house containing intoxicating drink or small fish brine, though these are not strict prohibitions.
