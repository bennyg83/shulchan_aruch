Unless it is a clear matter among poskim etc. From Rambam and Tur earlier it appears clear that even if it is clear among poskim, as long as it is not explicit in Scripture that the Sadducees concede, it is forbidden to rule — so too Bach; I wonder at the rav who permits a clear matter among poskim though he follows Mahariik whom Darkei Moshe cites — nevertheless he is alone against Rambam and Tur; he also is lenient; one who examines Mahariik's words will see he did not write so for truth of the matter but wrote: I had thought that regarding what they said "one who is intoxicated shall not rule" Semak MeTzurach disagrees — however now Semak MeTzurach is not before me etc.; so too appears in Keritot (daf 13b) that even for R' Yosi b'R' Yehuda it is permitted only to study Gemara, but to rule — even in a clear matter, as long as it is not explicit in Scripture, is forbidden — see there in Rashi; and perhaps what the rav wrote "clear among poskim" is a scribal error and must read "in verses." Bach: one who is distressed and his mind is unsettled shall not rule when he is distressed, as written "in distress he shall not rule"; so too Tosafot per Yerushalmi; it is proper conduct for every posek to be careful not to rule in all those cases in chapter Hador — such as on Yom Kippur when he is heated, or coming from the road with dust on his feet, or a house that has beer, or resin — but there is no prohibition even lechatchila until here.
