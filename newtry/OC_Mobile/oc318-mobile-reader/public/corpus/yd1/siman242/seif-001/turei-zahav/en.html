More than for his father. The Tur concluded that his father brings him to the life of this world and his teacher brings him to the life of the World to Come, and there is no honor greater than the honor of one's teacher and no awe like awe of him. The Sages said: "The awe of your teacher is like the awe of Heaven." The Perishah asked: if so, what does "more than his father" mean? For regarding his father too, the Tur wrote at the beginning of siman 240 that his honor and awe are like the honor and awe of God! He answered that every analogy is not fully equal to its source; but "the awe of your teacher is like the awe of Heaven" was derived from "es Hashem Elokecha tira" — to include one's teacher. Since both types of awe are written in one verse, it tells us they are completely equal. Thus far his words. But this is not compelling. For certainly whatever is analogized to something else has no distinction at all from the source. The Tur did not intend here to prove that the awe of one's teacher is greater than the awe of one's father — for that was already proven by the fact that the teacher brings him to the life of the World to Come. Rather, the difficulty here was regarding the statement that there is no awe like the awe of one's teacher — but the opposite appears to be true, since the awe of one's father was analogized to the awe of God! This would undermine the preeminence already stated regarding the teacher. He answered that regarding the teacher too we find this, as it says "the awe of your teacher," etc. Thus the preeminence of the teacher remains intact, based on what was already stated — that he brings him to the life of the World to Come.
