His father, etc. The Darkei Moshe writes: From the ruling that one's teacher takes precedence, one may learn that if his father is also his teacher, he should call him 'Rabbi' rather than 'my father and master.' This is also indicated at the beginning of the chapter HaZahav, where Rabbi Shimon called Rabbi, his father, 'Rabbi.' It appears to me that this applies only where his father is his primary teacher. This requires study, since the practice is not so. Rather, although one must honor his teacher more than his father, it is more fitting to call him by the title 'father,' which has been unique to him since childhood. Presumably the father also waives the honor due him as a teacher in this regard, as explained in Orach Chaim, siman 472, seif 5: a son reclining in his father's presence must recline even if his father is his primary teacher, although a student need not recline in his teacher's presence, because the father is presumed to waive his honor as a teacher. The same applies here. Rabbi was exceptional, for the Sages said that from the days of Moshe until Rabbi, Torah and greatness were not found together in one person.
