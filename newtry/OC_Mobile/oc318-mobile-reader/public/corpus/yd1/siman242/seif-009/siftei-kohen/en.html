Some write, etc. The Hagahot Derishah questions this from Eruvin 62b, where Rabbi Yaakov bar Abba asked about ruling in one's teacher's locale even concerning Megillat Taanit, a written and established text. Rav Yosef replied that Rav Chisda asked him even about eggs with kutach throughout Rav Huna's lifetime and did not rule. Rashi explains that no halachic work was written in their days except Megillat Taanit. This implies that even matters written in a book may not be ruled in one's teacher's presence, and even by a colleague-student. One answer is that only directly in his presence is forbidden, whereas outside his presence it is permitted. Another is that Megillat Taanit involved a matter novel to the questioner, whereas Maharam permits a written ruling only where it appears non-novel. Yet this cannot be correct, for Maharam explicitly discusses a matter novel to the questioner and permits reliance on an explicit ruling in the works of the Geonim. The first answer is also difficult, because the Gemara says 'in his locale,' which indicates even outside his presence. It therefore appears that Maharam understood the case of Megillat Taanit as requiring comparison of one case to another, rather than merely reading an explicit ruling.
