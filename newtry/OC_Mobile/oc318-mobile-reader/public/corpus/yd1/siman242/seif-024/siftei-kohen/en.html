He should not say, etc. Listeners ordinarily assume that a student's anonymous teaching was heard from his teacher. If he did not hear it from his teacher, he must identify from whom he heard it. A teaching heard from his teacher may be stated anonymously. The Bach writes that this applies only where it is known that he studied under no other teacher. If he studied under two, he must name the source. Nowadays, however, people generally assume that an anonymous statement is the speaker's own, so the practical rule changes. Nevertheless, it is certainly forbidden to present anonymously, and all the more so in one's own name, something heard from others, for that is adorning oneself with a garment that is not one's own.
