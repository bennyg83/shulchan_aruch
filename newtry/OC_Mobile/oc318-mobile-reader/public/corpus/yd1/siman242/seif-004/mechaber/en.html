It is forbidden for a person to rule before his rabbi forever; and whoever rules before him is liable for death: {Rama: And even taking permission does not help within three parasangs if he is an outstanding rabbi (Beit Yosef in the name of Tosafot, first chapter of Sanhedrin, Maharik shoresh 170, Agudah there, Semak end of positive mitzvot 13 in the name of R' Yehudah, chapter Af Al Pi).} And if he is far from his rabbi twelve mil and someone asked him a halachic matter incidentally, he may answer; but to establish himself to rule and sit and rule — even at the end of the world it is forbidden to rule until his rabbi dies or gives him permission: {Rama: All this is for an outstanding rabbi; but for a student-colleague, even within three parasangs it is permitted (Rif and Rambam as in Beit Yosef); and some say nevertheless directly before his rabbi it is forbidden (Rivash siman 271); and even not directly before him, if they began with honor of the rabbi to say they should ask the rabbi, or the rabbi is greatly advanced in wisdom and age — one may not rule in his city (Beit Yosef from Gemara, chapter HaDar).} And some say a full student within twelve mil is liable for death if he rules outside twelve mil he is exempt but forbidden: {Rama: (And some say specifically when the rabbi regularly comes to the student's city; but if he does not regularly come, only incidentally on the road, it is permitted however far beyond three parasangs (Mordechai in the name of Ribba).} A student-colleague within twelve mil is exempt but forbidden, and beyond twelve mil permitted — even though he took permission from one rabbi it does not suffice until he takes permission from all his outstanding rabbis: {Rama: "Outstanding rabbis" here does not mean like other outstanding rabbi that most of his wisdom is from him — for then he could not have many outstanding rabbis; rather it means a full student as opposed to student-colleague — one who grew in Torah and became a colleague to his rabbi, nearly as great as his rabbi (Maharik shoresh 170, Beit Yosef in the name of Rashbam, chapter Yesh Nochalin); nevertheless some disagree and hold if he took permission from one rabbi it suffices to rule beyond three parasangs (Teshuvat Rashba siman 111, Rivash siman 281); but within three parasangs it does not help him; and some say whoever is not outstanding rabbi — that is, most of his wisdom is not from him — is a student-colleague (Beit Yosef in the name of Rambam).}
