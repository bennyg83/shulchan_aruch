And if, etc. For in Sanhedrin 5b it is stated: "and it is taught — a student should not rule, etc." And there, Rav and Rav bar bar Chana could not rule without permission. Rambam explains this refers to a permanent position, as in the case of Rav and Rav bar bar Chana; and regarding this it was said "at that time they decreed, etc." The incident of Tanhum was incidental, as stated there "he happened to come," etc. — therefore they challenged him from the baraita "and it is taught, etc." And he said "until he dies" as stated in Eruvin there — "I did not rule in the years of," etc. And Tosafot in Sanhedrin there, s.v. "ela," also raised Rambam's difficulty from the beginning of chapter 6 of Eruvin, that Rav Hamnuna did not rule in Charta d'Argiz during the years of Rav Huna because he was his primary teacher and Rav Huna was more than three parasangs away; and similarly from Ravina who ruled on a knife in Bavel and a scholar said to him "Am I not a scholar?" — otherwise it would be forbidden — and Bavel was far from Rav Ashi; and also from the case of the student of Rabbi Eliezer who was liable for death [when ruling] beyond three parasangs — implying that for the prohibition it is no issue. Tosafot in Sanhedrin there answered that within three parasangs, even with permission it does not help. [And this is what is stated in the gloss: "and even taking permission, etc."]. And in Ketubot 60b, s.v. "ki," they wrote it does help, only the matter did not succeed for him — and perhaps because Rav Yosef was not his primary teacher, for Rabbah was his primary teacher. But in Eruvin there they answered, s.v. "Rav Chisda," that within three parasangs it is considered as if in his presence, and regarding this they said: in his presence it is forbidden and liable for death — therefore they did not raise the difficulty from the student of Rabbi Eliezer, only because he was beyond three parasangs. And what is stated "not in his presence it is forbidden and not liable for death" refers to beyond three parasangs; and a student-colleague within three parasangs is forbidden. And this is what is stated in Sanhedrin: "and it is taught — a student should not rule unless he is far away, etc." — and beyond three parasangs it is permitted, as stated above and in Eruvin there. Nevertheless, even within three parasangs, for a Torah scholar he is exempt from death, for we derive death from Moses who had a primary teacher — see Rosh there. And this is what the Shulchan Aruch states: "and some say a complete student, etc., a Torah scholar, etc." But Rashba siman 111 rejected their words, for in Sanhedrin "and it is taught — a student, etc." it implies any student, even a complete student. And so Tosafot themselves raised it — for we derive from Moses as stated above — only beyond three parasangs it is permitted by strict law. And what Eruvin states "in his presence" means literally in his presence; "not in his presence" refers to within three parasangs. And Tosafot's difficulty is not compelling, for it states "far" for emphasis, yet nevertheless due to a decree it is forbidden even beyond three parasangs, as stated there regarding Rav and Rav bar bar Chana. And this is what is stated there: "and since he had learned, why did he need permission" — implying that strictly he was permitted even though they were complete students. The practical difference is: within three parasangs, where it is forbidden strictly, taking permission from another rabbi does not help; but beyond three parasangs, where it is only due to a decree, one rabbi suffices. So wrote Ra'avad and Rivash in their name in siman 271, who wrote that a Torah scholar is permitted even within three parasangs — and this is necessarily his position, for in Eruvin it speaks strictly, and Rav Huna did not rule in Charta, etc. — and that must have been within three parasangs, yet he acknowledged in the years of Rav Chisda. And this is what Rashi wrote in Sanhedrin 23a, s.v. "bei dina," that Rav Chisda was a Torah scholar as stated in Eruvin, and Rav Chisda ruled, etc. And Tosafot in Sanhedrin there, s.v. "k'gon," disagreed with Rashi according to their own approach. And this is what is stated in the gloss: "and all of this regarding his teacher, etc." — for even according to Rambam it is so, as he explains "in his presence" to mean literally in his presence, as written.
