And if, etc. In Sanhedrin 5b we say, and a Baraisa teaches, "A student should not issue a ruling," etc. Earlier there, Rav and Rabbah bar bar Chanah were unable to issue rulings without authorization. Rambam explains that those cases concern issuing rulings on a regular basis, as in the incidents of Rav and Rabbah bar bar Chanah, and it was about that situation that the Sages said, "At that time they decreed," etc. The incident involving Tanchum, however, was an occasional ruling, as indicated by the statement there that he happened to arrive, etc.; therefore the Gemara challenged him from the Baraisa, "A student should not issue a ruling," etc. The prohibition continues until the teacher dies, as stated in Eruvin there regarding one who did not issue rulings during the lifetime of his teacher. Tosafos in Sanhedrin there, s.v. "rather," also raises Rambam's difficulty from the beginning of chapter 6 of Eruvin. Rav Hamnuna did not issue rulings in Charta D'Argiz during Rav Huna's lifetime because Rav Huna was his primary teacher, even though Rav Huna was more than three parsahs away. Likewise, Ravina inspected a slaughtering knife in Babylonia and justified himself by saying, "I am a Torah scholar"; otherwise it would have been forbidden, even though Babylonia was very far from R' Ashi. The Gemara also challenges from the student of R' Eliezer who incurred death even though he was outside three parsahs, implying that the Gemara did not find the ordinary prohibition difficult there. Tosafos in Sanhedrin answers that within three parsahs even authorization does not help. This is what the Rama means by "even receiving authorization," etc. In Kesubos 60b, however, Tosafos writes that authorization does help, only that the matter will not meet with success. Perhaps that case is different because Rav Yosef was not his primary teacher, since Rabbah was his primary teacher. In Eruvin, Tosafos, s.v. "Rav Chisda," explains that the entire area within three parsahs is considered "in the teacher's presence." Thus when the Gemara says that in his presence it is forbidden and incurs death, it refers to within three parsahs. That is why the Gemara's difficulty from R' Eliezer's student arose only because the student was three parsahs distant. The statement that outside the teacher's presence it is forbidden but does not incur death refers to beyond three parsahs. A student-colleague is prohibited within three parsahs. This is also the meaning of the Baraisa in Sanhedrin, "A student should not issue a ruling unless he is distant," etc.; beyond three parsahs it is permitted, as explained above and in Eruvin. Even so, a student-colleague within three parsahs is not subject to death, because the death penalty is learned from Moshe, who was the person's primary teacher. See the Rosh there. This is the basis of the Mechaber's wording: "Some say that a full student," etc., and "a student-colleague," etc. Rashba, responsum 111, however, rejects their approach. In Sanhedrin the Baraisa says simply "a student," which implies even a full student, and Tosafos themselves ask that the rule is derived from Moshe, as explained above. Rather, outside three parsahs a student is permitted by strict law. When Eruvin says "in his presence," it means literally in his presence, while "not in his presence" means within three parsahs. Tosafos's difficulty is not compelling, because the Gemara stated the case of three parsahs to teach the greater novelty: even at that distance it was forbidden because of the decree, as in the incidents involving Rav and Rabbah bar bar Chanah. The statement there, "Since he had learned, why did he require authorization?" implies that by strict law he would have been permitted, even though Rav and Rabbah bar bar Chanah were full students. The practical difference is that within three parsahs, where ruling is forbidden by strict law, authorization from another teacher does not help. Beyond three parsahs, where the prohibition is only because of the decree, authorization from one teacher does help. Raavad writes likewise, and Rivash, siman 271, cites this in their names. Rivash further writes that a student-colleague is entirely permitted even within three parsahs. This is compelled by his approach, because the discussion in Eruvin concerns the strict law, and there it says that Rav Huna did not rule in Charta, etc.; necessarily that was within three parsahs, yet after Rav Chisda's death he did rule there. Rashi in Sanhedrin 23a, s.v. "beit din," likewise writes that Rav Chisda was a student-colleague, as stated in Eruvin, and that Rav Chisda issued rulings, etc. Tosafos there, s.v. "for example," disagrees with Rashi in accordance with their own approach. This is what the Rama means by "all this applies to one's teacher," etc. Rambam also agrees to this distinction, for he interprets "in his presence" literally, as indicated by his statement that whoever issues a ruling, etc.
