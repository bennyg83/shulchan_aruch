Because of disgrace. Beit Yosef wrote, and it appears for this reason they said in the gemara: "if she said Joshua son of Nun; I would not heed him" and they did not say "if she said Moshe"; end of his words. It appears he explains the disgrace is to Moshe Rabbeinu peace upon him; she says "if there were another man like him," which is impossible, for it is written there did not arise like Moshe. And in the book of Rashash on chapter HaChovel siman 53 he brings: "and it is written in responsa; one who quarrels with his fellow and says 'I do not accept from you even if you were Moshe Rabbeinu'; they flog him for disgrace"; end of his words. And this innovation is astounding to me, for the language's habit is not so audacious toward Moshe Rabbeinu; and further in general she did not intend insult but exaggerated in her qualities that she does not want to accept from him pardon or reconciliation; therefore from one such case we should not derive rules; one should be stringent with fasting on Monday and Thursday and two fasts and guard his tongue; end of his words. And according to his words, if she says like Joshua they also flog her; what is the difference? But it appears more correct as stated in Beit Yosef's name, for he brings proof from gemara, and so in Yevamot (48): "if he were Joshua son of Nun I would not give him my daughter":
But we do not heed him. At first glance these words have no explanation. And Levush wrote as follows: and this is correct; we heed him regarding others and we do not heed him regarding himself, for we are concerned lest he mislead us and liken things that are not similar to each other, and a person does not see fault in himself; but if it is a simple matter we heed even regarding himself; end of his words. And this is not correct, for at the beginning it also deals with himself; for regarding another he is believed in every manner to say he accepted this law, for he has no stake in the matter; and so explicitly Nimukey Yosef in chapter HaAreil. And it appears to me to explain Rama's words: since he says that what helps if he stated a reasonable matter is without any semichah upon him, but only that it appears from himself to say so; therefore he said "but we do not heed him," meaning we do not rely on that sage's reasoning here where he likens one matter to another, lest he err in this comparison, unless the comparison is simple in everyone's eyes; then we rely on the comparison and not on the teacher. And do not challenge: if so it is obvious we rely on this; one may say it teaches us we are not concerned lest he show favor in the matter when in truth it is not so. And in the gemara it states in chapter HaAreil: if one states a tradition in a sage's name and he is still alive, even at the time of the act we heed him; there it states "behold Shmuel lives and his court stands"; and Nimukey Yosef explained that when he is not alive we say he erred to state for his sake thus and thinks the sage said so; but when he is alive he examines well.
