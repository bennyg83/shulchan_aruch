Like Moshe. The Taz wrote: but if he said "like Yehoshua," they do not administer lashes, for we find in the Talmud in Yevamot, "even if he were like Yehoshua bin Nun, I would not give him my daughter"; end quote.
To him. The Taz wrote: at first glance these words have no explanation, for in the first clause it also speaks of him. It appears to explain that this is what he means: the case where it helps if he says a matter by reasoning is when he does not rely on him at all, but it appears on its own that this should be said. Therefore he says, "but we do not listen to him," meaning to rely on that sage's reasoning when he compares one matter to another, for perhaps he errs in this, unless the comparison is obvious to everyone; then we rely on the comparison and not on the decisor. One should not say: if so, what does he teach us? One may answer that it was necessary to say that we are not concerned lest he is merely giving an appearance of reason and in truth it is not so; end quote.
Plain. Meaning in the Talmud or in one posek. In the Talmud it is stated that if he said a teaching in the name of a sage who is still alive, even at the time of action we listen to him. Nimukei Yosef explained that when he is not alive, we say perhaps he erred and thinks that this is what he says; but when he is alive, he is precise. It is surprising that Rama did not bring this law. Taz.
