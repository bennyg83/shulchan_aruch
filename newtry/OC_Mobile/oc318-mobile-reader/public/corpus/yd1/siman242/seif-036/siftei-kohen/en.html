But if it is simple in Gemara or in one posek there. Pilpul on conduct of rulings in issur and heter by the rav the author, may his merit shield us. The rav wrote in Choshen Mishpat siman 25: wherever there is doubt in ruling issur and heter and it is d'oraisa, go stringently; if d'rabbanan, follow the lenient — specifically if the two disputants are equal; but one does not rely on a lesser against a greater in wisdom and number even in time of distress unless there was also great loss; and if one against many, follow the many in every case — end; he wrote thus per Rashba responsum 253 that Darkei Moshe cited; Bach wondered at him on "even in distress" unless there was also great loss etc. — I do not know what difficulty he has, for Rashba explicitly states there that specifically in distress with great loss it is permitted; moreover Bach himself concludes we hold like Rashba and not like Tosafot who permit even in other distress without great loss — end; certainly the rav's words are simple: one does not rely on him even in distress where there is no great loss unless there was also great loss; when there is great loss it is permitted, i.e. time of distress. Further Bach elaborated many refinements to prove Rashba did not permit in distress with great loss except one against many, but not lesser against greater in wisdom and number — unlike the rav; he took Rashba's words out of context; anyone who examines will judge the rav's words are clear and need not elaborate on simple matters. Also what he wrote: in one against many we find in several places Gemara ruled like one against many, but lesser against greater we do not find — this is not so, for we also find in several places Gemara ruled halachah like lesser against greater, as Resh Lakish in those three against R' Yochanan as in chapter HaCholeitz (Yevamot daf 36) and in Asheri first chapter of Bava Kamma he counts too several where we hold halachah like Resh Lakish against R' Yochanan — see there; we hold Rav and R' Yochanan — halachah like R' Yochanan even though Rav was greater than R' Yochanan, as in Elu Treifot (daf 54a) — R' Yochanan said: all those years I served R' in the yeshiva I served him standing — and who is the man? A man among all of them; chapter 3 (daf 95b) — all Rav's years R' Yochanan wrote to him "greet our teacher in Babylonia"; when he died he wrote to Shmuel "greet our colleague in Babylonia" — he said: he did not know anything from his teacher; I am the one who wrote — he sent him a burden of sixty years and thirteen scrolls of doubtful treifot; first chapter of Ketubot (daf 8a) — they say Rav is a tanna and argues with a tanna of the baraita and R' Yochanan is not a tanna to argue with a tanna as Tosafot wrote there; so too Mahariik root 170 — R' Yochanan who argues is called Rav because he was greater than him. Rav Chisda was a talmid chaver of Rav Huna as in Eruvin chapter Hador and end of Elu Metziot; Mahariik there proved at length Rav Huna was greater than Rav Chisda all his days; even so Tosafot chapter Mi SheMet (daf 72) and Rosh there in the name of R' Chananel and Raavan — halachah like Rav Chisda, for although he was his student we go stringently; so too other poskim there; similarly we find many times in Gemara and poskim halachah like lesser against greater — too many to count. Further wonder at his words: since in one against many we rely with great loss even though halachah like many is d'oraisa, as in chapter HaZahav regarding the bat kol "halachah like R' Eliezer in every place" and they did not heed him, for Scripture already wrote "follow the majority" — so throughout Gemara and poskim full of this — all the more lesser against greater; and how can it enter the mind that greater against lesser, not mentioned in Torah nor in known places in Gemara and poskim, should be preferable to many against one? Moreover Rambam, Semag, and other poskim I cite below hold there is no distinction between lesser and greater — they concede in one against many halachah like many; therefore the merit of many is preferable. Further wonder: "greater than him in wisdom and in number" means wisdom or number as Hagahot Raavan first chapter Avodah Zarah wrote — if this one is greater in wisdom and that in number, follow greater in number — meaning many students, or that most students say like him — end; Darkei Moshe and Semak siman 25 seif 17 cite this; it appears he derived from what we say in Gemara first chapter Yevamot (daf 14a) — Beit Shammai did not act like Beit Hillel though Beit Hillel were majority, even though Beit Shammai were sharper; even one who says they acted like their words concedes after the bat kol they did not act thus — see there; if so, when relying on one against many with great loss, all the more rely on lesser against greater in wisdom or number — this is clear. Although Raavad wrote at beginning of Eduyot on "a court cannot annul another court's words unless greater in wisdom and number" — number means years — one may say specifically regarding a court years matter to annul another court, but not between two sages; moreover Rambam chapter 2 Hilchot Mamrim and Semag positive 111 explain number as number of sages of the generation. Perhaps Bach did not write thus but against greater in wisdom and also in number; or perhaps he thought number means faces — nevertheless it appears even greater in years the law is so and the reasons above help; further certainly many is preferable as I proved. But the matter is clear as I explained — the rav's words are clear; I did not reach Bach's end who challenged him — see there. Further it appears clear from Bach's words that Rashba permits with great loss in one against many even in d'oraisa; he wrote "so too" at end of chapter Ha'arel (Yevamot daf 81) regarding cutting chatat etc.; wonder — one against many, halachah like many, is d'oraisa as written above — how uproot Torah law with great loss? Is this not Heaven's decree to follow the majority — what if there is great loss? Moreover they kill souls, excommunicate, declare ownerless, destroy property by majority; ir hanidachat is judged by majority — is there greater loss? Great difficulty — how can Rashba say even in d'oraisa issur the law is so? From where he learned — from first chapter Niddah (daf 9) R' said R' Eliezer is fit to rely on in time of distress — only rabbinic enactments from time to time are rabbinic; so we say in Eruvin (daf 46a) — Rav Papa to Rava: in rabbinic matters is there no difference between one and many? etc., until "but when many disagree with him, they said R' Eliezer is fit to rely on in distress" — therefore Rashba's language appears: it is fixed halachah that one does not rely on lesser against greater nor one against many unless there is great loss — you find relying as in first chapter Niddah etc., namely like that case in Niddah which is rabbinic issur; their reason "they said, they said" to rely on the individual with great loss; similarly we find many times they did not establish their words where there is monetary loss, as I wrote in siman 119 note 27 — all the more where there is one who permits; that case in chapter Ha'arel is no proof at all (even per Rashi, certainly per Tosafot there who explain in the alternative) — there we do not say with great loss rely on lesser or individual, but rabbinic view there is min b'mino batel; with small loss they were not concerned, with great loss they established their ruling — see there explained explicitly; moreover there although the core matter is d'oraisa issur, nevertheless it is rabbinic in that from Torah one in two is batel as in siman 110; so appears from the rav's words — from "unless there is also great loss" he concludes in the end; from the beginning in d'oraisa issur one does not rely on lesser at all, all the more one against many. However all this per the rav and Bach who follow Rashba — they did not attend to other poskim: Rambam chapter 1 Hilchot Mamrim wrote in general two sages or two courts etc. — in Torah law follow the stringent, in scribes' law follow the lenient — he did not distinguish lesser and greater; Semag positive 111; so too Gemara Yoma there — appears Rambam holds R' Yishmael b'R' Yosi argues on the first part too, as Kesef Mishneh wrote, or Rambam was doubtful whether they argue on the first part and ruled on doubt in Torah stringently and scribes leniently — if so no distinction between lesser and greater; so Semag and Gemara Yoma. However it appears specifically when the greater is not his rav; but if he is his rav, even for Rambam and supporters one does not rely on the lesser — fixed halachah in many Gemara places that halachah is not like the student in the rav's place; this law reverts to lesser versus greater per Rashba. Do not challenge Rambam and supporters — certainly we follow many; why not here follow the greater in number who has many students as explained in Hagahot Raavan — say Rambam holds like the first explanation in Hagahot that "many students" means he has many students; even if they do not state their view he is great in students; nevertheless Rambam and Semag hold this law in Hagahot that most students say like him — they need not write it here for it is obvious we follow many everywhere; only lest one think a student does not decide — Rambam already wrote chapter 1 Hilchot Sanhedrin that for tumah and taharah and issur and heter rav and student count as two; so Semag positive 58 from Gemara chapter Echad Dinei Mamonot (Sanhedrin daf 36), explained in Choshen Mishpat seif 18. Certainly one may not be lenient in d'oraisa issur against Rambam, Semag, and Gemara Yoma above; in rabbinic it would seem also like Rambam and supporters that in scribes' law follow the lenient — but since Bach is stringent and the rav wrote one does not rely on lesser against greater, difficult to be lenient; further I found in R' Yerucham end Netiv 2 and Ribash siman 379 and responsum Maharam Alashkar siman 54 they hold like Rashba; so appears Hagahot Raavan first chapter Avodah Zarah cited above; further there are many doubts in rabbinic to be stringent as I wrote in laws seif 19; also Mordechai chapter HaCholeitz wrote responsum Rabbenu Baruch that we find they disagree on rabbinic doubt and rule stringently; further some matters are rabbinic but touch d'oraisa issur — who can separate them? From what I wrote it is clarified what Bach wrote — there is room to examine Shulchan Aruch in places where the rav did not decide Beit Yosef to rule like one posek; sometimes he writes first in general to prohibit then says some permit or some disagree, sometimes the reverse etc. until Hagahot Shulchan Aruch writes the view of one who disagrees with what Shulchan Aruch ruled where the halachah did not spread like that one's words — this has the law of "the halachah was not stated neither like this one nor like that one" — end of his words; requires study — what he wrote about one against many relying on the individual with great loss was explained — only in rabbinic issur, not d'oraisa. If so why did Mechaber and other poskim write in several places even in d'oraisa issur the dissenting individual's view — one may say we learned in beginning Eduyot: why mention individual words among the many? If a man says "thus I received tradition" they say "say like so-and-so whom you heard"; we learned there that if a court sees the individual's words and relies on them etc., Raavad explained: if the later court sees halachah is like the first individual and relies on him, i.e. establishes halachah like him, as we find amoraim established halachah like early individuals in several places even though majorities disagree; if they had not found the first individual's words the later ones could not reject the first — until here; similarly if Mechaber and poskim wrote many's view in general one might say it is Torah of Moses or agreed in Mishnah and Gemara and no later one may disagree even with proofs — therefore they write individual words that if a later one brings clear proofs his words may be heeded and justified; or because we find many lands divided in laws, river to river, custom spread — therefore they wrote individual words lest there be a place or country that preserves thus and has basis to rely; so responsum Menachem Azariah siman 97 — know that author rabban of exile children — his way in fixed Shulchan Aruch for ruling: first bring the more agreed view; where another view is possible he brings it as "some say" (1); if he ruled first in general (siman 56) like Geonim to permit it is a complete decisive ruling per his view and he did not mention the second view except to honor its holder who are also great and many; in many places custom spread to forbid like them (as the rav wrote there that such is primary and so is practiced — see there); or when the author holds one in presumption of greater against lesser and the matter of greater against lesser is not clear, if he acted stringently per the lesser we do not overturn the act, as Bach also wrote; or lest that case occur in another place where there is another side of permission (as in siman 57 — see there); similarly we find often in poskim several sides combine for permission; or lest he find in other works also the individual's view on the matter so they are balanced or the individual becomes many as we find many times; or for a baal nefesh to be stringent for himself per the individual or lesser though fixed halachah is like many — nevertheless in books of poskim perhaps that individual has clear proof from Gemara or Tosefta or Yerushalmi, or he will find likewise in other books. What he wrote "if he acted" etc. — seems from his words specifically b'dieved; lechatchila not even with great loss; so too there in explanation — even in rabbinic issur one should not do so lechatchila; does not appear so in Rashba responsum; so appears from the rav — even lechatchila rely with great loss, but only rabbinic issur as I wrote above. It appears specifically to be lenient we rely with great loss on individual or lesser, not to be stringent — even if he acted per individual or lesser stringently we overturn the act, whether d'oraisa or rabbinic issur, since he did not act per halachah; what is permitted with great loss is because they did not establish their words where there is monetary loss — they said in place of great loss rely on individual to establish Torah law as above; so appears from Bach who wrote if he acted stringently per one who disagrees we do not overturn since it is not clear to us he is certainly greater etc. — implies if clear to us certainly we overturn; all the more one against many as above. The rav also wrote in Choshen Mishpat siman 25: even if the many do not agree for one reason but each has his own reason, since they agree on the ruling it is called many and we follow them — from Mahariik root 41; truly Mahariik there is not clear on this law and he did not write so except to explain Ashkenaz custom in blood; he concludes "all this is far in my eyes"; so appears from Agur in the name of Maharai that Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe cite siman 92 — one may not be lenient in such a case in d'oraisa issur; in our case of milk mixed in fat, doubt if sixty from milk in fat, afterward more fat added to pot — we do not say chanan in liquid for Raavan though proven in Asheri he does not hold this distinction between liquid and dry — nevertheless one need not be stringent for this because all agree chanan is only rabbinic; our case is permitted per all in Raavan's view because it is a wet matter, per Rosh because we do not say chanan except in a kli rishon — end (2); evidently if d'oraisa it would not be permitted since they do not agree for one reason — all the more where there is also one who disagrees on both reasons; examine. Summary of conduct of issur and heter rulings gathered from all Gemara and poskim by the rav the author, may his merit shield us — his spirit gathered them: (א) A sage who forbade and his ruling spread — permitted to ask two others only if they inform him the first forbade; if clarified the first erred in a changed matter or erred in judgment as explained in Choshen Mishpat siman 25 he may permit; but if he cannot clarify error only that he has kabbalah to permit or disagrees in judgment — even if the first retracts he cannot permit; if they permitted it is not permitted — it became a piece of issur. Some say even the second greater in wisdom and number cannot permit; some permit from a greater. If the first permitted the second may forbid. If both sit in beit midrash and the first's ruling has not yet spread the second may permit. All this on the same matter; on another act he may rule permission unless the first forbade as a stringency or fence. (ב) Two sages disputing tumah/taharah or issur/heter — if Torah matter follow the stringent even if lesser in wisdom and number, meaning he does not have as many students as his colleague; if the lesser is his student halachah is not like student in rav's place even if he is stringent in Torah issur; if the student became wise like his rav he is equal; if rabbinic — if both equal and unknown which is greater follow the lenient; if one greater in wisdom or number and lenient — even if they acted stringently per the lesser overturn; if the greater is stringent rely on the lesser in distress; if one greater in wisdom and the other in number — meaning many students even if they do not state their view, all the more if most students say like him — called greater in number against his colleague. If one greater in years he is not called greater for this; similarly rely on student in rav's place in rabbinic issur in distress; similarly in dispute one and many — if acted like individual stringently overturn; if many stringent rely on individual in rabbinic issur and distress. (ג) Distress means specifically great loss, or small loss for a poor person in an important matter, or for a rich person for Shabbat/Yom Tov honor or guest honor. In all issur except yayin nesech — if he can sell to a non-Jew without great loss it is not called great loss even if he loses all profit since he does not lose principal. (ד) All this is in general; if sometimes another permission side from Gemara or judgment etc., act as appears to his eyes — a judge has only what his eyes see if fit to decide and understand and rule; for this reason contradictory general rules in Gemara/poskim — there is some other side or reason. (ה) When Mechaber/Rama or acharonim write first one view in general then "some permit/forbid" or "some disagree" — primary seems the first view and the other is secondary; requires examination why secondary — lesser against greater, student against rav, individual against many, and the ways we said; if rabbinic one may rely on secondary to permit in distress; if secondary because presumed lesser against greater and not clear lesser against greater — if acted stringently in rabbinic issur do not overturn; if Torah issur do not rule like secondary to be stringent or lenient even in distress; baal nefesh may be stringent for himself in Torah issur. (ו) If rav and student agree on one view count them as two in issur/heter even if the student needs the rav's reasoning; while the rav teaches him the law he relies on him. (ז) If the many do not agree with one mind but each has his own reason yet agree on the ruling — do not rely on them to permit Torah or rabbinic issur. (ח) Wherever early words are written in a book and famous and later poskim disagree — follow the later; but if sometimes a geonic responsum not in a book and others disagree — need not rule like later ones who perhaps did not know the geon; if they had heard they would retract; some disagree that we do not heed later against great rishonim — one may be stringent; nevertheless if the later brings the early and disagrees, if famous that he is fit to disagree one may rely on him and must settle the matter; however if the posek can decide he may rule as he wishes; anywhere do not disagree with ancient geonim; before Rif generations of geonim already ruled. (ט) As forbidden to permit the forbidden, so forbidden to forbid the permitted even for a non-Jew, even without loss — for usually elsewhere there is a lenient side because prohibition became stringency leading to leniency; though it seems no lenient side will come, forbidden lest it roll and leniency come after a hundred things; therefore if forced to forbid due to doubt or stringency where issur is not clear as noon, the posek must say the issur is not clear but we are obligated to be stringent; all the more if permitting in distress he should say so. (י) One court in one city that divided — forbidden for some to rule thus and some thus because "do not cut yourselves" — do not make factions; this causes dispute in Israel and Torah becomes two Torahs; forbidden for each sect to act per its words or practice so even without explicit ruling, even if both equal in wisdom and number — they must debate much until all agree on one view; if impossible — if Torah matter all do per stringent; if rabbinic all per lenient; two courts in one city may rule these thus and those thus — specifically known two courts; if two courts not known in the city forbidden for part of city thus and part thus even if through this dispute two courts arose — all the more forbidden as this increases dispute; if one court greater than the other — their law is like two sages disputing where one is greater as explained above.
