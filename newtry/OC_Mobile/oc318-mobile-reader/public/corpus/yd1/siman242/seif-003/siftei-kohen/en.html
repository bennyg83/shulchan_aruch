Anyone who establishes a study hall for himself. The Kesef Mishneh writes that this means specifically where he establishes himself to issue halachic rulings. The Bach, however, does not appear to understand it so, for he writes that by establishing a study hall he challenges his teacher's authority. Proof is that even examining a slaughtering knife, which is not a halachic ruling at all, was forbidden in the Talmudic era because of one's teacher's honor. See Responsa Maharik of Krakow, beginning of siman 19.
However, etc. The words of Maharik, shoresh 170, do not appear to agree. He writes there that even if one had been his student, if he later became equally learned he may dispute him and rule in his presence, even in practical halachah, as Reish Lakish disputed Rabbi Yochanan. It is difficult to say that Maharik knew that Reish Lakish disputed him only by reasoning. As for Mahari Isserlein's proof there that this was the way of Torah from the days of the Tannaim, Amoraim, and Geonim in many places, one may answer that this was with permission, or after the teacher's death, as below in seif 4. This requires study.
