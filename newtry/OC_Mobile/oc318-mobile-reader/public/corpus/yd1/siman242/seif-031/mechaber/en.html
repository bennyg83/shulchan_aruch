Any Torah scholar whose thoughts are settled does not speak before one greater than him in wisdom, even if he learned nothing from him: {Rama: And a person should not rule until forty years if there is one greater than him in the city, even if not his rabbi (Beit Yosef in the name of Ran, Semak, Rashi, chapter HaNechnakim). A sage who forbade — his fellow may not permit by weighing opinions; but if he has a tradition that he erred (Rabbeinu Yerucham in the name of Geonim), or erred in a mishnah, he may permit (Ran, first chapter of Avodah Zarah, Raavad, Rashba, Ramban, Tosafot, Rosh, Rabbeinu Yerucham end of netiv 2); and even if he erred in weighing opinions, one may argue with the forbearer until he retracts (Ran's reasoning); therefore there is no prohibition for the asker to ask two (there in Mordechai, Agudah, Tosafot, Rosh, Rabbeinu Yerucham), provided he informs him the first already forbade (Rabbeinu Yerucham netiv 2, Tosafot, Rosh); and even if the first permitted and his ruling already took effect, the second may not forbid by weighing opinions (so implied in Hagahot Ashiri, first chapter of Avodah Zarah); and all this in that ruling itself; but in another matter it is obvious he may rule as it appears to him (Maharik shoresh 172, Chiddushei Rashba, and see below).}
