[And even if he erred in shikul ha-daat. It appears this applies specifically when he erred in shikul ha-daat — meaning two tannaim or amoraim or poskim disagree and the one who forbade ruled like one of them while the general sugya follows the other, as explained in Choshen Mishpat siman 25. And so Tosafot in the first chapter of Avodah Zarah: a sage who forbade — his colleague cannot permit unless he can return him, such as if he erred in a matter of mishnah or even in shikul ha-daat while the general sugya follows the other; but if he cannot clarify that he erred in shikul ha-daat, even though he argues with the teacher until he retracts, he cannot permit — as implied from those Tosafot, and so in Asheri there in the name of R' Yitzchak, and in Rabbenu Yerucham end Netiv 2 what they wrote: if two see the first erred, let one go argue with him; if he can prove error in mishnah he returns him; if they disagree in shikul ha-daat and his reasoning leans to permit what the first forbade and he cannot prove from mishnah or from the words of an amora — let him say "I hold thus," but I do not permit what you forbade since it left your mouth to forbid and you made a piece of prohibition, and I cannot return you through mishnah or amora — end of his words. Thus whenever he cannot return him through mishnah or amora, even if he later retracts and agrees to his shikul ha-daat he cannot permit, since he made a piece of prohibition (and so in Ma'adanei Melech chapter 8, daf 151a, per the Rosh). Only when clarifying that his ruling was error in mishnah or mistaken shikul ha-daat of this kind can we not say "he made a piece of prohibition," for it was in error. And if it dealt with one who does not retract and concede to his words — why need the reason "I do not permit what you forbade since it left your mouth" etc.? It should follow simply that he cannot clarify the truth with him and it is forbidden; and why did they repeat and write "and I cannot return you through mishnah or amora"? Rather they mean: even if I return you, since I cannot return you through mishnah or amora I cannot permit — as explained. And it appears this is also the Ra'avad's and Rashba's view that the Ran brought in the first chapter of Avodah Zarah what they wrote: and these matters apply to a matter where there is no dispute except in shikul ha-daat; but if he erred in a matter of mishnah he retracts and permits purification etc.; but in shikul ha-daat certainly not — because it became like a piece of prohibition — end of his words; and it deals with where there is no error in shikul ha-daat, for where there is error certainly "piece of prohibition" does not apply. And that they did not distinguish shikul ha-daat itself is because generally in shikul ha-daat it may be impossible to clarify; but when he erred in mishnah his error can be clarified in mishnah. And so wrote Maharam of Tikatin in his gloss on Alfasi: "but in shikul ha-daat certainly not" etc. — meaning in shikul ha-daat where they cannot determine because no consensus fell to one side or the other; but shikul ha-daat where the general sugya is like this is like a matter of mishnah — so Tosafot state — end of his words. And although this is somewhat strained in their words, nevertheless it is preferable to align their words with Tosafot and the Rosh; and thus "he made a piece of prohibition" is understood, and also thereby resolved is the Ran's question on them from one chapter of monetary laws (Sanhedrin 33) which implies there that when he erred in shikul ha-daat they return his ruling with his consent, and the Ran concludes there thus for law — only he wrote that practically he has no power to disagree with the Avot HaOlam; per what I said, the Avot HaOlam also hold thus, and there is no difficulty; nevertheless we can establish that gemara there in chapter one of monetary laws: that what he did stands and he pays from his house, and they do not return his ruling in shikul ha-daat where there is no error — for otherwise why pay from his house since it is impossible to clarify that he erred? — all this is not like the Taz who held even in shikul ha-daat without error one can return him — this does not exist; rather as I explained.]
