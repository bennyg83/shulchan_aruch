There is no prohibition to ask the other. It should be:
And even if the first authority permitted it and the ruling has already taken effect, etc. The second authority may not issue a contrary ruling even to be stringent. This applies specifically when the first lenient ruling has already taken effect before the second authority disagreed. If it has not yet taken effect, the second authority may disagree and rule stringently. The Rama’s wording is imprecise. He should have written, “if it took effect,” rather than, “and it already took effect.” It appears to me that “took effect” means that some act of leniency was performed, such as that part of the item was eaten or it became mixed with permitted food. Mere acceptance of the first authority’s ruling is not enough to prevent the second from disagreeing stringently on the basis of reasoned judgment. For the meaning of reasoned judgment, see Choshen Mishpat, siman 25.
