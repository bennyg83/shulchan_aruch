Because they had not yet come into the world. — I do not know the source for this and it puzzles me: why in seif 12 do we require that they stipulated specifically at the start of the partnership — why would it not help also afterward, so long as the fruits had not yet grown, as here? And if you say here is different because he had already received payment before the prohibition came into the world — if so he should not say simply "it is permitted to sell" but only if he received payment beforehand. And if you say the reason for the leniency here is that the sale is not a sale since it had not yet come into the world, and therefore it is as though he received the money for nothing from the non-Jew — that is no argument, for then according to Rabbeinu Tam who holds that money does not acquire a non-Jew’s goods except through meshikhah, as written below siman 320, it would be permitted to sell orlah fruits to a non-Jew for money — and that is certainly not so. This ruling requires further study.
