Outside Eretz Yisrael it is permitted. Kiddushin 39a states that orlah outside Eretz Yisrael is a halacha leMoshe miSinai. When challenged from the teaching that doubtful orlah outside Eretz Yisrael is permitted, the Gemara answers that this is how the tradition was given: its doubt is permitted, while certainty is forbidden. The Beit Yosef understood that this leniency had to have been stated explicitly, because an ordinary doubt concerning a halacha leMoshe miSinai is treated stringently, and therefore questioned the Tur's implication that the very distinction between a Torah verse in Eretz Yisrael and a halacha leMoshe miSinai outside it explains the leniency. In my opinion, the Tur's wording is correct. Had the tradition merely repeated the same prohibition outside Eretz Yisrael, there would have been no need for the Torah to teach the prohibition in Eretz Yisrael by verse and outside it through a separate oral tradition. The change in the source itself reveals a difference in law, namely that outside Eretz Yisrael doubtful orlah is permitted. The Gemara's phrase, “Say that this is how it was given,” means: understand that the separate oral tradition was given precisely to disclose this leniency. Thus, the Tur correctly inferred from the differing forms of the prohibition that the rule of doubt differs as well. This explanation fits both the Gemara and the Tur.

And all the more so a vineyard whose status is doubtful, etc. The Derishah understood this to mean only that one may buy grapes sold outside the vineyard, provided one does not see a non-Jew gathering them from the doubtful planting. In my opinion, this is incorrect. Doubtful orlah outside Eretz Yisrael is more lenient than doubtful vineyard mixtures, because the halacha leMoshe miSinai expressly permits the doubt. Whenever the grapes are not certainly orlah, the doubt is treated as complete permission. Therefore, if a vineyard contains both permitted and prohibited plantings and one does not know from which the grapes were taken, the grapes are permitted. All the more so, if it is uncertain whether the vineyard contains orlah at all, even an Israelite may take from it. Only where he sees the non-Jew gathering from a tree certainly known to be orlah is it forbidden. This differs from doubtful kilayim outside Eretz Yisrael. There, although the prohibition is rabbinic, the Sages required additional distance from possible transgression, and the Israelite may not gather with his own hand. The Gemara's comparison between doubtful orlah and doubtful kilayim is consistent with this. According to R' Yochanan, who holds that orlah outside Eretz Yisrael is a halacha leMoshe miSinai, the Mishnah's condition, “provided he does not see him gather,” explains that only certain orlah is prohibited, whereas every genuine doubt is permitted. Kilayim remains subject to the rabbinic safeguard against personal gathering. The Rama's wording, “a vineyard that is doubtful,” likewise indicates that the vineyard itself may be used without requiring the produce first to be sold outside it. The Mordechai similarly ruled that where four or five orlah plantings were present in a vineyard but their location was unknown, the entire vineyard was permitted, even to eat grapes while attached. The Shulchan Aruch follows the Tur's stricter view regarding certainty, but every true doubt is permitted as explained.
