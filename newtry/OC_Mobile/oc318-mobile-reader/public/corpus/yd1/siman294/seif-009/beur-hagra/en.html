How, etc. — see Rashi in Kedushin there 38 side b, and what Rashi wrote there s.v. heinu, doubt passed, etc. He did not write except regarding Eretz Yisrael and Syria; but what he wrote abroad "he goes down and buys," etc. — therefore not such a case, and as written in Berakhot 36 side a explicitly; and therefore in s.v. uvachul he wrote only the second explanation that is in it, etc.; but what he wrote in s.v. yored, etc., is very astounding, for this is certainly orlah and the gemara calls it doubt, as written heinu that we distinguish, etc.; and further, since kilayim applies abroad and in a gentile's [field], as above, why should picking of the gentile be permitted? Rather Ran explains: doubt is that there is in it orlah and permitted, and likewise kilayim and permitted — in Eretz Yisrael forbidden since sold at the opening of the garden it is evident; in Syria permitted but inside the garden forbidden; abroad even inside permitted, except in orlah specifically detached, but one who sees may pick even though he does not know if from permitted or forbidden; and in kilayim likewise permitted provided he does not pick himself from the garden even though he does not know if it is forbidden, as in chapter 1 of Orlah "planting of orlah and of," etc.; but one who sees may pick from forbidden in all places forbidden even in kilayim. But Rambam explained that the entire vineyard is orlah or kilayim, only he does not know if picked from this vineyard or another vineyard, and so in Tosefta and Yerushalmi at end of Orlah: which doubt of orlah — vineyard of orlah and grapes sold outside it — in Eretz Yisrael forbidden, in Syria permitted; R' Yosi: this too forbidden in Syria; which doubt permitted in Syria — vineyard of orlah and another vineyard beside it and grapes sold outside — in Eretz Yisrael forbidden, etc.; which doubt of kilayei hakerem — vineyard planted with vegetables and vegetables sold outside — in Eretz Yisrael, etc.; R' Yosi: this too in Syria forbidden; which doubt permitted in Syria — vineyard planted with vegetables and another vegetable field beside it and vegetables sold outside — in Eretz Yisrael forbidden, etc.; and see Mordechai at end of chapter 1 of Kedushin, and this is Rambam's wording in chapter 10 of Maaser Sheni: doubt of orlah and kilayei hakerem — in Eretz Yisrael forbidden, in Syria permitted — how so? There was a vineyard of orlah and grapes sold outside; there was a vegetable planted inside and vegetable sold outside — perhaps from this, perhaps from another; in Syria permitted; abroad even if he saw grapes emerging from vineyard of orlah or vegetable emerging from the vineyard he may buy from them, provided he does not see him harvesting from orlah or picking vegetable by hand; and commentators were entangled whether he ruled in kilayim like one who says "goes down and buys" also, and abroad permitted even certainly — they toiled in vain, for certainly he ruled like both "goes down and buys" as written, and it is specifically in its doubt as above, for the gemara calls doubt kilayim, as written explicitly in chapter 8 of Hilchot Kilayim and brought in Shulchan Aruch siman 296; and it is that he sees, etc., but its doubt is permitted. And he explains abroad "goes down and buys" even inside the vineyard — perhaps nevertheless from elsewhere; and in kilayei hakerem even one who sees may pick — for the mishnah — nevertheless since he does not know if those selling are from those he picked, permitted; but he ruled in both "goes down and buys" as in Kedushin there and as above; and even though Mar brei deRavina teaches leniently — in Yerushalmi there at end of Orlah he said stringently, and abroad "goes down and picks" — R' Yosi: still it is like the first case "goes down and buys," provided he does not see him picking; and so in Bava Batra 24 side a they forbade grapes found, as in siman 11; and if even seeing him pick from that vineyard is permitted, all the more so such a case; but according to Rashi and Tur who wrote there are plantings — it comes out well.
