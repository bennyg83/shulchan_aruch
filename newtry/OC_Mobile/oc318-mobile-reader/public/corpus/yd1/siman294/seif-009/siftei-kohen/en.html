And all the more so, etc. This requires study: why did he write it, since it is already explained in the words of the Mechaber in seif 10? It appears that because the Mechaber in seif 10 copied Rambam's language, and from this wording it implies that in a vineyard that is doubtful orlah, one may take from it by hand, since it is permitted and is like a permitted vineyard; and so wrote Beit Yosef. But the Rav does not hold so; rather he holds like the Tur and his supporters, that it is forbidden to take from it by hand, but it is permitted even if one sees the non-Jew harvesting from it. Therefore he needed to write, "and all the more so," etc. So too it appears from Ateret Zekenim, who wrote that the rule that a vineyard of doubtful orlah is permitted means that he should not pick by hand; rather another Jew should pick not in his presence, or a non-Jew even in his presence, and then it is permitted for him to take from it; so it appears to me, end quote. In Minchat Machvat he challenged Ateret Zekenim from what Beit Yosef wrote that it is forbidden, but I did not get to the bottom of his view, for here, regarding a vineyard of doubtful orlah, it appears permitted, as Ateret Zekenim wrote.
