And on a roof, etc. — from what is written in last chapter of Maasrot: onions that took root in an upper story — apparently like planted in a field; and even though we say in Menachot chapter 4 side b "roof on roof — no difficulty," etc. — that is specifically regarding bikkurim that require "from the praise of your land," unlike a ruin that is not cultivation, and dates on mountains and fruits in valleys; and on the contrary, from there proof to the contrary, for it says there chapter 5 side a: rather for R' Yosi it is difficult — it is a dispute of tannaim, etc., and do not infer anything from roof and ruin; and Rashi there s.v. tanai, etc. — must say roof and ruin are preferable to dates on mountains and fruits in valleys; and certainly for R' Eliezer he can reconcile them to him, but R' Yosi only challenged from pot and ship. And all this length is unnecessary, for explicit in Tosefta as above. (Likut) And on a roof, etc. — Rosh's proof from what is written in chapter 5 of Maasrot: onions that took root, etc., and he learned that even for terumot and maasrot he is liable, as in his responsum; but it is astounding, for they said only regarding impurity and not terumot and maasrot, as in Tosefta chapter 3 of Maasrot: onions that took root one in another in pots — they remain in presumption for maasrot and sheviit; if they were impure they did not leave their impurity and permitted to detach on Shabbat; took root one in another in ground of an upper story — likewise in presumption for maasrot and sheviit; if impure they left impurity and forbidden to detach on Shabbat; if detached — exempt; a collapse fell on them and they are exposed — behold these are like planted in a field, forbidden in sheviit and liable in maasrot; and so Rosh there; and what he wrote "fell," etc., and likewise in mishnah there — it does not refer to upper story but actual ground (end of comment).
Even though, etc. — even though in Yerushalmi R' Chiya holds all the more in a wooden one, and like Tosafot in chapter 1 of Gittin and Menachot there; but Rosh and Tur wrote here like Rashi's view, and specifically earthenware; but in Yerushalmi it implies like Rambam's words that in every matter, since they were in doubt whether it helps also for seeds, since the tree's roots already perforated it; but according to Rashi this is unnecessary, for it is perforated also according to all. And Rambam's view that he does not distinguish in all places between earthenware and wood — he holds specifically in a ship we distinguish between wood and earthenware, for water softens it; and Tosafot in Menachot there and chapter 1 of Gittin and Rosh in chapter 2 of Challah mishnah 2; and see Orach Chaim siman 336 note 8.
