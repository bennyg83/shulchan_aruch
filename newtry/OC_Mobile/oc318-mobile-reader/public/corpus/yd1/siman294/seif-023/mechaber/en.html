One who plants a food tree and does not intend it for eating but to be a fence for a field, or for the wood to burn or for building, is exempt—provided it is evident it is not for eating: if for burning, that they are planted close together and he does not space tree from tree as appropriate; if for building, that he prunes the branches so they thicken; if for a fence, that it is in a place that needs fencing. And even if one side, whether inner or outer, whether lower or upper, is for one of these purposes and the other side for eating—the side for eating is liable and the rest exempt. And if he later intended it for eating, it is liable for orlah and revai and one counts from the time of planting. But if he did not intend it until the end of the third year, it has no status of revai, for there is no revai without orlah. And if he planted it for food and afterward intended it for one of these, he is liable.
