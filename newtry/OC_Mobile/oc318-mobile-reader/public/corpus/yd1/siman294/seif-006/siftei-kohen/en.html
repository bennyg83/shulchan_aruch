They redeem them after they finish and are detached, etc., this is Rambam's language; and in Beit Yosef he wrote: not specifically, one may redeem initially even while attached, when he explains he does not cause redemption until gathered; and explicitly we taught in the last chapter of Ma'aser Sheni how one redeems neta revai: place the basket at the mouth of three, etc. Even for one who explains one does not redeem while attached because we are not expert in appraisal, only the modest acted with a sign in shemitah so as not to stumble, this is specifically to redeem while attached that it be redeemed while attached that we are not expert; but to redeem while attached that redemption not apply until detached, we are expert; and the mitzvah of redeeming neta revai initially is thus, as explained in that mishnah, end quote briefly; investigate. Ma'amar Mordechai agreed; and Bach challenged him that that mishnah speaks of the modest and did not speak correctly, see there. But I wonder: plainly the mishnah speaks of detached, as we taught "all that is gathered from this is redeemed [on] these coins," etc., and this is detached; and so it seems in Marbeh (69a): the language "all that is gathered" means detached; and it seems the language of Rash and Bartenura misled Beit Yosef and his supporters who wrote: they estimate how much a person wants to redeem for a sela, meaning to take for a sela the attached to the ground, end quote; and it is not so, on the contrary it speaks of detached; and they came to explain the mishnah that we do not estimate as it is worth now, for he already incurred expenses of detaching and gathering; such is the case, even though detached, estimate as if attached to the ground, how much a person wants to take for a sela attached to the ground. If so, Beit Yosef's main proof is no proof at all; and what he distinguished between redeeming actually attached and redeeming attached for when detached, in truth ostensibly proven thus in Tosafot chapter Marbeh and Rosh in explaining the mishnah, from their difficulties specifically why we establish it "all that will be gathered" and not "all that is gathered", the modest themselves explained there that "all that is gathered" means money was placed attached before gathering and he says this vineyard when gathered shall be redeemed; if not, when would they say so, and would they not cease a moment from saying so? Certainly they hold that even saying so while attached, since they say when gathered, it is as detached. But per what we establish "all that will be gathered", necessarily "all that will be gathered" means redeemed from now while attached, for if not, the Talmud's difficulty returns to its place: how can they redeem once gathered, for others who gathered already acquired and the owner has no power to redeem, see there in Tosafot and Rosh, for this seems clear. Nevertheless it seems per those who initially hold the reason one does not redeem attached is because we derive sacred from sacred from ma'aser, if so, when redeeming attached for when detached it is as detached and one redeems properly; therefore they did not challenge "all that is gathered." But per the conclusion that one does not redeem attached because we are not expert in appraisal, even redeeming for when detached we are not expert; and so from what Tosafot and Rosh conclude immediately like grapes for libation, etc., see there; investigate. And if so emerges: one should not redeem initially while attached; and also in Teshuvot HaRashba siman 192 he wrote: even though Tosafot's conclusion in chapter Marbeh is that we do not derive from ma'aser except because value is unknown and they redeem while attached, nevertheless since Semag, Semak, and Asheri wrote plainly one does not redeem attached, do not deviate from their words, end quote; only I do not know why he wrote from Tosafot it implies one redeems attached, for they did not write so except regarding the modest; but here not so, even though we do not derive from ma'aser, nevertheless one does not redeem because we are not expert, as Rabbeinu Shimshon wrote; and perhaps also Rosh and Semak hold this reason; however in Beit Yosef he wrote another reason that nowadays one may redeem attached, since nowadays one redeems even for a perutah appraisal does not apply; and Bach challenged this: what is the challenge, if so, also forbidden now to redeem for a perutah, for soon the Temple will be built, etc.; and this is not a challenge, for if so also forbidden to redeem now for a perutah. And so in responsum of Ramban siman 156 and Rashbash siman 384 they wrote plainly: nowadays when one redeems for a perutah, one redeems attached even initially; and so practiced daily, end quote. Nevertheless since the plain meaning of other poskim implies no distinction, be careful initially not to redeem except actually detached; but in pressing circumstances one may redeem attached that redemption not apply until gathered, as above.
Into the Great Sea, or, etc. See above, siman 146, seif katan 13.
And he should recite the blessing at the time of redemption, etc. Meaning, at the time of redemption, before casting the coin into the Dead Sea, so that the blessing precedes the performance of the mitzvah. Rambam, Hilchot Ma'achalot Assurot 10:17, likewise writes that after gathering them he recites the blessing, and afterward redeems them all.
Which weighs sixteen barleycorns, etc. Meaning, sixteen barleycorns by weight. A ma'ah is one quarter of a dirham, as is evident below at the beginning of siman 304. A perutah therefore weighs half a barleycorn, as the Mechaber also wrote in Choshen Mishpat, beginning of siman 88. The Shalshelet HaKabbalah reports in the name of R. Ovadiah Sforno that a ma'ah is a drachmon of sixteen barleycorns. He then compares this with the Prague weight and with a Roman drachmon described in Yosippon. The Terumat HaDeshen, siman 192, wrote that the common half-viner coin in those lands is worth more than a perutah and may be used for redemption. The Ma'adanei Yom Tov cites this.
The half-viner, etc. This does not mean that precisely a half-viner is required. According to the Rav's valuation below at the beginning of siman 305, a perutah is approximately one eighth of a viner, as the Sema also wrote in Choshen Mishpat, siman 88, seif katan 2. Since no smaller coin was available, he wrote that redemption with a half-viner certainly suffices, because it is worth more than a perutah. Accordingly, when redeeming with an item of value rather than a coin, value equal to one eighth of a viner would suffice. Alternatively, because in our time a perutah purchases almost nothing, perhaps a mere perutah does not suffice, and one therefore needs the value of half a viner.
