Revai fruits have the status of maaser sheni for every matter. And in the time of the Beit HaMikdash they had to bring them up to Jerusalem to eat them there, or redeem them and bring the redemption money there. And they are permitted immediately after redemption. And nowadays, when one cannot bring them to Jerusalem, one redeems them after they are complete and detached. And one may redeem all of them for the value of a perutah even if they are many; and one throws the perutah into the Great Sea, or pulverizes it and scatters it to the wind, or throws it into a river after pulverizing it. And one recites a blessing at the time of redemption: "Who sanctified us with His commandments and commanded us concerning the redemption of revai." Or one redeems with fruits that are worth a perutah, which is one thirty-second of a ma'ah of silver—that is, an aspero Ottomanis, which weighs sixteen barley grains, which is a quarter of a drachma (his own wording). {Rama: And the regular coin in these lands—the half and the vinar—is more than the value of a perutah, and one redeems on it (Terumat HaDeshen siman 192).} Revai fruits are exempt from leket, shichecha, pe'ah, and olelot.
