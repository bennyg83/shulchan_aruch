And likewise one who grafts, etc. — In chapter 1 of Rosh HaShanah the Ran raised a difficulty: we say in Sifrei that one who grafts is not subject to orlah? He resolved that there it refers to grafting a young shoot onto an old tree where it is nullified by it; and even young onto young — when the first young one reaches three years it is permitted, and so too the second. But here we are dealing with grafting onto a tree planted as a fence or for beams which is exempt from orlah — therefore the grafted shoot is subject to orlah; or grafting onto a sterile tree — even though he commits a transgression, it nevertheless has the law of orlah — end quote. Beit Yosef brought this nearby. Rashi wrote in the chapter of the anointed for war: regarding what we say “young in old is nullified by it and has no law of orlah” — that which the Mishnah teaches “one who grafts is subject to orlah” refers to young in young, where that branch too is subject to orlah; and even though it was cut from an old tree, it now becomes young, for this is its planting. Therefore the words of the Tur and Shulchan Aruch here are interpreted likewise — that he grafted a permitted branch onto a young shoot, and that branch too became subject to orlah, for all the days of the prohibition of the grafted one, as the Ran wrote.
