And likewise one who grafts, etc. The Ran explains that the Sifrei's statement that grafting does not create a new orlah period concerns a young branch grafted into an old tree, where it becomes nullified to the old tree. Even a young branch grafted into another young tree becomes permitted when the first tree's three years are completed. The present ruling concerns a branch grafted into a tree planted for a fence or timber, which was exempt from orlah, or into a non-fruit tree. The grafted fruit branch then begins its own orlah obligation. Rashi explains similarly that a permitted branch grafted into a young planting becomes subject to the remaining orlah period of the host tree.
So long as it remains attached and draws nourishment from it. The Yerushalmi teaches that even without severing it from the original tree, it begins a new orlah period once it no longer draws nourishment from the parent but lives independently. This may be recognized because when it draws nourishment from the parent, its leaves turn away from the parent's leaves.
