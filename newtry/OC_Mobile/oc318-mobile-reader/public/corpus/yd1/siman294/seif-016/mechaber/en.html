Whether one plants a kernel or a branch, or uproots the whole tree and plants it elsewhere, or grafts a branch onto another tree, or layers (buries a branch in the ground—that is, he makes a hollow next to the tree and lowers one of the tree's branches and buries its middle in the hollow and its head emerges from the other side and it becomes a tree)—all are liable for orlah. And this is specifically when he cut it from this side of the trunk of the tree, or that it does not draw nourishment from it; but as long as it is attached and draws nourishment from it, it is considered one of its branches and is exempt. And if after the layered branch grew he layered another branch in the ground and did so many times one after another—as long as the first is attached to the trunk of the tree, all are exempt. If the first was disconnected from the trunk of the tree, they are liable and begin counting for them from when it was disconnected, and the trunk is exempt. And if the trunk was uprooted from the ground, it returns to be subsidiary to the branch that was layered, and one counts for both the trunk and the branch from when it was uprooted.
