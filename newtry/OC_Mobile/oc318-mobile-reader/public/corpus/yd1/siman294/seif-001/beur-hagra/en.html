From the time of its planting. Torat Kohanim states: From when does he count for it. From the time of its planting. The same is stated in the Gemara in the first chapter of Rosh Hashanah (9b, 10a), and this is inferred from there.
For benefit. This is stated in the Mishnah and Gemara at the end of the second chapter of Kiddushin (56b).
Forever. Torat Kohanim states: “Three years.” One might have thought that it is forbidden during the three years but becomes permitted afterward. Scripture therefore states, “it shall be.” See Tosafos to Kiddushin 38a, s.v. “And the same applies to orlah,” etc.
And its blossom. It should read “its,” in the singular, and it refers to the pomegranate.
And the seeds of grapes. there, and the skins, and like R' Yosi in Nazir 34b; and likewise pits of all fruits, as written “and the pits,” and as Rambam and R. Shimshon wrote there, and as written above among the pits; only he included here zagim and dates and also wrote mishnah wording.
And the unripe fruits. This is the Mishnah there: “the sap of the unripe fruits,” etc.
And the dates, etc. ; mishnah there, and as written in Berakhot 40b, except for navelot; unspecified, all agree, etc., and as Arukh and Rosh and Rosh in Hilchos Ketanos explained; pagim that R' said for eating, and unripe dates, etc., and Orach Chaim siman 202 seif 9.
And the grapes, etc. ; R. Shimshon there, s.v. “anav kuklot”, etc., and their dispute regarding revai, but regarding orlah there is no difference; and from “and exempt” it implies like R' Yosi; and it implies R' Chiya bar Aba supports him, and so in Rambam's explanation; see there.
And there is an authority who says, etc. and as Rambam wrote in his explanation, and navelot, etc., only he explained navelot as dried dates (see Shach), and his words require study [and also Rosh's words require study, who brought an amoraic dispute there whether dried dates, etc., and it appears something is missing in the transcription and should read as Rosh there]; but Tur explained navelot; all of all fruits, but it does not refer to an unripe cluster; and his words are astounding, for from this he should have said “all of them,” since pits too are of all fruits; and in the Gemara he did not explain navelot but dates; and it should read “and navelot” after “and pits” before “forbidden”; and what Beit Yosef brought as proof from Yerushalmi; they said fruit you redeem and not unripe and not pagim; pagim are not like navelot, for navelot ripen by burying in earth, unlike pagim; and so he listed above pagim and navelot as two.
