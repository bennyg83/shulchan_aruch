Forever. Even after three years, and they have no redemption.
Whether the principal part, etc. For Scripture states, ‘its fruit,’ including that which is secondary to its fruit.
And their blossom. This means the blossom of the pomegranates. The Rash explains that it is the flower above the crown of the fruit.
And they are exempt from fourth-year produce. Regarding fourth-year planting, Scripture states ‘its fruit’ without the inclusive particle. We therefore expound: you redeem the fruit, but not that which is not fruit.
"And there are those who forbid" etc. In Bach it is written that the Mechaber was not precise, for there is no dispute here; and the novalot that the Rambam obligates are temari zikei etc., see there where he elaborated, and all his words are incorrect. At the outset, what Bach wrote, that the Tur explains the mishnah in temari zikei and the Tur deals with boshlei kmeri, is great forcing; the primary law stated in the mishnah the Tur would not bring, and another law not mentioned there he brings from his reasoning. Furthermore, the Rosh in Hilchot Orlah explains the mishnah both in temari zikei and in boshlei kmeri. Furthermore, in chapter Keitzad Mevarchin (Berakhot daf 40) we say novalot stamah is boshlei kmeri, how can the Tur explain novalot of the mishnah specifically as temari zikei? And although the Rosh explains both, we must say per his view that what the Gemara says there, novalot stamah all agree not disputing that it is boshlei kmeri (when they dispute novalot temari of demai etc.), that is novalot of Keitzad Mevarchin; but novalot stamah of another mishnah, zikei is also implied; nevertheless certainly boshlei kmeri is also implied. And the words of the Rambam in Peirush HaMishnayot appear clear, novalot mentioned stamah everywhere means boshlei kmeri and not temari zikei; therefore he explains in Peirush HaMishnah, novalot (that we bless shehakol) are fruits that fell from the tree before ripening, that is boshlei kmeri; but temari zikei all bless borei pri haetz as in the Gemara, temari zikei even if already cooked, only that they fell from wind that detached them. And in the beginning of Demai that teaches novalot temari, he explains novalot are dates falling from the palm when wind blows and most are hefker, meaning for demai even temari zikei are exempt from demai, since though they ripened properly, when they fell from the palm due to wind most are hefker; but for berachot, since they are complete fruits, one blesses borei pri haetz, hence the Gemara's question on temari zikei: is there one who does not bless borei pri haetz? The Gemara says: granted per one view (novalot temari of demai) temari zikei is why here it is called novalot stamah and there novalot temari; but per the view boshlei kmeri it should teach both times novalot temari or both times novalot stamah, difficult. This is the Rambam's reason who rules like the view temari zikei, per the view boshlei kmeri the question stands difficult. Therefore in the beginning of Orlah where the mishnah teaches novalot stamah, the Rambam explains novalot from trees, from fruit before finishing cooking, forbidden in orlah and revai, that is boshlei kmeri, as in Peirush HaMishnah. Therefore in Yad, Hilchot Berakhot chapter 8 law 8, novalot that are spoiled one blesses shehakol. Therefore in Hilchot Orlah chapter 9 he wrote stamah and all novalot are forbidden, that is boshlei kmeri. Therefore in the beginning of chapter 13 of Hilchot Maaser he wrote novalot temari that have not yet fermented, that is temari zikei, as Bach said. From all this it is clearly proven that novalot temari that the Rambam forbids is even boshlei kmeri, unlike Bach; also it seems Bach in Beit Yosef, what the Rambam explains novalot forbidden even in revai hints at the mishnah explanation, and he was precise in the language; also in Yad he did not mention explicitly even revai; if so, in Peirush HaMishnah he clearly wrote they fell before cooking, unlike Bach who did not pay attention to Rambam's Peirush HaMishnah. One cannot challenge: perhaps what the Rambam obligates is only if they fell before cooking, but if they remained on the tree they would cook, and the Tur obligates those that do not cook even without falling, forced division; furthermore the Rambam holds novalot of the mishnah obligates even revai, if so, the Tur's novalot obligates revai; furthermore from the Rambam's own words it is proven there is no distinction, in chapter 8 of Berakhot he did not mention they fell from the tree, only wrote that spoiled ones are novalot boshlei kmeri. And from the Geonim that caprifigs are not fruit for orlah so for berachah we do not bless borei pri haetz, no proof, since Rambam, Rosh, and Bartenura in Peirush HaMishnah and other commentators explain novalot boshlei kmeri obligates revai though we bless shehakol; rather what is not fruit for orlah certainly no borei pri haetz; but one can say what is fruit for orlah is sometimes found that we bless shehakol; furthermore in orlah they are truly exempt, only in revai they are obligated; explain the Geonim only like caprifigs, subsidiary to fruit, not the body of the fruit itself, examine. From all this the Mechaber's words are clear and Bach did not challenge correctly; on the contrary it seems difficult why he wrote the Tur's view in stam and the Rambam in the language "some forbid", it should be the opposite, since all commentators agree with the Rambam and so is primary.
