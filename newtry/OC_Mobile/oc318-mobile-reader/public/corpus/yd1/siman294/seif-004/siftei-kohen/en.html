It counts as a year for him. We rule that thirty days in a year are considered a year regarding planting, but a planting does not take root in fewer than fourteen days.
They likewise remain subject to orlah. A tree grows from most of the water of the preceding year, and anything that buds before the fifteenth of Shevat is attributed to the water absorbed before Rosh Hashanah. The Rosh wrote that nowadays no tree buds before the fifteenth of Shevat, and therefore orlah is observed for only three years. The Mechaber omitted this. According to the Ma'adanei Yom Tov, the Rosh wrote it only according to the Baal HaMaor, whereas according to Rambam and Rashi it is unnecessary, since even when budding occurs before the fifteenth of Shevat the period never exceeds three years. One might therefore say that the Mechaber omitted it because he regarded Rambam's view as primary. This is difficult, however, where one planted after Rosh Hashanah but before the fifteenth of Shevat. Perhaps planting is not normally done after Rosh Hashanah.
