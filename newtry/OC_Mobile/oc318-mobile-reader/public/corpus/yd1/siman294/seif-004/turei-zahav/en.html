From Tishrei. The verse states, “from the beginning of the year until the end of the year,” and the year begins in Tishrei.
And forty-four days. The Gemara teaches that any planting that does not take root within fourteen days will no longer take root. Thirty additional days are then required for the period to count as a year, totaling forty-four days.

All fruit that forms before the fifteenth of Shevat. Although Tishrei is the New Year for plantings, the fifteenth of Shevat is the New Year for trees.
They likewise have the law of orlah. Fruit that forms between Tishrei and the fifteenth of Shevat develops from the water absorbed before Rosh Hashanah and from sap produced before Tishrei. It is therefore considered as though it had formed before Tishrei. Accordingly, there is no basis to distinguish between a tree planted forty-four days before Rosh Hashanah and one planted later.
