And the Maggid Mishneh brought the beraita end of chapter 5 of Avodah Zarah: Israel and an idolater who accepted a field in partnership — Israel may not say to the idolater, take your share on Shabbat and I on weekday; and if they conditioned from the outset it is permitted; and if they came to accounting it is forbidden — end quote. And our teacher did not mention "they came to accounting," and it appears he explains like Rashi's explanation — this is what it means: if they did not condition from the outset and he also did not tell him take your share on Shabbat and I on weekday, but this one stood and took his share himself on Shabbat, and afterward they came to accounting — forbidden — end quote, Maggid Mishneh; and Beit Yosef and other later authorities in Orach Chaim siman 245 followed him. I did not grasp their final view — how can one say the Rambam's view is thus, for regarding orlah he wrote if they came to accounting it is forbidden even if they conditioned; and so the Tur in this siman in the name of the Rambam; and Shabbat and orlah are one law, as proven in the Gemara per the Rambam's explanation and as Ran wrote end of chapter 5 of Avodah Zarah. But to me it appears clear that he explains this that the Gemara says "and if they came to accounting it is forbidden" — not like Rashi, but like the Ra'avad whom Ran brought — it refers to when they conditioned; and he holds the meaning of what we said "and if they conditioned it is permitted" is specifically when they conditioned that the idolater eat two of orlah and Israel two of heter whether little or much, and they were not precise in accounting; but if they came to accounting — meaning they conditioned thus, that the idolater eat days of prohibition and afterward they calculate how much fruit the idolater ate on prohibition days until Israel eats corresponding to those fruits — if they conditioned thus it is forbidden; for only when they conditioned in stam that the idolater take on Shabbat and Israel on weekday whether little or much — if so the field on prohibition days belongs entirely to the idolater and Israel has no share, and so the field on permitted days belongs entirely to Israel; but when they conditioned that they calculate afterward — then it is like exchanging orlah fruits and is not considered a valid condition. According to this, Tosafot's and Ran's question is also resolved — why does the Gemara say there "if they came to accounting it is forbidden" — stam it should be permitted; perhaps to inform us that if they came to accounting, even if they conditioned, it is forbidden; certainly per their understanding that "if they came to accounting" means after time it is difficult; but per the Rambam's explanation nothing is difficult, for one cannot say it informs us that even if they conditioned it is forbidden — since when they conditioned that they come to accounting, from the outset the condition is void — examine.
