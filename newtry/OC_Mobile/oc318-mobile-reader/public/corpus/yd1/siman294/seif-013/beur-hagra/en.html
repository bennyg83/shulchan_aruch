A non-Jew, etc. This is Rambam’s wording.
Unless, etc. He rules stringently in the case of doubt, as explained in Orach Chayim, siman 245.
And if they stipulated, etc. ; according to his explanation from what he wrote: Rav Geviha, etc., and permitted them. Meaning: through stipulation from the outset; for regarding Shabbat one could say Rava did not hear the braita and erred, thinking a gentile acts for himself; unlike orlah, where it is impossible to err to permit exchanging orlah ab initio except through prior stipulation; and so he wrote “and he challenged him,” etc.
Provided that, etc. He explains “and if they came,” etc.; it refers even if they stipulated; and as written in Shabbat likewise, as written in Orach Chaim there; and from what he wrote “I would say the conclusion,” etc.; and Tosafot s.v. vaim, etc.; Ran explains that unspecified forbidden is a greater stringency than “they came to account” even through stipulation, and nevertheless according to Ran.
And some explain, etc. This follows Rashi there, s.v. “He permitted,” etc.
