Against those fruits. This is the Rambam's view; and there it is written here in this language — if they conditioned thus it is forbidden; and the Maharal was precise that only when they conditioned from the outset also on this that they come to accounting then it is forbidden; but if they did not condition from the outset, only the idolater eats three years of orlah and Israel three of heter — by law there is no accounting; therefore even if they came to accounting there is no prohibition, for the condition is he waives correspondingly that Israel eat against fruits the idolater ate in orlah years — end quote. In my humble opinion this is forbidden, for since they divide fruits together explicitly it shall be from whatever side — nevertheless if the idolater did not eat forbidden fruits he would not agree to give him so much heter fruit; since he says so explicitly it is like exchanging fruits; and the Rambam when he wrote "if they conditioned thus" is for a stringency — that even though at the outset he said conditioning from the outset helps, nevertheless such a condition does not help even from the outset, and all the more without prior conditioning; and so is the Rambam's intent, for in chapter 6 of Hilchot Shabbat he also wrote these laws and there only wrote: if they conditioned from the outset that Shabbat wages be for the idolater alone whether little or much and one day's wage against it for Israel alone — permitted — end quote. And if it were that there is permission when they come to accounting without prior conditioning, he should have informed us of this also for Shabbat; rather plainly the Rambam wrote here for a stringency, and also in Hilchot Shabbat teaches this stringency — the first condition must be in this manner that it not be by wage accounting whether much or little; if he did not do so his prior condition is ineffective; and all the more if he did not condition that afterward it is forbidden to do by accounting; and so the Tur explicitly: even if they conditioned from the outset, if they came to accounting — forbidden; and so Shulchan Aruch here wrote "provided they do not come to accounting" — implies in every manner; and so is primary both here and for Shabbat as appears to me.
