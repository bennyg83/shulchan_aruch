If they stipulated, etc. This is comparable to the arrangement concerning Shabbos, as explained at the beginning of the laws of Shabbos.
Corresponding to those fruits. The Rambam forbids an accounting arrangement in which the Israelite later receives permitted fruit corresponding to the non-Jew's orlah fruit. My revered father-in-law suggested that this is forbidden only when they expressly stipulated from the outset that an accounting would be made. I disagree. Whenever they divide the produce by calculating that the Israelite receives additional permitted fruit because the non-Jew consumed the orlah fruit, the Israelite is effectively exchanging permitted fruit for forbidden fruit. The Rambam's phrase, “if they stipulated this,” teaches that even an initial condition is ineffective when based upon such an accounting. The Rambam's parallel ruling concerning Shabbos likewise permits only an arrangement in which each partner receives the entire profit of his assigned day, whether large or small, without balancing the amounts afterward. If they later make an accounting, it is forbidden whether or not they stipulated initially. This is also the plain implication of the Tur and Shulchan Aruch.
And there are those who permit, etc. Regarding Shabbos, the non-Jew would otherwise act as the Israelite's agent in forbidden labor. Here, however, the agricultural work itself is permitted, and the non-Jew consumes the fruit because the agreement assigns him the produce of the years in which he works. The Israelite is not thereby regarded as selling orlah fruit. Rashi.
