And Rosh's view: even if a handbreadth remains, obligated; and from a handbreadth and above, exempt; and the Rav omitted his view, it seems because he holds Rosh's proof that he ruled like Ra'avyah because R' Shimon holds accordingly is not proof, for in Yerushalmi first chapter of Shevi'it they said Ra'avyah and R' Shimon said one thing; if so it is one view; and so Rabbeinu Yerucham part 1 chapter 2 brought Rambam's and Rosh's views and ruled like Rambam for this reason; but requires study, for behold Rosh himself brought that Yerushalmi and nonetheless ruled there like Ra'avyah since R' Shimon holds accordingly, see there. Rather Rosh's view seems correct: we do not say every such case is one view, except where halacha was not decided plainly like one of them or the sugya does not follow one of them; but where halacha was decided like one or the sugya follows one, if so when we say so-and-so and so-and-so said one thing, it comes to teach halacha is also like the other so-and-so regarding whom halacha was not stated accordingly; and therefore here since the sugya in Tosefta and many places follows R' Shimon, and so Rambam in explaining the mishnah first chapter of Shevi'it, and halacha is like R' Shimon because Tosefta has statements agreeing with his words, and so Bertinoro: halacha like R' Shimon; and so Agudah wrote both regarding plowing and orlah, if so when Yerushalmi says R' Shimon and Ra'avyah said one thing, necessarily it comes to teach halacha is like Ra'avyah because R' Shimon holds accordingly; or one could say we do not say every such case is one view except in Bavli, not in Yerushalmi; and on the contrary Rambam is difficult: in explaining the mishnah he ruled like R' Shimon, and so in his work chapter 3 of Hilchot Shemittah law 8 and chapter 10 of Hilchot Ma'aser Sheni law 13 he ruled like the sages against Ra'avyah; and explicitly Yerushalmi says R' Shimon and Ra'avyah said one thing (however what Kesef Mishneh challenged chapter 3 of Hilchot Shemittah: why rule like R' Shimon since sages dispute him, no difficulty, for as he wrote in explaining the mishnah because Tosefta has statements agreeing with his words), requires study.
