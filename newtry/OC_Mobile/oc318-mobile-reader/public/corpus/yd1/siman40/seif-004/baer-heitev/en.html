And Shach wrote that all the poskim elaborated on explaining this hagahah, and for practical law Bach concludes we are concerned for all the explanations stringently — that is: when found inside the heart — treifah, whether in the pouch toward the head of the animal or in the pouch toward the cavity of the animal; and when found in the large bronchus with the pouch toward the head of the animal — also treifah and not kosher, except where found in the large bronchus with the pouch toward the cavity of the animal — and specifically when there is no proof that it was already inside and returned backward; but if there is proof that it returned backward — we are concerned lest perhaps upon its return it punctured the heart or the heart canal and it is not recognizable. And if a bone is found inside the heart — I accept that there was an incident in Ashkenaz in an ox and the sages of the generation declared it kosher; their reason: because such is found in a deer's heart when the blood hardens it becomes bone, and it is possible that sometimes such is also found in an animal — see siman 41 end of his words. And Taz disagrees on this (and so Darkei Moshe); and Pri Chadash wrote: a bone all whose sides are thick and have no sharp point on any side — one may declare kosher; and he also wrote there is a species of oxen where such is innate by nature — see his words. A needle found stuck in the heart: if there is a recognizable sign outside on the wall of the ribs that it is known the needle came toward the face — one should investigate: if it is stuck in part of the wall still and did not pass into the cavity of the heart with an open puncture — even pouch facing outside — kosher, even with a thin needle — Maharshal in name of Mordechai. And he wrote: in a deer's heart itself it should inherently be kosher if the bone is placed in a matter where with a needle there would be prohibition on account of its end puncturing — what difference to me needle or bone; and even if you say in a deer it is kosher — from where do we learn to compare animal to deer, for one does not learn one species from what is not its species, and one should not be lenient in a matter that has no basis from early poskim — end of his words. And see in Sefer Nekudot HaKesef. And if there are two hearts — there was an incident and they declared treifah — Darkei Moshe daf 100b. Incident: one hen opened a chicken and a cat stood by her; she said, "The hen did not find a heart in the chicken." The owner said perhaps it was thrown to the ground and the cat ate it; the Gaon Chafetz Chaim in his responsa siman 74 permitted, reasoning that no creature in the world can live even one hour without a heart — see there where he elaborated.
