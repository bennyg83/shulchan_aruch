**(there seif 5) And you might wonder he is against Shas Chullin.** Per my view no difficulty—for initially they challenged R. Nachman and R. Akiva and R. Yitzchak who say person does not forbid what is not his from libation; and they answered it deals with partnership. And afterward they said R. Nachman and R. Akiva say even per view person forbids what is not his—difference gentile, but Israel “to pain him.” And thereby even per view person does not forbid what is not his—the reasoning “to pain him” applies where there is relevance—for regarding reasoning of “to pain him” they do not dispute. Therefore they challenged from libation—and this is for R. Nachman who holds person does not forbid what is not his; and he also holds reasoning of “to pain him”; therefore even though he has partnership inside—sufficient for issur of what is not his; but nevertheless we say “to pain him.” And in this no place Semag's reasoning that if he acted to pain he should have separated wine first and saved his own—for since person does not forbid what is not his, if they divide partnership he will not forbid and he had no ability to pain friend by forbidding wine through maintaining partnership; but for law we hold person does not forbid what is not his—Semag's reasoning stands correctly; think.
