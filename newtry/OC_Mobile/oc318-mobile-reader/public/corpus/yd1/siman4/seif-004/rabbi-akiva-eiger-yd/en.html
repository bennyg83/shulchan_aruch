**(Taz seif 4) And you might wonder on Beit Yosef.** Apparently wonder on his wonder on Ran—not so, only regarding even if he does not forbid what is not his—nevertheless forbidden in eating, for he did not forbid but did not permit; but per Shulchan Aruch ruling person forbids what is not his except we say “to pain him”—Ran's reasoning does not apply—for he said he did not slaughter at all for idol worship and thereby also permitted in eating; see nearby Shach seif 5.
**(there seif 5) And difficulty—if so even without intent.** This is Tosafot's difficulty Chullin (14) on that which we answer “owner's portion” in Israelite mumar—for otherwise forbidden because of slaughter of mumar {Taz cited this difficulty also for resolution “they warned him”; see Beit Yosef what Rambam holds change in answer in mumar is strained—if so otherwise forbidden because of slaughter of mumar; strain to say mishnah when he was not mumar from outset but became mumar in that slaughter, e.g. warned and accepted warning, etc.—appears Beit Yosef holds through warning he becomes mumar even without slaughtering for idol worship but called mumar in that slaughter since warning through slaughter; and reasoning is strained: if he accepted warning alone without act he is called mumar—automatically before slaughter—and tzarich iyun}; and they proved from this not mumar in one time; and if Maharshal's intent to challenge other poskim who hold mumar in one time—how will they answer Tosafot's difficulty—his answer did not help—for nevertheless sugya not resolved thus—for mishnah teaching one slaughtering for one of all these invalid—refers to reisha teaching for mountains invalid in eating permitted in benefit—and difficulty remains it should emerge because of slaughter of mumar.
**(there seif 5) And you might wonder he is against Shas Chullin.** Per my view no difficulty—for initially they challenged R. Nachman and R. Akiva and R. Yitzchak who say person does not forbid what is not his from libation; and they answered it deals with partnership. And afterward they said R. Nachman and R. Akiva say even per view person forbids what is not his—difference gentile, but Israel “to pain him.” And thereby even per view person does not forbid what is not his—the reasoning “to pain him” applies where there is relevance—for regarding reasoning of “to pain him” they do not dispute. Therefore they challenged from libation—and this is for R. Nachman who holds person does not forbid what is not his; and he also holds reasoning of “to pain him”; therefore even though he has partnership inside—sufficient for issur of what is not his; but nevertheless we say “to pain him.” And in this no place Semag's reasoning that if he acted to pain he should have separated wine first and saved his own—for since person does not forbid what is not his, if they divide partnership he will not forbid and he had no ability to pain friend by forbidding wine through maintaining partnership; but for law we hold person does not forbid what is not his—Semag's reasoning stands correctly; think.
**(in gloss) That Tur and Mechaber hold like Ran.** And that when not partner he does not forbid—meaning not that halachah is like Ran; rather as branch: since for Rambam and Rashi not so regarding partnership—“to pain him” does not apply; therefore he holds ruling combining Ran's reasoning forbidden in eating—therefore ruled so regarding partnership by combining reasonings.
