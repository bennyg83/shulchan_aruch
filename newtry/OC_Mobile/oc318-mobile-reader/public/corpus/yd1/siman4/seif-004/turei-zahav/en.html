**He did not forbid it.** Ran wrote: there is an opinion that regarding eating it is forbidden—even slaughtering friend's animal for mountains, etc.—for regarding issur of eating we cannot say a person does not forbid what is not his, for here he did not forbid it for her but did not permit it, and it is as if it did not slaughter but died by itself—end quote. And you might wonder on Beit Yosef and Rama who did not bring this—and Rashal chapter 2 Chullin siman 22 brought it; and Maharach challenged Ran: if so why does Gemara challenge those who hold a person does not forbid, etc., from two holding one knife and one slaughtered for mountains, etc., that his slaughter is invalid—for apparently he forbids what is not his—what difficulty; we taught invalid but not forbidden in benefit, etc.; and he answered with strain—and in passing he did not examine this—for the main reason of eating issur for Ran is because his act of shechitah was removed and it is as if he did nothing to it—and this applies only to individual slaughterer, not two holding knife, etc.; even if you say that one's slaughter for idol worship was removed and as if he were not here, nevertheless there is no nevelah here, for the second slaughtered for permitted matter; rather certainly this one who slaughtered for mountains forbids the shechitah, and if so a person forbids what is not his—and this is straightforward.
**And if they warned him.** Tur wrote he certainly intended to forbid, etc. And difficulty: if so even without intent to forbid it is forbidden like all slaughter of mumar. One may say: here it comes to forbid in benefit, and this is not in plain slaughter of mumar.
