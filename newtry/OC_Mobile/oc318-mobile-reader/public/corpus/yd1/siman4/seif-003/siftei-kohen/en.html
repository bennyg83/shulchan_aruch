**And if Israel, etc.** This law Rashba learned in Terumat HaDeshen from that which was difficult for him: let it emerge that slaughter of mumar, etc.; and for poskim who hold one does not become mumar in one time—tzarich iyun; and difficulty below siman 119 Beit Yosef brings Rashba's responsum itself: regarding chillul Shabbat and idol worship specifically when he was established three times, etc.; and also for poskim (above siman 2 seif 17) that in that shechitah he did not become mumar—tzarich iyun; and Rashba himself concludes there it is not proven from here anything—that it deals with one who worships at completion of slaughter; and so Ra'ah in Beit HaBechirah 9 permits even in eating; and so appears Tur Chayyim in chapter HaShochet; and so is reasonable—therefore one should permit at least in benefit; and also Rashba himself there and briefly appears he did not forbid here except in eating—see there. That which Shas brings proof from baraita teaching sacrifices of the dead—means: as there when he slaughtered to throw himself it is sacrifices of the dead, so here when he slaughtered for gentile to throw—it is invalid; think.
**Even if gentile thought for idol worship—it is kosher.** Even for eating; and implies even if slaughterer heard gentile thought—we do not say “this one thinks and this one acts”; and so it appears to me from Shas in chapter HaShochet (Chullin 39b) regarding the incident of those Arabs—see there; and Bach in siman 2 and in this siman forbids in eating; and per my view as I wrote: when gentile or Israelite heretic slaughters for idol worship—then forbidden in benefit, for stam machshavah toward idol worship; but gentile or Israelite mumar not attached to idol worship—if we hear they thought—forbidden in benefit; if not—permitted in benefit, for with those we do not say stam machshavah toward idol worship; so is in Shas and poskim.
