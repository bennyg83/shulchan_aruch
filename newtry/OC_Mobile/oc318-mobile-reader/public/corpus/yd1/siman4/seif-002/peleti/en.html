Slaughtered stam: Shach wrote appears from Tur's words if he slaughtered explicitly for mitzvah of shechitah—even if afterward threw or burned for idol worship he does not disqualify—Shach; and truly so appears Tur's language “slaughtered stam”—implies if slaughtered explicitly for mitzvah of shechitah he does not disqualify. But I did not understand what of it—for he is thinking to throw or threw for idol worship; if so we see his intent was not to slaughter for idol worship but to throw was then his intent—and “his end proved his beginning”; if so what helps that he slaughtered explicitly for shechitah to nullify thought at end if his thought from beginning was so—tzarich iyun apparently. What appears: Tur's view is like Tosafot in several places that thought of pigul is not mere thought but requires full speech—see Tosafot end chapter HaMafkid and Asifat Zekenim; I expanded in Magen Avraham. At any rate Tosafot's view requires speech; therefore one thinking at shechitah to throw blood for idol worship also needs speech—not sufficient in thought alone, for we derive thought from avodah to avodah outside from inside—as inside requires speech, so outside requires speech; but this is thought from avodah to avodah. But slaughterer thinking at shechitah he slaughters animal for idol worship—this is essence of shechitah for idol worship; here all agree no speech needed, for this is full act of slaughtering for idol worship; we do not derive outside from inside at all, and no need—for this is essence of idol-worship avodah; see Gemara 39a “except slaughter of idol worship,” etc.; see Tosafot there—straightforward and clear. If so difficulty: we say if he thought at time of throwing “his end proved his beginning” that from beginning intent was so—what of it that intent was so; although he thought explicitly—mere thought without speech does not help; and shall we say “his end proved his beginning” from speech at beginning that he did not speak at all and “words not heard, voice not heard”? Certainly not, and not comparable to Rashbag's incident where he ruled “his end proved his beginning”—they said his thought was from beginning so he said; but to say speech at beginning is not reasonable at all. Must say when we say “his end proved his beginning” the intent is that from beginning his intent while slaughtering in his mind was to slaughter for idol worship—in this all agree only thought needed, no speech as above; not that intent was to throw for idol worship—for that thought alone does not help. If so if he slaughtered explicitly for mitzvah of shechitah—the concern he intended to slaughter for idol worship and throw for idol worship does not apply—for that does not forbid by thought alone. Per this, per many authors even for pigul thought alone suffices; and so appears Rambam chapter 4 Hilchos Pesulei haMukdashim: “thought does not follow except after doer—even if we heard owners made pigul,” etc.—from outside law is likewise; one may question Tur's ruling as I wrote—what of it he slaughtered for mitzvah of shechitah—perhaps then his thought was on throwing as above; if so one should be stringent if this law itself is not general stringency that we hold like Rabbanan we do not say “his end proved his beginning”—tzarich iyun.
This is doubtful, etc.: see Taz seif 5 who brought in name of Rashal one should permit in benefit like Rabbenu Yerucham; Beit Yosef forbids in benefit following Rambam who rules like Rashbag that we say “his end proved his beginning.” Per my view even for Rambam no benefit issur here—for cannot escape doubt; if so establish presumption—in eating doubt in shechitah: if there is idol-worship thought here it is slaughter of sacrifices of the dead, and every doubt in shechitah we are stringent—for animal stands in presumption of issur; but for benefit animal stands in presumption of heter; if doubt we say establish presumption and it stands in presumption of heter. Rabbenu Yerucham's words are correct and one should not nitpick that in Gemara they gave reason Rashbag says “his end proved his beginning” because he shouted from beginning thinking he would not get it—this does not apply here; if so why was he silent and did not think immediately—see Chullin Tosafot s.v. ki lo, etc.; and see Choshen Mishpat siman 145 Mechaber distinguishes between gift of Shmuel and clear gift—appears also from this reason that with Shmuel “it did not come to his hand” does not apply for all know Shmuel's words as if it came to his hand. Must say this is Rashbag's reasoning doing—he thought certainly, not doubt; we say certainly he protested only he was silent for reason above; but where that reason does not apply—it is doubt. Even here Rashbag admits it is only doubtful sacrifices of the dead, not certain; if so even for Rashbag doubt plainly one may be lenient. I am doubtful: if no witnesses that he thought for throwing—only he says he thought so and says he did not think so at shechitah—if not believed in miggo “I did not think at all”; or say since “his end proved his beginning” it is like miggo in place of presumption—tzarich iyun, for I did not find this from authors.
