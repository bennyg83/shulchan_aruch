**This is doubtful sacrifices of the dead.** Reason: doubt whether we say “his end proved his beginning”—that from beginning too he slaughtered for idol worship; therefore they said in Gemara in this case there was an incident in Caesarea and they said neither issur nor heter; and Rashal chapter 2 Chullin siman 20 wrote: since Rabbanan who disagree with Rashbag hold we do not say “his end proved his beginning,” and we hold like Rabbanan—if so that which they did not say here heter is out of honor for Rashbag; perforce it suffices that we not permit in eating, but in benefit permitted, as Rabbenu Yerucham wrote—and not like Beit Yosef who disagrees and holds even in benefit forbidden, for he follows Rambam who holds there is doubt whether halachah is like Rabbanan or Rashbag, as in Choshen Mishpat siman 245 seif 11 regarding zechiyah through another, etc.; but other poskim ruled like Rabbanan—and therefore wonder on Tur who ruled here there is doubt whether we say “his end proved his beginning,” and in Choshen Mishpat siman 245 brings Ra'osh's view that we do not say “his end proved his beginning,” etc.; and Rashal wrote further in name of Ran and Tosafot that throwing and burning that disqualify for idol worship—specifically when he thought at time of shechitah to throw blood for idol worship; but if slaughtered properly afterward the animal is not disqualified even if he threw blood or burned fat for idol worship—even though in kodshim animal is disqualified in four avodot, for there each renders offering fit; but chullin—all permitting depends only on shechitah—once slaughtered properly it cannot be disqualified; and this is not like Rashi's view—end quote. And he explained this matter: if another slaughtered and owner came afterward and threw blood for idol worship—it is permitted; and so Rashal there; but regarding himself certainly in every case there is concern “his end proved his beginning,” even if he said explicitly at first he slaughters as God commanded—nevertheless in his mind this is the mitzvah per his error, and if so certainly forbidden also in this as above. And Maharach wrote we do not say “his end proved his beginning”—and per my view I wrote.
