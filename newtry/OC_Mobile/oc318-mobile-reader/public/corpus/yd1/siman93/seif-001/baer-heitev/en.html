The pot. Shach wrote: and there is no distinction between a metal pot or earthenware pot — always one must estimate against the entire pot; and Ba'er Heitev follows his reasoning that he ruled we do not practice relying on a gentile's tasting, therefore he wrote one must estimate against the entire pot; and even though it implies in many places that what is inside the vessel is not sixty against the entire vessel, nevertheless we sometimes find the pot's width is so great that it holds water that would be sixty against it — such as when its copper is thin and its width great; and nevertheless that which requires estimating against the entire pot is in an old pot where we do not know how much it absorbed; but if we know how much it absorbed one need not estimate but only against what is absorbed in it, as above siman 92 seif 5 in the gloss and siman 94 seif 6; and likewise even if we do not know how much it absorbed but it is known how much one cooked in it within me'eit l'eit from cooking milk this time — that which one cooked in it before me'eit l'eit is noten ta'am li'fegam — even if one cooked meat in it within me'eit l'eit from the first meat cooking one does not need sixty but only against the second meat (and one may also say that here he says one must estimate against the entire pot and therefore the cooked dish is forbidden) end of his words.
To cook. Shach wrote: however if one cooked meat in it within me'eit l'eit before cooking milk in it — it is obvious the cooked dish is forbidden; but if one cooked milk in it — it is obvious it is permitted b'dieved; but other things that are neither of meat nor of milk — such as vegetables and fish and the like — it is permitted to cook even l'chatchila; and that which in siman 94 seif 5 Ba'er Heitev wrote they practice to forbid the vessel that is not ben yomo (even for other things and even if only a ben yomo spoon was inserted in it — all the more here where meat was actually cooked in it) — one must say that here the matter is stated plainly for the law.
On a cover. Shach wrote in name of Maharshal: the reason they are stringent with a cover is because they are short at the top and have a kind of hollow, and then it is impossible to wipe them well that they be clean of the steam and boiling that rose there; and even though the short place is a small matter, nevertheless meat steam rises upward up to sixty against it until what is in the cover becomes like nevelah in substance, and afterward the cover becomes nevelah — for perhaps there is not in it sixty times sixty-one slightly less against what is in substance; but in those that are wide at the top and the hand can control them to wipe well — one does not say it is forbidden in substance and permitted; and Taz wrote: it appears to me to practice thus — if there is in the cooked dish sixty against the short place on the cover then there is no concern of prohibition on the cover more than on the pot; and Bach wrote the reason is that boiling always rises there and sticks greatly and it is impossible to wipe it well; and Taz challenged him that there is no place for this stringency, and whoever offers reasons to be stringent in this what is not found in Talmud and poskim — the proof is upon him.
To sweat. Shach wrote: and it appears the hand must be yad soledes bo in the sweat.
Everything. Darchei Moshe wrote: even the pot itself is permitted l'chatchila when one wipes or rinses above where it touched the cover; and even though the moisture was hot, nevertheless it is permitted because what separated from the cover is like a kli sheni; and one might say for iruy that a single drop — the force of its heat is nullified and it is not like a kli rishon, end of his words; and requires further study regarding what he permits also the pot — behold the cover itself with hot moisture touched the pot; however all this is when there is moisture on the cover, but if dry it is obvious that for all opinions everything is permitted; however from the presumption we say there is moisture in it (see Shach).
Food. One must say his explanation refers to above where Rama wrote: if one placed a meat cover on a milk pot and both are hot and both have food — both are forbidden; on this he writes here that if there was no food in both — even though they are hot, both are permitted, for it is like two pots, etc.; and I saw in Taz who elaborates here if milk stood cold me'eit l'eit in the pot and afterward meat was cooked in it — it is forbidden by force of kavush k'mevushal even in an old pot, and we do not say seven for absorption and it did not absorb from milk at all, see there where he brings many proofs for his words; but what he wrote that if one placed permitted matter in a forbidden vessel me'eit l'eit while cold it is forbidden — it does not appear to me, for after me'eit l'eit it is noten ta'am li'fegam; and see above siman 105 (and in a new earthenware pot where milk stood all night meat is forbidden, for a new pot absorbs even when cold — Taz s.k. 15).
