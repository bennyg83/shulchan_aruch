This mortgage. This means the mortgage of Sura mentioned in the Shulchan Aruch, seif 1. The permission in this case applies when the lender has already taken possession of the field, as stated in siman 164. Any other mortgage is forbidden according to all opinions, as stated in siman 164. The Tur’s conclusion here follows the opinion of the Ramah that with other mortgages it constitutes fixed interest if, at the time of the mortgage, he stipulated that the lender would later sell the field back to him in exchange for its produce, or rent it to him for money. This applies even if the lender rented it to another person and that person rented it to the borrower, whenever it was stipulated initially that this would be done. This is unlike the Rosh, who holds that even with such a stipulation it is not fixed interest unless the lender says, ‘As compensation for waiting for your money, I will return and rent it to you.’ Rather, whenever it was initially mentioned that the lender would later become obligated to return it and rent it to him, it is fixed interest. The Beit Yosef wrote as follows in the name of Rabbeinu Yerucham: In those places where money is advanced against houses as a mortgage, and the owner of the house continues to live in it as his own, while the lender never takes possession of the house, and another person obligates himself to pay the lender a fixed amount each year, my teacher, R’ Avraham ben Yishmael, wrote that if the person who obligates himself does so of his own accord, it is permitted, like the case where one says, ‘Here are 4 zuz; lend 100 zuz to so-and-so.’ If, however, he acts as the borrower’s agent, it is forbidden, and according to all opinions it is fixed interest. End quote from the Beit Yosef. It is clear that the ruling mentioned by the Shulchan Aruch, that if another person rented the property from the lender the borrower may rent it from that person, is unrelated to Rabbeinu Yerucham’s ruling. The law concerning another person is permitted only in a mortgage of Sura, as we have stated, and as the Shulchan Aruch explicitly wrote in siman 164, seif 2. In any other mortgage it is forbidden even through the interposition of another person, because it appears like interest. This is unlike a mortgage of Sura, which is regarded as an outright sale. Rabbeinu Yerucham’s ruling, however, is permitted even with other mortgages, for there is no interest here at all. Even with an ordinary loan it is permitted to say, ‘Here are 4 zuz; lend to so-and-so,’ as stated in siman 160, seif 13. The Tur recorded a third case that was practiced in his time, where another person rented the property with the intention that the borrower would live there. Therefore, he wrote that Reuven remained in the house, meaning that he did not leave it even at the time when the other person rented it, and the lender knew that the other person was acting for this purpose. Regarding this, he concludes in the name of the Ramah that it is fixed interest, as stated above. I have written this because the Levush wrote: If another person rented it from the lender, for example, Reuven mortgaged his house to Shimon, Levi rented it from Shimon, and Reuven remained in the house and paid the rent to Levi, while Levi paid the rent to Shimon, this is permitted. Levi, in relation to Shimon, is like one who says, ‘Here are 4 zuz; lend 100 zuz to Reuven,’ since the payment does not pass directly from the borrower to the lender. Although in siman 160 we say that the borrower must not induce him with his own money, that applies only to fixed interest. A mortgage such as this is like a purchase or a lease of land and involves only the dust of interest; therefore, we are not concerned. End quote. His words imply that he permits this even when Levi rents it for Reuven, for he compares it to one who says, ‘Here are 4 zuz,’ etc., and he also raises the difficulty afterward from siman 160. I say, with all due respect to him, that here he has permitted an explicit and complete prohibition. This precise case was described by the Tur as practiced in his time. The Rosh wrote that it is the dust of interest, while the Tur and the Ramah disagreed and ruled that it is fixed interest when it was stipulated initially. Here it was stipulated initially, for he compared it to one who says, ‘Here are 4 zuz; lend 100 zuz to so-and-so.’ Even without an initial stipulation, it is nevertheless the dust of interest according to all opinions. It is forbidden even with a mortgage of Sura when Reuven remains in the house and the lender has not taken possession of it, as stated in siman 164, where actual possession is specifically required. The Shulchan Aruch permitted the case of another person renting it only where that person rented it for himself and the borrower subsequently rented it from him. Even this is permitted only with a mortgage of Sura. The ruling mentioned by the Levush bears no resemblance to the case of one who says, ‘Here are 4 zuz,’ etc., which the Beit Yosef cited in the name of Rabbeinu Yerucham. According to the Tur’s conclusion, the Levush’s case is fixed interest. Nowhere do we find that something forbidden when done by the lender himself becomes permitted through another person, except in the case in siman 160 of one who says, ‘Here are 4 zuz,’ etc., and that is specifically where the borrower did not induce him to do so. This is Rabbeinu Yerucham’s case. If the borrower induced him, it is obvious that it is forbidden in every form. This ruling should be forgotten and not stated.
