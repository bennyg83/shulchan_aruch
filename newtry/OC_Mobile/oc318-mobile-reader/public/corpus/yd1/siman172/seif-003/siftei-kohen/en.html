There is one who forbids. That is Ramban, whom the Maggid Mishneh brought, who wrote the reason Suran mortgage is permitted is that if a river sweeps it away and such — he takes nothing and cannot collect from other assets; hence if the borrower accepted responsibility — it is forbidden. But according to poskim who wrote the reason is because he wrote thus in the document, or because the lender has no power to collect his debt, as above s.k. 1 — it is permitted.
It is not redeemable etc. So it is in all books and it is a scribal error; and likewise in Pri Megadim — it is redeemable by halves, as in Rashba's responsum; and what Mechaber wrote in CM siman 74 seif 4 that if he pledged his field or even two fields he cannot coerce him to return half the collaterals — that means the borrower can eat the produce until he pays all; know that Mechaber's words are Ramban's words that Beit HaTalmud brought in chapter 69, which wrote there: what they said — the borrower redeems in halves — means he redeems half, only the buyer eats the whole field, etc.; and likewise R. Yerucham 12 in the name of Ramban and Rashba in responsum: redeems in halves and the lender perforce receives the half, and nevertheless the lender eats all the produce, etc.; and Beit Yosef brings it in CM there.
In a prior debt. Even with nekiya — forbidden; for by what does he acquire coins — there is no coin here, there is no ingot here — there.
We follow the custom. And in a place where the custom is that if he does not redeem it by a known time — such as at the beginning of each year or fifteen days before the year ends — he can no longer redeem it that year: the Raavad wrote this is considered a place where they do not remove, since he cannot remove him every year after the time passes; and our master the Rosh wrote it is properly considered a place where they remove, since he can remove him at the start of the year — end of Tur; and Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Rashba, the Maggid Mishneh, and students of Rashba — like the Raavad.
And see Choshen Mishpat. In siman 67 seif 2 the law of shemitah is explained; in siman 175 seifim 57 and 58 the law of bar metzra is explained; and in siman 278 end of seif 3 the law of inheritance of a firstborn in a mortgage is explained, which the Tur wrote in this siman. But what is mentioned in the Gemara and Tur, that a mortgage in a place where the borrower can remove the lender, a creditor does not collect from it from the lender's heirs, is not mentioned in Tur Choshen Mishpat, and the Mechaber did not write it anywhere, because the reason is only that movables of orphans are not pledged to a creditor, as Rashi explained; that is according to Talmudic law. But nowadays one collects from movables, as Tur and Shulchan Aruch wrote in Choshen Mishpat at the beginning of siman 107; if so there is no practical difference in this.
