There is an authority who forbids. This is Ramban, cited by the Maggid Mishneh, who explained that a Sura mortgage is permitted because if a river sweeps the property away or the like, the lender receives nothing and cannot collect from the borrower’s other assets. It follows that if the borrower accepted responsibility, it is forbidden. According to the poskim who explain that the permission rests on the wording written in the document, or on the lender’s lack of power to collect the debt, as above in seif katan 1, it is permitted.
It is not redeemed, etc. This is the text in all the editions, but it is a scribal error. The Levush also has the correct reading, “it is redeemed in halves,” as stated in Rashba’s responsum. The Mechaber wrote in Choshen Mishpat, siman 74, seif 4, that if one pledged his field, or even two fields, the borrower cannot compel the lender to return half of the collateral. This means that the lender may continue consuming the produce until the entire debt is paid. This is clear because the Mechaber’s words are Ramban’s words cited by Beit HaTerumot, sha’ar 69: “When they said that the borrower may redeem in halves, they meant that he may redeem half, but the purchaser continues consuming the produce of the entire field,” etc. Rabbeinu Yerucham, netiv 12, likewise wrote in the name of Ramban, and Rashba wrote in a responsum, that he may redeem in halves and the lender must accept half the payment, yet the lender nevertheless consumes all the produce, etc. Beit Yosef cites this in Choshen Mishpat there.

We follow the custom. This requires study, because Beit HaTerumot wrote the opposite. Rashbatz likewise cited both views without deciding between them. Beit Yosef, however, wrote: “It appears to me that in a place where the only customary form is a Sura mortgage, an unspecified mortgage is presumed to be of that type and is permitted.” This applies specifically to a Sura mortgage, not to a mortgage with a deduction. As Beit HaTerumot wrote, it is enough that some authorities permit a deduction, and we should not extend the leniency further. Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe cite this. The matter requires study.
Likewise, if, etc. The Gemara states that the ordinary term of an unspecified mortgage is one year, so that if the borrower wishes to remove the lender during that year, he cannot do so. The Maggid Mishneh wrote in the name of Rashba that this applies in places without a contrary custom. If the local custom allows removal during the first year, the custom is followed. Beit Yosef cites this and writes that the Rambam’s words also appear to support it. Nimukei Yosef likewise wrote so, and this also appears to be the Rama’s view.
We follow the custom. In a place where the custom is that if the borrower does not redeem the property at a specified time, such as at the beginning of each year or fifteen days before the end of the year, he may no longer redeem it during that year, Raavad wrote that it is considered a place where the borrower may not remove the lender, since after the deadline passes he cannot remove him during that year. My master and father, the Rosh, wrote that it is properly considered a place where the borrower may remove the lender, since he may remove him at the beginning of the year. These are the Tur’s words. Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Rashba, the Maggid Mishneh, and Rashba’s students that the law follows Raavad.
See Choshen Mishpat. In siman 67, seif 2, the law of Shemittah is explained. In siman 175, seifim 57 and 58, the law of one whose property borders the field is explained. In siman 278, end of seif 3, the law of a firstborn’s inheritance in a mortgage, mentioned by the Tur in this siman, is explained. The Gemara and the Tur state that where the borrower may remove the lender, a creditor of the lender may not collect from the mortgaged property in the hands of the lender’s heirs. This was not mentioned by the Tur in Choshen Mishpat, nor did the Mechaber write it anywhere, because its entire basis is that movable property inherited by orphans was not subject to their father’s creditor, as Rashi explained. That was the law in the time of the Gemara. Nowadays, however, a creditor may collect from movable property, as the Tur and Shulchan Aruch wrote in Choshen Mishpat, beginning of siman 107. Consequently, this ruling has no present practical consequence.
