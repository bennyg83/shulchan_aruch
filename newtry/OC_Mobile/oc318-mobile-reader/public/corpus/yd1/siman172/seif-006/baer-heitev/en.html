When the tax. The Shach writes that the Rav's language is awkward here, for in the Baal HaTerumot, from whom the Rav derived this law, it is explained that when the tax is placed on the body of the land, even though the lender eats the produce, the borrower may pay the tax, since the land is his. But when the tax is placed on the produce, such as where the lord takes a tithe of the grain and the like, it is forbidden for the borrower to pay the lord unless he pays from produce that belongs to the lender. The Taz writes that this is the explanation of the Rav's words: if the tax is paid in money, it automatically relates to the body of the land, which belongs to the borrower, for he did not sell him through deduction except the produce that grows in it for a certain time; therefore the borrower is required by law to pay that tax. The rent. This means if the lender was living in it by deduction and the like, so that it was not even avak ribit but like a sale, according to the Rashba. The Taz writes that this requires study, for in the responsum of the Rashba it is mentioned that the borrower accepted responsibility, so why did the Rama not mention here that he accepted responsibility? Here one cannot say that responsibility is a scribal omission. Therefore he rules as halachah that if he did not explicitly accept responsibility, he pays him only the money he lent, without deduction, even though he was forced to pay rent; this is not a case of assumed responsibility. In Nekudot HaKesef he writes that his words are not compelling, for one can say that since the deduction is like a sale regarding the produce, it is like an actual sale and we say responsibility is an assumed scribal condition.
