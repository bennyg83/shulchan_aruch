When the tax. Shach explains that when the tax is imposed upon the land itself, the borrower may pay it even though the lender eats the produce, because the land remains the borrower's. If the tax is imposed upon the produce, such as a tithe taken from the crop, the borrower may not pay it unless payment comes from produce belonging to the lender. Taz understands Rama similarly where a monetary tax relates to ownership of the land.
The rent. This refers to a lender who lived there under a deduction arrangement, which is treated as a sale rather than even avak ribbit. Rashba. Taz notes that Rashba's responsum stated that the borrower accepted responsibility and therefore rules that without express responsibility the borrower need repay only the principal, without deduction, even if the lender was forced to pay rent. Nekudot HaKesef argues that because deduction is treated as a sale of the produce, ordinary sale responsibility may be presumed.
