One who gives his ruin, etc. See above, end of siman 166, gloss.
The borrower, etc. The wording of Rama is imprecise. What emerges from Baal HaTerumot in the name of Ramban is as follows: When the tax is imposed upon the land itself, even though the lender consumes the produce, the borrower may pay the tax, since the land remains his. But when the tax is imposed upon the produce, such as where Hashem takes a tithe of the grain or the like, it is forbidden for the borrower to pay it to Hashem unless he pays from produce belonging to the lender. Baal HaTerumot further writes that one may not lend another one hundred on condition that the borrower pay the tax from that sum. He may, however, say: 'I will lend you one hundred; if you profit with it, first pay the tax and the remainder shall be yours, and if you do not profit, I will pay from my own funds.' See there, and below, siman 177, seif 8.
He must return, etc., including the rent. This applies when the lender resided there under a deduction arrangement and the transaction did not involve even a mere appearance of interest, but was treated as a sale. Rashba.
