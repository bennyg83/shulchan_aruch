One who gives his ruined property, etc. See above, at the end of siman 166, in the gloss.
The borrower etc. The Rabbi’s language is unclear here; and what emerges from Beit HaTalmud in the name of Ramban is: when the tax is levied on the body of the land — even though the lender eats the produce — the borrower is permitted to pay the tax, for the land is his; but when the tax is levied on the produce — such as when the lord takes a tithe of grain and similar — it is forbidden for the borrower to pay the lord, unless he pays from produce that belongs to the lender; and Beit HaTalmud further wrote there that it is forbidden to lend his fellow a maneh on condition that he pays the tax from that maneh; but it is permitted to say to him "I will lend you a maneh, and if you profit from it you first pay the tax and the rest is yours, and if you do not profit I will pay from my own" — see there; and see below siman 177 seif 8.
He must return, etc., including the rent. This refers to a case in which the lender lived there with a deduction, in a manner that did not constitute even avak ribbit, but was regarded as a sale. Rashba.
