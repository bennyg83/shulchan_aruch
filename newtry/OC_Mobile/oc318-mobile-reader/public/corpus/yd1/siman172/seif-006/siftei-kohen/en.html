One who gives his ruined property, etc. See above, at the end of siman 166, in the gloss.
The borrower, and so forth. Rama's wording here is difficult. What emerges from Sefer HaTerumot in the name of Ramban is this: when the tax is imposed on the land itself, although the lender eats the produce, the borrower may pay the tax because the land remains his. But when the tax is imposed upon the produce, such as where the lord takes a tithe of the grain or the like, the borrower may not pay the lord unless he pays from produce belonging to the lender. Sefer HaTerumot further writes that one may not lend another person one maneh on condition that the borrower pay the tax from that maneh. But he may say, "I will lend you one maneh. If you profit from it, first pay the tax and the remainder will be yours; if you do not profit, I will pay the tax from my own funds." See there and below siman 177, seif 8.
He must return, etc., including the rent. This refers to a case in which the lender lived there with a deduction, in a manner that did not constitute even avak ribbit, but was regarded as a sale. Rashba.
