One who gives his ruined property, etc. See above, at the end of siman 166, in the gloss.
The borrower etc. The language of the Rav is stammering in this; and what emerges from Sefer HaTerumos in the name of the Ramban is that when the tax is imposed on the body of the land, even though the lender eats the produce, it is permitted for the borrower to pay the tax, for the land is his; but when the tax is imposed on the produce, such as where the master takes a tenth of the grain and the like, it is forbidden if the borrower paid the master, unless he pays from produce belonging to the lender; and Sefer HaTerumos wrote further there that it is forbidden to lend one's fellow a maneh on condition that he pay the tax from that maneh; but it is permitted to say to him: I will lend you a maneh, and if you profit from them you shall first pay the tax and the remainder shall be yours, and if you do not profit I will pay from my own; and see there, and see below siman 177 seif 8.
He must return, etc., including the rent. This refers to a case in which the lender lived there with a deduction, in a manner that did not constitute even avak ribbit, but was regarded as a sale. Rashba.
