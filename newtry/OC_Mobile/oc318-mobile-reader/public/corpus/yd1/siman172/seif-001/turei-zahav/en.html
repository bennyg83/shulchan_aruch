Like a mortgage of Sura, etc. However, an ordinary mortgage with a deduction, in the manner mentioned by the Rama in the gloss in the name of some authorities, is forbidden according to this opinion. The reason is stated by the Beit Yosef in the name of the Ramban: In a mortgage of Sura, responsibility does not rest upon the lender, and if a river floods it, he receives nothing other than the produce it yields. He cannot compel the borrower to repay his debt, neither from this property nor from the borrower’s other assets. This is not so with an ordinary mortgage, where responsibility rests upon him, and if he wishes he may demand payment of the debt from the borrower; if a river floods it, he may collect from the borrower’s other assets. End quote.
There is no distinction in this regard between a field and a house. This is the opinion of Rabbeinu Tam, in contrast to Rashi’s opinion cited by the Tur, who distinguishes between a house and a field. Regarding a field, a deduction is permitted because there can sometimes be a loss; regarding a house, however, the arrangement is close to profit and far from loss. Rabbeinu Tam holds that there can also be a loss regarding a house, for perhaps it will burn. The Tur wrote: Even according to Rabbeinu Tam, it appears to me that if the borrower writes for him a guarantee against his other assets, it is forbidden, for if the house collapses or burns he will immediately collect from the borrower’s other assets. End quote. He then cited a responsum of the Rosh concerning this, that it is not fixed interest, since he did not stipulate interest upon the loan, and also, if the house collapses or burns, Shimon will lose his money, etc. This reasoning is not clear, etc. It appears that the Rosh’s statement that Shimon will lose his money means the principal, for although he has a guarantee against the borrower’s other assets, it is nevertheless possible that they will all burn or be ruined and he will have nothing from which to collect. The Tur does not accept this concern. It appears possible to bring proof for the Rosh’s view from what we learned in the chapter Lo Yachpor, Bava Batra 20a, concerning one who is obligated to distance an oven from a wall, and if he causes damage he must pay for the damage. Rashi explains that if he causes damage after distancing it by the prescribed measure, he must pay; nevertheless, the neighbors may prevent him from placing it there, lest he set fire to their houses and then have nothing with which to pay. End quote. Here too, there is this concern, and his principal is therefore not secure. The Beit Yosef said that the Tur implies that he will lose his money, meaning the principal, and he challenged this: Why is the fact that he wrote a guarantee relevant? Even without it, he would not lose his money so long as the borrower did not write that he would have repayment only from this property, as stated in Choshen Mishpat, siman 117. My father-in-law, of blessed memory, challenged him from Choshen Mishpat, siman 72, where the principal is not lost when the collateral burns. According to what I have written, everything is resolved, for the Rosh does not refer to the legal rule that by law he will lose it, but to the lack of security, since he may find nothing from which to collect. This appears to me correct as practical halacha, that it is not fixed interest, in accordance with the Rosh’s opinion. The Beit Yosef also wrote that it appears to him that there are several difficulties and grounds for loss in this matter.
And some permit lending against sacred books, etc. This requires further study, for why did the Rama rely upon this opinion, when in Choshen Mishpat, siman 72, and in the Beit Yosef’s newly collected responsa, section 11, he cites a responsum of the Rashba that it is fixed interest? Likewise, the Mordechai at the end of the chapter Eilu Metzi’os forbids it without a deduction. At the very least, he should have cited, in the name of some authorities, the stringent view.
