The lender, etc. There are six views here. According to the Rif, as explained by the Raavad and other authorities, a mortgage without deduction is fixed interest, as stated there, 67a, regarding Ravina's act, and this applies all the more so to a mortgage, as stated there. With deduction it is also forbidden, following Rav Kahana, Rav Pappa, and Rav Ashi there on 67b, for they constitute the majority, and they did not act this way merely as a personal stringency, as is evident from the statement there, “but the one who forbids,” etc. Nevertheless, it is only rabbinic interest, as stated there on 67a, “Mar said in the name of Rav Yehudah,” etc., “if he consumed more,” etc.; and from the entire discussion there on 67b, including the statement, “Mar Zutra said in the name of Rav Pappa: this,” etc. It is obvious that this refers to a case with deduction and, after the fact, if he consumed the produce. Nothing is permitted except a Sura-type mortgage, as stated there on 67b. The Rosh and other authorities challenged the Rif from the statement there on 62a, “but a mortgage,” etc., which indicates that it is only rabbinic interest. It is likewise difficult to say that the entire discussion cited above, which speaks in terms of prohibition, addresses only the law after the fact. The Raavad therefore wrote that in a locale where the borrower may remove the lender, a mortgage without deduction is fixed interest, as stated there regarding Ravina's act, while with deduction it is rabbinic interest, as above, regarding “if he consumed more,” etc. In a locale where the borrower may not remove the lender, a mortgage without deduction is rabbinic interest, as in the case there on 62a, while with deduction it is permitted initially, as the discussion cited above addresses the initial law. Rav Kahana and the others were personally stringent, as stated there regarding a Torah scholar, etc., whereas Ravina holds that it is entirely permitted. See Tosafot there, s.v. “Ravina,” and Rabbenu Tam's explanation there. We rule like Ravina. The Raavad likewise explained the Rif's words in this manner. According to this, their only disagreement is whether the ruling follows Rav Ashi or Ravina. This cannot be correct, for if so, why did the Rif need to explain that the statement “if he consumed more,” etc., concerns a mortgage with deduction? He should have explained it as a mortgage without deduction in a locale where the borrower may not remove the lender. His unqualified wording also makes this clear. It appears to me that the reading on 62a, “Ravina said to him,” should be omitted, and that the Amora there agrees with Rabbah bar Rav Huna and with Rabbah on 67a that it is rabbinic interest. The Rambam, in the name of his teachers, also holds that locales where the borrower may or may not remove the lender are governed alike, but he distinguishes differently. Regarding a house, without deduction it is fixed interest, and with deduction it is rabbinic interest. Regarding a field, without deduction it is rabbinic interest, and with deduction it is permitted initially. This also resolves all the passages, in accordance with Rashi on 64b, s.v. “it teaches us,” etc. In the name of some Geonim, however, the Rambam wrote like the Rif, except that he wrote that every case without deduction is fixed interest. This accords with my explanation of the Rif, that there is no distinction at all between a locale where the borrower may remove the lender and one where he may not. This is the seventh view. Tosafot there, s.v. “and it is not,” disagreed with Rashi; see there. Their view, and Rashi's view, is that in a locale where the borrower may not remove the lender, even a mortgage without deduction is permitted initially. See Rashi there on 67b, s.v. “in a locale,” and Tosafot there, s.v. “when the term is completed.” In a locale where the borrower may remove the lender, a mortgage without deduction is rabbinic interest. Ravina's act described there is explained as a case of mistaken waiver, as Tosafot states there, s.v. “what of the produce?” With deduction, Ravina and Rav Ashi disagree, and the ruling follows Ravina; see Tosafot there, s.v. “Ravina.” Rashi's view is that all this applies to a field, but with a house, in a locale where the borrower may remove the lender, it is forbidden even with deduction, as above. In a locale where the borrower may not remove the lender, however, since it is permitted even without deduction, there is no distinction between a field and a house. The Rashba's view is that with deduction it is permitted even in a locale where the borrower may remove the lender. Without deduction, in such a locale it is fixed interest, while in a locale where the borrower may not remove the lender it is rabbinic interest. He also agrees with Tosafot that there is no distinction between a field and a house. See the Nimukei Yosef. The statement there, “one should not consume the produce of a mortgage in a locale where the borrower may remove the lender,” is expressed as a greater novelty. Some texts read, “in a locale where the borrower may not remove the lender, one should not consume,” etc., and they explain that the same applies in a locale where he may remove the lender. The Rashba brought proof that a mortgage with deduction is permitted even in a locale where the borrower may remove the lender from the statement on 68a, “or alternatively, when it reaches him,” etc. In a Sura-type mortgage, however, the borrower cannot remove the lender. The Rambam explains that passage as referring to a Sura-type mortgage in a locale where the borrower may remove the lender, in accordance with Tosafot on 67b, s.v. “when the term is completed.” This is the meaning of the ruling here, “the lender,” etc. It follows the Rif's view that only a Sura-type mortgage is permitted. This is also the view of Rabbenu Chananel, as Tosafot states there at the end of s.v. “Ravina.” The phrase “a house or a field,” etc., likewise follows the Rif's view that there is no distinction between a house and a field. This entire seif is the Rambam's language in the name of some Geonim, namely Rabbenu Chananel and the Rif.
Even if he enlarges, etc. This is like a case with deduction according to the authority who permits it, as stated there, where the law is derived from an ancestral field, even though the lender may consume a great amount of produce, etc.
And likewise if, etc. Meaning, even if the borrower may remove him, it is permitted, based on the passage in the chapter HaOmanin, as explained above, and in accordance with Tosafot there, s.v. “when the term is completed,” etc.
Since, etc. This nevertheless resolves the Raavad's objection to the Rambam, who permits it where the borrower may remove the lender. The Raavad asked how this differs from an ordinary mortgage with deduction, like Tosafot's question cited above. The answer is that although the borrower may remove the lender, the lender may not withdraw from the arrangement. He also wrote in the name of the Ramban that it is because the document is not phrased as a loan but as a sale, in accordance with Tosafot cited above. He further wrote that here the borrower bears no responsibility, and if a river floods the property, the lender has no claim against him. See seif 3.
And some say, etc. This is the view of Tosafot.
And some say, etc. This is the view of the Rashba.
And there is no distinction, etc. This accords with Tosafot and the Rashba.
Or other, etc. This is the Mordechai's view, unlike the Nimukei Yosef, who wrote in the name of Rabbenu Tam that the permission applies specifically to land, like an ancestral field.
And some permit, etc. As explained above in siman 160, seif 18. See the Shach.
And all this, etc. Meaning, this applies to a house, as Tosafot writes on 64b, s.v. “and it is not,” etc., in the name of Rabbenu Tam, unlike the present case. Although he also wrote, “or when he will not need it,” that is not the principal reason but merely an additional consideration. The principal reason is the first one. This is the ruling of the Darkei Moshe, unlike the Beit Yosef.
And in every place, etc. This is from the Gemara there on 67a.
Since, etc. Meaning, this applies specifically in such a case, as explained above in siman 166, seif 3, and as stated there in the gloss, “and the rationale,” etc.
However, etc. As explained above in siman 161, seif 4. The Mechaber omitted the entire discussion there on 67a and 67b because, according to the Rif's approach, it does not accord with the halachah, since nothing is permitted except a Sura-type mortgage. The Rama also omitted it. See the Nimukei Yosef there for his explanation of the discussion; according to his explanation as well, some of its rulings do not accord with the halachah. The Tur, however, records the entire discussion in accordance with Rashi's explanation.
