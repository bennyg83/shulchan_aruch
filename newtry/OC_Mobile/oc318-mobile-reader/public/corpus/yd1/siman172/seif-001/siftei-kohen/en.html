That after ten years. And the same for more; and see in the responsum of Maharam Elshaker siman 115 regarding the law of mortgage.
And likewise if he stipulated etc. That is to say: even if the borrower stipulated that he can remove him, etc. — and all the more if he did not stipulate — it is permitted.
From the field etc. That is to say — not only a field, which sometimes yields no produce and enters into doubt whether it will yield produce; but even a house and courtyard whose produce — meaning the dwelling — is regularly available, is also permitted; for sometimes too there is damage to the house, as below in the Hagahah.
He can remove him etc. See above s.k. 26 — what is called able to remove him.
Even a small amount. Even less than the measure of an ancestral field, which is for seeding a kor of barley — a sela and a pondion per year — Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe.
And some permit. See Maharil's responsum there — it implies even fixed ribbit has grounds to permit, comparing it to orphan funds; and according to what he concludes there — to forbid orphan funds as fixed ribbit — and so the Rabbi wrote above siman 160 seif 18 — the same applies here; and likewise Maharshal chapter HaHovel siman 72 — see there where he elaborated.
Except when the responsibility of the collaterals etc. This does not mean that if the collaterals are lost the borrower is not obligated to pay his debt — for it implies from the Maggid Mishneh in the name of Ramban that even if he collects from other assets it is permitted; rather it means to exclude when he writes responsibility on other assets, for then when the collateral is damaged he immediately has another collateral; but when he did not write responsibility on other assets — even though if the collaterals are damaged he collects from other assets, nevertheless since he does not immediately have another collateral — it is permitted; and so Tur implies; and Bach seif 12 wrote that even though he collects from other assets it is permitted (and per what is written there is no need to strain and interpret the word "immediately" that Tur wrote as Bach wrote; rather it means he should not seek other assets since immediately he has another collateral); and Pri Megadim did not understand so — see there where his words are confused.
But etc. For if the house falls or burns — immediately he repays from other assets — Tur; and even though one can say he will not need the house and will not find tenants, as Tosafot wrote — on that reason alone we do not permit, for that reason is only a branch to other reasons such as fire and falling — Darkei Moshe; and not like Beit Yosef.
The fruits he ate etc. And all the more if he ate more than the amount of the debt — we do not extract from him.
Since etc. That is to say — it is not comparable to above siman 167 seif 3, for here the mortgage is initially in the lender’s possession that he lent on it, as stated there in s.k. 11.
If he said etc. Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Rashba's students: some explain specifically before a court; and some explain even not before a court.
Even etc. It implies this means even where he is in a place they do not remove except until a set time — we deduct all that he ate before the removal time, per Ramban’s view that Beit HaTalmud and Rivash brought; and he wrote nevertheless it is impossible to remove entirely since he transferred it to him; rather he eats produce at assessed value and we deduct from him the full value of all produce as appraised by the court — end quote. And thereby it is resolved that the Mechaber’s words in CM siman 74 seif 3 — regarding a mortgage debt from which he eats produce in a place they do not remove: if he wishes he does not receive money before the time unless he lets him eat the produce — and the Rabbi wrote simply per his words — there it means he is obligated to let him eat at assessed value. However the Raavad’s view that Beit Yosef brought is that before the removal time he cannot protest and we do not deduct from him, since he is in a place they do not remove; and so is proven the Rashba’s view that the Nimukei Yosef brought there regarding “I will trouble and bring money”; and it seems to me this is a doubtful matter of law, and the burden of proof is on the claimant.
We deduct from him etc. For since he protests that he should not eat — it is robbery — Nimukei Yosef.
