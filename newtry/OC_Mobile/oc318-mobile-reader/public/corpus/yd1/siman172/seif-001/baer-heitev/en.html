Forbidden. The Taz writes that nevertheless it is only avak ribit. They deduct. Since this one protests that he should not consume, it is theft. Whether he must say this specifically in beit din or not, the Beit Yosef writes two views. It implies that specifically where there is avak ribit do we deduct for him when he protested, but where it is not even avak ribit, such as with deduction in a place where the borrower can remove him, since it is permitted, even if he protested we do not deduct until he brings the money. In a place where he cannot remove him, and there was deduction, and the time ended, and this one continues eating after the time with the same deduction as before, this is permitted even though he did not write, "eat with deduction as long as this remains in your hand." So the Beit Yosef writes in the name of the Rashba. The Shach writes: do not ask from what the Mechaber wrote in Choshen Mishpat siman 74 seif 3, that a debt whose pledge he consumes produce from, in a place where the borrower cannot remove him, if he wishes he need not accept the money before the time unless the borrower leaves him the produce to eat, and the Rav ruled simply like him. There the meaning is that the borrower must let him eat by valuation, and we deduct the value of all the produce as beit din appraises it for him. However, many poskim hold that before the time of removal he cannot protest and we do not deduct, since it is a place where he cannot remove him. It appears to me that this is a doubtful law, and the burden of proof is on the claimant.
