Through scraping alone. And it is less than a peel, for a peel must be somewhat thick so it can all be removed as one, unlike scraping — Ran. However scraping suffices even though a stam knife has grease congealed on its surface, for by law rinsing suffices like with turnip, since cold with cold; but because of its moisture one cannot rinse it, for on the contrary through rinsing it absorbs more; therefore scraping suffices, as Ran said; and in Turei Chayim there seif 10 he brings another reason in the name of Issur VeHeter; see there.
Turnip. It is what they call in foreign language "ribin," and so too in Maharil beginning of laws of Issur VeHeter.
That one cut after the turnip is permitted. At first glance it appears from this language that l'chatchila it is forbidden to cut a radish with it to eat with meat even though one already cut turnip with it, as Turei Chayim wrote there in the name of Issur VeHeter; but Maharshal in Orach Chayim and in his book there disagrees with Issur VeHeter and permits even l'chatchila; and so is implied from the words of Beit Yosef who wrote on Rashba that he does not permit when one cut turnip except for beet: "But Sh"D and Semak and Rabbeinu explicitly permit cutting even a radish" — end of his words; and in truth so is implied in the language of Semak and Sh"D and Tur; see there — investigate; and so too Agudah chapter 25 siman 126. One should not challenge: since the radish in any case needs rinsing, let us be concerned l'chatchila lest he soak and eat with meat without rinsing, as we forbid in the end of siman 91 to place meat in a cold forbidden bowl — Mahari Tzahalon's reason that this case is different, for there immediately when placed in the bowl rinsing is needed) — unlike here, where if he wishes to eat it so it is permitted; therefore we are not concerned lest he soak and eat with meat; and similarly I distinguished in siman 69 s.k. 83; see there. Or alternatively this case is different here, for rinsing too is only a mere stringency, for the taste was already nullified in the turnip; also in Sh"D and Semak and Semag this rinsing was not mentioned. However, certainly to cut l'chatchila turnip in order to cut a radish with it afterward — it appears in the Gemara and poskim that it is forbidden.
