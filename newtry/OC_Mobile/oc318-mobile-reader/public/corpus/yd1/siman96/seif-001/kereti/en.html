Radish — Mechaber's view: since they are sharp, it makes it like noten taam bar noten taam as one noten taam; even if cooked we do not say so, for sharp taste makes it more; Peri Chadash disagrees, and the primary view is like Mechaber.
That it is not well scraped — that is, when we know certainly; but a stam knife suffices with a peel.
That one tastes it — and it is permitted l'chatchila relying on this tasting; Peri Chadash, unlike Shach.
The same applies when not ben yomo, for sharpness makes it for improvement.
Against what the knife touched — that is, if it is open the amount of noten taam; otherwise sixty against removal of place suffices.
Against the entire radish — each small piece absorbs; if the knife is smaller one needs only against the place of the knife — this is R' Eliezer's view; but according to R' Tam who holds chein nafsho in all prohibitions, one must estimate against the entire radish even if larger than the knife, for each piece is nevelah. Shach wrote: where it needs removal of place and was cooked, sixty against removal of place suffices but not against the entire radish, for we do not say absorbed prohibition, unlike Maharam of Lublin.
The greens, etc. — nevertheless they have some sharpness, and likewise the tails need a peel — Turei Chayim.
And if there is doubt, etc. — Shach and Peri Chadash challenged: since if certainly cut it is forbidden as certainly a sharp thing, it is a Torah doubt to be stringent; Issur VeHeter deals only when it is not clear to us that it is sharp, and one must answer their words: this vegetable has a presumption of permissibility — establish the matter on its presumption that it was not cut with a forbidden knife, and even for Torah law we follow leniency.
