(Siman 96 seif 1) And some say the same when not ben yomo and it is well scraped. Beit Lechem Yehudah brought in the name of Knesset HaGedolah who agreed to Sefer Afrei Rabbati that Shach brought, who is lenient when well scraped and not ben yomo even with sharp thing; Beit Lechem Yehudah ruled thus when well scraped. If so the same regarding pickling, and all the more when not ben yomo except the measure they put water on fire. This is Magen Avraham's view in laws of Pesach siman 447 s.k. 28, and there is a printing error; Chayei Adam challenged him in vain. Beit Lechem Yehudah also brought in the name of Peri Chadash that according to Ran chapter 25 he does not say chein nafsho with sharp thing that is not through cooking. It is already explained in Sefer Arukh from Shach I brought siman 105 that in Peri Megadim one may rely on this. And see there is place where he permitted even without Peri Megadim where otherwise poskim are lenient, such as with other fruits; see Shach siman 22. If so one need not for what Magen Avraham wrote siman 447 seif 8 regarding olives it is not nevelah because before Pesach it is still permitted. With this one may answer Mahari Tzahalon's difficulty who was lenient with lemon juice cut with a forbidden knife and did not say chein nafsho there. Mahari Tzahalon proved from the Rav in Turei Chayim that if cut with a forbidden knife that you need to forbid it, this is considered b'dieved and removal of place suffices; he wrote so too in responsa Maharam of Lublin. It appears in any case when well scraped and not ben yomo one may rely on this, for there are two sides to permit. With sour apples he is lenient in Sefer Beit El; and likewise with meat knife for noten taam bar noten taam even ben yomo, and with non-Jew's knife not ben yomo. Peri Megadim in Mishbetzot Zahav Orach Chaim 447 left in doubt whether pressure and sharpness emit from food without sauce; and if cut with dairy knife a radish cut with meat knife — whether the dairy knife is forbidden, for perhaps pressure and sharpness are greater than hot with hot without sauce (one may say it is no worse than pressure and heat in kli sheni that does not forbid more than removal of place, and we hold a knife with which one slaughtered is forbidden to eat with it hot, and if pressure helps with heat in kli sheni, all the more here).
