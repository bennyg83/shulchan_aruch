(Siman 96 seif 1) The amount for removal of place, etc., or that one tastes it. It appears plain tasting must be a full taste: if one cut the radish in two, automatically the knife along the entire length of the radish at finger-depth — tasting one fold from one place of the cut place is also insufficient, lest in that place one tastes the knife did not emit much and elsewhere emitted, as if cut one after the other in two places — plainly one does not help from this on that; so too cutting at once — so it appeared to me for practical halachah. But accordingly it is difficult for me in what Rashba elaborated to refute those who hold a peel suffices; plainly one cannot say so, for if so how is tasting relevant, since one must taste along the entire length of the cut place — plainly such tasting automatically peels all the peel that is hair-thin. Requires study.
