(Siman 96 seif 1) The amount for removal of place, etc., or that one tastes it. It appears plain tasting must be a full taste: if one cut the radish in two, automatically the knife along the entire length of the radish at finger-depth — tasting one fold from one place of the cut place is also insufficient, lest in that place one tastes the knife did not emit much and elsewhere emitted, as if cut one after the other in two places — plainly one does not help from this on that; so too cutting at once — so it appeared to me for practical halachah. But accordingly it is difficult for me in what Rashba elaborated to refute those who hold a peel suffices; plainly one cannot say so, for if so how is tasting relevant, since one must taste along the entire length of the cut place — plainly such tasting automatically peels all the peel that is hair-thin. Requires study.
(There Shach s.k. 3) And it is not well scraped, etc. If they did not rinse intentionally beforehand; and he wrote in the hagahah there that if rinsing, one must rinse so water flows from head to end and end to head without delay, for all grease stuck on it blocks water from flowing — end Turei Chayim law 4. Minchat Yitzchak wrote the same regarding wiping with a hard thing helps; l'chatchila one should not cut without inserting ten times; in responsa Chut HaShani he is uncertain with one who says the knife was clean — if we say a matter not dependent on a person; however in Turei Beit Shmuel (siman 10 s.k. 118) he brought that without well scraped the law of insertion — even b'dieved forbidden with good rinsing and no insertion.
(Be'er Heitev) We are not accustomed to rely even b'dieved — see above (siman 42 in Taz s.k. 2) and Mishbetzot Zahav there.
(There s.k. 9) One does not need sixty only against the knife. At first glance it appears sixty against the knife's peel helps, for we hold below (siman 120 s.k. 7) that through insertion it is permitted to cut a radish, and plainly insertion emits only what is in the knife's peel; if so proven the radish emits only what was absorbed in the knife peel, and sixty against the knife peel suffices. However it is difficult: that which makes for improvement even when clean forbids the amount for removal — behold in the knife peel it is minimal and rinsing suffices as Shach wrote (siman 94 s.k. 28); and since sharpness and pressure emit only what is in the peel, rinsing should suffice for the radish like not ben yomo and well scraped, for even nevertheless sharpness makes for improvement what is in the knife peel — to Shach it appears and proven that sharpness and pressure emit more than what is in the knife peel; if so why does knife insertion help for cutting radish? Requires study.
