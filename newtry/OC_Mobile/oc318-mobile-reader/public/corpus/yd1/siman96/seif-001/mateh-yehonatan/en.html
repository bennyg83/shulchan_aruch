(Siman 96 in Shulchan Aruch seif 1) Radish, etc.; and some say the same when not ben yomo, etc. — because the sharpness of the radish makes it for improvement. Rashba challenged in responsa 397: accordingly how does the Gemara in Avodah Zarah 66b derive nat bar lichtamil is forbidden from purging — that it cannot fail to spoil somewhat, lest therefore the Torah commanded purging so a Jew can use it for a sharp thing that makes for improvement? I say it appears accordingly: what we hold regarding a vessel that absorbed — where the permitted absorbed suffices with hag'alah even in a vessel requiring libun, as written above siman 93 in the name of Rashba — if so it is well proven that nat bar lichtamil is forbidden, for otherwise libun would never be needed; since it cannot fail to spoil somewhat, it would always be permitted absorbed; and it is difficult why the Torah commanded that a vessel whose use is through fire requires libun — hag'alah should suffice since permitted absorbed, for one has not yet used a sharp thing and it is permitted absorbed and hag'alah helps, and afterward one may use even a sharp thing; or nat bar lichtamil is forbidden and it is prohibited absorbed and hag'alah does not help in something requiring libun; see below siman 192 what is written there.
(There in the hagahah) And if there is doubt whether it was cut with a forbidden knife, we follow leniency. See Shach s.k. 12 who wants to forbid the radish — since all agree it makes for improvement, it is a Torah doubt to be stringent. I say the law is with Rama, for we hold everywhere that even Torah prohibition of sixty we establish on presumption and it is permitted.
