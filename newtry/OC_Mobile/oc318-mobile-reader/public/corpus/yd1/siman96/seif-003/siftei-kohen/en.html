Spices that were ground, etc. And the same applies to salt that was ground and all other sharp things — specifically when ground, but not when they were placed in a meat mortar, as above end of siman 95 and 105 and in Orach Chaim siman 447 seif 6.
Ben yomo. Semak wrote: spices ground in a meat mortar may not be permitted with milk because of noten taam bar noten taam, for spice is strong and sharp and is like a mortar like sourdough, and pepper too is sharp and is like a radish cut with a meat knife that is not permitted because of noten taam bar noten taam but is forbidden because of actual taste absorbed there — end of his words. It appears that even when not ben yomo it forbids, for he compares it to a radish, and furthermore he gave a reason because of actual taste absorbed there; if so it is difficult on Mechaber why he wrote plainly ben yomo — behold the "some say" in seif 1 forbids even when not ben yomo; and so too Mechaber below siman 103 seif 6: "Some say that if they put pepper in a forbidden pot that is not ben yomo all is forbidden, for its sharpness makes it for improvement" — end of his words. Therefore it appears according to the "some say" that the Rav brought in the hagahah: since we hold like the "some say" in seif 1, the same applies here; and likewise in siman 95 end of seif 2 and siman 122 seif 3 the Rav wrote in the hagahah plainly that even not ben yomo it forbids regarding spices; see there. And so is the view of all the Acharonim, and so is the custom.
