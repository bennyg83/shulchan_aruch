And salted fish. It appears that according to Mechaber they are forbidden the amount for removal of place, and according to the Rav l'chatchila all of it is forbidden and b'dieved one must estimate the amount for removal of place. But in Turei Chayim there seif 9 he brings in the name of Issur VeHeter that if one cut salted fish with a forbidden knife one needs scraping, and if one cut it before it was salted, rinsing suffices — end of his words; and so too above siman 69 s.k. 89 in the name of the Rav. One must say Mechaber deals with when they are very salted, for then through the sharpness of the salt and the pressure of the knife it absorbs more; but because of a small amount of salt they are not considered sharp, as above siman 95 seif 2 in the hagahah.
That they have designated vessels. It appears that if we know certainly it was cut with a non-Jew's knife, even not ben yomo it is forbidden, for it is like a kort of chalita; and so too in Turei Chayim there seif 7 explicitly. However in Semag regarding laws of berachot (daf 113b) it appears explicitly as Issur VeHeter said, that even if certainly cut with a moist ginger knife it is permitted; if so one must say it does not apply to chalita and other very sharp things; therefore even if not moist it makes it for improvement, unlike ginger. And so too Maharshal in his explanations to Semag that it does not apply to chalita; and what the Rav wrote in Turei Chayim in Yoreh De'ah siman 192 appears that a stam vessel of a non-Jew not being ben yomo alone does not suffice to permit ginger — I know no implication; therefore it appears one should not be so stringent with ginger, since even with a radish many poskim permit when not ben yomo; and likewise in Sefer Afrei Rabbati daf 62b he permits through a peel when not ben yomo, but he distinguishes there and wrote that this is specifically with dry ginger, but in Issur VeHeter there it is explained that with dry it is plainly permitted) — and every dry sharp thing is permitted through rinsing; see there.
