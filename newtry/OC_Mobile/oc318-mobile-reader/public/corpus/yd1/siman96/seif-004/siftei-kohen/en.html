Lemon juice, etc. Shulchan Leket wrote: lemon juice of non-Jews is forbidden by law because they cut them with their knives and through their sharpness they absorb; but because they cut many and fill a barrel from them, one may say the knife taste is nullified in the first and second, etc. Bach wrote one must say he holds we do not say sharpness spoils except for chalita; and since a stam vessel of gentiles is not ben yomo, there is no prohibition except from congealed grease on the knife that was cleaned in the first and second, etc. If so, for us who hold like Semak, all these lemon juices are forbidden; and so too Tur in Orach Chaim siman 446 regarding Semak's view of olives that were pickled and cut before Pesach with a chametz knife that is not ben yomo — all the olives returned to chametz (and so too Mechaber there seif 8, and so too Tur and the Rav in the hagahah below siman 114 seif 8; see there) — unlike Hagahat Shulchan Aruch, end of his words. In my view the language of Shulchan Leket that the knife taste was nullified, etc., implies what was absorbed in the knife; but it appears he agrees to Semak that a radish and the like that are very sharp — sharpness spoils them for improvement; but he holds that lemon juice and the like, since they are not as sharp as a radish, sharpness does not spoil except what was absorbed on the sides of the knife that was nullified in the first and second, and this spoils it, and the lemon juice no longer has power to make for improvement what was absorbed from inside — only what was already nullified; for certainly with a radish and the like that is very sharp, even what was absorbed from inside makes it for improvement, unlike lemon juice; and this is compelled from the words of the Rav, for otherwise his words would contradict each other, for he wrote in seif 1 that if one cut the radish finely one needs sixty against all of it — thus the knife taste was not nullified; if so it is well that olives that are not so sharp, even like lemon juice, do not nullify the knife taste even what was absorbed on the outside of the knife — investigate. Or one may say olives are sharp like a radish, as we say in Eruvin that the dove said "let my food be bitter like an olive," etc.; or certainly regarding the halachah one may not rely on this that the knife taste was already nullified; and therefore the Rav wrote that in a place where they are accustomed to be stringent one should not change — that is, olives are forbidden only because with lemon juice and the like they are accustomed to permit; the Rav wrote one may resolve the custom and one may rely on this reason that it was already nullified, since one may also say they cut them with a new knife, for they have designated vessels for this, as we say regarding ginger in seif 2; but from doubt we forbid them, and therefore we rely on this view that it was nullified, etc., to resolve the custom, since otherwise there are poskim who permit even with a radish with a knife that is not ben yomo, and likewise there are poskim who say only a radish is sharp and not other things, as written in s.k. 12 in the name of Maharai; see there — that is, the Rav wrote "and even if some of them became forbidden," etc., it appears even some of them are not clear that they became forbidden; however without the reason that it was nullified we would not permit the lemon juice, even though above in seif 1 they buy radishes from gentiles because there is doubt whether cut with a forbidden knife — we follow leniency, because there one may rely on presumption and half-and-half, unlike here where it is likely they cut them with their knife.
