Object. For behold they did not decree on butter, and impure milk does not curdle.
L'chatchila. Taz wrote: one should wonder at this law, for behold one may not nullify a prohibition l'chatchila; but Shach wrote in the name of Beit Yosef and Turei Chayim: this is not called nullifying a prohibition, for perhaps there is no prohibition here at all; and furthermore his intent is not to nullify but only that the milk residues should go away — end of his words.
With them. Even though his intent is to return, he eats with them; not so with other prohibitions — Darkei Moshe in the name of Issur VeHeter: what they eat it in that place is because there they make it in a permitted manner without any concern in the world; and if they would make it here as they do, they would also eat it — Taz. And Beit Yosef wrote at the end of this siman: one who transgressed and curdled milk in sour milk of a gentile, and took this milk and curdled another in it, and from that other another — it is permitted to take this third milk and curdle milk in it and eat it, for after three times the gentile's milk is already gone.
