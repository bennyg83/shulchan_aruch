Butter of a gentile, etc. In butter they were lenient in a place where there is no concern of prohibition — namely of impure milk it is obvious there is no concern since it does not curdle; and if you say he mixed impure milk into it initially so as not to make butter but only to eat milk — nevertheless since they cook it, the milk residues of impure milk that are in it went away; and what did not go away and remained there is nullified in its minority; and even though regarding cheeses we forbid shortly even in a place of permission, butter is different since it was never within the decree in the Mishnah; therefore it is not relevant here to say they do not distinguish. And in Issur VeHeter some are lenient in places that forbid because they are not careful to mix prohibition into it and bowls that are not wiped; and as Rama wrote it is permitted to cook l'chatchila according to Beit Yosef's words that this is not nullifying a prohibition l'chatchila, for the intent is only to eliminate the prohibition; and if a little remains by chance it is nullified in a majority — I wondered at this, for in this we properly nullify a prohibition l'chatchila since it is a rabbinic prohibition, as the Maggid Mishnah wrote chapter 15 of Hilchot Maachalot Assurot; and these matters are according to the Rambam's view as explained in Beit Yosef; and the Rambam holds that in every rabbinic prohibition we nullify l'chatchila whenever it is not an actual Torah prohibition, as he wrote in Kesef Mishnah chapter 15 of Laws of Forbidden Foods; and therefore the Rambam wrote this specifically b'dieved — meaning that even though this prohibition is rabbinic, nevertheless it is not proper to take from the gentile in order to nullify the prohibition; and as Beit Yosef wrote in the name of the Raavad; therefore the Rambam wrote that if one took the butter — language of b'dieved.

And it is not relevant here to apply the stringencies of the place he left, for what they eat it in that place is because there they make it in a permitted manner without any concern in the world; and if they would make it here as they do, they would also eat it; only that the majority were not accustomed here — so too Issur VeHeter; and not as the Levush wrote on this according to whom it is a light prohibition, for whatever prohibition what difference to me light what difference to me severe; and furthermore if so also one who travels from a place of permission to prohibition would eat there — contrary to what is written; rather as I explained.
Also forbidden even if there is a distinguishing mark, etc. The reason is that it is like permitted things that others practiced as forbidden; Beit Yosef wrote at the end of this siman: one who transgressed and curdled milk in sour milk of a gentile, and took this milk and curdled another in it, and from that other another — it is permitted to take this third milk and curdle milk in it and eat it, for after three times the gentile's milk is already gone.
