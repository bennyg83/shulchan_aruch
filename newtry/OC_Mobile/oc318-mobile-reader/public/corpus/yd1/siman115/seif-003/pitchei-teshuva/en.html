That it is forbidden, etc. See in responsum Chut HaMeshulash vol. 4, section Teshuvot HaTashbetz from the first column siman 32 [and see in responsum Chasam Sofer siman 107 where he elaborated to bring proofs that milk was forbidden by vote like yayin nesech, etc.; and therefore also in places where there is no concern of impure milk at all, whenever a Jew does not see it it is forbidden, and not like Radbaz and Peri Chadash. And even if for him the halachah would be like Radbaz, our fathers already accepted upon themselves that view that is stringent, and it is forbidden for Ashkenazim by law and has no permission; and it is close in my eyes to a vow of Torah — that is if the halachah would be like Radbaz to be lenient; but if the essence of the law is to forbid, it is not a vow but the transgressor is a violator of a rabbinic prohibition — hence its stringency is its leniency (see there siman 214) — and see there further about one city where they are careful even from milk that was milked and a Jew does not see it, nevertheless it is clear to them for complete permission to eat bread that was kneaded with that milk, and they buy from the baker explicitly. And he wrote that great investigation is needed: from where did this permission spread — is not its mixture forbidden even in vessels? And it is possible that just as the Kesef Mishneh wrote in the view of the Rambam that they decreed only on milk in its visible form and not on butter that already changed, so one may say that when it is kneaded with flour and their combination becomes dough and is baked, new aspects come here and it is as if it changed; and there is basis for this in tractate Kerisos folio 5, etc.; but Heaven forbid to rely on this — see there.]
