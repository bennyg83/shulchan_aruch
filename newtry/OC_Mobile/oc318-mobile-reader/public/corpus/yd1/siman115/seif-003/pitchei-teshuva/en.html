[We do not protest. See in responsum Chasam Sofer siman 79, where he was asked about a woman who took butter from a gentile in a place where they practice permission, and it then became known that this butter was made from the remnants of the cheese remaining in the kettle after they removed the first cheese and the second, which is called zworich, and then butter was made from those remnants — whether there is in it the prohibition of cheese; and he answered that if the cheeses were set with the skin of a keivah, certainly also the remnants of the cheeses from which butter was made are forbidden; but if they were not set with the skin of a keivah, although the cheeses are forbidden because of a matter that was forbidden by vote, nevertheless butter was not forbidden by vote. And in a place where they practiced permission with butter, this too is permitted; and although it does not appear to permit l'chatchila for the common people to buy such butter, nevertheless b'dieved one should not forbid the mixture and the vessels — see there; and see Be'er Heitev above seif katan 10.]
That there is no custom. See Peri Chadash who challenged the Mechaber and his view is that it is permitted even where there is a custom; and so he wrote in Chochmat Adam in his name; and see in responsum Noda B'Yehudah Tinyana Yoreh De'ah siman 66 who rejected his words (and see Biur HaGra of blessed memory); and he wrote further that one rav permitted butter of gentiles in a place where they practiced a prohibition, by rinsing three times, and his reason is according to what Beit Yosef wrote, brought in the Taz seif katan 14, one who transgressed and set milk etc., that after three times the milk of the gentile is already gone, therefore; and he wrote that the reason is reasonable, and even so, since we have not found this reasoning, it is difficult to permit. And see there further, and in siman 65, regarding if they wish to permit the custom that they practiced a prohibition with butter of a gentile, whether they have a release.
To bring it. See in responsum Noda B'Yehudah Tinyana Yoreh De'ah siman 64, regarding one whose residence was in a place where they practiced permission with butter of gentiles, and afterward he established his residence in a place where they practiced a prohibition, and he brought with him butter from his first place, and one teacher instructed that it is permitted for him to eat that butter, and he brought a proof from the Gemara in Chullin 17, limbs of meat of nechirah etc.; and he of blessed memory disagreed with him for several reasons. First, there is a double doubt toward stringency: perhaps the halachah is like Rashi; and if you say like the Rosh, behold also according to the Rosh it is an unresolved inquiry; and further, we say so only regarding something that was forbidden by force of time, similar to limbs of meat of nechirah — see there, a matter with reason. And in my humble opinion I am astonished why he did not mention the words of the Rama here, who wrote "but it is forbidden to bring it with him," which implies that even in such a case, that he brought it with him and he is eating and going and there remains leftover in his hand, it is forbidden if there is no distinguishing mark in it; and according to Maharil even if there is a distinguishing mark in it. And I found in responsum Beit Ephraim Yoreh De'ah siman 39 who noticed this and wrote to distinguish: that the case of the Rama, since his main residence is established in a place where they practiced a prohibition, it is obvious that what was permitted to him in a temporary way while he was in a place where they practice permission does not avail; but the case that was asked in Noda B'Yehudah is dealing with one who was living in a place of permission etc. And he wrote further that in any case that law of the Gaon of blessed memory requires further study, for what has this to do with that of the Rosh regarding one who vows, for there we expound the implication of the language of the vow, which is not so here, for still even without this it is permitted, for he is permitted to eat in a place of permission even though he is from a place of prohibition and his intention is to return. But in a place where they practice a prohibition he is forbidden because of suspicion and because of mar'it ayin; and if so, what does it help us that he already ate from it? And if it is dealing with a place where there is no suspicion and mar'it ayin, for example that most of the butter brought there is from a place of permission — if so, even if the inquiry in Chullin is toward stringency, there is no place to be stringent in this — see there.
