The butter, etc. Thus the Rambam: butter of gentiles — some Geonim permitted it, for behold they did not decree on butter and impure milk does not curdle. And meaning as is written folio 35b: if he sets it for cheese, etc.; and it is written: and some Geonim forbade it because of milk residues that remain in it, etc., as the Gemara says there; and the other answer, etc.; and the Geonim disagree in the establishments there: one holds the second reasoning follows Rabbi Chanina because it is impossible, etc., as Rashi explained; and if so the same applies to butter; and the first reasoning follows Shmuel because they curdle, etc., and not on account of milk residues; and therefore butter is permitted and was not within the decree; and in this also depend the two resolutions there: one — if he requires, etc.; and the other answer, etc.; and so holds (the Rambam) like Shmuel as is written in seif 2; and so wrote the first edition there as is written: impure animal milk does not curdle and stand in pure milk; and if impure milk became mixed with pure milk, when one curdles it the pure milk will stand and the impure milk will go out with the residue; and because of this the law is that all milk found in a gentile's possession is forbidden lest he mixed impure animal milk; and gentile cheese is permitted because impure animal milk does not become cheese; but in the days of the Mishnah sages they decreed, etc.; but according to Tosafot d"h ligvinah that when the gentile made the cheeses one need not be concerned even for the latter answer as above, since his intent is so he will not mix; and Rabbi Chanina who says because it is impossible, etc., refers to the continuation of Rabbenu Levi's words; and accordingly butter is permitted according to this reasoning; and this is Semak's view who permits; and he wrote another reason that there are no holes in butter; and Semag and Hagahot Maimoniyot wrote in the name of Baal HaItur that butter is one of eighteen things as is written in Yerushalmi chapter 1 of Shabbos: Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai taught on that day they decreed on their bread and their oil and their cooked dishes and their salted foods and their cheeses and their butters; and Sefer HaTerumah wrote: however in our Yerushalmi it is not written "their butters." And Beit Yosef decided: we do not object, etc.; and if, etc.: (Collection) The butter, etc. — now chapter 6 folio 2 that forbids, and he elaborated on this — see there (end).
And in a place, etc. Language of the Rambam: it appears to me that if one took the butter from the gentile and cooked it until the milk residues went away, this is permitted, for you cannot say they became mixed and were cooked — they are nullified in their minority; but butter that gentiles cooked is forbidden on account of absorption from gentile vessels, as is written; and the Raavad challenged him for nullifying a prohibition l'chatchila in a majority; and in several places it is written: because there are many doubts — perhaps it did not become mixed; and if you say it became mixed, perhaps none remained; and if you say some remained, perhaps it will be melted on the fire. But the Rashba siman 110 challenged him that in these prohibitions they were stringent in their doubt, for behold every doubt is for leniency; and here doubt upon doubt — perhaps it did not become mixed, etc. — as above; and nevertheless they forbade; and likewise we do not follow the majority here, for if not why did they not say let him taste a spoonful? Rather they decreed to distance from their prohibitions more than Torah prohibitions; and this is what is meant by because they curdle in skin, etc., even though it is not by majority; and there is no difficulty from all this to the Rambam, for there is no doubt at all in cheese even for Rabbi Chanina, for which reason he said it is impossible without, etc.; and Shmuel who said because they curdle, etc., wrote there because a curdling agent even in a thousand is not nullified; and he further challenged him that if so cheese is more so in cooking; and he himself already resolved this: cheese is forbidden everywhere on account of a decree; and so milk; and as is written seif 62 according to Tosafot that if he milked with intent to make cheese the cheeses are permitted if a Jew made them; and nevertheless the milk itself is forbidden; and so are the Rambam's words; and because of this, etc., that all milk, etc., and cheese, etc.; and in Tosefta chapter 5 it implies like the Rambam's words: it is taught there one does not buy cheese of Beit Hinayki except from the expert; and burnt cheese is bought from anywhere — meaning even from a suspected Jew; and the same applies to butter even from a gentile, for although cheese is forbidden from a gentile on account of the decree they do not distinguish as above, not so with butter. And Beit Yosef decided: in a place where there is no custom one should rely on the Rambam's words.
