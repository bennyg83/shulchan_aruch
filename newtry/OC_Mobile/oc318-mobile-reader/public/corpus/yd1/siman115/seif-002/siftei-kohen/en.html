And even if they curdled it in herbs it is forbidden. For they already decreed on all cheeses of gentiles, whether they curdled it with a forbidden substance or curdled it with a permitted substance — Rambam and the poskim; and see in the poskim who wrote other reasons for this — see there.
And if the Jew, etc. This is according to what Darkei Moshe and Turei Chayim wrote klal chapter 1: and thus Mordechai end of chapter 25 wrote: even if a Jew sees the milking and the gentile made it cheese, nevertheless they do not practice to buy from gentiles; but in Agudah chapter Shoel he wrote: and thus: a person buys a quantity of cheeses and the gentile fixes them on Shabbos and a Jew sees and guards and does not speak; and they already practiced like the Agudah's words — end of his words; but in truth in my humble opinion the Agudah does not disagree with the Mordechai, for the Agudah does not deal there except regarding Shabbos prohibition, and therefore he wrote this law in tractate Shabbos chapter Shoel and not in Avodah Zarah chapter one of Avodah Zarah regarding cheeses of gentiles; rather certainly it does not deal with the law of prohibition of gentile cheeses; and it is possible that in truth the cheeses are forbidden on account of gentile cheeses, but on account of Shabbos they are permitted; and there is a practical difference that it is permitted to buy the quantity of cheeses thus on Shabbos and sell them afterward to gentiles; or it is possible it deals in places that practice permission with gentile cheeses according to Rabbenu Tam whom he brought in chapter one of Avodah Zarah; and that which he wrote "and a Jew sees and guards" means that he guards that the gentile will not do any adulteration in the cheeses; or in this that the Agudah is different: the Jew bought the cheeses initially and behold they are his, and there is no concern of gentile cheeses here, as is written below; but certainly one may not distinguish between whether the Jew saw the making of the cheeses or not, since the Sages forbade gentile cheeses — all cheeses they forbade, for behold even if they curdled in herbs they forbade on account of they do not distinguish; if so the same applies if he saw gentiles made them; and that is what the Mishnah in chapter one of Avodah Zarah taught anonymously: cheeses of gentiles are forbidden; and regarding milk of gentiles the Mishnah taught there: milk that a gentile milked and a Jew does not see it is forbidden, and if a Jew sees it is permitted — hence in cheeses there is no distinction between whether he saw the making of the cheeses or not; and so all the poskim wrote anonymously and did not distinguish in cheeses as they distinguished in milk of gentiles; rather certainly gentile cheeses in every matter are forbidden; and so it is in responsum of Maimonides siman 8 from responsum of Rabbi Shimshon son of Avraham: and I never saw to take gentile cheeses even if he saw the milk from when a gentile milked it until he made from it, for there is no concern of prohibition, etc. — end of his words; and so it is in responsum of Maharam of Rothenburg siman 474: regarding cheese that a gentile curdled in the presence of a Jew — he requires that afterward a Jew perform an act, that a Jew place the rennet into the milk; and if a gentile places it — it seems to me even if a Jew stands over him and even if the rennet is valid it is forbidden; and what Hagahot Ashiri and Issur VeHeter wrote (and responsum of Maharam siman 217 in the name of Rabbenu Tam): where it is known that the gentile's intent is to curdle — even if he was not at the beginning of the milking it is permitted — there it does not deal except from the law of milk that a gentile milked and a Jew does not see it; and they say it is not forbidden on account of mixtures of impure milk, since the gentile's intent is to curdle to make cheeses from it — if so why would he mix impure milk that does not curdle? Therefore the milk is permitted, meaning that the Jew will make the cheeses; and so it is in Issur VeHeter klal 44 din 5 explicitly; and this is what he wrote this law in klal 45 regarding prohibition of milk that a gentile milked, and he did not write it in klal 46 regarding prohibition of gentile cheeses; rather certainly as I explained; and so in responsum of Maharam and in Hagahot Ashiri there it appears they permit only when the Jew made them afterward; and what spread as custom that they buy cheeses from the cheese merchants even though gentiles make them is because they practiced to rent the animals; and if so the cheeses belong to Jews; and we do not find any prohibition anywhere with cheeses of Jews that gentiles made, for they decreed on gentile cheeses and not on cheeses of Jews that gentiles made; and so they also practice that the gentile in a Jew's house makes the Jew's cheeses; and the same applies when they do not rent the animals and buy a quantity of cheeses from him — if so the cheeses at the time of their making belonged to Jews; but if the gentile makes cheeses for himself, even though a Jew was there from beginning to end it is forbidden on account of gentile cheeses — anything in which a Jew did not perform an act in it, as we say.
And if he saw the making of the cheeses, etc. This he wrote according to his method; but in my humble opinion the poskim do not deal with a case where gentiles made the cheeses, but rather from the law of milk that is permitted when the gentile's intent is to make cheeses and the Jew must make the cheeses afterward, as is written in the previous seif katan.
One should permit b'dieved. In Hagahot Ashiri and Issur VeHeter there it states one should not permit this except in a place of great loss (and so it is in responsum of Maharam siman 217); and so in Darkei Moshe and Turei Chayim klal chapter 1 din 2; however where he saw the milking only that he was not there at the beginning of the milking, it appears there in the hagahah one should permit b'dieved even without great loss; and so it appears in responsum of Maharam there; and it is possible that in such a case the Rav wrote here in the hagahah anonymously to permit b'dieved.
To permit b'dieved. And specifically when one buys from him a quantity of cheeses or by weight, for since impure milk does not curdle he does not mix an impure thing into it; but if one buys by measure — Issur VeHeter; and so in Turei Chayim there.
Since he made, etc. The language is not precise, for even if he did not make cheese but only the milk was milked it is called b'dieved, as Issur VeHeter wrote and Beit Yosef brings in Darkei Moshe and Turei Chayim there; and all the more so according to what I explained above that the poskim deal specifically with a case where the Jew made the cheeses; and it is possible one should read: since Israel made, etc.; or the Rav came to say that specifically in such a case it is called b'dieved; rather he comes to say that since he makes cheeses there is no concern for mixtures of impure milk — understand well.
Since his intent, etc. Meaning that we know with certainty that his intent is to make cheese; and so it appears in the poskim' words; and it is in responsum of Maharam there that even if he had an impure thing in the flock it is permitted b'dieved for this reason that the gentile's intent is to curdle — see there.
And nevertheless it is forbidden to eat the milk thus. Since he did not see the milking, even if we know the gentile's intent is to make cheese.
