And the Raavad resolved the first difficulty like Tosafot, and the second difficulty he resolved that whatever the Sages forbade of gentiles, nullification in a majority does not help, so they will not breach, since it is common in them to impart taste; and from this understand what Beit Yosef wrote in the name of the Rashba shortly on the Rambam's words that some Geonim forbade butter, etc.; and this came out for the Rav because they curdle it in the skin of rennet of a carcass, etc. — meaning that the essence of the stringency in cheese is because it is a curdling agent; but if it were not curdling it would be properly nullified in butter; and therefore the Rambam had to give a reason why butter does not nullify the residue — namely milk residues; and he resolved that since it is not mixed. But the Rashba wrote that even though it is not mixed it would properly be nullified; only that which the residue is not nullified is because what the Sages forbade of gentiles, nullification does not help in it, as I wrote in the name of the Raavad; and so too the Maggid Mishnah in the name of the Acharonim; and see what I wrote siman 104 seif 1.
And even if they curdled it in herbs. For cheeses are mentioned in the Mishnah explicitly for prohibition, and the Sages did not distinguish since there are those who curdle it even now in the skin of a carcass; not so in a matter where they decreed on account of exposure in siman 116 that it is permitted nowadays since exposure is not relevant at all, for snakes are not common among us. And Beit Yosef further wrote in the name of Semak: for Rabbenu Tam who rules that the prohibition of cheeses is on account of exposure, it is forbidden also nowadays; and even though with water they did not decree nowadays on account of exposure, it is different there since there is no other reason except exposure; therefore everyone knew that in a place where there is concern of exposure they would forbid; not so here where there are other reasons in the Gemara; therefore if you permit where snakes are not common, they will come to permit also where they are common — meaning they will think the essence of the prohibition is for another reason, and where it would be permitted for another reason they will not be concerned about exposure; so it seems the explanation of his words.
One should permit b'dieved for there is no concern, etc. This is the view of Rabbenu Tam that some poskim wrote in his name that he permits when it is known the gentile milks for cheese, but not when he milks to eat or for kutach; and Issur VeHeter ruled like him. And I did not reach the end of Rama's view, for Beit Yosef and Shulchan Aruch already ruled like the Rambam that even if they certainly curdled in herbs it is forbidden because they did not distinguish; the same applies here it is forbidden since he was not at the milking; and furthermore it comes to permit also eating the milk, for in siman 110 the Rashba forbade also the piece that is not fit on account of the fit piece that is close to error even if everything is rabbinic; all the more so here who will distinguish to say the milk is forbidden since the cheese made from it is permitted? And furthermore in milk there is concern of mixtures of impure milk which is a Torah prohibition; and Rama himself wrote shortly that one should not break a fence even in a place of permission; and in the Levush I saw he wrote on this that which he forbade shortly even in a place where they certainly make it with permission — that is because other reasons combine there, and I did not know what reason to combine to this; for since it is known it is made with complete permission the prohibition is only because they do not distinguish; the same applies one should forbid because they do not distinguish — what difference to me whether he was there or not, since it is known it is made with permission and nevertheless we forbid it; and there is no proof from Issur VeHeter who did not bring the Rambam's words at all; therefore it appears in my humble opinion one should not be lenient also in this, and one should not permit unless a Jew was at the milking according to the Rambam's view; and furthermore Beit Yosef wrote that also now there is prohibition according to those who forbid on account of exposure for Rabbenu Tam, as I cited shortly in the name of Semak.
