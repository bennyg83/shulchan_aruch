(Siman 115 in Shulchan Aruch seif 1) Milk that a gentile milked, etc. Behold Beit Yosef brought in the name of the Mordechai that even if there is no impure thing in the flock — and the same in our time that impure animal milk is not common — nevertheless if a Jew does not see it is forbidden; and his proof is from Avodah Zarah folio 16, where the Gemara challenges: it is obvious — the Gemara should have challenged: why need one sit at all? Rather even if there is no impure thing one must sit; and because they do not distinguish, etc.; and if you say so there is still a difficulty: what does it challenge as obvious? Say that this very thing teaches us that even if there is no impure thing one must sit because they do not distinguish, etc. One may answer that this baraita comes to inform us of permission and not prohibition; for if not it should have said: if a Jew does not see it is forbidden. And behold many wish to say that where there is no impure thing in the flock it is permitted, for it is like a Jew sees and it is permitted; and proof from chapter one of Avodah Zarah folio 35: if he sets it for cheese, the same applies that the gentile sets it for kutach; and it is impossible that they do not distinguish, for even if he sets it for cheese we should also forbid. And necessarily one must say that where there is no concern of mixture, such as in cheese, or there is no impure thing in the flock — and for us there is generally no majority of impure animals — a Jew need not see; and if you say: accordingly where does it challenge if there is no impure thing it is obvious it should have said why need a Jew sit and watch at the side of the flock? And it appears that in any case the Jew must sit, for even if there is no impure thing in the flock, nevertheless since in another flock there is an impure thing there is concern lest impure milk come from the other flock and become mixed; therefore a Jew must sit at the side of the flock to remove this concern; only the question is that it is obvious. And according to the Mordechai who holds they do not distinguish, one must say he holds that what the Gemara says — if he sets it for cheese, the same applies — means: why did the Sages enact to forbid even if we establish there is no concern of mixture of impure milk; and that is because they do not distinguish, since most milk stands for cheeses and the minority of a minority that is for kutach — they do not have to forbid the majority on account of the minority; for regarding a majority on account of a minority there is no reason to forbid because they do not distinguish; and it answers: if he sets it for cheese, etc.; for if the majority of cheese he sets, the same applies that we do not forbid because they do not distinguish, but on account of kutach — meaning that the majority is for kutach; if so it is fitting to say because they do not distinguish. And from now Rama in this siman adopts the Mordechai's language for us, and for us this is primary; and even though impure milk is not common, nevertheless it is forbidden because they do not distinguish. And what Rama wrote: and b'dieved one need not be concerned for all this — that is even if he did not see in the vessel at the beginning, as Shach wrote seif katan 8 — see there; meaning because there is no impure thing; and that we do not require all this is because they do not distinguish — meaning that since he saw the milking, he has done the Sages' enactment and it is no longer relevant to forbid because they do not distinguish — understand well.
(There in the hagahah) And if a gentile milked some of the animals, etc. See in Shach seif katan 13 where he elaborated to give a reason why that milk which was milked before the Jew's arrival was not forbidden; and it is possible that certainly if that milk that was milked after the Jew's arrival stands alone in a vessel, in truth even Rama would agree it is forbidden; only it deals with milk already mixed with milk milked after the Jew's arrival; if so one may say that through mixture they did not forbid so extensively because they do not distinguish.
(There) Milk of a gentile that was forbidden does not help, etc. See Shach seif katan 8 who wrote: specifically stam milk; but when we know, etc.; and he is from Tosafot chapter one of Avodah Zarah; and this is not compelled, for one may well say that all this is according to their view who hold that wherever there is no concern of mixture they did not forbid gentile milk at all; and if so, if we know he sets it for cheeses and there is no concern of mixture it is permitted; not so for us who establish that even if there is no concern of mixture, once it comes to a Jew while it is still milk it is also forbidden because they do not distinguish; if so, even if the gentile's intent was to make cheese and there is no concern of mixture, nevertheless since it came to a Jew while it was still milk it is forbidden even without any mixture at all — understand well.
