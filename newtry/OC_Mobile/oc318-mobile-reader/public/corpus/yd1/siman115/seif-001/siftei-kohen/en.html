And a Jew does not see it is forbidden, etc. It makes no difference whether he milked for his own need or for a Jew's need — Tur and the agreement of the poskim; and it is simple.
And a Jew sits outside. See above seif katan 8.
For a Jew's need. But for his own need he must actually see at the time he milks that he does not mix into it something impure, since there is something impure with him in the flock.
That he fear lest he stand, etc. And accordingly it appears that even if he goes out and comes in it is permitted, since the reason is that he is afraid; and so we say below siman 118 and at the end of siman 129: one who leaves a gentile with things that if switched there is a Torah prohibition in them — it is permitted with going out and coming in on account of fear; and so too in Perishah seif 63; and he added that the same applies even if he does not sit constantly but goes out and comes in and sits — permitted; and all the more so going out and coming in to the place of the milk.
And l'chatchila, etc. This applies even to the first clause where there is no impure thing in the flock; and so it is in the poskim and in Darkei Moshe and Turei Chayim klal chapter 1 din 6; and it is simple.
At the beginning of the milking, etc. Bach wrote: however b'dieved Rabbenu Tam permitted if he did not see the beginning of the milking, as Hagahot Ashiri wrote at the end of chapter one of Avodah Zarah — end of his words; and this is not relevant here, for Rabbenu Tam did not permit except when it is known he milks for cheese, as will be explained.
And they practiced to be stringent, etc. Even if he sees at the beginning in a vessel that has no impure thing in it, for we are concerned lest milk residues remained in it and the Jew will not notice. Issur VeHeter.
However b'dieved one need not be concerned for all this. Meaning: one need not be concerned that all this is required; it suffices that he was there at the beginning of the milking even if he did not see in the vessel; and this is according to what Darkei Moshe and Turei Chayim wrote there in the name of Issur VeHeter: b'dieved if he did not see in the vessel but only saw the beginning of the milking it is permitted — end of his words; and so in the simanim there; but it is uncertain, for in Issur VeHeter beginning of klal 45 it appears explicitly the opposite: and thus: a Jew must be there at the time the gentile begins to milk and see that there is no milk in the vessel at the beginning and that there is no impure thing in the flock; and for this reason they practice in several places — and it is a kosher custom — not to let the gentile milk in a vessel called gelt in a foreign tongue, even though it is designated for milking and is a cold thing and not sharp at all, for one should be concerned lest there be in it initially a little impure milk and the Jew will not notice; and b'dieved however the milk is permitted even if the gentile milked in that vessel of his, only that the Jew saw initially in the vessel and afterward even if he went out and came in — end of his words; hence even b'dieved we require that the Jew saw initially in the vessel; and so it appears in Semak (folio 112 side b) and other poskim explicitly — see there; and so in the law that since milk that a gentile milked is forbidden on account of lest he mixed impure milk into it — if so what help is it that he saw the beginning of the milking if he did not see in the vessel, lest there be impure milk in the vessel before the beginning of the milking that will become mixed afterward in the rest of the milk? And it appears that also the Tur who wrote that a Jew sits outside — their intent is in a matter where there is no concern that something impure will become mixed into it initially; namely that he saw initially in the vessel; and what Issur VeHeter wrote that going out and coming in afterward suffices — so it is in Semak there; and it appears that even if the gentile milked for his own need, going out and coming in suffices; for the reason of going out and coming in is not on account of fear alone but because since he knows there is no impure thing in the flock, even though he cannot see at the time he milks there is no concern for any adulteration, except that the gentile will leave the flock and adulterate; and since he goes out and comes in and sees the gentile does not leave the flock it suffices; and so is proven in the Gemara in Avodah Zarah (folio 39 side b): where there is no impure thing in the flock and the Jew sits outside, even if he cannot see at all at the time he milks it is permitted on account of: since he sat outside he sees it is impossible to adulterate; and so it appears from the Tur's words, and not like Perishah seif 62 who wrote the reason we require a Jew sitting outside is on account of fear.
Animals in a Jew's house. See below seif katan 13.
Or in their pen. And in Turei Chayim there he wrote: if the pen is behind the house it is forbidden l'chatchila; but Darkei Moshe wrote in conclusion: but Maharai wrote that they practice permission also in this; and so inside — Turei Chayim there concludes according to Maharai's words at the end; and so it appears from his words in the hagahah here that he did not distinguish in this; and so is the view of Maharshal siman 82 — see there.
But if a gentile's house separates, etc. And in Darkei Moshe and Turei Chayim there: also in such a case it is permitted b'dieved according to Maharai; and so in the simanim there; and so it appears to be Maharshal's view there.
Minor or minor female. Nine years old — Turei Chayim there in the name of Issur VeHeter.
And if a gentile milked some, etc. I do not know from where the Rav took this, and I did not understand his reasoning; if he wishes to rule like Maharai who wrote that nowadays it is not relevant at all that there is an impure thing that is not found, and they forbade only milk that a gentile milked from his own animals and a Jew does not see it — and the Rav deals with an animal of a Jew — if so even if the Jew did not come until after all were milked it is permitted for this reason; and if he holds like Issur VeHeter that even nowadays it is forbidden even b'dieved whenever a gentile's house separates — if so even if some of the animals were milked it is forbidden. And it appears that what compelled the Rav to distinguish between some of the animals and all of them is from what is written in Shadal siman 82: Rabbenu Tam permitted Rabbi Eliezer of Metz who had ewes milking in a pen behind his house and his maidservant would go and milk without a Jew seeing — end of his words; and on this Maharai wrote his above words that so is the custom according to Rabbenu Tam; and in siman 81 Shadal wrote that Rabbenu Tam was strict with one whose ewes were with the gentile and he sent his daughter there every morning to see the milking and she did not manage to come until most or all were milked — end of his words; and so Semag in the name of Rabbenu Tam, and Beit Yosef brings it; from this the Rav derived that Rabbenu Tam was strict only when most or all were milked, but not some of them; but in truth it is not reasonable at all to distinguish between all and some; rather it appears that Rabbenu Tam was strict if his daughter came only after some were milked; and the incident was thus; and so it is in responsum of Maharam of Rothenburg siman 215 and 216 (Prague edition): Rabbenu Tam was strict even if some were milked since he was not there from the beginning of the milking — see there; and that which he permitted Rabbi Eliezer of Metz in a pen of a Jew is different, for the gentile is afraid; and on this Maharai came to say that although Shadal wrote on Rabbenu Tam's words that it is unseemly to do so l'chatchila (and so too in Issur VeHeter end of klal 45), nevertheless so is the custom since an impure thing is not common; and he says that with animals of gentiles, even in a pen of a Jew it is forbidden; and so it appears explicitly from Maharshal's words there: for Maharai both are required — his animal and a pen of a Jew — see there; and also in Turei Chayim there he wrote: in a Jew's house it is permitted to milk his animals even when a Jew does not see; and if the pen is behind the house or a gentile's house separates, it is forbidden l'chatchila and b'dieved in every matter permitted — end of his words; and he does not distinguish between all were milked or some were milked; and perhaps some textual error fell in his words here in the hagahah. And all this is when he milks for kutach or to eat the milk; but if it is known the gentile milks for cheese, its law will be explained in seif 2 — see there.
Forbids vessels. And some say regarding bishulei goyim above end of seif 113 seif 11 that it does not forbid vessels (and so is Issur VeHeter's view klal 43 din 6), for it is different there since the essence of their prohibitions is from their words on account of wedding decrees; but here they decreed on it on account of concern for a Torah prohibition that is in the milk itself; and so Issur VeHeter distinguishes klal 45.
That was cooked, etc. And so Issur VeHeter; and even though Issur VeHeter holds that even cheeses of gentiles themselves that fell into a dish are nullified in a majority, as below seif katan 17 — one must say that here it deals when there is not a majority in the dish against the vessel; or l'chatchila it is forbidden to cook in it even if there will be a majority in the dish, for it is forbidden to add to any prohibition and nullify it, as above siman 99 seif 6 — see there; but if it was cooked in it b'dieved and there is a majority in the dish it is permitted according to Issur VeHeter.
Even in a place where they practiced prohibition, etc. See above siman 119 seif katan 20.
As above regarding bread, etc. It appears that a majority is nevertheless required in the dish, as above siman 112 seif 14 regarding bread of a gentile; and accordingly it appears the Rav's view is that cheese of a gentile is not nullified in a majority in a dish; but in Issur VeHeter klal 47 din 7 he ruled explicitly that cheese of a gentile is nullified in a majority in a dish (and not in dry if it is within twenty-four hours) — see there where he elaborated his reason; and one must say the Rav holds not like him in this; and it seems to me there is proof for the Rav's words from what the Rashba wrote in Teshuvot HaRosh end of bayit 3, and Beit Yosef brings it end of seif 113: whatever the Sages forbade, its exudate is forbidden like it, for even oil of the forbidden sinew that is only from the custom of the pious forbids its mixture whether in mixtures of its substance or in its juice and gravy; and so all rabbinic prohibitions like chelev and cheese of a gentile, etc. — end of his words; behold it was explained in this seif katan seif 2 and in several places that oil of the forbidden sinew forbids until sixty; and if so the same applies to cheese of a gentile; and even though the Rashba forbids also bishulei goyim until sixty, and it was already explained in siman 112 seif katan 23 that the Rav holds in this like Issur VeHeter — nevertheless regarding cheese of a gentile the Rav disagrees with him and holds it is different in Shulchan Aruch since there is no concern of a Torah prohibition; not so cheese of a gentile that they decreed on account of concern of a Torah prohibition in its substance; and know that Issur VeHeter himself forbids vessels in which cheese of a gentile was cooked, and permits in klal 43 din 6 vessels in which bishulei goyim were cooked; and so in bishulei goyim he holds that even an important substance is nullified in a majority, as is written in siman 112 there; and in cheese of a gentile he holds it is not nullified, as above siman 110 seif katan 62 in din 18; rather certainly cheese of a gentile is more stringent than bishulei goyim. I further found thus in Nishmat Chayim chapter Ha'arel who wrote that cheese of a gentile requires sixty and their breads and cooked dishes are nullified in a majority — see there; and even though he wrote there that butter also requires sixty, nevertheless the Rav does not hold like him regarding butter, for certainly butter is lighter than bishulei goyim; and so in Be'er Sheva folio 94 he wondered at this that sixty is required in butter; and it is possible that this also deals with that butter where their practice is to mix impure matter into it, but not with stam butter; however in Be'er Sheva he compared cheese of a gentile to bishulei goyim — see there.
Does not help, etc. Specifically with stam milk; but when we know that the gentile's intent was to make cheese from the milk, it is permitted for a Jew to make cheese afterward from it, as is written below seif 2.
