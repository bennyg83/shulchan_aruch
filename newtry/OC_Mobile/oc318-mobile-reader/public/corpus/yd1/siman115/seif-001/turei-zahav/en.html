That a gentile milked. In the Tur it is written: it makes no difference whether he milked for his own need or for a Jew's need — meaning that with milk for his own need there is reason to permit, for certainly he did not mix impure milk and spoil his milk since it does not curdle; even so it is forbidden.
Even if the Jew, etc. But in any case we require that he sit outside at the side of the flock; and so is proven in the Gemara that is in the baraita: a Jew sits at the side of the gentile's flock and the gentile milks for him and brings it to him. What are the circumstances? If there is no impure thing in the flock it is obvious; and if there is an impure thing in the flock, why ever? There is always an impure thing, etc. And the Mordechai wrote: some say that if it is clear to us there is no impure animal in the flock it is permitted to take from that gentile even if a Jew does not see it — and this is an error, for if so the Gemara should have challenged: if there is no impure thing in the flock why need a Jew sit in the flock and see? Rather one must answer: if there is none, etc. — it is obvious that sitting at the side of the flock suffices — end of his words. And Beit Yosef wrote that so is the view of the Rosh, that a Jew must be there at the beginning of the milking lest he mix or the gentile place impure milk in the vessel before the Jew comes, even if there is no impure thing in the flock; it appears one needs sight even when there is no impure thing, and sitting alone does not suffice, since the Mordechai mentions language of sight. And it is difficult: what does it challenge if there is an impure thing — for behold there is sight? And one must say that the questioner also knew that at the time of sitting one cannot see, but he did not know the reason of fear; and necessarily it challenges: why does sitting help since at that moment he does not see? And it answers: nevertheless since he can see when standing he is afraid. But the Tur wrote explicitly like Shulchan Aruch here that he cannot see him; and so it is in Tosefta HaRav HaAruch; and one must say they resolve the Mordechai's difficulty that without impure thing, etc., one must at least sit outside the flock — meaning that he see that he does not introduce something impure at the time of milking. And Maharach of blessed memory equated the Mordechai's view with the Tur's view, and it is not clear.
To see him when he is standing. It appears all the more so it helps when he goes out and comes in, for even regarding slaughter by a Samaritan it helps when a Jew goes out and comes in, as is in the first chapter of Chullin folio 3; and so too in the Derishah.
And l'chatchila one must be, etc. This view is the Rosh's view that I mentioned shortly; only that Issur VeHeter added to it that he should see in the vessel, etc., as Rama concludes here. But I wonder at what Rama wrote: however b'dieved one need not be concerned for all this — the language implies even when there was an impure thing in the flock he says this, for behold this hagahah refers to the above; and this is obviously not so; and even without an impure thing it is written in Issur VeHeter klal 45 that it is forbidden so that no ruin come from it; rather a Jew must be there at the time the gentile begins to milk and see in the vessel, etc. And also for Rabbenu Tam, whom Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Hagahot Asheri in his name that he permits b'dieved if there was no Jew there at all — that is specifically if it is known the gentile milks for cheese, as he wrote in his name in Issur VeHeter and Darkei Moshe; but not to eat the milk; and so too in Shaarei Dura in the name of Rashi: without a Jew seeing, even if there is no impure thing in the flock it is forbidden; one who breaks a fence will be bitten by a snake. And necessarily one must say Rama refers to the first clause where there was no impure thing, and says that l'chatchila we are not lenient like the Tur's words, and it suffices that he sit outside the flock and not see the milking at all, but must see, etc., like the Rosh's words we mentioned; and on this he concludes that b'dieved one need not be concerned when there is no impure thing if he did not see at all, but it suffices that he stand outside, etc.; but if he does not stand outside it is obviously forbidden even b'dieved even if there is no impure thing. And Rama states his words anonymously here, and there is a stumbling block before one who reads and does not analyze. And regarding the halachah it appears one should be stringent and forbid even b'dieved whenever the Jew does not see the beginning of the milking, even when there is no impure thing there.
Minor or minor female. Darkei Moshe wrote in the name of Issur VeHeter: that is a nine-year-old.
And if the gentile milked some, etc. This applies to one who milks animals of a Jew in a pen behind a Jew's house; and explicitly he concludes there in Hagahot Shadal and Darkei Moshe: however milk that a gentile milked from his own animal we do not permit at all unless a Jew sees it — Maharai — end of his words.
Forbids vessels. And the same applies if it was pickled there from time to time — so it is in Issur VeHeter.
That if it was mixed with permitted matter all is permitted. Meaning: in a majority of permitted matter; for behold in responsum Maharil siman 35 he bases this permission on the law of bread of a gentile; and as Rama concludes roughly here; and above siman 112 seif 4 Rama wrote that bread of a gentile is nullified in a majority whether moist or dry, etc.; it appears we require a majority of permitted matter, and so too here; and see what I wrote at the end of siman 127.
