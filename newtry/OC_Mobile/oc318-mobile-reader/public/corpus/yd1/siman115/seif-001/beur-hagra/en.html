Lest he mixed, etc. Gemara there.
If it is known, etc. There, 4d: "if, etc." See Baer Heitev, seif katan 3. And that he knows, etc. Tur — for otherwise he would not be afraid. Ab initio, etc. Meaning: even when it is known that he has no four-by-four-cubit area in his flock; and as above — where it is required that a Jew sit outside; and they said there [the concern is] on account of mixing, and not actual mixing — for there is no four-by-four-cubit area there; and furthermore, we do not worry about this since he gains no [financial benefit] from it — only that he is not careful lest some [milk] remain in an impure vessel. And so wrote Mordechai and Smak. And the custom is, etc. This is derived from the above — meaning: even if he sees [the milking] from the beginning. But after the fact, etc. Meaning: even if he did not see [the milk] go into the vessel; and likewise [in the case] where it was milked into a vessel whose usual [purpose] is so, etc. But if he did not see at the beginning of the milking, even after the fact it is forbidden — as written in Darkei Moshe and Torat Chatat; and as stated in the Gemara and Shulchan Aruch above. And see the Shach, who holds that even seeing [the milk go] into the vessel is not sufficient after the fact — as Smak and Mordechai wrote, that for this reason a Jew must be sitting [there], as above; and that so too wrote Ohel Ha'ed — unlike what Darkei Moshe wrote in its name. And maidservants, etc. Like [the case of] a Jew sitting [there] — for [the non-Jew] is afraid; and so too here [in the case of a maidservant] he is afraid. And even a minor Jewish [child], etc. See Beit Yosef, siman 130, in the name of Rashba; and so wrote Mordechai, chapter 2, siman 830 — concerning what is stated in the Gemara (38b): "a Jew puts [on the fire] a piece of meat, etc. — a woman sets a pot, etc." — and Mordechai wrote in the name of Ravyah: "we are not concerned about exchanging or mixing, as explained in the Jerusalem Talmud — for example, when the infant is sitting and watching." However, if, etc. As above in the Shulchan Aruch — that specifically a Jew sitting, etc., even though he is, etc. — and see the Shach, who ruled that even if he was not present for part of the milking it is forbidden — unlike [the ruling of] the Rav [the author]. Milk of a non-Jew, etc. Like all rabbinic prohibitions — as above, at the end of siman 113; and even the Ra'ah agrees here, since there is a concern for a Biblical prohibition. But not, etc. For it is only forbidden based on custom — like the bread of a non-Jew. Milk of a non-Jew, etc. Gemara there: "and alternatively, even if you say he established [a fixed place] — etc." — and likewise butter; and as written in seif 3 — see there; and even if he cooks the butter. And that is the meaning of "and everything, etc."
