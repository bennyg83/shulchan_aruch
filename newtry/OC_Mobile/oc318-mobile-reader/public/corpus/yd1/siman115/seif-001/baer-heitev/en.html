Gentile. In the Tur he wrote: whether he milks for his own need or for a Jew's need (and so is the agreement of the poskim).
To see him. Taz wrote: but in any case we require that he sit outside at the side of the flock so that he see that he does not introduce there something impure at the time of milking.
Standing. Shach wrote: even if he goes out and comes in it is permitted, since the reason is that he is afraid; but if the gentile milks for his own need he must actually see at the time he milks that he does not mix into it something impure, since there is something impure with him in the flock; and if he milks for a Jew's need it is permitted in this if he can see him when he is standing.
At the beginning. This applies even to the first clause where there is no impure thing in the flock; and Bach wrote: however b'dieved Rabbenu Tam permitted if he did not see the beginning of the milking, and Shach challenged him and wrote that this is not relevant here, for Rabbenu Tam did not permit except when it is known that he milks for cheese — end of his words.
B'dieved. And Taz wrote: and regarding the halachah it appears one should be stringent to forbid even b'dieved whenever the Jew does not see the beginning of the milking, even when there is no impure thing there.
Jew. Darkei Moshe and Turei Chayim wrote: also in such a case it is permitted b'dieved according to Maharai; and so is the view of Maharshal.
Minor. Nine years old — Turei Chayim in the name of Issur VeHeter.
All of them. Shach wrote: this applies to one who milks the animal of a Jew in a pen behind a Jew's house; and if so one should wonder at Rama why it is forbidden if the Jew came after all were milked; and also Turei Chayim permits b'dieved in every matter; and perhaps some textual error fell in his words here in the hagahah. And all this is when he milks for kutach or to eat the milk; but if it is known that the gentile milks for cheese, its law will be explained in seif 2 — end of his words. (And Taz wrote: this applies to one who milks the animal of a Jew in a pen behind a Jew's house; however milk that a gentile milked from his own animal we do not permit at all unless a Jew sees — see there.)
That was cooked. And the same applies if it was pickled there within twenty-four hours — so too Issur VeHeter; and Shach wrote: l'chatchila it is forbidden to cook in it even if there will be a majority in the dish, for it is forbidden to add to any prohibition and nullify it, as above siman 99 seif 6 — see there; but if it was cooked in it b'dieved and there is a majority in the dish it is permitted. And some say regarding bishulei goyim end of seif 113 seif 11 that it does not forbid the vessels, for it is different there since the essence of their prohibition is from their words on account of wedding decrees; but here they decreed on it on account of concern for a Torah prohibition that is in the milk itself.
Was mixed. Shach wrote: it appears that a majority is nevertheless required in the dish, as above siman 112 seif 14 regarding bread of a gentile; and accordingly it appears that Rama's view is that cheese of a gentile is not nullified in a majority in a dish; but in Issur VeHeter he ruled explicitly that cheese of gentiles is nullified in a majority of a dish; and Shach's conclusion is like Rama's view to forbid cheese until sixty.
Helps. Specifically with stam milk; but when we know that the gentile's intent was to make cheese from it, it is permitted for a Jew to make cheese afterward from it. Shach.
