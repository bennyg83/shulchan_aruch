Shach in the name of Raavan (he wrote: I saw several times from many halachic authorities in a bird whose wing broke or was uprooted — called in Yiddish "bilik," meaning the chest — and they declared it kosher, for this is its way in youth when they handle it excessively, and they did not declare it treifah because of the lung hidden there, end of his words):
Broke. Taz wrote: for that which we are stringent in siman 53 regarding a broken wing near the body — if so, all the more so regarding one broken rib in a bird in the place where the lung is hidden, one must be stringent for practical treifah, end of his words; and so Bach; and it is implied from his words there that even in an animal it is treifah when there is a spur on the broken rib — even if the fragments rejoined, if one sees there was a spur — treifah; but if doubtful whether there was a spur — kosher from double doubt; and even though we declare treifah in a bird's wing even without a spur — nevertheless, since it is only from the force of a stringency, we are lenient in ribs, since in any case it is implied one may distinguish between wing and rib. And Shach wrote further: nevertheless requires further study in these laws, since all early and later poskim did not mention this, and wrote anonymously: its ribs broke — kosher, end of his words; and Ran wrote: when a minority of ribs broke there is no distinction whether the break inclines outward or into the cavity, whether skin and flesh remain or not — nevertheless kosher; and Taz wrote: from this it is proven not like Taz who forbids one broken rib in a bird:
