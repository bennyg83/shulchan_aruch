(2) The animal has eighteen vertebrae on its back from where the neck joint ends until its end, and so they are arranged. The first vertebra adjacent to the neck joint has two ribs stuck in it — one rib on the right and one on the left — and the rib is stuck in the vertebra like a peg in a hole; and these two ribs are called chest. After this vertebra there are eleven more vertebrae in which are stuck the twenty-two large ribs that have marrow — eleven on the right and eleven on the left; and these ribs are also stuck in the vertebra from this side and that like a peg in a hole — and these are called large ribs. After these vertebrae there are three more vertebrae in which six ribs without marrow are stuck — three on the right and three on the left — stuck in the vertebra like a peg in a hole; and these are called small ribs. After these vertebrae there are three more vertebrae in which no ribs are stuck at all — and these are called in the Gemara "the end of Keifli." Behold in all eighteen vertebrae, as explained in mishnah Ohalot chapter 1 that there are eighteen vertebrae — see there. Levush siman 12 seif 1 ot 1, Beit Yitzchak ch. 1. And we examined sheep that are among us, both lambs and goats, and there are only thirteen ribs in all, both small and large, stuck in thirteen vertebrae — thirteen on this side and thirteen on that; and there are five more vertebrae without ribs, but the vertebrae are eighteen; if so, it is found that the first two ribs cut with the chest — one from here and one from here — are stuck in one vertebra; and there are eleven more vertebrae in which large ribs are stuck, eleven from here and eleven from here; and afterward there is one vertebra in which two small ribs are stuck, one from here and one from here; and afterward five vertebrae without ribs called the end of Keifli. Zivchei Tzedek ot 2; and see below ot 10.
