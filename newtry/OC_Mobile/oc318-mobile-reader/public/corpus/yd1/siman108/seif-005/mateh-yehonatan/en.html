Behold the Rav Tzemach Tzedek wrote to permit soap called zeif in Yiddish that needs tasting during manufacture whether it lacks salt or has excess salt — that tasting is permitted since tasting is rabbinic and nat bar lichtamei is rabbinic and here since it is nat bar lichtamei on account of ash which is the ashes — it is two rabbinic matters and permitted l'chatchila; and they challenged him from what is taught in Chullin daf 111: a bowl in which one salted meat — forbidden to eat hot radish and cold onion cut with a knife in kutach, etc.; and the conclusion is because this one can be tasted and this one cannot be tasted — and it is not as Tzemach Tzedek wrote regarding the bowl too one can taste afterward and it is nat bar lichtamei with two rabbinic matters and permitted to taste; and one may say certainly not all heter taste regarding soap is rabbinic only because what they forbade tasting is lest one come to eat it — that is regarding issur that is not for spoilage where there is room to decree lest one come to actually eat — unlike issur for spoilage like soap and the like where certainly there is no room to decree — therefore permitted; unlike there regarding the bowl one may well decree tasting lest eating, for because of this spoiled taste in the bowl he will not refrain from eating — and one may well decree tasting lest eating:
