(Seif 5 in hagahah) But forbidden to taste it even though one does not swallow it. And this is specifically a definite issur; but a doubtful issur — permitted to taste and spit out to clarify if there is issur at all, like regarding tasting the bitter water above siman 42; and see in Taz siman 98. And in what I wrote — do not challenge what the hagahah wrote here; and one may say: there he does not take anything in his mouth, only licks with his tongue; unlike here where he takes wine in his mouth — there is concern he will absorb it, which is a Torah issur of doubtful forbidden mixture — forbidden from the Torah; and such a case Beit Yosef wrote in Orach Chaim siman 613 in name of Semak regarding Yom Kippur, and as Mechaber wrote in Orach Chaim siman 567 regarding public fast — forbidden to taste and spit out — see there; however it appears there that the reason is not lest he absorb, for even if he knows to restrain himself from absorbing — forbidden to taste; and so Orach Chaim end of seif 10 — permitted to taste and spit out without a blessing and we are not concerned he will absorb; and one must say that in issurim they were stringent even when he knows to restrain himself from absorbing — the tasting itself is forbidden; and so the language of the hagahah here [and language of Turei Chayim end of general rule 36 — forbidden to taste anything even if he does not eat, for it is no less than reicha being significant — so appears explicitly as he wrote; and Mahari Mintz there challenged Ribash — lest he come to eat it; and according to what I wrote — do not challenge] and Orach Chaim siman 567, and regarding blessing of enjoyment where there is no benefit — therefore permitted. And see in Sefer Be'er Yaakov; but Maharshal forbids to taste and lick with his tongue, and so Acharonim brought Mahari Mintz general rule 88 s.k. 3; and in liver it is good, for mostly bitter taste will be found. And also Ra'ah permits without qualification, and because of blood one may say he needs to rinse visible blood from it, and he only permitted in spoiled issur — see there; and so in responsum Tzemach Tzedek siman 47 he permitted to taste lye because it is spoiled and the Sages did not forbid in spoiled except eating and not tasting; and so Tosafot in Yevamot:
