In the oven. Taz and Shach wrote that this applies specifically when one of them is fatty — even the kosher one — for the heter goes and seasons the issur [and even though we establish everywhere that what became forbidden cannot go and forbid where the issur itself cannot go — Maharai resolved that reicha is different because seasoning conveys smell and weak taste with it]. And see above siman 105 seif 5; but when both are lean — permitted for all, even l'chatchila; only Turei Chayim wrote that it is good to be stringent l'chatchila — but Taz and Shach challenged him; and Taz also wrote that if they are of liver or of meat salted in a vessel without holes — it does not forbid through reicha; and Maharshal permits with liver and forbids with meat salted in a vessel without holes; and all this is l'chatchila according to the halachah, but b'dieved all is permitted. And regarding bread — poskim wrote in name of Rabeinu Tam that reicha does not apply at all, therefore bread does not forbid chametz on Pesach baked with matzah — end of his words [Issur VeHeter wrote that our challah, which is rabbinic, is permitted l'chatchila to bake with bread]:
Permitted. For reicha is not significant b'dieved; but bread that he baked with the roast — Rambam and Mechaber wrote above end of siman 97 that it is forbidden to eat it with kutach, for since it is possible to eat it without kutach — it is called l'chatchila:
And its mouth. Taz wrote, and it implies that we require both — large and also its mouth open; and even though in siman 97 Tur wrote: specifically in a small oven like theirs, but in our large ovens — permitted — and he did not mention open — it appears plain that they hinted in what they wrote "in our ovens," for it is known that all our ovens are open; and Shach wrote: specifically if its mouth is open truly like a stove, but if only open a little above — even in a large oven — forbidden l'chatchila; and so Turei Chayim — see there:
For meat. Meaning: not to bake meat with milk in one oven:
Small. Explanation: even if closed. Taz:
To eat it. Even in a large oven with its mouth open above [since he has heter to eat them with meat — it is l'chatchila]; and so appears in Turei Chayim simanim and see below siman 122 seif 6 in the hagahah:
To buy. Turei Chayim wrote; nevertheless the custom is to be lenient to buy from gentiles things cooked in their pots, for there is no concern of gentile cooking, for stam their pots are not within their day; and even though nat bar lichtmile is forbidden l'chatchila — nevertheless we do not consider the purchase l'chatchila, therefore they practice leniency here in Krakow to buy whole nuts that gentiles cook, or other things:
Sharp. Or that was made for smell and baked even with bread — in large ovens we certainly say reicha is significant, even b'dieved, even if the oven mouth is open on the side — end of Issur VeHeter:
Permitted. Issur VeHeter wrote; however l'chatchila it is good to be stringent:
In milk. Shach wrote: this refers to a small amount of bread or where there is recognition in the bread; but in much bread without recognition — forbidden even to eat it alone — a decree lest he come to eat it with milk, as above siman 97:
It sweated. And even if made for smell (and its smell is still in the oven) — no concern one after the other if the pan does not sweat beneath both of them. Shach:
Loss. This means great loss — Shach; and Taz wrote: even though in roasting too we say absorbed issur does not go from piece to piece without gravy — nevertheless regarding reicha one may be lenient, for whatever enters the doubt assists in nullification:
Small. Shach wrote — requires study: in Tosafot Avodah Zarah it appears explicitly that even in large ovens it is forbidden — see there; and one must say the Rav holds Tosafot follow their reasons, for they hold reicha is significant in small ovens, therefore they are more stringent; but for us who establish reicha is not significant — one need not forbid at all in large ovens — forced and requires study — end of his words:
To distinguish. Shach wrote: it appears that for Mordechai there is no distinction between an issur that forbids in any amount and other issurim; and so appears in Orach Chaim siman 447 seif 1; and requires study — in Mordechai it is not found except that reicha does not apply to chametz bread baked with matzah, because we do not find smell of bread in bread; but in that reasoning of "any amount" we do not find that Tosafot disagree regarding a dish; however in responsum of Maharil I found that for one who says reicha is not significant — there is no distinction between chametz that forbids in any amount and other issurim — end of his words:
