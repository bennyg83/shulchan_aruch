One may not roast kosher meat, etc. — even if the kosher is of a large species and the issur thin — for the smell of the kosher seasons the issur and returns and forbids the heter; so Rashi explained in chapter "How to roast"; and above siman 105 seif 5 I wrote Maharai's words in what he disagrees between this and that which "what became forbidden cannot forbid except where the issur itself can go"; and Maharai wrote in his rulings siman 76 that nevertheless permitted l'chatchila to roast liver in an oven with a fatty dish, for one who says reicha is significant — for all blood is lean and the concern that the dish seasons the blood and returns and forbids the dish does not apply, for blood absorbed in liver is a weak thing, etc.; and Rashal in his Or Zarua siman 35 on this — specifically with liver that is entirely heter he permitted; but meat forbidden on account of blood, such as salted in a vessel without holes — forbids meat roasted or cooked with it in an oven, for we say the piece itself becomes nevelah even regarding blood; and Maharach disagrees — all blood is lean and does not forbid through reicha; and so appears Maharai's language in my opinion; and all this is l'chatchila according to halachah — b'dieved all permitted; and it is plain that if the dish too is a lean thing — even l'chatchila permitted; and so Beit Yosef siman 97 in name of Terumat HaDeshen and Maharai in my opinion — he only deals with a fatty dish; and so Beit Yosef cited his words in siman 97; and for this reason he wrote on him that its taste does not appear to be lenient; and Turei Chayim general rule 35 law 3 brought Maharai's words in my opinion regarding both lean — and wrote on this that one should be stringent l'chatchila, since Beit Yosef wrote its taste does not appear and did not examine this as I explained; and regarding bread — poskim wrote in name of Rabeinu Tam that reicha does not apply at all — therefore bread does not forbid chametz on Pesach baked with matzah:
And if the oven is large, etc. It appears we require both — large and also its mouth open; and so Tur wrote here; and even though in siman 97 he wrote: specifically in a small oven like theirs, but in our large ovens — permitted — and did not mention open — it appears plain they hinted in "our ovens," for all our ovens are known to be open; and Darkei Moshe forced to disagree — above deals with meat and milk and here with other issurim; and Maharach resolved — above deals with b'dieved and here l'chatchila — and all this unnecessarily, for it is plain as he wrote; and Maharai wrote in Sha'arim and Rashal chapter Gid HaNasheh siman 14 brings him — according to Ashkenazi view who tied the reason because their ovens were covered — we would not need to be stringent when the oven is somewhat large that vapor can spread, even if it does not hold twelve se'ahs:
And b'dieved to be lenient even in a small oven. Explanation: even closed:
For this is called b'dieved for this matter. Turei Chayim wrote general rule 35; nevertheless the custom is to be lenient to buy from gentiles things cooked in their pots, for there is no concern of gentile cooking, for stam their pots are not within their day; and even though nat bar lichtmile is forbidden l'chatchila — nevertheless we do not consider the purchase l'chatchila; therefore they practice leniency here in Krakow to buy whole nuts that gentiles cook, or other things:
And even b'dieved forbidden if both uncovered. This is Or Zarua's view to explain thus Mordechai's words in Perek 3, as Turei Chayim wrote in his name — even in a large open oven forbidden; but Rashal in Piskei HaGadol siman 15 wrote on Mordechai's words — specifically in a small or covered oven, but in a large open oven — all permit; and Maharach agreed. And in my opinion it follows Rama's ruling, for in Mordechai there he compares to hot bread and open barrel — which is forbidden even b'dieved, as Shulchan Aruch wrote in seif 4:
Even everything in the oven, etc. For we can say they should not join, for even in roasting we say absorbed issur does not go from piece to piece without gravy — nevertheless regarding reicha one may be lenient, for whatever enters the doubt assists in nullification:
