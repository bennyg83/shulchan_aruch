Since most members of that trade, etc. At the end of this siman, the Beit Yosef cites the Rokeach as follows: "It appears that if a woman touched a cauldron or pot and a small area of her flesh became blackened, she is not particular about it, even though a small amount adhered to her flesh." End quote. On this basis I ruled leniently concerning a woman who immersed and afterward found a black mark on one place of her body where she had touched the walls of the bathhouse, which were black from the bathhouse smoke. She does not require another immersion, for this resembles the cited case, and it follows all the more so. Her hands must be cleaner than her body, since her hands are always exposed and she is more particular about their cleanliness than about that of her body. This is not comparable to ink, which constitutes a chatzitzah as written at the beginning of seif 15, because there an actual substance of ink constitutes the chatzitzah, whereas this black appearance is merely superficial dirt. The Beit Yosef similarly cites the Rashba as distinguishing between dye and ink, as appears to me.
