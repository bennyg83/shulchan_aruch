These are the things that interpose: wool-threads, linen-threads, and straps with which they wind the hair on the head — she should not immerse with them until she loosens them; and if they are within the braid of her hair, loosening does not help. And if they are wound on other places of the body, she should not immerse with them until she loosens them — except if they are wound on the neck, for they do not interpose, since she does not tighten them. But a “katla,” which is a smooth, broad strap that she winds around her neck, interposes, because she tightens herself strongly so that her flesh will protrude and she will appear plump; and since the strap is smooth and broad, it does not injure her (Rashi).
