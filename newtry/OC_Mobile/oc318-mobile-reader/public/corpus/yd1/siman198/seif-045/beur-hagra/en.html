(Addendum) She should not immerse, etc. Mishnah 2 of chapter 9. (End of addendum.)
And if she immersed, there are, etc. According to our version of the Tosefta at the beginning of chapter 8: If his feet were covered with dust and he descended and immersed in a mikveh containing forty seah, he is pure; if he rubbed them or entered hot water, he is impure. This version is difficult, for logically the reverse should be true. The concluding clause likewise states: If a kettle was filled with coals and one immersed it, it remains impure; if he rubbed it or immersed it in hot water, it is pure. The opening clause should therefore be corrected accordingly. The Rash copies it this way in chapter 10, Mishnah 4, and the Mishnah cited above compares the two cases. Rambam does not record the clause concerning rubbing in the case of a person, and omits the Tosefta's ruling concerning a kettle entirely. This requires study. (End of addendum.)
