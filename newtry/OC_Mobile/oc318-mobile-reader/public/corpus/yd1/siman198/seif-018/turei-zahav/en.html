Excrement beneath the fingernail, etc. The Tur concludes: "Nevertheless, people are not ordinarily particular about this. Therefore, for one who is not particular, it does not constitute a chatzitzah." End quote. The Beit Yosef writes that this applies also to dough, and he wonders how the Tur could distinguish in this manner when the Mishnah made no such distinction. Therefore, he did not include this ruling in the Shulchan Aruch. The Rama explains in the Darkei Moshe that the Tur discusses an exceedingly small amount about which people are not particular, as the Semag and Semak write in Rabbeinu Tam's name, that even excrement or dough, if there is such a minute quantity that people are not particular about it, is a minority that one does not mind and therefore does not constitute a chatzitzah. End quote.
When it is beyond the flesh, it constitutes a chatzitzah. The Beit Yosef cites the Semag in Rabbeinu Tam's name that this applies specifically to clay resembling dough that adheres very strongly, such as potter's clay, but not to other clay or excrement. The proof is that we learned in Mikvaos that these do not constitute a chatzitzah: smears of excrement upon one's flesh and excrement beneath the fingernail. End quote. The Shulchan Aruch does not mention this substantial leniency here. Nevertheless, it appears to me that this resolves the difficulty raised by the Levush as to why people are not careful about this when washing their hands. According to what I have written, the matter is clear, for regarding immersion of the hands we may certainly rely upon Rabbeinu Tam that only potter's clay and the like constitute a chatzitzah.
