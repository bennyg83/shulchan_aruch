She should not immerse standing upright, because there are places that are concealed in her; and she should not bend too much until her concealed places stick to one another. Rather, she bends a little, until the concealed places of the beit ha’ervah are visible, as they appear when she arranges [them]; and that under her breasts is visible, as it is visible when she nurses her child; and that under the armpit is visible, as it is visible when she weaves while standing. And she does not need to spread her thighs apart from one another too much, and also not to spread her arms away from the body too much; rather, as they are when she walks. And if she changed, for example, she bent too much or stood too upright, the immersion counted for her (Aruch and Sefer Mitzvos Gadol and Rashba”tz and Rambam); and there is one who says that it did not count.
