Like a woman weaving while standing. So appears in the Gemara, but the Tur writes, "like one harvesting olives." The Beit Yosef wonders why he altered the Gemara's wording, since harvesting olives is stated in the Gemara concerning a man, and he gives his resolution. It appears to me that the Tur was uncertain because regarding negaim it states, "like a woman weaving with her right hand," as cited by the Beit Yosef. He was uncertain whether this measure is required specifically for the right side, while nothing is required for the left, or whether the Gemara merely described the ordinary case. He therefore used the measure that was certain to him regarding a man, applying it likewise to a woman.
And she need not distance, etc. The Beit Yosef asks that the Tur already gave another measure, namely like a woman kneading, etc. It appears to me that the Tur followed the Gemara's wording. Reish Lakish said that a woman should immerse only in the manner of her normal growth, meaning her normal posture while walking, as we learned: a man is viewed like one hoeing and harvesting olives, etc. Just as that measure applies regarding the examination of negaim by a kohen, so too it applies regarding immersion. However, not everyone knows precisely how to evaluate those measures. Therefore, the Tur gave a measure that each person can estimate for herself, namely that her limbs should be slightly separated as they ordinarily are while walking. Both descriptions refer to the same measure. This is likewise the Tur's intent. After recording the measure stated in the Gemara, he explains that since not everyone can estimate it, a woman might mistakenly think that she must spread her thighs very widely and distance her arms far from her body to avoid doubt. He therefore states that this is unnecessary. She should estimate the separation according to her normal manner of walking. My revered father-in-law, of blessed memory, explains that this refers to haughty women who lift their arms out to their sides. It is far-fetched to say that the Gemara and poskim established their measure according to such women, for this is the conduct of the daughters of Zion that Scripture condemned.
And some say that her immersion was not valid. I find this difficult. Why should it be invalid after the fact? How is this different from seif 30, where we say that the water reaches first? Here too, the water reached those folds before they adhered through her excessive straightening or bending. The difficulty is even greater according to the Tur, who is not concerned even with mud upon her feet, as I mentioned above in seif 33. One can answer that here there is concern that the folds of the body may adhere above the water. Thus, when she enters the water they are already pressed together. According to this, if she first entered the water up to her neck, the immersion is valid after the fact in every case. It appears proper for every woman to do so, for this at least provides an added advantage if she bends excessively without noticing.
