Two hairs, etc. In Tur this law appears twice in this siman — once here and second where he wrote majority of hair tied strand, etc.; and his intent we find this law twice in Gemara — once in chapter What woman all agree regarding hair that does not interpose — meaning if she wound head hair with other hair does not interpose because she does not tighten well; and second in last chapter of Niddah Beit Yosef brings one strand interposes two I do not know three do not interpose — that deals with woman's own hairs if majority tied one by one then interposes specifically; and from this Tur below deals; and here deals adhesion of other hair to her hair; and practical difference between those two — here any interposition even minority is interposition like wool threads seif 2 and as I wrote there; therefore Tur did not write here need majority of her hair as below; but below deals with own hairs tied — need specifically majority for otherwise not particular; and Beit Yosef wrote on Tur's words here deals with very particular, etc. — not precise for this applies only below for reason I mentioned; but certainly regarding number they are equal — as with own hair depends how hairs tied one by one or two by two — likewise if wound one hair on her hair it interposes even if wound many hairs around this not in that place — in each winding one hair because tight; but if in each winding took two hairs or more no interposition; and this Tur wrote here in alternative when of two hairs — meaning each winding as I wrote; and these words also fit Ran Beit Yosef brought — "they agree regarding hair threads do not interpose" — meaning specifically on girls' heads hair does not interpose because cannot tighten well; but to tie one strand alone or strand with companion they tighten for we say in last chapter of Niddah one tied strand interposes, etc.; and even to wind those hair threads in her hairs also permitted because impossible those hair threads be more than one strand, etc. — end quote; behold he also wrote first law deals with winding around her hair and tying one strand deals with her own hair; and what he wrote "or one strand with companion" — also called one strand whether alone or with companion as Beit Yosef afterward wrote; and properly ruled like R. Yochanan Beit Yosef brings; and in vain Bach wrote here ruled like Rava — rather Ran concluded here in winding other hair no interposition because they do not make this winding in one strand; and Tur wrote if made from one hair it is interposition and no law distinction between them except factual circumstance; and per this Shulchan Aruch's words explained — what seif 4 wrote hair threads do not interpose means winding law we mentioned and there too distinction how winding made one hair or more; and there interposition if made from one hair even not majority and not particular for like wool threads seif 2 water does not enter until loosened; and here seif 5 deals with second law — her own hairs and depends on particular as Shulchan Aruch concluded here.
