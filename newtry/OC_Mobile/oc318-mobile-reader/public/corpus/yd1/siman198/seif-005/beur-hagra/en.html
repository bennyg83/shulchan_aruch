And this, etc., until there is, etc. As stated above in seif 1. See Rashi on Eruvin 4b, s.v. 'most of it,' etc., and the Sema. Although Rashi follows his own view that there is no interposition on the flesh, while Tosafot disagree, as stated there, s.v. 'by Torah law,' etc., and the Sema, nevertheless most halachic authorities agree with Rashi on this point, that a majority of the hair by itself constitutes an interposition.
(Addendum) Most of her hair. Rambam writes this in the name of the Geonim, but disputes them and holds that the head is counted together with the body, so that only a majority of the body constitutes an interposition. The Raavad, in his glosses, agrees with the Geonim, and this is also the view of the other halachic authorities. It appears to me that there is proof for their position from Bava Kamma 82a, which states that by Torah law one must inspect lest the hair be knotted, etc. Knots are found only on the head, yet by Torah law an interposition requires a majority together with being particular about it. One cannot say that the head is combined with the body, for the Gemara then says, 'alternatively, lest there be something repulsive,' etc., implying that the first concern is solely for knots. (End of addendum.)
