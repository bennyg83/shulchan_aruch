A partially detached fingernail. The Beit Yosef discusses this at length because he found it difficult to understand why there is a chatzitzah when a minority remains attached more than when a majority remains attached. He was therefore compelled to explain that the case concerns some other intervening substance upon the detached portion. The Mordechai, cited by the Beit Yosef, writes: "When most of it remains, it constitutes a chatzitzah, specifically because most is considered as the whole. When only a minority remains, it does not constitute a chatzitzah, since it stands to separate and has already begun to separate." End quote. Nevertheless, it remains unresolved why the small portion still attached should not constitute a chatzitzah once most has separated. Furthermore, how does this differ from a partially detached limb that stands to be cut, which constitutes a chatzitzah even when most has separated? My revered father-in-law, of blessed memory, explains that a craftsman is required to cut the limb, but I do not understand his distinction, for ultimately it stands to be cut. It appears to me that there is certainly no chatzitzah at the portion still attached, since that place was never exposed, as the Beit Yosef stated in his question. Rather, the law concerns the separated portion. If most of the nail remains and only a minority has split, the opening is narrow and water cannot enter the split. But if most has split away, the opening is widened and water can enter there, so it is fit for water to reach. Regarding a partially detached limb or flesh, even when most has separated, one does not leave it hanging, for that causes great pain. Rather, she constantly holds it as close to the body as possible, and therefore that place is not fit for water to reach. This appears to be the proper explanation, with God's help.
