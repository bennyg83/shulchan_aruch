Therefore, etc. See Eruvin 4b and Sukkah 6a: 'interpositions,' etc.; and, 'his flesh,' excluding, etc.
If, etc., even, etc. This follows from the Tosefta cited by the Rash in chapter 9 of Mikvaot regarding necklaces, etc., and rings, etc. The Raavad objected that this is a minority about which one is not particular. He answered that she is particular about it while kneading. See the Rosh, chapter 10, siman 26. Likewise, regarding wool and linen threads, the Rosh wrote there, siman 25, that she is particular about them while washing. The Torat HaBayit likewise rules this way at its conclusion. See there.
Or even, etc. In chapter 9 of Mikvaot, Mishnah 2 and its concluding clause regarding a woman, and Mishnah 3 and its concluding clause regarding a man, Rabbi Eliezer says, 'one,' etc. It follows that, according to the first Tanna, the distinction does not depend on whether this individual is particular, but on the normal practice of most women, etc. This is the point of their dispute. The Torat HaBayit likewise writes this.
And initially, etc. See the end of chapter 7 of Bava Kamma, 82a, and Tosafot there, s.v. 'and that she should,' etc.
