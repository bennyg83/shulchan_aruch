Her entire body at one time. For we expound, "He shall bathe, and the sun shall set, and he shall be pure": just as the setting of his sun is entirely at one time, so too his bathing must be entirely at one time.
And if people are accustomed, etc. The explanation of this wording appears to be as follows. If some people are particular about this, although most people are not, and this woman too is ordinarily particular about it at other times but is not particular now, it constitutes a chatzitzah. Likewise, even if she is never particular about it, if most people are particular about it, it constitutes a chatzitzah. However, if only some people are particular about it and she is never particular, it does not constitute a chatzitzah. See what I wrote in seif 18.
Since most people are accustomed, etc. The Beit Yosef was uncertain regarding a case in which other people are not particular but this woman is particular. The Darkei Moshe wrote that the Mordechai states explicitly that it constitutes a chatzitzah, and so too wrote my revered father-in-law, of blessed memory.
And if it covers most of the body, etc. According to Torah law, a substance constitutes a chatzitzah only when it covers most of the body and one is particular about it. The Sages decreed concerning a substance covering most of the body even when one is not particular, because of a case in which it covers most of the body and one is particular. They likewise decreed concerning a substance covering a minority when one is particular, because of a case in which it covers most of the body and one is particular. However, when it covers a minority and one is not particular, there are two grounds for leniency, and they made no decree at all, for that would be a decree upon a decree. What the Rama writes later, that this is a decree because of substances that constitute a chatzitzah, even when they cover only a minority, refers to the ideal practice. After the fact, however, we do not make a decree upon a decree.
