Since it has been clarified above that a woman's nail-cutting is only a matter of custom that they were stringent about upon themselves — it seems to me regarding what is written in Mordechai, laws of niddah, cited by Hagahot Shaarei Dura: that if her immersion falls on Chol HaMoed it is proper that a non-Jewish maidservant cut her nails — since having a non-Jew perform labor is a rabbinic prohibition, and in the presence of a mitzvah they did not enact the decree; and if she has no non-Jewish maidservant, she should carefully scrape the dirt from beneath the nail — end of quote. This only applies on Chol HaMoed; but on Shabbat and Yom Tov, if she forgot to cut her nails, she should not have a non-Jewish maidservant cut them — for since nail-cutting is not a complete mitzvah when scraping beneath them suffices alone, why should we nullify the rabbinic prohibition of having a non-Jew perform labor? And furthermore, it is a stringency that leads to leniency: even though the non-Jewish maidservant is cutting, nevertheless the Jewish woman assists by stretching her hands toward her — it is as though she herself is doing this labor, which is biblically forbidden on Shabbat and Yom Tov — comparable to what we find in siman 181 regarding rounding the corner of the head, that even the one having his head rounded is liable if he assisted by turning himself to allow rounding; and this is unavoidable when she allows the nails to be cut by a non-Jew without the Jewish woman stretching her hands toward her. And one cannot say she simply should not immerse when this occurs on Shabbat or Yom Tov — for on Chol HaMoed they also permitted it through scraping without a non-Jewish maidservant; why should Shabbat and Yom Tov be worse than Chol HaMoed without a maidservant? Rather it is clear: on Shabbat and Yom Tov she should not cut them through a non-Jewish maidservant, but should scrape them well and immerse. Mordechai only permitted cutting through a non-Jew specifically on Chol HaMoed — on which they were even lenient to cut them herself; and so we hold in Orach Chayyim siman 532 regarding immersion for a mitzvah according to all opinions, as Rema wrote there; and Mordechai — being a student of Maharam who was stringent that nail-cutting on Chol HaMoed is forbidden except through a non-Jew, as Beit Yosef wrote there — required cutting through a non-Jew according to his own view. But according to how we hold in siman 532, permitting cutting on Chol HaMoed without modification, on Shabbat and Yom Tov one should not cut them at all, but should scrape them well. So it seems to me clearly. And I have seen someone who erred in this and ordered cutting on Yom Tov through a non-Jew — and this was not done correctly in my view.
