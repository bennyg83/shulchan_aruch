Her companion should not hold her. We learned in the Mishnah, chapter 8 of Mikvaos: One who holds a person or utensils and immerses them leaves them impure, but if he first rinsed his hands in water they are pure, because the liquid upon his hands joins the mikveh water and there is no chatzitzah. R' Shimon says that he should loosen his hands so that water enters them. The Rambam and Ramban explain that the first Tanna is more stringent than R' Shimon. According to R' Shimon, loosening the hands suffices; according to the first Tanna it does not suffice, because of a decree lest he fail to loosen them. Therefore, he ruled that one must specifically rinse his hands first. I already wrote in siman 120, seif 2, that he may rinse his hands even with detached water, unlike the Rama there. Their explanation implies that whenever he rinsed his hands first, he need not loosen them at all. Even if he grips the immersed person or utensil tightly, there is no concern, since his hands were already wet. Otherwise, what benefit is achieved by rinsing, for even after rinsing we should decree lest he grip tightly? There is further proof from the Rashba. In Toras HaBayis HaAruch he challenges the Rambam and Ramban's explanation, because in many places no such decree is made, such as rings and bracelets, which the Tur mentions shortly, where immersion is permitted when they are loose. The Ra'ah answers in Bedek HaBayis that only when one holds a person or utensils for immersion is there concern that he may grip tightly so they do not fall, whereas elsewhere why should we decree without cause? Thus, if he rinses his hands first, there is no concern even if he subsequently grips tightly. The Rashba explains that R' Shimon is stringent. The first Tanna holds that rinsing alone suffices, while R' Shimon holds that since one ordinarily presses his hands upon the person or utensil being immersed, there is no connection even through liquid wet enough to moisten. This is unlike the feet of one immersing upon the floor of the mikveh, mentioned below in seif 30, where the water reaches the feet first and a wet layer remains connected to the mikveh. Here, R' Shimon maintains that one holding utensils for immersion ordinarily presses his hands upon them so they do not fall, and likewise when holding another person, thereby preventing the liquid upon his hands from forming any connection. The first Tanna is not concerned for this and treats the wet layer as connected. The halacha follows the first Tanna, so one need not loosen his hands. If he does loosen them, however, it certainly helps. The Rashba concludes that even according to the first Tanna he must not press excessively when holding the item for immersion. So it is written in Toras HaBayis and Mishmeres HaBayis, authored by the Rashba himself. The Tur agrees with the Rashba, and the Shulchan Aruch therefore rules accordingly, that either loosening or first rinsing her hands suffices. My revered father-in-law, of blessed memory, wonders why he ruled unlike the Rambam and Ramban, but there is no difficulty. The Rashba and Tur are certainly reliable, especially in a rabbinic matter, since according to Torah law only a substance covering most of the body and about which one is particular constitutes a chatzitzah. There is another dispute between the Rambam and Ramban on one side and the Rashba on the other. According to the Rambam and Ramban, as I proved above, even a tight grip does not constitute a chatzitzah if the hands were rinsed first. The Rashba explicitly writes that although the halacha follows the first Tanna, one must not press excessively, but should hold in the normal manner, and we are not concerned that he might hold tightly. The Mordechai appears to agree, for he rules that one should not place a full utensil inside a bucket of water and immerse it in a pit, because the weight of the utensil creates a chatzitzah, as I cited in siman 120, seif 2. This accords with the Rashba, since the utensil's weight resembles a person gripping it tightly. Accordingly, when the Shulchan Aruch rules here like the Rashba that loosening helps, and then writes that rinsing her hands first helps even without loosening, it appears to me that although she did not loosen her grip, she still must not hold her companion tightly. Otherwise, prior rinsing would not help. We merely do not suspect that she will grip tightly, as I explained in the Rashba's view. The Derishah writes that from here there is complete permission for a woman who immerses while her companion pushes her into the water to be held by the hand during the push, provided that the companion first rinsed her hands in water. She need not separate her hands from the immersing woman. This is unlike the practice of our women, who separate their hands at the moment of pushing because they believe there is no possible permission to hold her. End quote. He writes correctly, for an explicit Mishnah teaches that one may hold a person, and all the poskim rule accordingly. They were not concerned that she might grip excessively. This is certainly so according to the Rambam and Ramban, who maintain that even a tight grip causes no harm whenever she first rinsed her hands.
In water beforehand. This means even detached water. When it subsequently joins the mikveh water, it becomes valid as well, since liquid wet enough to moisten is considered connected. This is unlike what the Rama writes in siman 120, seif 2, that specifically mikveh water is required, as I demonstrated there that he was imprecise in this matter.
