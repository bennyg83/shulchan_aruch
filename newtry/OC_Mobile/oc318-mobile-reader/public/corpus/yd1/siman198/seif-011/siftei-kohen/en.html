And if it is not visible, etc. Beit Yosef was uncertain about the meaning of 'not visible' and 'visible' — to be stringent: anything that is level with the flesh is called 'visible,' and when it is sunken it is called 'not visible'; and it implies anything sunken that is not level with the flesh — even if the wood appears lying within her inner flesh into which it is stuck — is called 'not visible' since it does not appear level with the flesh; and so Taz.
If flesh grew over it, etc. The Beit Yosef wrote that this means the splinter or metal is not visible, but if it is visible it interposes. This requires study, for the language of the poskim implies that even where it is visible, since flesh has grown over it and it is embedded in the body, it is treated as part of the body and does not interpose. Also, if it is not visible at all, why would it need to be stated? It is obvious that a concealed object covered by flesh cannot interpose. Rather, it appears that the case is where its end is somewhat visible, and nevertheless it does not interpose because it is fixed in the body and she does not object to it. However, if it protrudes in a way that she is particular about it, or it is loose and fit to be removed, then it interposes. This is the correct distinction.
