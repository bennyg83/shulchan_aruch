The dry discharge of the eye, etc. It appears that the entire discussion here, and the distinctions between within three days and after three days, or between outside the eye and inside the eye, apply only to eating terumah and other taharot. For with taharot, since the Torah is strict even about an attached hair and the like, as stated in Shas and poskim, they were also stringent about these matters. But for a woman immersing for her husband, there is no chatzitzah in this, as the eye is a concealed place, and every case here is no worse than the inside of the mouth. Although the Sages required that concealed places be fit for contact with water, this discharge does not prevent such fitness. This is also implied by the Tur and the poskim, who wrote this law in the laws of chatzitzah for taharot and did not mention it regarding a woman's immersion for her husband except insofar as the custom is to be careful. Nevertheless, lechatchilah one should be careful about anything that could be called filth or a substance upon the body, but bedieved, and certainly where she cannot remove it without pain or danger, it appears that one may rely on the lenient view.
