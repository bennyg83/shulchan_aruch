It requires study why the ruling follows Rabbi Shimon ben Shalosh, since we hold that the halachah follows him only in two matters, as stated in chapter 4 of Chullin, 75b. And those who have, etc. As stated above in seif 5, two hairs do not constitute an interposition. Moreover, since they cannot be removed, this is their normal manner of growth, as stated in chapter 8 of Yevamot, 78a, regarding a fetus. The same principle applies to vessels. A Tosefta cited by the Rash on chapter 10 of Mikvaot, Mishnah 1, teaches: all handles of vessels that were inserted in their normal manner, etc.; if not in their normal manner, etc. The reason is that when inserted abnormally they are destined to be removed. See the Mordechai at the end of Hilchot Niddah.
