Because the water reaches first. This means that when she placed her feet there, the water reached and touched her feet before they reached the floor, and that water remained connected to the mikveh. So writes the Beit Yosef in the Rosh's name. This reasoning implies that specifically mikveh water must first reach her feet. If her feet were already wet with detached water, that would not suffice. Otherwise, he should have said merely that her feet were already wet, as the Tur indeed writes. Rather, the validity depends upon the mikveh water itself first reaching them. According to what I wrote above in the Mordechai's name, that when the immersed utensil has weight, prior wetting with detached water does not help, the matter is clear. Since she stands upon the floor, there is pressure, which nullifies the connection created by a wet layer. Therefore, the mikveh water itself must provide the validity, as I wrote above in siman 120 in the Mordechai's name. According to the Rambam and Ramban cited above, who maintain that even a tight grip does not constitute a chatzitzah if one first rinsed his hands, and consequently the same applies to the weight of a utensil, we must say that the reason given here, that the water reaches first, does not specifically require mikveh water, but applies equally to other water. Toras HaBayis explicitly writes this, as the Beit Yosef cites: "Because the water reaches first, and moreover there is liquid wet enough to moisten, etc." Therefore, the Tur wrote only that her feet are moist, and did not say that mikveh water first reached them, for he follows the Rambam and Ramban's view discussed above.
