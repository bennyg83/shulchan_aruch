A vessel. Even if broad and fixed so there is no fear she will fall. Shach.
Boards. Taz defends the custom of placing wooden boards in a mikveh. Since they are not made as movable supports but as part of the mikveh floor, especially when nailed in place, they are not treated as utensils susceptible to foot-treading impurity. Maharam Padua and Maharam of Lublin support preserving the custom, though when building anew it is preferable to use materials valid according to all views.
Immersion. Bach is unwilling to be lenient because Rashi and Tosafot invalidate it. Shach rejects his concern and rules like the Mechaber.
The steps. Taz writes that wooden steps are also permitted based upon the preceding analysis. They must nevertheless be sufficiently broad so the woman will not fear falling, because fear may prevent proper immersion.
