And she should not stand on vessels, etc. The Ateret Zekenim challenged the custom of making steps or planks in a mikveh, because wooden vessels can contract tumah, and anything susceptible to tumah is invalid as a support for immersion. His words are not compelling. Simple wooden planks that are fixed into the building of the mikveh, or steps made for standing and not as utensils, are not in the category of vessels susceptible to tumah. Moreover, when they are attached to the ground or made as part of the structure, their status is like the ground. Therefore the common practice has a clear basis. Still, one should ensure that they are not actual movable vessels, such as boxes, benches, or receptacles, and that they are fixed securely so that the woman can immerse calmly without fear. Where the steps are made only to allow safe descent into the mikveh, and not as independent utensils, there is no concern.
