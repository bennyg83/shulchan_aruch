One may not immerse, etc. As stated in Pesachim 17b concerning impure vessels, etc. See Tosafot there, s.v. 'rather,' etc. One may say that the Sages imposed a decree, etc. This principle appears in many other places as well, and explains the words, 'therefore,' etc., below.
Therefore, etc. This is unlike Rashi's explanation on Shabbat 65a, s.v. 'and mats,' etc., since a mat can contract impurity, as stated there in chapter 9, 84b, and in the Sema. Rather, the explanation follows Tosafot there and the Raavad. See below, seifim 33 and 34. Rashi on Niddah 67a, s.v. 'and mats,' however, writes that women walk across them. See also the end of seif katan 44 below.
She should not stand, etc. This follows the Mishnah in chapter 5 of Mikvaot, Mishnah 2, 'provided that,' etc. See the Rash there. It must therefore be explained as a rabbinic decree, etc. The Raavad writes that this is why Niddah speaks specifically of standing on an earthenware vessel, because such a vessel does not contract impurity from its outer surface and cannot become impure through treading, as stated in chapter 9 of Shabbat, 84b. Likewise, a basket of this type cannot contract impurity at all. With a wooden vessel, however, even its outer surface, or any object fit for sitting or lying upon, is included in the decree concerning bathhouses, like the bench in the Mishnah cited above. The same applies to planks and similar objects mentioned there. That Mishnah cannot be explained as a concern that the woman will be frightened, since it discusses vessels. It must therefore be because of the decree concerning bathhouses. A practical difference exists even regarding a person: if the concern is fear, the immersion is valid after the fact, but if it is the bathhouse decree, the immersion is invalid even after the fact. This is the view of the Rashba and Ran. The Rosh and Rambam, however, hold that the bathhouse rationale was rejected in the final analysis, and that the Mishnah refers specifically to the outer surfaces of vessels, which were prohibited lest one immerse inside them. See the Rosh at the end of siman 9, 'provided that she did not,' etc., and the view introduced as, 'some explain,' etc.
Even if they are attached, etc. The building does not nullify them, as stated in Bava Batra 65b in the baraita concerning a pipe, etc.
Because they are flat wooden utensils. Meaning, they are fit for sitting or lying upon, as explained above. Stones and earthenware vessels are different. Even though Shabbat 66a states that such an object contracts impurity rabbinically, Tosafot there, s.v. 'and it is different,' and Tosafot in the first chapter of Sukkah, 5a, together with the Sema, explain that this applies specifically to something that serves both a person and his accessories, excluding a ladder. This is stated in Torat Kohanim, cited by Tosafot in both places.
And the step should be, etc. See Tosafot on Eruvin 98b, s.v. 'if,' etc.
