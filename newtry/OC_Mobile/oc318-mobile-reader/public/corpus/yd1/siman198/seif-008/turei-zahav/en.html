And if she would open, etc. This means that such is her usual manner. This is likewise implied by the Tur's wording, "they would open," meaning without intention, and so too wrote my revered father-in-law, of blessed memory. The Tur writes in the Rambam's name that the explanation is that her eyes were open. It appears to me that the Rambam explains that she did so at the time of immersion, opening her eyes, for it is obvious that no woman keeps her eyes open at all times. Thus, the Rambam indeed has a third explanation, beyond the two explanations previously stated by the Tur. This is unlike the Beit Yosef, who writes that the Rambam follows the Tur's first explanation. A proof is that the Rambam writes shortly afterward that if she opened her eyes excessively, her immersion is invalid. This certainly refers to the time of immersion.
