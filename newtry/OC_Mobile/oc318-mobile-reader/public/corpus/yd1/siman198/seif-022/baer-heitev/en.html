Maharam of Lublin wrote in responsum — since this is not from law but from stringency, it appears they were stringent only when she was found to have forgotten one nail immediately after immersion before she slept with her husband; but if she did not find it until the next day, one should not be stringent lest he cast aspersion on her relations, and even if she did not have relations with her husband that night the matter is unseemly, and she needs no other immersion if no filth was found under it — end quote; and Siftei Kohen wrote on this — and it appears where she can return and immerse, even if she did not find until the next day and even if she says with certainty there was no filth under it, she should return and immerse because the nail itself interposes even without any mud, and it seems to me so is practiced to rule; but where not possible one should not be stringent since the night passed; and Taz wrote that if she already had relations she should not immerse again lest she conceived, and if you come to be stringent requiring second immersion you cast aspersion on that child (and all this when there is no excrement or dough under it; but if there is under it, even if she had relations with her husband she needs another immersion per law) — and he wrote further that if her immersion occurred on chol hamoed, Mordechai wrote she should not cut them herself but through a gentile maidservant; but we hold in Orach Chayyim siman 532 that she may cut them without any change (and Mordechai was stringent only through herself not through gentile maidservant); but on Shabbat and Yom Tov it appears (if it happened she forgot to take nails on Shabbat and Yom Tov she should not tell a gentile to cut for her, since there is no absolute mitzvah in taking nails for one can scrape under them — why nullify rabbinic Shabbat prohibition of telling a gentile; and further a woman assists by lowering her hand to him and it is as if she does melachah on Shabbat and Yom Tov — end quote) she should not cut at all even through gentile but scrape them well; and I saw one who erred and commanded cutting on Yom Tov through gentile and did not intend correctly — end quote (and Nekudot HaKesef challenged him at length and concluded one may tell gentile to take them for they did not decree rabbinic prohibition in place of mitzvah; and so ruled Magen Avraham siman 343 one may tell gentile to cut them for assisting has no substance; and in responsum part 98 question 2 he challenged Siftei Kohen in Nekudot HaKesef and agreed with Taz since it is stringent) — nevertheless since one can take them without a utensil why be lenient with a utensil; but without utensil the matter is plain permitted and no law requires six — end quote; see there.
