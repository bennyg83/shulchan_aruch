She must. Maharam of Lublin writes that because nail cutting is only a stringency, it applies when she discovers the forgotten nail immediately after immersion before being with her husband. If discovered only the next day and there was no dirt beneath it, she need not immerse again, to avoid casting aspersions on prior relations. Shach is more stringent where reimmersion is possible, because the nail itself may interpose. Taz writes that after relations she should not reimmerse, lest this cast aspersions on a possible child. If dirt or dough was beneath the nail, all agree she must immerse again. On Shabbat or Yom Tov, Taz prefers cleaning beneath the nails rather than having a non-Jew cut them; later authorities dispute this and permit non-Jewish cutting for the mitzvah where necessary.
