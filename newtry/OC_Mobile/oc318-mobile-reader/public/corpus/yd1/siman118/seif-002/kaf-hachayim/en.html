B) And meat—even with majority of Israelite slaughterers we require seal within seal for meat, since the Mechaber ruled (siman 63) that meat lost from sight is forbidden—Shach s.k. 1, Peri Chadash s.k. 1, Lechem HaPanim s.k. 1, Erekh Shai. However, at first glance it is not so from the Rashba (siman 466) who wrote—and know in a city of Israelite slaughterers and majority of sellers selling slaughtered meat and also nevelah meat found there in a non-Jew's hand is permitted; nevertheless it seems one does not send l'chatchila meat via a non-Jew without a seal, etc., even though b'dieved one may permit when found in a non-Jew's hand, end of his words—brought in Bedek HaBayit; and so noted in Sefer Chadarim Da'ah. One may say the Rashba follows his view disputing the Rambam and permitting when found in a non-Jew's hand like our Gemara (Chullin 95)—unlike the Mechaber who ruled like the Rambam that even then forbidden, therefore we require seal within seal; but in Knesset Yehudah he wrote to reject the Peri Chadash—for since from the Torah we follow the majority and the prohibition is only d'rabbanan, one seal suffices; therefore he established the Mechaber's words only where there is no majority of Israelite slaughterers—see there; and so Yeshuot Yaakov s.k. 2; and so in Minchat Chinuch (klal 32, s.k. 52); and so from the Perishah, letter 11—see there; and see in Shach below s.k. 25.
