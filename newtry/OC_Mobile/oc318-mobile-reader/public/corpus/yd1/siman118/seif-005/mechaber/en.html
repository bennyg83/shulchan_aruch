If one sent via a non-Jew a thigh without a seal, if it is cut the way an Israelite cuts it after removal of the gid—it is kosher; and likewise any piece in which Israelite nikkur is recognizable, such as through removal of the foreleg tendon and the tendon near the breast, and the like: {Rama: And the other pieces with it—if there is concern about them that they were switched, they are forbidden (so ruled the Terumat HaDeshen, siman 200, in the name of R' Meshullam Ashiri, responsum 378); and this is specifically for meat or another d'oraisa prohibition; but if he sent a d'rabbanan prohibition, such as cheeses where there is concern that perhaps the non-Jew switched them—if he recognizes some of them that not all were switched, they are permitted if those that were not switched are the better ones that he sent, for certainly if he switched he would take the best among them (there).}
