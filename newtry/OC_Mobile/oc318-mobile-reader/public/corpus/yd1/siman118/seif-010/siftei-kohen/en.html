One who leaves, etc.—in Chullin 3a he derives from the language 'one who leaves' that it is b'dieved; however the Gemara concludes there that even l'chatchila permitted with going out and coming in; therefore what the Tur wrote this language is because of the end: he stayed long and did not inform he intends to stay, which is permitted only b'dieved; and so the Rav below siman 129, seif 1—forbidden l'chatchila in such a case—see there; this is from the Mordekhai, chapter R' Yishmael, in the name of Ra'avan; or as Tosafot in Chullin there: 'one who leaves' also implies l'chatchila.
If he goes out and enters, etc.—it seems even a child going out and entering helps, as above siman 115, seif 1 in the hagahah (and even for a d'oraisa prohibition—and see responsum of the Rashba that Beit Yosef brought in siman 130 regarding relying on minors' guarding, etc.), as the Mechaber writes in siman 69, seif 10, regarding rinsing—see there. Even though one could say there is different because blood he cooked does not absorb, and there is only rabbinic prohibition, as Maharshal permits with non-Jew who rinsed and as explained in Beit Yosef and Torat Chatas, end of klal 41—it seems specifically for reliability one can distinguish: for d'oraisa prohibition he is not believed with non-Jewish rinsing except a woman's testimony; but for fear there is no distinction.
And when he did not lock the house on him—he does not mean one must know he did not lock; but from presumption it is forbidden unless it became known it was locked—for in Avodah Zarah 69b it is explicit that from presumption we are not concerned he locked the door; rather he means it was not known the door was locked; and so explicit in siman 129, seif 1—see there.
