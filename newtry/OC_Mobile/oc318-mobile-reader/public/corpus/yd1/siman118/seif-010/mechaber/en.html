One who leaves a non-Jew in his house and there are things in it that, if switched, involve even a Torah prohibition—if he goes out and comes in, or even if he stayed a long time and did not inform him that he intends to stay, it is permitted and we are not concerned that perhaps he switched, even if he benefits from switching; and this is when he did not lock the house on him (Tur and Mechaber, siman 129)—because he fears at every moment, saying, "Now he will come and see me." But if he informed him that he intends to stay, it is forbidden; and if he does not benefit from switching, it is permitted in every case, for we are not concerned that perhaps he switched to cause him to stumble, since he has no benefit in the matter.
