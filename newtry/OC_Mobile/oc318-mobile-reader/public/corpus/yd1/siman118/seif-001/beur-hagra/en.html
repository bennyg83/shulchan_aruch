Deposited or, etc.—sent: 39b, two; and as Rashi explains there, forbidden, etc., with a seal, etc.; deposited: 31a, one; and as R' Yochanan holds, and so Rava explains there, seal within seal—clearly he holds so; and so holds there 39b, two; and so Rav Ashi, 29b, two, regarding cooked wine and vinegar; and Rav himself, 31a, one, ruled like R' Eliezer; and there 69b, two, halachah like R' Eliezer, etc.; Rabbenu Tam explained there 39b, two deals with a suspect Israelite who is worse than a non-Jew trusted to be believed; and so Rava and Rav Ashi—and thereby the case of Rav Bibi cutting three, etc., is resolved even though it is only one sign; and R' Yochanan explains there and there with a non-Jew: one seal suffices for a depositor who returns and sees his seal, for the non-Jew fears—in such a case dispute of R' Eliezer and the Rabbis, halachah like R' Eliezer; or a sender who informs through his writing about his seal and the non-Jew knows, he fears—then Rav Bibi's cutting, whose cut was known, as Rashi there, is resolved; but a sender who does not inform requires two seals. Rashba and Ran answered that even R' Eliezer requires seal within seal; they dispute only that for R' Eliezer lock and seal suffice, for the Rabbis specifically two seals, text 'his lock and seal'—and so Rif, Rosh, and Shulchan Piskei; and Tosafot there, both of them, halachah like R' Eliezer; they said one seal means with a lock—and so from Rif who ruled like R' Eliezer and said how seal within seal, etc.—and this is what 'deposited or sent' means per the Ran, and so I have explained, unlike R' Yochanan's view. In non-Jews' hands—to exclude Rabbenu Tam's view.

And everything whose prohibition, etc.—as explained: chavivta with seal within seal because they are d'oraisa; but chamfig only rabbinically—Ba'er Heitev.
(Likkut) Yerushalmi explains the reason: every matter whose prohibition is intrinsic—seal within seal because of mixture; one seal—apparently the Rambam explains because of rabbinic mixture prohibition; proof: bread whose prohibition is intrinsic—and Or Zarua; but Ra'ah wrote like the Yerushalmi's reason; now the text is fine—they deleted Rashi's 'bread—for what do we concern,' etc.; Tosafot there, bread, etc.; but accordingly it is resolved because its prohibition is intrinsic—except there 29b, also vinegar and cooked wine whose prohibition is intrinsic, he also said thus, that he does not trouble and forge with one seal; only for stam yeinam because of affection of libation requires seal within seal; therefore it must be as the Rambam: because it is rabbinic—and so the Ran (end of citation).
Deposited—and the same for sent, following the Rambam's language.
And some say, etc., and even, etc.—R' Yochanan's view; and in siman 130 he states the first view without a disputant.
(Likkut) And some say, etc., and even, etc.—as R' Yochanan explains to resolve Rav against Rav and other difficulties of R' Eliezer who permits with one seal specifically for a depositor who will see his seal, etc.; and we say there: two, the sender of a barrel, etc.; Rashi on the barrel, etc.; and all the more via a non-Jew—clearly like R' Eliezer, yet recognition of the seal is needed because it is one seal and the recipient knows—one seal suffices even for a sender; but for seal within seal no seal recognition is needed; and per Rabbenu Tam who establishes the baraita of 'the recipient knows' like the Rabbis, nevertheless from rabbinic law we learn for R' Eliezer as Tosafot wrote—see there 69b, nevertheless, etc.; but Rabbenu Tam explains for R' Eliezer no seal recognition is needed at all, per his view establishing the baraita of knowing like the Rabbis and not deriving for R' Eliezer, because for R' Eliezer we are not concerned for forgery at all, as in the Gemara 69b, and per his view resolving the difficulties not regarding sender and depositor but distinguishing suspect Israelite and non-Jew—and this is the hagahah 'and some say,' etc.; but he also explains the baraita of knowing is not because of one seal but because he returned to his seal and found it damaged; but in ordinary cases no seal recognition is needed, no distinction between one seal and seal within seal, neither for R' Eliezer nor the Rabbis, even seal within seal—and this is seif 2 'once,' etc., and siman 130 seif 8 'sealed,' etc., and Tosafot there and there (end of citation).
