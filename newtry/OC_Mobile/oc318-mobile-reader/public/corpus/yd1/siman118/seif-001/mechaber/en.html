The law of a piece of meat or a food item sent via a non-Jew. It contains 13 seifim: Wine, meat, and a piece of fish that has no distinguishing mark, that was deposited or sent in the hand of a non-Jew, requires two seals; but cooked wine and beer, or wine into which other things were mixed, such as honey, and likewise vinegar, milk, morayes, bread, and cheese, and everything whose prohibition is from the words of the scribes (d'rabbanan), that was deposited in the hand of a non-Jew—is permitted with one seal. And some say that even for things that require two seals, they said so only regarding sending via a non-Jew who is not going to see the seal; but for one who deposits it, who will see his seal, one seal suffices, for he will fear. And even the sender—if he informed his fellow the form of the seal, and also told the non-Jew that he informed his fellow—one seal suffices. {Rama: And some say that we do not require two seals except for a Jew who is suspect; but with a non-Jew, everything is permitted via one seal (Tur and poskim in the name of Rabbenu Tam). And b'dieved one may rely on this (Arukh, rule 23, and Hagahot Maimoniyot and Tosafot).}
