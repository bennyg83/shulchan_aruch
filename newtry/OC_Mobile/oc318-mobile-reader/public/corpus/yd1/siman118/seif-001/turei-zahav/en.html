Requires two seals. In the Tur he wrote the reason in the name of Rashi: for wine, meat, and fish (chavivta) two seals are required, and for cheese, bread, and wine (chamfig) one seal—for chavivta their value is dear, and for wine because of the affection of libation; according to this it is specifically those. But the Rashba attributes the reason that for chavivta there is concern for a d'oraisa prohibition, and for chamfig a d'rabbanan prohibition, and he counts cheese among them. The Derishah challenged: behold milk is also a d'oraisa prohibition through substitution of impure milk—and he answered that the mixture is d'rabbanan.
Only for a suspect Israelite—for he is worse than a non-Jew, who is trusted that they will believe him and he does not fear so much; and even though in siman 86 the Tur wrote in the name of the Rambam that a non-Jew is worse than an Israelite not established in kashrut, there in any case he is not suspect with certainty, whereas here "suspect" means in something the public are careful about, as stated in seif 8.
But with a non-Jew everything is permitted, etc.—for Tosafot challenge: here we say regarding wine, which is among chavivta, that two seals are required, and above in that chapter we say the halachah is like R' Eliezer who said a lock and seal—and some texts read a lock or seal, which suffices; therefore Rabbenu Tam answered that there regarding chavivta it deals with a suspect Israelite, and above with a non-Jew. Tosafot challenged him and explained that there is a distinction between a messenger and one who deposits, as the Shulchan Aruch writes nearby; and in the text of R' Eliezer that I cited there is a distinction—for Rabbenu Tam's text reads "or seal," and the Rashba reads "and seal," and according to him a lock is not a seal at all where only one seal is needed, but only where two seals are needed does a lock help in combination, as the Shulchan Aruch writes in seif 4. Now, according to what the Rama wrote here, following Or Zarua, that b'dieved one may rely on Rabbenu Tam and permit sending via a non-Jew with one seal—from that, a lock alone is also permitted according to his text that reads "lock or seal"; and if so, when the Shulchan Aruch wrote in seif 4 that for things where one seal suffices a lock does not help, the Rama should have written on it that b'dieved a lock helps, for one reason is that since we rely on Rabbenu Tam we rely on this too, for one depends on the other; therefore one should wonder at Or Zarua, who in klal 22 ruled to permit b'dieved like Rabbenu Tam, as the Rama rules here, and afterward wrote in the name of the Tur that a lock is not like one seal—and it may be that he wrote this for l'chatchila, but b'dieved certainly a lock suffices, and so explained Mahari Cohen; yet he brought proof from the Mordekhai, chapter R' Yishmael, and I wonder greatly on this, for perhaps the reason is that he fears being seized as a thief on wine even if the inn is locked, since if the Jew came he would fear like a thief, etc.—but Mahari Cohen's explanation that "and the keys in their hands" means in the Jews' hands is not correct; rather it means in the guards' hands, as the Mordekhai explains in the chapter HaSocher in this incident that R' Chaim Cohen wrote explicitly "even if he gave him the key," etc.; therefore one must rely on the non-Jew's fear, but truly if it were in a Jew's hand it would be permitted because of a seal according to Rabbenu Tam; so it seems clearly that Mahari Cohen intended the law, and the Rama abbreviated where he should have written the leniency for us b'dieved in seif 4.
And b'dieved one may rely on this—so too Or Zarua, klal 26; also in Tosafot (folio 69) it is so; and in my opinion it seems that all agree (regarding wine) [in this time] that the main reason we are concerned with wine requiring two seals, even though it is d'rabbanan, is because of the affection of libation wine, as I wrote in seif 1; and in our time they are not idol worshippers, as written below—therefore the affection of libation does not apply, and all agree that one seal suffices.
