And meat—Shach wrote: this means even where the slaughterers are Israelite; and it is difficult regarding what is said in the Gemara, siman 63, that if meat was in a non-Jew's hand where most sellers are Israelites it is kosher—one should distinguish: there it deals with meat found in a non-Jew's hand that they bought from the market, where there is no concern he bought nevelah and no concern he switched with nevelah in his house—for what purpose would he switch; but here it deals with sending or depositing in a non-Jew's hand where there is concern he switched with nevelah he still has, and moreover it stays a long time with him and he has opportunity to switch—unlike there, buying from the market; and see siman 128. Nevertheless, with seal within seal for Torah prohibitions and one seal for rabbinic prohibitions, it is permitted even l'chatchila to send via a non-Jew; and what the Mechaber wrote "deposited," etc.—the language of b'dieved is not precise; and so the Mechaber wrote explicitly in siman 130—it is simple; and as with a non-Jew, so with a suspect Israelite in this matter, end of his words—see there where he elaborated somewhat.
Honey—Shach wrote: it must deal with mixing so much honey that the wine's taste changed, for then there is no stam yeinam concern—see siman 123; and what he wrote "and likewise vinegar" means vinegar of wine, when it bubbles as thrown on the ground, then it is full vinegar unfit for libation, and if so why concern—if for affection of libation it is unfit for libation; if lest he switch with stam yeinam vinegar, since stam yeinam is only d'rabbanan we are not concerned and one seal suffices; and the same for morayes, cooked wine, and wine mixed with other things—they suffice with one seal. And see there siman 6; and milk: even though concern he switched with impure milk which is d'oraisa—impure milk is recognizable as turbid, so no concern he switched all or most, only perhaps a little which is only d'rabbanan, for min b'mino is nullified in majority from the Torah; and morayes: concern he switched with their morayes forbidden where they put wine in it, as siman 114, seif 1; but where they do not put wine, or wine is dearer than morayes, clearly no seal needed; and bread: obviously where they practice leniency with non-Jewish bread no seal is needed. (And Peri Chadash wrote: even where they practice leniency with pat palter, a seal is needed lest they switch with pat baal habayit forbidden everywhere.) And cheese: even though concern he switched with their cheese forbidden because they curdle with nevelah stomach lining—d'oraisa the lining is nullified in minority, and what is not nullified is d'rabbanan; or the main concern is perhaps he curdled with herbs and still forbidden lest he curdled with nevelah lining—it is properly d'rabbanan—understand, end of Shach.
And some say—and below siman 130 the Mechaber states the first view without a disputant—and understand.
Informed him—Shach wrote: the same if he does not inform his fellow but tells the non-Jew he informed—he still fears, for he thinks it is true; all this follows the second view, unlike the first—and understand; nevertheless even when sending with two seals it is proper to inform the fellow the seal's form so he will inspect afterward.
Suspect—for this matter he is worse than a non-Jew who is trusted they will believe him and does not fear much; it deals with one suspect to switch—not suspect to eat what he sends, for we hold one suspect to eat forbidden things is not suspect to switch them; perhaps those things the public are not lenient about—he is also suspect to switch if suspect to eat them, as the Mechaber writes in siman 119; but the Rashba wrote eating non-Jewish cheese is lenient in the public's eyes, and so the Tur in that siman: one suspect regarding cheese is not suspect to switch, end of Shach; and the Taz ruled that with a non-Jew one seal suffices and a lock counts as a seal (and Nachalat Shiva objected that a lock only helps where two seals are needed, then lock and seal suffice, but not where only one seal is needed—see there).
