(There, seif 11) An Israelite and a non-Jew who set two pots, etc.—permitted and we are not concerned, etc.—behold, if the Israelite certainly turned away, if we forbid the dish, Hagahot Asheri brings two views: it implies that if the Israelite turns away we are concerned; and from this the Rav Peter rules in the name of Rabbenu Tam that if two pots were on one stove, etc., and the Israelite turned away we are concerned lest the non-Jew throw nevelah and it is forbidden; and R' Margenstern permits, end of his words. Some want to prove that Tosafot also hold to forbid, for Tosafot challenged at the beginning of Avodah Zarah 13a: perhaps he turns back, etc.—and if so, even here permitted even if he turned away—then Tosafot's question is not difficult, for truly here too permitted like other prohibitions; therefore it must be they hold that if he turned away it is forbidden, and their answer seems to imply this: if he turned away forbidden—from their answer that here it deals where he is not seized as a thief; but in truth their words give no decisive ruling—certainly they hold permitted even if he turns away; rather their question is: the reason we are not concerned, etc., should be taught that it has the law of going out and coming in—for this Tosafot answered that going out and coming in does not help here because he is not seized as a thief; therefore they had to say we are not concerned when he turns away—and understand well.
