One should be careful even with two pots—in D.M. in the name of the Mordekhai, chapter Ein Ma'amidin; the reason is lest they be switched one for the other, and he derives this from the chapter Keitzad Tzolin that one does not roast two Paschal lambs in one oven, even a kid and lamb, because of mixture of bodies; accordingly, if there are two Israelite pots, one of meat and one of milk, there is no prohibition even l'chatchila to cook them in one oven, since switching does not apply. Those who seek sources wrote that the Rama's words are in the Tur and it is an error: it is written in the Agudah, chapter 3, that once Jews were seized and the non-Jews took all they had; they were ransomed and returned, and found their pots in goose fat, and said they recognized them by sight as when they left them—it seems to me to permit, for it is no worse than visual recognition of a vessel; and it is also in the Mordekhai, chapter Ein Ma'amidin, that when an Israelite gives a non-Jew a cooked dish in a pot to bring to the non-Jew's house, the Israelite should warn him not to wash the bowl but leave some residue of the food, and likewise bring it back the same day, etc. One may wonder how he relies on telling the non-Jew to leave food residue—perhaps he will use it and then return and put food residue in it. Mahari Cohen cited a responsum of Maharar Menachem of Cordova left among non-Jews: an Israelite had already taken a little fat from the pot with a spoon, and when the pot was returned with the fat he recognized the depression in the fat from which fat was taken—it seems to permit, for in such a case visual recognition applies, etc.
It is written in Agudah, chapter Gid HaNasheh: once Jews were seized and the gentiles took everything they had; they reached a settlement and [the possessions] were returned to them; and they found their pots with goose fat, and they said they recognized them by tavi'at ayin — [that they were] just as they had left them. It seems to me to permit, since it is no worse than recognition of a barrel by tavi'at ayin, as stated in the Talmud. Furthermore, it says in Mordechai, chapter Ayn Ma'amidin: when a Jew gives a gentile a dish in a pot to carry to the gentile's home, he should warn him not to rinse the pot; rather he should leave a little of the remaining food in it, and bring it back that same day — end of quote. But this is astonishing — how can one rely on telling the gentile to leave remnants of the food in it? For perhaps the gentile will use the pot and then put back remnants of the food afterward. And my father-in-law of blessed memory quoted a responsum of Maharr Menachem about a pot that remained with gentiles — a Jew had already taken a small amount of the fat from the pot with a spoon, and when the pot was returned with the fat, the Jew recognized the indentation in the fat from which the fat had been taken, which was just as he had left it — [and ruled] it seems to permit, since in such a case tavi'at ayin applies — end of quote.
