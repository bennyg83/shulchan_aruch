Known—Taz wrote: I wonder greatly—perhaps it was truly an Israelite's and he made a sign to recognize it among treifah pieces, and afterward learned this was treifah, and therefore it is now in a non-Jew's hand—whence to be lenient in a d'oraisa doubt (and Nachalat Shiva answered: it deals where there is a seal or "kosher" written, which is not customary until known with certainty kosher—see there).
And b'dieved—Shach wrote: meaning if he already bought from the non-Jew (see Peri Chadash, s.k. 31); and it seems it deals where there is no concern stamps remained with non-Jews, etc.; but if there is concern stamps remained with non-Jews, forbidden even b'dieved; however the Levush wrote in great loss one may permit even then b'dieved; and Bach wrote the custom is to buy wines non-Jews bring from the land called Muscovy via their seals, for they bring writings from Israel—see siman 130, seif 6.
