And likewise permitted to buy cheese, etc.—this is all the more from meat, which the Mechaber wrote is d'oraisa; the Rav wrote only because of the end.
And b'dieved, etc.—meaning if he already bought from the non-Jew; and it seems it deals where there is no concern stamps remained with non-Jews, etc., only we do not know who wrote or sealed; but where there is concern stamps remained with non-Jews and they affixed them on their cheeses—forbidden even b'dieved; see responsum Avnei Nezer, part 3, siman 1. However, the Atzei Chayim wrote at the end of the siman: in great loss one may permit even in such a case b'dieved; see end of siman and what is written there. He wrote in Torat Chatas, klal 81, law 4: the custom is to buy wines non-Jews bring from the land called Muscovy via their seals, for they bring writings from Israel, end of his words; and so in Simanei Torat Chacham there: for wine they practice leniency—see below siman 130, seif 6.
