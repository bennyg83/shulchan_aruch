G) But cooked wine—in Beit Yosef below (siman 123) he brought the Rashba in Torat HaBayit: what is cooked? Whatever was boiled is cooked, and so received from the Geonim; some say it is not called cooked until its measure diminishes through cooking; it seems impossible to boil without diminishing measure, and the Geonim's reception decides, end of his words; and so the Ran; also the Rosh agreed with the Ra'avad in the name of the Geonim that if boiled it is certainly cooked; and so in Sefer HaTerumah; and so the Tur ruled there: from when is it called cooked—from when it boiled on the fire; the Shach wrote there s.k. 7 that this means its measure diminishes through boiling, end of his words; and so ruled Or Zarua (chapter 2 of Avodah Zarah, siman 215); responsum Tashbetz, part 1 (siman 29); Orchot Chaim Yoreh Deah (page 247); and more; the Geonim's words in Sha'arei Teshuvah (siman 258)—see there. However, Kenesset HaGadol (siman 123, Hagahat Beit Yosef letter 14) wrote in the name of the Riaz: the measure of cooked wine is whatever is not drunk as wine [and see Kenesset HaGadol there letter 50 regarding vinegar]. However, Shulchan Gavoah (there s.k. 7) wrote the Riaz's view is nullified against all these rabbis who hold whatever was boiled on the fire, however much, is considered cooked; brought to law in Sefer Einei David (chapter 6, page 3) and Zikhronenu LeChaim, part 2 (folio 31b)—see there. We find the Rambam (chapter 6 of Issurei Mizbeach, law 9): cooked on fire until its taste changed through cooking (unfit for libation); therefore it is proven that even for stam yeinam prohibition we require cooking until taste changes; as written (chapter 1 of Mishneh Torah, law 9)—see there; and so proved in Mishor Ba'ahalei Teshuvah (siman 123, s.k. 5) and R' Menuchah—see there; and so the Meiri (Avodah Zarah 29:) wrote: the end of the matter in this topic—in my opinion whatever is cooked until its taste changed from what it was, etc., its charm is nullified and there is no affection of libation on it, end of his words; the publishing rabbi noted the Rambam's words there—see there; and so for law in Sefer Kikar Le'aden (folio 162a) in the name of Maharam of Lunzano: what is written 'cooked wine has no stam yeinam' is not ordinary cooking but extensive cooking until taste changed in cooking—brought in Sefer Ikrei HaDat (siman 13, letter 13); and Maharach Palaggi in Ruach Chaim (siman 123, s.k. 2) explained the Riaz's view—that is also until taste changed from wine taste—see there; see Sefer Vayomer Yitzchak (folio 114b); we found accordingly companions to the Riaz, unlike Shulchan Gavoah that his view is nullified against all poskim and he did not mention the Rambam's view; and so the Meiri and R' Menuchah—as said; and so ruled Ben Ish Chai, part 2 (parashat Balak, letter 7); see Sefer Beit Aharon Krispin (folio 44b); responsum Shevitat Yom Tov (Yoreh Deah, siman 1-4); see Kisei Eliyahu (siman 123, s.k. 2)—and understand. For law, in my opinion l'chatchila one should heed the Rambam and his camp and require seal within seal as long as wine has not reached the measure that taste changed; but b'dieved one should not be stringent, since many poskim hold boiling alone suffices; and so is the Mechaber's view we accepted; therefore if there was only one seal one may be lenient.
