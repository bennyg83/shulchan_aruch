A public thoroughfare, etc.—meaning even where Jews are not commonly found but only non-Jews—this non-Jew fears lest other non-Jews see him and tell the Israelite, as the Tur and Shulchan Aruch write in siman 129, seif 4.
However, l'chatchila, etc.—in Torat Chatas, klal 32, he wrote that the custom is to send via a non-Jew in a place that is a public thoroughfare and is an Israelite neighborhood where non-Jews are not found, etc.—for it is known that it belongs to an Israelite. I wonder greatly: behold there is concern that it was truly an Israelite's and he intended to eat it and made a sign to recognize it among other treifah pieces, and afterward he learned this one was treifah; and the Rashba himself wrote this reasoning elsewhere in Mishmeret HaBayit (folio 118, end of column 2); and Tosafot wrote it in chapter 3 (folio 94), regarding if a non-Jew cut at the place of the gid—we are concerned perhaps afterward he learned it was treifah and therefore sent it to another non-Jew for nikkur, etc.; whence do we not concern ourselves here, and we are lenient in a d'oraisa doubt?
