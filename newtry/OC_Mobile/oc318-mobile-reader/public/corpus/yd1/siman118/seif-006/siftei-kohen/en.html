Forbidden—the reason: perhaps the non-Jew switched good for bad and slaughtered himself for the one he switched; and slaughter is not a sign to rely on. Perishah, seif 11: specifically where there is no majority of Israelites or majority of Israelite slaughterers—therefore we concern he switched with nevelah and do not rely on a slaughter sign; unlike above siman 1, where there is majority of Israelites and slaughterers we rely on the majority found at slaughter who are experts, end of his words. It is difficult—for the Taz seems to deal here even where majority of slaughterers and Israelites are Israelite; and so explicit in Rokeach, siman 406, and Bach's last pamphlet: specifically here we concern he switched to profit and slaughtered himself for the one he switched; but if his kids and chickens were lost and found slaughtered—they are kosher and we are not concerned a non-Jew slaughtered them, for since he recognizes them as his and they were not switched, no concern a non-Jew slaughtered for profit since he has no benefit, and presumably Israelites slaughtered them, and most found at slaughter are experts, end of his words. I did not grasp his view—for it seems explicit in those cases (folio 24b) that even one who finds a slaughtered animal not his is permitted with majority of Israelites; and so the Tur, seif 11; and as explained in siman 63. The Mechaber in siman 1, seif 4 wrote 'if his kids were lost'—he stated it as common usage, or the Mechaber follows his view that meat lost from sight is forbidden in siman 63, therefore he requires specifically his kids that he recognizes as his; unlike us who hold meat lost from sight is permitted. It seems clear there is no dispute with siman 1—for there it was never in a non-Jew's hand, only we did not know who slaughtered, therefore we follow the majority; but here, sending via a non-Jew, concern he switched good for bad, therefore even where majority of Israelites are found we concern he slaughtered himself for profit for the one he switched; and this is unlike meat found in a non-Jew's hand permitted with majority of Israelites, as above s.k. 1—see there.
For a slaughter sign is not a sign to rely on. Mordekhai, responsum 378, brought by Beit Yosef, siman 63: an incident—a slaughtered chicken sent via a non-Jew without a guard; they doubted perhaps another was switched; they asked the slaughterer if he slaughtered this chicken; he answered: I have no visual recognition; however now I slaughtered one chicken; but the owner recognized it and said this is it, and I permitted it since the owner recognized it, end of his words; and so Maharshal, first chapter of Chullin, siman 23, and responsum 378; and he added: but if they did not ask the slaughterer, it is not a sign, end of his words—and it is simple.
