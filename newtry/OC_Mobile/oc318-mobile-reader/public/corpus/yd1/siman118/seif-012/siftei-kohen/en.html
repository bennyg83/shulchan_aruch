And he does not go out and enter—the Rav's view seems that with going out and entering one need not be stringent; at first glance in the Rosh and Tur it does not seem so—for after writing the law of two pots as the Mechaber in seif 11, they wrote: on this we rely to leave pots with maidservants when going to synagogue, which is like going out and entering; and it is fit for God-fearing to be stringent, for mishaps occurred many times; and also to improve her portion she puts milk in the pot, end of his words (and see in the Rosh himself)—it seems even with going out and entering one should be stringent. What the Rav learned from the Rosh's responsum that if an Israelite goes out and enters it is permitted—in the responsum his view for the law is permitted, for he wrote there: as we say in the first chapter of Avodah Zarah, you did not go to Tyre all your days and see a non-Jew and Israelite who set two pots, etc.; and in his rulings, first chapter of Avodah Zarah, he wrote on this 'you went to Tyre,' meaning for the law; but it is fit for God-fearing to be stringent, see there. But the Mechaber who wrote 'one should be stringent,' etc., as the Rosh in his rulings 'and fit for God-fearing to be stringent,' etc.—even with going out and entering he speaks; and one can say the Rav understood from the Rosh's not answering the questioner there that l'chatchila one should be stringent even with going out and entering—therefore with going out and entering there is not even a stringency; and what he wrote 'fit for God-fearing,' etc., applies when going to synagogue, because he does not hold going out and entering applies since they stay in synagogue a while and maidservants know they stay; but with actual going out and entering one need not be stringent (and see siman 113, seif 4). Do not challenge from the previous seif that l'chatchila one should be careful even with two pots—it is simple that Israelite and non-Jew who set pots is no worse than going out and entering, for there the reason is sparks that fly, as in the previous s.k.
And if a mishap occurred, etc.—forbidden food in the pot, etc.—in Shibolei HaLeket it is written she put forbidden food in the pot to anger, etc.; and so the Rav cited in Torat Chatat, klal 32, law 9; here the word 'to anger' was omitted because it is not precise—one reason he wrote: so they not learn to do so, applies also if her intent was for their pleasure; he mentioned 'to anger' to exclude if she did not know that thing she put in the pot was forbidden to an Israelite—and this means she put forbidden food in the pot to anger, meaning she knew that thing was forbidden to an Israelite and nevertheless gave it. He also wrote in Beit Yosef in the name of Shibolei HaLeket: a non-Jew who said do not eat from this pot, for I threw milk in it—we do not accept from him, rather it is permitted, end of his words; and so the Rav in Torat Chatas, klal 32, law 9—and he wrote this is simple from the case of a non-Jew bringing fruit to market: a non-Jew is not believed neither for prohibition nor permission—see siman 122, seif 3, and siman 127.
One should not feed her that food, etc.—in Shibolei HaLeket: not to that non-Jew nor other non-Jews; and so in Torat Chatas and Simanei Torat Chacham there; here the Rav wrote 'one should not feed her' without qualification—it must be he does not argue.
