L) And milk—and even though gentile milk is forbidden lest he switch with impure milk, a Torah prohibition—nevertheless one seal suffices because impure milk is recognizable, as Avodah Zarah (35:) pure white, impure green; therefore no concern he switches all or most, only perhaps a little forbidden only d'rabbanan—for min b'mino nullified in majority d'oraisa—Perishah s.k. 4, Shach s.k. 8, Taz s.k. 1, Peri Chadash s.k. 5, Lechem Mishneh chapter 13 of Mishneh Torah law 10, Shulchan Gavoah s.k. 10, Arukh HaShulchan s.k. 2. But Noda Biyehudah Mahadura Tinyana (Orach Chaim, siman 66) challenged the Shach: taste differs between impure and pure milk—it would be not its species; even though the Prisha (siman 98, s.k. 4) wrote where similar in name but different in taste, if not a complete taste change but slight, it is min b'mino—who testified the difference between impure and pure milk is only slight? Therefore the Noda Biyehudah wrote: the Shach's reasoning is fine that d'oraisa nullified in majority even noten ta'am gamur, because we hold taste like substance d'oraisa only for negative commandments with lashes, but impure milk is only a positive commandment—we do not learn taste like substance from gentile utensils or sheretz; it is d'rabbanan, therefore one seal suffices, end of his words; and in the Meiri (Avodah Zarah 39:) he wrote similarly to the Shach; if he held like the Noda Biyehudah he would not state such a great novelty obscurely; and as the Meiri wrote in Ritva's commentary and Rashba in Torat HaBayit (house 3, gate 6) and Beit HaLevi (chapter 3 of Mishneh Torah, law 16)—see there; in Sefer Be'er Yaakov (folio 218b) he also challenged the Shach like the Noda Biyehudah: since we follow taste, impure and pure milk tastes are not the same, as the Shach wrote (siman 107) regarding impure and pure fish—that would be not its species—see there; not necessary—for there too we find the Gra'a and Beit Meir who wrote they are min b'mino; and I found a tannaitic support: the Meiri (Chullin 97:) wrote explicitly—see there; responsum Nefesh Chayah (simanim 24-25); one may say here too impure and pure milk tastes are the same; and so I saw the Gaon Beit Meir (this siman) agreed with the Shach that impure and pure milk tastes are the same, like impure and pure fish—see there; in any case one may say their tastes are close; and we come to the Prisha's view that in such a case they are min b'mino; also the gentile may mix a little impure milk in pure so it will not be noticed in tasting; responsum Eiruvei HaBosem, part 2 (siman 117); Sefer Eiruvei HaBosem on Yoreh Deah (siman 81, s.k. 4); responsum Shem Aryeh (siman 52)—see there.
L) And one must see well in responsum Tashbetz, part 3 (siman 143): milk a gentile milked and a Jew was present—doubt d'oraisa because of impure milk mixture, and perhaps he mixed much—see there; and so Mar bar Rav in responsum of the Rashbash (siman 555)—we learn two things from them: A) it would be not its species and therefore Torah prohibition—unlike the Shach and camp; B) taste like substance d'oraisa even for positive commandments like impure milk—unlike the Noda Biyehudah. I found in responsum Maharsham, part 2 (siman 42): asked on Or Zarua (klal 31, law 14): impure milk that fell in a pot would be a doubt, implying unlike the Noda Biyehudah who holds his taste is d'rabbanan; he answered Or Zarua holds like Tosafot Bekhorot (6:), whom the Peri Megadim brought (siman 81)—impure milk is d'oraisa and lashes apply, disputing the Rambam that there are no lashes—see there. In my opinion it is strained to say so of the Rashbetz and Rashbash—for presumably they hold like the Rambam that it is a positive commandment; especially since the Mabit also wrote in Kiryat Sefer (chapter 3 of Mishneh Torah) it would be a doubt—see there; see Be'er HaGolah (siman 105, s.k. 1) who brought the Rashbetz's view [there is a textual error in his citation] and challenged: if so why does one seal suffice for milk? Be'er HaGolah answered siman this s.k. 2: perhaps he holds like Bach that the reason is he has no profit from switching, therefore they were not concerned even though d'oraisa—see there; see Kenesset HaGadol Hagahat Beit Yosef letter 12; nevertheless for those Tosafot (Bekhorot 6:) otherwise— they hold like Tosafot (Avodah Zarah 39:) that the reason is chamfig has no prohibition intrinsically except through mixture—see there.
L) At first glance I would attribute this to the dispute among Acharonim whether for prohibition coming from half measure we say taste like substance d'oraisa—Chochmat Adam (siman 105, s.k. 5) holds taste like substance d'oraisa only for full measure, unlike half measure; if so, the same for impure milk without lashes—it would be like chatzi shiur where we do not say taste like substance; and so the Rambam (chapter 3 of Mishneh Torah, law 6): milk of impure animal and eggs of impure bird are forbidden d'oraisa without lashes—'one who eats them is like eating chatzi shiur,' see there; unlike those who dispute Chochmat Adam—this too would be chatzi shiur taste d'oraisa; but one may say unlike chatzi shiur, for this comes from a prohibition that has lashes at full measure, unlike here where lashes do not apply to it; I found in responsum Avnei Nezer, Orach Chaim, part 2 (siman 259) who challenged the Noda Biyehudah: this is like chatzi shiur as the Rambam wrote; and since chatzi shiur taste is d'oraisa as proven in Chullin (98)—so too impure milk—see there; not necessary—one may say as written; and so explicitly responsum Shem Aryeh (Yoreh Deah, siman 52); but neither addressed Chochmat Adam; even for chatzi shiur one may say it is d'rabbanan; in Darkei Teshuvah (siman 98, s.k. 32) he brought also Knesset HaPanim holds like Chochmat Adam; nevertheless for milk mixture they challenged in Avnei Nezer and Shem Aryeh: here it is different—taste and substance; no need to learn from sheretz and gentile utensils since there is an olive's bulk in any case—see there.
L) And behold, even though in responsum Avnei Nezer (Orach Chaim, siman 259) he rejected the Noda Biyehudah with both hands and wrote the matter is clear to all poskim that even here taste like substance d'oraisa, and the Noda Biyehudah alone was lenient—see there—nevertheless several Acharonim tread in the Noda Biyehudah's hem; even Shem Aryeh does not dispute him for law; as he himself wrote in Sefer Eiruvei HaBosem Yoreh Deah (siman 92, s.k. 5) like the Noda Biyehudah; and so there (siman 107); responsum Yehudah Yaaleh Asad (Yoreh Deah, siman 130); Sefer Darkei Horaah, part 2 (chapter 71); responsum Levushei Mordekhai, part 1 (Yoreh Deah, siman 63); responsum Amudei Or (siman 51)—see there in conclusion he wrote he did not rely on this in practice; one may say; see responsum Pnei Meivin (Yoreh Deah, siman 103); responsum Sha'arei Rachamim Franki, part 2 (siman 5); however in responsum Be'er Yitzchak (Yoreh Deah, siman 13) he wrote to reject the Noda Biyehudah; also responsum Eiruvei HaBosem, part 2 (siman 117) left the Noda Biyehudah in doubt; there is room to rely on the Noda Biyehudah; responsum Maharsham, part 2 (siman 42) supported challenging the Noda Biyehudah; again established in Sefer Ein HaRo'im (folio 131a) who brought it to law; and so Mar bar Rav relied on it in his responsum (there, letter 71).
L) In Be'er HaGolah s.k. 2 he challenged the Shach on this per Rav's view (Avodah Zarah 39:) that milk suffices with one seal—for he holds min b'mino is not nullified d'oraisa; answer per above: since he has no profit he does not trouble and forge, as Bach—see there; and in Degel Machaneh Ephraim and Hagahot Ra'anan they addressed this—see there; see Yeshuot Yaakov s.k. 3; responsum Chatam Sofer (Yoreh Deah, siman 97); responsum Be'er Yitzchak (siman 13); Eiruvei HaBosem (siman 117) above; Sefer Devarim Aharon Polonski (siman 11)—and understand.
