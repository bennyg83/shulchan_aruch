And enters—Shach wrote: the Rav's view seems that with going out and entering one need not be stringent (the main stringency is when going to synagogue, where maidservants know they stay a while; but with actual going out and entering one need not be stringent at all—Shach and Peri Chadash); and from the previous seif that l'chatchila one should be careful even with two pots, it is simple that Israelite and non-Jew who set pots is no worse than going out and entering, for there the reason is sparks flying, as written above.
To feed her—in Shibolei HaLeket: not to that non-Jew nor other non-Jews—meaning she knew that thing was forbidden to an Israelite and nevertheless gave it; and he also wrote: a non-Jew who said do not eat from this pot, for I threw milk in it—we do not accept from him, rather it is permitted, end of his words; and so Torat Chatas: a non-Jew is not believed neither for prohibition nor permission—see siman 122, seif 3, and siman 127; and Mordekhai wrote: when an Israelite gives a non-Jew a cooked dish in a pot to bring to the non-Jew's house, the Israelite should warn him not to wash the bowl but leave some food residue, and likewise bring it back the same day, end of his words. One may wonder how he relies on telling the non-Jew to leave residue—perhaps he will use it and then return and put residue in it. Bach cited a responsum of Maharar Menachem of Cordova left among non-Jews: an Israelite had taken a little fat from the pot with a spoon, and when the pot returned with the fat he recognized the depression in the fat from which fat was taken—it seems to permit, for in such a case visual recognition applies, end of Taz.
