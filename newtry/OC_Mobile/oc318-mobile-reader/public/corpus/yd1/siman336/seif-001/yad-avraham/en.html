(Siman 336, seif 1) The Torah granted a physician permission to heal, and it is a mitzvah. This is provided that his heart is directed toward Heaven and he recognizes that true healing comes from Hashem. He should seek Hashem and should not intend that everything depends upon a particular drug or upon the physician, as it is written, 'Even in his illness he did not seek Hashem, but only the physicians.'
(There) And if he is a physician with authorization of the court and erred, etc. And if he killed and it became known to him that he acted inadvertently, he is exiled on account of him. Sefer Maaseh Rokeach asks: how is this different from a teacher who strikes his student, or an agent of the court who struck a litigant who refused to come to judgment and inadvertently killed him, where the Rambam rules in Hilkhot Rotzeach that since he erred and killed him while performing a mitzvah, he is exempt from exile? One may answer that here it is different, because he did not perform a mitzvah with his treatment when the patient died. This differs from all those cases the Rambam lists there, where even though the person died, he was performing a mitzvah by teaching him Torah or summoning him to judgment. Perhaps this is what the Tashbetz intended in the responsum brought in Sefer Birkei Yosef, where he wrote that one can distinguish between a physician and those cases. But what the author of Birkei Yosef wrote about this is remote and forced; see there.
