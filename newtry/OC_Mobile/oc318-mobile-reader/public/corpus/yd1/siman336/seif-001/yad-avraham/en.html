(Siman 336, seif 1) The Torah gave the physician permission to heal, and it is a mitzvah. This is provided that his heart is directed toward Heaven and he knows that true healing comes from Hashem, and that he seeks Hashem rather than imagining that everything depends entirely on a particular medicine or physician. Scripture criticizes Asa because "even in his illness he did not seek Hashem, but the physicians."
(There) If a physician authorized by beit din erred, and so forth. If he caused death and later learned that he had erred, he goes into exile. Ma'aseh Rokeach asks why this differs from a teacher who strikes a student, or an agent of beit din who strikes a litigant who refuses to appear, where Rambam, Hilchot Rotzeach, rules that if he accidentally kills while performing the mitzvah, he is exempt from exile. One can answer that here, if the patient died through the mistaken treatment, the physician did not in fact perform a mitzvah by that treatment. In the other cases listed by Rambam, even though the victim died, the act itself was still the mitzvah of teaching Torah or enforcing appearance before beit din. Perhaps this is what Tashbetz meant in the responsum cited by Birkei Yosef when he said there is a distinction between a physician and those other cases. Birkei Yosef's own explanation appears strained.
