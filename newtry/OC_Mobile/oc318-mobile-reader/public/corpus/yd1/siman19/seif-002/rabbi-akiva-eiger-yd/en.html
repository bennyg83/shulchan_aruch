(Seif 6) And afterward they brought him more. So is the Tur's language, and according to his reason: he concludes that even if while some of those on which he blessed are still before him they brought others before him, he must bless. The same applies if immediately after he blessed they brought before him to slaughter—he is obligated to bless on the others and obligated to cover first. But the Mechaber ruled in seif 7 that all the time he is occupied with shechita he need not bless on the others; and the Beit Yosef inferred thus from the Rosh's language, who wrote: one who blesses with intention to slaughter one chaya, and after it was slaughtered they brought him more, etc.—it appears specifically after it was slaughtered and not after he blessed; and see the Shach, seif kaf yod. If so, the Mechaber should have written the Rosh's language "after it was slaughtered"; and so at the end of the seif, before he blessed on the first covering—behold also after he blessed on all that he did not cover, he need not bless on the second covering—and requires further study.
