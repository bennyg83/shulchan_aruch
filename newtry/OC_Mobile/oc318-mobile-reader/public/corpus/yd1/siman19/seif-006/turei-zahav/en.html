He covers the blood of the first. For he changed his mind—and there was an interruption between the first blessing on the shechita; and his law is like one who spoke idle words of Chullin, in the first view in seif 5; and they learned this from the attendant who must bless on each slice at a meal: they challenged him—what is different from "give us and we will bless" and he wishes to eat more, that he must bless HaMotzi, but he need not bless Birkat HaMazon on what he already ate? For there it is different, for Birkat HaMazon is praise to the Holy One, blessed be He, for man's enjoyment, and one acknowledgment suffices for two eatings; but the act of covering completes the shechita and he must complete it before beginning the other—so too the Rosh.
Of the first species. This is Ba'al HaItur's view in the Tur; and it appears he compared it to fruits, as written in Orach Chaim siman 206: he blessed on fruits before him and afterward they brought him more of that species—he need not bless, even if they were not before him when he blessed, end of his words; and from here it is difficult for what the Beit Yosef wrote—that it is not specifically of that species literally, but we mean that it should be of the type of the first blessing, end of his words; but here Ba'al HaItur distinguishes between chaya and bird, and their blessings are the same, and nevertheless he must return and bless; and the Tur concludes here, and the Rosh did not distinguish—meaning, even if they brought him of the first species he must return and bless; and it appears he holds it is not similar to fruits we mentioned, for in eating a person's nature is to continue and add more, and therefore it is as if they were before him at the time of the blessing; not so with shechita; and the Beit Yosef wrote that the Ran in the name of R' Meshulam, the Raavad, and the Rambam hold like the Rosh; if so, Ba'al HaItur is alone against them.
