He covers the blood of the first, etc.—because he changed his mind and his intention was not initially on them; and his law is like one who spoke, in seif kaf vav—so too all the poskim; and see in Orach Chaim siman 169 and 179.
And some say that if they brought him, etc.—and in the Ittur he concludes thus, and such is the essential ruling; and see in Orach Chaim siman 206; and so it is in some editions in the words of the Rambam, and so wrote Darkei Moshe that the Taz in Orach Chaim siman 206 ruled without qualification like Ba'al HaItur and not like the words of the Rosh, therefore; and in the Derishah he wrote as follows: and you may say—according to what they compared the blessing on shechita to the blessing on fruits, if so, when they brought him another species too, as the Beit Yosef wrote in siman 206, and so in the Shulchan Aruch there (and so it appears from the words of the Rambam there, and so ruled the Levush there); and further, according to this it is difficult—the words of the Tur who wrote nearby that even if some of that species on which he blessed are still before him and they brought him more of that species, he must return and bless on what was brought; and this is against what is written in siman 206; rather it must be as the Beit Yosef wrote, that there is a distinction between eating, which has a fixed measure, and shechita, which has no fixed measure, etc.; and so in Sefer Lechem; and he wrote that so it appears explicitly from the words of the Rosh, like the Tur's view and not like Ba'al HaItur; and he ruled thus, see there; and so it appears explicitly from the words of all in Kol Bo; and so the implication of the poskim; and so it appears from the words of Maharshal there; and so Rabbenu Yerucham, Netiv 1 end of part 2—that when one circumcised one infant and his intention was not initially on the second, he must bless on the second; and the Rambam himself brought this in siman 265 seif 5; and so the Ittur there; and it is explained there in Rabbenu Yerucham that the law of milah and shechita is the same in this; and so in Avudraham, laws of milah, and in Mordekhai at the end of the chapter on kisuy hadam, see there.
