(1) [Seif 1] One who slaughters must bless, etc.; and if he slaughtered and did not bless—it is kosher. So too the Rambam, chapter 1 of Hilchot Shechitah; and the Rosh and Mordekhai at the beginning of Chullin—not like the laws of Eldad HaDani, who wrote if he slaughtered and did not bless it is pigul; and also not like what the Mordekhai wrote in the chapter on kisuy hadam, who found Rabbenu Yitzchak bar Avraham in a responsum of the Geonim that one who slaughtered deliberately and did not bless is forbidden to eat from it, but for others it is permitted, and he is liable for lashes (Beit Yosef). And so the Shach, seif kaf alef, in the name of several poskim, that even if deliberate and he did not bless it is kosher even for himself, see there. And so in ShakhneHag in the Hagahah Tashbetz, part 3, chapter 248, section 2, Lechem, section 3, Kereti, section 1, Shemen Chafetz, section 1, Shulchan Gavoah, section 1. However, where the people are lax regarding the mitzvah of kisuy hadam—who do not cover as required, or who do not bless on covering or on shechita—one should penalize him and obligate him lashes; and also forbid him shechita (for the transgressor, but for others it is permitted) because of fence-making (Rashal, chapter on kisuy hadam, siman 11). And so Lechem there. Lechem Yehudah there. Shemen Chafetz there. And so the Shach below siman 28, seif kaf gimmel. Pri Chadash in this siman, section 1, Kometz, section 1, Zivchei Ra'anan, siman 18, in Temidim, section 3, Menachem Nachum, section 1, Zivchei Tzedek, section 1.
(2) There. One who slaughters must bless, etc.; and likewise one who slaughters for others blesses (Zivchei Ra'anan there in additions, section 1; Menachem Nachum there). And so in Orach Chaim siman 432, section 27, regarding examination of chametz, in the name of several poskim, that if the homeowner is obligated on another to examine it, the other blesses after he blesses, see there. And so below, siman 265, regarding milah—that the circumciser blesses even though it is not his son, see there. And see below, section 7.
(3) Ben Paku'a that spread out on the ground and requires shechita from doubt—he blesses on the shechita (Peri Chadash, section 1). But the Peri To'ar, section 1, wrote he should not bless, see there; and we already wrote on this above siman 13, section 20, see there.
(4) There. One who slaughters must bless beforehand, etc.—for all mitzvot one blesses before doing them (Pesachim 7b).
(5) There. One who slaughters must bless beforehand, etc.—meaning we are not concerned lest it become invalid in his hand, nor lest treifah be found—for we establish the presumption of kashrut, and the slaughterer does not desire nevelah (Peri To'ar, section 2; Zivchei Tzedek, section 2). And see below siman 28, section 110.
(6) And if he forgot and slaughtered and did not bless before shechita—he does not bless after shechita (Shach, seif kaf gimmel; ShakhneHag in Hagahah Tashbetz, section 4; Peri To'ar, section 3; Kereti, section 3; Shemen Chafetz, section 1; Pri Chadash, section 2; Shulchan Gavoah, section 3; Orach Chaim, siman 4, section 2; Chokhmat Adam, general rule 5, section 1; Pithei Teshuvah, section 3, in the name of responsa Tiferet Tzvi siman 3; Zivchei Ra'anan in Temidim, section 4). And the reason: since he was fit to bless beforehand and pushed himself aside—for since it was pushed aside it was pushed aside (Zivchei Ra'anan there). And even within the time of speech after completing shechita he does not bless (Kereti there; Shemen Chafetz there; Orach Chaim there; Zivchei Ra'anan there; Zivchei Shalom, section 1; Zivchei Tzedek, section 2). And although there is an opinion that he blesses—they are not difficult, for one does not bless because he holds so. However, if possible, he should return after another behama or bird to slaughter and bless on it and intend to discharge this one too (Shemen Chafetz there; Lechem Yehudah there; Zivchei Ra'anan there; Zivchei Tzedek there).
(7) There. "And commanded us upon the shechita." And they instituted to bless "ba'al" and not in the language of "to slaughter," because it is a mitzvah that can be fulfilled through another even if he is not the owner of the behama or bird; therefore they bless "ba'al," as the language "al" comes to say the Torah was not particular that the animal be slaughtered if he wishes to eat it and the slaughterer be whoever it may be (Levush). And if he blessed in the language "to slaughter" he fulfilled, as written in our words in Orach Chaim siman 432, section 7, see there.
(8) There, hagahah. And if he slaughters something in which a defect may develop, etc.; and when it is found kosher he blesses, etc.; and even though we wrote above, section 6, that one does not bless after shechita—here it is different because he could not bless beforehand, it was not pushed aside (Shach, seif kaf dalet; Kereti, section 3; Shemen Chafetz, section 2; Pri Chadash, section 4; Chokhmat Adam, general rule 5, section 1; Zivchei Ra'anan in Temidim, section 7; Zivchei Tzedek, section 3).
