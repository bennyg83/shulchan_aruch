(Siman 19, seif 1, in the hagahah) And when it is found kosher he blesses after the shechita. I am astonished at the conclusion of the Shach, seif kaf gimmel, to halachah that if he forgot and did not bless before shechita he does not bless afterward—one may say the same applies when a defect developed in it: he does not bless afterward, for it is not similar to immersion of a convert, where we say: since initially he was not fit, it was not pushed aside—for since in any case he was not fit to bless beforehand, when the sages instituted this berachah of immersion of converts initially, here it was instituted to bless afterward; but the berachah on shechita they fixed and instituted before doing it; only in this matter where a defect developed in it he may not bless from safek—one may say: since it was pushed aside it was pushed aside, and there is no distinction between pushed aside due to transgression and unintentionally or due to coercion. Apply this yourself to a baal keri, who does not bless beforehand: if he ate while his keri was still upon him, or slaughtered then and immersed for his keri—can we say he blesses afterward on what he ate or slaughtered, since initially he was not permitted to bless? Or it appears: since the foundation of berachot they instituted before doing them, only now there was something preventing him from blessing—he does not bless afterward. And the source of the Rambam's law is the Or Zaru'a; and the Or Zaru'a follows his method, for the Hagahah of Ashrei in his name, first chapter of Chullin, wrote: regarding all mitzvot, if he did not bless before doing them he blesses afterward; but for us, who hold like the Rambam, one may say that here too he does not bless afterward—and requires further study for practical law. And in the foundation of the dispute between the Rambam and the Hagahah of the Rosh mentioned above, the Peri Chadash wrote to prove like Ba'al Berachat Avraham from that which we say in the first chapter of Pesachim regarding lulav: from the time it was gathered he exited—behold he blesses afterward; and the Taz wrote on this: if so, the baraita itself is difficult—why did they not institute l'chatchila to bless before doing it like all mitzvot, since it is impossible to bless beforehand, as Tosafot wrote (Sukkah 39); if so, here the Rambam concedes it is like immersion of a convert that it is not pushed aside at all, see there. And it is seemingly astonishing—for still it is difficult how the baraita teaches: all berachot one blesses before doing them except immersion—why did it not also teach except lulav? And it is strained to say two baraitot dispute on this, that this tanna holds it is possible to bless beforehand. And in my humble opinion one may say: the baraita cited except immersion—for regarding this specifically [blessing is] after doing, and even b'dieved one does not fulfill if it was in his hand beforehand—for at the time he blessed he was not fit for the mitzvah, and it is not proper to say "and commanded us"; he did not bless at all—it is like invalid and he returns and blesses afterward. But regarding lulav—even though l'chatchila there is no [need] to bless beforehand, for while the lulav is in the vessel it is not reasonable to bless, as Tosafot wrote in Sukkah—nevertheless it is obvious b'dieved if he blessed while it rests in the vessel he fulfilled. And with this it is settled: for the Raavad, who holds regarding Birkat Eirusin we bless after betrothal—from concern lest he retract from consecrating; and why is this not counted in that baraita, to teach except immersion and Birkat Eirusin? According to the above it is fine: in any case b'dieved if he blessed before betrothal he fulfilled—therefore it cited only immersion, for here it is deficient even in doing it beforehand.
