Kosher. Even if he acted deliberately and did not bless, it is kosher—even for himself; and so wrote the Beit Yosef, and Darkei Moshe, and Maharshal in the chapter on kisuy hadam, seif 1; and so it appears in the BeHag at the beginning of daf 126; and the Bach did not rule thus—and see below siman 28:
A matter in which a defect may develop, etc. And Maharshal there, siman 5, ruled like Rashi (and the Bach in this siman wrote on this Maharshal like the Or Zaru'a; in my humble opinion he did not examine carefully all Maharshal's words on this)—that he blesses before the shechita, for even if it becomes treifah the shechita avails for it to be purified from nevelah status; and it does not appear so in the Yerushalmi in the chapter "HaRo'eh," in what they say there regarding the blessing on shechita: the presumption is that the intestines are kosher—and see below siman 28, seif kaf vav:
And when it is found kosher he blesses, etc. And in Darkei Moshe he brings in the name of the Hagahah of Ashrei on the first chapter of Chullin that even on a kosher animal, if he forgot and did not bless before the shechita, he should bless after the shechita, end of his words; and so it is found in Miktzat Shechitat HaAchronim, and in the Hagahah of Ashrei there he concludes that every mitzvah on which he did not bless—he transgresses its performance, and he blesses after doing it, as the Avnei Nezer explained in Berachot from the Or Zaru'a, end of his words. But in the first chapter of Berachot they wrote as follows: where one did not bless before the mitzvah, he blesses after the mitzvah and fulfills his obligation; however, regarding a meal, where it is forbidden for a person to benefit without a blessing, once he transgressed and ate and the time for Birkat HaMazon arrived, since it was pushed aside it was pushed aside—or so the Or Zaru'a (end of his words). However, this is not the Rambam's view—in chapter 11 of Hilchot Berachot, law 6, he wrote that if one slaughtered without a blessing, even if he separated terumot and maasrot or immersed and did not bless, he does not return and bless after the deed; and so for all similar cases, end of his words. And in Sefer Berachat Avraham, end of part 1 and beginning of part 10, he greatly extended to refute the Rambam's words with rhetoric, and his main foundation is that if it were so that one does not bless after doing them, then where do we permit a convert (as below siman 268 seif 2) and similarly other those obligated in immersion (as below siman 120) and other mitzvot—to bless l'chatchila after doing them; and if because he could not bless beforehand the blessing would not apply at all, end of his words—and all his words do not appear correct to me, for we certainly hold that all berachot are d'rabbanan except Birkat HaMazon, as stated in Berachot in several places and among the poskim; if so, they said to bless before the mitzvah and not after the mitzvah at all, and they said regarding a convert and similar cases that while the person is still not fit he blesses after the mitzvah—and so it appears in chapter 3 "that they ate" (daf 51a) where they say there that Ravina said: therefore even if he finished eating he should return and bless, as it is taught: one who immersed and ascended says upon his ascent, "Blessed are You, Lord our God, King of the universe, Who has sanctified us with His commandments and commanded us concerning immersion"—but that is not so, for there initially the person was not fit, here initially he was fit, and since it was pushed aside it was pushed aside; therefore it is only regarding immersion and similar cases because initially he was not fit, but where he was fit initially and did not bless, he does not bless afterward, and even though in Sefer Berachat Avraham he wrote that this applies also to shechita and similar, that is not so, for there it is Berachat HaMitzvot, etc., but it is preferable from it, etc.; however this is not required at all, and so R' Yonah in chapter 3 "that they ate" there—that the same applies to all other berachot that are said before doing the mitzvah: if he did not bless he does not bless afterward, see there (and further clear proof: they taught in the first chapter of Pesachim (daf 6b) that in the house of Rav they say: all berachot one blesses before doing them except immersion, for the person is still not fit; it is also taught thus: one who immersed and ascended says upon his ascent, "Blessed... upon immersion"; and if it were so, what proof do we bring from this baraita—perhaps only one who immersed and ascended, but not l'chatchila; and like Ravina who explains it as b'dieved only, for regarding other mitzvot too if he did not bless initially he blesses afterward; rather certainly the Gemara holds that if we do not say "the person is still not fit" and permit l'chatchila, then even b'dieved he does not bless, since it was pushed aside; and so it appears explicitly from the words of the Tosafot in the chapter "Lulav HaGazul" (Sukkah daf 39a) and the Rosh there, who wrote that if he took the lulav in his hand without a blessing, it must be that he blesses afterward, since the mitzvah is not yet fully completed, for it requires shaking, end of his words—it appears that if he completed the entire mitzvah, and so generally where this reason does not apply, he does not bless (in Nekudat Kesef they delete from "and further proof" until here; and some emend there "if so, even b'dieved he does not bless, since it was pushed aside," and examine this). And also in Piskei Rekanati siman 72 he brought the words of the Or Zaru'a and afterward the words of the Rambam in the conclusion—it appears that he holds thus; and so wrote Rabbeinu Avudraham in the name of Ba'al HaMaor like the Rambam; and so it appears from the words of the Rif in a responsum regarding the blessing on milah that R' Moshe Alshakar brought in his responsum siman 18; and further, we hold that every safek in berachot we are lenient:
He blesses, etc. The Ittur wrote that we are accustomed that when it happens that one will slaughter something that has a safek of treifah, he slaughters a chicken or another bird before it—and his intention is also about this; and so wrote the Bach; and this is a correct matter; and so it is below siman 28 regarding kisuy haba al yad terefah; nevertheless the law of the Rambam is true where it is not possible to slaughter another bird—and examine this.
