If he wishes to slaughter. I have a safek: if he began to slaughter one and blessed on shechita and it became treifah—can he slaughter others without a berachah? In the Yerushalmi: he took a knife intending to bless and it fell from him—he must return and bless; if so, this too is similar—for it fell from him, for it became treifah; and in Orach Chaim siman 206, seif 6, the Mechaber ruled he must return and bless; and the Rambam emended: namely, that his intention was not to eat of that species; if so, here too if his intention was clearly to slaughter another behama he need not bless. But in siman 100 the Mechaber brought two views, see there and in the Magen Avraham—and if so it fell into dispute. But to me it appears it is not similar—for there the berachah was that he would eat the fruit; since the fruit is lost, his berachah and thought are in vain; but here the berachah was to fulfill the mitzvah of shechita, and since he slaughtered the animal according to law and custom, only it was treifah—the mitzvah of shechita was fulfilled, for so is the mitzvah to slaughter and not to be concerned lest treifah be found, for we establish the presumption of kashrut; if so he did the mitzvah, and even though it became treifah and lacks benefit, nevertheless the mitzvah did not move from its place, and he too blessed on the mitzvah and blessed correctly. And in my humble opinion the reasoning is correct; nevertheless it is difficult for me to rely on this; therefore if his intention was not clear to slaughter others and it became treifah, he should not slaughter others—for why bring himself into safek berachah? Only if there is another slaughterer there, leave it for him to slaughter a behama or bird and bless and intend to discharge him—and thus he can complete slaughtering the remaining animals without concern—and examine this. And behold, from all the Shulchan Aruch's words we learn: if he has many birds or chayot to cover, he blesses on the first covering and afterward covers and continues without berachah; and it is difficult—for in the Gemara Chullin 87 we say: if the mitzvah of covering was his and another came and covered, he must give him ten [coins]; and the Gemara asks: reward for mitzvah or reward for berachah? A practical difference for Beit HaMikdash, where there is only one mitzvah and four berachot; and it is difficult why it says a practical difference in another matter not related to the animal, and does not say here: if many chayot or birds were before him to cover and he blessed on one and covered, and before he came to cover the second another came and covered—if the reason is mitzvah he must pay for he stole the mitzvah; but if the reason is berachah he did not steal, for he was not blessing; and perhaps one may say: if initially his intention in the berachah was on all he would cover, if afterward he covers he does two mitzvot—mitzvah of covering and also berachah—even though he does not bless on it, nevertheless since initially his intention was on this covering, it is as if he blessed on this covering; simple explanation—and the first berachah ascends for all and he takes reward for berachah and covering; if so, when another comes and covers, he does two evils with him—covering and berachah that he takes—and there is no practical difference—and examine this.
