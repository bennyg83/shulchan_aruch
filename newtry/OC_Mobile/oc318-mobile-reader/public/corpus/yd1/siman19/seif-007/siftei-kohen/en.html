Many were before him, etc.—and the same applies if one was before him and after he blessed, before he slaughtered, they brought others before him—he need not return and bless; and so it is proven in the Beit Yosef.
And l'chatchila, etc.—because the Tur holds that always whatever was not before him when he blessed requires a blessing unless his intention was on all that they will bring him; therefore, to remove himself from the dispute and to rule like all, he should l'chatchila set his intention on all that they will bring him; and similarly wrote the Rambam in Orach Chaim siman 206; and Maharshal, Perishah, and the Bach wrote that a hired slaughterer designated to slaughter permanently in a place—his intention is always on all that they bring him, even of two species, and he need not bless as long as he did not slaughter in the meantime.
