And even if he has grown up. See the Taz who elaborated bringing proof for the ruling of the Shulchan Aruch; and see in Responsa Shivat Tzion siman 61 who wrote regarding this: at first glance it is difficult, since the Taz in siman 340 se’if katan 15 ruled that a minor who has reached the age of chinuch must observe all the laws of mourning; and it appears that in any case there is a practical difference — that based on the reason of chinuch it only applies when the minor has a father who is obligated to train him in mitzvot, but where there is no father there is no chinuch at all, as the Magen Avraham wrote in siman 343 se’if katan 1 and in siman 616 se’if katan 2. There is a further practical difference: if the minor goes to school to study Torah and he came of age within the seven days — in that case too chinuch does not apply, since by this you are keeping him from Torah study; in this case the ruling applies that all laws of mourning are annulled for him. But a minor who is engaged in a trade and does not study Torah and has a father — he observes mourning because of chinuch. Now all this applies to a minor male, but regarding a minor female, the Magen Avraham wrote in siman 343 that the father is not obligated to train his daughter; and I have seen in Tosafot Yeshanim in tractate Yoma folio 82 that the father is obligated to train his daughter — end of his words, see there. But if he treated it lightly. Radbaz, new responsa 521, discusses one who heard that his father died, but a relative falsely told him the report was untrue, and two days later admitted it was true. The relative acted improperly and caused him to miss the Torah-level mourning of the day of burial, since recent news is like the day of burial. Legally, however, he counts only from the first report. [Citation 7.]
