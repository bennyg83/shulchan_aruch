(Siman 396 in Taz s.k. 2) He elaborated to prove like the Rosh, though not for his reason, from Perek Nigmar HaDin. In my humble opinion, there is no proof from there at all. There, the dead person was not fit for anyone in the world to mourn over him at all, and therefore, since mourning had already been nullified from him at the first hour, which is the essence of bitterness, there is no further bitterness over which to mourn him. Therefore we say that since it was deferred, it remains deferred. Rashi’s question from the festival is because he compares it, that during a festival too the dead is not fit for anyone in the world to mourn over him. But here, the dead is fit to be mourned, and the bitterness of his heart is upon him immediately and continues. If so, the bitterness of his heart is still within him, except that the minor is not subject to obligation; when he becomes adult, he is subject to obligation. This is clear; examine carefully. However, what he distinguished between havdalah and here is correct, and so it is written in the Shach, that one can easily distinguish between them; see there.
