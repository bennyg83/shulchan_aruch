And permitted even for drinking. This is according to Rabbenu Tam in the Tur who disagrees with Rashi ; for Rashi there is no distinction between touch of his body and touch through something else ; both forbidden in benefit with intention and unintentionally for drinking; but R' Eliezer says touch through something else unintentionally has no name touch at all but the name force; and perforce permitted entirely unintentionally like force permitted in all manners if unintentionally whether he does not know it is wine or does not know of the touch; but Ran wrote a third view of the Geonim's approach who hold like Rabbenu Tam regarding unintentional touch of a non-Jew through something else permitted even for drinking ; except they did not give it the name force but it is stricter regarding if he intends touch even though he thinks it is beer ; in this forbidden even through something else; and for them Rama's challenge in perek R' Yishmael "behold he touched the stopper" is resolved well ; since in any case there is prohibition in this touch even though he did not know it was wine; but for Rabbenu Tam who gives it the name force one must strain and say thus he challenges "behold he touched wine through the stopper" ; namely since it is full he touches wine with his body through the stopper; and Beit Yosef challenged whence Ran says the Geonim disagree with Rabbenu Tam on this and it is not difficult at all, for since the Geonim did not give it the name force of a non-Jew ; no need to strain like Rabbenu Tam's explanation in what he challenges "behold he touched the stopper"; rather Rabbenu Tam must perforce say thus.
And likewise if he intended to touch, etc. He stated specifically these two types; but if his intent was touch but he had occupation in one of the occupations mentioned in this siman ; he has no permission for drinking; and proof from what they said in the mishnah measuring with a reed and sold; and as the Rosh wrote perek R' Yishmael: and in truth we establish intentional touch without intention is stricter than where there is no intention at all ; non-Jew touched a lulav unintentionally permitted for drinking; but if they measured with a reed forbidden for drinking ; end of his words; and likewise it appears in Rambam chapter 13 of Forbidden Foods: for touch by hand occupation is worse than no intent of touch, for there in that chapter he wrote a non-Jew touched wine and did not intend for it ; behold wine permitted in benefit only; how so ; such as fell on a leather bottle of wine or extended hand to a barrel thinking it was oil and found wine ; end; and afterward wrote there measuring wine with a reed, etc. ; permitted in benefit only ; end; it implies but by hand forbidden in benefit; and likewise Beit Yosef according to his view in this siman: by hand occupation does not help to permit in benefit; behold occupation is worse than no intent of touch. And it further appears for Rambam unintentional touch through something else without intent of touch is permitted even for drinking like Rabbenu Tam; and Rif ; if like Rashi who forbids for drinking why write he extended hand to barrel and thinks it was oil ; not permitted except in benefit to write a stringency even touch through something else; rather certainly through something else truly permitted even for drinking since unintentional touch or he does not know it is wine; but regarding occupation he holds through his hand forbidden even in benefit and through something else permitted in benefit ; and this is clear; and I am astonished Beit Yosef did not notice this to bring Rambam's view on this that he also holds permitted for drinking; and I have a place to be astonished here: since Rabbenu Tam's reason for touch through something else permitted for drinking is because touch through something else is not called touch but only the name force ; as Ran brought whom Beit Yosef brought ; in this there is a distinction between Geonim and Rabbenu Tam: for Geonim did not give it the name force and perforce forbidden with intent of touch even though he did not know it was wine, and permission is only without intent of touch at all; but for Rabbenu Tam who gives it the name force ; permitted even with intent of touch except he does not know it is wine; and likewise the Tur to permit also in this; perforce the Tur holds here there is the name force in touch through something else; and it is astonishing accordingly: with touch through something else with intention there is prohibition only for drinking like force with intention; and likewise Ran explicitly whom Beit Yosef brought: and I already wrote Rabbenu Tam holds touch through something else is only his force; and according to one who says his force even with intention does not forbid except for drinking ; likewise the law in his touch through something else ; end; and if so why did the Tur write in siman 125 in conclusion there is no prohibition in force with intention except for drinking, and in siman 124 wrote touch through something else with intention forbids even in benefit ; behold both have one force; and it is very difficult to resolve.
All their touch is called unintentional. Meaning they have no intent to pour libation and it is like a child in this siman who forbids only for drinking; and wherever there is one favorable aspect ; permitted for drinking for us; except Rama mentioned only two matters: if he touched with his hand ; namely without intent of touch; or he did not know it was wine ; it implies he intended touch and knew it was wine but has occupation ; not permitted, for I already brought proof nearby from the Rosh's words that occupation does not permit like those two; and likewise explained in responsum Rav Laviev siman 41 from which is the source of this Rama's law: in siman 41 regarding an incident where they made wine from raisins and filled the barrel with water and on Shabbat it was very fermenting and spilled through boiling and they called a non-Jew to shake the barrel with one staff so it would not spill ; he answered on this that this is complete wine since it had three days, etc.; rather one may permit on grounds Rashbam wrote in the name of Rashi that gentiles in our time are not idolaters; and combine with this what Rambam wrote: wherever we said in this matter regarding our wine that it is forbidden for drinking and permitted in benefit because of a side of touch that a non-Jew idolater touched it; but if its prohibition is because of a non-Jew who is not an idolater, such as an Ishmaelite who touched our wine unintentionally or patted the mouth of the barrel ; behold permitted for drinking; and likewise all similar cases ; end; behold if we take these two reasons it is possible in the case before us to permit the wine for drinking if the non-Jews' touch was only through something else ; then even if she knows the nature of avodah zarah, since her touch is without intent of libation it does not forbid except for drinking; and since we hold her as not knowing we descend a level and permit even for drinking; but if she touched with her hand one may not permit in any manner for drinking, for if we held her as knowing the nature of avodah zarah we would forbid even in benefit; and now we descend a level and permit in benefit but not for drinking ; end. What the rabbi wrote: if we held her as knowing the nature of avodah zarah it would be forbidden even in benefit ; this is according to Rambam who holds touch by his hand even if he has occupation forbidden in benefit, as Shulchan Aruch wrote seif 19; and perforce Mahari Chai wrote: for us who establish like the Rosh whom he brings there seif 19 that he is not forbidden in benefit in occupation even in touch by hand ; perforce when we descend a level it will be regarding idolaters in our time permitted even for drinking if he has occupation such as saving wine even touching with his hand in wine to save it; and if so we grasp for us the leniency of Rambam regarding descending a level from idolaters to non-Jew in our time and the leniency of the Rosh for Talmudic law of touch by hand; and perforce for us permitted for drinking; but for Rambam certainly forbidden for us for drinking in touch by hand with occupation; and perforce Rambam wrote the permission in Ishmaelite if he touched unintentionally ; namely without intent of touch at all ; that by Talmudic law permitted in benefit alone in touch by hand as Rambam explicitly wrote in chapter 12 of Forbidden Foods ; therefore in this specifically permitted in Ishmaelite; and likewise patting the mouth of the barrel ; in this too by Talmudic law permitted in benefit since there is no way of libation in that even though he touches by hand; therefore we need to combine the Rosh's view we mentioned; and examine carefully that the leniency we mentioned in the name of Rambam of descending a level for non-Jew in our time helps only for unintentional touch or he does not know it is wine to permit for drinking; but occupation does not help in this even according to the Rosh, for the Rosh wrote beginning perek R' Yishmael: and it is possible those who practiced permission of benefit in touch of a non-Jew relied on this ; we establish like the rabbis of R' Nasan who forbid measuring by hand ; sell; idolaters in our time all their touch is like touch of measuring; and further all their touch cannot come to libation ; end; behold this reasoning does not help for permission of benefit; and one may say there are two favorable aspects here ; one from the non-Jew himself being like measuring; and further he is truly occupied in measuring and similar in saving wine ; this is not so, for the essence of the leniency in occupation of measuring or similar is because there is no intent of libation ; what difference if he has this advantage once namely from himself not fit for libation and what difference if he has another time from being occupied in measuring and not intending libation; and further if so regarding an idolatrous child we should say by Talmudic law his touch permitted even for drinking since he has two favorable aspects ; one from himself not expert in avodah zarah and second from not intending touch as the Rosh wrote there that Rav's reason the child does not make yayin nesech to forbid in benefit is because he does not intend touch and not because he does not know avodah zarah ; for if so it should be permitted even for drinking like children of maidservants; and if so there is a difficulty what is truly the reason ; occupation does not help as you would say regarding non-Jew in our time with two favorable aspects why combination helps to permit drinking and all the more the child in Talmudic times should help; rather certainly combination does not help; and one may say behold we find combination of two favorable aspects helps ; namely unintentional touch through something else permitted even for drinking even by Talmudic law; this is not because combination helps but because touch of a non-Jew through something else is called his force alone for Rabbenu Tam whom we establish like; and in his force permitted unintentionally ; therefore also in touch through something else permitted unintentionally. Further it is difficult for me on permission of drinking for us in touch of non-Jews by hand with occupation because of Rambam's words we mentioned ; if so we descend a level; if so there is difficulty on those poskim who hold touch through something else with intention forbids only for drinking as Beit Yosef wrote in this siman in the name of Ran that Rabbenu Tam holds thus ; why do we say in the mishnah non-Jews measured with a reed ; sell; why not say we descend a level and through occupation of measuring it should be permitted for drinking since even with intention there is no prohibition of benefit; what difference non-Jews in our time from touch of non-Jews through something else of Talmudic times ; in both there is only prohibition of drinking; and why would combination of occupation help more here than there; rather perforce one must say combination of occupation does not help to permit drinking at all, except specifically without intent of touch; and as Rama wrote here; and this did not enter that level Rambam mentioned that Ishmaelite has over other non-Jews ; specifically in what he mentioned namely without intent of touch, etc., he has an advantage. Also from the Tur there is proof for prohibition, for he wrote unintentional touch of a non-Jew through something else ; such as he has a reed in his hand and touched wine unintentionally or with intent of touch but does not know it is wine, etc. ; permitted for drinking for practical halachah; behold he mentioned only these two but did not permit because of occupation ; rather combination of occupation does not help at all for permission of drinking; and likewise regarding non-Jew for us it does not help; nevertheless since the elder Mahari Chai already ruled to be lenient for drinking in saving wine by placing his hand on the hole ; one may give a reason: since there is no touch in all the wine but only preventing it from coming out, there is only prohibition of force of non-Jews as Ran wrote in perek R' Yishmael in the name of Ramban in Rav Papa's incident where the spigot was removed ; all wine that was fit to come out is called his force and forbidden for drinking; and force unintentionally the rabbis did not decree on it; and likewise Rambam chapter 12 of Forbidden Foods in this language; and found everything comes from his force ; one may say also in this matter that since he has occupation in saving wine he is called for this purpose unintentional and permitted for drinking; but if he placed a finger in the hole passing over the rims, even though he does so to save, since there is actual touch inside as all poskim and Tosafot wrote in the name of Sefer HaTerumah ; forbidden even for us for drinking as stated.
And likewise if he touched even with his hand, etc. In Maharil's responsum siman 38 he wrote that a wine barrel was in a non-Jew's hand sealed well with the stopper, only in the stopper there was concern lest he drank through a hollow reed; and he ruled permitted even if he drank through that reed, being touch of a non-Jew through something else; and if because of a returning drop of wine nullified in sixty; and he wrote there is no concern here except lest he drew through a bent spigot that Shulchan Aruch mentioned in seif 23 ; this is not common and not everyone knows that craft ; end; and accordingly in Galilee where bent spigot is common one should certainly forbid; nevertheless we learn here there is no prohibition if he drinks through a hollow reed; and in truth this matter is discussed in dispute, for it is found in the Gemara perek R' Yishmael (Avodah Zarah daf 58): a non-Jew agradim ; meaning appointed by the king over measures ; pierced with a spigot; Rashi: hollow reed and raised wine to his mouth; and we say there forbidden in benefit all who know it is wine; and the Rosh wrote there it appears from Tosafot the drop of wine that was in his mouth forbids everything in any amount, etc. ; it implies forbidden only from mixture; perforce for us who establish in siman 134 stam yeinam nullified in sixty ; permitted in the case above; and likewise it appears from Rambam chapter 12 of Forbidden Foods that prohibition is from mixture from what was in his mouth and fell there; but Ran wrote there wine is forbidden entirely because the lower became a base for the upper and it is like touching all wine in the barrel; and from the Tur's words who considered this spigot law under prohibition of non-Jew's touch ; it appears he also holds like Ran, for he compared this to drinking from the barrel; and Rashba in Terumat HaDeshen daf 133 wrote like the Rosh; and likewise Tosafot in perek Gid HaNasheh (daf 97 end of side 1) regarding the spigot case: if the non-Jew preceded and ruled forbidden ; meaning l'chatchila but b'di'eved permitted, for prohibition is only because it fell from his mouth and it is nullified in sixty; and since Ran and the Tur are alone against them ; we establish to permit as Maharil wrote above. And in Derishah siman 130 he wrote: one must give a reason why we do not permit b'di'eved in our time if a non-Jew drank through a tube in a small hole in a barrel, etc.; and he brought Ran's words and it was already explained one should not rule like Ran here; but the language he wrote "why we do not permit," etc. ; it implies it is customary practice to forbid and we did not hear such custom to forbid; on the contrary I recall in my youth such a case occurred in Pozna and all scholars there agreed to permit, and so is the essence; and one should not be stringent in this in a place of great loss ; so in my humble opinion. And likewise if the non-Jew drew wine by hand from the barrel through a small spigot that wine merchants regularly use, and drew an amount the spigot holds and he touches with his hand wine at the mouth of the spigot ; likewise one may permit b'di'eved in great loss if returned to the barrel, for it is one law. And what he wrote in the hagahah permitted and one should not publicize, etc. ; in my humble opinion even though Rama wrote to be lenient regarding touch of non-Jews in our time ; nevertheless force of non-Jews forbids even in our time, for Rambam wrote in this law simply in chapter 12 of Forbidden Foods and beginning chapter 1 wrote a general rule: wherever stam prohibition is written ; regarding idolater for prohibition of benefit, and regarding non-idolater such as Ishmaelite for prohibition of drinking; perforce also regarding non-Jew in our time it is so; nevertheless in my humble opinion one should be lenient in this in all manners, for prohibition is only in the lower and not the upper ; in such stream it is obvious we do not say connection.
