Permitted. Shach writes that whenever Talmudic law forbids only drinking, nowadays it is permitted even for drinking in a case of loss. Outside a case of loss we still treat contemporary non-Jews as idolaters for this purpose. Where strict law permits benefit because the person is not an idolater, such as a minor or ger toshav, drinking remains forbidden even in a case of loss, because lack of idolatry itself never permitted drinking. Taz writes that if the non-Jew knew it was wine and intended to touch it but was occupied, it remains forbidden for drinking. Yet because Bach ruled leniently where he saves wine by covering the hole with his hand, one may distinguish: where he merely prevents wine from flowing out, there is only the prohibition of his force, and his preoccupation with rescue renders it unintentional. If he inserts his finger through the hole and directly touches inside, it remains forbidden for drinking. In a case where rescue is impossible otherwise and no Jew is present, Shach permits it in a case of loss. Maharil permitted wine in a sealed barrel where a non-Jew might have drunk through a hollow reed, since this is indirect touch and any returning drop is nullified in sixty. Taz agrees that drinking through a hollow reed does not forbid, especially in a great loss.
Intended. Even if he intended, it would be permitted nowadays; the text merely states the actual circumstances. The practical difference is that it is permitted even without financial loss. What flowed outside is forbidden because he presumably touched it. We do not suspect that he touched the wine with his hand while it flowed, thereby forbidding the wine inside through a connecting stream, because he had no reason to do so. Taz challenges Rama from the law of a maidservant who is not believed to say she drew wine from the barrel, implying that if she certainly drew it, even the barrel is forbidden. He explains that one who draws through a spigot ordinarily touches the outgoing stream, constituting direct touch. If the spigot empties into a hollow tube where this cannot occur, the barrel may be permitted in a great loss. Mordechai distinguishes between narrow and wide openings. Nekudot HaKesef disagrees and permits nowadays even where he touched the stream, because the touch was unintentional.
