Intended. Even if he intended, it would be permitted nowadays; the text merely states the actual circumstances. The practical difference is that it is permitted even without financial loss. What flowed outside is forbidden because he presumably touched it. We do not suspect that he touched the wine with his hand while it flowed, thereby forbidding the wine inside through a connecting stream, because he had no reason to do so. Taz challenges Rama from the law of a maidservant who is not believed to say she drew wine from the barrel, implying that if she certainly drew it, even the barrel is forbidden. He explains that one who draws through a spigot ordinarily touches the outgoing stream, constituting direct touch. If the spigot empties into a hollow tube where this cannot occur, the barrel may be permitted in a great loss. Mordechai distinguishes between narrow and wide openings. Nekudot HaKesef disagrees and permits nowadays even where he touched the stream, because the touch was unintentional.
