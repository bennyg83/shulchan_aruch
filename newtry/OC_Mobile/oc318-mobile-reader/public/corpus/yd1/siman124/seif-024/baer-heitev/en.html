Permitted. Shach wrote: and it implies that likewise in every place where by Talmudic law it is forbidden only for drinking, for us permitted even for drinking; however it was already explained above siman 123 that not in a place of loss we establish that even nowadays they are idolaters; and I received from my teacher the Gaon may his light shine that one should not be lenient on the grounds that non-Jews nowadays are not idolaters except in a place of loss, and he said he likewise saw this from many great decisors; however wherever permitted by law in benefit because he is not an idolater, such as a minor and ger toshav and the like at the beginning of this siman — even for us forbidden for drinking even in a place of loss; for what of it that non-Jews in our time are not idolaters — without this, even if he is not an idolater it is forbidden for drinking — end of his words. And Taz wrote that if the non-Jew knew it was wine and intended to touch it but was occupied, it is forbidden for drinking; nevertheless since the elder Bach already ruled to be lenient for drinking in saving wine by placing his hand on the hole, one may give a reason: since there is no touch in all the wine but only preventing it from coming out, there is only prohibition of the non-Jew's force — one may say in this matter that since he has occupation in saving the wine he is called for this purpose unintentional and permitted for drinking; but if he placed a finger in the hole passing over the rims, even though he does so to save, since there is actual touch inside it is forbidden even for us for drinking as stated — end of his words (and see in seif kaf 42 what Shach wrote: if it is a matter where he cannot save unless he inserts his finger and there is no Jew there, permitted in a place of loss even for drinking — examine carefully). And in Maharil's responsum he wrote that a wine barrel was in a non-Jew's hand sealed well with the stopper, only that in the stopper there was concern lest he drank from it through a hollow reed; and he ruled permitted even if he drank through that reed, for it is touch of a non-Jew through something else; and if because of a returning drop of wine it is nullified in sixty. And he wrote there is no concern here except lest he drew through a bent spigot that the author mentioned in seif 23 — this is not common at all, for not everyone knows that craft — end of his words. And Taz wrote on this: accordingly in Galilee where bent spigot is common one should certainly forbid; nevertheless we learn here that there is no prohibition if he drinks through a hollow reed; and I recall in my youth such a case occurred in Pozna and all the scholars there agreed to permit, and so is the essence; and one should not be stringent in this in a place of great loss. And likewise if the non-Jew drew wine by hand from the barrel through a small spigot that wine merchants regularly use, and drew an amount that the spigot holds and he touches with his hand the wine at the mouth of the spigot — likewise one may permit b'di'eved in great loss if returned to the barrel, for it is one law — end of his words.
Intention. Even so, even if he intended it is permitted for us — rather he stated the truth of the matter; and the practical difference is that it is permitted even not in a place of loss. And that which is forbidden what went outside is because presumably he touched it. And that we are not concerned lest he touched with his hand the wine when it went outside — for then what is inside is also forbidden through stream — why would he do so? As written end of siman 129 and siman 130 seif 1 — end of Shach's words. And Taz wrote there is a difficulty on Rama from what he wrote end of siman 129 regarding a maidservant who is not believed to say she took wine from the barrel — it implies if she clearly took it, even the wine in the barrel is forbidden; and likewise it appears siman 129 seif 1 that the reason for prohibition even for us when a non-Jew drew through a spigot is that it is close that he touched the spurting that comes out, for such is the custom of one engaged in drawing wine from a barrel through a spigot, and it is actual touch; and if the spigot is inserted in a hollow reed where one cannot say so, one may permit the wine in the barrel in great loss as written beginning siman 126; and in Mordechai perek HaSocher he further distinguishes between a narrow hole and a wide one, for easily one touches in a wide one — nevertheless Rama did not permit here except when piercing with a knife which is a small hole and it is not close to touch in drawing the knife in the thin spurting that comes out; and see siman 130 seif 1 that here it deals when the knife is still there, but if the non-Jew already sealed it, forbidden — end of his words (and in Nekudat HaKesef he disagrees with all this and holds for us permitted through a spigot even if he touched the spurting, for it is unintentional touch of a non-Jew — end of his words).
