For the Rambam. This is astonishing. According to the Rambam, who rules that wine moved by a non-Jew's force is forbidden for benefit, it is indeed forbidden even when the act was done by the non-Jews of our time. Nevertheless, according to our ruling that wine moved by a non-Jew's force remains permitted for benefit, together with our acceptance of the Rambam's principle that the status descends one level, wine moved by the non-Jews of our time should remain permitted even for drinking. This resembles the Bach's ruling concerning wine measured by a non-Jew, cited by the Taz in seif katan 31, and the Shach states this explicitly in siman 125, seif katan 2. Moreover, in my humble opinion, there is room for leniency even according to the Rambam. When a non-Jew intentionally touches wine through another object, the wine is ordinarily forbidden for benefit. Nevertheless, when this is done by the non-Jews of our time, the wine is permitted, because it is contact through another object without intent to touch the wine directly. Here too, the movement caused by his force should be regarded as force applied without the relevant intent, which is permitted.
