(There seif 19) Or if he took a barrel and threw it in his anger into a pit — permitted in benefit and forbidden for drinking. See Taz seif kaf 16 who is astonished at Rambam and the author who wrote it is forbidden for drinking, while in the mishnah it is taught explicitly "this was an incident and they permitted it" — it implies entirely permitted even for drinking. And my teacher may his light shine answered to resolve: certainly according to R' Shimon who holds measuring with a reed is permitted even for drinking, the mishnah was properly taught "and they permitted it" even for drinking; but for us, since we establish that measuring with a reed is forbidden for drinking — likewise when he threw it in his anger into a pit it is forbidden for drinking — examine carefully.
(There in the hagahah) One may not compare matter to matter in these things. And Taz challenged seif kaf 18: behold this is from the Rosh's words who disagrees with Raavad and holds that rescue is not called occupation; if so, how did Rama himself rule below seif 23 like Raavad that saving wine is called occupation and is like acquiring a document for two — see there in Taz. However my teacher may his light shine answered to resolve: one may say Rama holds that Raavad also holds one may not compare occupations to each other; and that he did not distinguish thus and gave another answer between "there was a Jew there," etc., is not because he compared them to other occupations, but this itself is like the case of the barrel that burst, that this non-Jew came and embraced it and it is permitted to sell — namely because he is occupied in rescue; likewise in the case of the wine barrel whose spigot was removed — and the matter does not depend on comparison; but regarding other occupations Raavad properly holds one may not compare them to each other — examine carefully.
