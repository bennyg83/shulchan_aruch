It's like a broken oil. In any case, an epich is permitted in its drinking as a buffer for a writer as a “D:”:
And the Tur wrote about this law: "and if [the sloshed wine] mixed into another [batch], the payment for the sloshed portion is forbidden for benefit and the remainder is permitted for benefit" — and this is difficult: it is obvious — is it any worse than all stam yeinam that mixed into something, as stated below in siman 134? Granted, in the Talmud there is no difficulty since the incident occurred like that — but regarding the Tur it is difficult, what need is there for this? And one might say: lest you think that since [the gentile] sloshed [the wine], it is like definite yayin nesech and has no remedy — [the Tur] therefore teaches us that it is only stam yeinam. There is also a superfluous phrase: it says "and he sloshed it with his hand" — what need is there for "with his hand"? And one might say: it thereby teaches us, by hinting with "his hand" — meaning, this is not [real] wine, rhetorically. He thought it was oil, etc. Hence according to us [Ashkenazim] it is permitted even for drinking, as is explained below in seif 24.
