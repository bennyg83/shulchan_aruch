A non-Jew who entered, etc. This is Rambam's wording, only the author added the word "and he shook it" following the approach Kesef Mishnah wrote challenging Raavad: and this incident of a non-Jew who entered a shop and said "is there wine to buy," etc., and he put his hand on the leather bottle and shook it and said "this is not wine" and they forbade it in benefit because of the stirring ; end; and Kesef Mishnah explained the essence of the challenge is that Rambam did not write "and he shook it" but simply "and touched," while in the Gemara it says "and he shook it"; and Darkhei Moshe resolved on this that Rambam did not need to write "and he shook it" since he already wrote in that chapter that touch alone does not forbid except stirring ; end; and it is astonishing, for if so he should have brought only in this language the incident of a non-Jew who stirred; and further Raavad already wrote above in that chapter that we require stirring; and it appears Raavad comes here on two matters: first to challenge what Rambam wrote "he extended his hand while searching and touched wine" ; he forbids it, for he intended for wine; and there is a difficulty: how do we know that at the time of that touch he intended touch ; perhaps the touch itself was unintentional as a person searches; and if you say he intended touch ; perhaps he thought it was water ; end; he brings the Gemara that said "this is not wine" ; through this it is known he intended touch in wine. Further Raavad comes here as proof for what he challenged at the beginning of the chapter regarding what Rambam wrote how is touch when he touched wine itself and stirred; but if he extended his hand to a barrel and they seized his hand before he removed it and did not shake it, etc. ; the wine is not forbidden ; it implies not forbidden at all; perforce all who did not stir but mere touch is permitted even for drinking; but if he stirred forbidden in benefit ; on this Raavad disagrees above and said: when they seized his hand did he not touch wine? Rather they said thus: they seized his hand so he would not stir ; for it does not forbid in benefit unless he stirs ; end; perforce Raavad has prohibition of drinking even in mere touch without stirring; and Kesef Mishnah explains thus to resolve both Rambam's words; and Rashi also explains thus in perek R' Yishmael (Avodah Zarah daf 59); and perforce Raavad comes here to bring proof for this, that there is prohibition of drinking even without stirring ; for behold in this incident where he was stirring they said of it forbidden in benefit because of stirring ; it implies without stirring there is at least prohibition of drinking; and this is proven there in that discussion where they said stirring makes yayin nesech ; it implies stirring causes yayin nesech which is prohibition of benefit; without stirring there is prohibition of drinking.
And the Tur wrote in this law: and if it mixed with other wine the value of what was stirred is forbidden in benefit and the rest permitted in benefit; and it is difficult ; obviously; who is worse than any stam yeinam that mixed, as below siman 134; granted in the Gemara it is difficult for us; but on the Tur it is difficult why need this; and one may say: since he stirred it is definite stam yeinam and has no remedy ; it informs us it was only stam yeinam. Further there is extra language that he wrote "and he shook it in his hand" ; this "in his hand" why? One may say it informs in this that he hinted with his hand ; meaning "this is not wine" in wonder.
He thought that it was oil, etc. Consequently, according to our practice it is permitted even for drinking, as explained below in seif 24.
