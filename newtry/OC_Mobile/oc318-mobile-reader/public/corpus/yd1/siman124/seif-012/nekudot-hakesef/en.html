(Ibid., seif katan 25) I was asked, etc. This matter requires study. Since the wine was flowing and the non-Jew stopped it with his hand, he certainly touched with his hand both the wine beneath his hand and the wine between the boards. This should be comparable to the case above, seif 14, where the spigot was removed and the non-Jew inserted his finger until he touched the wine, in which case all the wine is forbidden. The permission concerning a barrel split lengthwise applies to their earthenware barrels, where the wine was not flowing. Even if one argues that there was no direct contact because he touched only the wine flowing outside the crack, the wine inside should nevertheless be forbidden because of the connecting stream. Perhaps the case is one in which he did not touch the wine at all, in a manner that does not even create a connecting stream, but the plain description of the incident does not imply this. Still, in a case of financial loss in the present era, there may be grounds for permission based on the reason Rama gives in seif 24.
