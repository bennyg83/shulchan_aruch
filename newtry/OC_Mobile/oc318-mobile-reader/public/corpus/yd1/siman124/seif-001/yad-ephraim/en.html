No Jew was present there. See Baer Heitev and responsa Panim Me'iros, part 3, siman 69. A Jew hired non-Jews to remove large barrels from a cellar and place them upon wagons. They were skilled craftsmen who knew how to roll the barrels onto the wagons. During the rolling, one barrel overturned, its stopper fell from its mouth, and the wine began to pour out. A non-Jew placed his hand over the opening, and other non-Jews came to assist and lifted the barrel until its mouth faced upward. Jews stood there and witnessed everything. He ruled the wine permitted, comparing the case to that discussed by the Taz in siman 125. Here too, the Jews could not perform the work because they were not skilled in it. The non-Jewish workers feared that they would be required to pay for the damage, or at least lose their wages. The non-Jew therefore intended only to save the wine, not to offer it as a libation. For us, the wine is permitted even for drinking, and even outside a case of substantial loss. See there.
