(Siman 124, Shulchan Aruch, seif 14) A barrel whose stopper was removed, etc., but if it does not pass through the entire thickness of the bottom, etc. See Shach, seif katan 34, who challenges the Mechaber from below at the beginning of siman 155, where the Mechaber rules that wine is prohibited through nitzok caused by the force of a non-Jew. If so, the same should apply here; see there in the Shach. My master, my father, said that the Mechaber can be defended by distinguishing this case from the case below. There, what flows out becomes forbidden because it is directly the non-Jew's force, for without his force nothing would have flowed out, namely, he lifted the vessel. Thus, the entire extraction of the wine came about through the non-Jew's force, and for that reason the wine remaining inside is also forbidden because of nitzok. Here, however, he does not cause the outflow itself; he merely removes the stopper and the wine then flows out on its own. This is more lenient than the case of his force below in siman 125, and therefore all the wine does not become forbidden because of nitzok. This is why the Mechaber was precise here in his wording, saying that it is "like his force." This is clear.
