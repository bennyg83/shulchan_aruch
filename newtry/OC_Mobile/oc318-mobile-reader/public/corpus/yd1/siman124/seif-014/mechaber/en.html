A barrel from which the spigot was removed, and a non-Jew inserted his finger into it until he touched the wine — all of it is forbidden. And likewise if he removed the spigot that was stuck in the hole and it was reaching until the wine, for it is impossible that he did not agitate. {Rama: This is specifically when he knew that the spigot passes through the full thickness of the bottom; but if he did not know, this is touch of a non-Jew unintentionally by means of another item, which is permitted even for drinking (Mordechai, perek R. Yishmael; Hagahot Maimoniot, chapter 12; and it is simple, as below seif 24, as will be explained).} But if it does not pass through the full thickness of the bottom, in a way that it is impossible for him to agitate when removing it, it is like his ko’ach: what remains in the barrel is permitted even for drinking, and what came out is forbidden for drinking. {Rama: And if he removed it unintentionally, even what came out is permitted, for the ko’ach of a non-Jew unintentionally is permitted. And see below siman 125. If there was a long spigot inside the barrel and within that spigot there was a hole, and they insert a small spigot to plug its hole: if he removed only the small spigot, we judge it like one that does not pass through the thickness of the bottom (Beit Yosef in the name of Mordechai and Kol Bo).}
