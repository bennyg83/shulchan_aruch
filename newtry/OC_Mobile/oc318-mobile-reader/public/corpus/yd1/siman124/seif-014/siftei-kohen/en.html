Until he touched the wine — all of it is forbidden. — In benefit — Tur; and for us permitted in benefit in a place of loss.
But if he did not know. — That the spigot passes through all the rims, or that he did not know it was wine — permitted even for drinking, as below seif 24.
If he did not know, etc. From the words of Hagahot Maimoniyot, chapter 12, in the name of Semak, who wrote that even though that piece of wood touched the wine, since one can attribute that the non-Jew was not aware, it is permitted, end quote, and Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe bring it below in seif 22 regarding unintentional contact of a non-Jew through another object, it is explicit that wherever we do not know whether he knew or not, we attribute leniently in the matter of yayin nesech. So wrote R. Eliyahu Mizrachi in responsum siman 56, that where there is doubt whether he touched intentionally or not, we attribute leniently in the matter of yayin nesech, because it is a rabbinic decree, and in rabbinic matters we attribute leniently. So too it is implied by Rosh regarding a barrel whose tap was removed, where he wrote about Raavad's words: since one can attribute to permission, why should we forbid it out of doubt? See there. Similar attribution to leniency appears below in siman 125 seif 6. With this it is settled that the Rav wrote below in seif 24, regarding one who pierced with a knife, that we say he intended only to pierce the barrel and not to touch, since it is possible to pierce and not touch; examine carefully. And what remains in the barrel is permitted even for drinking. So wrote the Tur. This requires study, for the Tur follows his own view below at the beginning of siman 125, that if one poured wine from a utensil, what remains inside is permitted, for they were not stringent regarding the force of a non-Jew to forbid what is connected to it by nitzok. So wrote Mordechai in the name of Raavyah, and Beit Yosef cites it, that the reason here, when removing the tap, is that although what went outside is forbidden, nevertheless since it is not full libation, for he did not shake it, they were not stringent regarding nitzok, end quote. But according to the Mechaber's ruling at the beginning of siman 125 that nitzok is forbidden through the force of a non-Jew, the same should apply here. It appears this is what Rashba wrote in the name of Ramban regarding a tap, that all the wine is forbidden, because he holds as below in siman 125, unlike Beit Yosef who wrote that he forbade only the wine up to the hole because of his force. Nevertheless, for us everything, even what went outside, is permitted for drinking in a case of loss, as below in seif 24. Regarding if the tap was removed and the non-Jew stopped it up, it will be explained below in seif 23. Forbidden for drinking. Beit Yosef wrote that from Ramban's words cited by Rashba it implies that even if he later stopped the hole, the wine in the barrel up to the hole is forbidden, since through the force of the non-Jew it was uprooted to pour. But Ran wrote Ramban's words in Perek Rabbi Yishmael, and from his wording it implies that if he stopped the hole it is permitted, end quote.
