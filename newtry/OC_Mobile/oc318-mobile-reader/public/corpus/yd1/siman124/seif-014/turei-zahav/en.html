Mahari Chai wrote: even though inserting his finger was to save wine, nevertheless we are concerned lest after inserting his finger and the wine being saved he will again shake it to pour a libation to avodah zarah ; end; and he was not precise in this, for certainly we are not concerned for this at all according to Rashba whom Rama brings in seif 23 ; if so there too we should say that initially when he placed his hand on the hole his intent was to save wine and afterward intended to pour a libation like other non-Jews who forbid in preventing wine from coming out; rather certainly all the time there is rescue of wine on his hand there is no concern of libation in it; and likewise inserting his finger in the barrel's hole to save wine; rather he establishes that incident in the Gemara where a non-Jew rested his hand upon it and they forbade it ; there was a Jew to save it and the non-Jew preceded and saved it, then he is not called occupied; and this seif is words of Sefer HaTerumah following that Gemara incident mentioned in seif 23 where there was a Jew; but if there was no Jew certainly it is called occupied all the time his finger is inserted for that purpose; and Shulchan Aruch did not mention here anything of this whether there was a Jew, for he follows his reason who holds like the Rosh that saving wine is not called occupation; and Rama did not write here anything since he relied on what he will write in seif 23 to distinguish in this.
And what remains in the barrel is permitted. This means that the hole was sealed. Even if it was not sealed and more wine would have continued flowing from the barrel to the outside, it is nevertheless permitted, for the only prohibition here is due to his force, since the wine went outside through his force, and this forbids it only for drinking. For this reason as well, we do not say that the wine inside should be forbidden because of the connection of the stream, since here the prohibition itself is not a complete prohibition. However, if the non-Jew inserted the spigot there, even though it does not pass through the entire thickness of the barrel, nevertheless the wine inside is also forbidden, namely whatever would be drawn after that opening. Here there is a prohibition because he prevents the wine from flowing out, and this is a complete prohibition even for drinking. This law is mentioned in seif 23.
