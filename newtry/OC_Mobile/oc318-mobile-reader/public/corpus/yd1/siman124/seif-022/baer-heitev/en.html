For benefit. The Taz wrote: but drinking is forbidden, for this is contact of a non-Jew through another object. If you ask, then when he touches the outside of a vessel it should be forbidden for this reason, that is not difficult. When the vessel is whole, “touching” does not apply, for contact through another object means that the one touching brings another object to the wine at the time of contact. But with a whole vessel, the vessel already had wine inside it, and this is no contact with the wine. Here, however, where the barrel cracked, it is as though it separated from the wine; therefore drinking is forbidden, but benefit is permitted, because he intends to save the wine. This speaks where there is no Jew present to save it, unlike Levush who wrote that here, even if a Jew is present, benefit is permitted; he was not precise at all.
And embraced it. The Ran's language is that it is permitted to tighten it through a non-Jew, and the Beit Yosef and Darkhei Moshe cite him.
