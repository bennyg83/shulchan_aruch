A barrel that cracked lengthwise, and a non-Jew preceded and embraced it so that the shards would not separate — this is permitted in benefit. But if it cracked widthwise and he put his hand on it and pressed on it until he glued it and prevented the wine from going out, it is permitted for drinking, because he is not touching and pushing the wine of the barrel; rather, he is doing only an act of “brick”, for it is like a brick placed on the mouth of the barrel and weighing it down. And regarding a wooden barrel that has hoops (called tzirka'lish in the vernacular), even when it cracked lengthwise and the non-Jew embraced it, it is permitted for drinking (Ran in the name of Tosafot; and it is in our Tosafot; and in Hagahot Maimoniot in the name of Rashbam; and Mordechai in the name of Rashi).
