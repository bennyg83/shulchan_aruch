Nevertheless, here he descends one level. It is evident that he holds that the rule concerning a non-Jew who does not worship idolatry, whose contact renders wine forbidden for drinking but permitted for benefit, is accepted by all authorities. Even the Rosh agrees that such a person is inferior to a resident alien. This also appears from Eruvin, where the matter is presented without dispute. Seemingly, the distinction is that a resident alien formally accepted the seven Noahide commandments. According to this, however, it is surprising that the Gemara asks, "But was it not taught: Who is a resident alien?" and is forced to explain that the definition concerns the obligation to sustain him. Why did it not answer simply that the definition determines whether wine touched by him remains permitted for drinking? The baraita itself discusses this matter, for it teaches that one may leave wine alone with him. The reason no concern is entertained regarding his contact is that wine touched by him remains permitted for drinking, as the Rosh writes. This requires study.
