(Siman 124 in Taz seif kaf 2) And Rashal wrote that Karaites have the law of a ger toshav and it is permitted to drink with them wine of Israel. So it should read; and in my books I expanded on the law of Karaites, and here I will write briefly the view of Maharshal and Beit Chadash that it is permitted; and it appears from their words that they are better than a ger toshav; and likewise in Orach Chayyim in the name of Rambam's responsum, and Beit David brings it. But in responsum Mahari Beit part 2 siman 38 was copied a responsum of Rabbi Shimshon: and these Karaites who desecrate the festivals are as though they desecrate Sabbaths and as though they deny the entire Torah, and their wine is yayin nesech like idolaters — end of his words; it appears at first glance that they forbid wine through their touch like idolaters; and likewise it appears at first glance in Rashba's responsum that Beit Yosef brought in siman 119; and likewise Beit Yosef's view in Beit David siman 128 that their touch is forbidden and stam yeinam even if they swore they did not touch are not believed — see there; and likewise in responsum Rabbi Betzalel siman 3 he greatly expanded on laws of Karaites and wrote they are truly like complete idolaters, and it appears from his words there that they also forbid wine through their touch; and he wrote there that Rambam in a responsum deals specifically with Karaites in his time, unlike Karaites in our time who corrupted their deeds — see there. However it appears one should forbid stam yeinam only for drinking, but in benefit for everyone permitted, since they do not worship avodah zarah they are no worse than a ger toshav.
(Hagahah there seif kaf 15) And many disagree, etc. But according to what appears to me in my humble opinion to be lenient, etc., he brings proof from Rashba in our text in Beit Yosef — nothing is mentioned of lifting; rather he wrote "but if he carries or shakes it" and did not mention lifting; and one may say regarding lifting Rama's law is correct, and even for Taz's text in Terumat HaDeshen one may say the phrase "and shakes it" refers to carrying and not to lifting; but regarding lifting and shaking one may say Rashba agrees with Rama's law. And carrying on his shoulder is not called lifting, and behold it is like placed on the ground; and know that Rambam's law is from what we say in the Gemara regarding Zika — whether full or lacking it is permitted, etc.; see Beit Yosef, and from this Rambam learned that even without lifting; and if what he carries on his shoulder is called lifting, then what non-Jews carry would be called lifting, and whence does Rambam learn that stirring without lifting is forbidden — perhaps there it is different because non-Jews carry and it is lifting; rather certainly as we said — examine carefully.

(There) And I have a place to be astonished, etc.; and if so why did the Tur write, etc.? It is difficult, for the Tur in his father's approach the Rosh stated it; for behold at end of perek R' Yishmael the Rosh wrote in the name of Rabbenu Tam himself, and Beit Yosef brought it beginning siman 125; and behold Rabbenu Tam holds his touch through something else unintentionally is permitted even for drinking, as found in Asheri and all the poskim; and it is found in Asheri there explicitly several times that touch through something else with intention is forbidden in benefit; and also in the case of the non-Jew who climbed a palm tree and descended and touched the top of the lulav the Rosh wrote: why does Rashi hold touch of a non-Jew through something else unintentionally is forbidden for drinking — and he challenged Rabbenu Tam, etc.; and he challenged from below that Ravina challenged Rav Ashi: he took a barrel and threw it in his anger into a pit — this was an incident in Beit Shean and they permitted it in his anger, not when not in anger — there he goes back and forth; meaning the non-Jew touched until it fell into the pit; and when not in anger we are concerned lest he touched the wine and poured a libation; but in his anger we are not concerned lest he touched, even though he touches wine through the barrel — it is unintentional touch of a non-Jew through something else and permitted for drinking. Or alternatively when not in anger it is intentional touch of a non-Jew through something else and forbidden in benefit, for the rabbis did not permit measuring with a reed except because he intended for measuring; but if he did not intend for measuring it is forbidden to extend his hand in it, etc.; therefore it appears to Rabbenu Tam that Rabbenu Tam's text does not read "top of the lulav" but "he touched the wine" — it is touch of a non-Jew in his body unintentionally; but touch through something else unintentionally is permitted even for drinking — end of his words; behold explicitly that although for Rabbenu Tam touch through something else unintentionally is permitted even for drinking, nevertheless with intention it is forbidden even in benefit; therefore intentional is worse than force; and likewise it appears from other poskim; and Ran himself also appears that one opinion holds touch through something else with intention is worse than force — see there.
(Ibid. s.k. 31) He went at length to prove that through preoccupation one does not descend a level, and all his proofs amount to nothing, for in our time since all non-Jews are not actual idolaters, it is different from other non-Jews who are not actual idolaters; and with this all his words are refuted — and analyze.
(There seif kaf 32) He properly ruled to permit in great loss, but not for his reason; rather as I challenged in my books on Maharil who concluded there in his responsum: and because of stream there is no prohibition — for that case of the spigot all the more here where there is great loss — end of his words; for in the spigot case itself it appears explicitly from Tosafot perek Gid HaNasheh (Chullin daf 96) end of side 1 that according to the one who holds stream is connection it is forbidden; and likewise it appears from what Ran wrote in the name of Raavad and Beit Yosef brought it beginning siman 126, that the reason in the spigot case is no concern of stream because what comes through the Jew's spigot does not come through the non-Jew's spigot — it implies in a spigot where everything is through the spigot there is concern of stream; and likewise it appears explicitly in Asheri itself: nevertheless forbidden because of stream, for he wrote: and one may not prove from the case of piercing with a spigot that is taught "because a returning drop of wine forbids in any amount" — and if stream were connection why need a drop of wine, since he pierced with a spigot and drips into wine until he touched with his mouth, all the wine is forbidden through stream; one may say this conclusion refers only to the taste from the cup and he returned it to the barrel — end of his words; behold according to the one who holds stream is connection all the wine is forbidden through stream and nullification does not apply; and accordingly one may say Ran's view is likewise; and that which he wrote it is like touching all of it means according to what we establish stream is connection; and Tosafot perek R' Yishmael and Rashba wrote thus for the plain meaning of the baraita that even according to the one who holds stream is not connection the wine is forbidden through the returning drop; and I further found clear proof for this from what is found in last perek of Avodah Zarah end of daf 72: a man carried wine in a leather bottle and a non-Jew came and touched the leather bottle — they forbade all the wine; Rav Papa said to him: through what? Through stream — learn from this stream is connection. Here even without that it is forbidden; and likewise in a spigot; and accordingly one may not permit dripping through a spigot except in great loss, for we establish in such a case stream is not connection, as below beginning siman 126.
