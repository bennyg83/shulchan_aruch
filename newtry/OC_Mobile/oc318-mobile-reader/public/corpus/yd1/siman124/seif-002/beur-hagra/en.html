A resident alien, etc. Avodah Zarah 64b.
Meaning, etc. There, in accordance with the Sages. However, Torat HaBayit, Gate 1, writes that the dispute between Rabbi Meir and the Sages concerns the duty to sustain him, as stated there on 65a, "Rav Yosef said to him: but was it not taught," and so forth. With respect to permitting benefit from his wine, however, anyone who does not worship idolatry is permitted, even if he did not formally accept before three judges not to worship idolatry, as there, where it states that he sent a sacrifice and said, "I know," and so forth. The same applies to their wine, whose prohibition is because of idolatry, as stated there on 29b; it is prohibited only for drinking because of intermarriage. See Tosafot there, s.v. "wine," and there 57b at the end of s.v. "to exclude," where they write, "one may say that what," and so forth. The Rambam himself likewise writes there: "and likewise any non-Jew who does not worship idolatry, such as these Ishmaelites," and so forth, as I wrote above in seif 6. See Kesef Mishneh and Beit Yosef there, who write that he mentioned a resident alien as a greater novelty, that even so his wine is prohibited for drinking.
Likewise, a convert who circumcised, etc. For he is not circumcised until he accepts all the mitzvot, as stated in Yevamot 47, and he is superior in this respect to a resident alien. So write Torat HaBayit and Tosafot on 64b, s.v. "one may not," except that Rabbeinu Yitzchak there permits even the touch of a resident alien for drinking, according to the explanation there of "one may leave wine in his possession," and all the more so a convert who circumcised but did not immerse. Only his ordinary wine is prohibited for drinking, as stated in the gloss, "some are lenient," and so writes the Rosh there. Rashi there, however, s.v. "but one may leave," writes "he did not touch," implying that his touch does prohibit; the Rashba and Ran rule likewise. Even Tosafot there, at the end of the aforementioned passage, write that nevertheless he did not wish to permit it, and so forth.
All this, etc. Tosafot in the aforementioned passage, from the incident in which they roasted it, and so forth. See below, siman 268.
But, etc. The Gemara there states that his wine is like his oil, as explained above.
