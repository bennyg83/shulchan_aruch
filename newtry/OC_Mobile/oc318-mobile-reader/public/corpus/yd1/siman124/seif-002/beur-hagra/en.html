A resident alien, and so forth. Avodah Zarah 64b.
Meaning, and so forth. This follows the Sages there. Torat HaBayit explains that the dispute of Rabbi Meir and the Sages concerns the duty to sustain him, but for benefit from his wine it is enough that he does not worship idolatry, even without a formal acceptance before three judges. The same follows from the sacrificial gift sent by the non-Jew there and from the principle that the prohibition of non-Jewish wine derives from idolatry. Such wine is prohibited only for drinking because of intermarriage. Tosafot and the Rambam support this distinction, including the Rambam's ruling concerning Muslims. Beit Yosef says 'resident alien' is stated as a novelty, for even his wine remains forbidden for drinking.
Likewise, a convert who circumcised, and so forth. One does not circumcise a convert until he accepts all the mitzvot, Yevamot 47, so his status is stronger than that of a resident alien. Tosafot and Torat HaBayit discuss whether even his touch permits drinking. The Rosh is lenient, while Rashi, Rashba, and Rabbeinu Nissim prohibit wine he touched.
