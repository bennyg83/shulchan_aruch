For benefit. See Baer Heitev and responsa Panim Me'iros, part 2, siman 164. A Jew hired wagons to transport wine. Toward evening, the Jew walked ahead of the wagon drivers. A wagon carrying barrels overturned, and the driver placed his hand on the stopper so that the wine would not escape. As soon as the Jew arrived, he pushed the non-Jew away and allowed the wine to drip until others gathered and raised the barrels back onto the wagon, after which he replaced the stopper. During the lifting and movement, one board cracked and wine began to drip. A wagon driver brought a clod of moist earth resembling clay and smeared it over the crack. He ruled that, for us, the wine is permitted even for drinking in a case of minor loss, because the situation is treated as though no Jew were present to save it. See siman 164 in Shach, s.k. 64, citing the Rosh, who permits even where the non-Jew placed his hand directly upon the wine, and all the more so where he touched only the stopper. See also Shach, s.k. 68. In our case it is also possible that the non-Jew did not know that the stopper reached the wine. The wine is therefore permitted for us even where the loss is minor, and even for drinking. See there.
