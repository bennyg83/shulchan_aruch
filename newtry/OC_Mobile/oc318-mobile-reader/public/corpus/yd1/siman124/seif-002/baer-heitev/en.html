Commandments. Bach writes that even if he merely accepted not to worship idolatry, his touch is permitted for benefit. The seven commandments are mentioned to teach that even after accepting them, his touch still forbids drinking.
Properly. Shach explains that this means in the manner described below at the beginning of siman 268. Rama's statement that some are lenient even regarding touch means all the more so where a convert was circumcised and accepted all the commandments.
Forbidden. Meaning, for drinking. Bach writes that wine belonging to a ger toshav, or to a non-Jew who does not worship idolatry, is permitted for benefit only when it is known that a non-Jew did not touch it. Nowadays, however, it is permitted for benefit in any event in a case of loss, as explained at the beginning of siman 123. Maharshal writes that Karaites have the status of a ger toshav and may drink with a Jew. Nekudot HaKesef disagrees that their touch forbids drinking, and even an oath that they did not touch is not accepted, though their touch does not forbid benefit.
