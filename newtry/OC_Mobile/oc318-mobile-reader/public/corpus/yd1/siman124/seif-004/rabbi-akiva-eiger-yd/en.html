And it is as though he touched all of it. At first glance, the words of the Rashba and the Ran cited by the Taz in seif katan 8 imply that this is only because of the law of a connecting stream. This creates a difficulty for the Mechaber, who rules that all the wine is forbidden for benefit. According to the Mechaber's ruling in siman 126, seif 7, a connecting stream, even where the wine was touched by a non-Jew, is not treated as though he touched all of it. Rather, the stream is treated as though it became mixed into the wine, in accordance with the Rashba's view. Consequently, a connecting stream to ordinary non-Jewish wine should remain permitted for benefit, because even when such wine becomes mixed in, the mixture may be sold after deducting the value of the wine forbidden for benefit. The Rashba states this explicitly, as cited by the Beit Yosef there. Accordingly, here too the wine should be permitted for benefit. We would therefore have to say that the Mechaber considers this case more severe than an ordinary connecting stream, so that it is literally as though the non-Jew touched all of the wine. I subsequently saw that the Nekudot HaKesef, seif katan 33, states explicitly that when the Ran says it is as though he touched all of it, this is because of the law of a connecting stream. For this reason, in a case of substantial loss he rules leniently, since in such circumstances we maintain that a connecting stream is not deemed a connection. See there. The Shulchan Aruch's ruling that all the wine is forbidden for benefit therefore remains difficult, as explained above.
