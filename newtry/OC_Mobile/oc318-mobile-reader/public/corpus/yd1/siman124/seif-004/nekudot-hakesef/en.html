(Gloss, ibid., seif katan 15) And many disagree, etc. He writes that, in his humble opinion, the matter should be treated leniently, and he brings proof from the Rashba. Yet according to our text in Beit Yosef, the Rashba says nothing about lifting; he writes only of carrying the vessel or shaking it, without mentioning lifting. It may therefore be said that, in a case of actual lifting, Rama's ruling is correct. Even according to the Taz's version of Torat HaBayit, the phrase 'and shakes it' may refer to one who carries it, not to one who lifts it. Where he both lifts and shakes it, the Rashba may agree with Rama's ruling. Carrying a vessel on one's shoulder is not considered lifting for this purpose; it is regarded as though the vessel remained resting on the ground. This is evident from the Rambam's ruling, which is derived from the Gemara's statement that a wineskin is permitted whether full or partially empty; see Beit Yosef. From there the Rambam learned that shaking is forbidden even without lifting. If carrying it on one's shoulder were considered lifting, then the non-Jews' carrying of the wineskin in that case would itself be lifting. How, then, could the Rambam derive that shaking without lifting is forbidden? Perhaps that case was different because the non-Jew carried it and thereby lifted it. Rather, the explanation is necessarily as stated, and this is clear.
