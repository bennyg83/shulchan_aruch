If a non-Jew measured the pit that has wine in it with a reed; or if he was tapping on the mouth of boiling barrels in order that the boiling should settle; or if he took a barrel and threw it in his anger into the pit — it is permitted in benefit and forbidden for drinking. {Rama: For in all these there is proof that he did not intend to pour a libation, but only for his work, and it is the touch of a non-Jew unintentionally, which forbids only for drinking. And there are those who say that even if he measured it with his hand, it does not forbid in benefit (Rosh, Ran, Rashba, Tur, Raavad, and Rabbeinu Yerucham), since he is occupied with his work; and one may be lenient in a place of loss. A non-Jew who trod wine: if they guarded it and saw that he did not touch with his hand, the wine is not forbidden in benefit, only for drinking, since he is also occupied with his work (Tur); and see above siman 123. And likewise, if he inserted his hand into a barrel and does not know that there is wine there, it does not forbid in benefit (ibid.). And even if it became known to him that it is wine: if he removed his hand immediately when it became known to him, it is permitted in benefit, since when he inserted it he did not know it was wine (Tur in the name of Raavad and Rosh and Rabbeinu Yerucham). And we do not compare one matter to another in these cases; you only have what the Sages said (Tur, Rosh, Raavad, and Rashba).}
