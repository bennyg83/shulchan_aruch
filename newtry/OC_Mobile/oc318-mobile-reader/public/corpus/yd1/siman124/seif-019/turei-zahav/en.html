Or if he took a barrel and threw it in his anger. Likewise Rambam — this is very astonishing, for in the mishnah it is taught explicitly in this language "this was an incident and they permitted it" — it implies entirely; and likewise Rashi and all poskim explicitly that it is permitted even for drinking, as the Tur and Shulchan Aruch wrote siman 125; and behold in Hagahot Maimoniyot he wrote from this that there is a copying error in Rambam and it must read he threw it not in his anger; and Shulchan Aruch's view who copied Rambam's words as they are — I do not know how to resolve them; also Raavad wrote on these Rambam's words they are corruption — if in his anger permitted even for drinking; and Levush properly did not copy this law of throwing in anger here; and see siman 125 seif 5.
A non-Jew who treads wine if Israel guarded, etc. In the Tur it is written in this language; and likewise if he treads wine with his feet permitted in benefit since he is occupied treading through something else that Israel guarded he did not touch wine; but if Israel did not guard forbidden in benefit since certainly he touched, for affection of libation is upon him; and Rashba wrote that even if he touched with his feet wine with intention of touch permitted in benefit, for there is no way of libation in that; and impossible — the Rosh forbade it in benefit — end of his words; and in Asheri he did not write this "something else," etc.; and perforce Beit Yosef's view that even if he engaged with his hand in grapes at the time of treading to turn grapes and stop them — permitted in benefit since occupied in his work; and not like the Tur's view who forbids in this by hand; and in my humble opinion the Tur also holds thus, for since treading is considered occupation — what difference foot and what difference hand, all that is the way of treading and its needs, namely to gather grapes; and the Tur did not require guarding except lest he touch not because of his work — for it is possible it is a way of libation by foot even though there is also libation according to the Rosh's view; nevertheless since the foot is always occupied in treading there is no concern in it; but the hand has no need for treading except occasionally to gather grapes — therefore there is concern lest he touch with his hand at a time when there is no occupation in it while he is still treading; and all the more if he bound his hand — for the Rosh holds we do not decree bound against unbound, as the Rosh wrote beginning perek R' Yishmael; and that the Rosh did not write guarding is needed because of hand — there is no need to write it, for since he informs us that even by foot there is prohibition if he does it intentionally — all the more by hand where there is no need, certainly guarding is needed; on this as the law in every place where there is prohibition. And that they challenged the child "behold he pours libation by hand" and needed to say he bound her — for there it comes to teach that even for the latter mishnah there is permission to tread with the non-Jew; and on this they challenged "behold for the latter mishnah he would pour libation from when he began to draw" and needed to answer he bound her hand — namely guarding that we mentioned; nevertheless it is difficult for me why the Tur wrote in siman 123 "and even if he did not touch it but treaded it" one should forbid what is drawn by his force to the pit — behold it is force unintentionally since he is occupied in treading; and end of siman 125 explicit that force of a non-Jew unintentionally is permitted even for drinking — namely he does not know wine comes from the leather bottle; and likewise like it, since he is occupied like in touch there is no distinction between occupied and not knowing it is wine; and it appears to resolve: specifically regarding prohibition that depends on touch — namely that forbids even in benefit because of affection of libation — there is permission if he is occupied in some occupation such as treading or measuring wine even by hand according to the Rosh — then he does not pour libation; but regarding force where there is no way of libation in that but nevertheless the Sages forbade all that comes from his force — if so even if he is occupied in his work nevertheless the essence of his work is to draw wine and this itself the Sages forbade regarding a non-Jew who acts to draw wine from his force; and they did not permit in end of siman 125 force unintentionally except that he does not know he will draw wine at all; and likewise if he does not know it is wine.
One may not compare matter to matter, etc. It is difficult for me — this is from the Rosh's words who challenged the incident of Rav Papa where the spigot was removed and a non-Jew rested his hand on the hole — why forbidden in benefit, he is occupied saving wine and it is like measuring by hand; and he answered in the name of Raavad: it deals when there were Jews and the non-Jew preceded and placed his hand there — there are others to save and therefore not called occupied; and for libation he did so; and on this the Rosh disagrees: say he did it to find favor; and the original difficulty does not exist, for we have no reason to compare occupations to each other except what we find in the Talmud called occupation — for the agradim who tastes wine was fit to be called occupation like measuring by hand, and nevertheless not called occupied and forbidden in benefit; and likewise saving wine is not called occupation — end. And one may say the Rosh holds the reason there is because he did not stir, as Rashi explained whom Beit Yosef brought nearby; and even though Beit Yosef concludes on Rashi's words — namely it is touch through something else since he intended to save wine — end; in truth Beit Yosef's words here are not in precision, for since Rashi explained he did not stir — permitted in benefit even with intentional touch by his hand; and one may say on Rashi himself: why did he need to explain the reason because of stirring — why not explain it was occupation and touch through something else? One may say he holds like the Rosh that saving wine is not called occupation; and behold Rama ruled in seif 23 like Raavad that saving wine is not called occupation — and how did he write here "you have only what the Sages said," behold we add to them; and what is this addition from another addition. I again saw in Rivash siman 310 in the name of Rashba also like these words that one may not add to the Sages' words; and he himself holds saving wine is occupation; and one may say for Rashba and Raavad who hold that in truth there is this occupation of saving wine in the Gemara — why did they permit in a barrel cracked lengthwise; and not like Rashi who permitted because he did not stir, rather he holds perhaps there was stirring; and likewise in Terumat HaAfelah there was recognizable stirring through the halves.
