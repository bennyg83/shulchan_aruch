And he is believed to say, etc. Beit Yosef wrote: it is written in Shibbolei HaLeket — an apostate who touched wine and afterward said he converted between himself and himself, and R' Yitzchak permitted the wine because he deposited his purse on Shabbat; and Rashba forbade it because one may say he is deceiving the amora'im — end; and in Orach Chayyim he wrote: an Israelite apostate to avodah zarah — his wine is yayin nesech until he repents, and he is believed to say he converted — end of his words. And it is difficult at first glance why the author ruled like Orach Chayyim, for R' Yitzchak and Rashba disagree with him; and it is possible he understood they deal only where he touched wine and afterward said he converted; but if before that he said he converted he does not make yayin nesech — strained; and more likely to distinguish: where it is known he was an apostate — in such a case Rashba deals and he is not believed to say he converted; but where it is not known he was an apostate, even though it is also not known he was an Israelite, he is believed to say he repented in a miggo that if he wanted he could say he never apostatized and is an Israelite; and in such a case Orach Chayyim and the author deal; and likewise the poskim; and Taz below siman 268 seif 10 regarding a convert who said he converted — he is not believed except specifically when we did not know he was an idolater, for then he is believed in a miggo that if he wanted he could say he is an Israelite.
