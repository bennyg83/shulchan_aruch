He does not make yayin nesech, etc. — For libation does not depend on circumcision, only on being an idolater; and this one who extended his foreskin — his heart is for Heaven — there.
And he is believed to say, etc. This is the language of Beit Yosef: it is written in Shibbolei HaLeket that a mumar touched wine and afterward said that he converted back privately; R. Yitzchak permitted the wine because he had entrusted his purse on Shabbat, and Rashba forbade it, because one can say he is acting deceitfully, end quote. In Orchot Chaim he wrote: a Jewish mumar to avodah zarah, his wine is yayin nesech until he repents, and he is believed to say, "I converted back," end quote. At first glance it is difficult: why did the Mechaber rule like Orchot Chaim, since R. Yitzchak and Rashba disagree with him? Perhaps it seemed to him that they spoke only where he touched the wine and afterward said he had converted back; but if he said beforehand that he had converted back, he does not render the wine yayin nesech. This is forced. It appears better to distinguish: where it is known that he was a mumar, and this is the case in Rashba, he is not believed to say he converted back. But where it is not known that he was a mumar, even though it is also not known that he was Jewish, he is believed to say, "I repented," with a migo that had he wished he could have said, "I never apostatized and I am Jewish." This is the case in Orchot Chaim and the Mechaber. So too the poskim, Tur and Shulchan Aruch, wrote below in siman 268 seif 10 regarding a convert who says, "I converted": he is believed only when we did not know he was a non-Jew, for then he is believed with a migo that had he wished he could have said, "I am Jewish."
