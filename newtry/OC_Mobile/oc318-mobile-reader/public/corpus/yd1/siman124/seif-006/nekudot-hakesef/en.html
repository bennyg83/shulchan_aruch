(Ibid.) This is astonishing. In my humble opinion, the Rambam wrote this ruling in accordance with the position of his teacher, the Rif. The Rambam found it difficult why the Rif did not rule in accordance with Rav Ashi. He therefore understood that the Rif explains the Mishnah's phrase 'and they permitted it' as permitting benefit, even when the non-Jew acted in anger. The Rif maintains that the reason for the permission is the non-Jew's preoccupation, not the fact that the act was one of throwing. Since the non-Jew touches the wine, what difference is there whether he does so by throwing or by direct contact? If he does not act in anger and there is no preoccupation, the wine is forbidden even for benefit. Why, then, did the Gemara challenge Rav Ashi only from a case in which the act was not done in anger, rather than from the case done in anger? This is no difficulty, because Rav Ashi could have answered that the Mishnah means that they permitted the wine even for drinking. The Gemara therefore had to challenge him from the case not done in anger. This appears to me correct. I later found that Kesef Mishneh explains that the Rambam follows the Rif, his teacher, and that the halachah does not follow Rav Ashi, although he does not fully explain the baraita. Perhaps he understands it as I have written; examine carefully.
