For benefit. Shach writes that nowadays, if he inserted his finger, it is permitted for benefit in a case of loss; if he inserted a long spigot, it is permitted even for drinking in a case of loss, as below in seif 24. If the wine cannot be saved without inserting a finger and no Jew is present to save it, it is permitted even for drinking in a case of loss. Bach ruled this way in practice.
Flows out. Shach writes that if the siphon does not reach the bottom of the barrel, only the wine up to the level it reaches is forbidden for benefit; below that point it is permitted, like the spigot discussed nearby. Nowadays the entire barrel is permitted for benefit in a case of loss even if the siphon reaches the bottom. Taz writes that placing one's hand directly over the mouth of the siphon, upon the wine, is like touching all the wine in the barrel. If he merely lifted the siphon slightly and held it from outside to stop the flow, only the wine that exited is forbidden, like wine poured by a non-Jew, and nowadays it is nullified in the barrel.
The other. Beit Yosef explains that the case is where a reed is inserted in the barrel's hole, so a distinction can be made between the stream in the channel and the remaining wine. Without such a reed, all the wine is forbidden because of the connecting stream. See siman 126:2.
