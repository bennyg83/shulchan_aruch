A barrel that had a hole in its side and the stopper slipped out from the hole, and the non-Jew placed his hand on the place of the hole so that the wine would not go out: all the wine from the top of the barrel until the hole is forbidden in benefit; and from the hole and below it is permitted in benefit and forbidden for drinking. {Rama: And he should sell all of it except for the value of the wine that is forbidden in benefit (Beit Yosef in the name of Ran, and as explained). And this is specifically when a Jew was there to save the wine and a non-Jew came and saved it; but if there was no Jew here to save it and a non-Jew came to save it, it is no worse than measuring it with his hand, since he is occupied with his work, and everything is permitted in benefit (Beit Yosef in the name of Rashba and Raavad). And all this is stated only where he placed his hand over the hole, or he stuck a spigot there and it does not pass through the bottom; but if he inserted his finger inside, or a long spigot, it has already been explained that all of it is forbidden in benefit. And if it is in a manner that if the non-Jew would not have placed his hand on the hole, all the wine would have gone out — such as where they make a bent spout whose one end is placed into the wine in the barrel and the other end outside the barrel, and they suck the wine and thereby all the wine goes out — if the non-Jew placed his finger on the mouth of the spout and prevented the wine from descending, all the wine in the barrel becomes forbidden in benefit, for all would have gone out and been drawn had it not been for his hand, and it is found that everything is as coming by his ko’ach. And if the non-Jew did not touch except the stream of the wine that is standing in the gutter, it is forbidden because of nitzok; but all the other wine that is inside the jug is nitzok bar nitzok and is permitted even for drinking.}
