(There seif kaf 33) All his words are incorrect; know that what he wrote "but if the spigot was inserted passing all the rims and the non-Jew drew out the spigot, forbidden even for us," etc., is his own wording and is not in Mordechai; and in truth it is not found, for one who examines Mordechai there will see it is responsum of Rabbi Shmuel bar Baruch who wrote thus for practical law that even if he touched with a knife, since the thief did not intend except to pierce so wine would come out — it is force unintentionally and permitted as Rabbenu Tam's text reads, etc.; and this law of his touch through something else even with intention is permitted for us is written in Mordechai there in the name of Rabbi Chizkiya much later, and many responsa are found in Mordechai there similarly permitting on grounds of touch through something else unintentionally — and all of them are according to Talmudic law; and the practical difference is in a place where there are actual idolaters; but for us it is simple that even if the non-Jew drew out the spigot with intention it is permitted, for touch through something else even with intention is permitted for us. And what Rama wrote "since the non-Jew did not intend except to pierce," etc. — I already wrote in Shach seif kaf 53 that even so, even if he intended it is permitted for us — rather he stated the truth of the matter as found in Mordechai; and the practical difference is it is permitted even not in a place of loss, whereas we do not rely on the reason that non-Jews in our time are not idolaters except in a place of loss; and likewise he challenges what Rama wrote end of siman 129 regarding a maidservant who is not believed, etc. — it is difficult, for Rama concludes there end of siman 129 "however in our time when non-Jews do not pour libations, permitted," etc., and it refers to all he mentioned before, as Shach wrote there seif kaf 42 and 43 — unlike what he wrote there letter lamed 32. And what he wrote "and likewise it appears siman 129 seif 1" — certainly an error, and it must read "and likewise it appears siman 130 seif 1," namely as he wrote there letter aleph — and it is not as he wrote there. And what he wrote "and it appears the reason for prohibition even for us when the non-Jew drew through a spigot, that it is close he touched with his hand the spurting that comes out," etc. — it is astonishing, for even if he touched, behold he does not intend to touch and it is unintentional touch of a non-Jew that is permitted for us even if he touched with his hand; and what he wrote "and likewise I saw in Mordechai perek R' Yishmael," etc. — on the contrary, this Mordechai is his refutation, for he wrote: when he drew the spigot and returned it, impossible he did not touch with his hand the spurting that comes out, and it is unintentional touch of a non-Jew and forbidden — end of his words; behold he forbade only because it is touch with his hand unintentionally; and if so for us where unintentional touch is permitted it is simple it is permitted; and likewise what he wrote in Mordechai perek HaSocher regarding a wide hole — namely when he touches intentionally; and see further on this end of siman 129 and beginning siman 130.
