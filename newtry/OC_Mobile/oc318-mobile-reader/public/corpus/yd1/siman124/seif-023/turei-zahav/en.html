I was asked about a non-Jew who repaired a wine barrel to strengthen it and through striking the barrel a crack opened and wine was spurting through it and he sealed it with his hand; and an Israelite woman stood there with a lamp in her hand to illuminate for him to repair; and meanwhile her husband came and rebuked him and he sealed the crack — what is the law of this wine? Answer: even though at first glance one may say forbidden since there was an Israelite and he preceded and sealed; but after examination it appears permitted, for several times R' Yitzchak in the incident where the spigot was removed from the barrel and a non-Jew jumped and rested his hand upon it — there they forbade in benefit; and the Rosh wrote: why do we forbid in benefit, behold he was occupied saving wine; and Raavad answered there were Jews standing and if not that he intended libation why did he precede — not clear to me, say he did it to find favor, etc.; rather it appears this is not occupation, etc. — end; it implies wherever there is reasoning to be lenient and say he did not intend libation we are lenient; and therefore the Rosh challenged: according to his reasoning that saving wine is considered occupation he should have been lenient even when there are rescuers lest he did it to find favor; and Raavad did not hold this reasoning that for finding favor he nevertheless does not disagree with the Rosh that one may suspend for leniency wherever one can suspend and Raavad did not forbid when there are rescuers except because the non-Jew had no connection to the wine barrel — then there is proof he intended libation, for one who places it for a person who does kindness to save Israel's wine where he can be saved by others — and for this intent itself they said there in the Talmud in Rav Papa's incident that they forbade the wine — interpretation: the non-Jew jumped and sealed, for it is difficult why need to mention this; and even though the incident was so — nevertheless he should have said a non-Jew came and sealed it; and further he said doubled language "a non-Jew came jumped" — since he said came why need jumped; and great proof from what we find in the cracked barrel incident that is permitted — the Gemara said "jumped" and did not say "a non-Jew came jumped" as said regarding Rav Papa's incident which is for prohibition; rather it is clear this is the essence of the proof that the non-Jew intended libation and not because of rescue, for it was not at the barrel's place and uprooted from its place to seal the hole — which is against the reasoning; and likewise proven is the Tur's language here who wrote "and the non-Jew preceded and saved — certainly intended libation" — end; it implies precedence causes; but in the cracked barrel permitted incident it does not deal thus, rather the non-Jew was close to wine at the time of jumping; and all the more in some texts where "jumped" is not mentioned at all in that cracked barrel, etc.; and it was fit to learn from this that if the non-Jew stands at the barrel and the Israelite is somewhat distant — the non-Jew is called occupied; but it does not appear to be so lenient unless distant by measure that there is loss before the Israelite comes; and I again saw thus in the long Terumat HaDeshen daf 135: and here impossible to save quickly through Israel, etc. — end; nevertheless in my humble opinion it is clear if the non-Jew had kosher wine that Israel made and the barrel was pierced and the non-Jew sealed the hole with his hand even if there is a Jew there — he is panicked about his property and therefore hurries more to save his wine; and since there is no proof we suspend to be lenient — end; also in our case where the non-Jew engaged in repairing the barrel and through his actions it was damaged and a hole was made — and by their laws he is liable to pay and also by our laws as found perek HaBayit VeHaAliyah — the builder who demolished a wall and caused damage through his striking is liable to pay — we have no proof he intended libation, rather he was panicked lest he need pay the damage; therefore he preceded and saved and properly we call him occupied; and permitted for us since we establish nowadays touch of non-Jews unintentionally is permitted as Rama wrote in seif 24 — so in my humble opinion; however this matter is novel and was not explicitly mentioned in the poskim, but it is clear and has no doubt in my humble opinion — end.
