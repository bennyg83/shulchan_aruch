The meaning is: he inserted a hollow tube into the hole of the barrel, and the stream descends through the tube onto the wine in the barrel — this is nizokeik bar nizokeik [stream-of-a-stream-connection]; but if the stream descends from the barrel itself [without a tube], then what is in the barrel constitutes nizokeik [connection] but not bar-nizokeik — and that is forbidden, as Beit Yosef wrote at the end of siman 126 in the name of R' Yerucham. And this applies only when there is no great financial loss — as stated in siman 126. And Beit Yosef likewise explained these words that he cited in the name of Kol Bo; but I find a difficulty: according to this, regarding what Beit Yosef wrote on what the Tur wrote — "but if he only touched the stream coming out, the wine is permitted" — Beit Yosef offered [as one explanation] that it constitutes nizokeik bar nizokeik, meaning what is below the hole; if so, why was all the wine inside — even the upper portion — forbidden? Therefore the truth is certainly as Beit Yosef wrote in his first explanation: that the stream flows to waste — and therefore there is no complete nizokeik at all. And with this it is also resolved what the Tur wrote — "and Raavad forbids": for he [Raavad] holds that even when [the stream] flows to waste it constitutes nizokeik for what is above it, as the Tur wrote in his name in siman 126 regarding a Jew who pours [wine] into refuse, etc. — and this is not as Beit Yosef explained what the Tur wrote in Raavad's name as referring to a separate matter, namely that Raavad forbids what is below the hole — but rather as I explained: it is the same law itself.
