All the wine from the top of the barrel. For all the wine held back there because of the non-Jew, which would have flowed out had he not held it, is considered as though he touched that entire portion, since he prevents it from flowing out. The wine below the hole is forbidden for drinking because of its connection to the forbidden upper portion. This is more stringent than the connection of a stream, for how could the upper portion be forbidden while that which lies beneath it is permitted? So wrote the Mordechai and the other poskim.

For drinking it is a copying error and it must read in benefit, for it is no better than measuring in seif 19; and the reasoning that saving wine is called occupation is Raavad's view that I mentioned in seif 19, and Rashba agreed with him; and I saw in Levush who wrote: and specifically in such a case regarding a hole where there is no craft to place hand on the hole and the Jew can also do so; but above regarding a barrel that cracked where there is craft ; even if there is a Jew and the non-Jew preceded they did not forbid, etc. ; he was not precise at all and the error followed him because he saw Rama arranged this distinction here and not in seif 22; but in truth it refers to everything as Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Rashba to distinguish; and likewise regarding the barrel; and he did not even examine Beit Yosef; and he further wrote the non-Jew did so to find favor, etc. ; in this too he was not precise, for this reasoning is neither Raavad's nor the Rosh's as explained in the Rosh I will mention nearby in a responsum. I was asked about a non-Jew who repaired a wine barrel to strengthen it and through striking the barrel a crack opened and wine was spurting through it and he sealed it with his hand; and an Israelite woman stood there with a lamp in her hand to illuminate for him to repair; and meanwhile her husband came and rebuked him and he sealed the crack ; what is the law of this wine? Answer: even though at first glance one may say forbidden since there was an Israelite and he preceded and sealed; but after examination it appears permitted, for several times R' Yitzchak in the incident where the spigot was removed from the barrel and a non-Jew jumped and rested his hand upon it ; there they forbade in benefit; and the Rosh wrote: why do we forbid in benefit, behold he was occupied saving wine; and Raavad answered there were Jews standing and if not that he intended libation why did he precede ; not clear to me, say he did it to find favor, etc.; rather it appears this is not occupation, etc. ; end; it implies wherever there is reasoning to be lenient and say he did not intend libation we are lenient; and therefore the Rosh challenged: according to his reasoning that saving wine is considered occupation he should have been lenient even when there are rescuers lest he did it to find favor; and Raavad did not hold this reasoning that for finding favor he nevertheless does not disagree with the Rosh that one may suspend for leniency wherever one can suspend and Raavad did not forbid when there are rescuers except because the non-Jew had no connection to the wine barrel ; then there is proof he intended libation, for one who places it for a person who does kindness to save Israel's wine where he can be saved by others ; and for this intent itself they said there in the Talmud in Rav Papa's incident that they forbade the wine ; interpretation: the non-Jew jumped and sealed, for it is difficult why need to mention this; and even though the incident was so ; nevertheless he should have said a non-Jew came and sealed it; and further he said doubled language "a non-Jew came jumped" ; since he said came why need jumped; and great proof from what we find in the cracked barrel incident that is permitted ; the Gemara said "jumped" and did not say "a non-Jew came jumped" as said regarding Rav Papa's incident which is for prohibition; rather it is clear this is the essence of the proof that the non-Jew intended libation and not because of rescue, for it was not at the barrel's place and uprooted from its place to seal the hole ; which is against the reasoning; and likewise proven is the Tur's language here who wrote "and the non-Jew preceded and saved ; certainly intended libation" ; end; it implies precedence causes; but in the cracked barrel permitted incident it does not deal thus, rather the non-Jew was close to wine at the time of jumping; and all the more in some texts where "jumped" is not mentioned at all in that cracked barrel, etc.; and it was fit to learn from this that if the non-Jew stands at the barrel and the Israelite is somewhat distant ; the non-Jew is called occupied; but it does not appear to be so lenient unless distant by measure that there is loss before the Israelite comes; and I again saw thus in the long Terumat HaDeshen daf 135: and here impossible to save quickly through Israel, etc. ; end; nevertheless in my humble opinion it is clear if the non-Jew had kosher wine that Israel made and the barrel was pierced and the non-Jew sealed the hole with his hand even if there is a Jew there ; he is panicked about his property and therefore hurries more to save his wine; and since there is no proof we suspend to be lenient ; end; also in our case where the non-Jew engaged in repairing the barrel and through his actions it was damaged and a hole was made ; and by their laws he is liable to pay and also by our laws as found perek HaBayit VeHaAliyah ; the builder who demolished a wall and caused damage through his striking is liable to pay ; we have no proof he intended libation, rather he was panicked lest he need pay the damage; therefore he preceded and saved and properly we call him occupied; and permitted for us since we establish nowadays touch of non-Jews unintentionally is permitted as Rama wrote in seif 24 ; so in my humble opinion; however this matter is novel and was not explicitly mentioned in the poskim, but it is clear and has no doubt in my humble opinion ; end.
If the non-Jew placed his finger, etc. It appears that this applies specifically when he places his hand over the opening of the siphon directly upon the wine. He is then considered as though he touched all the wine in the barrel, since all of it would be drawn after this wine were it not for his holding it back. However, if he did not touch there, but merely raised the siphon slightly and held it from the outside, thereby preventing any more wine from flowing, as is commonly done when one wishes to stop the stream, then the only prohibition results from his having poured from the siphon into the barrel. This is governed by the law of a non-Jew who pours from one vessel into another, where the wine that goes outside becomes forbidden; according to our practice, that amount is nullified in the barrel.
For all of it would flow out and be drawn after it. This applies when the siphon reaches the bottom of the barrel, as Rashi explicitly explained, and not according to Rabbeinu Tam. Tosafos there agreed to this. In the Gemara, Rav Pappa sought to forbid all the wine because of the connection of the stream, even without this reasoning, and wished to prove from here that a stream constitutes a connection. Rava told him not to derive this, for the reason is that all the wine is drawn after it, and it is therefore considered as though he touched all of it. According to this, since we rule in siman 126 that a stream constitutes a forbidden connection even without this reason, a practical difference arises where the siphon reaches only halfway down the barrel. The lower portion is then forbidden only because of the connection of the stream. Consequently, in a case of substantial loss, the lower portion is permitted, as I wrote in siman 126, that in a case of substantial loss we do not apply the rule that a stream constitutes a connection. This is unlike a case where the siphon reaches the bottom, in which case it is forbidden even where there is substantial loss.
In the spurting wine standing in the gutter. Meaning a hollow reed inserted in the barrel's hole and spurting descends through the reed onto wine in the barrel ; it is stream capable of stream; but if spurting descends from the barrel itself ; what is in the barrel is stream and not capable of stream and forbidden as Beit Yosef wrote end of siman 126 in the name of Rabbeinu Yerucham ; namely if there is no great loss as written siman 126; and likewise Beit Yosef explained these words he brought in the name of Kol Bo; except it is difficult for me according to this on what Beit Yosef wrote challenging what the Tur wrote: but if he only touched the spurting that comes out ; the wine is permitted; Beit Yosef in one answer said it is stream capable of stream ; namely what is below the hole ; and if so why was all wine inside, namely even the upper, forbidden ; end; the truth is certainly as Beit Yosef wrote in the first answer that then the spurting goes to loss, therefore there is no stream at all; and with this what the Tur wrote and Raavad forbids is resolved, for he holds even though it goes to loss there is stream law for what is above it as the Tur wrote in his name siman 126 regarding Israel pouring to the refuse heap, etc.; and not like Beit Yosef who explained what the Tur wrote in the name of Raavad is from what he wrote to forbid what is below the hole ; it is a different matter; rather as I explained in this law itself likewise.
