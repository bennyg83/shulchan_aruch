Forbidden for benefit. See Baer Heitev and Panim Meirot, volume 2, responsum 164. A Jew hired wagons to transport wine. Toward evening he walked ahead of the wagoners. One wagon carrying barrels overturned, and the wagoner placed his hand over the stopper to prevent the wine from escaping. As soon as the Jew arrived, he pushed the non-Jew away and allowed the wine to drip until help gathered. They raised the barrels back onto the wagon and closed the stopper. During the lifting and movement, one stave cracked and wine dripped out. A wagoner brought damp earth like clay and smeared it over the crack. The ruling was that according to our practice, even with only a small loss, the wine is permitted for drinking, because this is treated as though no Jew were present to save it. See siman 124 in the Shach, seif katan 64, citing the Rosh, who permits even where the non-Jew placed his hand directly upon the wine, and all the more where he touched only the stopper. See also Shach, seif katan 68. In this case it is especially possible that the non-Jew did not know the stopper reached the wine, so according to our practice it is permitted even with a minor loss and even for drinking.
