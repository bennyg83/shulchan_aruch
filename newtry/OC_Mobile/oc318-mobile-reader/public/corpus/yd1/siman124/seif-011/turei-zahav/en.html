Beit Yosef wrote in the name of a responsum of Rashba that the type of vessel described is called in the Talmud "kani shakiz" — and I do not know what this is; in any case what is in its mouth is forbidden and all the wine flows after it — why should it be inferior to nizokeik-chibur [a connecting stream]? — end of quote. And in Mishmeret HaBayit, folio 134, Rashba also writes there that the form of the kani shakiz was not clarified to us, and therefore one cannot permit it on account of nizokeik; and in any case it does not accord with Rashi's explanation. At the end he wrote that the kani shakiz was arranged in a way that there was no nizokeik-chibur. And Ran wrote in the name of Raavad: to resolve [the matter] — we do not say nizokeik-chibur unless all [the wine] exits through the same opening; but here it exits through the other hole through which the gentile is drinking — that is different. And it seems to me to resolve [it] as follows: as long as [the wine] is not sloshing it does not cause [the wine] to be forbidden; and as long as he is drinking, [the wine] is not sloshing — it is being drawn toward him; it only sloshes downward when he stops and removes [his mouth from the vessel]. Therefore there is no prohibition as long as the gentile has not stopped [drinking]; and with this vessel the wine flows by itself into the tubes — not through sucking — for if it were through sucking it would be the law of the minekes [nursing vessel] which I discussed in siman 124 seif 24 in the name of Maharil. And regarding the ruling: it seems that since this matter was not clarified even to Rashba, and he wrote that Raavad too found it difficult — therefore we have no basis on which to derive any leniency from this vessel, and one should not drink with a gentile from any vessel.
