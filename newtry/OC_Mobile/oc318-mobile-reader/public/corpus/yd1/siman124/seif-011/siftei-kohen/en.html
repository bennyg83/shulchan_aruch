But they are believed, etc. — See above siman 119 seif kaf 18 and below siman 127 seif kaf 19.
With his foot. — See above siman 123 seif kaf 42 and below seif kaf 53.
Through something else in his hand. — Specifically intentionally; but unintentionally there is a distinction between touching with his hand and touching through something else: with his hand he forbids for drinking, but through something else permitted even for drinking, as below seif 14 and seif 24.
And he will shake it. But touch alone without stirring does not forbid in benefit — according to Tur and Rashi and Rambam; and likewise in seif 13 he copied Rambam's words that Beit Yosef learned from his words that touch without stirring does not forbid in benefit; and as Beit Yosef: inserting his hand is not called stirring, for it is only slight movement; and likewise it appears from what we say (perek R' Yishmael daf 59 side 2) regarding the etrog that fell into a wine barrel — a non-Jew came and took it; Rav Ashi said: he took it in his hand so he would not shake it — it implies we are not particular about insertion — end of his words; and accordingly he wrote that Tosafot and the Rosh hold touch without stirring forbids in benefit (and after him Maharshal and Perishah followed); meaning since they had to explain that the etrog case was not forbidden in benefit through inserting his hand because he did not intend to touch but to take his own — and it is difficult: what difference insertion and removal; and further behold the Tur itself wrote below that it is not forbidden through inserting his hand even if he knew it was wine since he does not intend except to take his own; rather certainly stirring of insertion is like stirring of removal; and perforce Tosafot and the Rosh and the Tur had to explain that the etrog case was not forbidden through inserting his hand because he did not intend to touch but to take his own; but otherwise they hold touch alone does not forbid in benefit (one version: in the barrel) — only stirring; and this is how Tosafot conclude: but after he took the etrog one should be concerned lest he set his mind and shake — end; and likewise Raavad's view in his hasagot beginning chapter 12 of Forbidden Foods that he forbids in benefit only if he shakes.
Such as if he drank from it. — Whether he drank from the barrel itself or drank from the cup and returned the Jew's permitted amount to the barrel — it is forbidden.
That he sucked with a pump-reed in the barrel and drew it up, etc. — Tosafot wrote, end of tractate Avodah Zarah daf 58: it deals when he did not touch the wine with the pump-reed, such as when they first placed the pump-reed in wine and its head was outside the barrel and sealed the mouth of the barrel and the mouth of the pump-reed; and when one wants to open it, one drills the pump-reed, etc.; alternatively when he drilled he did not touch the reed to wine, but when he wants to draw up the wine he tilts the barrel until the wine reaches the pump-reed — for otherwise even if he did not draw up it would be forbidden in benefit because of touching wine through the pump-reed (unlike measuring with a reed below seif 19 — there he had no intention of stirring at all); but here he intended to stir and it is forbidden in benefit — end of their words; however for us the touch of non-Jews through something else is permitted even for drinking in a place of loss, as below seif 24.
Forbidden in benefit. — This is by the strict law, but for us permitted in benefit in a place of loss.
One may, etc. — As Beit Yosef in the name of Tosafot: as a measure of piety one should be careful, lest the non-Jew cut the stream first; and Darkhei Moshe brings it.
