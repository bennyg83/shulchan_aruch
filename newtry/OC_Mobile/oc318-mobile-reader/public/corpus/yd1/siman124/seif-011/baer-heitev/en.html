That he drank. Whether he drank directly from the barrel or from a cup and the Jew returned the remainder to the barrel. Mere touching without agitation does not forbid benefit. There is no distinction between much agitation and little agitation; all is forbidden. See siman 123, seifim 21 and 22, and below seifim 14 and 24.
For benefit. Shach writes that this is the strict law. Nowadays it is permitted for benefit in a case of loss. The case is where he did not touch the wine with the siphon, but tilted the barrel until the wine reached the siphon. Otherwise, even if he did not draw it up, the wine would be forbidden for benefit because he touched it through the siphon. This differs from measuring with a reed in seif 19, where he had no intention to agitate at all. Nowadays, indirect touch by a non-Jew is permitted for drinking in a case of loss, as stated below in seif 24.
That he should stop. Beit Yosef writes in the name of Tosafot that as an act of piety one should be careful lest the non-Jew stop the flow first.
Himself. Shach writes that this implies it is forbidden even for benefit. Nowadays, however, even if he touched the wine itself it is permitted for benefit in a case of loss.
