In my work I challenged the Mahari Weil's responsum, which concludes that the wine should not be forbidden because of a connecting stream, as in the case of kanishkanin, and certainly not here where there is substantial loss. Yet in the case of kanishkanin itself, Tosafot in chapter Gid HaNasheh clearly imply that according to the view that a connecting stream creates a connection, the wine is forbidden. The same appears from the Ran's citation of the Raavad, brought by Beit Yosef at the beginning of siman 126. The reason kanishkanin does not involve a connecting stream is that the wine reaching the Jew's spout does not reach the non-Jew's spout. This implies that with a suction tube, where all the wine follows the tube, there is a prohibition because of the connecting stream. The Rosh likewise explicitly indicates that the wine is forbidden for this reason. He writes that one cannot prove otherwise from the baraita concerning one who drew wine through a suction tube, which states that the returning drop of wine forbids the mixture in any amount. If a connecting stream creates a connection, why is the returning drop needed? Once he drew through the tube and sucked the wine until it touched his mouth, all the wine should become forbidden through the connecting stream. The Rosh answers that the conclusion concerning the returning drop refers only to one who tasted from a cup and returned it to the barrel. This shows that, according to the view that a connecting stream creates a connection, all the wine is forbidden through the stream and nullification does not apply. The Ran may also mean this when he writes that it is as though the non-Jew touched all the wine, according to our ruling that a connecting stream creates a connection. Tosafot in chapter Rabbi Yishmael and the Rashba explain the baraita as teaching that even according to the view that a connecting stream does not create a connection, the returning drop forbids the wine. A clear proof appears at the end of Avodah Zarah 72b, in the incident of the man who drew wine through a siphon and a secondary siphon. A non-Jew placed his hand on the siphon, and all the wine was forbidden. Rav Pappa asked whether this was because of the connecting stream, which would prove that a connecting stream creates a connection. The Gemara answered that the case was different because all the wine was drawn after the siphon and secondary siphon. This shows that, according to the view that a connecting stream creates a connection, the wine would otherwise be forbidden, and the same applies to a suction tube. Accordingly, wine drawn through a suction tube should be permitted only in a case of substantial loss, because in such a case we rule that a connecting stream does not create a connection, as explained below at the beginning of siman 126.
