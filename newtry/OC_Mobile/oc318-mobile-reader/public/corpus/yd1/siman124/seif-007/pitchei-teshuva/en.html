To provoke. See Chatam Sofer responsa siman 127 regarding Reuven, who made kosher wine at a non-Jew's premises and sealed the wine according to law, but could not bring it home until the authorities took their tithe. When the official came to collect the tithe, he noticed the Jewish wine, opened the seals, put his finger in the wine, and said explicitly: "Now the Jew will drink from the wine; I have already touched it." Immediately after the official left the cellar, however, the non-Jew closed the cellar, went and informed Reuven that his wine was open, and Reuven went with the non-Jew and resealed the wine; there was a great loss involved. In this wine there were two concerns: first, the official's touch, according to the words of the non-Jewish cellar-owner; and second, the wine was left alone with the cellar-owner without a seal while the official was in the cellar and afterward. For who tells us that he came to the Jew immediately; perhaps he delayed some time. And who says that he is telling the truth? Perhaps long before the official came, the cellar-owner opened it to adulterate it and profit by exchanging it, and now he blames it on the official and says he did so to provoke. He discusses this at great length and concludes in practical halachah to permit the wine for several reasons; see there.
