And the Shulchan Aruch made no mention of this here. In any event, the Mechaber could not have ruled otherwise, for in seif 19 he follows the Rif and the Rambam that wine measured by a non-Jew's hand is forbidden for benefit. The Rama's ruling can also be explained. Where the non-Jew actually touches the wine with his hand, there are two grounds for stringency. First, the Rosh holds that saving wine is not considered a preoccupation. Second, measuring wine with one's hand renders it forbidden for benefit. They therefore did not wish to be lenient in such a case. By contrast, in seif 19, where he measured the wine with his hand, the act is certainly a preoccupation, and the Rama relied upon the authorities who permit the wine for benefit. Similarly, in seif 23, where he placed his hand upon the spigot, the Rama relied upon the view that saving the wine is considered a preoccupation. In that case, even according to the authorities who forbid wine measured by hand, one may maintain that the wine is permitted because the non-Jew did not directly touch it, as the Shach writes there in seif katan 65. But where he actually touched the wine with his hand, relying upon the preoccupation of saving it would encounter two defects that favor stringency. This appears correct to me.
