It is forbidden. Derishah was uncertain whether this might be permitted because of enmity, as in siman 148. Taz writes that his words are astonishing. How can he be uncertain about something explicit in the verse, “Lest you make a covenant,” etc., “and he will invite you, and you will eat”? The Torah commanded us to maintain enmity with them in order to distance us from their daughters, so how can we permit this because of enmity, etc.? See there.
Of his own. The prohibition exists because of the invitation. Taz writes: “It appears to me that even in a Jew's house there is a prohibition when the non-Jew sends food or drink to many people so that they should gather to eat or drink. They eat there because of his invitation, so it is like the non-Jew's house. Sending food to a Jew's house was permitted only when he does not tell them to gather for this meal. If he mentions that they should gather together, it is an actual invitation, and they are forbidden to gather together because the verse states, ‘and he will invite you, and you will eat from his sacrifice.’ Rather, each person should take his portion home.”
