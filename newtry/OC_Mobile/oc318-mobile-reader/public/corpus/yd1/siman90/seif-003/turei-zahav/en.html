And the same law to roast this on this. It deals with salted udder — otherwise in any case one must whiten the spit because of udder meat that has blood in it.
For after roasting the udder its law is like other meat for everything. Maharshal there chapter 25 siman 47 wrote: even after roasting it is forbidden to cook it — even though for liver it is permitted; and if so the same for udder — do not say so, for in liver the reason is because of extra blood — therefore they did not permit its cooking l'chatchila except after roasting; but udder whose prohibition is a decree lest one cook without tearing in two directions and with meat there is no difference, end of his words — and this is l'chatchila only.
Pockets full of milk, etc. The reason there in Orach Chaim: chalav shechutah is a rabbinic decree because of basar b'chalav and anything they did not specify they did not decree; and moreover it is no worse than blood of limbs that was not specified in roasting which is permitted; but if it was not torn as required it is milk stored inside and is called specified and forbidden d'rabbanan, end of his words.
And we do not say pickling is like cooking. The reason there in Orach Chaim: since chalav shechutah is d'rabbanan the Sages did not decree in it cold to say pickling is like cooking even though milk is clear in appearance; however if other meat was soaked in brine of chalav shechutah, or even the udder itself after it was specified once, it is forbidden because of pickling; and I am astonished at his words, for in basar b'chalav itself it is permitted from the Torah in pickling; if so it is a decree on a decree; and so is Rama's view here that he did not copy the prohibition on other meat in pickling because he retracted from what he wrote in Tur Chaadash, end of his words.
