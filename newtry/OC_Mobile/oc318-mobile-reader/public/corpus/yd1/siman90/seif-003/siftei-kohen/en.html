Permitted to cut hot udder, etc. This law applies even for us if they tore in two directions and smeared on the wall, as the Rav wrote in the gloss; and some say for a pot that they practice not to cook it alone — even through tearing in two directions and smearing on the wall — there it is because of a decree lest they come to cook it with meat together; and this does not apply regarding a knife — Semak siman 213 and Hagahot Maimoniyot:
And all the more live udder, etc. In Rivash it is written: even live udder — and the amplification refers to that it is full of milk; and the Rav who wrote all the more live udder that is not roasted — more correct to emend the Rav's language: and so live udder, etc.:
And specifically when it was roasted as required, etc. And Maharshal wrote in Orach Chaim: if it was roasted as required, even if they did not smear on the wall but only tore in two directions, it is permitted to cut it with a meat knife and one need not smearing except when it was not roasted as required; but after it was fully roasted and also torn well, I call one who is stringent a pious fool and an ignoramus, end of his words — see there; and see above siman 69 s.k. and siman 76 seif 4 and what he wrote there:
All these matters are forbidden l'chatchila, etc. And from Maharshal's words in Orach Chaim it appears that if they did not tear in two directions and smear on the wall, the spit must be whitened — and otherwise even b'dieved forbidden; and the Rav's words appear so; and further from Rashba and Ran that Beit Yosef brings briefly it explicitly appears that even l'chatchila it is permitted to roast on one spit sometimes meat and sometimes udder even without tearing at all; and one may say that even Maharshal speaks l'chatchila:
