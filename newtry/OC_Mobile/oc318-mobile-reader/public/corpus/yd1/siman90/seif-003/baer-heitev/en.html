To cut. This law applies even for us if they tore in two directions and smeared on the wall; and some say for a pot that they practice not to cook it alone, even through tearing in two directions and smearing on the wall — there it is because of a decree lest they come to cook it with meat together; and this does not apply regarding a knife — Shach.
On a spit. Taz wrote that this deals with salted udder, for otherwise in any case one must whiten the spit because of meat of the udder that has blood in it.
Roasting. Rashi wrote that even after roasting it is forbidden to cook it, even though regarding liver it is permitted — for udder is different, for it is forbidden because of a decree lest one cook it without tearing with other meat; and Taz wrote this is only l'chatchila specifically.
As required. Maharshal wrote that if it was roasted as required, even if they did not smear on the wall but only tore in two directions, it is permitted to cut it with a meat knife; and one who is stringent is called a pious fool and an ignoramus.
Pockets. The reason is in Orach Chaim that chalav shechutah is a rabbinic decree because of basar b'chalav, and anything they did not specify they did not decree; and moreover it is no worse than blood of limbs that was not specified in roasting which is permitted; but if it was not torn as required it is milk stored inside and is called specified and forbidden d'rabbanan.
Pickled. Taz wrote in name of Orach Chaim that if other meat was soaked in brine of chalav shechutah, or even the udder itself after it was specified once, it is forbidden because of pickling; and he challenged this, for in basar b'chalav itself it is permitted from the Torah in pickling, for it forbade only by way of cooking — if so it is a decree on a decree; and so too is Rama's view here, that he did not copy the prohibition on other meat in pickling because he retracted from what he wrote in Tur Chaadash, end of his words. (And in Nekudot HaKesef he disagrees with Taz on this, for we find in many places that the Sages decreed a decree on a decree, and one should not compare one decree to another, see there; and I too require study on what Rama wrote that we do not say pickling in such a case — for Mechaber ruled in siman 87 on milk found in the stomach that if it stood a full day it is like pickled, and this too is called chalav shechutah — investigate.) (And Peri Chadash wrote like Orach Chaim and wrote from what was written Chullin daf 154 side 2 that one may not eat udder after cheese without wiping, rinsing, and rinsing hands; and so too those who practice six hours between cheese and meat — afterward one must also wait if he comes to eat udder; and one must pick his teeth if he comes to eat meat after it, end of his words, see there.)
