(siman 90 seif 1) The udder, etc., if they transgressed and cooked, etc. — this whole seif is Rambam's language; and Maggid Mishneh wrote: it was not explained in our master's words in cooking without meat whether tearing is required or not — and it is difficult, for it is proven l'chatchila tearing in two directions is required, for Rambam holds roasting requires slight tearing — if so the case of fatty meat attached to the udder is with slight tearing; and the Gemara challenges: but we teach one tears it — meaning tearing in two directions is required; and it answers: that deals with a pot — and therefore for a pot without meat — otherwise how could Rav say he does not transgress and it is permitted, for Rambam holds with meat without tearing even b'dieved is forbidden — if so certainly l'chatchila in Rav's first version in the baraita it deals without meat.
(there) And we do not estimate it except when it absorbs, etc. — this law too is Rambam's language; and he wrote similarly that Rambam explains what the Gemara established: do we estimate in it or in what came out from it — meaning we are in doubt whether we estimate as it comes before us or in what came out from it, meaning we estimate as it was at the time it fell; and concludes in the udder itself; and Beit Yosef challenged: if so how does the Gemara say plainly we estimate in the udder itself — if per this view we know how much it was at the time it fell; and I say this certainly must be that sixty is not required against all the milk in it — otherwise the Gemara's question: udder in sixty what does it want here in sixty — it should taste the spoonful — if so it must be it is not forbidden
(there in the gloss) And some say if it fell first into a pot, etc. — see Acharonim who wondered at Mechaber's words that he did not bring this view that Tur brought in name of Rashba; and it appears impossible: forbidden basar b'chalav that was already absorbed in a piece is like other prohibitions — and noten taam naaseh nevelah per Rabbenu Ephraim's view; if so here in udder per Rambam's view that meat taste of the udder is not forbidden at all because of basar b'chalav but only if cooked with meat — if so the udder is not forbidden only because basar b'chalav was absorbed in it; in such a case there is no noten taam naaseh nevelah; if so Rambam and Mechaber who hold like R' Eliezer — this law is unnecessary; indeed Tur who copied this law even though he too holds like Rabbenu Ephraim's view — because of the stringency of the matter he brought it — investigate.
