(Taz s.k. 4) It appears even if it fell first into less than sixty, etc. — to combine also the udder — so he raised in Mechaber's view: in rabbinic prohibition a piece does not become noten taam naaseh nevelah — therefore also when it fell into another pot the udder is from the count; and for the gloss requiring sixty without the udder because he holds in all rabbinic prohibitions we say noten taam naaseh nevelah. And in my humble opinion it does not appear proven from Mechaber — one may say Mechaber and Rama follow their reasons; and in what they still disagree — Mechaber like Rambam: cooking in a pot alone b'dieved even without tearing is permitted; and Rama forbids — and their dispute is explained in Rishonim: Mechaber's view that absorption of udder meat from its milk or its milk from udder meat was not forbidden because of meat and milk but only when cooked with meat
