With the udder like sixty in the udder, etc. Rashba wrote in Torat HaBayit the long version that Rambam gave a reason for this: since udder prohibition is d'rabbanan they made a heker and were lenient in it; and even though gid fat requires sixty though it is rabbinic, since gid hanasheh is Torah law they were stringent also in its fat — and Rashba rejected this reason, for many rabbinic prohibitions require sixty; and he wrote the reason is because it itself is meat and joins with the meat to nullify the milk in it; and he explained his words in Mishmeret HaBayit on what Bedek HaBayit challenged: behold the udder became nevelah — and he wrote this udder never became within this pot a forbidden piece, for the milk was nullified in sixty within what was in the pot.
