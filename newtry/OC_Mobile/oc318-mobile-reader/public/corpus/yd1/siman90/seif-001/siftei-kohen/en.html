The udder, etc. — until end of seif is Rambam's language chapter 9 of Forbidden Foods; and the Rav in the gloss seif 2 disagrees in many laws and did not write in language of 'some say' but arranged his reasoning all together to abbreviate:
Whether of a young animal that did not nurse. And so in all laws of udder there is no difference between of an adult or young that did not nurse — and so the poskim:
By itself. Explanation: without meat; but it is plain that in water and vegetables and other things it is considered like cooking by itself — investigate:
We estimate it against sixty. It is in the Gemara and agreed by all poskim that we require sixty against all the udder and we do not estimate what came out from it, for we do not know how much came out; and Mechaber did not write it because it is plain by itself, and he already wrote similarly in siman 69 seif 11 regarding meat cooked without salting that sixty is required against that meat and not against blood within it — and so in many places; or it is included in what he wrote that we estimate it against sixty, meaning we estimate the udder itself against sixty:
And some say if it fell first into a pot, etc. — meaning into a meat pot that does not have sixty, even with the udder — meaning it fell into less than fifty-nine — so the milk that comes out from it was not nullified — if so the udder is forbidden by law; but when it fell into sixty so the milk coming out was nullified, the udder is not forbidden except because of marit ayin; even in a second pot the udder joins the sixty, for it is no worse because it was forbidden — for in the first pot the udder joins sixty even though it itself is forbidden; and the reason poskim agreed: the udder prohibition is not because it has more milk taste than other meat but because there are pockets in the udder where milk is stored and that milk has taste from meat cooked with it — and that
