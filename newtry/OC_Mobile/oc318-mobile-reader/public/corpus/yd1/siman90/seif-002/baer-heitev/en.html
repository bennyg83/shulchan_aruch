In a pan. Shach wrote in Beit Yosef it implies even on a griddle, and we do not practice so.
Great loss. Shach wrote: even with meat; but alone it is permitted even without great loss; and Bach forbids even with great loss and disagrees with Shach, who says one may rely on Rama especially in a d'rabbanan prohibition.
Preferable. Rashba wrote: and if they placed it under the press it is like smeared on the wall.
Permitted. Even if it is known they were turned over, for since they tore in two directions and smeared on the wall it is considered like actual meat for this matter.
And the lower. Shach wrote: and it appears that if it is known both were skewered equally on the spit and neither was above or below — both are permitted.
To roast it. The reason is that our spits lie lengthwise and sometimes this end is up and sometimes that end is up.
Prohibition. Explanation: even through tearing in two directions and smearing on the wall.
B'dieved. Without sixty; but if they did not tear in two directions and did not smear on the wall, even b'dieved it is forbidden until there is sixty in water against the udder and the udder is in the count, end of his words — Shach. And Taz wrote in name of Maharshal that it is forbidden even b'dieved even if they tore in two directions and smeared on the wall; even though it is not from the law at all, nevertheless since the Geonim wrote that all Israel practice not to cook udder at all, it is like something forbidden by number, etc.; and nevertheless when dry it does not appear to him to be stringent, end of his words.
Permitted. Explanation: Shach — even without tearing; and also if we see it is truly dried, it is plain that even within thirty days it is permitted.
On a griddle. Shach wrote: and that which we hold above end of seif 88 that pastry even without a griddle has the law of cooking in a pot — this case is different, since by law it is permitted and only forbidden because of a decree; if so we decree only specifically on a griddle.
Dried out. Shach wrote it implies; but if it dried out it is permitted in pastry even on a griddle, and Maharshal's view is not so — rather his view is that on a griddle it is forbidden l'chatchila even when dry.
Stringency. Shach wrote: but in pastry of udder with milk it is certainly forbidden by law, for certainly udder is actual meat and it is like the law of other basar b'chalav baked in one oven, as will be explained below siman 97 and 108.
