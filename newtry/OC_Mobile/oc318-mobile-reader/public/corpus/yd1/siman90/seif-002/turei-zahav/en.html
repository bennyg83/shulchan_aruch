And if they transgressed and cooked it. Explanation: with meat.
But otherwise one may not permit. Explanation: without sixty; but if there is sixty in the meat in the pot with the udder against the udder, then the meat is permitted and the udder is forbidden — in any case so Tur ruled in name of Rashi and so written Tur Chaadash general rule 6.
And for a pot without meat they practice prohibition, etc. The reason is because of a decree lest they cook it with meat and without tearing in two directions and smearing on the wall.
And if they transgressed and cooked it in a pot alone b'dieved it is permitted, etc. Maharshal wrote chapter 25 siman 47: it appears forbidden even b'dieved; and even though it is not from the law at all, nevertheless since the Geonim wrote all Israel practice not to cook udder at all, it is like something forbidden by number, etc.; and nevertheless when dry it does not appear to him to be stringent, end of his words.
