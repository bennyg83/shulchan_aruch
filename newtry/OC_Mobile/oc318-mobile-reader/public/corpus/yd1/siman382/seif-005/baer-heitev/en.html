However, where there is no concern of non-Jewish wickedness, one should remove shoes immediately in the cemetery, as above at the beginning of siman 375 and siman 376. End quote from Shach. The Taz wrote that according to what the Mordechai wrote, that if one goes before a minister and the minister is particular if he goes without shoes, he should put earth in his shoes and go, the same applies in a dangerous place and anywhere he needs to go with shoes: he should do so. He further wrote in siman 381 in the name of his brother, the Gaon R. Yitzchak, that one may permit a woman to wash for the white-garment count even within seven days. What the Rama wrote, that within seven days it is forbidden to wash in order to wear white garments, is astonishing, for this is not washing for pleasure. Immersion washing is forbidden because it is unnecessary, since she is forbidden in marital relations; but why should washing for the white garments be forbidden? In any event, it appears that even the Rama meant only that she should not wash, since she can wear white garments without full washing, provided the garment is clean, as written in siman 196 seif 3 in the gloss, that in pressing circumstances this is enough. Responsa Mas'et Binyamin permits even washing within seven days to wear white garments and disagrees with the Rama. Nevertheless, in practice it appears to me that she should wash only the lower area and wear a clean garment, not actually white. One who is stringent to delay wearing the white garments entirely is among those who astonish. End quote. Maharshal wrote: Maharam was a baal brit within thirty days for his father and washed on the night before the milah; he gave the reason that it is like a festival for him, and our ancestors' custom of stringency in washing is only a stringency. End quote.
