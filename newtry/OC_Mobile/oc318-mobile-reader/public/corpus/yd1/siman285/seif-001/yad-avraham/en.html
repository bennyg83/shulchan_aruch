(Siman 285 seif 1) And whoever is careful with it — his days are lengthened; and if not careful — shortened. And Tur concludes from affirmative you hear negative. Beit Yosef challenged from Kiddushin (61a) dispute of Rabbi Meir and Rabbis on double condition: for Rabbi Meir it is written "if you do well, bear up; if not well, sin crouches," "if you walk in My statutes," "if you despise My statutes," "if you consent and listen, the good of the land you shall eat," "if you refuse" — but for Rabbis why the doublings; answered: lest one say if you do well reward and if not no reward and no judgment — implying for all we do not derive punishment from affirmative alone. And to resolve: we conclude in chapter 5 of Shevuot that Rabbi Meir lacks "from affirmative you hear negative" in prohibition involving money, but in standalone prohibition he has it; and see Tosafot what they wrote that all those in Kiddushin involve money — if so Beit Yosef's question on mezuzah is no question at all, for there is no money; and similarly all say from affirmative we hear negative even for punishment; and accordingly one can resolve Maharsha's question in part 1 Shabbat why he did not challenge according to Rabbi; and similarly Ran did not give reason "that you multiply" teaches death regarding mezuzah and Torah study; and according to what was said one can say they hold like the initial suggestion that even regarding prohibition punishment is not derived from affirmative for one who requires doubling; and Rabbi holds doubling in Yevamot (53a).
