(Siman 285 seif 1 in Hagahah) That it is a bodily obligation takes precedence. It appears in the plain sense the intent is that tefillin is an obligation placed on his body and he has no way to exempt himself from it; but mezuzah — the obligation is not upon him to build a house and affix a mezuzah, only if he has a house he must make a mezuzah in it, as we say "mezuzah is an obligation of dwelling" — therefore tefillin precede. And similar to this he wrote in Hagahah in Orach Chaim (siman 17) — see there. And I saw in Beit HaLevi who wrote per the plain sense of Rama — tefillin is bodily obligation, meaning it is a garment and the mitzvah is literally on his body, unlike mezuzah; and from this he wrote that likewise tefillin precede sukkah and lulav, for they too are bodily obligations relative to him; and likewise regarding tzitzit. And in my humble opinion the essence is as written — unlike sukkah and lulav, where perhaps sukkah takes precedence for he is also obligated on the first night, and likewise lulav; only regarding tzitzit the law is with him, for it is truly like mezuzah — it is not an obligation on the body, only if he wears a tallit garment he must make tzitzit — so it appears to me. Afterward I saw in Yerushalmi that the source of this law emerges from it, and I found explicitly as written — and so it is stated there: "What is R. Huna's reason? For so it is practiced by seafarers and desert travelers" — see there; and this is as written — that with tefillin he has no way to exempt himself, but with mezuzah it is possible he is at sea and has no obligation at all, unlike lulav where even at sea the obligation is upon him — and this is clear. However one may argue from another reason that tefillin precede sukkah and lulav — namely tefillin is a positive commandment practiced all year, and lulav only once a year; and as Shmuel holds in Yerushalmi that mezuzah precedes tefillin, for mezuzah is practiced even on Shabbat and festivals unlike tefillin (and this is frequent versus infrequent); except R. Huna disputes and holds tefillin, being bodily obligation, takes precedence; and if so tefillin and lulav, both being obligations of dwelling — per the other's view: since tefillin are practiced all year, tefillin precede; and specifically tefillin and lulav; but tzitzit and lulav — it appears to me lulav precedes tzitzit, although tzitzit all year, nevertheless lulav is bodily obligation — as written, so it appears to me. And that we rule like R. Huna against Shmuel that tefillin precede mezuzah — see in Rosh Laws of Tefillin, who wrote it is reasonable the halachah is like R. Huna that bodily mitzvah takes precedence, and Gemara too explains his reason; and see Korban Eidah who challenged this and wrote another reason to rule like R. Huna; and in my humble opinion another reason to rule like R. Huna, and the manner of their dispute between Shmuel and R. Huna will also be explained. And I will quote Yerushalmi's language: "Tefillin and mezuzah — which precedes? Shmuel said mezuzah precedes; R. Huna said tefillin precedes. What is Shmuel's reason? For so it is practiced on festivals and Sabbaths. What is R. Huna's reason? For so it is practiced by seafarers and desert travelers. Mishnah supports Shmuel — worn-out tefillin one makes them into a mezuzah; worn-out mezuzah one does not make them into tefillin — why? We elevate in sanctity and do not lower" — end Yerushalmi. And it appears their dispute between Shmuel and R. Huna depends on what is explained in Zevachim (91) — Gemara wanted to establish that frequent and sanctified — sanctified precedes, from mishnah "peace-offerings of yesterday and sin-offering and guilt-offering of today — peace-offerings precede"; but both are of the day — sin-offering and guilt-offering precede; and although peace-offerings are more frequent, Rava said "you say frequent? We are in doubt about frequent, we are not in doubt about common" (Rashi explained: you say common — its frequency is not obligatory but it is always more available than its fellow). R. Huna son of R. Yehuda said to Rava: is common not frequent? — see there. Behold it is clear Rava holds common is not called frequent since its frequency is not obligatory; and R. Huna son of R. Yehuda holds it is frequent. If so, one may say Shmuel holds like R. Huna son of R. Yehuda, and therefore holds mezuzah precedes, for it is practiced even on Sabbaths and festivals — which is frequent relative to tefillin; and R. Huna holds like Rava that common is not frequent; automatically tefillin which are practiced at least on weekdays are obligatory, and mezuzah perhaps all year without mezuzah — for if he wishes he will be among seafarers and desert travelers — and this is what Yerushalmi says in R. Huna's reason "for so it is practiced by seafarers and desert travelers" — meaning through this tefillin are frequent and mezuzah is only common and not frequent (and for this reason Sefer Sha'agat Aryeh wrote counting the omer precedes musaf on weekdays, for counting the omer is frequent relative to musaf — for if he wishes he will not eat — see there). Although we find only that frequent and infrequent — frequent precedes — meaning if both are before him to precede one over the other; but here with tefillin and mezuzah where one is deferred from the other, frequent precedes has no relevance; nevertheless also for deferring, frequent precedes, as Rosh wrote (chapter 2 of Shabbat siman 12) — frequent precedes even to defer the infrequent; and so appears plainly in Sukkah (54b) Tosafot s.v. ve'ama'i frequent, etc. And since we conclude in Zevachim there like Rava that common is not called frequent — automatically we hold like R. Huna that tefillin precede. And besides our explanation per Yerushalmi's words, it appears to me the proof is correct to rule per "and tefillin precede" — because tefillin are frequent relative to mezuzah; and although it is an unresolved question in Zevachim whether sanctified precedes frequent, nevertheless we hold tefillin are more sanctified than mezuzah, as stated in Shulchan Aruch below (siman 291 seif 1) — see there. And per our words the proof that Yerushalmi wrote "worn-out tefillin," etc. is well settled — for at first glance what proof is this? Nevertheless one may say for bodily mitzvah takes precedence; and per our words it is proper — the essence of R. Huna's reasoning is that tefillin are frequent relative to mezuzah; and for this Yerushalmi properly says that even if we grant R. Huna's view that tefillin are frequent, nevertheless mezuzah takes precedence since it is more sanctified; and Yerushalmi plainly holds sanctified and frequent — sanctified precedes; and nevertheless we rule like R. Huna since we hold tefillin are more sanctified than mezuzah — understand well.
