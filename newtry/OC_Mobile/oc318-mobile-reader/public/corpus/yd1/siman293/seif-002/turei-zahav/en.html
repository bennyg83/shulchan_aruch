Whether from gentiles' [grain] — so wrote the poskim in explanation, and Mahari Chagiz wrote that there is clear proof that gentiles' [grain] is not subject to chadash, from the first chapter of Rosh Hashanah (daf 13): they asked Rav Kahana: the omer that Israel offered when they entered the Land — from where did they offer it? You would say it entered by gentile hands — "your harvest" the Merciful One said, not gentile harvest. From where that they offered? Perhaps they did not offer? It cannot enter your mind, for it is written "and they ate from the produce of the land on the morrow after Pesach" — on the morrow after Pesach they ate; initially they did not eat, for they offered the omer; from where did they offer? He said: anything that did not bring a third by gentile hands. Now if you say chadash applies even to gentiles' [grain], he would not need to prove "initially they did not eat" — in any case he should have said: if they did not offer, how could they eat on the morrow after Pesach, behold it is forbidden because of chadash? Rather, necessarily chadash does not apply to gentiles' [grain]; and therefore he inferred "initially they did not eat" — for if it entered by gentile hands and they did not offer the omer initially, why did they not eat? Behold with gentiles' [grain] there is no chadash prohibition; or they offered the omer from Israel's [grain], and therefore they did not eat initially from Israel's [grain]; from where did they offer? — end. And I say: his soul rests in goodness, but he was not precise in these matters, for what he asked — why did the Gemara not ask from where they ate, behold forbidden because of chadash — there is not even the beginning of a question, for it is possible they ate from old grain from last year; it is possible it became clear to them that this is old grain, and even in doubt Rosh wrote in responsum in the name of Avi HaEzri that it is permitted, for there is a double doubt; and Tur too wrote — end; therefore the questioner needed to prove from "initially they did not eat" that there was chadash prohibition there, and on this he asks from where was it permitted, and on Mahari Chagiz's explanation that he concludes they offered the omer from Israel's [grain], etc., there is to wonder: if so, why did he ask from where Israel took grain for the omer — he should have asked more: from where did Israel take grain to eat, since now we hold that the verse "and they ate from the produce of the land" refers to Israel's [grain]? Rather it is clear that even gentiles' [grain] is forbidden, as all the poskim wrote; and from this is clear proof of prohibition, for at that time all the grain was gentiles' according to the questioner's view, and nevertheless there was chadash prohibition — only that he was asked from where they took the omer.
