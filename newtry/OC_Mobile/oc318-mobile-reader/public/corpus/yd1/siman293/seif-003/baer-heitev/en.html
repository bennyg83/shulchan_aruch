The omer. If the grain took root before the omer, the omer permits it even if it was harvested afterward. Taking root before the omer means three days earlier. Shach.
Doubt. Although new grain is a matter that will later become permitted, and such matters remain forbidden even under compounded doubt, Rosh's responsum limits that rule to mixtures. Here there may be no new grain at all, so compounded doubt remains effective. See Shach in siman 110.
Better. Taz does not accept the leniency as sufficient in itself. He instead permits reliance in pressing circumstances upon the first opinion in the Mishnah that new grain does not apply outside Eretz Yisrael, especially where livelihood depends upon barley or oat beer. Shach limits this to genuine public need and requires protest where only a single doubt exists. Lechem Mishneh seeks an additional leniency for beer because only liquid is extracted, but the responsa of Rosh and other authorities imply prohibition. Nekudot HaKesef argues that new grain outside Eretz Yisrael is forbidden by Torah law.
