(Siman 300, seif 6) One who drives animals and inserts ropes, and so forth; but if he tied them all together they become kilayim, and he may not wrap the ropes around his hand. Behag cites some who permit this because he does not intend to derive benefit. It appears that the Mechaber’s prohibition is primary, because this is an inevitable consequence, as the Shach writes. This accords with the anonymous mishnah in Shabbat 54, “provided that he does not wrap them,” and does not contradict the anonymous mishnayot in Kilayim and Nazir, which follow Rabbi Shimon. According to the permissive view, however, the anonymous mishnayot would contradict one another. Moreover, if Shabbat contained an anonymous mishnah following Rabbi Yehudah, why would the Gemara challenge Rabbi Yochanan’s prohibition from the anonymous mishnah in Nazir? One could answer that he followed the stringent anonymous mishnah. The Rambam, Tosafot, Rosh, Rashba, Hagahot Ashri, and Ran all support the prohibition. The passage in Menachot 5 is also difficult for the permissive view. It says that kilayim was permitted for the Temple service. If lack of intent for benefit already removes the prohibition, what special permission was needed, since the Temple case involves no intent for benefit? Likewise, how could one derive from kilayim in tzitzit that a positive commandment overrides a prohibition?
