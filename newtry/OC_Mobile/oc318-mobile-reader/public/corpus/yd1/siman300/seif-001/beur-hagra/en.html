Since, etc.; how so, etc. He explains nuz like Rashi, namely woven, and rules that shua or tavui or nuz is enough. So he wrote in his commentary to Mishnah 8, according to the text cited by Kesef Mishneh: shua is, etc., nuz is, etc., and that they should not be, etc.; until “this is kilayim by rabbinic law.” Some geonim said this, but to me this statement is not true; each one of them is Torah kilayim. What is stated in Niddah is not the language of the Gemara but the language of some commentator. Therefore in his code he omitted that statement, “one who throws,” etc., and wrote: from where do we know all these prohibitions are Torah law? For Scripture needed to permit kilayim in tzitzit, etc., and tzitzit are only tied threads, etc. This is Tosafot's question in Yevamot 5b s.v. “until,” etc. He did not want to explain like Rabbenu Tam, as Ri HaLavan challenged there; so too the Rash challenged there. Rabbenu Tam's answer is not compelling, as written below. Likewise in the Yerushalmi, brought by the Rash on Mishnah 9: let it teach shua, etc.; if so, they would say nuz is permitted, etc., because they return to weaving. Thus nuz means woven. It also implies from this Yerushalmi that each alone is Torah-forbidden. So too it appears from the Mishnah, which says “is not forbidden,” etc., implying Torah prohibition, and afterward says “felts are forbidden,” etc., pif, etc., also implying Torah law. Meaning: or tavui, spun together and afterward woven, for spinning alone is not a garment and is not kilayim even rabbinically, since that is not a manner of wearing, as stated at the beginning of Shabbat chapter 6: the sages taught three things, etc. The Rif and Rosh there wrote that it is not woven; so too the Rambam wrote here, halakhah 20: fringe, etc., because it is not the manner, etc., and as written in Shulchan Aruch siman 301 seif 14. Rather, the principal concern is their joining together: either shua together and made into felt, or woven together, such as pif and the like, such as stitching, or spun together and afterward made into a garment. This is what the Mishnah says, “is not forbidden,” etc., then explains spinning alone, then shua, felts, etc., then weaving alone, pif, etc., cords, etc., until the end of the tractate. This is what he says here: how so, etc.; or if he beat them together, etc.; or sewed, etc.
Even if he sewed them, etc. See the commentary to Mishnah 9 and “from here that it is forbidden,” etc. Even the Rash, who disagrees there, nevertheless agrees here.
Or if he sewed, etc. Mishnah 10: the marks of the weavers, etc.
Or tied, etc. As above, from kilayim in tzitzit, and the Mishnah there: he should not tie, etc.
