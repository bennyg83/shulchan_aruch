Since... how so. Rambam explains noz like Rashi, meaning woven material, and rules that shatnez is violated by material that is either pressed, spun, or woven, as he explains in his commentary to Mishnah 8. Kesef Mishneh cites his text there: "shua means... and noz means... provided that they are not..." until, "it is kilayim rabbinically. Some of the Geonim said this, but in my view this statement is incorrect; rather, each of these forms is kilayim by Torah law." The statement in Niddah is therefore not the Gemara's own wording but that of an early commentator, which is why Rambam omits the statement concerning one who places wool and linen together. In Mishneh Torah he writes: "From where do we know that all these prohibitions are biblical? Because the Torah needed expressly to permit kilayim in tzitzit, and tzitzit are only threads tied together." This is Tosafot's question in Yevamot 5b. Rambam did not wish to adopt Rabbenu Tam's explanation, which Ri HaLavan and Rash challenge. Rabbenu Tam's answer is not compelled, as will be explained below. The Yerushalmi cited by Rash on Mishnah 9 also says: "Let it teach shua... If so, they would say noz is permitted... because they return to being woven," showing that noz means woven material. That Yerushalmi also implies that each form alone is biblically prohibited. The Mishnah's phrase "nothing is forbidden" likewise appears to mean biblical prohibition, and when it afterward says "felts are forbidden" and discusses piping and similar forms, those too appear biblical. The meaning is that either wool and linen are spun together and afterward woven, because merely spun fibers are not yet a garment and are not kilayim even rabbinically, since that is not a normal manner of wearing, as appears at the beginning of chapter 6 of Shabbos. Rif and Rosh there write that it is not woven, and Rambam here likewise writes that a decorative plate is permitted because that is not the normal manner. This is also the ruling in Shulchan Aruch siman 301, seif 14. The essential concern is their actual joining together, whether they are pressed together into felt, woven together such as piping or similar work, stitched together, or spun together and then made into a garment. This is the meaning of the Mishnah's sequence and of Mechaber's language here, "How so... or if he pressed them... or sewed them.
