So the Rambam inferred from the language of Mishnah 8 in Kilayim chapter 9, which teaches in the first clause “only spun and woven.” For spinning alone, one is liable only if it is woven so as to join them together; but for shua, felted material, one is liable, and the same applies to weaving alone, which is no worse than shua of felt. Perhaps this is because the words shua and nuz are explicitly written, but the word tavui is not written explicitly. In my humble opinion, the reason he exempts spinning is that if its explanation is like Rabbenu Tam, the Rash in his Mishnah commentary, Rabbenu Yerucham in the name of Ramah, and the Rosh, that the spinning is flax by itself and wool by itself, this is not “together.” And if it is like Rashi's explanation, that the two are spun together, as long as they were not woven this is not the manner of wearing. The Rambam wrote that what appears in the Gemara, Niddah 61b, “and the halakhah is like Mar Zutra, who says that by Torah law it is not prohibited until it is shua, tavui, and nuz,” is not the language of the Gemara but the language of some commentator. He proved this from the fact that the Torah needed to permit kilayim in tzitzit, which is only tying.
