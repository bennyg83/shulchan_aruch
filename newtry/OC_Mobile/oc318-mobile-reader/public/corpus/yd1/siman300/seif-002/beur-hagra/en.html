Gathered. This is the Mishnah there, which states that one who makes a single stitch is liable. Rambam explains it as even one stitch, meaning one insertion at the edge of the garment, and certainly two stitches. In his commentary, however, he explains the case as two stitches, as do Rash and Rosh. They further write that the threads must be tied, as stated in chapter 7 of Shabbos 74b. This is the basis of Rama's gloss, "and some say." Rambam holds that the tying requirement is specific to Shabbos. (Likkut) And some say. The Yerushalmi there records that R. Yonah and R. Yose both say the case involves tying on both sides. The words of the Sages there appear to disagree, citing Rav that one who stretches the sides on Shabbos is liable for sewing. Yet if tying were always required, he should be liable for both sewing and tying. Since with regard to Shabbos we rule that the stitches must be tied, as stated in the Gemara, the same applies here. The stretching case is different because it involves many stitches and remains intact without a knot.
