They bore two females, etc. This refers to seif 29, to two women of two men, neither of whom had previously borne. I find it difficult: why write here of two males and two females, where there is no definite bekhor because each can say a female emerged before me? Let him state the greater novelty even with two males and one female, where one is certainly a bekhor, since neither had previously borne, and nevertheless both sons are exempt from redeeming themselves, because each can evade and push the obligation to the other by saying he is the bekhor. Indeed the Tur wrote so, that they are exempt. It appears that this seif is the Rambam's language, and he follows his own view in seif 26 that the sons are obligated when their father died within thirty days and each cannot push the obligation to the other; the Tur follows his own view, ruling there too that one can push the obligation to the other. But I find this difficult, for there is an explicit baraita here that he is exempt. The Gemara says: Rav Huna taught, two males and one female, the kohen has nothing; and our tanna did not teach it because, since it is found only with two men and not with one man and two women, he did not teach it. Rashi explains that this is with two women of two men; our tanna did not teach this exemption except with two females and one male, or two females and two males, because the exemption of two males is found only with two men. With one man and two women it is not found, for there, since there is certainly one bekhor, the father is obligated to give five selaim to the kohen. Thus it is explicit that by law they are exempt with two women of two men even with two males and one female. Why then did the Rambam and Shulchan Aruch not rule this? If you say that this sugya follows the second version above, that each can push the obligation to the other, while he rules like the first version, the complaint against the Rambam is even stronger: since the anonymous Talmud and the baraita of Rav Huna follow the second version, he should have ruled so.
