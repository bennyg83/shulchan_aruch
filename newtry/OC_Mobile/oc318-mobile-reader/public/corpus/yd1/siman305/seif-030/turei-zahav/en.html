They gave birth to two females etc. It refers to seif 29, with two wives of two men, and both of them had not yet had a firstborn. And it is difficult for me: why did he write here with two males and two females, for then there is no certain bechor, for each one can say a female came out from me first — let him say a greater novelty: even with two males and one female, for then one is certainly a bechor, since both had not had a firstborn, and even so the sons are exempt from redeeming themselves, for each one will evade and push off onto his fellow, saying that he is the bechor. And indeed the Tur wrote so in this, that they are exempt. And it appears that this seif is the language of the Rambam, and he follows his own reason, for he holds in seif 26 that the sons are obligated when their father died within 30 days, and each one cannot push off onto his fellow; here too it is so. And the Tur follows his own reason, that he rules also there to push off onto his fellow. But it is difficult for me: there is an explicit baraisa here of exemption, for we say Rav Huna taught: two males and a female — the kohen has nothing. And our Tanna, since it is with two men that you find it, and with one man and two wives you do not find it, he did not teach it. And Rashi explained: and that is with two wives of two men. And our Tanna who did not teach this exemption except with two females and a male, or two females and two males, because this exemption of two males you find only with two men, but with one man and two wives you do not find it, for there, since there is one bechor, the father gives — he is obligated five sela'im to the kohen; therefore he did not teach it. Thus it is explicit that by law they are exempt with two wives of two men, even with two males and one female. And why did the Rambam and the Shulchan Aruch not rule so? And if you wish to say that this sugya comes according to the latter wording above, which holds that each one can say to push off onto his fellow, and he rules according to the first wording — all the more so there is a claim against the Rambam, since the stam of the Talmud and the baraisa of Rav Huna hold like the latter wording, he should have ruled so.
