To a kohen. See Responsa Chatam Sofer, siman 301, regarding whether the pidyon haben money may be given to the husband of a kohenet as with gifts of kehunah in siman 61. After discussing Tosafot in Kiddushin and the Rosh in Bekhorot, he concludes that the Ra'ah and Chinukh, the Rosh, and the Tur plainly forbid this, and one should not leave their certainty because of Tosafot's doubt. If the father cannot reach a kohen on the redemption day and does not want to delay the mitzvah, he should give the money that day to a kohenet without a berachah, on condition that he intends to fulfill the mitzvah only if he later fails to find a kohen; if he later finds a kohen, he redeems again with a berachah. If it is difficult to pay twice, he may give to the kohenet on condition that it be returned, even lechatchilah. Chavot Yair, siman 110, discusses a man presumed to be a kohen, after which a countryman swore that he knew his lineage and that he was a zar, and the man admitted it. Had he denied it, one witness would not remove him from priestly status; since he admitted, we are stringent to remove him from the privileges of a kohen but not lenient regarding prohibitions. He remains forbidden to women prohibited to kohanim and may not become tamei to the dead. All sons redeemed by him require a new pidyon, and he must return all five selaim; if he had denied the witness, he would have had to swear a Torah oath. Yad Eliyahu, siman 37, writes that if one established himself as a kohen, redeemed many children, and witnesses later show that he is a Yisrael, the known boys must be redeemed again and the money returned. What he already returned by custom may be deducted, for it is presumed to have been restitution, not a gift. Doubtful cases need not be redeemed again. If he is not a total zar but a ben gerushah or chalal, it requires study whether the boys need another pidyon, and therefore he need not return the money. He challenges Chavot Yair: the dispute in Makkot 11 about whether kehunah dies or is void concerns a chalal, not a zar.
