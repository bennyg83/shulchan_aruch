Through an agent. See Baer Heitev, who notes that the later authorities disagreed and wrote that the father may redeem through an agent. So too Shaar HaMelekh, at the end of Hilkhot Ishut 6, concludes and proves it from the fact that conditions work, as in the first chapter of Kiddushin and above seif 8; otherwise the condition would be void, since a valid condition must be capable of fulfillment through an agent. See what I wrote above in siman 234 s.k. 2 and siman 236 s.k. 1. Responsa Chatam Sofer, simanim 293-294, explains that nevertheless the custom is not to do this by agent, for it is fitting to make a remembrance of the Egyptian firstborn, regarding whom it is written “I, and not an agent”; here too the father should redeem himself more than in other mitzvot, where it is also preferable to do the mitzvah personally. In siman 257 he writes that the agency under dispute is where the agent gives his own money and does not transfer that money to the father, but wishes to redeem so-and-so's son with his own money as the father's agent, so that the father fulfills pidyon through “his agent is like himself,” similar to separating terumah from one's own produce for another's pile. The questioner in Rivash assumed that this does not redeem through agency. But when the owner's money is merely sent to the kohen by another person, that is not pidyon through an agent, only delivery of the money by an agent, and according to all there is no problem; many erred in this. Similar words were written by the Gaon of Lisa in Responsa Chemdat Shlomo, siman 32, who holds that the main law follows the Rama. Dagul MeRevavah wrote that all this applies where he appoints the agent after thirty days, but appointing an agent within thirty days to redeem at the proper time is not correct. Nevertheless, he says that if the father wants to travel he should appoint an agent; even if the agency does not take effect then, the agent can later redeem him through zachin, since we see that the father wants it. See there and see what I wrote below s.k. 18. Responsa Pnei Aryeh, siman 44, writes that a kohen or Levi, and perhaps also the husband of a kohenet or leviyah, should not lechatchilah serve as the father's agent for pidyon haben, since one who is himself exempt from pidyon haben is not fit to be an agent in a matter for which he is personally unfit; after the fact, if the bekhor was redeemed through a kohen agent, perhaps he need not redeem himself again when he grows up. Responsa Chatam Sofer, siman 296, first considered invalidating Levi'im for this agency, but wrote that a kohen is certainly valid, since he is the very person with whom pidyon is done, like husband and wife who are both fit as agents for a get, whether for delivery or receipt, although the husband is not divorced and the wife does not divorce. Only for another reason is it improper for a kohen to act as agent to redeem through another kohen, because it appears to slight his own kehunah, like a kohen serving as shaliach tzibbur who should not call another kohen. He later concluded that since kohanim and Levi'im are included in the positive mitzvah to redeem Israelite firstborns, and even a community made entirely of Levi'im that has Israelite firstborns among them must redeem them from their own money, they may also become agents for pidyon haben. Tzelach to Pesachim 121b writes that if one redeems through an agent, Shehecheyanu certainly does not belong to the agent, and the father also cannot recite it, so the berachah of Shehecheyanu is lost. Pnei Aryeh similarly writes that the agent should not recite Shehecheyanu, only “on pidyon haben.” Chatam Sofer, siman 298, however, writes that when the father is not present the agent recites both berachot, “on pidyon haben” and Shehecheyanu, on his own mitzvah; still, if possible, he should place a new fruit before him to remove doubt. He later found this explicit in Hagahot Perishah. See further Responsa Zikhron Yosef, end of Yoreh De'ah siman 26; Tiferet Tzvi, Yoreh De'ah siman 42; Yad Eliyahu siman 51; and Bigdei Kehunah, Yoreh De'ah siman 11. Regarding Baer Heitev's citation of the Taz that if the father died, etc., see Responsa Yeriyot HaOhel, siman 15, which lengthily refutes the Nekudot HaKesef and concludes like the Taz, that if the father died beit din cannot redeem him, and he ruled so in practice. Chatam Sofer, siman 295, also analyzes Nekudot HaKesef and concludes that as a better course beit din should redeem him with a berachah without God's name and kingship, using the orphan's money, or should acquire money for him and give it to the kohen as a gift on condition of return, and should hang a silver tablet on him engraved “doubtfully redeemed”; when he grows and brings two hairs, he should redeem himself again. Chemdat Shlomo, siman 31, also discusses this and concludes that it is very difficult to redeem in minority, and one should not act on it; passive inaction is preferable. Yet because the Shach wrote that the tablet is often lost and the mitzvah may be forgotten, he proposes a repair to satisfy all views: hang a tablet stating he was not redeemed, and transfer money to the kohen on condition that if, when the son grows, he does not know he is a bekhor and does not redeem himself, the money is now for pidyon; but if he does learn that he is a bekhor, he must redeem himself and the present money is a gift. In siman 32 there is a responsum of the Gaon of Lisa on this matter. He holds that the main law is that they can redeem from the minor's property, because pidyon haben money is unlike other mitzvot. Other mitzvot do not create a lien on property, but pidyon haben does; it is not merely due to suretyship, but the kohen has a share in the property itself, as seizure helps. If seizure helps to make him redeemed, all the more so beit din can collect from the minor's property. Nevertheless, in practice it requires study against the Taz's reasoning. Regarding the author's proposed condition, he writes that one certainly cannot condition the minor's own money to become a gift, and money from others is no repair because it is a personal obligation, unless the money is acquired for the minor on condition that it be used for pidyon. One can convey and acquire on condition: if he redeems himself in adulthood, it is a gift to the kohen; but if he does not redeem himself in adulthood, it is now a gift to the son on condition that he be redeemed with it. On this his view agrees, for whatever can be repaired to fulfill the Torah's mitzvah is good; see there.
