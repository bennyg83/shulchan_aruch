(Likkut) If there is no, etc. So wrote the Tur, and the Beit Yosef wrote that it is known that the halakhah follows the sages. At first glance his words are not precise, for although the sages dispute Rabbi Yehudah, Rav and Shmuel also dispute this matter with Rabbi Yochanan and Reish Lakish in Kiddushin 13, and Rav Pappa rules there that lien is Torah law. The Tur here follows Tosafot there, s.v. “Rav Pappa said,” in the name of Rabbenu Tam. But Rif and Rosh at the end of Bava Batra explain the case as a loan and answer the difficulty there differently, that the sages left it on its Torah law so as not to lock the door before borrowers. According to this, for a debt written in the Torah one could say it remains on Torah law, since it is uncommon and we do not worry about loss to purchasers. After analysis, however, one may say that even according to Rif, Rav Pappa spoke even of a debt written in the Torah, since he spoke plainly. This also appears from Bekhorot 49, where it says that all agree with Rav Pappa, etc. That passage also supports Rif's explanation, for it must then say there that all do not accept Rav Asi, etc.; according to Tosafot it would have needed only to cite Rav Pappa of Kiddushin. Rather, Rav Pappa spoke generally of a loan, and if a debt written in the Torah is not like a written document, it is included in Rav Pappa's rule. So wrote Rashal, unlike Shach in Choshen Mishpat 39 s.k. 2. There is no proof from Choshen Mishpat 119:4, for damages are different because they generate publicity, as stated in Bava Kamma 33b, and so wrote the Rashba. Still, Rambam, Hilkhot Malveh chapter 19, is unlike Tosafot in Kiddushin and Bekhorot, who included damages too and therefore had to press the passage in Bava Kamma 8a. With this the Beit Yosef's words above are correct: specifically because the halakhah follows the first tanna, the kohen does not collect.
