If he became a treifah. This appears to refer to a definite treifah. In such a case, even if he lived a long time afterward he is exempt from pidyon haben, and we do not say “a treifah cannot live” against a definite treifah. But in a doubtful treifah he is exempt from pidyon because one who seeks to extract bears the burden of proof. However, if he then lived twelve months he is obligated, for he has left the doubt of treifah by living twelve months, as above siman 55 s.k. 48. So it appears to me.
But he should not recite Shehecheyanu. This is not comparable to what is explained in Orach Chayim 223:2, that although his father died he recites Shehecheyanu over the inheritance. There it is different, since he acquires the inheritance; here that does not apply.
