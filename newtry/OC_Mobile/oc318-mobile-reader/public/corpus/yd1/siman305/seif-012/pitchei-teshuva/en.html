Accustomed to return. See She'elat Yaavetz I, siman 155, who wrote that nowadays the law has changed. What they said, that the kohen should not be accustomed to doing this, applied to their pedigreed kohanim; but with kohanim who are kohanim only by presumption, as today, although we are stringent with them in all matters and redeem firstborns through them because there is no alternative, we are not lenient with them to extract the father's money from doubt. One could almost say that by law they must return it, and at least every kohen should be concerned for himself and avoid possible theft, lest he is not a kohen. For this reason it appears proper nowadays to perform pidyon lechatchilah on condition of return. He further writes that to escape every possible doubt, the father should also redeem his bekhor through every kohen he can find, lest he encounter a definitely pedigreed kohen. For this reason, too, the son of a kohenet or leviyah should now be redeemed out of doubt, for it is no different from any Torah doubt where what can be repaired should be repaired; see there. Responsa Chatam Sofer, end of siman 291, wrote briefly that the words of She'elat Yaavetz in this matter do not appear correct to him. See what I wrote above, siman 322 s.k. 3, regarding this.
The son died within thirty days. See Dagul MeRevavah, s.v. “and there is not,” who wrote that if the father wishes to travel within thirty days, he should appoint an agent to redeem. But for the father to redeem wherever he is on day thirty-one, in my opinion he cannot recite the berachah, for perhaps the son died at home. Giving the child a presumption of life is also questionable, since as long as thirty days have not passed he does not have a presumption of life, for perhaps he is a nefel. One could discuss whether it helps as a presumption that comes from a majority, since most infants are not nefalim; the matter requires study. Therefore the best path is for him to appoint an agent here. End quote. See Responsa Beit Shmuel Acharon, Yoreh De'ah siman 29, regarding one who left his wife in one city and went to another country, and they wrote to him that his wife gave birth to a firstborn. Is he obligated to redeem his son after thirty days from birth, although he does not know whether the child is alive? He concludes that he certainly may redeem and recite the berachah, and there is no concern that the child died within thirty days, for we establish him on his presumption, and a presumption that comes from a majority is a presumption; see there at length. Responsa Chatam Sofer, siman 294 and the end of siman 295, also challenges Dagul MeRevavah from the end of the first chapter of Arakhin and concludes that it is better for the father to redeem him personally where he is than through an agent. He saw this practiced by his teacher, R. Natan Adler zt"l; see there.
Treifah. See Baer Heitev, who writes in the name of the Shach that in a doubtful treifah who lived twelve months one is obligated to redeem him. See Responsa Tur HaEven, siman 12, which analyzes this law.
