Or two males, etc. The Gemara there, 49a: Rav Huna taught, etc. Rashi's view is that it refers to what is stated in seif 29, two women of two men who had not previously borne; this is what the Gemara means by “and our tanna, etc.” But the Rosh wrote and marked the case “a male and female, the kohen has nothing,” then Rav Huna taught “two males and a female, the kohen has nothing.” So too wrote the Rambam and Raavad. The Raavad holds it refers only to the latter clause, to what is written here in seif 31, as stated there, “and our tanna, since with two, etc.” The Rambam wrote that it refers only to the first clause, to two wives of one man, one of whom had previously borne and one of whom had not, as in seif 28; his version read, “and our tanna, since with one man, etc.” But the Tur explains that it refers to both, for there is no reason to distinguish between them, and therefore he wrote it here and in seif 28. Yet what the Gemara says there, “and our tanna, etc.,” is difficult according to his view; he followed the Rosh's words, which did not mention that line.
