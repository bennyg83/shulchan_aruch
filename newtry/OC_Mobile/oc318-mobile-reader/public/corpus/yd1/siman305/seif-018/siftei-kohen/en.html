And the son of a kohenet is obligated in redemption. When he grows up, for the kohenet is not obligated to redeem him, since a woman is not obligated to redeem her son, as above in seif 2. The Israelite husband of the kohenet is obviously not obligated to redeem him, for he is not his father, since she became pregnant from the non-Jew. According to this, if she did not become pregnant from the non-Jew, but was only disqualified by relations with the non-Jew and afterward became pregnant from a Jew, the son is obligated in redemption.
The son is exempt from redemption. Even when he grows up, for perhaps she is lying and the Israelite is telling the truth, so the kohen is the claimant and must bring proof. This is the language of the Atteret Zahav, but it is an error. On the contrary, if the Israelite is telling the truth, the son is obligated, because she was disqualified by relations with a non-Jew. It must read: perhaps he is lying and she is telling the truth. Nevertheless, according to this reason it seems at first glance that even with his married or betrothed wife the law would be the same. But in Terumat HaDeshen, which is the source of this law, it does not sound so. He wrote: we do not attribute the child to him in any respect at all. Nevertheless, even according to him we believe him only for this matter, that the child is not his; but to believe all his words and say that she became pregnant from a non-Jew as he says, certainly we do not say. Nor is he believed because of Rabbi Yehudah's rule that a father is believed about a son among his sons to say he is a mamzer, for that credibility seems to apply only to his son born from his wife. In that case the Torah believed him to identify him to others, since he was born from his wife or betrothed, and ordinarily he would be attributed to him. When he says he is not from him and is a mamzer, he is believed. But here, where he has neither marriage nor kiddushin with her, how could one think he should be believed about a child born from her? These are his words. Since he did not distinguish that Rabbi Yehudah's case is different because she does not contradict him, it seems he understood that once the father is believed to say of his son among his sons that he is a mamzer, he is believed in every case. If so, even if she contradicts him, since he is believed to say the child is not his, automatically he is a mamzer, for she is a married woman. Although he concludes there at the end of the responsum, "all the more so regarding the five sela, for this son has monetary possession," etc., nevertheless it appears from his words that this "all the more so" is only an additional support to exempt him from redemption, not that even with his wife there is a distinction whether she contradicts him or not. According to this, with his wife, since he is believed to say the child is not his, even if she contradicts him the child is automatically a mamzer, she was disqualified by the relations with the adulterer, and the son is obligated in redemption when he grows up. The practical law still requires study.
