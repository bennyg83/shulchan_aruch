Exempt from the pidyon. See Be'er Heitev of the Rav Maharit of blessed memory, what he wrote that the Shach left this law as requiring further study. And one who looks in the Shach will see that it is not so, but rather he left as requiring further study whether also regarding his wife who is married or an arusah the law is thus; see there. And see in Sha'ar HaMelech chapter 15 of Hilchot Issurei Bi'ah, end of halacha 17, after he brought the opinion of many of the Rishonim that one who says "this is my son, a mamzer" is believed even when she contradicts him, and he wrote: and I am astonished at the Shach in Yoreh De'ah siman 305 se'if katan 23, that the matter is in doubt for him whether this law of Rabbi Yehudah who said a person is believed to say "this is my son, a mamzer" is even when she contradicts him, and he proved so from the words of the Rav Terumat HaDeshen, and in the end he left the matter as requiring further study; and how did the company of prophets that we wrote escape him, who hold that even when she contradicts him he is believed, except according to the opinion of Tosafot Rid; and nevertheless it appears that in his case he is exempt from pidyon, since according to the opinion of Tosafot Rid it is true, and also according to the opinion of Bahag and Rabbeinu Tam that Maran the Beit Yosef wrote in Even HaEzer siman 4, that they hold that specifically when there is recognition of a bechor he is believed to make him a mamzer, but otherwise he is not believed; also according to the opinion of Riaz that he wrote in Shiltei Giborim on the Mishnah of "this is my son, a mamzer," he holds that specifically when he says "this is my son and he is a mamzer" he is believed, but when he says "he is not my son" he is not believed; and if so he can say "I hold like those rabbis," and the one who would take from his fellow must bring proof; so it appears to me as the halacha, end of his words:
