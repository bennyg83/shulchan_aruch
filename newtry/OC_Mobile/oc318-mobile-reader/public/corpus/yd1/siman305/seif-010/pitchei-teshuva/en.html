What do you prefer. See in the teshuvah of Chut HaSheni siman 92, who wrote that the meaning is not that he gives him the choice, for if the father wanted to leave the son to the kohen he does not fulfill with this, as the Rama wrote nearby; rather, in order to endear the pidyon to his father he says this, to give him desire to redeem him, for perhaps his heart hesitates to redeem him with so much money, as if he were coerced by the speech, and then his son is not redeemed, as R' Chanina said to that man (in Bechorot daf 51b): you have not concluded that you gave anything; you have done badly; therefore his son is not redeemed. Therefore, so that he give willingly, the kohen says to him "with what do you prefer more," because it is said "for Mine is every bechor," and if the father desires not to redeem him he should be concerned lest the Holy One, blessed be He, return and take him from him etc.; see there. (And according to my humble opinion this requires further study: what he wrote that if the father gives the money as if coerced by the speech, his son is not redeemed — this chiddush we have not heard; and see above siman 61 seif 24, there it is stated that we place him in niddui etc., and one can distinguish. And the proof he brought from R' Chanina I did not understand, for there the father's intent was on condition to return, and R' Chanina did not want to acquire except with a complete gift, as Tosafot and the Asheri wrote there; therefore his son is not redeemed, which is not so here.) And he wrote further there as obvious that we do not compel the father to redeem his son, for since we learn from what is written "you shall surely redeem," that if his father did not redeem him he is obligated to redeem himself, it is as if the Torah gave him a choice that he can remove the mitzvah of pidyon haben from himself and leave it to the son himself, and it resembles a negative commandment that is remedied by a positive commandment, end of his words. And the teshuvah of Chacham Tzvi already challenged him, brought above se'if katan 1. And it is obvious that if the son has grown, certainly even according to the view of Chut HaSheni we compel the son to redeem himself.
Through an agent. See Be'er Heitev that the Acharonim disagreed about this and wrote that he can redeem through an agent (and so he concluded in the book Sha'ar HaMelech at the end of chapter 6 of Hilchot Ishut, and he proved this from what is stated regarding a condition, as it is stated in the first chapter of Kiddushin, and above seif 8, for if not so the condition would be void because we require a condition that it is possible to fulfill through an agent; see there, and see what I wrote above siman 234 se'if katan 2 and in siman 236 se'if katan 1) [and see in the responsa of Chatam Sofer siman 293 and 294, it is explained there that nevertheless they are not accustomed to do it through an agent, for it is proper to make a commemoration of the firstborn of Egypt, as it is written "I and not the agent"; so too one should redeem himself more than with other mitzvot, regarding which there is also more of a mitzvah in him than in his agent; see there. And there in siman 257 he wrote that the matter of agency about which they disagreed is where the agent gives money of his own and does not confer his money upon the father, but rather wants to redeem So-and-so's son with his own money by agency of So-and-so, and the father will fulfill the obligation of pidyon by reason of "his agent is like him," and like one who separates from his own produce for So-and-so's heap; and in this it is obvious to the questioner in the Rivash that he is not redeemed through an agent; but money sent from the owners to the kohen is not pidyon through an agent, but rather the arrival of money through an agent, and according to everyone there is nothing wrong with that, and many have erred in this; see there further. And similarly the Gaon of Lissa of blessed memory also wrote in the responsa Chemdat Shlomo siman 32, and his opinion is that the main ruling is like the Rama of blessed memory; see there] And see in Dagul Mervavah who wrote that all this is when he appoints the agent after thirty days, but to appoint an agent within thirty that he should redeem him at his time is not proper, etc. And even so I say that the father should make an agent if he wants to go on his way, and then the agent can redeem him even if we say that the agency did not take effect then; nevertheless he now becomes his agent by reason of "we confer benefit upon a person in his absence," for we see that it is agreeable to him, etc., end of his words [see there, and see what I wrote above se'if katan 18] And see in the responsa Pnei Aryeh siman 44 who wrote that a kohen (or a Levi, and possibly the same applies to the husband of a kohenet and a Leviyah; see seif 18; however, there is room to distinguish) cannot l'chatchila be an agent in place of the father to redeem his son, since he himself is exempt from pidyon haben he is not fit to be an agent, for regarding a matter that is not fitting for himself he cannot perform agency for others; but b'dieved if the bechor was already redeemed through a kohen agent, it is possible that he need not go back and redeem himself when he grows up; see there [and see in the responsa of Chatam Sofer siman 296, he wrote at first that it is possible that Levites would be invalid for this agency, but a simple kohen is certainly valid for agency because he is the one with whom they redeem, and it is like a man and a woman who are both valid for agency of a get, whether for delivery or for receipt, even though the man is not divorced and the woman does not divorce; nevertheless both are called as being involved in divorce; only for another reason it is not proper that the kohen be an agent to redeem through another kohen, for it appears as disparaging his own kehunah, as is written regarding a kohen sheliach tzibbur that another should not be called (see in Orach Chaim siman 135 seif 11). And then he wrote nevertheless, as a matter of law it appears to me simple, since a kohen and a Levi are bound by a positive commandment to redeem the firstborn of Israel, and even a community that are all Levites and among them there are firstborn of Israel they are obligated to redeem him from their own money; if so they also become agents for pidyon of firstborns; see there] And see in Tzlach on Pesachim daf 121b s.v. R' Simlai, who wrote that if he redeems through an agent, certainly the berachah of Shehecheyanu does not apply through an agent, and also the father cannot bless, and the berachah of Shehecheyanu is nullified when he redeems through an agent; see there. And so he wrote in the responsa Pnei Aryeh there, that the agent should not bless Shehecheyanu, but rather blesses "Who sanctified us with His commandments and commanded us concerning pidyon of the bechor"; see there [however in the responsa of Chatam Sofer siman 298 he wrote that in pidyon haben where the father is not here, the agent should bless the two berachot: on pidyon of the son, and Shehecheyanu on his own mitzvah; and nevertheless, to be extra good, if he finds something new such as a fruit, he should place it before him to take himself out of doubt; but the main ruling is to bless Shehecheyanu; and then he found this explicitly in the Hagahot Perishah; see there] And see further in the responsa Zichron Yosef, Yoreh De'ah, end of siman 26, and in the responsa Tiferet Tzvi, Yoreh De'ah siman 42, and see in the responsa Yad Eliyahu siman 51 and in the responsa Bigdei Kehunah, Yoreh De'ah siman 11. And what Be'er Heitev wrote in the name of the Taz if the father died, etc., see on this in Shu"t Yeri'ot HaOhel siman 15, who elaborated and refuted all the words of Nekudot HaKesef and concluded like the opinion of the Taz that if the father died the Beit Din cannot redeem him, and so he ruled as the practical halacha; see there [also in the responsa of Chatam Sofer siman 295 he analyzed the aforementioned words of Nekudot HaKesef and concluded that to be extra good, Beit Din should redeem him with a berachah without Name and Kingship, with money of the orphan, or they should confer the money upon him and give it to the kohen as a gift on condition to return, and they should hang on him a silver plate on which is engraved "safek redeemed," and when he grows up and brings two hairs he should go back and redeem himself; see there. And see in the responsa Chemdat Shlomo siman 31, who also elaborated on this and concluded that it is very difficult to redeem in childhood and one should not act in this, and sitting and not doing is preferable; but since the Shach wrote that most times the plate is lost and one comes to nullification of a mitzvah, therefore it appears to invent a takanah to satisfy all opinions, namely to hang a plate and write that he was not redeemed, as the Rama wrote, and to confer the coins upon the kohen (as a gift, end of his words, so it appears there) on condition that if when the son grows up it is not known to him that he is a bechor and he does not redeem himself, the coins now shall be for the sake of pidyon, and if it is known to him that he is a bechor he shall be obligated to redeem himself and the coins now shall be for the sake of a gift; see there. And in siman 32 there is a responsum of the Gaon of Lissa of blessed memory on this matter, and his opinion is that the main ruling is that they can redeem him from the property of a minor, because the money of pidyon haben is not similar to other mitzvot, for with other mitzvot there is no lien on property, but with pidyon haben there is a lien on property, and here it is not by reason of arevut, but rather that in the body of the property the kohen has a share, since seizure is effective; and since seizure is effective that he be redeemed, all the more so Beit Din collect from the property of the minor; and even so, in practice this requires further study against the reasoning of the Taz. And regarding the takanah that the Gaon the author of blessed memory invented, to redeem now on a condition, he of blessed memory wrote that with the minor's money it is certainly impossible to stipulate that they be gift-money, and with others' money there is no takanah since it is a bodily obligation (see what is written at the beginning of this se'if katan), unless they confer the coins upon the minor for the purpose of redeeming with them, and they can transfer and confer on condition that if he redeems himself in adulthood it shall be a gift to the kohen, and if he does not redeem himself in adulthood it shall be from now a gift to the son for the purpose that he be redeemed with them. And in this his opinion also agrees, for whatever it is possible to enact to fulfill a mitzvah of the Merciful One, how good; see there]:
