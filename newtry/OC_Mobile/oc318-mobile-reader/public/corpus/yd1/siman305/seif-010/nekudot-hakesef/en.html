(Siman 305, Taz s.k. 11) He challenged Tzedah LaDerekh, but in truth his proof is sound. The Gemara in Ketubot teaches that a minor convert is immersed by beit din because one may benefit a person outside his presence. There is no objection that this is a liability because the father or child would prefer to perform the mitzvah himself. Since the Rambam, Semag, and Tur rule that where another separates terumah for him, the terumah is valid, the fact that the mitzvah is his is not treated as a disqualifying liability. Tosafot in Ketubot called it only a slight liability because perhaps he would have preferred to exempt with one grain of wheat or to add more, not because he wanted to perform the mitzvah himself. Here too, when beit din redeems the child, they first transfer the money to the minor, and he is redeemed with his own money, so the mitzvah remains his. A proof comes from Gittin 52, where an apotropos for orphans separates tithes to feed them. The Rashba explains that by law the hand of the apotropos is like the hand of the orphans; only “to set aside” was restricted by rabbinic decree, lest he appear careless with orphans' property. The Rambam, Tur, and Shulchan Aruch imply this latter explanation of the Rashba, that the core law permits the apotropos to tithe and the distinction between feeding and setting aside is a special takanah for orphans, not a defect in terumah itself. Furthermore, beit din can acquire for a minor even where a slight liability accompanies the benefit. Kiddushin 42 derives from “one prince from each tribe” that beit din may act for minors, and Rashi explains that a liability that comes together with benefit may be done by beit din. This answers Tosafot's difficulty in Ketubot, who struggled because zechiyah functions through agency and a minor has no agency: beit din is different. And even regarding ordinary acquisition, there is strong basis that a minor has Torah-level zechiyah where another mind transfers to him. The Mordechai in Bava Metzia and Maharam discuss a non-Jew, who lacks zechiyah because he lacks agency; perhaps this only means one must belong to the category of agency. The Ran in Kiddushin, Tosafot in Sanhedrin regarding ben sorer, Tosafot in Gittin 64, the Rashba there, Tosafot in Kiddushin 19, the Rosh there, and the Ritva in Ketubot and Kiddushin all indicate that a minor can acquire on a Torah level where there is another mind conveying to him. Accordingly, beit din may redeem the minor bekhor, and the Taz's objection does not stand.
