(siman 305, Taz se'if katan 11) He raised a difficulty against the Tzeda LaDerech and elaborated with words of the lips, but it is superfluous, for all the words of the author of Tzeda LaDerech are correct, and his proof is clear from that of the first chapter of Ketubot: we immerse a minor convert according to the mind of beit din, for we acquire a benefit for a person in his absence. And one cannot say that here it is somewhat a liability, that he wants to perform the mitzvah of the pidyon himself — for since the Rambam, the Semag, and the Tur ruled that his terumah is terumah, it is proven that [the view] "the mitzvah is his" is no reasoning at all; for one cannot say that the reasoning is that it is somewhat a liability; rather, nevertheless, the reasoning of benefit is better; for if so, the Gemara should have raised a doubt about this, and from the fact that the Gemara stated "or perhaps the mitzvah is his" etc., it implies that the Gemara only doubts whether this reasoning that "the mitzvah is his" is considered a liability or not; and since we hold that his terumah is terumah, therefore necessarily "the mitzvah is his" is no reasoning at all. And so it is proven from what Tosafot wrote in Ketubot: that it is somewhat a liability, for perhaps he wants to exempt with one wheat kernel or to give extra. And if it were so, behold even if he would not want to exempt with one wheat kernel or to give extra, it would be somewhat a liability, that he wants to perform the mitzvah himself; rather, certainly this is not even called somewhat a liability. And furthermore, from Tosafot's writing: "but here, where it is a complete benefit for him, he has shelichut" — if so, the same applies here, for regarding immersion of a convert one can also say that it is somewhat a liability, that he wants to convert himself and perform this mitzvah himself, and even so we immerse him according to the mind of beit din; we learn that this is not even called somewhat a liability; if so, the same applies here. And this is the intent of the author of Tzeda LaDerech, and unlike what he wrote who peeked through the cracks in this; rather, the opposite: that he is the one who peeked etc.: And furthermore it appears to me that here it is not applicable to say that it would be a liability because he would want to perform the mitzvah himself. For only there, when he separates terumah from his own for his fellow's, it is applicable to say so, since he separates from his own and not from his fellow's; but here, when beit din redeem him, they acquire the money for the minor, for we acquire a benefit for a person in his absence, and if so they are redeeming him with the minor's money; and likewise when the minor already has money anyway; and if so, the mitzvah is the minor's. And therefore Tosafot needed to say "perhaps he wants to exempt with one wheat kernel or to give extra," and they did not write that it is somewhat a liability because he wants the mitzvah to be his, because since they separate from the minor's, the mitzvah is his. But in Nedarim it is dealing with a case where he does not acquire it for him, and only in that [case] does the Gemara raise the question; and if so, even if you say the question was not resolved, nevertheless here beit din redeem him when they first acquire the money for the minor. And it appears to me to bring a clear proof for this from that which we learned in HaNizakin daf 52a: a guardian of orphans is obligated to tithe; and it is in the Gemara there that that is to feed, but not to store. And he wrote in Chiddushei HaRashba: it makes no difference whether the orphans' father appointed him or beit din appointed him — he separates terumah by law; and that which was taught "you" and not guardians is a mere asmachta, for by law he separates both to feed and to store, for the guardian's hand is like the orphans' hand; rather, so that he not appear as treating orphans' property lightly, lest they not be precise in the matter, they forbade them to separate terumah to store, since they have no need for this; but to feed they left it according to the law, end of his words. Hence it is permitted for a guardian to separate terumah by law even to store, and we do not say perhaps the minor specifically wants himself [to perform] the mitzvah and it is a liability for him; rather, certainly since they separate from his, the mitzvah is his; if so it is not considered a liability because of this; and if so regarding pidyon as well, when beit din acquire for him, the mitzvah is his and it is not a liability. And even according to the first explanation that the Rashba explained initially, if storing is forbidden by law from the verse "you" and not guardians, one can say that that is specifically regarding a guardian, and for the reason the Rashba wrote there: since there is no enactment for the orphans here, they did not appoint them for that. And if so, the guardians are not their agents; but where beit din acquire for him and become his agents explicitly for everything, certainly they can redeem him. Or alternatively, specifically regarding terumah and maaser, and for the reason Tosafot wrote in Ketubot above: perhaps he wants to exempt with one wheat kernel or to give extra — which is not so here regarding pidyon: And furthermore it appears to me proven from the Rambam and the Tur and Shulchan Aruch that they hold like the Rashba's latter explanation, for the Rambam in chapter 4 of Hilchot Terumot law 10, and the Tur and Shulchan Aruch in siman 331 seif 38, wrote: the guardians separate terumah from orphans' property, and they did not distinguish between feeding and storing. And even though the Kesef Mishneh there wrote that he did not bother to write it here because he relied on what he wrote at the end of Hilchot Nachalot — it does not appear so, for nevertheless he should have explained here also regarding terumah. And furthermore, if so it is difficult regarding the Tur, who wrote nothing of this in Hilchot Nachalot; rather, certainly they hold that the verse is a mere asmachta, and by law he separates in all cases, and because of an enactment for orphans they should not tithe to store, as the Rashba wrote; and this is not relevant to the laws of terumah, only to the enactment for orphans; and therefore he did not distinguish in this in Hilchot Terumah, for there he only brings the laws of terumah; but in Hilchot Nachalot, where he deals with the enactment for orphans, he distinguished in this. And the Tur did not need to write this in Hilchot Nachalot because he wrote there several laws that are taught in the baraita, that the guardians should enact to feed and not to store; and if so the same applies to terumah. Or alternatively, since terumah is not practiced, they did not write it in Hilchot Nachalot. Even though in Siftei Kohen siman 331 se'if katan 68 I wrote that their separating terumah is to feed etc. — that is because I followed the plain meaning of the Gemara of HaNizakin according to the Semag in Hilchot Terumot daf 197, and the Kesef Mishneh and the Taz, who distinguished thus; and even according to this we learn that in our case regarding pidyon haben, beit din can redeem him, as I wrote above (and what the Semag wrote at the end of daf 266 is rejected from all that I have written; see there): And furthermore it appears clear that beit din can acquire for a minor even in a place where it is somewhat a liability, as is proven explicitly at the beginning of the chapter HaIsh Mekadesh (Kiddushin daf 42), where we read there: but that which Rav Giddel said in the name of Rav, from where [do we know] that a person's agent is like himself, as it is said "and one nasi and one nasi from a tribe"? Let me derive shelichut from here! And do you hold that this is shelichut? But minors are not subject to shelichut! Rather, it is like that which Rav Giddel said in the name of Rav: from where [do we know] that we acquire a benefit for a person in his absence, as it is said "and one nasi"? And do you hold it is a benefit? Behold there is also a liability, for there is one for whom a mountain is preferable and a valley is not, and there is one for whom a valley is preferable and a mountain is not! Rather, it is like Rava bar Rav Huna, for Rava bar Rav Huna said in the name of Rav Giddel in the name of Rav: from where [do we know] that when orphans come to divide their father's property, beit din appoint guardians for them for liability and for benefit — for liability, why? Rather, for liability in order to benefit; the verse states "and one nasi from a tribe you shall take," end of the words of the Gemara. And Rashi explained: like Rava — meaning, not merely benefit in his absence is to be derived from it for adults, but also to teach that for minors, even a liability that comes on account of a benefit, beit din are permitted to do for them etc., end of his words. Behold it is explicit that beit din can acquire for a minor even in a place where it is also a liability: And from here I have a difficulty as well regarding Tosafot in the first chapter of Ketubot daf 11a, who raised a difficulty on that which we say there: we immerse a minor convert according to the mind of beit din, and this is their language: and it is astonishing, for zechiyah is by reason of shelichut, for since it is a benefit for him we are witnesses that he makes him an agent, as is proven in the first chapter of Bava Metzia regarding a courtyard, because it was included as a "hand" and is no worse than shelichut; and if so how do we acquire for a minor, seeing that a minor has no shelichut, as we say in Eizehu Neshech etc.; and they entered there into strained explanations, as is clear from their words there. And according to me there is no difficulty at all, for beit din are different: even in a place where a minor has no shelichut and zechiyah, beit din can acquire for a minor. And furthermore I have a difficulty with what Tosafot answered there: that immersing him is d'rabbanan, for even where it is a complete benefit he has no zechiyah d'oraisa — for a convert is a complete benefit, as Tosafot themselves wrote there and at the end of the comment; and this is certainly not so, as is clear in the aforementioned Gemara of Kiddushin. And likewise regarding the other answer that Tosafot conclude there: that where it is somewhat a liability we do not acquire for a minor, only where it is a complete benefit we acquire for him, for then he has the law of shelichut — it is difficult, for in the aforementioned Gemara it implies that where it is a complete benefit we acquire for him even if he has no shelichut; and so according to my humble opinion is the main [view]. And with this it is also well that there is no difficulty regarding immersing him: even though a minor has no shelichut, nevertheless he has zechiyah even d'oraisa. And the main [view] is like the sefarim that Tosafot cited there, that they do not read in Eizehu Neshech "he has zechiyah d'rabbanan"; and so I found in a Gemara of parchment, an old ancient manuscript. And even according to the sefarim that read there "d'rabbanan," that is in a place where it is somewhat a liability. And from the first chapter of Bava Metzia as well it appears to my humble opinion that there is no difficulty: we say there only that a courtyard was included as her "hand" and is no worse than shelichut, and we acquire a benefit for a person in his absence with a gift; but indeed, even where there is no shelichut, a minor has zechiyah; see there and examine. In the end, I did not grasp the depth of Tosafot's words in this: Again I found in the Mordechai, first chapter of Bava Metzia, he wrote, and this is his language: a courtyard was included by reason of shelichut etc., and so we say in the chapter Eizehu Neshech that zechiyah was included by reason of shelichut; and from here Rabbeinu Meir ruled that a non-Jew, who has no shelichut, has no zechiyah, and if Reuven said to Shimon, acquire those coins for the non-Jew, the non-Jew has not acquired them until they come into his hand, end of his words. And apparently this is like Tosafot's view in Ketubot; but one can say that Rabbeinu Meir holds that where he comes within the category of shelichut he has zechiyah, and that which it says that zechiyah was included by reason of shelichut means that we require that he come within the category of shelichut; and therefore a non-Jew, since he does not come within the category of shelichut, has no zechiyah, as is clear in the Gemara of the chapter Eizehu Neshech: Again I saw in the Ran, chapter HaIsh Mekadesh, who also wrote that there are those who learn from the sugya there that zechiyah is not by reason of shelichut, and a minor has zechiyah d'oraisa even though he has no shelichut; except that the Ran rejected their words from what we say in Eizehu Neshech that a minor has zechiyah d'rabbanan, implying that d'oraisa he does not; and he strained to reconcile the sugya of the chapter HaIsh Mekadesh, and wrote that according to the conclusion he has no zechiyah d'oraisa. And according to my humble opinion this is strained, for we receded from the original sugya only from what it asked "behold there is also a liability," but if it is a complete benefit he has zechiyah d'oraisa. And what he brought from the chapter Eizehu Neshech — Tosafot already wrote that there are sefarim that do not read "d'rabbanan"; therefore the main [view] is like the "some say" that the Ran cited, as it appears to me: Again I found explicitly like my words in Tosafot at the beginning of the chapter Ben Sorer U'Moreh, who wrote that a minor has zechiyah d'oraisa where another mind conveys to him; and I searched in the other poskim and found that Tosafot wrote so again in Gittin, chapter HaMekabel, at the end (Gittin daf 64), and the Rashba in his chiddushim there, and Tosafot wrote so again in Kiddushin daf 19a, and the Rosh there siman 25 at length; and I placed joy in my heart, and also that the aforementioned Tosafot and poskim brought other proofs for this; see there. And according to my humble opinion there is also a clear proof from this sugya of Rav Giddel in the chapter HaIsh Mekadesh that I cited, as I wrote: And again I found [like my words] in the words of the Ritva, first chapter of Ketubot and the chapter HaIsh Mekadesh, and see there.
