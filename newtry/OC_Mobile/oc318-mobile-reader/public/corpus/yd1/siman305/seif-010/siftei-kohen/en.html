At the time that he gives, etc. That is, when the father is holding the five selaim in his hand and extends them to the kohen, before removing them from his hand into the kohen's hand, he recites the berachah and afterward gives them to him, so that he recites the berachah before performing the mitzvah. So wrote the Beit Yosef and the later authorities, and this is also implied by the words of the Tur and the Rav below, who write explicitly that he gives him the money, etc.
And he recites Shehecheyanu, etc. This implies that he always recites Shehecheyanu. See above, siman 265 seif 6.
And the father cannot redeem through an agent, etc. This is astonishing, for we rule throughout the Torah that a person's agent is like himself. So too it is explicit in the Ran, chapter Kol Sha'ah, that he can redeem through an agent. Maadanei Melekh, at the end of page 266, also wondered at this at length and wrote that he may redeem through an agent. So too in the book Tzedah LaDerekh by R. Yissachar Ber on Rashi's commentary to the Chumash, he wrote at length in one responsum that several great sages of the generation agreed with him that one can redeem through an agent. He wrote that even the Rivash meant only that we have not found a Torah obligation upon another person to make himself an agent in place of the father to redeem the child if the father does not wish to redeem him or if the father died, and that even beit din is not obligated by Torah law to make themselves agents in place of the father to redeem him. But certainly the father can redeem through an agent, and beit din can redeem him without the father if the father transgressed and did not redeem him, or if the father died before the child was redeemed; all the more so the father's father can redeem him in such a case. These are his words; see there at length. So too Maharshal ruled in the name of Maharam Mintz, as cited in Derishah seif 14, that he may redeem through an agent. Maadanei Melekh wrote there that beit din cannot redeem him when the father died while he is a minor, for as long as he is a minor he is not subject to obligation, etc. His words are not compelling; see below s.k. 20.
