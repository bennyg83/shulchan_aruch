(Seif 10 in the gloss) And the father cannot redeem through an agent. In Shaar HaMelekh, Hilkhot Ishut 6, he wondered from the law in seif 8: if he gave a gift on condition that it be returned, his son is redeemed, and this is an explicit sugya in the first chapter of Kiddushin. If it is true that one cannot redeem through an agent, then even if the money was not returned it should be effective, since whenever an act cannot be performed through an agent the condition is void and the act stands. He left this requiring study. In my humble opinion this is not difficult. Certainly, according to this view, “he cannot redeem through an agent” means that the agent cannot perform the act of pidyon by saying to the kohen, “With this money the son shall be redeemed.” But it is obvious that the father may convey the money to the kohen through an agent while the father himself says, “With this money that I am conveying to you through the agent, my son shall be redeemed.” Accordingly, in a gift on condition of return, the condition is not on the pidyon, as though if he does not return it the child will not be redeemed. Rather, the condition is on the transfer of the money: he acquires it on condition that he return it, and if he does not return it he does not acquire the money. That condition is valid, for conveyance can be done through an agent, while the father says to the kohen that with the money I have transferred to you on condition of return, the son shall be redeemed.
(Shach s.k. 12) That they are accustomed to redeem on a fast day. See Magen Avraham, siman 565 s.k. 10, and Responsa Pri Megadim, part 2, siman 95.
(There) And to make the meal at night. The reason for the custom not to make the pidyon and the meal together on the night of the thirty-first may be that sometimes twenty-nine days, twelve hours, and 793 parts have not yet elapsed, where the child was born at the end of the day. Therefore, if it was delayed until day thirty-two, and day thirty-two is a fast day, one should perform the pidyon and make the meal on the night before the fast. Responsa Noda BiYehudah, second edition, Yoreh De'ah siman 186.
