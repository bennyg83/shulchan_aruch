To redeem, etc. As is written in the first chapter of Pesachim (7b): the father of the son, etc., so too here, etc. And as is written above siman 265, and the same applies here. But regarding pidyon of his son it is explicit in the Gemara at the end of Pesachim that he blesses "al," and the reason see Bahag, and so the Rivash wrote according to his view. But Rashi wrote there that in the conclusion there is no distinction, and with this the [sugya] at the end of Pesachim is well, and one need not strange distinctions, for to circumcise him and to redeem him they are equal.
And some wrote, etc. Bava Kamma chapter 1, according to Rashi there and the Arukh, and see Tosafot of Moed Katan 8b s.v. "because, etc."
And the father is not, etc. And also, etc. See Shach. (Likut) And the father is not, etc. And also, etc. All this is astonishing, for it is not necessary to say regarding through an agent, for a person's agent is like himself; rather, even another can redeem even without the father's knowledge, for it is no better than what is written that another can redeem, as is written in chapter 4 of Maaser Sheni, and likewise neta revai as is written there in chapter 5, and likewise hekdesh as is written in Erakhin. And all the more so here, where it is only a mere debt, for the kohen collects from encumbered property, as is written in Bekhorot 48a–b, and there "the properties were encumbered, etc." And see there the entire sugya, and in Kiddushin 29b and in Tosafot there: and does one who pays another's debt not exempt the borrower? Rather, l'chatchila the mitzvah is more [incumbent] on the father, like milah and other things that the father is obligated in. And also in the Rivash there is nothing of this except in the words of the questioner there, and his words are also not compelling. And see Taz and Shach (etc.).
