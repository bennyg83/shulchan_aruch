To redeem, etc. As stated in the first chapter of Pesachim, 7b, “the father of the son,” etc.; so too here, and as above in siman 265, and the same applies here. But for redeeming his son it is explicit at the end of Pesachim that he recites “on,” and for the reason see Baer HaGolah; so wrote the Rivash according to his view. Rashi, however, wrote there that in the conclusion there is no distinction, and with this the passage at the end of Pesachim is settled and there is no need for forced distinctions, for circumcising him and redeeming him are equal.
And some wrote, etc. Bava Kamma, chapter 1, according to Rashi there and the Arukh; and see Tosafot, Moed Katan 8b, s.v. “because.”
And the father may not, etc.; and also, etc. See Shach.
(Likkut) And the father may not, etc.; and also, etc. All this is astonishing. Not only through an agent, for a person's agent is like himself, but even another person can redeem without the father's knowledge. It is no worse than what is said that another may redeem, as in Maaser Sheni chapter 4, and similarly with neta revai in chapter 5, and hekdesh as stated in Arakhin. All the more so here, where it is only a debt, for the kohen collects from encumbered property, as stated in Bekhorot 48a-b: the property became encumbered, etc.; see the whole sugya there, and Kiddushin 29b with Tosafot there. Does one who pays another person's debt not exempt the borrower? Rather, lechatchilah it is more of a mitzvah for the father, as with milah and the other matters incumbent on the father. Nor is any such ruling found in the Rivash; it appears only in the questioner's words there, and his words are not compelling. See Taz and Shach.
