That he gives. That is, when the father is holding the five selaim in his hand and extending them to the kohen, before they leave his hand and enter the kohen's hand, he recites the berachah and afterward gives them to him, so that the berachah is before the performance of the mitzvah. So wrote the Beit Yosef and the later authorities, unlike the practice of some who first place the money before the kohen and only afterward recite the berachah. Taz.
Shehecheyanu. This implies that he always recites Shehecheyanu; see above, siman 265 seif 6.
Wine. The Taz wrote that in a city where there is no wine, one recites Shehakol over beer. It appears that in places without wine, the pidyon should be done before the berachah of Hamotzi over the meal, for during the meal one does not recite a berachah over beer; whereas where there is wine, the pidyon is done after Hamotzi.
Agent. The Shach wrote in the name of several great poskim that they ruled as halakhah that the father may redeem through an agent, and that beit din can redeem him if the father transgressed and did not redeem him or if the father died before he was redeemed. The Taz wrote: it appears to me that as long as the father is alive, he may appoint an agent to redeem with the father's money in his place; for this reason the father's father is like his father, and another person may also give his own money in the father's place and redeem the bekhor, for he is like his agent. But if the father died, he should not be redeemed until he grows up; so it is in Maharil. End quote. In Nekudot HaKesef he disagreed with his words at length, and his conclusion is that if the father died, beit din may redeem him; see there.
