That he gives. Meaning that while the father still holds the five sela'im in his hand and extends them toward the kohen, before they leave his hand and enter the kohen's hand, he recites the blessing and then gives them, so that the blessing is recited before performing the mitzvah. Beit Yosef and the later authorities write so, unlike the practice of some who first place the money before the kohen and only afterward recite the blessing. Taz.
Our lives. It means that we have lived in the world and the United States of America
Wine. Taz writes that in a city where no wine is available, one recites Shehakol over beer. It appears that in places without wine, pidyon haben should be performed before the blessing of Hamotzi at the meal, because during the meal one does not recite a separate blessing over beer. In a place where wine is available, however, the redemption may be performed after Hamotzi.
An agent. Shach cites several leading poskim who rule that a father may redeem his firstborn son through an agent. Likewise, beit din may redeem him if the father failed to do so, or if the father died before the redemption. Taz writes that as long as the father is alive he may appoint an agent to redeem the child using the father's money. For this reason the paternal grandfather is like the father, and another person may also give his own money in place of the father and redeem the firstborn as the father's agent. But if the father died, the child should not be redeemed until he grows up. Maharil writes likewise. Nekudot HaKesef disputes Taz at length and concludes that if the father died, beit din may redeem the child. See there.
