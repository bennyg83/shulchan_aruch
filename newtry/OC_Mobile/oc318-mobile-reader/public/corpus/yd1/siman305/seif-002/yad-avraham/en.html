(Shulchan Aruch seif 10 in the gloss) And the father cannot redeem through an agent. There is a proof that it is well permitted from the fact that we rule that if he gives a gift on condition that it be returned, his son is redeemed, and it implies that the condition must be fulfilled; this is also implied in the first chapter of Kiddushin 6. If it were true that pidyon haben cannot be done through an agent, how could a condition nullify the act? We rule in Perek HaMadir that any matter that cannot be performed through an agent cannot be nullified by a condition. Even regarding a condition framed as “on condition,” where it is effective, we rule that there is no condition in chalitzah, and the Rivash in siman 175 writes that this is true even with “on condition.” Rather, we must infer that a condition is effective in pidyon haben because it can be done through an agent. I found that Sefer Shaar HaMelekh, Hilkhot Ishut, raised this question. Later I saw that Sefer HaPardes wrote regarding this that the giving of the money, in truth, can be through an agent, as is proven in Bekhorot 50, but the declaration must be made by the father himself; and regarding speech we do not require the laws of conditions, for this reason a condition is effective with vows, oaths, and nezirut. However, if his wife was explicitly appointed as an agent to redeem, perhaps it is effective, even though if she redeemed on her own it is not redeemed. So too it appears that in every place an agent is not appointed upon her automatically. His wife is different, for she is like the hand of her husband, at least when he explicitly appointed her as an agent. In Birkei Yosef he wrote in the name of Responsa Davar Moshe that if a woman redeemed a bekhor from her husband’s property, he is not redeemed, and when her husband comes he should redeem him without a blessing. Perhaps that case speaks where he did not explicitly appoint her as an agent, and therefore we do not say that the hand of the wife is like the hand of the husband, for regarding prohibitions we do not say this, as the Shach writes in siman 222. However, in Menachot 93, a verse is needed to exclude semikhah, that a woman does not perform semikhah on her husband’s offering; if not for that, I would have said that her hand is like his hand regarding a mitzvah that is not relevant to her. Therefore it appears that at least where he explicitly appointed her as an agent, perhaps it is effective.
And if another redeemed his fellow’s son with his own money, the Ran writes in Pesachim that, nevertheless, ab initio the owner’s consent is needed, so as not to deprive him of the mitzvah that is incumbent upon him.
