See Responsa Chut HaShani, siman 92, who wrote that this does not mean the kohen is truly giving him a choice, for if the father wanted to leave the son with the kohen he would not fulfill anything, as the Rama writes nearby. Rather, the kohen says this to endear the pidyon to the father and awaken his desire to redeem him, for perhaps his heart hesitates to redeem him for so much money, as though he is compelled by the divine command, and then his son is not redeemed, as Rabbi Chanina said to that man in Bekhorot 51b: you did not resolve to give anything; you acted badly, therefore your son is not redeemed. Therefore, so that he give willingly, the kohen asks, “Which do you prefer?” because it is said, “for every firstborn is Mine,” and if the father does not wish to redeem him he should fear lest the Holy One, blessed be He, take him back, etc.; see there. In my humble opinion this requires study. His statement that if the father gives the money as though coerced by the command his son is not redeemed is a novelty we have not heard. See above siman 61:24, where it says that we excommunicate him, etc.; perhaps one can distinguish. I also did not understand his proof from Rabbi Chanina, for there the father's intent was on condition of return, and Rabbi Chanina did not wish to acquire except as a complete gift, as Tosafot and the Rosh explain there; therefore his son was not redeemed. That is unlike our case. He further wrote plainly that we do not compel a father to redeem his son, for since we derive from “redeem, you shall redeem” that if the father did not redeem him the son must redeem himself, it is as though the Torah gave him the option to remove the mitzvah of pidyon haben from himself and leave it to the son, similar to a negative commandment repaired by a positive commandment. These are his words. Chacham Tzvi already challenged him, as cited above in s.k. 1. It is obvious that if the son has grown, even Chut HaShani agrees that we compel the son to redeem himself.
