We give to a kohen. See what I wrote above siman 61 se'if katan 1 regarding giving against his will, whether it is called giving or not. [And see in the teshuvah of Chatam Sofer siman 252, who was asked: when the day of his son's pidyon has arrived and there is no adult kohen here, only a minor, or one whom the adult kohen made an agent to receive the five sela'im for him, whether he can redeem through a kohen's agent or through a minor kohen. And he answered: our eyes have already seen what Pri Chadash wrote in siman 61, that one fulfills giving matanot kehunah by acquiring for the kohen through his agent with the kohen's knowledge and will, and his proof from seif 14 there is clear; except that he wrote according to the Sifrei that without the kohen's knowledge he would not acquire through an agent, and the reason is simple, for the kohen who receives matanot kehunah also performs a mitzvah in his receiving, and a mitzvah performed by himself is greater than through his agent; therefore sometimes it is not agreeable to him; but with the kohen's will the agent can acquire for him and the owners fulfill giving. And the same applies to a minor kohen, as is explained at the end of the chapter Lulav HaGazul. And even if we say a minor has no kinyan from the Torah, nevertheless regarding matanot kehunah it is not written "and he shall give into his hand" that a hand is needed to acquire etc. But since the Geonim enacted that the kohen should say "what do you prefer more" etc., and all their words are tradition and Torah, and it implies from this that the kohen himself also has power in the act of the pidyon, and for this he cannot make a Yisrael an agent, and likewise a minor has no power to take the bechor out of his obligation accordingly; therefore it is good to bring a kohen from another place; see there.]
To a kohen. [See in the teshuvah of Chatam Sofer siman 301 regarding whether one can give pidyon haben to the husband of a kohenet, like matanot kehunah above siman 61 seif 8; and he brought the words of Tosafot in the first chapter of Kiddushin and the Rosh at the end of Bechorot, and argued in their words, and concluded: since it is obvious to the Ra'ah (in Sefer HaChinuch, parashat Korach) and the Rosh and the Tur that it is forbidden, we do not abandon the obviousness of these because of the doubt of the Tosafot. Nevertheless it appears that one who, after all efforts, cannot reach a kohen on the day of his pidyon and does not want to delay, for who knows what a day will give birth to — in any event he should give on that day to a kohenet without a berachah, and it should be a gift that he does not intend to fulfill with it except if he does not merit to find a kohen; and if he finds a kohen afterward he should again redeem with a berachah as is proper, for then he will not have fulfilled retroactively, for so we stipulate when putting on tefillin of Rabbeinu Tam etc. And if it is hard for him to give the sum of the pidyon twice, he can give to a kohenet on condition to return, even l'chatchila; see there.] And see in the teshuvah of Chavat Yair siman 110 regarding one who held himself to be a kohen for several years, and afterward a guest from the people of his country came and said that he knows all his ancestors and he is a zar, and he swore a severe oath about this, and said that within a year he would clarify by documents that so it is, and through this this one admitted that he is not a kohen — whether he is believed to profane his kedushah for the future, to become tamei to the dead and to marry a divorcee; also regarding bechorim who were redeemed through him. And he wrote that if he were contradicting the guest, certainly we do not lower him through one witness, and even with an oath he is not believed; and even though this one wants to clarify, we say when he clarifies; only since he admitted, certainly we are concerned for him to be stringent, to distance him from all the advantages of kehunah, but not to be lenient, and he is forbidden with women whom a kohen is forbidden with, and he does not become tamei to the dead, like that which we learned: we place upon him the stringencies of kohanim and the stringencies of Yisraelim. And all the sons who were redeemed through him must be redeemed a second time, and he must return all the five shekels of the bechorim; and if he were contradicting the witness he must swear a d'oraisa oath; see there. And see in the teshuvah of Yad Eliyahu siman 37 on a similar matter, regarding one who held himself as a kohen and several infants were redeemed through him, and afterward it was clarified by witnesses that he is a Yisrael, and he ruled that this is obvious: the known sons who were redeemed through him must redeem again, and the money of five sela'im he must return; but what he already returned as is customary, this he may deduct, for presumably a person does not leave what is permitted, and certainly when he returned he did not intend to give a gift so that he would eat gezel, but he returned under the law of returning gezel; and the children who are in doubt whether they were redeemed through him need not redeem anew. However, if he was not a complete zar, only that it was clarified that he is the son of a divorcee, it requires further study whether they need to redeem again, and therefore the kohen need not return the money. And see there further that he challenged the aforementioned teshuvah of Chavat Yair, who wrote there that the mentioned question depends on a dispute in Makkot daf 11 whether kehunah died or kehunah was nullified, and he challenged him that there it is regarding one found to be a chalal, but not one found to be a zar; see there.

See Be'er Heitev, and see in the teshuvah of Chatam Sofer siman 134, where he elaborated to explain that these banknotes do not have the law of shtarot; and it is not necessary to say regarding ribbit, for the Taz already concluded in siman 161 at the end of se'if katan 1 that even with shtarot there is d'oraisa ribbit according to all; but even for other matters their substance is called money, and therefore we administer oaths on a claim of banknotes, and we give them the law of shomrim, and they are complete money even to betroth a woman with them, and we redeem hekdeshot with them; and see there. [But not with all banknotes is the law so, only with those that one is compelled to take in payment according to the law of the kingdom, and it will be explained, if Hashem wills, in Pitchei Teshuva to Choshen Mishpat siman 95 seif 1 s.v. "and shtarot."] But regarding pidyon of a bechor it requires further study, for it is possible to distinguish: specifically in a matter between a person and his fellow, even pidyon of hekdesh which is only buying from the gizbar and filling their lack, one can say that any coin that circulates in the world according to the king's decree upon buyers and sellers, we redeem with it, and it is called coin regarding an oath and shomrim; which is not so regarding pidyon whereby we redeem from the Holy One, blessed be He, and the amount of his pidyon is five sela'im, in which there are five lot and quint of refined silver etc.; see there.]
