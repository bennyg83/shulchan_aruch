(There) And beit din also does not redeem him without the father. See what was written by the Taz and Nekudot HaKesef. Nekudot HaKesef proves against the Taz that beit din can act for a minor even with a slight liability, as in the sugya of inheritance division, and this applies here as well. Ketzot HaChoshen rejected the proof, but the distinction is difficult. If another party is merely acting as an ordinary agent for a minor, there is room to say it does not work; but beit din has broader power to secure the minor's benefit. The same reasoning supports an explicitly appointed wife or agent acting for the father, whereas an unappointed redemption from the husband's assets is doubtful and should be repeated without a berachah.
