Whose limbs are not formed. See Maharik, shoresh 143, whose words imply that even if they did not examine whether its limbs were formed, it does not exempt the one who follows, and the next child is a bekhor for the kohen, for we place the woman on her presumption that her womb has not yet been opened, and we also presume the nefel's limbs were not formed; see there. Responsa Chacham Tzvi, siman 104, disputes this because Niddah 29 proves that there is a majority against the presumption; see there. Responsa Shevut Yaakov I, siman 83, also challenged Maharik in this, and Baer Heitev of Rabbi Maharit cited their words briefly. See Responsa Noda BiYehudah, second edition, Yoreh De'ah siman 188, who lengthily discusses this, rejects their words, and concludes like Maharik. So too She'elat Yaavetz I, siman 49; see there, and see also his Responsa Shivat Tziyon, siman 46, where he discusses it at great length. He further wrote that even if a non-Jew speaking casually said that its limbs were formed, he is not believed, for casual speech of a non-Jew is not believed in a Torah matter, and all the more so against a presumption; see there. See Nachalat Tzvi for my difficulty with Maharik on this. See Responsa Chatam Sofer, siman 299, a long responsum on this matter. He concludes that the main practical law is that if she miscarried and women examined it and saw that it was not formed, the child that follows is obligated in pidyon by the strict law, like Maharik and the Rama's conclusion. If one wishes to be concerned for Chacham Tzvi, whose words indicate that he disagrees even where women saw it was not formed because we are not expert, and this is not merely a stringency of the Tur as Maharik thought, since already in Talmudic times they said in Niddah 25 that a sac was never opened in Nehardea, then because of doubt concerning the berachah he should recite it without God's name and kingship. However, the kohen should recite Shehecheyanu and discharge the father, for at the end of Pesachim the Gemara initially thought the kohen blesses because benefit comes to his hand. Although the conclusion is that the father blesses, that is because the blessing on the mitzvah is preferable to the kohen's blessing, which is only a blessing over benefit. Thus where the father cannot bless because of doubt, the kohen at least should bless over the benefit and discharge the father. But where it was not seen at all, as in the case asked of him, if she had a presumption of pregnancy, certainly the kohen has nothing. If there is doubt about the strength of that presumption, although the kohen cannot collect from doubt, if the father wishes to fulfill his heavenly obligation from every doubt he should give the kohen on condition of return and recite no berachah at all. Even the kohen should not recite Shehecheyanu, because no benefit comes to his hand when it is given on condition of return. See there and siman 308.
