An eight-month child, etc. The Tur wrote this, and with due respect it is a great error. Regarding an eight-month child, there is no distinction anywhere between alive and dead. For this reason the Mishnah does not list it in the latter clause on 47b, though it lists matters simpler than this, such as a formed sac, full of water, etc., even though it is already proven from the first clause that specifically a formed sac matters. The Mishnah's phrase “although it emerged, etc.” is certainly not stated for the novelty of inheritance, just as the latter clause does not say “a nine-month child, although, etc.” as a novelty for kohen; rather, regarding the kohen there is no difference. On 46b Shmuel says the head is not, etc., and they challenged him; they did not establish Shmuel as referring to a dead nefel, even though “nefalim” more naturally means dead ones. Certainly only the head exempts, for why would the whole body be needed? The Gemara says on 47b that the case is specifically miscarriage on the fortieth day, and in Chullin 68a asks what the latter clause teaches, etc.; if you say it teaches that a face counts, we already learned it from placenta, etc. Thus even with placenta, the head exempts. See Tosafot there s.v. “placenta,” and Bekhorot there s.v. “refutation,” who ask: since he was refuted, etc.; after all, even dead ones, etc.; and answer. There are several further proofs, but there is no need to lengthen because the matter is very clear.
