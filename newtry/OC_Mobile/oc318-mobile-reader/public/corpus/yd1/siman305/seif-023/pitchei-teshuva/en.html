He should not recite Shehecheyanu. From Shach s.k. 17 it implies that if the father inherited him, since a one-day-old child inherits and transmits inheritance, he would recite Shehecheyanu. See Levit Chen, Parashat Bo, who wrote that he never recites Shehecheyanu; see there. See also Shach s.k. 12, who cited Responsa R. Eliyahu ibn Chaim regarding a firstborn infant who became ill and did not recover until day thirty-one: although the mitzvah of pidyon haben is performed at its principal time, the milah should nevertheless be advanced. In Levit Chen there he wrote that this is specifically where he recovered on day thirty-one, but if he did not recover, they should not wait; see there. So too wrote Beit Lechem Yehudah and Responsa Chatam Sofer, siman 300; see there. So too Responsa Shivat Tziyon, siman 54, and Responsa Tzemach Tzedek, siman 128.
