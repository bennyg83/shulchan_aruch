Its head was dead. Dagul MeRevavah explains that this means the fetus retracted and afterward its siblings emerged, such as with twins. If the entire fetus was born, however, it certainly exempts the one born afterward, because a dead eight-month fetus is no less than a formed fetal sac, which exempts the subsequent birth. See also Responsa Panim Meirot, part 1, siman 7.
That his limbs are not formed. See in Maharik there, shoresh 143, that it is implied from his words that even if they did not examine to see whether his limbs were formed, he does not exempt the one who comes after him, and he is a bechor to the kohen, for we establish the woman on her chazakah that her womb has not yet been opened, and also regarding a nefel in the chazakah that his limbs were not formed; see there. And see in the responsa Chacham Tzvi siman 104 who disagrees with this because it is proven in the Gemara Niddah daf 29 that there is a majority against the chazakah; see there. Also in the responsa Shevut Yaakov volume 1 siman 83 he challenged Maharik on this, and in Be'er Heitev of the Rav Maharit of blessed memory he brought their words briefly. And see in the responsa Noda BiYehudah Tinyana, Yoreh De'ah siman 188, who elaborated on this and rejected their words and concluded like the opinion of Maharik of blessed memory (and so he concluded in She'elat Ya'avetz volume 1 siman 49; see there. And see further in his book the responsa Shivat Tzion siman 46, where he elaborated greatly on this). And he wrote further that even if an idolater was speaking in innocence that his limbs were formed, he is not believed, for one speaking in innocence is not believed in a d'oraisa matter, and all the more so against a chazakah; see there (and see in Nachalat Tzvi what I questioned on Maharik regarding this).
