(There seif 12) If he became a treifah before thirty days passed over him, he is not obligated in redemption. The Shach in s.k. 16 writes that this means a definite treifah, but with a doubtful treifah he is exempt from pidyon; however, if he then lives twelve months, he is obligated, for he has left the doubt of treifah, since he lived twelve months. His words are not agreed upon, for there are poskim who hold that a human treifah can live, as Tosafot wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Tam at the end of Zevachim, and so too the Hagahot Mordechai at the beginning of the third chapter of Chullin wrote in the name of Tosafot Shantz. In my work on the Rambam, Hilkhot Issurei Bi’ah chapter 10 law 11, I proved that this is also the Rambam’s view. If so, even if he lived twelve months he is exempt from pidyon, for one who seeks to extract money from another bears the burden of proof. In Sefer Minchah Chadashah he disagrees with the Shach and exempts for another reason, namely that a minority of treifot live, only the majority do not live, and in monetary matters we do not follow the majority. This is not so according to what Sefer Haflaah and HaMakneh wrote, that even in monetary matters we follow the majority regarding something that has practical relevance for another matter, and we establish it to follow the majority; consequently it is effective also regarding money. According to this, here too, this majority that they do not live, which we establish regarding prohibition, is effective also regarding money.
