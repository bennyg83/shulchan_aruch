Two Rhenish gold coins, and so forth. Derishah cites Maharshal: one must give the kohen refined silver weighing one thousand nine hundred twenty barley grains. I weighed this twice at different times and with different barley, and it consistently came to five lot and one quint. So writes R. Menachem Metz. I also heard that the chiddushim of Maharam of Rissenburg give the pidyon haben measure as five lot and one quarter lot. Mahari's writings say that we have a tradition that the amount is two Rhenish gold coins, and the opinions are close because the Geonim write that it equals five ounces of refined silver. Rama's statement that it equals two Polish gold coins reflects the coinage of his time, when Polish and Rhenish gold coins were equivalent. That is no longer true, and the distinction is obvious. One who wishes to fulfill the mitzvah in the best manner should give the value of five lot and one quint according to the imperial weight, not the Polish weight. We weighed the stated number of barley grains on the imperial scale and found it exactly equal to five lot and one quint, and even slightly more. With Polish lot the amount would be much larger, because sixteen imperial lot, one imperial pound, are considerably heavier than Polish lot. Eleven imperial lot and one quint equal a full Polish pound.
