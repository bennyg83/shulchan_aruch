The mitzvah of pidyon haben is stated in Kiddushin 29.
The amount is one hundred and twenty ma'ah, for in Moshe's time it was one hundred, and they added a sixth.
A derham is the weight of sixty-four grains of barley, and a ma'ah is sixteen grains of barley; the calculation of the sela follows from this.
The geonim investigated the matter and found that a perutah is half a grain of barley, making the sela three hundred and eighty-four grains of barley. According to later weights, the amount varies with the local standard of weight and the purity of the silver. The same principles underlie the calculations for ketubah money and the other silver obligations. Therefore, for pidyon haben one follows the value of refined silver by the exchange of local coinage, and the actual coin amount changes from place to place.
(Likkut) From Torat Kohanim and Bekhorot: one may increase the shekel but not decrease it.
