Exempt from pidyon. See Baer Heitev of Rabbi Maharit, who wrote that the Shach left this law requiring study. One who examines the Shach will see that this is not so; he left requiring study only whether the same law applies where she is his married or betrothed wife; see there. See Shaar HaMelekh, Hilkhot Issurei Biah 15, end of halakhah 17, who after bringing many early authorities who hold that one who says “this is my son and he is a mamzer” is believed even if she contradicts him, wrote: I am astonished at the Shach in Yoreh De'ah siman 305 s.k. 23, who was unsure whether Rabbi Yehudah's rule that a man is believed to say his son is a mamzer applies even when she contradicts him, and proved from Terumat HaDeshen and finally left the matter requiring study. How did he miss the group of authorities we cited who hold that even when she contradicts him he is believed, except according to Tosafot Rid? Nevertheless, in his case it appears that the son is exempt from pidyon, since according to Tosafot Rid he is truly exempt; and also according to Halakhot Gedolot and Rabbenu Tam, cited by Beit Yosef in Even HaEzer siman 4, who hold that he is believed to render him a mamzer only where there is recognition of the firstborn, but otherwise he is not believed; and according to Riaz in Shiltei HaGibborim on the Mishnah “this is my son and he is a mamzer,” who holds that specifically when he says “this is my son and he is a mamzer” he is believed, but if he says “he is not my son,” he is not believed. Thus the son can say “kim li” like those authorities, and one who seeks to extract bears the burden of proof. So it appears to me as halakhah. End quote.
