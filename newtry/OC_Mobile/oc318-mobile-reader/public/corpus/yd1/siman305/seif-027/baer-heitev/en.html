And a female. The Shach wrote that that is where one gave birth to a male and one to a female, and they became mixed so that it is not known which of them gave birth to a male — behold nevertheless he is obligated five sela'im to the kohen, for both of them, this is their first birth. And similarly when they gave birth to two males and one female and they became mixed, he is obligated only five sela'im, for we can say this one gave birth to two males and this one a female, and there is only one bechor here; even though it can also be that this one gave birth to a male and this one gave birth to a male first and afterwards a female, and behold there are two bechorot here — nevertheless, the one who takes from his fellow has the burden of proof. Therefore when one of them dies within thirty, he gives the kohen nothing, for the father can say a bechor died. End of his words.
