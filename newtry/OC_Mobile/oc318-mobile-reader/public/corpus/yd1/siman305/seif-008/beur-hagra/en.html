(Likkut) And the kohen shall not, etc. But the view of Rashi and the Rambam is that what is said there in Kiddushin, “and it is forbidden to do so because...” refers only to terumah.
And all the more so, etc.; nevertheless, etc. The Gemara in Kiddushin there.
And specifically, etc. The reason a gift on condition of return is a gift is that it is a condition, as stated there on 6a, “and he shall give” teaches that it is a condition, and as stated there on 6b that anyone who says “on condition” is as though he says “from now.” So too in a gift on condition of return, he gives him a complete gift forever, but stipulates a condition with him; it is like conditioning on some other matter, that he eat, do something, or give him something else. But if he does not initially give a complete gift and merely gives it so that it be returned, it is nothing. So the Rosh wrote in the fourth chapter of Sukkah regarding “one should not transfer a lulav to a child,” for if otherwise, let him transfer it to him only until he has fulfilled the mitzvah with it. See Orach Chayim 658:5 in the gloss. With this the difficulty of Tosafot in Bekhorot, s.v. “therefore, it is astonishing,” is resolved. There.
