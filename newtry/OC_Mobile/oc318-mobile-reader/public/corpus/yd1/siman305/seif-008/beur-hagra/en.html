And specifically, etc. For the reason a gift on condition to return is a gift is because it is a condition, as is written there 60a: "and he shall give" — a condition is, etc. And as is written there, 2: that whoever says "on condition," etc. And likewise with a gift on condition to return, he gives it to him as a complete gift forever, except that he stipulates a condition with him, and it is like a gift on something else: that he eat or do a thing or give him something else. But if he does not initially give a complete gift, only that he return it to him, it is nothing. And so the Rosh wrote in chapter 4 of Sukkah, where we say there: one should not transfer ownership of a lulav to a child, etc., and if it were so, let him transfer it to him only until he fulfills [the mitzvah] with it.
