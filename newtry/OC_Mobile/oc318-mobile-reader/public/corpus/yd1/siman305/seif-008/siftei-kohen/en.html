But he should not give to the kohen on condition that he return it, etc. That is, he does not state explicitly that he gives it on condition of return. In such a case, since for one moment he fully resolved that it be a gift, he has fulfilled the act of giving, as the Mechaber writes at the end of the seif. Rather, the case is where he never resolves to give it as a gift at all, but from the outset intends to give it only on condition of return; in such a case he has not fulfilled the mitzvah. So the Mordechai in the first chapter of Kiddushin, in the name of Raavyah, distinguishes, and the Darkhei Moshe cites it; similarly the Rashba distinguishes in responsum 198. This is also the view of the Rambam, Hilkhot Bikkurim 11:8. The Atarah Zahav likewise seems to have understood this way, and therefore wrote, in place of the Rav's words “and the kohen's intent was from the start,” “even though the kohen's intent...” The Derishah also appears to understand the Rav this way. Yet it does not appear to me that this is the Rav's view. Rather, he understands the Mechaber as follows: in the first clause, since the father did not state that he gives on condition of return, the kohen intended to receive it as a complete gift and not on condition of return, while the father intended that it be returned to him; therefore the son is not redeemed. In the last clause, since he stated his words and said that he gives on condition of return, and the kohen's intent is also so, he has fulfilled the mitzvah. So the Bach explained the view of Tosafot and the Rosh, and the Rambam's words can also be explained so. This is also implied by what is marked in Shulchan Aruch on the Rav's words “and the kohen's intent,” “so it appears in Beit Yosef”; in Beit Yosef nothing else is mentioned, only the words of Tosafot and the Rosh. Still, it is not forced that Tosafot and the Rosh mean this, for when they write that Rabbi Chanina did not wish to acquire except as a complete gift, it can mean that he acted so that people would give him everything as a complete gift, but after the fact the son is redeemed in any case. Rabbenu Yerucham, Netiv 1 at the end of part 3, understood their words so; and what the Gemara says, “therefore your son is not redeemed,” means that Rabbi Chanina told him so in order that people give as a complete gift. In any case, the Rav's view appears clear as I explained, and the Mechaber's view appears clear as I explained: he follows Raavyah and the responsum of the Rashba. Examine carefully.
And he returned it to him, etc. This is the Rambam's language, following Rashi's first explanation. But according to Rashi's second explanation, even if he did not return it, his son is not redeemed. So too it appears from the responsum of Rashba that I brought in the next s.k., and so the Rosh wrote. From this it is apparently proven that the Rosh's view is as the Bach wrote. Examine carefully.
And specifically where he said, etc. For in such a case he resolves in his heart for one moment that it be a gift. What he says “on condition” is like saying “from now,” meaning: from now it is given to you, but you shall return it to me afterward. But when he says, “Here are five selaim for the pidyon of my son, and you shall return them to me,” or the like, without the phrase “on condition,” his son is not redeemed. Even if he said nothing, but his intent was that the kohen return all or part of them, and without that he would not have given them to the kohen, even if the kohen did not return anything, his son is not redeemed. For in Bekhorot, chapter Yesh Bekhor, we read: Rav Kahana was accustomed, etc. So wrote the Rashba in responsum; that is, since he did not resolve in his heart for even one moment that it be a gift, his son is not redeemed. See Orach Chayim 658:4-5.
