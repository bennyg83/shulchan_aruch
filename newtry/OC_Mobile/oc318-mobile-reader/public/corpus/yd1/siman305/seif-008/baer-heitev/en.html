And he returned. This is the language of the Rambam according to one language in Rashi; but in the second language Rashi explained that even if he did not return, his son is not redeemed. And so it appears from the Rashba and the Rosh, end of the words of the Shach. And the Taz wrote that if the kohen also was thinking that he would return it to him, then their minds are equal regarding the return, and he is redeemed even if he returns it to him afterwards, for it is like one who says explicitly "on condition that you return it to me." And so is the custom every day, that the father places the pidyon money in a large sum, and he thinks that he will return it to him, and the kohen also thinks so, and he returns it to him afterwards, sometimes all of it, or he keeps for himself some small sum. End of his words.
