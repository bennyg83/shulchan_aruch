That he return. This means that he never fully resolves to give it to him as a gift; rather, from the start his intent is to give it only on condition that it be returned. In such a case he has not fulfilled the mitzvah. Shach.
And he returned it. This is the wording of the Rambam, following the first explanation in Rashi. But in Rashi's second explanation, even if he did not return it his son is not redeemed; so too it appears from the Rashba and the Rosh, end quote from Shach. The Taz wrote that if the kohen also intended to return it, their intentions agree on return and the child is redeemed even if the kohen later returns it, for it is as though he explicitly said, “on condition that you return it to me.” This is the everyday custom: the father places a large sum for the pidyon, intending that it be returned, and the kohen likewise thinks so; afterward he sometimes returns all of it or retains some small amount. End quote.
And specifically. For in such a case he resolves in his heart for one moment that it be a gift. What he says “on condition” is like saying “from now”: that is, from now it is given to you, but you shall return it to me afterward. Shach.
