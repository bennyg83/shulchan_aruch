One does not redeem him on Shabbat. Because it resembles buying and selling; for this reason one also does not redeem on Yom Tov, and so it is in Responsa Rivash, siman 157. It appears that pidyon may also be done at night, as is implied in the first chapter of Kiddushin 29a at the end, and in Rashi and Tosafot there; nevertheless the custom is to redeem by day. Where day thirty-one falls on a public fast, one must see whether the child reached twenty-nine days, twelve hours, and 793 parts before the fast day, which is day thirty. If so, he should be redeemed on day thirty after that time, as below at the end of s.k. 19. Perhaps one may say that for the meal alone we do not advance the pidyon to day thirty. If not, he should be redeemed on the night of the thirty-first, the night of the fast day; but he should not be redeemed on the fast day and have the meal on the night after the fast, for there is no publicizing in such a case. Terumat HaDeshen, siman 269, wrote similarly regarding when day thirty-one falls on Shabbat: if they redeem on Friday so the pidyon takes effect after thirty, how will they make the meal to publicize the mitzvah while the son is not yet redeemed? And if they make the meal on Shabbat, when he is already redeemed, who says there is publicizing in that way, when the pidyon is not seen at the meal? Nevertheless, I have heard that the custom is to redeem on the fast day and make the meal at night after the fast, and one can distinguish that case from Terumat HaDeshen. And it is clear. Responsa R. Eliyahu ibn Chaim, siman 79, discusses a firstborn infant who was ill and did not recover until day thirty-one. It appears that although the mitzvah of pidyon haben should be done at its proper time, the mitzvah of milah should be advanced, for milah is the sign of entering the covenant, and without accepting the covenant there is no obligation of pidyon at all. He writes that one should give the kohen the five selaim within thirty days so that the child be redeemed after thirty days; regarding the berachah, he should not recite it before thirty, as Maharai wrote in his responsa, siman 230, regarding when day thirty-one falls on Shabbat. Maharai wrote: I further protest the berachah that the father would recite on Friday, for the time of obligation has not yet arrived. End quote. This is not clear. If so, how can he recite the berachah after thirty, since at the entrance of day thirty-one the child is automatically redeemed and the mitzvah has already been performed? Maharai himself wrote in Terumat HaDeshen, siman 269, in that case of day thirty-one falling on Shabbat, that on Friday he should not bless because the time of obligation has not yet arrived, and on Shabbat it is not proper to bless because he is doing nothing now; the child is automatically redeemed at the entrance of Shabbat, which is day thirty-one. Although one could say that here, since it is the proper time of the mitzvah, it is proper to do so, it appears to me that one should not distinguish. Since the mitzvah has already been performed, he cannot bless afterward, as I concluded above in siman 19 s.k. 3, that all mitzvah berachot must precede performance, and if he did not bless beforehand he does not bless afterward. It is also not reasonable for several reasons to give the money before thirty days. Therefore one should circumcise him first on day thirty-one, and afterward redeem him and recite the berachah.
That one should not make, etc. Because one does not mix one joy with another.
And some permit, etc. So the Mechaber wrote in Orach Chayim 546:4.
And so is primary. This is the custom, for we do not recite “that joy is in His abode.” So wrote the Atarah Zahav. This is not a proof, for a betrothal meal is forbidden on Chol HaMoed, as the Tur and Shulchan Aruch wrote at the beginning of Orach Chayim 546, even though “that joy is in His abode” is not recited. Rather, the reason is explicit in Tosafot there: the prohibition of mixing joy with joy applies only to the joy of marriage. They did not mean marriage alone specifically, for the same applies to a betrothal meal, since it is related to marriage.
