On Shabbat. Because it resembles buying and selling; for this reason one also does not redeem on Yom Tov. It appears that pidyon may also be done at night, although the custom is to redeem by day. If day thirty-one falls on a public fast, one must see whether the child reached twenty-nine days, twelve hours, and 793 parts before the fast day, which is day thirty; if so, he should redeem on day thirty after that time. Perhaps one could say that for the sake of the meal alone we do not advance the pidyon to day thirty. If not, he should redeem on the night of the thirty-first, the night of the fast day. But he should not redeem on the fast day and make the meal on the night after the fast, for there is no publicizing in that way. Nevertheless, I have heard that the custom is to redeem on the fast day and make the meal on the night after the fast. End quote.
The festival. Because one does not mix one joy with another. Those who permit hold that this principle applies only to the joy of betrothal and marriage.
