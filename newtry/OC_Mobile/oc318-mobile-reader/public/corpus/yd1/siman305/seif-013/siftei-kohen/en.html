His son is not redeemed. Even if the money is still intact, since he was redeemed within thirty days.
And some say that if, etc. This is not comparable to kiddushin, where Even HaEzer at the beginning of siman 40 explains that she is betrothed even if the money was consumed, because there he could betroth her now; here he cannot redeem him now. This is the sugya in Bekhorot 49a. Maharshal, responsum 7, argued at length from Semag, positive commandment 144, that no one disputes that if he stipulated that the child be redeemed only after thirty days, the child is redeemed even if the money was consumed. What Tosafot in Bava Kamma 70a and Or Zarua wrote, that we rule like Shmuel that if the money was consumed he is not redeemed, refers only where he redeemed plainly within thirty days, in which case there is a distinction between whether the money was consumed or not, unlike Terumat HaDeshen siman 269, who wrote that Tosafot and Or Zarua disagree. His words are very forced. If so, when Semag first ruled that the child is not redeemed, he should have specified that this is where the money was consumed. It is also very forced to distinguish in a plain case between whether the money was consumed or not; if plain speech is like saying it should be redeemed only after thirty days, then even if consumed he should be redeemed. Rather, it appears that Semag did not have the text in the Gemara that the halakhah follows Shmuel in monetary law, as Maharik wrote in shoresh 96. The later ruling is somewhat suspect of textual error; see there. Semag holds that only later, with the tanna who taught before Rav Yehudah, the Gemara rules like Shmuel, since Rav Yehudah corrected that tanna to accord with Shmuel. Because that tanna taught plainly before Rav Yehudah, and Rav Yehudah corrected him, what did he correct if plain speech is like after thirty days? Rather, Rav Yehudah knew that Rav and Shmuel also dispute where one redeems his son plainly: whether his intent is from now, so the son is not redeemed even if the money was not consumed, or whether his intent is like after thirty days, where according to one view he is redeemed if not consumed and according to the other even if consumed. Therefore Semag first ruled generally that one who redeems his son within thirty days has not redeemed him, without distinguishing consumption, because plain intent is from now, following Rav Yehudah according to Shmuel, and the Gemara ruled so. Afterward, where he explicitly says the redemption should not take effect until after thirty days, Semag rules in the dispute of Rav and Shmuel regarding consumed money like Rav in an issur matter, since the Gemara did not rule there otherwise. Thus Terumat HaDeshen was correct that the Tur and Or Zarua disagree, for nothing can be proven from Semag. Consequently, one cannot say Tosafot refer only to a plain case; if in a plain case he is not redeemed when consumed only because its law is as if he explicitly said after thirty days, according to Semag, then when he explicitly says after thirty days he is also not redeemed when consumed. This is clear; examine carefully. It is possible that all the poskim agree practically that one who redeems plainly intends from now, for reason supports this, though they did not discuss it. The Rosh's statement in chapter Yesh Bekhor, that Halakhot Gedolot wrote plainly that one who redeems his son within thirty days is not redeemed and that one must establish it as where he said “from now,” was not meant to prove that plain speech equals after thirty days. Halakhot Gedolot merely wrote generally, and the Rosh said it must exclude a case where he explicitly said it should not take effect until after thirty days. Therefore Maharshal's ruling that where the pidyon day falls on Shabbat one should redeem on Friday and stipulate that it take effect after thirty days, and that then even if the money is consumed he is redeemed according to all, is not correct. According to Tosafot and Or Zarua he is not redeemed if it is consumed, and we must be concerned it may be consumed, as Terumat HaDeshen wrote. In all present versions of the Gemara the halakhah is ruled like Shmuel, that if the money is consumed he is not redeemed, and so it is in Sefer Yere'im, siman 353. The Bach also rejected Maharshal's responsum 7, though he did not explain his reason; perhaps he intended what I wrote. The Bach wrote that one should distinguish: if the child reached one month, meaning twenty-nine days, twelve hours, and 793 parts, early on Friday, then he should redeem him on Friday after he is already more than one month old. If he did not reach one month until Shabbat, he is redeemed on Sunday. I wrote at length in responsa, with God's help, with clear proofs that Sefer Yere'im rules so. His words are correct, as is explicit in Sefer Yere'im siman 353. From the Gemara and poskim saying simply “thirty days” there is no proof, for we often find in the Gemara and poskim that thirty days means a month, as above siman 189 s.k. 30. The words of Maharai and the Mechaber can also be explained so. The custom is not to redeem until day thirty-one where possible, but where twenty-nine days, twelve hours, and 793 parts have elapsed before Shabbat, he should be redeemed on Friday. Maharai and the Mechaber's phrase “if day thirty-one falls on Shabbat” means the end of twenty-nine days, twelve hours, and 793 parts. Examine carefully.
