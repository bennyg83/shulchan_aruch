From now. It is merely a gift, for within thirty days pidyon does not apply. So wrote Rashi, even if the money is still intact. Shach.
Redeemed. The Taz wrote: if so, why did he write in seif 11 that if the pidyon falls on Shabbat he should wait until Sunday? Why not redeem on Friday and say, “My son shall be redeemed after thirty days”? Terumat HaDeshen, siman 269, answered that one cannot then make the meal and the pidyon berachah. Maharshal in responsum 17 wrote that in truth he should redeem on Friday and say, “Here is his pidyon, and it shall take effect after thirty days.” The common practice follows Terumat HaDeshen. End quote.
To redeem him. The Taz wrote: it appears to me that this is without a berachah, for certainly in doubtful berachot we are lenient. The same applies if he spoke plainly at the time of pidyon, redeeming on day thirty, or erred in counting the days, and the money is no longer intact in the kohen's hand; he redeems afterward without a berachah. If he redeemed plainly on day thirty and did not say “from now” or “after thirty days,” Rashal ruled in responsum that if the money is still intact in the kohen's hand he is redeemed, and if not, he is not redeemed. From Maharai's rulings it implies that even plain speech is like saying “after thirty days.” The Shach disagreed with Maharshal on this at length; see there.
