Redeemed. The Taz wrote: and one should not say, if so, why did he write in seif 11 that if it falls on Shabbat he should wait until Sunday — why should he not redeem on Friday and say, "let my son be redeemed after thirty"? He answered in Terumat HaDeshen siman 169 that it is impossible then to make the seudah and the berachah of pidyon. And Maharshal in a responsum, siman 17, wrote in truth that he should redeem on Friday and say, "here is his pidyon," and it should take effect after thirty days. And the world customarily follows the words of Terumat HaDeshen, end of his words.
To redeem him. The Taz wrote, and it appears to me that that is without a berachah, for certainly safek berachot l'hakel; and the same law applies if he said without specification at the time of the pidyon that he redeemed on the thirtieth day, or he erred in the number of days, and the money is not extant in the kohen's hand — he should redeem them afterwards without a berachah. And if he redeemed on the thirtieth day without specification and did not say to him "from now" and not "after thirty days," Maharshal ruled in a responsum that if the money is still extant in the kohen's hand he is redeemed, and if it is not, he is not redeemed. And from the words of Mahara'i in his rulings it implies that even without specification it is like one who says "after thirty days," end of his words. And the Shach also disputed Maharshal in this, see there at length.
