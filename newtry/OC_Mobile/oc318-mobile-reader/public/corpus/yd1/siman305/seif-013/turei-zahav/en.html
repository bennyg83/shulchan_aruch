From now, his son is not redeemed. For it is merely a gift, since within thirty days redemption does not apply. So wrote Rashi.
And if he said to him, after thirty days, etc. Do not ask: if so, why did he write in seif 11 that he should wait until Sunday? Why not redeem on Friday and say, “My son shall be redeemed after thirty days”? Terumat HaDeshen siman 169 answered that he cannot then make the meal and the blessing of pidyon. Maharshal in Responsa siman 17 wrote that in truth he should redeem on Friday and say, “Here is his redemption, and it should take effect after thirty days.” The world follows the words of Terumat HaDeshen, as written here, especially since Maharil wrote that this is found in the large Mordechai.
And although the money, etc. If he said nothing, neither “from now” nor “after thirty days,” Maharshal ruled in the responsum cited above that if the money is still present in the kohen’s hand, he is redeemed, and if it is not present, he is not redeemed. From the words of Maharai in his rulings, siman 234, it implies that even unspecified language is like saying “after thirty days,” and it depends on the dispute between Rav and Shmuel, as the Rama wrote here on the Shulchan Aruch. Rav and Shmuel disagree in Perek Yesh Bekhor, Bekhorot 49, regarding one who says “after thirty days” and the money is not present in the kohen’s hand. Rav holds that his son is redeemed, like betrothal of a woman, where one who betroths a woman after thirty days, she is betrothed. Shmuel holds that he is not redeemed, for a woman is different, since if he wished he could betroth her now. In some editions of the Gemara it says that the halachah follows Shmuel, but the Rosh did not have that text and ruled like Rav, that he is redeemed. This is the view of the Tur. It is very astonishing that the Beit Yosef wrote that the Tur rules like Shmuel because the halachah was decided like him; it seems that he did not examine the Gemara at that time. Even more astonishing is my teacher and father-in-law, of blessed memory, who also wrote like the Beit Yosef and was not precise in this matter.
And it is good to be stringent, etc. It appears to me that this means without a blessing, for certainly in a doubt regarding blessings we are lenient. The same applies if he spoke without specification at the time of pidyon, when he redeemed on the thirtieth day because he thought that one redeems on day thirty or erred in counting the days, and the money is not present in the kohen’s hand: he should redeem afterward without a blessing, because according to the view of Maharai that I mentioned, Rav and Shmuel disagree about this as well.
