And if he said to him, "after thirty days," and so forth. One may ask: if so, why does seif 11 say that when the thirtieth day falls after Shabbos he should wait until Sunday? Why not redeem the child on Friday and say that the redemption should take effect after thirty days? Terumat HaDeshen, siman 269, answers that one cannot then properly hold the redemption meal and recite the redemption blessing. Maharshal, responsum 17, writes that in truth he should redeem on Friday and say, "Here is the redemption money, and let the redemption take effect after thirty days." Common practice follows Terumat HaDeshen, as codified here, especially because Maharil writes that the same ruling appears in the large Mordechai.
Even though the money is not, and so forth. If he said nothing, neither "from now" nor "after thirty days," Maharshal in the responsum cited above rules that if the money is still in the kohen's possession when the time arrives, the son is redeemed; if the money is no longer present, he is not redeemed. Mahari's rulings, siman 234, imply that an unspecified transfer is treated as though he said "after thirty days," and depends on the dispute of Rav and Shmuel stated here by Rama. Rav and Shmuel dispute in Perek Yesh Bechor, Bechorot 49a, where one says "after thirty days" and the money is no longer present in the kohen's hand. Rav says the son is redeemed, analogous to kiddushin where a woman betrothed to take effect after thirty days is married even if the original money is no longer present. Shmuel says he is not redeemed, because kiddushin differs: the man could have married her immediately, whereas pidyon haben cannot yet take effect. Some Gemara texts state that the halachah follows Shmuel. Rosh did not have that reading and rules like Rav that the son is redeemed. This is Tur's position. I am greatly surprised that Beit Yosef writes Tur rules like Shmuel because the Gemara decides accordingly. It seems Beit Yosef did not examine the Gemara carefully at that moment. I am even more surprised that my father-in-law follows Beit Yosef here without noting this point.
And it is proper to be stringent, and so forth. It appears this means he should redeem again without a blessing, because in a case of doubt regarding blessings we are lenient and do not recite another blessing. The same applies if the father made an unspecified redemption on day thirty because he thought redemption may be performed that day, or if he miscounted the days, and the money is no longer in the kohen's possession. He should redeem again afterward without a blessing. According to Mahari's position cited above, even an unspecified act of redemption falls within the dispute of Rav and Shmuel.
