And slaves and documents. See Baer Heitev. See Responsa Chatam Sofer, siman 134, where he explains at length that these banknotes do not have the law of documents. This is not only regarding interest, for the Taz already concluded in siman 161, end of s.k. 1, that even with documents there is Torah-level ribit according to all views; rather, even for other matters they are considered intrinsic money. Therefore one administers an oath on a claim involving banknotes, they have the law of guarded items, and they are full money even for kiddushin, and one may redeem hekdesh with them. See there. However, this is not true of every banknote, only of those which one is compelled to accept in payment by the law of the kingdom, as will be explained, with God's help, in Pitchei Teshuva to Choshen Mishpat 95:1 under “and documents.” Still, regarding pidyon haben this requires study. Perhaps one may distinguish: in matters between people, even redemption of hekdesh, which is only a purchase from the treasurer and a filling of its loss, any currency that circulates in the world by royal decree among buyers and sellers may redeem with it and is called coinage for oaths and guardians. But pidyon haben is a redemption from the Holy One, blessed be He, and the Torah fixed its redemption at five selaim containing five lot and a kvint of refined silver; see there.
His son is redeemed. See Chokhmat Adam, klal 150 din 2, who wrote that although the language of the Gemara and poskim, “he has fulfilled,” implies that lechatchilah one should not do so, it appears that this is not precise, for it should be no worse than other gifts of kehunah, above siman 61 seif 9. He further wrote that according to the Perishah in the name of Rashal, one must give it to one kohen at one time; see there. See Responsa Chatam Sofer, end of siman 297, who wrote: it appears to me that lechatchilah one may not divide the five selaim between two kohanim, for anything for which the Torah gives a measure, if one divides it, it is a half-measure. Although after the fact one fulfills through combination, nevertheless the preferred mitzvah is to consume the measure at once or to give the required measure at once. End quote. I do not know how he will answer the law in siman 61 mentioned above, where it is clear that the concern is only an amount worthy of giving, not the full required measure.
