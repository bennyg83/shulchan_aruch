And gid hanasheh — for there is life in it — Rashba's novellae and Ran in Perek Gid HaNasheh; and likewise eiver min hachai.
And an egg that has an embryo in it. In siman 110 the Beit Yosef was uncertain whether it is considered a beriah when there is a drop of blood in it, since the drop is swallowed in the whole egg (as above siman 66); and in responsum of R' Levi ibn Chaviv siman 84 (daf 111 rish daf 4) he wrote plainly that it is a beriah. In my humble opinion it is not a beriah, since there is not yet life in it; and we also do not find that an egg with an embryo is a beriah. From reasoning it appears it is not a beriah, for this egg at the beginning of its creation was permitted, and also at the time the embryo forms it is permitted, as above siman 86 seif 9 regarding an embryo from a treifah egg. Only in Rashba's novellae, Perek Gid HaNasheh daf 106a, he wrote that Rambam, Hilchos Ma'achalos Assuros perek 15, considers it a beriah, and the Beit Yosef followed him in siman 86. I already wrote above siman 86 s.k. 15 that this is not at all compelled from Rambam's words; therefore even though one may not be lenient with an egg with an embryo, nevertheless with an egg that has blood one certainly need not forbid at all, since there is no life in it. I again found in Bedek HaBayis to the Beit Yosef in siman 110 that after he was uncertain, he then resolved plainly that it appears it is not a beriah — and this appears to be his view here in Shulchan Aruch. In Sefer Lechem Chamudos daf 26 end 1a he wrote: and in siman 110 the Beit Yosef was uncertain whether when blood is found in it it is considered a beriah, and in his Shulchan Aruch siman 100 he ruled it is a beriah — end of his words. It is astounding, for in Shulchan Aruch he wrote an egg with an embryo specifically, but when blood is found in it, on the contrary his view is that it is not a beriah, as I wrote (and he answered: certainly his language needs emendation, and it applies regarding when an embryo is found in it that it is a beriah — end).
To exclude one grain of forbidden wheat — even though it is forbidden from the beginning of its creation, such as of kilayim and the like.
To exclude pure fowl that became nevelah — and all the more so treifah; and it appears from the poskim's words that we do not distinguish whether it became nevelah through its shechitah or not; and so ruled the Bach.
To exclude milk — for it is considered forbidden from the beginning of its creation and has neshamah since there is life in it like gid hanasheh; nevertheless it is not a beriah, since if one divides it its name "milk" still applies to it, unlike gid hanasheh and eiver min hachai, for if one divides them their names no longer apply but rather "piece of gid" and "piece of limb" are called.
And likewise it must be whole — to exclude if it was crushed or one limb was cut from it, even a limb upon which life does not depend; Rashba in Torat HaBayit in the name of Ramban and Ran in Perek Gid HaNasheh; and see siman 101 seif 6.
