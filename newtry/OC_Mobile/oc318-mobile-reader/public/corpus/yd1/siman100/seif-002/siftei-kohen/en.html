And the gravy through nosen taam — through straining, as in seif 3. However, that which requires sixty against the emission of the beriah applies specifically to impure fowl and the like, but not to an ant and similar disgusting things, according to the Mechaber and the Rav later at end of siman 104 and siman 107; and see end of siman 107.
And all the rest is permitted if there is sixty against its fat, etc.; and if there is sixty in the pot against the fat, etc. It is difficult: how did the Rav rule plainly like the Taz, and likewise in Turei Chayim klal 41 he copied in plain language the Tur, and likewise in Atteret Zahav — and this is only according to the Tur who holds we do not say chein nafsho in other prohibitions, and likewise we do not say it in isur davuk that becomes nevelah, as written in siman 72 s.k. 15. But for us, who rule above in simanim 72 and 73 and many places that even in other prohibitions in isur davuk one needs sixty against the entire piece to which the prohibition adheres if there is not sixty in the piece itself to nullify the prohibition — if so here too, a thigh cooked with its gid, the rest is not permitted, since there is not sixty in the thigh itself against its fat, until there is sixty in the rest against the entire thigh. On the contrary, the poskim derived this law of isur davuk from what we learn in the mishnah: a thigh in which gid hanasheh was cooked — if there is nosen taam in it, it is forbidden, as found in Shulchan Aruch siman 65, Hagahot Sha'arei Dura, Agudah Perek Kol HaBasar siman 121, and other poskim. One must say he relied on himself to the above simanim 72 and 73 and many places. And that which he wrote the Tur's words in plain language is because one can strain to explain that "a thigh cooked with its gid" means the gid was cut from the thigh and cooked with the thigh in the pot — and he stated the ordinary way of things.
And if it melted, etc. — for even though it has no taste, the Torah forbade it; see above siman 65 seif 9.
