(Shach s.k. 2) And the reason the Rosh and Ran wrote. And in my humble opinion one can say: as written "all in behemah you may eat"—which implies everything without any remainder; it is proved that the chelev and gid of a fetus are permitted; but regarding blood, even if it stands in prohibitions, nevertheless one can uphold the language "all in behemah" by cooking all the blood of the fetus and it becomes "blood in its cooked form" that is permitted; and with this reason I resolve Tosafot Pesachim daf 22a s.v. veharei dam, which implies blood is not included in behemah, etc.—and that is what they said: when neveilah was permitted, etc., "to the stranger in your gates" implies the entire neveilah may be given to him; and it is proved everything is permitted for benefit—including chelev and gid; but there is no proof from this that blood was permitted for benefit—for even if forbidden for benefit one can cook it and give it to the ger. And it is also resolved what Tosafot strained in Chullin daf 36a s.v. s'da, etc.—even though the derashah is not resolved, etc.; and according to the above, for blood too a verse is needed to forbid eating even through cooking.
(Seif 2) If it spread on the ground it requires shechitah. See Pri Chadash below (siman 61): regarding priestly gifts—for in the Tosefta of ben pakuah, since we do not read it as "from your slaughterers of sacrifice," it is exempt from gifts even if it spread—see there. And with this the Sages were not stringent to make it like another behemah—see there.
(There) Requires shechitah. And regarding the blessing, the Turei Zahav brought Rashba's responsum that one blesses; however in responsum Bashamayim Rosh siman 283 he wrote one should not bless.
(Seif 3 in hagahah) For we are concerned perhaps it is not nine months old. And if a flawed knife is found after shechitah—according to what I wrote above in siman 1, Taz s.k. 6: even though we hold like R' Chisda that without breaking a bone it is forbidden, nevertheless one can say it is only a doubt and is not comparable to a mikveh—see there. If so, one can say the offspring is permitted through its own shechitah through double doubt: perhaps the bone at the joint was damaged; and if you say in the hide it was damaged—perhaps its months were complete and its own shechitah helps. And nevertheless one can say there is double doubt against a presumption of prohibition, for it is not slaughtered; and even though we forbade the mother and did not establish the knife in its presumption—that now it was damaged—even though there is double doubt that perhaps he did not slaughter at the place of the flaw, this is because of presumption of prohibition that it is not slaughtered, as I wrote; and the first doubt is like certainty and the second doubt is doubt of prohibition; if so, besides the first doubt there is double doubt that perhaps he did not slaughter at the place of the flaw, and perhaps the offspring is a nine-month-old alive—nevertheless one can say that although if there were three doubts in a behemah it would help according to the Shach (siman 110) that one doubt is like certainty and again there is double doubt—for when we combine presumption of prohibition to make the first doubt like certainty, again there is double doubt without presumption of prohibition; but here, in its doubt of nine months, there is presumption of prohibition that the offspring is not slaughtered. And it is possible it depends on what we wrote elsewhere: in double doubt regarding one doubt there is presumption of prohibition, and regarding one doubt there is not presumption of prohibition—one can say it helps; if so, here the doubt that perhaps it is nine months—in this there is presumption of prohibition of the offspring; but the doubt that perhaps he did not slaughter at the place of the flaw—since on this side we judge that the offspring is eight months and is an offspring, and the behemah and offspring are one: if he slaughtered at the place of the flaw, both the behemah and offspring are forbidden; if not, both are permitted; and regarding presumption of prohibition of the behemah we already combined the first doubt that perhaps in the hide it was damaged to make it like certainty—and practical law requires study.
