Other treifot—and perforation of the windpipe or severance of the gullet: there is room to doubt whether this impedes the shechitah and shechitah is needed; however one may rely on the Rashba that even for chaladah and the like he permits—and the same here. And there is support for this from the Gemara of the bear that fell on it and R' Yehudah permitted slaughtering it—and from this we learn treifot do not disqualify it. And one must understand: in bear trampling there is concern for the simanim—perhaps he trampled the simanim and the minor portion fell—and it would be like perforation and severance, for we do not find he was commanded to check against the simanim—or it is kosher; and it is possible the Rashba derives that even if there was chaladah it is kosher, like perforation and windpipe.
Shechitah—and the Rashba in a responsum wrote that one should bless as on every rabbinic mitzvah. And the Pri Chadash questioned from the Ran's explanation in "With what may we kindle": in a courtyard that has two entrances he kindles in both—and the Ran wrote one does not bless, since there is only concern for suspicion—see there. And the case is not similar: there it is because of suspicion, and here because of marit ayin—and who compares this to that? Also there the explanation is not to bless at two entrances but at one entrance and exempt the other—for since it is because of suspicion, these two entrances are related to each other as one, and he considers and blesses on one and exempts the other. But here to exempt it entirely from a blessing while he fulfills this rabbinic mitzvah—we have not heard. And there is support for the Rashba's words from the Gemara in that which R' Ashi said to that one on whom a bear fell: slaughter it; and the Gemara challenges: behold the halachah is like R' Shimon ben Shezuri, who says shechitah is not needed. And one must understand: since it was trampled and would have died had it not taken its soul, as Rashi wrote—therefore in any case one must kill it; if so, what harm if he kills it by choking and nechirah or slaughters it—is it not better for it to have shechitah to fulfill the rabbinic matter they said: specifically shechitah where otherwise there is loss? And therefore he said to him simply "slaughter it"—meaning like other slaughtered animals, and he will bless; and if the halachah is like R' Shimon ben Shezuri that even rabbinically shechitah is not needed, there is no blessing here and it would be a blessing in vain—and examine.
