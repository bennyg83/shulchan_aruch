(Likkut) And some are uncertain in the matter. Because we hold everywhere like the later language; but in Teshuvot HaRashba he wrote that most books do not have the later language at all; and even for books that have the text "the halachah is like the earlier language"—in rabbinic matters we follow the lenient view, and especially here where it is only because of marit ayin—and the Razah does not have "some say" but only "Abaye said," etc.; and he explains that the two languages do not argue: the earlier language says "all agree," meaning the rabbis of R' Shimon ben Shezuri; and the later language refers to the son of ben pakuah—for R' Yochanan who says R' Shimon ben Shezuri agrees regarding his son that it is forbidden; but if the son of ben pakuah is cloven and his son too is cloven, the son too is permitted; but regarding ben pakuah itself, according to the later view it is permitted through one—that it is cloven; and such was the text in books before Rashi, that Rashi wrote "thus some say Abaye," etc.—before him it was not written "some say" (end of his words).
