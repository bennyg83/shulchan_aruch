It has no remedy—and if this offspring came upon a regular behemah and engendered—to the eye it appears that offspring too has no remedy—for here in this offspring there are some simanim as slaughtered; if so, also what is born from it has some simanim as slaughtered; and behold here there was shehiyah and the like. But it appears it depends on the dispute of the Bach and Shach below siman 14 s.k. 11 whether milk from that offspring is permitted—for according to the Shach it is treifah—see there; if so, when it came upon a regular behemah the offspring is permitted, for it is "this and this cause" and permitted—as Tosafot wrote explicitly in chapter Eizehu Mekoman in the sugya of Shichla Kama: there is a difference between concern for the father's seed versus "this and this cause"—regarding concern for the father's seed everything is permitted and forbidden; in "this and this cause," one treifah and one permitted—see what Maharsha wrote in HaBehemah HaMakishah in the section "why be concerned for its seed"—see there. But according to the Bach, who koshered its milk and it is not treifah—if so, concern for the father's seed applies, and also the offspring has no remedy; and see what I wrote below in siman 14 on this. And one may challenge according to the Shach that the offspring is treifah and its milk forbidden—if so, why did the Gemara toil in Zevachim to find in what the mishnah teaches "treifah"—perhaps it deals with an offspring such as this that is called treifah, and in truth it became mixed and has no recognition? And see what I wrote on this below siman 57 at length—and examine.
