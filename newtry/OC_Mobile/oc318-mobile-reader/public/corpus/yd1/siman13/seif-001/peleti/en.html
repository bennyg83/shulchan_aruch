Fish and locusts do not require shechitah—in the Gemara at the beginning of chapter 2 of Chullin he derives: as written "the flock and cattle you shall slaughter for them," and "all the fish of the sea you shall gather"—from which we learn an ordinary gathering suffices. And the Gemara challenges: if so, since it is written "and they gathered the quail," etc., and the Gemara answers: it is written "gathering" in a place of shechitah. And the Rambam brought this in chapter 1 of Hilchot Shechitah; and regarding locusts he wrote: as written "as one gathers the locust." And the Rashba wondered in Teshuvot HaRashba: behold it is not written in a place of shechitah. And this is fifty years ago when I was here in the house of my father-in-law, my grandfather the famous philanthropist, beloved of the rabbis, such as R' Mordechai Cohen z"l—I said they should return to the simple way, for certainly the language of "gathering" indicates ordinary gathering and not shechitah; only the Gemara's difficulty is thus: behold it is written "and they gathered the quail"—and this certainly requires shechitah—and therefore it is written the language of gathering: in the desert they truly did not slaughter but only stabbed ordinarily; if so, also from fish of the sea there is no proof, for then in the desert it was properly said "fish of the sea you shall gather," for shechitah was not practiced; and the Gemara answers: it is written in a place of shechitah—for it is written "flock and cattle you shall slaughter," and even they did not need shechitah; only the Torah spoke in the language that was practiced for their offerings and the like—shechitah; if so, also gathering of fish is certainly so, and not because of the heter of nechirah meat; and this is all in the desert. But in the time of the prophet, when nechirah meat was forbidden, and it is written "gather the locust"—with the language of gathering you cannot resolve shechitah; therefore they are permitted without shechitah. And many years later I heard it was said in the name of a great man like my view.
