(Siman 13, Taz s.k. 1) And this is written in a place where shechitah is mentioned. For from the language of gathering alone there is no proof—for it is also written "and they gathered the quail"—so it is in our sugya. And with this it is well resolved for me the difficulty of Tosafot Chullin daf 17a s.v. Ra'ak, etc.: "say—whence do we know they argue? Perhaps one said one thing and one said one thing and they do not argue"—see there, who left it in wonder. And in my humble opinion it is difficult at first glance how they derive that fish do not require shechitah from "if you gather all the fish of the sea"—for according to Ra'ak in the desert nechirah meat was permitted for them; if so, one could say therefore it is written "gathering," and now that nechirah meat was forbidden one could learn fish too require shechitah. But in truth it is not difficult at all—for in any case, since in "flock and cattle" it is written "you shall slaughter for them," even though nechirah would have sufficed, nevertheless it is called shechitah—for then nechirah in the simanim is like shechitah for us, as the poskim say: their nechirah is their shechitah. If so, since regarding fish it is written "gathering," therefore because even nechirah in the simanim is not needed; and since even nechirah is not needed, also for us shechitah is not needed. If so, this proof works well both for R' Yehudah and for Ra'ak: for R' Yehudah, who forbade them desire-meat and they had to dedicate and needed actual shechitah—the proof is as plain from "gathering" written regarding fish—for never was nechirah permitted for them; therefore because fish do not need shechitah. And for Ra'ak, who permitted nechirah—the proof works well too: nechirah of theirs is called shechitah language in flock and cattle; therefore regarding fish where "gathering" is written—because even nechirah is not needed. And since so, Tosafot's difficulty is resolved: if they do not argue, it would be difficult whence we know the rule that fish do not need shechitah—for there would be no proof from the verse; one could say flock and cattle, which had to be dedicated and needed actual shechitah—therefore "you shall slaughter" is written; but fish, which are not matters of offering, suffice with nechirah—therefore "you shall gather" is written; and there is no difference between fish and other living creatures; and for us, who forbade nechirah, fish too are forbidden—nevertheless they argue, and the proof goes well both for R' Yehudah and for Ra'ak—as above; and examine well.
(There) But other treifot do not forbid it. I am uncertain regarding perforation of the windpipe: if we judge that since the essence of the shechitah was according to law with a checked knife without shehiyah, derisah, and chaladah—it is like other treifot that do not forbid; or since we hold perforation of the windpipe is neveilah and its disqualification is in the shechitah as if it was not slaughtered. And at first glance one may challenge from the Rif's omission of R' Zeira's statement, who whitened the knife and slaughtered with it because we do not hold "spacious opening"—spacious opening is wide; but nevertheless there is a practical difference to establish the explanation that sharpening precedes whitening regarding ben pakuah that spread on the ground and slaughtered with a whitened knife: if we do not say sharpening precedes whitening, it is only burning of the simanim and as if not slaughtered; but if sharpening precedes whitening, then even though there is no spacious opening and there is perforation of the windpipe before completion of shechitah—which would be kosher here—nevertheless this too is not proper shechitah and it is disqualified in the shechitah. However, according to what the Turei Zahav wrote above (siman 9) that even in a fowl, if one grasped the windpipe alone and slaughtered with a whitened knife it is disqualified since it is not spacious—for the burning causes disqualification even where sharpening precedes whitening—and he proved this from the Rif's omission of R' Zeira—there is a practical difference in a fowl; see there. If so, one can say therefore ben pakuah too is disqualified—for the simanim are burned; and nevertheless this does not suffice—for this explanation and the touch of the sides still does not require sharpening precedes whitening from reasoning—for if so, how did the Rishonim prove from the Talmud's challenge, "and there are sides where the windpipe was perforated in the place slaughtered before the majority was slaughtered"—it is disqualified; one can say the Talmud's challenge and "there are sides" mean: because of this we do not say sharpening precedes whitening; nevertheless according to the change in "spacious opening," one can say for practical law that perhaps without spacious opening we do not say sharpening precedes whitening; if so, nothing emerges from uncertainty; and regarding ben pakuah, whose shechitah is because of marit ayin, one can judge it is a rabbinic doubt—perhaps even though there are sides, we say sharpening precedes whitening; therefore also because of perforation of the windpipe the shechitah is disqualified as if not slaughtered.
