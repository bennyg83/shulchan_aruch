(Siman 13, Taz s.k. 1) And this is written in a place where shechitah is mentioned. For from the language of gathering alone there is no proof—for it is also written "and they gathered the quail"—so it is in our sugya. And with this it is well resolved for me the difficulty of Tosafot Chullin daf 17a s.v. Ra'ak, etc.: "say—whence do we know they argue? Perhaps one said one thing and one said one thing and they do not argue"—see there, who left it in wonder. And in my humble opinion it is difficult at first glance how they derive that fish do not require shechitah from "if you gather all the fish of the sea"—for according to Ra'ak in the desert nechirah meat was permitted for them; if so, one could say therefore it is written "gathering," and now that nechirah meat was forbidden one could learn fish too require shechitah. But in truth it is not difficult at all—for in any case, since in "flock and cattle" it is written "you shall slaughter for them," even though nechirah would have sufficed, nevertheless it is called shechitah—for then nechirah in the simanim is like shechitah for us, as the poskim say: their nechirah is their shechitah. If so, since regarding fish it is written "gathering," therefore because even nechirah in the simanim is not needed; and since even nechirah is not needed, also for us shechitah is not needed. If so, this proof works well both for R' Yehudah and for Ra'ak: for R' Yehudah, who forbade them desire-meat and they had to dedicate and needed actual shechitah—the proof is as plain from "gathering" written regarding fish—for never was nechirah permitted for them; therefore because fish do not need shechitah. And for Ra'ak, who permitted nechirah—the proof works well too: nechirah of theirs is called shechitah language in flock and cattle; therefore regarding fish where "gathering" is written—because even nechirah is not needed. And since so, Tosafot's difficulty is resolved: if they do not argue, it would be difficult whence we know the rule that fish do not need shechitah—for there would be no proof from the verse; one could say flock and cattle, which had to be dedicated and needed actual shechitah—therefore "you shall slaughter" is written; but fish, which are not matters of offering, suffice with nechirah—therefore "you shall gather" is written; and there is no difference between fish and other living creatures; and for us, who forbade nechirah, fish too are forbidden—nevertheless they argue, and the proof goes well both for R' Yehudah and for Ra'ak—as above; and examine well.
