That offspring has no remedy, etc. Meaning: since that offspring was born from a regular animal and has no permission through its mother's shechitah—for behold its mother was not yet slaughtered—it needs its own shechitah by itself; and it itself was already half slaughtered—that is, from its father's side, who was ben pakuah—and half from its mother's side still needs shechitah; and when you slaughter it now there will be shehiyah between its half and its half. And the same applies if the father is a regular animal and the mother a daughter pakuah and engendered: the offspring has no remedy—and so Maharshal. But if that offspring was not yet born but after its mother was slaughtered it is found inside her—even though it spread afterward, nevertheless it was already permitted through its mother's shechitah; only afterward it needs shechitah because of marit ayin as above. And so if the father is a regular animal and the mother a daughter pakuah and she was slaughtered and an offspring is found inside her—the offspring is permitted, for the offspring never entered the category of its own shechitah at all; it was permitted in its mother's womb through "of the behemah you may eat," and even though afterward it spread, it does not require shechitah by Torah law—and this is that of "cloven son of a cloven mother" that I mentioned regarding "and some are uncertain" in seif 2. And it appears: since that offspring has no remedy, its milk too is forbidden—and I wrote from this in siman 14 seif 5.
