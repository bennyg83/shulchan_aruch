A nine-month-old, etc.—has no remedy through shechitah. For behold it is like one who has only one siman from its mother's side—for the second siman is "slaughtered and standing," since shechitah does not apply to it; and a behemah with one siman is not koshered; and this latter siman cannot combine with the former, for you have no greater pause than this—that the first was slaughtered from when it was born (Rashi and poskim). And even though ben pakuah from a treifah is permitted through its own shechitah—here, since the father of the offspring is a proper ben pakuah and did not require shechitah, his simanim are as if slaughtered; if so, the offspring born from him has one siman as slaughtered—the Rashba, Ran, and they are brought in Beit Yosef, Tosafot, Rosh, Rabeinu Yerucham, and other poskim. And therefore the Mechaber was precise and wrote "found in the intestines of a slaughtered kosher animal."
And it grew and came upon a regular animal, etc. The same law applies in reverse—that is, a regular animal that came upon a daughter pakuah and engendered her: that offspring has no remedy, for the reason mentioned; and see below siman 14 s.k. 11 that the milk that comes from it is also forbidden.
And engendered. It appears for this reason the Taz and other poskim were precise and wrote "and engendered"—to exclude where it did not give birth but was only found in the mother: then it is permitted through its mother's shechitah and has no siman at all, for behold it is as slaughtered whether from its mother's side or its father's side, and is permitted like a general ben pakuah—and so the Bach; and so it appears from Rashi's language in s.k. 12. And the Bach wrote—and this is his language: it appears the same applies to a regular animal that came upon a daughter pakuah and the pakuah was slaughtered and an offspring was found in her—that offspring too is permitted through its mother's shechitah, as proved from Abaye's later language that deals with such a case; and we do not say: since its mother does not require shechitah by Torah law and the offspring requires shechitah, if we are concerned for the father's seed then it is not permitted through its mother's shechitah—but we say the expansion "all in behemah" includes everything: in any case where the mother is permitted in eating, the offspring too is permitted in eating through its mother if found inside her—end of his words. And one may question: Abaye's later language deals with one that is cloven ben pakuah—that is, found in the womb of a cloven mother, and that cloven mother is not a daughter pakuah but her abdomen burst and this cloven one was found in her, as in s.k. 7; but if an offspring is found in a daughter pakuah that became pregnant from a regular animal, one can say: since this daughter pakuah does not require shechitah, the offspring is not permitted through its mother's shechitah and is like a yelud—it has one siman from its father's side, and the second—which is from its mother—is likewise as slaughtered, and has no remedy; and we do not expand from "in behemah you may eat" except where it is permitted through its mother's shechitah; but here, since its mother does not require shechitah, it is as if not slaughtered—and the offspring is like a yelud that has no remedy.
Behold his son and son's son, etc. Meaning: even if they were born in the normal way of birth, only that their sons too will come upon offspring of their kind like them—that are pakuot or were born from pakuot—and so Maharshal there seif 9.
Require shechitah by rabbinic decree. Since they spread on the ground.
