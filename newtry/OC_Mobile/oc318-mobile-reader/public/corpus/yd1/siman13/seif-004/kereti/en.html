And it grew and came upon a behemah, etc.—the same applies to a regular behemah that came upon a behemah that was permitted through its mother's shechitah—as the Shach wrote.
That offspring, etc.—for on one side from ben pakuah the simanim are as slaughtered, and on the other side they are not as slaughtered; behold here there was either half a siman slaughtered and half not slaughtered; and even though we hold "four simanim the Merciful One koshered"—that is when its mother was treifah, for it has no simanim of its mother; then the Torah koshered its simanim; and then if it came upon a regular behemah, in truth the offspring is kosher. But if its mother was slaughtered afterward—it has no simanim, and behold they are as slaughtered, and there is no shechitah here; and therefore the Shulchan Aruch was precise: "found in its mother's intestines, slaughtered kosher." And know: all this is forbidden from doubt—for this is all if we are concerned for the father's seed; and we are uncertain whether we are concerned for the father's seed.
It has no remedy—and if it (the mother) came upon a regular behemah—see Peleti, where I wrote according to the Shach in siman 14 one may kosher it; and according to the Bach, even that offspring has no remedy.
And if she is a daughter pakuah and a regular behemah came upon her and she was slaughtered and that offspring was found in her—it too has no remedy, for the mother's shechitah cannot permit it—for it is slaughtered and standing; and the offspring has a bit of a siman from the male—that it is a regular behemah—Shach.
