Through shechitah. And the reason is in the Shach: because behold it is like one who has only one siman from its mother's side—for the second siman is slaughtered and standing, since shechitah does not apply to it by Torah law; and you have no greater pause than this—that the first was slaughtered from birth. And even though ben pakuah from a treifah is permitted through its own shechitah if it is nine months old—for we say in the Gemara "four simanim the Merciful One koshered" [or through two of its simanim] or through two simanim of its mother; and if its mother is treifah it can be koshered through two of its own—here it is not so, for its father's siman is as if already slaughtered; and therefore if ben pakuah from a treifah came upon a regular animal, the offspring is like other regular animals—see above siman 14 that the milk too from ben pakuah that came upon a regular animal is forbidden—end of his words. And the Taz wrote: the same applies if the father is a regular animal and the mother a daughter pakuah and engendered—that offspring too has no remedy—end of his words. However, a regular animal that came upon a daughter pakuah and the pakuah was slaughtered and an offspring was found in her—the Bach wrote it is permitted without shechitah; and the Shach wrote it has no remedy—and practical law requires study.
From their words. Meaning: if they spread on the ground.
In them. And the Shach wrote in the name of Maharshal: even if they were born in the normal way of birth, only that their sons too will come upon offspring of their kind like them—that are pakuot or born from pakuot.
