And finds therein, etc. All the more so if born in a fowl—forbidden; and as written in siman 79 there.
Only what has a hoof was permitted. For so it is written: "every one that parts the hoof," etc., "of the behemah you may eat"—and Chazal expounded thus.
And some say even if its hooves are cloven, etc. This is the view of Tosafot and the Rosh and their supporters, who hold the matter does not depend on hooves but on a species that has a hoof; only it is difficult against what the Rav wrote: even if its hooves are cloven, only that it resemble an animal, etc.—for in such a case certainly no one argues, and it is the Gemara in Arukh chapter HaMakishah: a closed cow in the womb of a cow is permitted; and the derashah was difficult for him too—that it should teach us that even if it has no hooves at all it is permitted; and he wrote—and this is his language: it appears to me that the Rama holds the Rambam does not require that the behemah found inside a behemah have hooves, but we require that one find in it a behemah of a species that has hooves; and even if it has no legs at all, or legs like a dove's, it is kosher since this species of behemah has hooves in its kosher state. And the Rama added: even if a camel is found inside it that has no hooves at all but only dove's legs, or has no legs—and its species too have no hooves in their kosher state—but its hooves are cloven, meaning like a donkey whose hooves are entirely cloven—nevertheless, since this camel is a species of behemah, he means to say that from the species that is included under the name behemah there are behemot that have a hoof in their kosher state that are kosher, unlike a fowl that does not have the name behemah—end of his words. And his words are strained; and further, if so it is already explained in the Rambam's words that everything depends on resemblance—so what need did the Rav have to add "some say" anything? And further, who are these "some say"? And in my humble opinion it is clear the Rav understood the Mechaber's words as the Mechaber himself intended, as in s.k. 17; and he refers to the end where it says only what is found in a behemah that has a hoof—meaning: when it has a hoof, even if it is the form of a fowl, it is permitted; and he says "some say" that it is not permitted even if its hooves are cloven when it is the form of a fowl (and that he did not teach us a stringency that it is not permitted even if it has two hooves when it is the form of a fowl—because one hoof or two are the same; and so one must say in Darkei Moshe, as I wrote in my hagahot to Darkei Moshe; or the language of the Mechaber was imprecise and implies it is permitted even with one hoof) only that it resemble a behemah of a species that has a hoof—meaning everything depends on resemblance and not on the hoof; and this is the view of Tosafot, Rosh, Rashba, Ran, R' Yerucham, Tur, and the Maggid, who hold everything depends on resemblance: if it has the form of a behemah, even if it has no hoof at all it is kosher since the species has a hoof; and if it is the form of a fowl, even if it has a hoof it is forbidden—and so Maharshal there seif 2.
One who slaughters the animal, etc. This is the Rambam's language; and Beit Yosef wrote: it appears from his words that we require an actual hoof in the eye, and it does not suffice that the species has a hoof; if so, when it has an actual hoof, even if it is a fowl it is permitted.
