I am uncertain regarding perforation of the windpipe: if we judge that since the essence of the shechitah was according to law with a checked knife without shehiyah, derisah, and chaladah—it is like other treifot that do not forbid; or since we hold perforation of the windpipe is neveilah and its disqualification is in the shechitah as if it was not slaughtered. And at first glance one may challenge from the Rif's omission of R' Zeira's statement, who whitened the knife and slaughtered with it because we do not hold "spacious opening"—spacious opening is wide; but nevertheless there is a practical difference to establish the explanation that sharpening precedes whitening regarding ben pakuah that spread on the ground and slaughtered with a whitened knife: if we do not say sharpening precedes whitening, it is only burning of the simanim and as if not slaughtered; but if sharpening precedes whitening, then even though there is no spacious opening and there is perforation of the windpipe before completion of shechitah—which would be kosher here—nevertheless this too is not proper shechitah and it is disqualified in the shechitah. However, according to what the Turei Zahav wrote above (siman 9) that even in a fowl, if one grasped the windpipe alone and slaughtered with a whitened knife it is disqualified since it is not spacious—for the burning causes disqualification even where sharpening precedes whitening—and he proved this from the Rif's omission of R' Zeira—there is a practical difference in a fowl; see there. If so, one can say therefore ben pakuah too is disqualified—for the simanim are burned; and nevertheless this does not suffice—for this explanation and the touch of the sides still does not require sharpening precedes whitening from reasoning—for if so, how did the Rishonim prove from the Talmud's challenge, "and there are sides where the windpipe was perforated in the place slaughtered before the majority was slaughtered"—it is disqualified; one can say the Talmud's challenge and "there are sides" mean: because of this we do not say sharpening precedes whitening; nevertheless according to the change in "spacious opening," one can say for practical law that perhaps without spacious opening we do not say sharpening precedes whitening; if so, nothing emerges from uncertainty; and regarding ben pakuah, whose shechitah is because of marit ayin, one can judge it is a rabbinic doubt—perhaps even though there are sides, we say sharpening precedes whitening; therefore also because of perforation of the windpipe the shechitah is disqualified as if not slaughtered.
