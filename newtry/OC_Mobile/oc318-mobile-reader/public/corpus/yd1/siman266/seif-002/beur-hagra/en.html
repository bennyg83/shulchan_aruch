And permitted to move, etc. As written in chapter 3 of Yom Tov (26b) — there is no muktzeh, etc.; but Maharil wrote that immediately after circumcision he should throw the knife from his hand; and Darkei Moshe wrote it appears to him the words of Rabbeinu Yeruham, and it escaped him the words of Tosefta and Yerushalmi explicit like Maharil's words — in Tosefta end of chapter 2 of Sukkah: R' Yehuda — the first day of the festival that falls on Shabbat, once the eighteenth departed from it, it is forbidden to move it; and in Yerushalmi chapter 3 there — he placed it on the ground, it is forbidden to move it; and "there is no muktzeh" is not applicable here, for in truth it is muktzeh — only permitted because of need for a mitzvah; and so they challenged Taz — see there; and also what Maharil wrote that he must throw it from his hand — the law is with him; and even though regarding keli shemelachto le'issur when moving for its place it is permitted to store as written in Orach Chayyim siman 308 s.k. 3 — here it is forbidden; and proof from what is written at the end of chapter 2 of Shabbos — I heard that if he came, etc., place, etc. (and Magen Avraham siman 266 s.k. 19 and our master's words there). Specifically regarding keli shemelachto le'issur which is not muktzeh — permitted to move for the need of itself and its place — only from sun to shade is forbidden, for it is not for a need; for even R' Shimon who does not hold muktzeh at all concedes here, as Tosafot end of chapter 2 of Shabbos wrote there — it is not because of muktzeh; and like keli shemelachto le'heter not for need at all — here permitted since he moves for need; unlike what is forbidden to move even for its place. And the braita there they established like R' Nechemyah; and with this the Tosafot's question there 35b s.v. vehatanya is resolved — "say, etc." — it is not so, for if it were permitted for the need of itself and place it would also be permitted to store as above; and this is what the braita says "because one does not move," etc. — meaning entirely, as Tosafot wrote there; and according to Tosafot's explanation it is superfluous — even if permitted to move like R' Shimon's words, nevertheless forbidden to store.
