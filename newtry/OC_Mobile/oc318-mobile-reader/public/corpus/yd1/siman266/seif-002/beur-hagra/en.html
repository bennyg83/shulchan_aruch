(Likkut) And regarding those who are not, etc. This follows the conclusion of the Gemara, in accordance with the scholars of Nehardea, who establish the matter according to the Sages; and we rule according to the Sages. This is unlike the Baal HaMaor, who initially wished to rule against the baraisa. His reasoning was that all these sources are established according to an individual opinion, whereas regarding the statement of the scholars of Nehardea he maintained that we rule according to Rabbi Yose, because in Menachos 99b Rabbi Ami said, “From his words,” etc.; Rabbi Yose said in the name of Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai, etc.; and Rava, etc. This implies that all of them agree with Rabbi Yose. The anonymous Gemara in Nedarim 8a likewise states this. Nevertheless, the inference is not conclusive, and the Baal HaMaor himself ultimately rules otherwise. (End of Likkut.)
And it is permitted to move, etc. As stated in chapter 3 of Yom Tov (26b), “There is no muktzeh,” etc. Maharil, however, wrote that immediately after the circumcision he should cast the circumcision knife from his hand. Darkei Moshe wrote that Rabbeinu Yerucham's view appeared correct to him, but the explicit words of the Tosefta and Yerushalmi, which agree with Maharil, escaped his notice. The Tosefta at the end of chapter 2 of Sukkah states: Rabbi Yehudah says that when the first day of Sukkos falls on Shabbos, once one has fulfilled his obligation with the lulav, it is forbidden to move it. Likewise, the Yerushalmi at the end of chapter 3 there states: Once he placed it on the ground, it is forbidden to move it. The rule that there is no muktzeh is inapplicable here, for it is indeed muktzeh, and it was permitted only for the sake of the mitzvah. The Taz likewise challenged this ruling; see there. Maharil is also correct in writing that one must cast it from his hand. Although regarding a utensil whose primary function is prohibited, when one moves it because he needs its place he may carry it to a protected location, as stated in Orach Chaim, siman 308, se'if 3, here this is forbidden. Proof may be brought from the end of chapter 2 of Shabbos: “I heard that if one came,” etc., “to a place,” etc. See Magen Avraham, siman 266, s.k. 19, and the words of the Gra there. This applies specifically to a utensil whose primary function is prohibited, which is not inherently muktzeh and may be moved for use of the utensil itself or its place. It is forbidden only to move it from the sun to the shade, because that movement serves no permitted need. Even Rabbi Shimon, who generally rejects muktzeh, agrees to this, as Tosafos writes at the end of chapter 2 of Shabbos, because the prohibition is not due to muktzeh. It is comparable to moving a utensil whose primary function is permitted when there is no need at all. Here, however, carrying it onward is permitted because the initial movement was for a permitted need. This differs from an object that may not be moved even because one needs its place. The baraisa there was established according to Rabbi Nechemiah. This resolves Tosafos' question there, 35b, s.v. Vehatanya, “This is difficult,” etc. Their question does not stand, because if it were permitted to move the object for use of the object itself or its place, it would likewise be permitted to carry it to a protected location, as explained above. This is why the baraisa states, “because they may not move,” etc., meaning that they may not move it at all, as Tosafos writes there. According to Tosafos' explanation, that phrase is superfluous, for even if movement were permitted according to Rabbi Shimon, carrying it to a protected location would still be forbidden. This is unlike the Taz. His reliance on the principle that “their end was permitted because of their beginning” is irrelevant here, since that principle was stated only concerning the joy of Yom Tov. The Yerushalmi cited above explicitly states: “Once he placed it on the ground,” etc. On Yom Tov, however, the Sages permitted returning shutters and spreading hides before those who tread upon them.
And one may not bring, etc. This is the baraisa there, and Pesachim 92a.
But if, etc. This follows Rabbi Shimon's view there in chapter 1, and we rule according to him even regarding an optional matter. The statement in Pesachim concerning “three matters,” etc., follows the Sages; Rabbi Shimon likewise agrees regarding utensils that were resting in the house. See the Rif there.
