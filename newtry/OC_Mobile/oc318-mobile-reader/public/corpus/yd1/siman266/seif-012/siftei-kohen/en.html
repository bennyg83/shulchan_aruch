From a Jewish woman, etc. The Bedek HaBayit writes: It appears to me that even if he is married to an apostate Jewish woman, a son born to them is circumcised on Shabbos, since they remain obligated in all the commandments. The Ittur may have specified a Jewish woman not to exclude an apostate Jewish woman, but only to exclude a non-Jewish woman. The Bach cites this. To me, however, it does not appear so. The Bedek HaBayit concludes that we do not presume that the child will follow an evil path because his mother is Jewish, and the Tur cites this. This implies that one might have thought that because his father is an apostate, the child should be presumed likely to follow an evil path, but we do not make that presumption because his mother is Jewish. If his mother is also an apostate, however, it would be forbidden. This requires study.
One circumcises him, etc. The Bedek HaBayit cites the Rashbatz that a mamzer is circumcised on Shabbos, and Maharil writes likewise. The Bedek HaBayit further writes in the name of the Rambam that Karaites should be treated respectfully so long as they do not speak perversely against the rabbis or mock the words of our holy Sages of the Mishnah and Gemara. It is proper to greet them, even in their homes, and to circumcise their sons even on Shabbos when they observe our circumcision procedure, including milah and periah, since worthy descendants may emerge from them and return in repentance. Rav Hai Gaon also ruled this way. Rav Betzalel Ashkenazi, responsum 3, cites the Rambam and Rav Hai Gaon, and writes that the Karaites living among us in his time possessed none of the qualities mentioned by the Gaon. They continually deteriorated, did not properly circumcise their sons because they did not perform periah, and one who circumcises without periah is regarded as though he did not circumcise. They did not use our mohalim, did not admit Torah scholars into their homes, fled from them as from a snake, and did not pray according to our liturgy, including the Amidah. Since they differed from us in all these matters, it was forbidden to circumcise their sons on Shabbos. Because they did not accept periah, performing it for them was inappropriate, while circumcising without periah would desecrate Shabbos without fulfilling a mitzvah.
