From sunset. If he was born at sunset on Friday and is circumcised on the tenth day, that is Sunday.
According to Rabbi Yose. Meaning, twilight lasts only the blink of an eye immediately adjacent to full night. It was already explained in siman 262, seif 6, that once three stars are visible it is night. Maharam Alashkar, responsum 96, discusses a child born on Friday after sunset but before three medium stars appeared. He asks whether the child should be circumcised on Sunday, according to the Rif, Rambam, and Rosh, who maintain that twilight begins immediately after the sun sets below the horizon, or on Friday, according to Rabbeinu Tam, who maintains that there are two sunsets and twilight begins only at the end of the second sunset, after the sun has entered the thickness of the firmament, while before then it is fully day. This is also the view of the Ramban in Torat HaAdam, the Semag, and the Ran. See there at length, where he cites the approaches of the Geonim and poskim and explains their words. He concludes that this child is not circumcised until Sunday. See there.
According to Rabbah. Rabbah states in the chapter Bameh Madlikin, Shabbos 34a, that while the eastern horizon remains red it is still twilight. This period extends three quarters of a mil before nightfall. This is unlike Rav Yosef, who says there that it is still day. Therefore, the child is circumcised on the ninth day, but not on Shabbos.
