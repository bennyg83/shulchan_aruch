And the Baal HaMaor wrote, etc.; therefore, etc. This follows the Gemara in Shabbos there: Rabbi Yochanan said, “The halachah follows Rabbi Yehudah,” etc.
And if he was born, etc. His view accords with Rabbeinu Yonah, who writes concerning the dispute of Rabbah and Rav Yosef there that we rule stringently. The Rif, however, writes that this is not merely a stringency, but that the halachah follows Rabbah. See the Rosh there. There is no practical difference between them. Rabbeinu Yonah's words there are difficult, for he writes that there is a practical difference regarding an individual who accepted a fast upon himself, since it is permitted during bein hashmashos; according to him, it is not permitted until the bein hashmashos of Rav Yosef, which is the stringent view. This is incorrect. If we follow the stringent view, it should not be permitted until the bein hashmashos of Rabbi Yose, which begins after the bein hashmashos of Rabbi Yehudah has ended, as stated there: “The halachah follows Rabbi Yehudah regarding,” etc. Moreover, the commentators did not explain the reference to bein hashmashos as applying to the end of a fast, but to its beginning, and specifically to communal fasts, such as the final ten fasts and similar cases. The beginning of a day differs from the end of a day. See what the Gra wrote in his Likkutim to Orach Chaim, at the beginning of siman 562, and in the printed Likkut in siman 261, s.k. 11. This is also indicated in chapter 4 of Pesachim, 54b: “Shall we say that he maintains,” etc.; “but did we not learn,” etc. It is possible that Rabbeinu Yonah interprets the passage as applying to both the beginning and end of the fast. He was pressed to this interpretation by the statement in chapter 1 of Taanis (12a): “Any fast during which the sun did not set,” etc. The commentators, however, explain that this refers to the completion of sunset, meaning the emergence of the stars. Nevertheless, the plain language implies sunset itself, and it must therefore refer to the second sunset. This follows Rabbeinu Tam and the other authorities. According to the Gra in his Likkutim, however, bein hashmashos begins at the start of sunset, as explained above in siman 262, s.k. 9, and in Orach Chaim, siman 261. Yet this is the beginning of bein hashmashos, as it is likewise regarding Chanukah. According to this, however, that passage proves that the halachah follows Rabbah, because according to Rav Yosef the relevant time would be after sunset. It appears to me that the authorities require the emergence of the stars regarding both fasting and Chanukah because we rule stringently according to Rabbi Yose. The baraisa concerning Chanukah that says “from the time the sun sets” follows Rabbi Yehudah, as does Rav Chisda's statement regarding fasting. We, however, follow Rabbi Yochanan, who ruled stringently according to Rabbi Yose. Although we are lenient regarding a fast, because a doubt concerning a self-imposed fast is permitted, we do not combine two leniencies by also ruling according to Rabbi Yehudah.
