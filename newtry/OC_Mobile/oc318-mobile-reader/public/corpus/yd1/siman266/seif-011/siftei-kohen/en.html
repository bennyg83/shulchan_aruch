One circumcises him. For he is considered viable.
But one does not desecrate Shabbos for him in other matters. The Darkei Moshe writes: It appears to me that the Rif certainly agrees with the Rosh and the Tur that one circumcises him in any event. When he writes that one does not desecrate Shabbos for him, he means that preservation of life does not override Shabbos for him, since his hair and nails were not fully formed. Nevertheless, one circumcises him because either way there is no prohibited labor: if he is a seven month child, he is viable, and if he is an eight month child, cutting him is merely like cutting flesh, as stated in the Tur. This reasoning does not apply to other forms of Shabbos desecration. The Gemara establishes the case regarding preparations for circumcision according to Rabbi Eliezer because the discussion there concerns circumcision, while the poskim derived the law of other desecrations from it for practical ruling. They may not have stated explicitly that he is circumcised either way because they relied on the phrase, one does not desecrate Shabbos for him, which implies only that other desecrations are prohibited, while circumcision itself is performed because either way it involves no Shabbos desecration, as the Tur states. The Semag likewise writes explicitly that one does not desecrate Shabbos for him, meaning that one does not remove rubble from upon him, even though one circumcises him. See also the Derishah and Bach.
