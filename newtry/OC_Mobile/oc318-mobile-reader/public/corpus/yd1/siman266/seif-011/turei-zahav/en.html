In the Tur it is written regarding this — if he is complete, etc.; and it is reasonable to Beit Yosef that "complete" means complete in his limbs, but we do not care about his hair and nails that he mentions afterward — for even if his hair and nails were not completed we circumcise him on Shabbat; and his proof from that which even regarding doubtful eight-day-old he wrote one need not that his hair, etc., be completed; and I wondered — if according to this explanation "complete" comes to exclude seven-day-old — and it almost has no meaning, for we do not find concern for limbs anywhere regarding circumcision; and further it is difficult — for it is stated in chapter HaErel (Yevamos 80) — it teaches: who is an eight-year-old? All whose months were not completed for him; R' Yehuda says: his signs prove upon him — his hair and nails were not completed — behold before us that according to R' Yehuda all whose signs were not completed is called eight-year-old; and we rule like R' Yehuda as explained there in the Gemara — end quote, it appears to me necessarily Rambam and Tur wrote even regarding doubtful eight-day-old that he must be complete — meaning in these signs, for if they were not completed he is not called eight-day-old; and that the Tur mentioned first a complete seven-day-old unstated and afterward regarding one born to eight days wrote complete in hair and nails — to teach that for seven-day-old we need not be precise about this, or one not expert in these signs has no concern except that no deficiency appear clearly before us — therefore they said simply "complete" and did not come to exclude incomplete; rather this was written to say since we did not see with certainty any deficiency; but for eight-day-old he must have clear completeness in hair and nails. And what Beit Yosef challenged — behold even in doubt the Tur wrote we circumcise even without completion — for this understand that initially the Tur said what is agreed from all poskim — namely if he is complete; and afterward wrote what is disputed — namely in doubt; and he ruled that even there completion is not needed. And with this it works out for me what the Tur wrote that it appears to him from Rambam's language he wanted to say his entire hair was completed; and Beit Yosef wondered from where he saw this from Rambam's words; and for me it works out that it was difficult for him in Rambam since he wrote that even a definite eight-day-old needs that they be completed — and how does he permit in doubt without completion; rather certainly plainly he speaks of completion; and on this the Tur disputes — that even without completion we circumcise from doubt — if alive it is fine, and if dead it is merely cutting flesh and there is no wound at all; and in any case in all aspects we circumcise even without completion — this is the Tur's opinion, and it is the first opinion Rama brings nearby; the source from chapter R' Eliezer of Milah that Beit Yosef brings in the Talmud's conclusion in the resolution of Rav Ada bar Ahavah — even doubtful eight-day-old we circumcise from doubt. Only the Tur brought the Rosh's opinion who wrote that doubtful eight-day-old we do not violate Shabbat for him; and he wondered on him — behold we circumcise him from doubt; and Beit Yosef resolved the Rosh followed the Rif who also wrote thus, and their reason — that reasoning whereby we circumcise from doubt did not remain in the conclusion since in chapter HaErel they say the braita deals with without completion — then he was like a stone; but with completion he can live — we say that in doubtful eight-day-old circumcision from doubt does not help; and it is difficult — why say gratuitously in the Gemara's unstated that they dispute each other, and those amoraim mentioned in chapter R' Eliezer of Milah did not know the braita deals with without completion — impossible to say; and why not say they also knew the braita's explanation and even so we circumcise from doubt even without completion. And it appears to me this is the Rosh and Rif's reason — that certainly according to the Gemara's conclusion we circumcise in all aspects even without completion; only since if he were definitely eight-year-old we would not circumcise without completion since certainly he is not fit for circumcision and it does not help to say cutting flesh as the Tur wrote — "since he is like a stone why cut flesh not for need"; if so the primary permission is specifically in doubt; and for us doubt does not help, for perhaps what is doubt for us was certainty for Talmudic sages, as Tosafot wrote this reasoning in chapter R' Eliezer of Milah (Shabbos 135) s.v. ben shemonah, etc. — we are not expert and all are called doubt, etc.; even though they wrote this for permission regarding definite eight-year-old which for us does not exist — namely with completion as their words imply; but without completion it would be stricter from the reasoning we are not expert, as the Tur wrote siman 58 — "but if before us only that we are not expert in his hair — even rabbinically we do not rely to be lenient" — end quote; and likewise here — many times born bein hashemashot and we are not expert whether it was day or night, and perhaps in Talmudic sages' days they knew with certainty it was day or the opposite — so appears to me the Rif and Rosh's reason that they did not permit circumcising on Shabbat in doubtful eight-day-old unless completion, etc.; and this is Shulchan Aruch's opinion; and I do not know why Rama ruled leniently on Shabbat against Rif and Rosh's opinion; again I saw that Rama in Darkei Moshe wrote that Rif and Rosh also hold we circumcise from doubt and did not need to write it; and in Derishah he brought them — and it is a very remote way from several matters and no reasoning that it should be obvious to them; and in the Gemara they needed to say thus in the name of Rav Ada bar Ahavah; also in Derishah he disputed him. And so the meaning of Tosafot in chapter R' Eliezer of Milah that I mentioned — we require no deficiency at all in his nails and hair even in doubt — see there; and so Nekudot HaKesef in chapter HaErel — even with completion of his signs we would have to be stringent, only in this we rely on doubt; and so Rambam and Ritva — until here; and certainly it is not proper to be lenient at all against these to circumcise on Shabbat if his signs were not completed, as it appears to me; and so Maharshal ruled.
