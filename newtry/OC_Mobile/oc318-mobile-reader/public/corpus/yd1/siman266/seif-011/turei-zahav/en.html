One who was born in the seventh month. The Tur writes that he must be complete. The Beit Yosef understands this to mean complete in his limbs, while the completion of his hair and nails is not essential. I find this difficult, since no requirement of limb development is found elsewhere regarding circumcision. The Gemara identifies an eight-month infant by incomplete hair and nails, and the halacha follows that view. It therefore appears that even a definite seventh-month infant must show completion of these signs. The Tur's concise phrase, “complete,” means merely that no clear defect is visible; regarding an eight-month infant he requires clear completion of the hair and nails. The Tur later rules leniently even in a doubtful seventh-month case, based on the reasoning that if the infant is viable the circumcision is proper, and if not, cutting the flesh is not a prohibited wound. The Rif and Rosh, however, appear to require completed signs in a doubtful case before permitting circumcision on Shabbos. Their concern is that what is doubtful to us may have been ascertainable to the sages, and our lack of expertise should not create a leniency. Tosafos, the Nimukei Yosef, the Rambam, and the Ritva also indicate that the signs must be complete. One should therefore not circumcise such an infant on Shabbos unless the hair and nails are fully developed. This is also the conclusion of my revered father-in-law.
