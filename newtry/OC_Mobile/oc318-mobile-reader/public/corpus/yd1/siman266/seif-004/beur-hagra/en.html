They circumcised, etc. That which the Gemara states, “If he did not grind,” etc., refers entirely to the period before the circumcision. The circumcision is postponed if it is impossible to chew the medicine with one's teeth, even though afterward the child might be endangered. Nevertheless, one may not circumcise him and thereby bring him into that danger. The same applies when there is no hot water with which to wash him after the circumcision. This resolves Tosafos' question there, s.v. Lo'es. After the circumcision, however, if the medicinal ingredients became scattered, all necessary acts may be performed in the ordinary manner, just as suction is permitted. This is clear from the Gemara's statement, “It is obvious, since they desecrate,” etc. See Tosafos to Eruvin 67b, s.v. Veha, and this is also the ruling of the Rif and Rambam; see there.
(Likkut) They circumcised, etc. Ramban maintains that even if the medicinal ingredients became scattered before the circumcision, one performs the circumcision in the ordinary manner. The circumcision is not postponed on that account, even though afterward it will become necessary to violate a Torah prohibition, because once there is danger we do not take this into consideration. According to him, the incident of the infant whose hot water spilled refers to hot water needed before the circumcision. The Baal HaMaor and Rashba disagree, as stated there. Rashba adduces proof from the beginning of Yom Tov, where the Mishnah states that one may not slaughter unless he has earth already prepared, yet all agree that if he slaughtered, etc. Thus here too, he would slaughter first, after which the obligation of covering the blood would arise automatically, etc. The Geonim likewise explain that the infant's spilled hot water was needed after the circumcision. Rashba writes that the Mishnah permitting one to chew with his teeth or wrap the remedy around his finger, etc., refers to a case in which the need became known only after the circumcision, and even then it is done with an unusual method. Tosafos there, s.v. Lo'es, also takes this view, though afterward Tosafos concludes that the same applies even before the circumcision; see there and in the Ran. According to the Gra's text, however, this applies specifically before the circumcision, when only a rabbinic prohibition performed with an unusual method was permitted. After the circumcision, one may act in the ordinary manner. This is also indicated by the language of the Rambam and the Tur and Shulchan Aruch here: “They circumcised,” etc., “they perform,” etc., meaning in the ordinary manner. It is further indicated by the Rambam and the Tur and Shulchan Aruch in se'if 6, who write, “All preparatory acts,” etc. This shows that the preparatory acts under discussion are those needed before the circumcision, because acts needed afterward are no longer permitted because of the mitzvah of circumcision, but because of danger. A rabbinic prohibition performed with an unusual method was permitted just as a rabbinic prohibition performed through a gentile was permitted. (End of Likkut.)
