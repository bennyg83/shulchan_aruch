They circumcised, etc. Meaning from Shabbos — if he did not chew, etc., all before circumcision we postpone circumcision if he cannot chew with his teeth even if afterward there will be danger — nevertheless we do not circumcise him lest he come to this; and likewise when he has no hot water to wash him after circumcision; and with this the Tosafot's question there s.v. lo'es is resolved; but after circumcision they dispersed — permitted in the usual way all, and as permitted to suckle; and as written plainly from that they violate, etc.; and Tosafot Eruvin 67b s.v. vehalo, etc., and so Rif and Rambam — see there and there. (Likkut) They circumcised, etc. Ramban's opinion that even if they dispersed before circumcision we circumcise in the usual way, for we do not postpone circumcision even though afterward he will need to violate biblically, since there is danger we are not concerned; and that infant regarding whom hot water spilled — that is hot water before circumcision; but Raavad and Rashba dispute him, as written; and Rashba brought from what is written at beginning of Yom Tov — he does not slaughter unless he has a knife stuck, and they agree that if he slaughtered, etc., likewise he slaughters and afterward the covering is removed automatically, etc.; and so Geonim explained that infant — hot water after circumcision; and he wrote the Mishnah permitting chewing with teeth — wrapping on his finger, etc., when it was not known except after circumcision, and nevertheless with a change; and so Tosafot there s.v. lo'es, etc.; but afterward he concludes even before circumcision — see there; and in Ran — but according to Levush specifically before circumcision, and rabbinic prohibition through change they permitted; but after circumcision permitted in the usual way; and so he infers Rambam's and Shulchan Aruch's language here "they circumcised, etc., they do," etc., meaning in the usual way; and so from what Rambam and Shulchan Aruch wrote in siman 306 "all preparatives," etc. — evidently before circumcision what is for afterward is not because of milah but because of danger, and they permitted through change as they permitted rabbinic prohibition through a gentile (end).
