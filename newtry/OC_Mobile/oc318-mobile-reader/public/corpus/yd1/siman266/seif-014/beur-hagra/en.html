On the contrary, etc. At first glance, there is no proof from the sacrificial service, because service in the Beis HaMikdash was permitted outright on Shabbos, as stated in Yoma 46b. Circumcision, by contrast, appears merely to override Shabbos rather than to render it entirely permitted, as implied by the statement on 85b, “Just as circumcision,” etc. Saving a life certainly only overrides Shabbos, as Rambam writes at the beginning of chapter 2 of Hilchos Shabbos, and as stated in Menachos 64a, where Rava raised the question, etc. The same should seemingly apply to circumcision. This cannot be maintained, however, because the Gemara there states: “Rather, Rabbi Yehudah said in the name of Rav, and Rav Huna son of Rav Yehoshua said, they both stated one principle,” etc. Likewise, in Shabbos there the Gemara asks, “Who is the Tanna who taught: if he separated,” etc. If there were such a distinction, the ruling would apply even according to the Sages. The same is indicated there on the first side of the folio: “And both cases were necessary,” etc. It is therefore clear that there is no distinction. As stated there, according to the Sages, both on Shabbos and during the week the procedure is performed with three people and three baskets, etc. Regarding the statement there see Be'er HaGolah, “I acted,” etc., this was permitted even initially. The same is written in Orach Chaim.
