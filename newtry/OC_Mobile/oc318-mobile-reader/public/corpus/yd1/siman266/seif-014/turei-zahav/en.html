One must be careful that they not circumcise, etc. In Beit Yosef he derives this from that which we learn in chapter R' Eliezer of Milah — they gather the foreskin; Rashi explained: the strands that prevent; and if he did not gather — karet punishment; and they challenge: whose karet is this? Rav Kahana said: the craftsman is punished with karet — he violated Shabbat and did not fulfill a mitzvah; Rav Pappa challenged him: let him say to them — I did half the mitzvah, you did half the mitzvah; Rashi explained: meaning since when he began he began permissibly and a wound made permissibly — why is he liable? Let him say to you — you finish the work — end quote; and Beit Yosef wrote one should infer from his words that although there is no karet liability, nevertheless there is prohibition, since he did not complete the mitzvah; and according to this one must be careful not to circumcise with two craftsmen, etc.; and we wonder at his words — for if there were any reasoning for prohibition in this, what was Rav Pappa's difficulty — perhaps that reasoning helps also for karet liability; and who divided it to divide in this and challenge? Rather it is clear there is not even prohibition at all; and Rashi who wrote "why is he liable" — because in the braita it said liable — the challenger too said why liable, and it does not come for precision to say there is prohibition. And already the practice in many great places is two mohalim on Shabbat — this one circumcises and this one performs periah; and in Levush he wrote to dispute this ruling and agreed there is a hint of prohibition in Rashi's words — only he distinguished between Rashi's words and this ruling; also Maharshal distinguished differently; but it appears to me Rashi never intended to say there is prohibition there, and it is impossible to say there is prohibition in the matter as above.
