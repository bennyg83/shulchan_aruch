He lengthened unnecessarily, because the Rama himself rejected all the Beit Yosef’s arguments in the Darkei Moshe and in responsum 76, and there brought extensive proofs to permit it. He was concerned only because of a certain compilation he had found, perhaps because he knew that it rested on the authority of a great scholar. In any event, the custom has already spread to permit it, as the Ateret Zahav and Bach also write. The Beit Yosef himself writes in Bedek HaBayit that Orchot Chaim cites the Baal HaItur: Two mohalim may perform a circumcision on Shabbos, one performing milah and the other periah. The first bears no liability for making the wound because the second completes the mitzvah, as Rav Pappa said, I perform half the mitzvah and you perform the other half.
The Bedek HaBayit cites a responsum of the Rashbatz concerning the teaching that one may not set sail fewer than three days before Shabbos. The Baal HaMaor explains that the reason is that the voyage will inevitably lead to Shabbos desecration, so it appears that the traveler stipulated in advance that Shabbos would be desecrated. Since the three days before Shabbos are considered connected to Shabbos, the Rashbatz derives that one may not circumcise a convert on Thursday, lest the third day after circumcision occur on Shabbos and require Shabbos desecration. Likewise, if a sick child recovered on Thursday, one should wait until Friday to circumcise him. These words do not appear correct. The Gemara, Shabbos 19a, states regarding setting sail that the prohibition applies only to an optional journey, while travel for a mitzvah is permitted. All the poskim agree, as explained in Orach Chaim, siman 248, and there is no greater mitzvah than circumcision. Furthermore, the Rashbatz necessarily maintains that only the third day after circumcision overrides Shabbos, and not an earlier day, which is why he permits circumcision on Friday. But according to the Maggid Mishneh in chapter 2 of Hilchot Shabbos, citing some commentators, the Ramban, and the Rashba, the second day certainly also overrides Shabbos. The Rambam in his Commentary to the Mishnah, the Bartenura, and Rashi in the chapter Rabbi Akiva write likewise. According to that view, a circumcision not performed at its proper time that falls on Thursday would have to be postponed until after Shabbos, which is impossible. The Mishnah teaches that a child may be circumcised on the eighth through the twelfth day, but neither earlier nor later. If he was born at twilight and two festival days of Rosh Hashanah precede Shabbos, he is circumcised on the twelfth day. Yet if the two festival days fell on Tuesday and Wednesday, the twelfth day would be Thursday. According to the Rashbatz, since this circumcision is not at its proper time and does not itself override Shabbos, it should be postponed from Thursday until Sunday, making the fifteenth day. The Mishnah clearly excludes that possibility. Therefore, even a delayed circumcision may be performed on Thursday. Even those poskim who maintain that only the third day after circumcision overrides Shabbos may agree that a delayed circumcision is permitted on Thursday, as I have demonstrated from the view of most poskim. This appears to be the principal ruling.
