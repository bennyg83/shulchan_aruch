(There, seif 8) Even the second festival day of the Diaspora. This is difficult, for there is a compound doubt: perhaps it is the child's proper day and perhaps the day is actually a weekday. This resembles doubtful preparation on the second festival day, which the Mechaber permits in Orach Chaim 537:4. Perhaps there both doubts concern rabbinic law, whereas here they concern Torah law. Yet the prohibition of a matter that will later become permitted may not apply to a mitzvah, because the mitzvah should be performed promptly. Besamim Rosh 88 and 253 holds that a delayed circumcision overrides the second festival day. At least in a doubt concerning the proper day, where no explicit dissent is found, one may rely on this. A handwritten note by the av beit din of Berlin likewise permits circumcising on the second festival day where it is uncertain whether the infant is eight or nine days old. Tashbetz, part 3, responsum 284, says a doubtful circumcision does not override the second festival day, but his proof addresses a case certainly past its proper time and does not establish the doubtful case.
