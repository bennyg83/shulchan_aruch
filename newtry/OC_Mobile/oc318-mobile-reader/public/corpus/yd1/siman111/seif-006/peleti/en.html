I say to the place of the first issur, etc. — he challenged: why need the place of the first issur — behold this pot is forbidden, and if so it is obvious we suspend the issur's falling into that pot. The Bach resolved: even for d'oraisa issur, where we do not say "I say," regarding this we say by force of this reasoning we suspend to the place where the first fell, etc.; and the later authorities challenged that this too is only a mere reasoning — Heaven forbid to rely on it in d'oraisa doubt — and they resolved because of the latter clause. The Shach resolved that here it is worse since it had presumption of heter only now that it fell — and this is strained. It appears to me the Mechaber, according to his view that we do not say chein nafsho for other issurim — one may say it deals with issur falling in a pot that had only the taste of olives established, and thus the pot is forbidden; afterward heter increased by two olives and the pot returned to heter (and it deals with wet in wet where squeezing out is not possible, forbidden); afterward another piece fell and it is unknown whether heter or issur — if we judge, since the pot is in presumption of issur, this piece too we establish in presumption of issur, for otherwise the pot is forbidden — that is, the pot is permitted because heter increased upon it and it returns to heter; but if we judge, since issur was established at the issur's falling, this pot too was established that issur fell there and the pot is forbidden — and what of that afterward they returned to heter? On this the Mechaber intended.
