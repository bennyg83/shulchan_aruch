"And it is known into which it fell — explained in Torat HaBayit, and this is its language: and it does not have enough to separate, and its minority is forbidden, etc., until here. One should not challenge: if so, behold it was already certainly forbidden, the one — and this is that which the beginning of the siman inquires, one pot heter and one issur, that we follow leniency; it appears it teaches here that even though there is no Torah issur here, for it is nullified in the majority, only that it was forbidden because of minority d'rabbanan without sixty — we feared not to suspend to issur here; it teaches us. And we find such reasoning in siman 129: if we suspend on blood of purification to save her friend — there is a dispute there in the Tur's words and Maharach wrote that in this law, even if d'oraisa issur fell, we suspend to be lenient, for all that is written here from two pots of heter and two pieces before them until the end of the siman — there is no distinction between d'oraisa and d'rabbanan issur; and so Issur VeHeter, general rule 26, seif 13, end of his words — and it is not correct at all, for it is proven in chapter ha'Areil (Yevamot daf 82) that for every d'oraisa issur we do not say "I say," and here all permissions are by force of "I say" to the place where the first issur fell, etc. And explicitly Torat HaBayit wrote initially: two pots, one heter and one issur, issur fell into one of them, etc., that issur must be nullified in the majority, and he adjoined: if two pots of heter, etc., I say to the place where the first issur fell the second also fell, from the reason we said, end of his words — behold he tied the reason of heter to what is above, and we require it be permitted from the Torah at least, whether in the issur itself that is only d'rabbanan or in the measure of its nullification that is permitted from the Torah even though the issur itself is from the Torah; and so in all that he cited until the end of the siman — Heaven forbid to be lenient here in d'oraisa issur and d'oraisa measure, such as not nullified in the majority. And another clear proof from siman 107 regarding eggs or fish that we are concerned lest they remained at the end, etc. — why do we not say as here that whatever entered doubt of issur all combines? Rather necessarily there it is species in not its species — the impure fish has another name than the pure fish — and therefore Torah sixty is required; and here we do not say leniency in the whole siman except where permitted from the Torah. And in Issur VeHeter end of general rule 26 he copied the Tur's words on these matters from the beginning of the siman until the law: I say to the place where the first fell, etc., and wrote on this: it appears that in all this there is no distinction whether issur is d'oraisa or d'rabbanan, end of his words — behold he included all laws of this siman; and his intent is that even d'rabbanan measure, as written, is itself before d'oraisa issur and heter greater than issur — and this is plain to the understanding.
