"Combine." The Shach wrote that in Issur VeHeter he wrote one resolution: specifically when neither alone has enough to nullify it, only in both together; but if one has enough to nullify and the second does not, we do not say in this that it is permitted because it entered doubt — for that one which has enough to nullify did not enter doubt at all. And it appears clear that even according to what we establish chein nafsho for all issurim, nevertheless they combine, for we say chein nafsho only where it is known where it fell; but here where we do not know, we say we view them as if they were mixed at the outset at the time of falling and everything entered doubt, end of his words.
"Will become mixed." The Taz and Shach wrote, and it appears that even for one person, if it is known clearly that they will not become mixed — for example, one of meat and the second of milk, or one has something sweet and the second something pickled like vinegar, or the like — they do not combine.
"To permit." The Taz wrote, and these are his words: it appears to me astonishing at this ruling that whatever entered doubt combines to nullify it — why did he rule above, siman 105, seif 15, that if it is not known whether he touched all of them all are forbidden; why do we not say that all combine to nullify?
"That he mix them." And the Taz disagrees on this and rules that even if they were not mixed, since by Torah law it is permitted through nullification in the majority but d'rabbanan requires sixty, and since there is doubt into which it fell and both entered doubt — both combine to nullify and each is permitted by itself, since they will become mixed we view them as if already mixed, end of his words (and see siman 23).
