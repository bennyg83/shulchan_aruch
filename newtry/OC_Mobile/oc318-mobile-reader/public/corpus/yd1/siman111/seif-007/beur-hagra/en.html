"If there were here, etc." Mishnah chapter 4 of Terumot: Two baskets and two storerooms, etc.
"And not only, etc." Tosefta chapter 6 and Yerushalmi chapter 4: Two baskets in two attics and two storerooms in one attic; and the Yerushalmi says there: But two storerooms in two attics — no. What is the difference between baskets and storerooms? Baskets are accustomed to be emptied, storerooms are not accustomed to be emptied. And Rabbi Shimon wrote there: According to this reason it applies when all six belong to another person who is accustomed to empty and mix — and now that which the Tosefta teaches, in this one a hundred and in that one not a hundred — I say it fell into the one of a hundred — what is the difference? Rather because of two people it was needed. And all this is that we suspend to be lenient in what is permitted from the Torah, as written in the earlier books; and likewise they said (there) in Yerushalmi on the baraita of Reish Lakish: He pressed in a circle, etc., s.v. they ascend, etc. — Rabbi Avin said one needs two litra in each, and all the more so enough to be nullified in the majority — meaning, with the litra; and even though it is d'rabbanan issur, one may say he holds like Rabbi Elazar in chapter ha'Areil (2a) that even for d'rabbanan a majority is required; and for Rabbi Yochanan two litra suffice; nevertheless for d'oraisa there must be permitted dry in dry by majority and species not in its species wet in wet by sixty — and here, species in its species by majority, as above.
"And some, etc." According to the Raah, that even species in its species wet in wet — no, only dry in dry, or d'rabbanan issurim and d'rabbanan measures; and therefore, s.k. 20.
"However, etc." In Yerushalmi there it does not appear so, for they say: Rabbi Zeira, Rabbi Chiya in the name of Rabbi Shimon — before him were two baskets; in this fifty se'ah and in that fifty se'ah; a se'ah of terumah fell into one of them and he does not know into which — if he wished to separate from this he separates, from that he separates, half from this and half from that he separates; and likewise two mikvaot, etc. — thus it deals when they were not mixed, only to satisfy the stringent; since it is possible he satisfies even the stringent, and there is no concern of nullifying issur l'chatchila, for they combine when dry; but when wet it is that they do not combine, because he will taste it in eating or it is fit for noten taam; and since he mixes before eating it is permitted, as written in Yerushalmi chapter 4 of Terumot on the mishnah: Black figs elevate the white ones, etc., and it says he combines and permits; and likewise wheat — he grinds and permits.
