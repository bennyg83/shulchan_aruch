Combine — see Kereti who challenged that this is chein nafsho; see there what the later authorities resolved with difficulty; and see above beginning of siman 92. I find it difficult: why chein nafsho from here — we hold squeezing out is forbidden; if so, at least that pot is forbidden — how will the second pot help nullify? Therefore one may say: certainly if it were d'rabbanan issur all this would not be needed, for we say "I say" — all this is d'oraisa issur and therefore sixty is required; and chein nafsho, squeezing out — all is d'rabbanan, and if so the matter returns to "I say" and combination is not needed; it requires further study.
And some permit — the later authorities stood on this from what is taught: two paths, one tamei and one tahor, and both went, one on this and one on that; we hold: if they come to ask one after the other, both are tahor and we suspend each on the tahor path he took — and if so here too with two people, if they come to ask one after the other we purify them and say to each that heter fell. The main challenge is below in laws of niddah regarding two women who found a stain on one garment — likewise the challenge. The main resolution, as written: with two paths, if one comes to ask a sage — I walked one path and do not know which — he need not mention that his fellow also went on one of the two paths; thus the sage asked only about him permits; and so for two. But here, if one comes to ask a sage about an issur piece that fell into a pot, the sage certainly forbids unless he mentions his fellow's pot too, that there are two pots and it is not known into which — and since he must mention his fellow's, it is as if asked about him and his fellow, and forbidden; investigate.
