Both combine. And the same even for a hundred — whatever entered doubt because of issur combines to nullify it — Tur from Semak; and in the Arukh he wrote one resolution: specifically when neither alone has enough to nullify, only in both together; but if one has enough to nullify and the second does not, we do not say in this that it is permitted because it entered doubt — for that one which has enough to nullify did not enter doubt at all — for even if you say it fell inside, it is permitted except for the measure of \"I say\" in that which has enough to nullify it fell, end of his words; and the Beit Yosef brings it; and although he wrote there another resolution, nevertheless for the law both may stand; and also Darkei Moshe brought this resolution plainly, and I brought it in s.k. 18; and the Raah in Beit HaBechirah agreed to this resolution; and accordingly where \"I say\" does not apply, such as d'oraisa issur, it is forbidden; and even without this the Raah disagrees that two pots combine, as written in s.k. 20:
Combine, etc. The Bach wrote that this is specifically according to the view that we do not say chein nafsho except for meat and milk and not for other issurim; but for us, who establish chein nafsho for all issurim, combination does not help even if the pots were mixed afterward unknowingly together, end of his words. It is difficult, for all the later authorities — Issur VeHeter end of general rule 26, Maharshal Piskei HaGahot siman 57, the Rav in Turei Zahav beginning of general rule 51, and the Magen Avraham — all copied the Rashba plainly that two pots combine; and it is strained to say the practical difference is when dry mixed with dry and neither alone has a majority but together they have a majority they combine, for with dry we do not say chein nafsho, as above siman 92, seif 4 in the gloss — for there it deals with two pots, that is, wet; and also strained to say that which the Rav rules here plainly like the Mechaber is according to what is written in siman 92 and in a case of great loss we do not say chein nafsho for wet — for if so why did the Rav bring here the view of those who are stringent, for without that one may not permit except in great loss; and further, Darkei Moshe wondered at the stringent and concluded: I do not know his reason, and if it is a received tradition we accept it; and if it is so, most poskim hold we say chein nafsho for wet, as the Rav wrote in siman 92. Rather it appears clear that even according to what we establish chein nafsho they combine, for we say chein nafsho only where it is known where it fell; but here where we do not know, everything that entered entered doubt and it will become mixed — we view it as already mixed, as the Tur wrote from Semak; and if so, since we view it as already mixed before the falling, it is as if it fell initially into the pot that has enough to nullify the issur — clear proof from the words of the Rav HaMagid, part 9 of Ma'achalot Assurot, law 9, as written in siman 92, s.k. 8, see there (and one may say according to what is written in siman 99, s.k. 15, that where nevelah, even though heter was added afterward before it was known, it is forbidden — that case is different, for afterward it became known where it fell, and thus at the time of falling not everything entered doubt, unlike here where it was never known where it fell, thus everything entered doubt; and investigate):
Will become mixed, etc. The Magen Avraham wrote: it appears to me that even for one person, if it is known clearly that they will not become mixed — for example, one dish of milk and one of meat — they do not combine; and even if both are meat and one person will not eat them until he mixes them together, then the issur is nullified in its taste itself; however he may mix them l'chatchila and there is no nullifying issur l'chatchila here, for the issur is already nullified through combination, end of his words; and so in Darkei Moshe, as the Tur and Mechaber wrote in seif 5: but if one of them has enough to nullify, etc., and these are his words: it appears here it deals when the two pots are not one species and therefore they do not combine together to nullify; but when they are one species and stand to be mixed together, both combine to nullify the issur if they belong to one person. From the Rashba that the Beit Yosef brings, one may explain another resolution: we do not say that two pots combine to nullify except when both are in issur doubt, that is, when neither alone has enough to nullify the issur; but if one has enough to nullify we do not say they combine — so the Rashba resolved, end of his words; and from his words it appears that whatever are two species, even if permitted to mix them, as long as they are not accustomed to become mixed they do not combine; and so Maharshal, Piskei HaGahot, siman 56, and these are his words: the same applies to matters that do not stand to be mixed, even though all are one species — only that one was cooked with sweetness and one with sharpness in vinegar, or the like — they do not combine, end of his words; and it appears the Magen Avraham's view is likewise, and he mentioned meat and milk only as an example, and the same for anything known not to be accustomed to mix:
Of two people, etc. The Bach wrote that the Rashba does not deal here except with d'oraisa issur, therefore he did not write to permit except for one person; but for d'rabbanan issur, although they do not combine, nevertheless we suspend to be lenient with two people when they come one after the other, end of his words. It is not so, and he did not examine Torat HaBayit (daf 113, 2b) where it is explained there that even for d'rabbanan issur we do not suspend to be lenient with two people who come one after the other with two pots of heter or more — because since you must in any case forbid one of them from heter, all are forbidden: on what basis did you forbid this one, lest the opposite — therefore all are forbidden, see there; and nevertheless the Rashba's words are weak; and presently the Ramban's words that the Rashba brought there: he explained that the case of a litra of dried figs deals when they belong to one person or two who come to ask in one house; but if they come one after the other we suspend to be lenient, and when there is one pot of issur, even if they come in one house we suspend to be lenient — end, for thus it is necessarily proven from chapter 1 of Pesachim (daf 10) and chapter ha'Ishah she'Nitarmelah (Ketubot daf 26) that even when both are heter we suspend to be lenient for d'rabbanan issur when they come one after the other, see there. And what the Rashba challenged on him — if so, he should have explained that mishnah of a litra of dried figs that it deals with one person or one house — it requires further study, for there it does not deal with suspending to be lenient at all, only with combination where Rabbi Eliezer and Rabbi Yehoshua disagree whether lower ones elevate upper ones; and likewise what the Rashba further challenged from that two people do not combine since they will not mix — according to the Ramban, combination is not needed either, for there too it deals only with combination and not suspending to be lenient, and it deals when both came in one house — and all this is clear; and so they ruled in Hagahot Maimoniyot of Chullin that even two pots of heter we suspend to be lenient with two people one after the other for d'rabbanan issur, and the Bach ruled; and perhaps the Taz and other later authorities who copied the Rashba's words agree to this — that although the Tur also copied regarding two pots of heter and there is no distinction whether of one person or two, one may say it deals with d'oraisa issur; and so what they copied here that with two people they are forbidden appears to deal when they came in one house, as I explained:
For need of loss. That is great loss, as written in siman 108, s.k. 12, see there; and so it appears in the law that one may not permit except in great loss, for the Raah in Bedek HaBayit (daf 113, 2b) disagrees with the Rashba on this, and these are his words: these leniencies of two baskets combining were not said except for d'rabbanan issurim or Torah issur with d'rabbanan measures, and likewise dry in dry where there is no taste, or wet where there is sixty (except terumah and the like whose measure is more than sixty); but where there is taste — certainly wherever it gives taste or is fit to give taste, such as wet in wet, species in its species, without sixty — we do not say these leniencies, even for d'rabbanan issurim; and this law was not mentioned in anything that sits to give taste, for taste is never nullified, or fit to impart taste even if there is no taste yet, such as species in its species; end of his words.
