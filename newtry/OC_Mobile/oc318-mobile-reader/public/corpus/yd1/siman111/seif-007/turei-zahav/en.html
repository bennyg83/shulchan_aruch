"And some are stringent that even for one person, etc." In Darkei Moshe likewise in the name of Issur VeHeter end of general rule 26 in the name of Maharish who wrote: we do not rule so; and so in general rule 12. Darkei Moshe wondered: why do we not establish thus, for there is no dispute on this; and below he will copy the words of Issur VeHeter. It appears to me astonishing at this ruling that whatever entered doubt combines to nullify it — why did he rule above in Shulchan Aruch, siman 105, seif 15: if it is not known whether he touched all of them, all are forbidden — why do we not say all combine to nullify? And for a similar reason he wrote in Issur VeHeter, general rule 12, seif 9, that we do not establish like this ruling of siman 14.
"Except after he mixes them." Derishah uPerishah wrote, and these are his words: however, in Issur VeHeter, general rule 26, Maharish responded that we do not rule so, even for one person; and even for one who would permit, one may not eat except after they mix together, for then the taste of issur is already nullified, end of his words. It appears to me astonishing at what he wrote that one who permits does not permit except after mixing — this is not so, for the Rashba permits, and he brings in Torat HaBayit HaArukh, daf 114, Yerushalmi: Rabbi Zeira and Rabbi Chiya in the name of Rabbi Shimon — before him two baskets, in this fifty se'ah and in that fifty se'ah; a se'ah of terumah fell into one and it is not known into which — if he wished to separate from this he separates, from that he separates, half from this and half from that he separates; and therefore if in one there were a hundred and in one fifty, I say they fell into the hundred — and from there he raises and not from the fifty, end, meaning for giving the kohen's portion one gives only from where terumah was nullified. And if you say there is no heter except after mixing — how is it fit to say he separates half from this and half from that? Behold they are already mixed and all became one; otherwise all is forbidden. And further: if there is no heter except after mixing, why did he use the language that both combine — combination is not applicable here since they are already one through mixing; therefore it appears plain that even if they were not mixed, since by Torah law it is permitted through nullification in the majority but d'rabbanan requires sixty, and since there is doubt into which it fell and both entered doubt — both combine to nullify and each is permitted by itself, since they will become mixed we view them as already mixed. And what Issur VeHeter wrote: even for one who permits — he did not intend the Rashba's view who permits here, rather: one should not rule thus for halachah to be lenient, and even one who wishes to permit for halachah should at least not permit except after mixing; but forever the Rashba's view is permitted even without mixing — that after mixing it is permitted even for two people, for one may say that with two people it is nevertheless forbidden l'chatchila to mix because of nullifying issur l'chatchila, as above siman 99, seif 5; and for one person it is permitted l'chatchila to mix — for if you say permitted l'chatchila to mix because we view it as already mixed, we too say that in truth they are permitted even without mixing, since we view them as already mixed. Rather necessarily in truth it is so, only he was stringent here to mix first — and this is also the Rama's intent here. I wrote all this because Maharach wrote that the permission here is only for those who hold chein nafsho applies only to meat and milk, as written in siman 92 and 99; but for us, who establish chein nafsho for all issurim, there is no permission — for what fell into one of them became nevelah — this does not appear at all, for since both entered doubt it is as if they were already in one vessel and afterward fell there, as we proved shortly — and this is a plain, clear matter: for even according to those who say chein nafsho for other issurim, nevertheless one does not nullify issur l'chatchila — and if so, since here we left that rule and say we nullify l'chatchila, it itself also helps for everyone regarding not calling it nevelah — so it appears clear and plain to the understanding.
