And the same law if there was only one pot of slaughtered meat and one of these two pieces fell into it, etc. It appears that here it deals with when the two pieces were recognizable by themselves before falling which was heter and which issur; but if they mixed first one with one, then since issur was established in both pieces each is judged as the substance of issur and we no longer say a d'rabbanan doubt to be lenient. Shach, s.k. 4. Knesset HaGedolah in Hagahot HaTur note 5, Yeshuot Yaakov note 1, Chavat Daat note 3. However, Peri Chadash note 3 disagreed with the Shach and concluded that even when issur was established, a d'rabbanan doubt is lenient; and if so here in every case it deals whether the pieces were recognizable or not, and even though issur was established we suspend to be lenient — see there. And Peri Toar note 2 agreed; Peleti note 2; Kereti note 4; Lechem HaPanim note 5; Binyamin Zeev note 4; Siftei Daat note 4; and so Beit Meir in Kuntras Dinei Kavu'a, daf 38a, top of the page, on the Peri Chadash — the law is with him. And so Chacham Adam, klal 64, note 4: therefore, although regarding eating the pieces both are forbidden, nevertheless the pot is permitted and we establish the pot on its presumption — end. And therefore l'chatchila one should be concerned for the Shach and his colleagues, and in a case of loss one may be lenient.
