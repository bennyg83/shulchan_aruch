"If there were two, etc." And it means where they do not combine — for example, two storerooms in two attics, as written in seif 7 and the like, as written in Yerushalmi chapter 4: You say baskets ascend and storerooms do not ascend — how is it done? Two storerooms in two attics and a se'ah of terumah fell into one of them — both are forbidden; and likewise regarding a mikveh in Tosefta of Mikvaot chapter 2, and the Rosh brought it, chapter 2: Two mikvaot that do not have forty se'ah and three log fell into one of them and it is not known into which it fell, and afterward rain fell and they were filled — Rabbi Yosi says they tell him not to immerse in either of them, etc., and on this.
"And even for d'rabbanan issur." From the mishnah, chapter 4 of Terumot, a litra of dried figs, etc., and all are forbidden, as written at the beginning of Yoma (4a).
"But if there is, etc." Tosefta there (chapter 6 of Terumot): In this one a hundred and in that one not a hundred — I say it fell into the one of a hundred; and there (chapter 2 of Mikvaot): One has forty se'ah and one does not have forty se'ah — I say they fell into the one of forty.
