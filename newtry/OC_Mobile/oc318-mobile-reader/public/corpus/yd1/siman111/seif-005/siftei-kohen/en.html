Forbidden — both of them. Because we do not say \"I say\" except where we suspend the issur into issur — we do not forbid the issur more than it already was; but here, where both are heter, if you suspend the issur in one and permit the second, behold you forbid one of them from its permissibility — and if so, since you forbid in any case one of them, on what basis did you forbid this one, lest the opposite — and therefore both are forbidden — so the Rashba in Torat HaBayit; and likewise we say below, siman 129, seif 43, and likewise regarding a stain, see there; and accordingly it appears explicitly that even with two pots of heter that are not equal but one small and one large, we cannot permit the large and say issur fell into the small — for in the end you forbid one of them from heter — on what basis, etc.; and the Rashba and those who follow him did not distinguish this at all, and so it appears to me, and not as Issur VeHeter, general rule 29, seif 5, who wrote we suspend to be lenient that it fell on the small one — see siman 151, seif 68, side 3:
But if one of them has, etc. \"I say\" the issur fell into that one which has enough to nullify — Tur from Semak; and this is specifically d'rabbanan issur, but for d'oraisa we do not say \"I say,\" for d'oraisa doubt is stringent, as below, s.k. 14:
Both permitted. And there is no distinction whether the two pots belong to one person or two people, end of Tur from Semak; and in the Arukh it is explained that he wrote so to exclude the Ramban, who holds that when belonging to two people and they come to ask one after the other, even if neither has enough to nullify we permit each one and suspend that it fell in his fellow's — see below, s.k. 19, the main view follows the Ramban. The Bach challenged: why do we not say the two pots combine to nullify when they belong to one person, as below, end of seif 7? The Shach resolved that here, since there are two pieces, it is worse; and so Maharshal in his hagahot to the Tur, and the Perishah brings it, seif 10 — and it is not reasonable to distinguish this also in the Rashba's words in Torat HaBayit, for it appears he makes no distinction here, only that here it does not deal with the law of \"I say\" but with combination he does not deal here at all; and it is possible here it deals with those cases in which combination below does not help, as written in s.k. 18; and so in Darkei Moshe; and in Derishah, seif 9, I again saw in Maharshal, Piskei HaGahot, siman 56, that he himself retracted his words and explained like the Shach, except that afterward he wrote at the end of the siman that for the law it is a sound reasoning that with two fallings they do not combine, see there — and this reasoning is weak; and so in Darkei Moshe that I brought below, s.k. 18, it appears explicitly that with two fallings they combine:
