And one may say: here it deals with belonging to two people, for the Tur wrote here that there is no distinction whether of one person or two; and in the Perishah he wrote in the name of Rashal that here it is different because there are two fallings, one heter and one issur, and this is worse than below where there is only one falling — and so Maharach; and he strengthened this reasoning, and these are his words: thus it is proven from our master's words in Orach Chaim, laws of chametz, that he did not distinguish between two people and one person except regarding one pile of chametz and two checked houses, etc., and he did not write so regarding two piles, one chametz and one matzah, and two houses that he mentioned before — rather certainly, with two piles, since the mouse certainly put chametz in the checked house, we do not suspend to be lenient — until here his language; and he was not precise here, for two paths themselves are like two piles and two houses, for there is one tamei path and one tahor and two people went, one on each, and each purified himself, and even so we distinguish there between two people and one person. And further: there they are more lenient with two people than one person, and here at the end of the siman they are more stringent with two people — rather necessarily this topic does not apply here. And the distinction itself that I cited in the name of the great ones above, between two fallings and one falling, appears not to exist, for the Rashba in Torat HaBayit HaArukh wrote, and all the more so when there is only one issur piece that fell into one of them, there is room to suspend to be lenient and say it fell into issur, for we did not see anything fall into heter — until here his language implies one law for them, only that it is all the more so from the law of two fallings; and whence do we distinguish and say that with one issur and one heter there are two fallings and one falling equal, but regarding two of heter there is a distinction regarding combination? For abundance of the matter I bring another proof from Torat HaBayit HaArukh, daf 113, that brings there from chapter ha'Areil: two baskets, one hullin and one terumah, and before them two se'ah, one hullin and one terumah, and they fell one into the other — permitted, "I say" terumah into terumah, etc.; and they disagree there, Rabbi Yochanan and Reish Lakish — Reish Lakish holds we require hullin in the se'ah to exceed terumah in the basket; Rabbi Yochanan holds even if hullin did not exceed terumah. The Rashba analyzed there: even though Rabbi Yochanan does not require increase, nevertheless we require hullin equal to terumah, but not if hullin are less than terumah. He challenged: why not be lenient even when hullin are less — what is different from that which we learned in Tosefta Terumot: two spice-measures, one hullin and one terumah, he put from one and it is not known from which — I say from hullin he put — thus we always suspend to be lenient in their matters? Meaning: there is no heter at all, only what he put from one of them; and it appears to me that since terumah certainly fell here, only it is not known into which — not in this case is it a d'rabbanan doubt to be lenient, end. And if there were a distinction between two fallings and one, he should not have challenged at all from two fallings to one falling, lest there also be a distinction between them — rather certainly there is no such distinction at all. And the essence: the Rashba there challenges Rabbeinu Shimon who distinguishes between two people and one person, as written here at the end of the siman — why was this distinction not mentioned in the Gemara, and on this he disagreed with him, only from stringency he did not wish to be lenient against Rabbeinu Shimon; and if there were a distinction between two fallings and one, he should also have challenged why he did not distinguish this. One who examines Torat HaBayit HaArukh will see the Rashba labored to explain all distinctions in this law — why did he not also explain this distinction to clarify whether there is a distinction between two fallings and one? Rather certainly it does not exist at all. Regarding the challenge above in the Tur and Rashba's words: one who examines the Rashba there in the order of his words will see there is no challenge at all — for initially he wrote there that the Ramban holds there is a distinction here with two baskets of hullin into which terumah fell, like the case of a litra of dried figs that he brings there, that nullification is required and we do not say d'rabbanan doubt to be lenient — when they belong to one person or two who come to ask in one house, but like two paths; but if they belong to two people, nullification is not required at all, even if in both there is only a minority of terumah, nevertheless permitted because each says it fell in his fellow's, like two paths. The Rashba disagrees there that even with two people there is no heter here except through nullification specifically; but if there is nothing to nullify and you wish to permit by saying on each pot that it did not fall there but in the other — that is not so; not to speak of one person, which is obvious, for in any case it is issur to him, but even with two people we do not say each suspends on his fellow, since if you suspend on him you forbid him because he lacks measure to nullify. On this he said: there is no distinction between one person, etc.; and that which he wrote in what case when neither has enough — he did not mean that in both there is enough to nullify, only that perhaps in both there is not enough to nullify; rather here it deals where in any case one does not have enough to nullify — in this there is no distinction between one and two, and forbidden. Regarding combination of two he adjoined this so that afterward he would write there is a distinction between one person and two regarding combination — and why did he write in the Arukh truth that combination of both helps with two pots of heter, and did not distinguish whether one has enough to nullify? Because there he wrote according to his view that combination helps even with two people, unlike Rabbeinu Shimon who distinguishes. But the short version was stringent like Rabbeinu Shimon, that with two people combination does not help; therefore he did not write the law of combination first and relied on what he would distinguish afterward between one person and two regarding combination. All these matters are plain — no challenge arises except for one who does not examine well and thinks that which the Tur wrote above, and there is no distinction whether the two pots belong to one person, etc., refers to that which he said that specifically in one of them there must be enough to nullify — this is not so; it does not deal above except with the matter that nullification is in any case required, unlike the Ramban, and it does not yet deal with combination; but he wrote his law afterward regarding combination, that there is a distinction between one person and two. That which the Rashba cited here his law with two fallings intends to teach that even with two "I say" we suspend to be lenient, even with two of heter; and he cites wherever "I say" applies, and teaches here that truly there is no distinction between two fallings and one falling, even with two pots of heter. And although afterward in the law "I say" to the place where the first fell the second also fell, he cited only one falling — there it is no difference except to suspend the second on the first; and in this there is no greater leniency in two than in one, for there we know where the first fell — therefore he also cited in the second time one falling, only that it is known into which the second fell. One may challenge how the Rashba in the Arukh rejected the Ramban's words above, that he holds no nullification is needed with two people one after the other, from that below, if they belong to two people nullification is required and even combination does not help — nevertheless it is difficult from what the Ramban brought as proof from two paths that are permitted one after the other, and what is different here that even with two people one after the other nullification is specifically required, and even with one falling like below. One must say the Rashba holds we do not learn from two paths, for there it is different because doubt of tumah in the public domain is lenient — therefore we follow leniency there; and this distinction is in Rabbeinu Shimon, chapter 7 of Terumot, regarding two "I say." But from that which we learn in chapter 7 of Terumot in the mishnah: if one ate from one he is exempt because perhaps they are hullin — and Rabbeinu Shimon brings there from Yerushalmi that Rabbi Yochanan challenges this that we should follow Rabbi Yehudah with two paths who exempts with two people one after the other — and he concludes that all agree one after the other — it appears there too the leniency of two paths regarding one after the other exists also for terumah; and if so, at least with one falling why not be lenient one after the other without nullification? And likewise Hagahot Maimoniyot of Chullin learns from Rabbi Yochanan's two paths that I cited with two pots of heter and issur fell into one — we follow leniency with two people one after the other. I saw Maharach also challenged that they do not combine with two people one after the other — what is different from two paths? — and he brings that Hagahot Mardakhai of Chullin and resolves, and these are his words: it is possible that specifically for d'rabbanan issur we suspend to be lenient when they come to ask one after the other, but for d'oraisa issur we do not suspend to be lenient, as proven in laws of chametz; and since the Rashba wrote these laws plainly, even for d'oraisa issur, therefore he did not write to permit except for one person, end of his words — and he was not precise here, as I will write shortly. It appears to me that for Rabbeinu Shimon one must say that with two people they do not combine to nullify when two people come to ask in one house — then there are two stringencies: one, nullification is required, and second, they do not combine; and for him everything is satisfactory: that litra of dried figs has both leniency and stringency — stringency that nullification is required, leniency that all vessels combine; it deals specifically with one person, but the Raah's view that even with two people one after the other nullification is required — we do not know how to answer what is different from two paths, for in Yerushalmi they make them correspond as I cited above; and investigate.
