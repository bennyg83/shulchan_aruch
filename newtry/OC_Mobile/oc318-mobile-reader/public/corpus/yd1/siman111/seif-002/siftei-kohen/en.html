In what case is this stated that we suspend in all these, etc. All this is the Tur's language from Torat HaBayit; but it is difficult for me, for from the Rashba's words in Torat HaBayit it does not appear so, for he mentioned there only two pots; and afterward he wrote (daf 113, end of 1a), and these are his words: It is difficult regarding d'rabbanan issur — why need a majority for Reish Lakish, and even one in one for Rabbi Yochanan? For since we doubt whether terumah fell into hullin or not, it is a d'rabbanan doubt to be lenient; and we learned in the Tosefta: Before him were two spice-measures, one hullin and one terumah, and he put from one of them and it is not known from which — I say from hullin he put — thus we always suspend to be lenient in their matters; and it appears to me that since terumah certainly fell here, only it is not known into which — not in this case is it a d'rabbanan doubt to be lenient, end of his words. Thus specifically with two pots, because issur certainly fell there, only it is not known into which — for Rabbi Yochanan one in one is required; but with two pieces and one pot it is possible that no issur fell into the pot at all — permitted even if issur pieces are greater than the pot, analogous to putting from two spice-measures and it is not known from which. And in the short Torat HaBayit (house 4, gate 2) he did not write this "in what case" that when issur is greater than heter we do not suspend so much to be lenient, only with two pots; and although he wrote there afterward: there were two pieces and one pot, etc. — permitted, "I say" the heter piece fell here, even though the heter in the pot is not greater than the piece that fell here, as we explained, end of his words. It appears the Tur took this from that he wrote "as we explained," and also from that he wrote "even though the heter is not greater than the piece," and he did not write "greater," and even though the heter is less than the piece — thus with two pots and two pieces in one pot the law is one; but one cannot say so, as proven in the long Torat HaBayit and as written — and the Tur did not see the long Torat HaBayit, as the later authorities wrote in several places; and also from his short words it appears so, for if he held one law for them he would have combined and taught them and should have arranged two pots and one pot all in one section as the Tur arranged, and he should not have interrupted there with many other laws — but since he interrupted the topic, we learn that with two pieces and one pot, even if the issur piece is greater than the pot, it is permitted; and what he wrote "as we explained" refers only to what he wrote before that for d'rabbanan we say "I say," and on this he says "as we explained" that we say "I say" for d'rabbanan; and that which he says "even though the heter is not greater," etc. — it is that even if the heter is less it is permitted, but he taught it thus because he must teach in the latter clause: if it is Torah issur we do not permit until heter exceeds issur.
