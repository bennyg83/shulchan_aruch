(There, seif 4) If the heter was greater, etc. — the two pots were of one species, etc. This law is from the Rashba in Torat HaBayit HaArukh; later authorities wondered at it, for he himself brought in Torat HaBayit, house 4, gate 1 [in the Berlin edition, daf 101b] that Rashi at the beginning of Chullin daf 95 holds that the Pesachim sugya deals with species in its species — otherwise the taste is like the essence of d'oraisa, and nevertheless it challenges from an olive in the measure of eating a peras that we should decree even in its species and not say "I say"; therefore the Gemara had to say it deals with terumah that is d'rabbanan, see there. And if so, it is necessarily clear that for d'oraisa issur, even in species in its species with a majority, we do not say "I say" — and if so the Rashba's words contradict each other. The Gaon Rabbi Eishel of Prague wished to resolve that the Rashba in Torat HaBayit did not decide whether we hold like Rashi that taste is d'oraisa or like Rabbeinu Tam that taste is not d'oraisa; if so, from doubt we rule stringently that taste is d'oraisa — and all this is in species not in its species itself; but our case is only a d'rabbanan decree of species because of not its species, and we say taste is not d'oraisa and for this it is a d'rabbanan doubt to be lenient. One who examines the Rashba's words will see this resolution does not fit well, for the Rashba did not base the law here on taste not being d'oraisa. However, it appears to me to reconcile: it is apparently astonishing how one can say to decree because of not its species when everyone knows not its species is more severe than its species — for by Torah law one in two is nullified and not its species requires sixty; but regarding terumah, its species is more severe than not its species d'rabbanan, for by Torah law one in a hundred and not its species suffices by noten taam — and therefore it is fit to decree there species because of not its species; and therefore the Gemara challenges well that there, in terumah, it should not have said "I say," etc., and on this the Gemara answers it deals with terumah that is d'rabbanan; but for other issurim certainly the challenger did not think to decree species because of not its species — and the Rashba's words and the Mechaber stand.
