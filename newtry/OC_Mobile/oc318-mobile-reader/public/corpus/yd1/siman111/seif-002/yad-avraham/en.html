(In Shach, s.k. 19) He challenged the Rashba who forbids d'rabbanan issur that fell into one of two pots, even belonging to two people, unknown which, when they come to ask one after the other — for Pesachim (daf 10) and Ketubot (daf 26) prove plainly that we suspend to be lenient one after the other with two people for d'rabbanan issur. It appears as the Kesef Mishneh wrote in chapter 2 of Hilchot Chametz uMatzah, law 11, in the Rambam's reason there: he need not check two houses a second time because it is a double doubt — on each one it is possible to say the mouse did not enter it but his fellow's; and if you say it entered his, perhaps he ate it. And in Ketubot regarding saffron, the Rosh also wrote it is a double doubt: perhaps it was hidden, and if not hidden, perhaps it did not become impure — see Keren Orah who wrote it is like doubt of entry, for which we are lenient in any case for d'rabbanan. Since we arrive at this, that in Pesachim and Ketubot there are two doubts — therefore it is more lenient than d'rabbanan issur. There is no contradiction from there to the Rashba's view — know that in Ketubot they are lenient even with two people, and likewise in that case in Pesachim according to the Rambam. In my work I expanded further on these matters: an incident occurred — eight meat pieces were salted on the table and three or four on the bench; after they were rinsed and mixed together, ten treif pieces were found among them; it is known that one was on the bench, only it is unknown whether the rest on the bench were also treif and the kosher ones were not salted with them at all or not — and I permitted it. For besides what I raised regarding doubt of salting in a responsum to siman 110 nearby, there is further room to permit from this siman [111, seif 4]: two pots, one heter and one issur, and a nevelah piece fell into one — we say it fell into issur; and although there the falling was known immediately, nevertheless if it was not known until the two pots were mixed while cold and chein nafsho was found among them and it is unknown into which pot it fell initially — we say it fell into the place of issur, and we suspend the piece found now in the spoiled one, as proven in siman 129, seif 61.
