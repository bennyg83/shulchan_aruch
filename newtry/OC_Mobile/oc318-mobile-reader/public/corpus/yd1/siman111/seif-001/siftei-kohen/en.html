From d'rabbanan issur. For a d'rabbanan doubt we rule leniently:
Such as the fat of the gid. And cheese of a non-Jew and the like; even an issur that has a Torah root — so too the Rashba in Torat HaBayit:
And even if the heter in the pot, etc. The Nekudat HaKesef, chapter ha'Areil, wrote that it is possible regarding stringent issurei mashahu we do not say \"I say,\" even for d'rabbanan issurim with less than a majority, end of his words; and he brings it in Darkei Moshe:
And the same law, etc., from these two pieces, etc. It appears that here it deals with when the two pieces were recognizable by themselves before falling which was heter and which issur; but if they mixed first one with one, then since issur was established in both pieces each one is judged as the substance of issur itself and we no longer say a d'rabbanan doubt to be lenient, as written in the laws of double doubt, seif 41; and what the Perishah wrote, seif 62, that it deals with when the two pieces mixed first one with the other — applies explicitly to two pots, for even if we judge each one as the substance of issur itself, nevertheless there is a d'rabbanan doubt to be lenient with two pots, unlike one pot; and investigate:
And it is not known into which it fell, etc. Even if the pieces are worthy of honor, for by Torah law it too is nullified, but the Sages said a piece worthy of honor is not nullified; and in such doubt that one pot is nevelah they did not say so, end of Perishah, seif 64; and it appears to me the same applies to two pieces and two pots — the law is thus; and so the Nekudat HaKesef there: with two pots, one slaughtered and one nevelah, and before them a piece of nevelah and a piece of slaughtered meat that fell one into the other and it is not known into which they fell — by Torah law it is nullified in the majority, we suspend to be lenient since there is increase, and even for an important piece, for even a davar sheb'minyan by Torah law one in two is nullified, end of his words; and since he permits d'oraisa issur with increase, all the more so d'rabbanan issur itself; and it appears to me the same with two pieces and one pot — permitted even without one in one, for nevertheless we hold a d'rabbanan doubt is lenient; and on the contrary here we say more a d'rabbanan doubt than with two pots, as written below, s.k. 6, from Issur VeHeter, see there:
