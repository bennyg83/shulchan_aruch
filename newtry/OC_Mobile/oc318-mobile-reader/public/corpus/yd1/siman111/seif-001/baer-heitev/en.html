"For example." And the same applies to cheese of a non-Jew and the like; even an issur that has a Torah root — so too the Rashba.
"Greater." The Nekudat HaKesef wrote that it is possible regarding stringent issurei mashahu we do not say "I say," even for d'rabbanan issurim with less than a majority. And the Peri Chadash disagrees.
"The pieces." The Shach wrote that even if the pieces are worthy of honor, for that too is only d'rabbanan; and the same applies to two pieces and two pots, or two pieces and one pot — it is also permitted even for a piece worthy of honor, for nevertheless it is a d'rabbanan doubt to be lenient. It also appears that here it deals with when the two pieces were recognizable by themselves before they fell which was heter and which was issur; but if they became mixed first one with one, then since issur was established in both pieces each one is judged as the substance of issur itself and we no longer say a d'rabbanan doubt to be lenient, as written in the laws of double doubt, seif 41; and the Peri Chadash disagrees.
"D'rabbanan." The Taz wrote that it appears one should challenge what is written in this siman, that if there is a doubt whether d'oraisa issur fell, it is permitted at a d'rabbanan measure — and this contradicts the rule stated in siman 66, seif 4, that in this we do not rule leniently but it is like certain d'oraisa; and it requires further study, for here the heter is by force of "I say" that no issur fell at all — this is more lenient.
