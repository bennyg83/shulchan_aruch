"The pieces." Avodat HaTaharah; and see Shaar HaMelekh chapter 10 of Hilchot Mikvaot, general rules of d'rabbanan doubt, rule 1 and rule 2 — he disagrees with the Peri Chadash on two points and with the Shach on one: that which the Peri Chadash wrote, that even when the pieces were recognizable by themselves it is called established issur — the main view appears like the Shach, that for d'rabbanan doubt we require full establishment of issur, etc.; and also that the Peri Chadash's view that even with presumption of issur we say d'rabbanan doubt to be lenient — the halachah is not so (and I already wrote this in laws of double doubt, note 21). But he also disagrees with the Shach: he wrote that even when the pieces are not recognizable, when issur was established we suspend to be lenient to say the heter piece fell — and specifically when d'rabbanan issur mixed one in one and afterward one was lost we do not say d'rabbanan doubt to be lenient since it was established; but when one fell into a pot of heter where there is presumption of heter against it — establishing the pot on presumption of heter — we suspend to be lenient, see there; and see responsum Toldot Yitzchak siman 420 what he wrote on this matter.
"We suspend to be lenient." See responsum Shemen Rokeach vol. 1, simanim 37, 38, and 39, who wrote that even if one of the pots is forbidden only by doubt, nevertheless we suspend regarding it; therefore he wrote in an incident that there stood three pots of heter and d'rabbanan issur fell into one of two of them, and in the third pot there was still no doubt; and afterward d'rabbanan issur fell into one of the pots and doubt arose in the third pot and on one of the first ones. The first two pots are certainly forbidden, as below, seif 5; and the third pot is permitted, for we suspend it on the two pots already forbidden — but if the first doubt was not known until after the second arose, one may reconcile the matter and in a case of great loss one may be lenient, see there. He also wrote that if the first doubt of those pots was not known until the second was known, then one may say somewhat that even the first of the first ones in which there is no doubt is permitted because of the second — but he did not have the heart to permit on this, see there for his reason.
