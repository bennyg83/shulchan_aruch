"For Torah law, species in its species, etc." The same applies to species in its species and not its species — for we say that what is not its species is as if absent, and its species is greater and nullifies it, as stated in siman 98.
"The two pots were of one species." Not specifically — rather the issur is not the species of the heter.
"Until heter exceeds issur by sixty, etc." This deals with a piece worthy of honor, for d'rabbanan sixty does not help; nevertheless here in doubt they were lenient — so in the Rosh, end of chapter ha'Areil, and in Torat HaBayit HaArukh he wrote, and these are his words: even though the Sages required more than sixty, end of his words — meaning, sometimes they require a hundred to nullify, such as terumah — that is specifically in not its species, as in chapter 2 of Orlah.
