He ages them. The knots must be untied, as I will write shortly.
Rinsing suffices for them. This implies rinsing with cold water, and so it appears in Hagahos Ashri and the Mordechai there. They write, however, that this applies specifically to laundering in which the practice is to beat the cloth several times. Tosafos likewise writes this. From the Rosh, however, it appears that it is permitted only with the type of laundering in which the practice is to wash several times with ashes, then with hot water in a kli sheni, and then with cold water. It appears that they do not disagree, because Tosafos, Mordechai, and Hagahos Ashri conclude that it requires further study whether, regarding prohibitions, one should distinguish between a substance that adheres and one that does not adhere. The Rosh there explains why all tablecloths upon which a hot prohibition fell are permitted even where the prohibited substance adheres, because in such a case laundering specifically with hot water and ashes is required. So it appears to me.
