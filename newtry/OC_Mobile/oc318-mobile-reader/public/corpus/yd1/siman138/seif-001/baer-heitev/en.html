Pitching. Meaning, after the pitching was completed. During pitching, rinsing is like throwing water into mud. Shach. Taz writes that although a winepress is not used for permanent storage, wine remains in it throughout the pressing season, so it resembles a storage vessel and is forbidden even after brief use. It is not fully equivalent, however, and does not require three days of filling and pouring. Rashba.
Moist. Meaning, moist enough to moisten another object. Rashba. Taz notes that in siman 135:1 a non-storage vessel initially requires filling and pouring rather than mere wiping, unlike here, and he explained the distinction there.
Filling and pouring. For three days, as stated in siman 135:12. Shach writes that all the kashering methods in seif 7 are required initially. If one used the winepress or its vessels without kashering, the wine is permitted after the fact because there is ordinarily sixty against the absorbed layer, as stated in siman 137:2. An exceptionally broad and shallow press may lack sixty against its surface layer, and then the wine is forbidden unless the press was first rinsed.
Tow. Taz writes that this is not limited to tow. If cracks remain entirely unsealed, the vessel is forbidden.
Hagalah. Derishah asks why hagalah appears preferable here to filling and pouring, while in siman 135 the Tur rules that an earthenware storage vessel requires filling and pouring and hagalah does not suffice. Taz answers that wherever hagalah is effective it is superior. Earthenware ordinarily cannot be kashered through hagalah because the Torah testified concerning it, and therefore filling and pouring works better in this special area. According to our ruling that hagalah works for earthenware regarding this prohibition, earthenware has the same law as other materials and hagalah is preferable.
Wood. Bach disagrees with the Mechaber and requires wiping even for wood. Taz rules that the Mechaber's words are primary.
