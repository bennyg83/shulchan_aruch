A law of making kosher wine vessels; it contains 11 se’ifim.
A stone winepress that a non-Jew pitched, and he put wine into it at the time of pitching; or that a Jew pitched, and he put wine into it and a non-Jew touched it while liquid was tofe’ach upon it — it requires wiping-dry. But if the way of the non-Jew is to tread in it when pitching it, wiping-dry does not suffice; rather, he should peel off the pitch and afterwards wipe it dry; or one may do iruy without peeling; or leave it to age 12 months. And a wooden winepress: if a non-Jew pitched it and put wine into it at the time of pitching — even if he did not tread in it — or if he pitched it and later trod in it; or if a Jew pitched it and put wine into it and a non-Jew touched it while liquid was tofe’ach upon it — all have one law: it requires peeling and wiping-dry, or iruy without peeling. And if there is flax tow between board and board, or worn-out cloth, wiping-dry does not suffice; rather, it requires milui v’iruy or hag'alah. And an earthenware [winepress]: if a non-Jew pitched it and put wine into it at the time of pitching — even if he did not tread in it — or if a Jew pitched it and put wine into it and a non-Jew touched it while liquid was tofe’ach upon it, it is not permitted through peeling and wiping-dry, but only through milui v’iruy without peeling. And if a non-Jew pitched it and trod in it, it requires peeling and milui v’iruy, or hag'alah without peeling, or aging 12 months, or firing it in a kiln until the pitch softens. And all this is when it is pitched; but if it is not pitched, one must distinguish: if its first use was by a non-Jew, it requires wiping-dry (and for earthenware, iruy if he trod in it) (Tur); and if its first use was by a Jew, rinsing suffices if it is of wood or stone, but if it is earthenware it requires wiping-dry.
