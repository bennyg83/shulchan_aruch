If it was sealed with 2 seals and it returned to him and he did not recognize it, or if he found it broken, it is forbidden. {Rama: And this is specifically when we see that it was broken intentionally by a person; but in a place where we can attribute that it was broken and damaged on its own, or by an animal, or by a young child unintentionally, it is permitted after the fact (Terumat HaDeshen siman 205, and Arukh rule 22). And even if it was damaged intentionally, one may permit if a Jew goes out and comes in constantly (Beit Yosef in the name of Semahot Gedolot and Semag).} But he does not need to recheck it unless he informed the Jew to whom it was sent that it is sealed with 2 seals; even if he did not inform him the matter of his seals, since the second Jew found it sealed with 2 seals it is permitted. Nevertheless, it is proper to inform him the form of the seal so that he will recheck it. {Rama: However, if he wrote letters upon it, there is no need to inform him, for a typical non-Jew is not expert to write letters (Ran and Rabbeinu Yonah). And if the non-Jew says that the barrel was dripping and he tightened it and touched the wine, he is not believed to forbid it, even if a bit of wine is still noticeable on the outside and there are circumstantial indications in his words (Terumat HaDeshen siman 202).}
