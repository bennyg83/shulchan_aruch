If he sealed it, etc.; but, etc.; nevertheless it is proper, etc. See Tosafot 31b and 69b, in the passage cited above, and as explained above.
Specifically, etc.; even if, etc. Sefer HaTerumah and Semag challenge the statement on 31b, 'otherwise it is forbidden,' even according to Ri's explanation that he returned and found it damaged, from Taharot, chapter 8, Mishnah 4, 'one who leaves,' etc. This should apply all the more to yayin nesech, as stated in Avodah Zarah 70b regarding a courtyard, etc. They answer that the Mishnah in Taharot concerns one who goes out and comes in. Terumat HaDeshen writes that Semak and Mordechai permit it and bring proof from the Tosefta of Taharot, cited by the Rash in chapter 7, Mishnah 7: One who leaves his utensils, etc. It appears they do not disagree. Sefer HaTerumah and Semag discuss a case where it is seen that the seal was deliberately disturbed by a person, and therefore cite the Mishnah, which concerns such a case. Semak and Mordechai discuss a place where the disturbance can be attributed to another cause, and the Tosefta concerns that situation. Mordechai, siman 1,334, distinguishes between the deposit discussed in Avodah Zarah and the Tosefta's case, which does not concern a deposit. Rash writes similarly there.
