And some permit, etc. Bach wrote: nevertheless, nowadays the custom is not to permit sending wine at all in a wooden barrel from city to city except through a trusted Israelite guarding it day and night, because it is common that gentiles pierce with a small drill or knife between the boards and steal wine; and one should not change this to be lenient; however, if they cover all the walls of the barrel with hoops and also the bases with boards, one may permit, as Tosafot in the chapter all utensils wrote — end; and it appears this is specifically l'chatchila; and likewise in the responsum of Masat Binyamin siman 29, that b'di'eved they permit as the Rav wrote.
And also that there should be no tap there. The responsum of Masat Binyamin there wrote that this law is not an absolute requirement but for greater assurance; but b’di’eved the matter is clear that one seal suffices even in a wooden barrel and even without any other fix such as a leather cover and hoops, or placing the barrel in a sack — see there where he elaborated; however if he did not cut the taps, even b’di’eved it is forbidden, for one can easily remove the tap and draw wine.
And if he found a knife, etc. This is the wording of Darkei Moshe: what Isur VeHeter wrote, that the wine is forbidden when he found a thorn inserted, see above siman 129 and 124 where there is reason to be lenient, end quote. He means that there it is explained that non-Jews nowadays are not worshippers of avodah zarah; and if they touched intentionally through another object, such as if they pierced there with a knife to remove wine, it is permitted in a case of loss. The same applies here. According to this are his words here in the gloss. And the reason we are not concerned that when he drew the wine he touched the wine with his hand, thereby forbidding what is inside through nitzok, is that why should he do so, as I wrote at the end of siman 129. But what he wrote, see above siman 128, is a scribal error and should read 124; so it is in Ateret Zekenim.
