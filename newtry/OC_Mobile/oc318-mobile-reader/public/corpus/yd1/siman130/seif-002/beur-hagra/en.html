If he entrusted, etc. Gemara 31a, as above.
(Likut) If he entrusted, etc. Rambam, as stated there on 31a, follows the Sages. See Rashi there, s.v. 'He designated,' and s.v. 'And the Sages.' This proves that Rambam rules like the Sages and reads, as in our text, 'a key or a seal,' unlike Ran. Rambam therefore mentioned a key and seal only in the baraita on 61b, holding that only there do they suffice. According to the version of Halachot Gedolot and the Rif, that baraita follows Rabbi Eliezer; according to Tosafot's version it can follow even the Sages. See below, seif katan 19, and siman 131, seif katan 7. Tosafot 31a, s.v. 'Both of them,' holds that a key and seal are considered two seals. Rambam ruled like the Sages although Rav ruled like Rabbi Eliezer, because Rabbi Yochanan and Rabbi Elazar disagreed with Rav, Rav later retracted on 39a, and Rav Ashi also disagreed on 29b. The passage on 69b, however, is difficult. It seems Rambam understands all of that as Rava's own statement, like the other teachings there. Although Rava said on 31a, 'a cover over the mouth,' to explain the Sages, his words were rejected by Rav Ashi and by Rav and Shmuel on 39b. The Rif, Rosh, and other authorities omit this law because they explain like Tosafot there, s.v. 'One who entrusts,' that Rabbi Yehudah ben Beteira follows his own view, which we do not accept. According to the version of Halachot Gedolot and the Rif cited by Rashba and Ran, which reads 'a key and a seal,' the ruling follows Rabbi Eliezer: he designated a corner that he locked with a key and seal, and the wine is permitted for drinking. (End of Likut.)
