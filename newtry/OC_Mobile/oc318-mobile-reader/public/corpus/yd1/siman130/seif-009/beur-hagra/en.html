(Likut) If it was, etc. This follows the first Tanna, who accords with Rabbi Eliezer. The Rif supports the explanation of Rashba and Ran that he ruled like Rabbi Eliezer, but understood that a key and seal are required, as above. They explain that the Rif necessarily understood the baraita on 31a concerning one who purchases or rents, etc., as discussing a different courtyard, for otherwise a key and seal would not be required, as stated here. Rabbi Meir and the Sages here therefore dispute the same issue as Rabbi Eliezer and the Sages there. The statement, 'The Sages say it is always forbidden,' must mean with a key and seal, but with two seals it is certainly permitted according to Rabbi Yochanan there. According to Tosafot, this case concerns a key or a seal, and here too the halachah follows the first Tanna, who accords with Rabbi Eliezer. See Ran and siman 131 in the gloss. (End of Likut.)
