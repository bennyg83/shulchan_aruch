In what case is this said — regarding one who rents or buys a house in the courtyard of a non-Jew — but in the courtyard of a Jew itself, where he did not buy it and did not rent it from a non-Jew, even if a non-Jew lives in that courtyard and a Jew does not live there, it is permitted, even though there is no key and seal in the Jew’s hand, since the non-Jew has no connection to it; and even if he was found standing beside the wine, for in any case he would be caught regarding it as a thief. And this is specifically by day, but at night it is forbidden. {Rama: And specifically in such a case one should forbid it at night; but in other places where we said above in simanim 128 and 129 that the non-Jew is afraid, there is no difference between day and night, and this case is different, since the non-Jew has a connection to the courtyard, and presumably they lock it at night since a Jew does not live there, and he is not as afraid. Therefore, even by day, if it was locked and there is no hole or crack by which they can see the wine, it is forbidden; but through a hole or crack it is permitted by day, even if it was certainly locked. And at night it is forbidden in such a case even if it was presumably locked, and this is the distinction between day and night.}
