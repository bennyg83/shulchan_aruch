Sealing the barrel with clay. In the Gemara it is thus: Rava said "a barrel on its mouth of a barrel — smeared and sealed" is two seals; and if not — a basket and tightening is two seals; and if not — no; and Rambam explains this is the language of Shulchan Aruch; and the Tur: plastering — smearing with clay and drying is one seal in all cases; but without drying it appears it is not a seal, for very easily he can remove wet clay and put another in its place; and likewise Rashbam wrote in the Gemara that Beit Yosef copied here: "attached to the barrel and dried, etc." — end. And their way was to place some vessel on the mouth of the barrel when plastering it — for such is Rambam's language here as the way everyone seals; only if he inserts the barrel in a basket and tightens it, it is considered one sign since it is a change; and it is found in the Gemara this "a barrel on its mouth of a barrel smeared" is one seal and "sealed" — meaning he makes a second seal — whatever it may be — is two seals; but if he did not seal another sealing, it is only one seal, since their way was to place a vessel there at time of plastering — it is only as if there were plastering alone; and likewise the Tur's language is explained: he plastered the mouth of the barrel and sealed it — meaning he made a second sealing after the plastering; and what he wrote afterwards "and he inverted a vessel on it and tightened it" — this "and he inverted" is like "or he inverted"; and such is the way of language in many places — meaning: either he does not make a second sealing but inverts on it and tightens — that is also a second seal and joins to the plastering of the barrel mouth; and such is Rambam's language — Shulchan Aruch copied here only that the Tur did not mention plastering in the end of "tightening a vessel on it" and relied on the plastering of the barrel mouth that he mentioned at the beginning; and Rambam mentioned plastering on the tightening vessel — and the intent is one: all matters of plastering help whether on the barrel mouth or on the tightening vessel — so it appears to me clear and correct; and Beit Yosef did not descend to this and challenged several difficulties on the Tur and made a dispute between Rambam and Tur; and according to what I wrote nothing is difficult and there is no dispute here; and I did not need the forced interpretations I saw in explaining the Tur's words — let one who wishes examine them. And since Rama already wrote in siman 129 end of seif 1 that in sealed barrels it is permitted in any matter in great loss — if so, if the wine was in a gentile's possession without guarding and the barrel was bodied and plastered with clay and dried — permitted in great loss, for it is one seal and not bother and forgery; and it appears to me this is specifically when the barrel rests in the gentile's house; but if one conveys it on the road alone and knows the Jew sailed — plastering with clay does not help at all, for it is simple that because the wagon constantly shakes the wine goes upward and softens the clay and falls — a very common matter; and the way of wine carriers is to repair the clay constantly because of this around the body; therefore it is like fully open without a seal. And I saw in Masat Binyamin they asked about wine conveyed without Israel's guarding and the barrel was sealed with a vine tendril and around it plastered with clay and among the tendrils a thick tree branch was inserted to strengthen the sealing as is the way of new-wine carriers — and he permitted the wine because this is one seal — as Rama wrote siman 129 that I brought above; except he distinguished there if the wine ferments as new wine does and rises upward and spoils the barrel's plastering — and his words are astonishing, for then all new wine ferments and there is no way to do thus with tendrils except when it is still fermenting; and in essence of law there is no distinction between fermenting and not fermenting, for certainly also without fermenting the wine goes upward through the wagon's motion; and therefore it appears in no case is there permit in conveying wine because of plastering alone without another sign — only when resting; then plastering helps — so it appears to me.
And likewise if the mouth of the skin is narrow, etc. In the Tur it is written: tie and seal the mouth of the wine skin and put it in a sack with the skin's mouth downward and tie the sack above — two seals; the skin's mouth upward is not two seals; and in the Gemara it is also: skin in a strap — tie and seal its mouth downward, etc.; it appears straightforward this "and seal" does not mean another sealing as I explained regarding "a barrel on its mouth smeared and sealed" — for here the sealing was conditional on the tying itself: granted regarding plastering there is no distinction since once it dried there is no bother to make another and wait until it dries — unlike regarding tying: whatever is not a strange matter is not a sign at all, for an ordinary tie certainly has no bother at all to untie; rather we certainly require a strange tie — as written nearby "two strange ties, etc."; therefore they said in the Gemara: if he tied and sealed — meaning he made a recognition in the matter of the tying — automatically it was one seal; and its mouth downward was seal two; or what he folded the skin's mouth inward — with this are also the Tur's words we mentioned explained: the tie of the skin was seal one because it is strange — he made it in the manner of sealing; if the skin's mouth was downward and the sack tie above — meaning an ordinary tie that is not strange — two seals; and as we mentioned, so it appears to me to explain the Tur's words also here; settled what Beit Yosef inferred on the Tur here; and according to this putting in the sack was no seal — only the tie and inverting the skin's mouth or folding its mouth inward was two seals; and it appears the reason is: whenever the skin is in a sack and its mouth upward, immediately when he unties the ordinary sack tie the skin's mouth is exposed as if without sack — therefore when the Tur wrote "but he folded the skin's mouth inward" — even though its mouth is upward in the sack it is two seals — likewise if it is without sack at all; and as Beit Yosef and Shulchan Aruch wrote here from Rambam's language who did not mention putting in a sack at all and did not write "even though its mouth is upward" but since at the beginning he said if it is in a sack upward it is not a seal — here he said it is permitted here even though there is no seal because of putting in the sack; and Maharach ruled because of the Tur's language that putting in a sack is required at least; and it appears to me since from Rambam it is clear we do not require a sack and Shulchan Aruch brings it — we have no reason to dispute his ruling, since in the Tur it is not proven a sack is required; and that Shulchan Aruch seif 1 wrote "put the skin in a sack, etc." — there it deals with wood as explained there, and one should concern lest he draw wine through its boards; and nevertheless Rama concludes there one is accustomed to be lenient; but if the skin is of leather and its stitches are not visible from outside — it appears clear we do not require putting in a sack, only two seals as I explained; and the tie was also a seal — namely it is somewhat strange from other ties as we proved above.
