(Extract) How, etc. Rashi explains there "a barrel on its mouth of a barrel — smeared and sealed" — meaning a vessel smeared and attached to the barrel; and smeared and sealed is one matter, like sealing its mouth downward, etc. — it is a knot; and likewise "he tied and sealed" is one matter and it is one seal; and the barrel body itself attached with clay to the barrel is a second seal; and likewise a basket — and tightening is seal one and the body seal two; and what is needed here is tightening because it is not attached with clay to the barrel but the barrel enters into it; and likewise in a wineskin the knot of the skin is seal one and the knot of the strap seal two; and likewise in a pouch its mouth requires two knots; but Rambam explains "a barrel on its mouth of a barrel" — the vessel is the body for the barrel; smeared and sealed as Rashi explained is one matter attached with clay to the barrel as is the way of all bodies and it is seal one; except since the way of a body is not in a vessel and it is a change — it is two seals; and likewise a basket is also a body for the barrel; smeared and sealed — except since the way of a body is in a basket, therefore tightening is required; and this is the change that there is no way to be tightened: in a strap the strap is not tied only the skin; but the change that is in the strap and its mouth downward is like seal two; and likewise tying its mouth, etc. — also not tied downward but upward; and Rambam considers in the second instance from one that it is a greater stringency; and the first section of each includes in what is written "and likewise any change, etc."; and plastering or tying, etc. But Tosafot 69b in the passage "what is the reason, etc." explain: smeared is seal one and sealing is seal two (end).
