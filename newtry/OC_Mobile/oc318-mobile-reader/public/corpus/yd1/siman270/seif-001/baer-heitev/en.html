To sell it. Beit Yosef cites R. Manoach that this applies specifically to a Sefer Torah made for public reading. Private books may be sold and the proceeds used as one wishes. See there. Bach explains his words. See Orach Chayyim 153:10. Shach.
Pressing need. Rather, he should accept charity from the collectors, as stated at the beginning of siman 253, because a poor person is not required to sell his household utensils. Bach rules likewise.
A new one. Bach writes that the prohibition is not limited to selling an old Sefer Torah in order to buy a new one. The same applies if he first spent his own money to purchase a new Sefer Torah and afterward wishes to sell the old one in order to reimburse himself. It is permitted only when one ascends in sanctity. Where there is no increase in sanctity, even if the two are equal and there is no descent, exchanging one Torah for another is still prohibited. See Orach Chayyim siman 153.
And it is not. Taz writes that Rashi does not appear to hold this way. Rashi writes that one who purchases a Sefer Torah performs a mitzvah, while one who writes one performs a greater mitzvah. Taz further writes that he found it stated that one who purchases a Sefer Torah from a gentile who acquired it from a Jew is not considered merely one who seized a mitzvah; rather, it is regarded as though he wrote it.
