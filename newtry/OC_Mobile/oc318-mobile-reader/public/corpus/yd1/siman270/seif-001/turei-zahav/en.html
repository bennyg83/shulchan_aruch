It is a positive mitzvah, etc. — as it says "And now, write for yourselves this song," meaning the entire Torah until the end of the song of Ha'azinu, which is the whole Torah; and it is in the Gemara that Tur brought: one who buys a sefer Torah from the market is like one who snatches a mitzvah from the market — he wrote it or corrected in it even one letter, it is as though he received it from Mount Sinai. And Nimukei Yosef explained "like snatching a mitzvah" — he does not have as great reward as one who toiled in writing it; and there is room for strict judgment to distinguish and say: if not for the toil he would not have done it — whether he wrote it himself or hired a scribe to write it and toiled in preparing the parchment and bore the scribe's yoke until it was written — as though he received it, there is room for mercy to say: just as he toiled for this, so he would have toiled to go in the desert to receive from Mount Sinai; even if one letter was mistaken and he corrected it as though he wrote it, there is room for mercy to say: just as he corrected this, so if he found many errors he would correct them — end. And it is in Tosafot in HaKomatz Rabbah: one letter — meaning in a sefer Torah he bought from the market, he is no longer considered like snatching a mitzvah, for it was with his fellow [in sin] who was delaying [a scroll] that was not corrected — I am elevated on this as though he wrote it — end. And Maharshal on this: and according to this, one who buys a sefer Torah from a gentile who took it from an Israelite is not like snatching a mitzvah but as though he wrote it — end. And Rama brought this nearby and wrote that what he wrote "like snatching from the market" and one does not fulfill through this is against Rashi's words, who wrote that if he bought it he did a mitzvah, and if he wrote it it is a greater mitzvah.

Menachem deduced from the Gemara's question: what is the law — may one sell an old sefer Torah to buy a new one, since it does not ascend in sanctity it is forbidden, or perhaps since there is no other elevation, etc.; come and hear: Rabbah bar bar Chana said R. Yochanan in the name of Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel: a person should not sell an old sefer Torah to buy a new one; and there it is rejected on account of negligence (lest after selling he not buy again and the money be lost). When we say for example that it is written and ready to be taken out — what (since it is already in the scribe's house and is not delayed except payment of money) — it implies from this that R.
