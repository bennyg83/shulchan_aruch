He is not permitted to sell it, etc. The Beis Yosef cites Rabbeinu Manoach that this applies specifically to a Torah scroll made for public reading. Privately owned books, however, may be sold and the proceeds used as the owner wishes. See the Beis Yosef and the Bach for an explanation of his words, and see Orach Chaim, siman 153, seif 10.
Even if he has nothing to eat, etc. Rather, he receives charity from the charity administrators, as stated above at the beginning of siman 253, for he is not required to sell his house and household utensils. Thus wrote the Bach. See there, where he discusses this at length.
Even to sell an old one, etc. We are concerned about negligence, lest he fail to purchase the new one. Therefore, if the new Torah scroll has already been written, is in the scribe's possession, and lacks only payment, he may sell the old one to purchase the new one. Thus writes the Atteres Zahav, based on Rabbeinu Manoach as cited by the Beis Yosef. This is not the primary ruling, however. The view of all the authorities appears to be that even in such a case it is forbidden, as written by the Derishah and the Bach. The Ran explicitly writes that since there is no elevation in sanctity, it is forbidden to exchange one Torah scroll for another, for one may sell it only when thereby elevating the degree of sanctity. The Rivash writes likewise, as cited by the Beis Yosef in Orach Chaim, siman 153. The Bach further writes that the prohibition is not limited to selling the old scroll in order to purchase a new one. The same applies where one first paid for and purchased a new scroll with his own funds, intending afterward to sell the old one and reimburse himself from its proceeds. This too is forbidden, because the essential reason for the prohibition is that the sale does not produce an elevation in sanctity. Permission exists only when sanctity is elevated. Where there is no elevation, although the two are equal and there is no reduction in sanctity, exchanging one Torah scroll for another is nevertheless forbidden. See Orach Chaim, siman 153.
The same applies for the redemption of captives, etc. See above, siman 252, seif katan 1.
