Nowadays, etc. The Perishah writes that the Rosh means as follows: The positive commandment was stated specifically regarding these books and not regarding a Torah scroll. In their days, when the Oral Torah was studied orally and not from written texts, they needed to study from a Torah scroll properly crowned and exact in defective and plene spellings and in the divisions of the cantillation. In our time, however, when permission has been granted to write separate books and leaves, why should we gratuitously diminish the honor of a Torah scroll by studying from it when there is no need? It follows that in these generations, when people do not study from a Torah scroll, there is no positive commandment to write one. This appears to be the primary interpretation of the Rosh, unlike the Beis Yosef and the Bach, who write that according to the Rosh there certainly remains a positive commandment to write a Torah scroll even nowadays, but there is also a mitzvah to write Chumashim, Mishnah, the Talmud, and their commentaries. The Atteres Zahav and the Maggid Mishneh at the beginning of Hilchos Sefer Torah also follow the interpretation of the Beis Yosef.
