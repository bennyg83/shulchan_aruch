And fit for this. See in Tashbetz part 1 siman 33 who elaborated on this and wrote we find lamdanim that honor is done for them only for a sage fit to appoint a communal leader and they required conditions for him. First: that he learned all the Talmud in practical matters, excluding halachah for the Messiah. Second condition: that his learning be fluent in his mouth and he know to extract rulings on the straight line. Third condition: that he not depart from his Talmud for worldly matters, not for necessary livelihood or communal needs imposed on him — for if he taught and explained more, he is worse than an ignoramus. Fourth condition: that he not be "sani shome'a" — if so, not fit to be concerned for his honor; and on the contrary permitted to disgrace him. However one may say these words were said only when there are sages greater than him (obvious this refers only to the first two conditions); but if there is no greater than him — "yiftach bedoro keShmuel bedoro." And these matters of requiring the above conditions are regarding if he was excommunicated for his honor; but if excommunicated because of a sin he was obligated for nidui — if he excommunicated him, even the smallest in Israel, even a slave and even a maidservant — his excommunication is excommunication. And he also wrote communities have no power to fence a fence to prevent sages from excommunicating for their honor (and see above siman 334 seif 18 in the gloss); and likewise they have no power to prevent any person from excommunicating to separate from prohibition; and all their power is only that there not be permission in one of the people to decree on a matter of sin except in the hand of the sages of the place — end quote; see there; and see on this in responsum of RaNACH part 2.
