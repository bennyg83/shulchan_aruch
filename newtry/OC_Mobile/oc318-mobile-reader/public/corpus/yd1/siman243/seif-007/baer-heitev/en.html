That he appease. The Beit Yosef at the end of siman 334 wrote that it appears the reading is "when he appeases," with the resh vocalized with a patach, meaning that he should ask forgiveness from him. If he asked forgiveness from him several times and he was not appeased, nevertheless they release him, since he did what was incumbent upon him. Although the Talmud in Yoma implies that for his teacher he goes to ask forgiveness even a thousand times until he is appeased, that is regarding the need to placate him, but not regarding whether they release his niddui only once he is appeased. See above siman 334 seif 47, and see several responsa in Responsa Mabit regarding one who shames a sage.
