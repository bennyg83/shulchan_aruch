One regarding whom witnesses testified that he disgraced a Torah scholar — even with words (even not in his presence) — beit din would excommunicate him and not release him until the sage whom he was excommunicated for agrees; and if he disgraced the sage after death, beit din would excommunicate him and they release him when he repents: {Rama: But the law of a Torah scholar in our time is not that the disgracer give him a litra of gold. (Maharik shoresh 163 and Mahariiv siman 163.) Nevertheless, beit din fines the disgracer according to the disgracer and the disgraced. (Rivash.) Provided the sage did not begin a quarrel to disgrace people improperly and thereby cause himself to be disgraced and attacked. (There too.) Nevertheless, even if the sage began somewhat, no person may be insolent against him to answer him brazenly. (Hagahot Mordechai, Kiddushin.) And everything is according to what the judges see.}
