They would publicly place him under a ban. So rule the Tur and Rambam. See Beit Yosef, who derives this from the Gemara. The Mechaber rules likewise below, siman 334. See Maharam Galanti, responsum 8.
Until the sage is appeased, etc. Kesef Mishneh, chapter 6 of Hilchos Talmud Torah, and Beit Yosef below at the end of siman 334, explain that the word means until he appeases the sage. It does not mean that even after repeatedly asking forgiveness, if the sage remains unwilling to forgive, the ban may not be released. That would be very surprising, because once the offender has done what is required of him, why should the ban not be released? Although the end of Yoma implies that one must seek forgiveness from his teacher even a thousand times until the teacher is appeased, that concerns the obligation to appease him, not the rule that the ban may not be released until he is appeased. Rama rules likewise below, siman 334, seif 47. The same appears from the Tur, who writes that the ban is not released until the offender appeases the sage. See Orach Chaim, siman 606.
The humiliation is assessed according to the person humiliated, etc. See Choshen Mishpat, siman 420, seif 24.
Everything depends upon the court's assessment, etc. See the many responsa of Mabit concerning one who humiliates a Torah scholar.
