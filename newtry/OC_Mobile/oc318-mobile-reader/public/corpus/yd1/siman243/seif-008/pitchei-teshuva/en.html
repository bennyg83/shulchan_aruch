For his honor. Hagahot Maimoniyot limits this to one who verbally disgraced a Torah scholar. Mere failure to stand or show honor does not justify excommunication. Shach and Mahari of the House of Levi cite this view, while Panim Meirot raises a difficulty from Moed Katan.
And fit for this. Tashbetz requires that a scholar who excommunicates for his own honor be fit for communal leadership: learned in practical Talmud, fluent in deriving law, devoted to Torah except for necessary livelihood or communal needs, and free of a bad reputation. Where no greater scholar exists, the leading scholar of the generation may suffice. These qualifications apply only to excommunication for personal honor; for a genuine transgression, even an ordinary person may impose nidui. Communities cannot prevent scholars from acting to separate people from prohibition.
