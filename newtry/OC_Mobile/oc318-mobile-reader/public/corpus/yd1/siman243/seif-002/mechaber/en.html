In what case is this stated? When everyone goes out himself; but if they do not go out themselves but hire others in their place, or collect money from the people of the city to do it — if it is a matter needed for human life, such as water cisterns and the like, they are obligated to give their share (and if at first they went themselves and afterward decided to hire others, Torah scholars are obligated to give their share) (Teshuvat Maharam in Mordechai, chapter HaShutafin); but a matter needed for guarding the city, such as city walls and its towers and guards' wages — they were not obligated to give them anything, for they need no guarding, for their Torah is their guarding; and therefore they were exempt from all types of taxes, whether taxes fixed on all city residents, whether a tax fixed on each person alone, whether fixed or not fixed; and the city residents were obligated to pay on their behalf even those fixed on each person: {Rama: And even if the governor said the Torah scholars themselves should pay — the public is obligated to pay for them; and if the public excommunicated Torah scholars to pay, their excommunication has no effect, and the Torah scholar could excommunicate and ban the public to pay his assessment. (Beit Yosef in the name of Teshuvat R. Nehemiah in Alashkar siman 19.) And there is no distinction whether the Torah scholar is rich or poor. (Beit Yosef in the name of Rambam, Ramah, and R. Yehudah HaLevi.)} And specifically Torah scholars whose Torah is their craft; but those whose Torah is not their craft are obligated; however, if he has a little craft or a little commerce to sustain himself — enough for his living and not to become wealthy — and every hour he is free from his business he returns to words of Torah and learns regularly, he is called one whose Torah is his craft: {Rama: And there is no distinction whether he holds a yeshiva or not, only that he is established as a Torah scholar in his generation who knows to decide and determine in Torah and understands on his own in most places in the Talmud and its commentaries and in the rulings of the Geonim, and his Torah is his craft as explained. (Terumat HaDeshen siman 342.) And although in our generation there is no sage for the matter of giving him a litra of gold if they disgrace him, nevertheless for exempting from tax they are not punctilious about this, only that he be established as a Torah scholar as explained. (There siman 341.) Nevertheless, some places practiced to exempt Torah scholars from tax and some places practiced not to exempt them. (There siman 342.)}
