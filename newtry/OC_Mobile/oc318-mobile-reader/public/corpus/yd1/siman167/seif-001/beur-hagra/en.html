(Supplement) A loan, etc. And this is not analogous to the case of Rav Chama (69b) who lent money and accepted all the responsibility — for there the entire business was for the sake of the recipient, and he set a fixed amount for the giver; as written above in siman 177, seif 6. But here the entire business is for the sake of the giver, and [the agent] is like his messenger; and nonetheless it is necessary that responsibility fall on the giver — since [the transaction] is done for his benefit, so that he will thereafter have a loan in [the agent's] hand, and the profit is his; but since he gives him a wage for his labor and his upkeep, he is permitted to engage in [the giver's] deposit, even though it will thereafter be a loan in [the agent's] hand. And like the case of Rav Chama; and as with an iska where it is permitted to engage in [the investor's] deposit portion even though [the agent] acts on account of the loan portion — since he gives him a wage for his labor. And like the case of the Jerusalem Talmud that I cited — that he said "until Chanukah, etc." But nevertheless this law does not seem correct to me — and as stated there: "he did not find a permitted third, etc. — and alternatively, etc." And it is not analogous to the Jerusalem Talmud case — for there he said: "after Chanukah it shall be yours," whether or not there was profit or loss before Chanukah; and likewise in the tarsha of Rav Chama — they were not concerned at all about the carrying [i.e., the trip]: if there was profit or not — but on the return, the profit belongs to them. Unlike here. Furthermore it seems to me that it is only permitted as an iska, but in a loan it is forbidden; and greater than this is siman 177, seif 14: "one who says, etc." — even though it is permitted by law; and as written there in seif 18: "one who sells, etc." — nonetheless it is forbidden in a loan — as Rashba wrote there: by way of a loan it is forbidden; see there. And furthermore the Tur wrote there, seif 34: "one who gives an iska to his fellow, etc. — he should not write a promissory note against him, for it would be like a loan, etc." And what does it help that he gives him a wage for his labor — for even a courtyard that does not normally stand for rental and a man who does not normally hire himself out — it is forbidden to dwell in his courtyard [in exchange for a loan]; and all the more so "lend me and dwell, etc." — and all the more so when he stipulates that he engage in his deposit on account of his loan, even though he gives him a wage; as written in siman 160, seif 23: "the lender, etc." — and the same applies in reverse: that on [the condition] that whatever work [the lender] gives to the borrower he must accept from him — is also forbidden. And here [the arrangement] is only permitted as an iska or as in the Jerusalem Talmud case; or as written in the gloss here. (End.) And provided, etc. The Rosh there — and like the tarsha of R.
