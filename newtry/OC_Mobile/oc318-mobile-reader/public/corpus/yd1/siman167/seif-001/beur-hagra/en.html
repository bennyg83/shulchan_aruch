A loan, etc. This is like the credit sale of Rav Chama, as the Rosh and other poskim write. The same appears in the Yerushalmi: R' Chiya gave a dinar to a certain person and said to him, "Whatever profits there are from now until Chanukah belong to me; from then onward I have no involvement with you. If they decrease or increase, it is yours," etc.
(Likkut) A loan, etc. This is not comparable to the case of Rav Chama, Bava Metzia 69b, who rented out money and accepted all the risk. There the entire business was for the benefit of the recipient, who fixed a definite payment for the investor, as written above in siman 177, seif 6. Here, however, the entire business is for the benefit of the investor, and the recipient is like his agent. Nevertheless the risk must be upon the investor, because the recipient performs the work for the investor's benefit so that afterward the money will become a loan in his hands and the profit will be his. Since the investor pays him wages for his labor and sustenance, however, he may work with the deposit even though it will afterward become a loan in his hands, just as in the case of Rav Chama, and as we write regarding an iska that one may work with the deposit portion even though he does so for the sake of the loan portion, because he receives payment for his labor. This is also like the Yerushalmi cited above, where he said that until Chanukah, etc. Nevertheless, this ruling does not appear correct to me, as stated there concerning one who did not find a surplus of one-third, etc., and the alternative explanation there. It is not comparable to the Yerushalmi, because there he said that after Chanukah it would be entirely the recipient's, regardless of whether there had been profit or loss before Chanukah. Likewise, in Rav Chama's credit sale, they did not care at all whether there was a profit on the outward journey; in any event, the profit on the return journey belonged to them. Here it is different. Furthermore, it appears to me that this is permitted only when he gives the money as an iska, but not as a loan. Indeed, in siman 177, seif 14, regarding one who says, etc., although it is intrinsically permitted, as stated there in seif 18 concerning one who sells, etc., nevertheless it is forbidden when structured as a loan, as Rashba writes there: by way of a loan it is forbidden. See there. Tur further writes there in seif 34 that one who gives another an iska, etc., should not write a loan document for it, because that turns it into a loan, etc. What help is it that he pays wages for the labor? Even regarding a courtyard that is not normally rented and a person who normally does not rent, it is forbidden for the borrower to live in the lender's courtyard; all the more so if he says, "Lend me and live there." Certainly it is prohibited if he stipulates that the borrower work with his deposit in consideration of the loan, even if he pays him wages, as stated in siman 160, seif 23, concerning a lender, etc. The same applies in reverse, if he stipulates that every task he gives to the borrower must be accepted. Thus here it is permitted only as an iska, like the Yerushalmi's case or as stated in the Rama's gloss here. End.
