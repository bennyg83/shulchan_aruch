Lender, etc. As in Trashei of Rav Chama — Rosh and Shulchan Aruch. And so Yerushalmi: Reish Chodesh gave a dinar to someone and said, what is mine from here until Hanukkah is mine from there and beyond I have no business with you — we do not decrease and do not increase, yours, etc. (Extract) Lender, etc. And it is not similar to Rav Chama (folio 69 side 2) who converted money and accepted all responsibility, for there the entire business was for the recipient and he cut a known amount for the giver, as stated siman 177 seif 6; but here the entire business is for the giver and he is like his agent; nevertheless responsibility must be on the giver since he acts for his benefit that afterward there be a loan in his hand and the profit is his — except that since he gives him wages for his labor and sustenance, he may engage with his deposit even though afterward there will be a loan in his hand, as that case of Rav Chama; and as stated regarding iska that one may engage with part of the deposit even though acting for the lender's portion, since he gives him wages for his labor, as that Yerushalmi case I brought where he said until Hanukkah, etc.; but nevertheless this law does not seem correct to me, as stated below not found permitted one-third, etc.; and one can say, etc.; and it is not similar to that Yerushalmi case, for there he said after Hanukkah it will be yours even if he does not profit or lose before Hanukkah; and so in Trashei of Rav Chama they do not care at all about transport whether there will be profit or not — nevertheless upon return the profit is theirs, unlike here. And further it seems to me it is permitted only when the giver gives him in iska; but in loan it is forbidden; and greater than this siman 177 seif 14: one who says, etc. — even though permitted by law, as stated seif 18: one who sells, etc. — nevertheless forbidden in loan, as stated Rashba that in the manner of loan it is forbidden — see there; and Tur wrote there seif 34: one who gives iska to his fellow, etc. — he should not write a document on him, for it is a loan, etc. And what helps that he gives him wages for his labor — even a courtyard not standing for rent and a man who does not engage in renting, it is forbidden to dwell in his courtyard — all the more a lender and dweller, etc.; all the more one who stipulates with him that he engage with his deposit for the sake of his loan even though he gives him wages, as stated siman 165 seif 23: lender, etc.; and the same reversed — that on condition all work the borrower gives him he also accepts — forbidden. It is permitted here only in iska and like the Yerushalmi case, or as stated in Hagah here (end).
