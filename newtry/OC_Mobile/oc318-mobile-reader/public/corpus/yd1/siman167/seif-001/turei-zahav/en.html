And it shall be on the lender's responsibility. In Derishah he wrote: it appears if he stipulated with him initially and gives him wages so that the agent accept responsibility — similar to stipulating with him for his labor for a small amount, similar to stipulating with a guarantor to be as borrower in CM siman 291 — end quote. I wondered at these words — did they come from the mouth of the righteous? The essence of the permit in this law is because at the time he profits a maneh it is still deposit in his hand and not a loan; and if he accepts responsibility at that time too, the money would be a loan in his hand, as Beit Yosef wrote siman 169 regarding another matter: if responsibility for the money was on him, it is completely as if he lent Israel his money — end quote — and there is full ribbit here; and what helps that the lender give the borrower wages for accepting responsibility — this is like one who gives the borrower gifts that he lend him money at ribbit — likewise here. And the proof he brought from guarantor to be as borrower does not apply, for there it is not his money lent, but here it is his money lent and it is ribbit; and even in a fully permitted matter we find this distinction — at beginning of siman 166: one should not do work with his slave, for the prohibition is only because of the loan, as stated there, and it is Talmud; and it is not similar to what Rama wrote siman 169 seif 18: if they stipulated with each other that the first lender, etc., shall be obligated on his responsibility, etc. — for there the first already sold to the second all right he had on the gentile's collateral, only he guards it and can stipulate to be as borrower; unlike here where afterward he is an actual borrower, only initially he is not borrower since on lender's responsibility — and this is the essence of the permit — end quote: responsibility must not ruin the permit; and further, specifically regarding collateral one can say stipulating to be as borrower, for it is not given for spending; but not regarding money; and Tur wrote explicitly siman 169 in words of Rama: some say regarding money it does not return as is, unlike collateral that returns as is — stipulating to be as borrower; therefore here it is all loan — how can he take more than he lent him? Certainly full ribbit; and so Rosh in responsum klal 75 siman 2: one who receives half profit is forbidden to accept all responsibility; and some say to guarantor to be as borrower — for there he is always guardian and can obligate himself as law of borrower; but here where he trades assets, since he accepted all responsibility, the name of deposit expired from him and he became lender — end quote his language; meaning always must not expire from him the name of deposit; therefore do not rely on this permit Derishah wrote; see what is written siman 177 seif 6 in explanation of R. Yeshaya's words, that even if the recipient acquires from the giver his profit portion — forbidden; and likewise proven siman 172 seif 3 — see our words there; and so it seems to plain understanding. In Teshuvat HaRashba siman 302 he wrote to find permit for one who gives money to his fellow for half profit and wants his principal secure — he can say the borrower shall not be believed regarding loss except on testimony of rabbi and beadle; and in such manner people sitting in tents in a city with knowledge of commerce — even though both intend it not be so that they testify, and they do not stipulate thus except to take ribbit permissibly; he brings proof — see there; and concludes there this permit is only for one who gives to iska where there is no Torah ribbit even without any condition, since he did not fix with him only if there be profit — only rabbinically forbidden as close to profit and far from loss, meaning his principal should be secure at least — therefore permitted; but one who fixed with the borrower to give him a fixed amount of profit and does so to make principal secure should not rely on this condition and take ribbit unless two people testify — end quote. Levush expanded here and distinguishes between principal and profit: regarding principal he can stipulate thus to be secure, for clearly he has principal in hand, only wishes to exempt himself with claim of loss — on this he says I do not believe you except before designated witnesses; but not regarding profit, which is doubtful from the start whether there will be profit at all — correct distinction; only it implies from his words that regarding principal this condition helps even if he fixed with him initially, such as here where he stipulated it be on lender's responsibility until there be two hundred — even here it helps that designated witnesses are required specifically for loss of principal — puzzling that he did not see end of Rashba's words we mentioned, that specifically where there is no fixed cut one may rely on this. And even where there is no fixed cut but gives half profit — permitted to make this condition that he not be believed on loss of principal except on testimony of rabbi and beadle — in a matter where rabbi and beadle have some knowledge of this borrower's business, as stated explicitly they sit in tents with knowledge, etc.; unlike those who choose witnesses who know nothing of commerce and do so so he cannot testify on this loss — certainly complete prohibition, close to profit and far from loss entirely. Levush wrote to find remedy for lenders on documents that he write the borrower owes the lender merchandise he now has in his house and sells him cheaply — every person may sell his merchandise cheaply even worth two hundred for one hundred, he will assign to lender at set time; if he transgresses he must pay him much more even double what he gave — complete permit; provided he knows truth that borrower has that merchandise in his house at that time and he acquires merchandise with kinyan immediately he makes on the debt — end quote; words of Rivash brought in Shulchan Aruch siman 177 seif 18; see what I wrote there; it seems clear this permit has no place unless he did not first say lend me money, but immediately afterward: behold I have merchandise, I will sell you cheaply, and if not I will assign to you at set time I will give you such amount — but if he first said lend me a maneh and afterward makes this permit with him — forbidden, as we find siman 163 seif 3: he said lend me a maneh, he said I have no maneh, I have wheat for a maneh, etc. — forbidden since he first said lend me; and there even though he made complete sale of wheat at value and what he returned and bought from him at ninety is also complete permit — nevertheless forbidden; all the more here where he sells cheaply initially — appears more as ribbit evasion; and not only that but full ribbit here per Beit Yosef there siman 163 in name of students of Rashba: if they were worth ninety initially and he gave for one hundred — full ribbit, as I cited above — likewise here selling much cheaper; one reason: we see there is no real sale; and specifically because he first said lend me; and further here worse, for at time of giving money he fixed explicitly to give more than he gives — therefore do not rely if he first said lend me money, since he initially comes with him in manner of lending money and he pays money and gives more than he took — do not make permit when he first said lend me money, only that afterward he sells him merchandise he has cheaply without any condition and after an hour returns and sells to him per what is mentioned siman 174 seif 2; and this only if he has all merchandise he sells, not sufficient with a little as in loan, as we mentioned siman 173 seif 1.
