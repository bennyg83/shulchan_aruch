Chafifah in a place of hair should not be with cold water, because it entangles the hair; rather, with hot water. Nevertheless, even sun-heated hot water suffices. And she should not wash with neter, called in Arabic “tafel” (Rambam in the commentary to the Mishnah, perek 2 of Keilim), and in the vernacular “gerida” (Rivash), because it cuts the hair and it again becomes tangled; and not with “ohel,” because it entangles the hair; and not with anything that entangles the hair. {Rama: And all this is l’chatchila; but if she washed with neter or the like, and she saw that her hairs are not tied and not entangled, it is permitted (Mahari”k, shoresh 159). And a woman whom physicians commanded not to wash her head with water but only with wine: one should ask the physicians whether the wine entangles the hairs; and if they say that it does not entangle, one may rely on them. And if the physicians are not expert in this matter, the woman should first test for herself whether the wine does not entangle the hairs (also this there).}
