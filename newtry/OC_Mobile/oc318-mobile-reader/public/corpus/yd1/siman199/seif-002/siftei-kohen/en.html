And in the vernacular “grida.” Taz wrote this is what is called in Ashkenazic German “kreida,” etc.; but Perishah wrote: some say “kreida,” etc., and they are mistaken in it, etc.; and so I heard from a vernacular speaker, etc.; however it is implied that with natron, which we call in Ashkenazic German “zaif,” she may perform chafifah — for it does not cause tangles and on the contrary cleanses the filth; and so implied in Perishah above siman 135 seif 16; and so they practice and consider this a mitzvah.
But if, etc. — permitted. Meaning: permitted this time since she already did it and need not return to perform chafifah; but that she should rely on this and again perform chafifah with natron and ohel and cold water — no; and so explained in Mahari”k there that he only permitted with wine and other beverages not mentioned in Sha”s and poskim that they tangle hair, for he combined also the reason that one has nothing other than what the Sages said; and therefore he permitted performing chafifah again through checking that she checked first three times; but with natron and ohel and cold water — no; see there, explained explicitly. And so implied from the Rav's words. And similarly we say above siman 57 s.k. 48 that all that the Sages said is tereifah and does not live — even though we see it lives it is tereifah. And Taz wrote: all this lechatchila; but if she performed chafifah, etc. — permitted; therefore this woman performs chafifah with natron and ohel, etc. — and he was not precise.
The woman should try. Three times — Mahari”k there; and in Taz he wrote two or three times; and one should not object: let us see ourselves whether wine tangles hair or not — one may say the nature of each person's hair is not the same, as physicians and researchers have written.
