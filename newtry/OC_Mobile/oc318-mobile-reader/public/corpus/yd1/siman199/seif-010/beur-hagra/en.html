She combed, etc., between this, etc. Rambam and per our girsa in the first language "close," and in the later language "on that day"; and he interprets — she need not comb and immerse, for she does not need another chafifah before immersion; but another immersion she always needs. And therefore he said to comb; and he interprets — one amora: close to chafifah is immersion, per Tosafot there s.v. chad amora, etc.; and regarding chafifah per his method he ruled like the later language. But method of Rif and Rosh — specifically close, and then she does not need even another immersion; and girsa of Rif is reversed as above 1a; and he interprets "need not," etc., even immersion. But Rashba wrote in the name of Ra'avyah — what is "semuch bo bayom," and I do not understand his words for it is the first language. And words of Beit Yosef are strained — that he wrote Raavad's girsa is the later language "need not to comb nor immerse," and in the first language we do not say so. And his words are astounding — for if so it should have said "one amora" in such a case; and in Rif and Teshuvot HaRosh all girsa is as written in Gemara before us "to comb and immerse" — except they dispute in explanation as above whether it refers to chafifah or immersion; and he says to comb per the commentators because of the law that she needs to comb, for even chafifah requires.
Even though she was, etc. So Beit Yosef infers from words of Rambam [and Rashba mentioned there in the name of Ra'avyah]. And it is like our girsa in Gemara "to rely," etc., "to comb," etc. — meaning in each case leniency and stringency. But Rif and all poskim do not have it for him; and likewise in words of Rambam it does not imply so — one may say all the more when close; and Ran also wrote like girsa of Rif; and he wrote — and some girsa "to comb by day," etc., and do not have "to rely on chafifah immersion," and he explains in a strange and far way.
