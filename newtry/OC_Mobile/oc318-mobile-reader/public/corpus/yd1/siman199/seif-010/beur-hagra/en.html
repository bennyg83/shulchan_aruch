If she performed chafifah, etc.; whether in the meantime, etc. This is the Rambam’s ruling, following our version of the Gemara, in which the first formulation requires proximity and the latter formulation requires only that it be on the same day. He explains that she need not perform chafifah and immerse again, meaning that she does not require another chafifah before the second immersion, though she always requires another immersion. This is why the Gemara mentions chafifah. He explains the practical difference as whether immersion must immediately follow chafifah, in accordance with Tosafot there, s.v. ika beinaihu, etc. Regarding chafifah, he follows his own approach and rules according to the latter formulation. The Rif and Rosh, however, require immediate proximity, and when it was immediate she does not require even another immersion. The Rif’s version reverses the formulations, as explained above in seif 1, and he interprets ‘she need not,’ etc., as meaning that she need not even immerse again. The Rashba, however, writes in the name of the Razah that ‘close’ means on that same day. I do not understand his words, for that is the first formulation. The Beit Yosef’s explanation is strained. He writes that according to the Raavad’s version the latter formulation reads, ‘she need not perform chafifah or immerse,’ whereas the first formulation does not read so. This is difficult, for if so the Gemara should have identified that very case as the practical difference. In the Rif, Terumat HaDeshen, and all versions, the text is as in the Gemara before us, ‘to perform chafifah and immerse.’ The authorities disagree only over its interpretation, as explained above, whether it refers to the chafifah or to the immersion. According to the commentators, the Gemara mentions chafifah because of the independent law that she must perform it, since even chafifah is required.
Even though it was, etc. The Beit Yosef infers this from the Rambam’s words, and from the Rashba cited above in the name of the Razah. This accords with our version of the Gemara, which speaks both of placing immersion close to chafifah and of performing chafifah by day, meaning that each position contains both a leniency and a stringency. The Rif and all the poskim, however, do not have this reading. Nor is this implication clear from the Rambam, for one may say that proximity is certainly sufficient. The Ran likewise records the Rif’s version. He adds that some read, ‘to perform chafifah by day,’ etc., and omit the phrase concerning placing immersion close to chafifah; he explains that version in a forced and remote manner.
