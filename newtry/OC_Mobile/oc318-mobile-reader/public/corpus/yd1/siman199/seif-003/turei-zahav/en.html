Immediately before her immersion. The Tur cites the dispute between Rashi and the She’iltos concerning this. According to Rashi, it is preferable that the washing and combing be done during the day before her immersion rather than at night, because at night she hastens home and will not wash and comb properly. The She’iltos explains that it is preferable to do so at night so that it will be immediately before her immersion. Therefore, they adopted the proper practice copied here in the Shulchan Aruch.
When she must wash and comb during the day. For example, she will be traveling and will not have hot water at night, or there is some other unavoidable circumstance. Washing and combing during the day suffices even according to the She’iltos. Likewise, when it is impossible to wash and comb during the day, doing so at night suffices even according to Rashi. Their statements apply only where either option is conveniently available and there is no impediment. It appears clear that when we say that in a pressing circumstance she may wash and comb during the day, she must nevertheless inspect and examine her entire body at night before immersion. Her law is exactly like that explained in seif 6 regarding the days between washing and combing and immersion.
