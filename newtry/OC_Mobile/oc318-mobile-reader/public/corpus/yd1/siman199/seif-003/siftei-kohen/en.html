Chafifah, etc. And in my humble opinion from the entire sugya of Sha"s (Niddah 67 and 68) per view of Rashi and his supporters that it is better that chafifah be by day where possible, as written in the books — see there; and therefore if her night of immersion falls on motza'ei Shabbat or even on motza'ei Yom Tov that happens to be after Shabbat, she combs on erev Shabbat and immerses on motza'ei Shabbat or motza'ei Yom Tov — since it is possible that chafifah be by day on a weekday. But where her night of immersion falls on the night of Wednesday after Yom Tov — such as when there happen to be two days of Yom Tov after Shabbat, or when there happen to be two days of Rosh Hashanah or of the diaspora on Thursday Friday and her night of immersion is on motza'ei Shabbat — behold there are three days between chafifah and immersion; and in such a case it is not possible that chafifah be so far, as Rav Chisda and Rav Yeimar hold that in such a case she combs on the night of her immersion since it is too far; and this is what we say in Sha"s — "halachata — a woman combs by day," and "halachata — a woman should not comb except at night" — halachata against halachata is not difficult: this where possible, this where not possible — understand; and Sha"s relies on the clear statements of Rav Chisda and Rav Yeimar mentioned above. And it appears to me that Rashi's view is also thus, for he explains — this where possible to comb by day she combs by day; this where not possible to comb, such as motza'ei Yom Tov, she should not comb except at night — end quote (and likewise in Sefer Yere'im siman 192). And what he wrote "such as motza'ei Yom Tov" — meaning of Rosh Hashanah; and he relies on the above that in such a case it is not possible — no, it is possible to comb on Yom Tov; and with this it fits well that the entire sugya above stands — unlike Tosafot and poskim who need to strain, for then the sugya above goes away from them. And further difficult on their words — for it implies there explicitly from words of Rav Huna and Rav Chisda and Rav Yeimar and Mar Yemar that thus the daughters of Israel practiced in their time — to comb by day on erev Shabbat or erev Yom Tov and immerse on motza'ei Shabbat; for behold they bring proof from their custom that it is permitted to comb on Sunday and immerse on Wednesday or Thursday of the week — except Rav Chisda and his colleagues dispute on weekdays that one does not infer possible from impossible; and thus Mar Yemar concludes there halachata. If so, how can we say the sugya below that concludes halachata disputes the sugya and halachata and custom above? Rather certainly as I explained — this is my view in Sha"s and Rashi — except that poskim did not explain thus and did not write thus in the name of Rashi, and I do not know from where they have this. And for practical law it appears that in every case she may rinse with hot water by day on erev Shabbat and erev Yom Tov, and also comb then, and return to rinse and comb on the night of her immersion if it is a weekday; and so poskim, and likewise on weekdays the proper custom is that chafifah begin from erev Yom Tov and she be engaged in her chafifah until dark, as poskim and Tur wrote. And although where her night of immersion is three days distant — such as Shabbat and two days of Yom Tov — in such a case we conclude in Sha"s that she combs on the night of her immersion which is a weekday, one may say the intent is not that erev Shabbat and erev Yom Tov suffice since it is so far, but rather she must also comb on the night of her immersion; and also on erev Shabbat and erev Yom Tov since chafifah by day is preferable — and thus it is proper to do more, since our women comb on erev Shabbat and erev Yom Tov and also on the night of their immersion. Therefore one should not be lenient for them to distinguish between two days distant or three days lest they come to error; and likewise in Shulchan Shel David siman 10 that even in such a case she combs three days beforehand as well. And possibly this is because we say in Sha"s that Mar Yemar derives halachata like Rav Chisda as Rav Yeimar resolves — he adopts stringencies: stringency of Rav Chisda on two days of Yom Tov after Shabbat that she combs on erev Shabbat, for it is better to comb by day; and stringency of Rav Yeimar that she combs on the night of her immersion since it is so far; and this is what is written in Mordechai in Shevuot perek 2 of Niddah — we rule like Rav Chisda that a woman combs on erev Shabbat and immerses even on Wednesday night when there happen to be two days of Yom Tov after Shabbat; and likewise Ra'avyah in his book like Rashi that it is better to comb by day than at night — end quote. For at first glance it is difficult — for we rule in Sha"s as Rav Yeimar resolves; rather certainly he holds like Rav Chisda and as Rav Yeimar resolves — meaning as he explained, and not like Rashi there; and language "as Rav Yeimar resolves" fits well with this explanation; but per Rashi's explanation it should have said "and like Rav Yeimar" — for he disputes — understand. And from this reason it appears poskim did not distinguish between two days of Yom Tov that happen after Shabbat that she combs on motza'ei Yom Tov, and one day of Yom Tov that happens after Shabbat as we distinguish in Sha"s in this — since they wrote even on other Yom Tov it is proper to be stringent to comb from erev Yom Tov and also on motza'ei Yom Tov — therefore there is no distinction — understand; for all this is clear in my view. I again found in responsum of Maharshal siman 6 that he decided like those who dispute Rashi without compelling reason. And in my humble opinion I further wrote — Maharshal wrote there regarding what they practiced formerly to begin combing by day, and now they enacted to comb at night and be engaged in chafifah specifically one hour so there not be tumult in her house — permitted even per Rashi since there is concern of issur that they not sense their immersions; and also sometimes modest women come to neglect obligatory immersion; and likewise Rav in responsum siman 21 — see there that they extended on this.
