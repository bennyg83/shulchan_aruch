Washing the hair, etc. In my view the entire sugya in Niddah 67-68 supports Rashi and those who follow him, that where possible it is preferable for the washing to be by day. Therefore, if immersion is Motzaei Shabbos, or even the night after a festival that followed Shabbos, she washes Friday and immerses afterward, since daytime washing before Shabbos is possible. But where the immersion night follows several consecutive sacred days, so that washing Friday would be excessively remote from immersion, Rav Chisda and Rav Yeimar hold that she washes on the night of immersion. This is the meaning of the Gemara's two rulings, 'a woman washes by day' and 'a woman should wash only at night': where daytime washing is practicable, she washes by day; where it is not practicable, she washes at night. Rashi's example of the night after a festival can be understood as Rosh Hashanah or another sequence in which prior daytime washing is too remote. This preserves the entire preceding sugya and the practice of Jewish women described there. Although the later poskim did not explain Rashi this way, in practice it appears proper in every case to rinse in hot water and wash on Friday or the eve of the festival, and to wash again on the immersion night when that night is a weekday. On an ordinary weekday, the proper custom is to begin washing before nightfall and continue until dark. Even where the immersion is three days later, such as after Shabbos and two festival days, she should wash before the sacred days and again on the immersion night. This avoids confusion and accords with Shaarei Dura.
She should bring a comb with her, etc. And in these countries they practice rinsing and performing chafifah and bathing and combing everything in the bathhouse immediately near the immersion — and this is proper.
That she must perform chafifah by day. Specifically, and not at night — such as when it is impossible to perform chafifah at night because idolaters have a festival that night and they do not permit kindling fire in any house, or some other compulsion; and as stated in Beit Yosef. And likewise when she cannot perform chafifah by day because of some compulsion but only at night — specifically at night. But whenever possible both by day and at night — she performs chafifah by day and at night; and this is simple.
