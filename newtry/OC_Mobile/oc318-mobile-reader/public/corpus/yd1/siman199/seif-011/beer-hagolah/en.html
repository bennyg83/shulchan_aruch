The Tur from the words of the Rashba in Torat HaBayit. Beit Yosef writes that his reason is the baraita in Chullin 10a concerning one who immersed and ascended. In my humble opinion, this is astonishing, because he returns to the lenient ruling while bringing proof for stringency. Rather, according to the Rashba, that baraita must concern matters of ritual purity, as Tosafot writes there in the name of Rabbeinu Chananel, that greater stringency was applied to ritual purity than to a woman's permissibility to her husband. Rambam, however, at the end of chapter 2 of Hilchot Mikvaot, interprets it as applying equally to her husband and rules that her immersion is invalid unless she says with certainty that the interposition was not upon her beforehand. The Rashba likewise cites this in the name of the Raavad.
The Tur. The Taz explains that she washed on the preceding day, such as on Friday and immersed after Shabbat. If she did not wash at all, the Tur already wrote above in seif 8 that even if she inspected herself immediately after immersion, the immersion is invalid. Bach explains it differently. See there.
