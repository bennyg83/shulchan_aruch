When does this apply, etc. See Tosafot there, s.v. im samuch, etc.: that which the Gemara says, etc., refers to a case where she did not, etc. Rabbeinu Yitzchak further explains, etc. The practical difference between this answer and the first answer is that according to this answer, if she subsequently handled a substance that might adhere, we are more inclined to attribute the intervening matter to that activity, even where she did not immerse immediately after chafifah. The case is specifically one where she performed no chafifah at all, but merely inspected herself, which is a Torah requirement. The Rashba and Tur adopt this answer. Nevertheless, their words require study, for if she performed no chafifah at all, she must in any event immerse again, as explained above in seif 8. Tosafot therefore explain the passage as referring to matters of ritual purity. See Shach. The Rambam cites the baraita in its plain sense, in accordance with his approach that the distinction concerns only chafifah, and he therefore does not require Tosafot’s answer. In seif 10 the Shulchan Aruch records the Rambam’s ruling, whereas here it records the view of the other poskim.
