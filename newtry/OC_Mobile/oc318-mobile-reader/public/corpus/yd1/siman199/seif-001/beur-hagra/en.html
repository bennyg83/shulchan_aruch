And to comb, etc. See Rashi in Bava Kamma there, 82b, s.v. chafifah, etc.
(Supplement) And to comb, etc., and likewise, etc. For chafifah applies only to the head, as is evident from Nazir 42a and elsewhere: ‘A nazir may wash and separate his hair,’ etc.; from the second chapter of Shabbos, 31a, where Hillel washed his head; from the third chapter of Yom Tov, 27b, and elsewhere; and likewise from Bava Kamma there, where the Gemara states that it is a Torah requirement, and asks: What is it? Hair. See Ran. End of supplement.
That she should not, etc. Gemara there.
And likewise she must, etc. The Gemara there states that inspection is required by Torah law, whether under the first explanation or the alternative explanation. This reasoning applies to the entire body. By contrast, regarding chafifah, which was instituted by Ezra, Tosafot explain in the name of Rabbeinu Tam that it applies only to the hair, and they bring several proofs. See Tosafot there, s.v. veshetei, etc.; Niddah 66b, s.v. im, etc.; Chullin 10a, s.v. ad, etc.; the conclusion of the Rosh; and similarly the Rashba and other poskim. They write that inspection, however, is required by Torah law. Rashi writes that her entire body requires chafifah. Tosafot and the Ran write that, even according to Tosafot’s interpretation, the customary practice was to wash and rinse the entire body. This follows from the phrase, ‘something was found upon her,’ which implies that it was found on her body, and the ruling depends on whether the immersion was close to the chafifah. Likewise, see Niddah 68a, ‘My beloved, you were lacking,’ etc. See also Tosafot in Bava Kamma at the end of the cited passage and Tosafot in Niddah at the end of the cited passage, who write, ‘Although we are drawing an analogy,’ etc. This is the meaning of the ruling that she should wash her entire body and rinse it. Initially, the Tur states the essential law, that a woman must inspect, etc.; afterward, as a stringency, he writes that she should wash, etc. See Taz.
Close to immersion. The Gemara there states: if she immersed and emerged, etc. The Rif’s version reverses the order of the two formulations. The practical difference between them is whether immersion must immediately follow chafifah. The Ran explains that according to the latter formulation it must be close, and we rule according to the latter formulation. The Gemara likewise concludes on 65a: ‘The halachah is that a woman performs chafifah,’ in accordance with the explanation of the She’iltos there, with which all the poskim agree. See below, seif 3.
