And her concealed areas. The Tur writes, “the folds and all her concealed areas.” The Beit Yosef wondered, for these are one and the same, whereas in the Gemara Rava mentioned the folds separately. The Darkei Moshe answered that this accords with what the Mordechai wrote in the name of the Ra’em: “the folds” means beneath the armpits and beneath the thigh, while “the concealed areas” means inward areas, such as the openings of the nose and ears and the inner side of the lip, end quote. This wording of the Tur appears in the Gemara in Niddah 66: “They raised an objection: the folds and the concealed areas,” etc. It is astonishing that the Beit Yosef saw the words of Rava but did not see the objection that follows them.
And not with other liquids. For when some of their moisture remains within a fold, the stain of the liquid will remain there.
Likewise, a woman must examine, etc. The Tur wrote concerning the words of the Ramban that it appears he is more stringent regarding examination of the body, etc. In truth, the Tur’s words are difficult, for he wrote that examination of the head is from the enactment of Ezra, whereas this is not so. Certainly, the essential obligation of examination of the head is according to the Torah, as the Beit Yosef wrote. The words of the Tur therefore require correction. It appears that they should be understood as follows: “examination of the head” means washing and combing the head, which for us serves in place of the Torah-required examination. The Tur inferred from the Ramban’s words that he mentioned examination of the body immediately before immersion, whereas regarding washing and combing the head he did not state this. Evidently, with respect to washing and combing they did not insist that it be performed literally immediately before immersion. So too the Beit Yosef wrote in the name of the Ran: “Since washing and combing is only rabbinic, they were lenient regarding it, as we state below that a woman may wash and comb on erev Shabbos and immerse on Motzei Shabbos,” etc., end quote. The Tur did not intend merely to teach this leniency, for there is no need to derive it from the Ramban, since it is an explicit Gemara. Rather, he teaches here that even when she distances the washing and combing from the immersion, she must in any event not distance the examination at all, as the Beit Yosef concludes later in this siman.
And she shall wash and comb her entire body. His language implies that she must first inspect and examine her entire body, and afterward wash and comb her entire body. This is difficult, for why should washing and combing the entire body alone not suffice? At that time she is also examining herself, and there is no greater examination than this. Although she cannot see the place that she is washing and combing, this is sufficient, for otherwise how could she do so on her head and in places that she cannot see? They did not require her to show herself to another woman. They required another woman to stand over her specifically during immersion, but not during washing, combing, and examination. It is obvious that examination by touch constitutes proper examination according to Torah law, and all the more so under Ezra’s enactment of washing, combing, and rinsing. It therefore appears that the latter clause explains the former. What he first wrote, that she must examine herself, etc., refers to the Torah law; he then concludes that she should be stringent in the manner of that examination by washing and combing her entire body. It is obvious that if she inspects and examines herself while washing and combing, this suffices, and a separate examination is not specifically required.
