And likewise a woman needs to examine, etc. Tur wrote on words of Ramban — it appears he is more stringent in examination of the body, etc.; in truth his words are astounding — for he wrote that examination of the head is enactment of Ezra, and this is certainly not the essence of examination, for from Torah examination is in the head as Beit Yosef wrote; and Tur's words need correction. And it appears thus: examination of the head means chafifah of the head, which is for us in place of examination from Torah; and Tur learned from words of Ramban that he mentioned regarding examination of the body close to immersion, and regarding chafifah of the head he did not mention so — rather certainly for chafifah they did not insist it be literally close to immersion; and likewise Beit Yosef in the name of Ran — and this is their language: and because chafifah is merely rabbinic they are lenient in it as we say below "a woman combs on erev Shabbat and immerses on motza'ei Shabbat," etc. — end quote. And Tur did not intend this leniency — for one need not learn this from words of Ramban, rather Gemara is arranged in any case to teach here that even where she distances chafifah from immersion, nevertheless examination one should not distance at all; and as Beit Yosef concludes afterward in this siman.
And she should comb her entire body. From his language it implies we require first examination and bedikah of the entire body, and afterward she does chafifah of the entire body — and this is astounding, for why is chafifah of the entire body alone not sufficient, for behold then she also checks — for you have no greater bedikah than this; for although she does not see in the place she combs, it suffices thus; for otherwise how will she do on her head and places she cannot see — for they did not require that she show her friend, rather specifically at immersion they said another woman stands over her, and not in chafifah and examination; but a simple matter that through handling it is proper examination by Torah law; and all the more per enactment of Ezra in chafifah and rinsing. And it appears the latter portion is explanation of the former — that which he first wrote she needs to examine herself, etc. — this is Torah law; and on this he concludes afterward that he is stringent on her in place of examination she should do chafifah on entire body; and simple matter that if she examines and checks herself at time of chafifah it suffices, and one does not need special bedikah specifically.
