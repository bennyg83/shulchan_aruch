And its hidden places (beit hasetarim). The Tur writes "wrinkles" (kematzim) and "every hidden place"—and Yosef Dea wondered, for they are one thing. In the Gemara Rava said: wrinkles are separate, etc. He answered: as the Mordechai in the name of the Ra'am—wrinkles are under the armpits and under the thigh; hidden places are inward, such as nostrils and ears and inward from the lip, etc. This wording of the Tur appears in the Gemara in Niddah (66b): "They challenged from 'the wrinkles and the hidden places'..." And it is surprising on the Beit Yosef that he saw Rava's words and did not see the challenge that follows.

And likewise the woman must examine, etc. The Tur wrote on Ramban's words: "it appears he is more stringent regarding examination of the body, etc." — in truth his words are puzzling, for he wrote that examination of the head is from Ezra's ordinance, and this is not so — certainly the principal examination at the biblical level is of the head, as Beit Yosef wrote. The Tur's words require correction. It seems to me they should be read as follows: what the Tur learned from Ramban's words is that examination of the body was mentioned close to the immersion, whereas washing the head was not mentioned so — meaning Ramban held that for the head there is no requirement that it be immediately before the immersion; and as Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Ran: "and since washing the head is only rabbinic, they were lenient about it, as we say later: a woman may wash her hair on the eve of Shabbat and immerse on Saturday night, etc." — end of quote. And the Tur did not intend this leniency, as it requires no derivation from Ramban's words — it is established Talmudic law; rather the Tur teaches us that even where she extends the washing far from the immersion, nevertheless the examination at least should not be extended at all — and as Beit Yosef concludes afterward in this siman. And she should wash all of her body. His language implies that first she should examine and check all of her body, and then wash all of her body — which is puzzling: for why is washing all of her body alone not sufficient, since she is also checking thereby? For there is no greater checking than this — even though she cannot see where she is washing, it suffices; since otherwise how would she manage with her head and places she cannot see, for they did not require her to show herself to another; only for immersion did they say another woman should stand over her, not for washing and examination. Rather it is obvious that the touching itself constitutes the biblical-level examination, and all the more so regarding Ezra's ordinance of washing and rinsing. And it seems the latter clause is here the explanation of the former: what he first wrote — that she needs to examine herself — is the biblical law; and on this he concludes that in place of examination she should wash all of her body. And it is obvious that if she examines and checks herself during the washing that suffices — and no separate prior examination is required.
