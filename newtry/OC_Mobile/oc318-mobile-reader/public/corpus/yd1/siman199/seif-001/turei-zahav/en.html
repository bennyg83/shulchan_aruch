And her hidden areas. In the Tur it is written: folds and all her hidden areas. The Beit Yosef wondered at this, since they are one matter, and in the Gemara Rava said only folds. Darkei Moshe answered that it is as the Mordechai wrote in the name of the Raem: folds means beneath her arms and beneath her leg, and hidden areas means inward places, such as the openings of the nose and ears and inside the lip; end quote. This language of the Tur appears in the Gemara in Niddah 66: they challenged from the baraita, "between the folds and between the hidden areas," etc. It is surprising that the Beit Yosef saw Rava's words but did not see the challenge that follows them.
And not with other liquids. For when their moisture remains inside the fold, a stain of the liquid will remain there.
And likewise the woman must inspect, etc. The Tur wrote on the words of the Ramban that it appears he is more stringent regarding inspection of the body, etc. In truth his words are astonishing, for he wrote that inspection of the head is from Ezra's enactment, and this is not so; certainly the main inspection is biblical regarding the head, as the Beit Yosef wrote. The Tur's words require correction. It appears as follows: "inspection of the head" means washing the head, which for us takes the place of biblical inspection. The Tur learned from the Ramban, who mentioned proximity to immersion regarding inspection of the body but did not mention this regarding washing the head, that regarding washing they were not particular that it be immediately adjacent to immersion. So too the Beit Yosef wrote in the name of the Ran: because washing is only rabbinic, they are lenient with it, as we say below that a woman washes on erev Shabbat and immerses on Motzaei Shabbat, etc.; end quote. The Tur did not intend this leniency, for this does not need to be learned from the Ramban; it is an explicit Gemara. Nevertheless, he teaches here that even where she distances washing from immersion, in any case the inspection must not be distanced at all, as the Beit Yosef concludes afterward in this siman. And she should wash her whole body. From his language it implies that first inspection and checking of the whole body are required, and afterward she should wash her whole body. This is astonishing: why should washing the whole body alone not suffice, since then she is also checking? There is no greater checking than this. Although she does not see the place she washes, this suffices; otherwise, how could she do her head and places she cannot see? They did not require another woman to see except at immersion, not at washing and inspection. It is obvious that by feeling with the hands there is proper biblical inspection, all the more so according to Ezra's enactment of washing and rinsing. It appears that the latter clause explains the first clause: what he first wrote, that she must inspect herself, etc., is the biblical law; then he concludes that she should be stringent and in place of inspection perform washing of the whole body. It is obvious that if she inspects and checks herself at the time of washing, that suffices, and a separate checking is not specifically required.
