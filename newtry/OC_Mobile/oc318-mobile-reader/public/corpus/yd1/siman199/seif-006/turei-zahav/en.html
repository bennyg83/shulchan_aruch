Likewise, if two days, etc. The Tur writes, “or if Yom Tov occurs on Motzei Shabbos.” The Beit Yosef wondered that this follows all the more so from the first law, where two days of Yom Tov occur on Thursday and Friday. Some answer that in the latter clause the Tur is speaking of two days of Yom Tov occurring on Sunday and Monday, but this is still difficult, for that is identical to the first clause. The Derishah answered that were it not for the latter clause, I might have said that only in the first case, where Yom Tov occurs on Thursday and Friday and the immersion is on Friday night, may she immerse, because on Yom Tov she can heat a small vessel of water to rinse her folds, as the Tur writes nearby. This is not so when her immersion is on Motzei Shabbos, since on Shabbos she may not rinse them and on Motzei Shabbos she is anxious to return home. Therefore, the Tur teaches that even so it is permitted. This also explains why in the first clause the Tur wrote that she washes and combs on Wednesday and did not mention bathing, because she can bathe on Yom Tov. In the latter clause, however, he wrote that she bathes and washes and combs on erev Shabbos, end quote. According to his words, a woman who immerses on Friday night when Yom Tov occurs on Friday must rinse her folds and concealed areas on Yom Tov before Shabbos. His words are correct.
She must also take care, etc. This is from the words of the Tur. His intent is that although she must in any event inspect and examine her body and head before immersion, as the Shulchan Aruch concludes here, she should nevertheless avoid matters that create an intervening substance during the intervening days. This is because at the time of immersion she will not wash and comb again, but will rely on the washing and combing performed on Wednesday. This resolves everything that the Beit Yosef found difficult in the Tur’s words here. The Tur writes: “If she immersed without examining herself beforehand, then if she was careful not to engage in anything that could create an intervening substance, her immersion is valid; if not, her immersion is invalid,” end quote. His reasoning is that since she already washed and combed and examined her entire body and head on Wednesday, and afterward exercised care during the intervening days, the immersion is valid. My father-in-law, of blessed memory, wrote that the Tur’s statement, “if she did not examine,” does not refer to examination of the body, for without that the immersion would certainly be invalid. Rather, it refers to an additional examination, such as between her fingers, etc. His words are difficult, for it is an established halacha that after the fact an earlier examination is effective, as the Tur wrote in the name of the Rashba and as the Shulchan Aruch rules here in seif 8.
