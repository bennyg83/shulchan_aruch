She should be careful. This refers to every case where the washing is separated from immersion by a day or two. Shach.
She should inspect. The Shach wrote that according to all views she must inspect and check at the time of washing and at the time of immersion, and be careful between washing and immersion not to touch interposing things that are prone to adhere, if possible, as the Rav wrote; and so is the custom.
And she should rinse. But not the entire body. The same applies on Shabbat: she may wash in water heated before Shabbat her face, hands, and feet, or other limbs, as long as she does not wash her entire body, as written in Orach Chaim at the beginning of siman 326. In Responsa Mishnat Binyamin, siman 5, he wrote: it seems to me that women did not customarily wash or wear white garments on Shabbat and Yom Tov. Perhaps this is because a woman does not know how to distinguish between hot water heated before Shabbat or before Yom Tov and water heated on Shabbat or Yom Tov, and she also does not know how to be careful regarding the law of squeezing; end quote. The reason for their custom not to wear white garments on Shabbat is that then they would need to immerse on Motzaei Shabbat and would distance immersion from washing. The proof is that on Yom Tov they do wear white garments, and it is on Shabbat that they customarily do not wear them. Regarding wearing white garments on Tishah BeAv, see Orach Chaim siman 551 seif 3 in the gloss; and regarding mourning, see below siman 381 seif 5 in the gloss; end quote of the Shach.
