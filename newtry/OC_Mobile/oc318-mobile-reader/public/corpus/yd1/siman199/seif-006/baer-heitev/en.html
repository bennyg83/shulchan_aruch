She should be careful. This refers to every case where the chafifah is distant from the immersion by a day or two. Shach.
She should inspect. The Shach wrote that according to all views she must inspect and check at the time of chafifah and at the time of immersion, and if possible she should be careful between the chafifah and immersion not to touch items that interpose and are likely to adhere, as the Rav wrote; so is the custom.
And she should rinse. But not the whole body. The same applies on Shabbat: she may wash with water heated before Shabbat her face, hands, and feet, or other limbs, as long as she does not wash her whole body, as stated in Orach Chayim at the beginning of siman 326. Responsa Mas'et Binyamin, siman 5, wrote: it seems to me that women did not have the custom to wash or to put on white garments on Shabbat and Yom Tov, perhaps because a woman does not know how to distinguish between hot water heated before Shabbat or Yom Tov and water heated on Shabbat or Yom Tov, and also does not know to be careful about squeezing. End quote. The reason for their custom not to put on white garments on Shabbat is that then they would need to immerse on Saturday night, which would distance the immersion from the chafifah. The proof is that on Yom Tov they do put on white garments; it is specifically on Shabbat that they had the custom not to. Regarding wearing white garments on Tishah Be'Av, see Orach Chayim 551:3 in the gloss; regarding mourning, see below siman 381:5 in the gloss. End quote from Shach.
