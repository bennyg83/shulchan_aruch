(Siman 288, Taz, seif katan 1) This is surprising to me, etc. There is no difficulty. Regarding the writing, the sections must appear in the order in which they are written in the Torah. His assertion, 'This is likewise evident from Rambam,' proves nothing, for Rambam did not write in chapter 1 as he quoted him. Rather, he wrote: 'In a mezuzah and tefillin one may not suspend even a single letter; if even one letter was omitted, they must be placed in genizah.' Therefore, in chapter 5 he had to write separately that if one placed a later passage before an earlier one it is invalid, because the reason in chapter 1 is the prohibition against suspending letters. In any event, it proves nothing, for even though the later ruling follows all the more readily, Rambam nevertheless wrote it explicitly, as is often found among the authorities. His statement, 'In siman 290 I proved, etc.,' will be addressed in siman 290, where it will be shown that it proves nothing. See there.
