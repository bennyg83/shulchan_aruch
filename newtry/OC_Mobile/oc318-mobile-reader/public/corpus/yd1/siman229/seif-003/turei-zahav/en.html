Until he is asked about it, etc. In Tur siman 238 at its end he wrote in this language: oath that I will not eat this loaf, oath that I will not eat this loaf — the second does not take effect; and if he is asked about the first, the second takes effect — end quote; from that he wrote the second does not take effect — it implies he has no power to release the second before release of the first, since it has not yet taken effect; and similar to what is written siman 228 seif 17 "they do not release the vow until it takes effect," etc.; and so it appears from the words of the Rosh in the second chapter of Nedarim page 18: evidently as long as he was not asked about the first, the second does not exist, etc. — end quote; and since it does not exist, how can he release it; except from the Rambam's words at end of chapter 6 of Laws of Oaths it does not appear so, for he wrote: oath that I will not eat today, oath that I will not eat today, oath that I will not eat today — and he is asked about the first, he is liable because of the second; and likewise asked about the second — liable because of the third; asked about the third alone — liable because of first and second; asked about the second — liable because of the first; if so why did they say an oath does not take effect on an oath — that if he was not asked and ate, he is liable only once — end quote; it implies he can release the second or third even before releasing the first; and if so one must say this is not similar to that in siman 228 seif 17 that I mentioned, for there the oath is not in the world at all; unlike here where the second and third oath exist in the world, only they did not find place to take effect yet. This thing Rabbi Levi Chaviv invented in his responsum siman 139 for the Rambam's words; and from this he invented another leniency: namely as we say siman 228 seif 46 they release several vows for one person even with one release — the same law for one who swore on one matter many times, one release suffices for all; not like Shulchan Aruch said there that for this each needs release, from the reason the second does not take effect before release of the first as written there. And in my humble opinion it appears Shulchan Aruch ruled well there: first, for according to the Rosh and Tur that we mentioned, it certainly does not take effect at all — they certainly dispute the Rambam on this and he cannot release it before releasing the first; and this is further proven from their words, for they did not distinguish between "this oath did not take effect" and that in siman 228 that we mentioned — they should have informed us this distinction, for it is a great novelty. Rather certainly they do not hold at all like the Rambam on this; and furthermore even for the Rambam who is lenient that release of the second helps before the first — nevertheless it does not appear he is lenient with the other leniency of Rabbi Levi Chaviv that he can release them at once; and there is correct reason: for that he can release it before the first — this is that in his release there is nothing contradicting this release, for he releases only one oath; unlike when he releases at once also the first — behold when he said permitted on the first, with this he establishes the second that from now it shall take effect — and how can we continue the sage's speech saying also permitted on the second, and they contradict each other, for when this falls that one stands; end quote, one release cannot fall for both; and this is something reason requires; and whoever wants to be obstinate must bring clear proof, and from these words of the Rambam there is no proof for them. So it appears correct in my humble opinion; and even the first leniency — to release the second before the first — it appears one should not rely to be lenient on a Torah matter, since the Rosh and Tur imply they do not hold so; and as Shulchan Aruch wrote siman 228; and proof further from responsum of the Rosh end of klal 12: one who said at time of his vow that no sage can release this vow — this too is a vow, and it is like vow within vow; first they release that vow that he vowed he will not release, etc. — end quote; behold he compared one who vowed he will not be asked — meaning what is mentioned in this siman seif 9 — to what is mentioned siman 228 seif 46 for vow within vow; namely he vowed on one matter twice, as mentioned chapter 2 of Nedarim page 17; and he wrote on this that first they must release that it takes effect now; and Maharshal wrote based on Rabbi Levi Chaviv's words above that an error escaped before Beit Yosef — and the matters are clear as written; and in responsum of Rabbi Levi Chaviv there he also wrote he saw one who ruled not like his words, only disputed him.
