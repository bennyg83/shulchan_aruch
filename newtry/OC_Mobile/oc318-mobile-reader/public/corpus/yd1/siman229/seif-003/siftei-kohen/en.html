They are all prohibited, etc. This applies even when, several days later, he says concerning another person, “He shall be like so-and-so.” The same applies if one vowed against meat and then extended the vow to bread by association with the meat. But when his fellow says, “And I,” each person must speak within the time required for a brief greeting after the preceding person. Then even one hundred people are all prohibited, provided each one spoke within that interval after the preceding person, although more than that interval elapsed after the first person, because each person associates his vow with the person immediately before him, as explained above, siman 204.
Likewise, one who vowed, etc. Bedek HaBayit writes in the name of Rabbeinu Yerucham: If a woman vowed and another person associated himself with her vow by saying, “And I am like you,” and her husband or father then nullified her vow, she is permitted but the person who associated himself remains prohibited. I do not know why he cited this in the name of Rabbeinu Yerucham, for it is an explicit Gemara and is agreed upon by all the poskim, as explained below, siman 234, seif 51. See there.
Although he sought annulment for it, etc. Meaning, his intention was only to seek annulment of the first vow. But if he sought annulment of all of them at one time, a single annulment is sufficient, as explained above, siman 228, seif 46.
He remains prohibited until he seeks annulment, etc. Although an oath does not take effect upon an existing oath, as explained below, siman 239, seif 13, nevertheless, once the first oath is released, the second takes effect retroactively.
