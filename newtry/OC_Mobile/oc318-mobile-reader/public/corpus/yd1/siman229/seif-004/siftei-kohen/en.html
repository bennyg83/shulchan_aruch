Specifically in a case such as this, etc. Meaning, specifically where he first made a vow and afterward declared concerning that vow that if he sought annulment for it he would not be a Jew, the vow may not be annulled, because one does not annul a vow containing an element of transgression. But if he said, “If I do such-and-such, I will not be a Jew,” or even explicitly said, “If I do such-and-such, I will deny Hashem,” this is neither an oath, nor a substitute expression for an oath, nor an incomplete formulation of one, since he uttered no wording that even alludes to an oath or vow. This is explicit in the rulings of Terumat HaDeshen, responsum 152, and in Darkei Moshe. This is unlike Ateret Zahav, who explained that when one says he will not be a Jew, the wording means that if he seeks annulment he will not act as a Jew, and therefore one may not annul his vow, since he thereby makes himself into a denier. But, according to Ateret Zahav, if he says, “If I do such-and-such, I will not be a Jew,” he does not even require annulment, because that wording would mean that if he performs the act he will cease inherently to be a Jew, and how could he alter a known reality when everyone sees that he is a Jew? This explanation is incorrect.
