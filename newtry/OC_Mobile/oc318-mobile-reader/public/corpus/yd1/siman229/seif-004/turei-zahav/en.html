Regarding what he said without release. For this matter was like an oath by itself — so Beit Yosef in name of Rashba and the Rosh; and it is difficult — behold he ruled in seif 1 in the first opinion it was not another vow until he says konam a second time, and this is like Rabbi Shimon in the Gemara that we mentioned there; and if so here too one release should suffice, since saying "without release" is one oath with the beginning of the oath; and so Beit Yosef siman 228 page 273 side 1 in name of responsum of the Ran: that which requires special release for one who swore on one matter and said he will not be asked about the oath — this is for Rabbi Yehudah; but for Rabbi Shimon he must say again "oath that I will not be asked," etc.; and one must say the first opinion in seif 1 holds that specifically regarding "part released all released" he holds like Rabbi Shimon that all who did not say oath again — it was one general matter; but here where he said "without release" — it was as if he swore twice on one matter, for he doubled language on one matter; end quote, two releases are needed — so in my humble opinion.
They release first the last cherem, etc. It appears in the last cherem itself two releases are needed: first on what he said "and no release for release," and afterward on what he said they shall not make release, and afterward on the essence of the first cherem; and proof for this from what Beit Yosef wrote in name of the Ran siman 228, and he brings it here in Shulchan Aruch in seif 5 regarding one who said at time of his vow that if the vow is released then prohibition of eating fruit shall apply to him, and so every time and forever; and the Ran wrote there that every amount he mentioned once was an oath by itself; and he wrote there: and we released him from the prohibitions many times as we estimated he said so, and so every time, etc.; from now there is no concern even if he later confesses he said a thousand times so, since when he was asked he did not think fruit prohibition would continue from this — there is no concern — end quote; and it is further clear that although he said "and so forever" — it is without end — nevertheless it is counted only once.
