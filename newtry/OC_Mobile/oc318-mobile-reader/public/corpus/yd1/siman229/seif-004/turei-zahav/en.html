Regarding what he said, “without release.” This statement is considered a separate oath. So wrote the Beit Yosef in the name of the Rashba and the Rosh. This is difficult. In seif 1, according to the first opinion, the Shulchan Aruch ruled that there is no separate vow until he repeats the word “konam.” That follows R’ Shimon in the Gemara, as mentioned there. If so, here too one release should suffice, since the phrase “without release” is part of the original oath. The Beit Yosef likewise wrote at the end of siman 228, page 273a, in the name of a responsum of the Ran, that requiring a separate release when one swore regarding one matter and said that he would not seek release applies according to R’ Yehuda. According to R’ Shimon, he must say a second time, “I swear that I will not seek release,” etc. We must say that the first opinion in seif 1 follows R’ Shimon only regarding the rule that when part of a vow is released, the entire vow is released. So long as he did not repeat the word “oath,” it remains one general statement. Here, however, when he says, “without release,” it is as though he swore twice regarding the same matter, for he doubled the language concerning one matter. Two releases are therefore required. So it appears to me.
They first release the later cherem, etc. It appears that the later cherem itself requires two releases. First, they release what he said, “and no release shall be made for the release.” Afterward, they release what he said, that they should not make a release. Then they release the original cherem itself. Proof may be brought from what the Beit Yosef wrote in the name of the Ran in siman 228, which the Shulchan Aruch cites here in seif 5, concerning one who stated at the time of his vow that if the vow were released, a prohibition against eating fruit would take effect upon him, and the same would recur every time and forever. The Ran wrote that every number of repetitions he mentioned constitutes a separate oath. He wrote: “We released him from the prohibitions many times, according to our estimate of how many times he had said, ‘and so each time,’ etc. From then onward there was no concern even if he later admitted that he had said it one thousand times, since at the time he sought release he did not think that the fruit prohibition would continue as a result,” end quote. It is further clear that although he said, “and so forever,” which has no limit, it is nevertheless counted as only one additional repetition.
Regarding a non-Jew, it is forbidden to seek release, etc. The Mordechai concludes there, “lest he deny the matter.” The Terumas HaDeshen wrote in his rulings, siman 192, that in truth there is neither an oath nor excommunication here. Nevertheless, regarding release of a vow, the Sages were concerned wherever it could lead to a stumbling block, as in the rule that one does not release a gambling vow. Therefore, the Terumas HaDeshen ruled there that if he said, “If I do such-and-such,” etc., as copied here in the latter clause, it is not a vow at all. That case does not concern seeking release.
