He swore, etc.; and likewise, etc.; and likewise, etc. See above, siman 228, seif 46. The Ran writes that regarding the phrase, 'forever,' one release suffices, since it is a single utterance. Regarding the phrase, 'release shall not serve as a release,' however, he requires release for each and every prohibition, as explained there above. A proof that release is effective for all of these formulations is that in chapter 4 of Gittin (36a) and chapter 3 of Makkot, the Gemara identifies only an oath taken with the consent of the public as one that cannot be released. This also proves that even an oath extending forever may be released at one time. Otherwise, there would be no limit to the number of releases required and it could never be released, and the Gemara should have identified such a case. This is the meaning of the statement in seif 5, 'He prohibited,' etc.
