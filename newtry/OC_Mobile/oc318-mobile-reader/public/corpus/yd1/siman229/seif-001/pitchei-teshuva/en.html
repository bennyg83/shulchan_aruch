That part of it was released. Maharshal chidushei dinim siman 93 wrote regarding a document with many conditions and at the end written "and he swore on everything" — if they asked about one before a court of three and released him, everything was released — it is a vow whose part was released since shevuah is written at the end on everything — see there; and likewise responsum Maharshal part 1 siman 104. See Shaar HaMelekh chapter 12 of Laws of Nedarim halachah 10 regarding one who had two animals before him and vowed "these are olah" and one was found blemished or tame (and likewise if he forbade them with konam and one was tame or treifah — per first opinion above siman 225 note 45; however one may say here it is a comprehensive prohibition — examine) — do we say since the vow did not take effect on that animal it also did not take effect on the other, i.e. vow whose part was released is entirely released, or perhaps we only say vow whose part was released when the vow took effect initially and afterward part was released through an opening, but if from the outset there is no place for partial application, in such a case perhaps we call it "whatever comes from his mouth he shall do," and he brought that Ritva's view is clear that it did not take effect at all; however from Ran in Nedarim 86 it appears he disagrees with Ritva and expanded there — see Nekudot HaKesef what he wrote.
