A vow, etc. This accords with Rabbi Akiva and Beit Hillel. See Ran, Nedarim 26b, s.v. leRava, and regarding the practical halachah, etc.
How, etc. This is the Rosh's explanation of the Mishnah there, s.v. potchin, etc.
Or, etc. See Ran there, s.v. man tana, etc., and s.v. ule'inyan, etc. The Rosh there writes likewise, and so does the Tur: even if he said, 'to you and to you,' it constitutes a collective formulation. The Beit Yosef writes that this wording is imprecise, since even without conjunctions it is collective; therefore, the Shulchan Aruch wrote it without conjunctions. There is no difficulty, however, because the Tur follows Rashi's version of Rabbi Yochanan's statement in Shevuot 38a, 'All agree regarding: not to you,' etc., with a conjunction. This is also the version of the Rosh in his commentary to Nedarim there, s.v. lezeh korban, etc., 'but the Sages,' etc. The Beit Yosef follows his own position, for he writes in Kesef Mishneh, as stated below, that the primary version is that of Tosafot in Shevuot there, s.v. hacha, etc., 'and the passage,' etc., and 'in the text,' etc. He therefore wrote it without conjunctions, to teach a greater novelty. Tosafot in Zevachim 30b, s.v. kezayit, etc., likewise write this.
And according to the Rambam, etc. For his version in Nedarim 26b reads: 'for example, where he made them dependent,' etc., 'so-and-so, and so-and-so, and so-and-so.' This is as the Rosh writes in chapter 9, seif 5. Although the Rosh himself explains the passage according to our version, as he writes in his commentary to 26b, s.v. vesifa, etc., the Rambam explains it according to its plain meaning and rules like Rabbi Yehudah according to Rabbi Yochanan, following the version of Tosafot cited above. So it appears. Yet in chapter 7 of Hilchot Shevuot he rules like Rabbi Meir, that both formulations are particularized, and like Rabbi in Zevachim, whether one said 'an olive's volume, an olive's volume' or 'an olive's volume and an olive's volume.' In Hilchot Ishut he rules specifically like Rabbi Shimon, where one said, 'Be betrothed, be betrothed.' His reasoning is as stated in chapter 6 of Chullin (85a): 'Rabbi saw the words of Rabbi Shimon,' etc. Likewise, in Kiddushin the anonymous Mishnah follows Rabbi Shimon, and the Amoraim discuss the matter according to his view, as the Ran and Rosh write. Here, although the anonymous Mishnah likewise follows Rabbi Shimon, in the first clause he rules like Rabbi Yehudah according to the version cited above. This requires study. In Shevuot, however, where the anonymous Mishnah follows Rabbi Meir, he rules like Rabbi Meir.
And some say, etc.; and some say, etc. See Ran, Nedarim 27a: 'The Ramban, of blessed memory, further wrote,' etc. The Rosh likewise records the Ramban's words in his rulings there, although in his commentary there at 66a, s.v. konam, he writes like Tosafot. See there, for he also rules like Rabbi Yose and according to Tosafot's version. His words therefore contradict what he wrote in chapter 9 there, as explained above.
