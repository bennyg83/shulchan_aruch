The law of a vow part of which was released, or one who vowed regarding one matter twice; it contains nine se'ifim. A vow part of which was released — the whole of it is released. How so? He vowed to fast or not to eat meat for a known time, and Sabbaths and festivals occurred in it — they say to him, "If you had set your heart that Sabbaths and festivals would occur in it, you would not have vowed," and the whole is released (Rosh and Rambam). And likewise if he said to a group of people, "Konam that I benefit from all of you," or if he said "that I benefit from you, from you, from you" — it is a generalization; and if one of them was released, all are released. But if he said "Konam that I benefit from you, Konam that I benefit from you" — each vowed independently, and if one was released, not all are released. And according to Rambam, if he said "that I benefit from this one and this one and this one" — if the first was released, all are released; if the last was released, the last alone is released and all are forbidden. "That I not benefit from this one, this one, this one" — he needs an opening for each and every one. And there is one who says we do not say "a vow part of which was released — the whole is released," except when released through an opening, but through regret, no. And there is one who says that even if released through regret as well. And these matters are stated only regarding one who vowed for himself; but if he vowed or swore to his fellow to pay him or do work for him, and his fellow released part of it — the rest is not released for him.
