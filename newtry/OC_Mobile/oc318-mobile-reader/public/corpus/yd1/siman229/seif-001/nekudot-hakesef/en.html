(Siman 229 in Taz s.k. 5) And puzzling are the words of Maharshal z"l. This is as he wrote s.k. 4; also what he wrote also in Darkei Moshe ruled as he wrote — it is not so; and he omitted Darkei Moshe's words that are in siman 232, and I brought them in Shach there.
(There, seif katan 10) He means. He means that the Tur and Rosh disagree with the Rambam. In my humble opinion, this is not proven. The Tur merely uses the language of the Gemara in Nedarim, chapter 2, that the second oath does not take effect. As the Rambam explains there, this means only that one is liable for a single violation; their words can be understood the same way. The Rosh’s statement that “the second is not present” likewise refers to that Gemara, meaning that one incurs only one liability. The words of Maharl ibn Habib also appear correct. Since both oaths presently exist, they may be released together. We need not say that when one falls the other rises; on the contrary, he does not permit the second to arise, but immediately releases it. The proof cited from Responsa of the Rosh, rule 12, is not valid, because there the person vowed that no scholar would be able to release him. The Bach correctly challenged the Beit Yosef that nothing can be proven from the Ran. Nevertheless, one should be stringent, as written in the Shach, siman 228, seif katan 110.
