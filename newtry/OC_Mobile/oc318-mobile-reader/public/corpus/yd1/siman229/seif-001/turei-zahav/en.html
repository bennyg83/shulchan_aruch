The entire vow is released. For initially he vowed only with the understanding that the whole vow would remain in effect. The Ran wrote in the name of the Yerushalmi that the verse states, “According to everything that comes from his mouth he shall do,” implying specifically when the entire statement remains in effect.
Not to eat meat. The Tur mentions only fasting. The Beit Yosef explains that the Tur holds that on Yom Tov and Shabbos one can delight himself without meat, whereas according to the Rosh the same law applies even to one who vowed only from meat. This is astonishing. How could one think that a vow from meat would not nullify the mitzvah of delighting in Shabbos? Rather, the Tur certainly also agrees with the Rosh and merely chose one of the two examples. So too wrote my revered father-in-law, of blessed memory.
That I will not benefit from you, from you, etc. In the Tur it reads, “and from you,” with a vav. The Beit Yosef wrote that this is not precise, for we rule like R’ Shimon, who says that the vow is not divided unless he states an expression of a vow for each individual. The Rambam, however, rules like the Sages, who hold that the matter depends upon the conjunctions. Thus, “from this one and from this one” is one general vow, whereas “from this one, from this one,” without conjunctions, constitutes a separate vow for each. In seif 4 it appears that we rule like the Rambam, since it rules there that what he said, “without release,” constitutes another vow even though he did not repeat an expression of a vow regarding each matter. See what I write there.
When it was released through an opening. Release through an opening somewhat resembles a vow made in error. Since there was some error in the vow, the entire vow is released. This differs from release through regret, where there was no initial error and only his present opinion changed. The latter opinion holds that even regret is effective for this purpose, since the sage uproots the vow retroactively.
And his fellow released part of it for him. This means that the fellow says, “I regard myself as though I have received part of the payment,” which is equivalent to receiving partial payment. Nevertheless, the remainder is not released. Regarding an obligation owed to another person, we do not say that when part is released the entire obligation is released, because he already became obligated to pay the entire amount. The creditor does not forfeit the remainder merely because he waived part. See what I wrote in siman 228, seif 49, from which it is implied that one who swore to pay his fellow does not fulfill his oath until he pays everything. In siman 232, seif 8, it will be explained that we say, “When part is released, the entire vow is released,” only when the release changes the substance of his original statement. The same applies in every place where we use this principle. So wrote the Beit Yosef there in the name of the Ramban and the Ran. One should therefore not be lenient against them, especially since no authority explicitly disagrees. The words of my revered father-in-law, of blessed memory, who ruled leniently here, are astonishing. The Darkei Moshe also ruled as I have written.
