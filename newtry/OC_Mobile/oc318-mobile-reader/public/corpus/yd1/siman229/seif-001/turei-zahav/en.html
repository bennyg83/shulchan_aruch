I benefit you to you, etc. In the Tur it is written "and to you" with vav; and Beit Yosef wrote it is not precise, for we hold like Rabbeinu Shimon who said until he says vow language for each one; but the Rambam ruled like the Sages that the matter depends on vav, meaning "to this and to this"; but "to this to this" — each is a vow by itself. And in seif 4 it implies we hold like the Rambam, since he ruled there regarding what he said "without release" — it was another vow, even though he did not say again vow language, every matter; and see what is written there.
When released through an opening. Because through an opening it is somewhat similar to a vow in error; and since there is some error in the vow it is released; unlike through regret where there is no error from the outset, only his mind changed now. And the latter opinion holds even through regret it helps, for the sage uproots the vow from the outset.
And his fellow released part for him. Meaning: he says "behold I am as if I received partial payment" — it is as if they paid him part; nevertheless the rest was not released, for regarding another person we do not say "all released" since he already became obligated to pay him everything; only he did not lose in what he waived partial payment for him. See what is written in siman 228 seif 49, that it implies from this regarding one who swore to pay his fellow — he does not fulfill his oath until he pays everything. And in siman 232 seif 8 it will be explained we do not say "all released" when part was released unless he changed his words from what he said initially; and the same law here everywhere we say "all released"; and so Beit Yosef there in name of Ramban and Ran — end quote, one should not be lenient at all against them, especially since none explicitly disputes them. And puzzling are Maharshal's words who ruled to be lenient here, also in Darkei Moshe ruled as he wrote.
