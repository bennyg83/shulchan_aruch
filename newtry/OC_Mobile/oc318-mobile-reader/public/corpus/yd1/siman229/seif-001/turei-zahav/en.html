Not eating meat. It is not written, but rather to give thanks to Hashem’s glory, and to Hashem’s mercy, but to the Holy Spirit, the Holy Spirit, and He will not be able to be saved from Hashem’s mercy, and he will not be able to do so with him, and he will be able to give him a blessing
That I may not benefit from you, from you, etc. The Tur writes "from you" with a conjunctive vav [indicating "and"], and Beit Yosef writes that this is not required — for we hold like R' Shimon, who says [the vow links them only] when he uses the language of a vow regarding each; but Rambam rules like the Rabbis, that the matter depends on the conjunctive vav — meaning "to this one and to this one," whereas "to this one, to this one" [without a vav] makes each a separate vow; and seif 4 implies that we rule like Rambam, since he ruled there that if he ate without a release it constitutes a new vow — even though he did not again use the language of a vow. See what I wrote there. When it is released through a "opening" [petach]. Because through a petach it somewhat resembles a vow made in error; and since there is an element of error in the vow, it is released — which is not the case with regret [charatah], where there was no error at the outset but rather his mind has now changed. And the latter opinion [yi"a batra] holds that even through regret it takes effect, since the sage uproots the vow from its foundation. And his fellow released part of it for him. Meaning he says: "I hereby consider myself as if I received partial payment" — so it is as if he paid him that portion; nevertheless the remainder is not released, for regarding another person we do not say "if part is released, all is released" — since the other person was already owed the full amount and loses nothing by waiving part of it. And see what I wrote in siman 228 seif 49 — it is implied there that one who swore to pay his fellow has not fulfilled his oath until he pays in full. And in siman 232 seif 8 it will be explained that we do not say "all is released when part is released" unless one changes from what he first said — and the same applies in every case here where we say "all is released." Beit Yosef wrote there in the name of Ramban and Ran — therefore one should not be lenient against them at all, especially since no one explicitly disagrees with them. And the words of my father-in-law of blessed memory who ruled leniently here — and also ruled so in Darkei Moshe — are puzzling.
