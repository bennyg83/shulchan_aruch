And for the Rambam. {Rama: for it is like saying this second shall be like this first and this third like this second, and we hold like the Rambam} And Beit Yosef in name of Rivash: one vowed or swore not to eat grapes and not to wear silk garments, and was asked about one of them; the second was not permitted; to the Rambam; and Shach wrote: and it appears if he said konam grapes upon me, konam figs upon me; even for those who dispute the Rambam it is two vows since he said konam konam.
An opening. This resembles a mistaken vow, and when part of the vow is found mistaken the entire vow is annulled. Taz writes that the rule that annulment of part releases the whole applies only where the petitioner changes his original account, as explained in siman 232. Bach is lenient here, and Shach agrees because this is not an original mistake but a release through formal annulment, so the whole vow is released without changing his words.
