His condition is ineffective. Meaning, one might have thought that since he stipulated this condition, no request for release could ever help him. We do not say so, for it is proven in the Gemara that there is no vow in the world that cannot be released through a request. So wrote the Beit Yosef in the name of the Rivash. From the wording in the latter clause, “for otherwise they first release,” it appears that when he did not say that he would not seek release, but merely said that the vow should never be released, he requires only one release, namely, release of the underlying oath. He does not require a separate release for having said that it should never be released. I am puzzled by the distinction between this case and the phrase, “without release,” in seif 4. It appears to me that the Rivash, whose opinion is cited in this seif, also holds that saying, “without release,” requires only one release, either because he did not repeat an expression of a vow, following R’ Shimon as I explained in seif 4 from the Ran, or because whenever he does not place a separate obligation upon himself not to seek release, it is not considered a second oath. Alternatively, the phrase “without release” may be considered as though he mentioned himself and stated that he would not be the one to seek release, unlike the phrase that it should never be released. As practical halacha, however, it appears that even here two releases should be performed, one for what he said, that it should never be released, and one for the underlying oath. There is no difference between this and saying, “without release,” in seif 4. So it appears to me.
