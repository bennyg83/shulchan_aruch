Or he extends his hand outside the ship, etc. And the Bach ruled that at the head of the ship—even slaughtering directly into the sea or in a vessel—it is permitted, for it is impossible to soil the ship's sides outside; but standing inside the ship—even though he soils the ship's sides, they are washed by their movement in the water; and he wrote that such is Rashi's view, and he challenged Tosafot and Beit Yosef who wrote that even at the head of the ship it is permitted only when slaughtering on the ship's sides and from there the blood pours and goes into the water—and I did not reach the end of his view. And so too the Ran in the first chapter of Chullin explicitly like Tosafot; and also Beit Yosef concludes that Rashi too holds like Tosafot, and so it appears to me—that is the Rambam's view in chapter 2 and Semag and Rabeinu Yerucham (and the Mechaber)—who did not mention the law of the head of the ship at all, for in the Talmud they only wrote in general that on a ship one slaughters on its sides—therefore they hold there is no distinction; and so is proved from the Rashba and Tur there; and see in the siman afterward how they practice nowadays.

On top of vessels and the blood pours, etc. So too Rashi, the Ran, and R' Yerucham (and they were not brought in Beit Yosef); and he explains that one takes a long vessel and places part of it on the ship and part outside the ship on a slope toward the outside, and slaughters on the vessel and the blood pours and goes down into the water—and so wrote the Perishah. But the Rashba and Tur wrote: he may slaughter on top of vessels provided he does not slaughter into them; or he extends his hand outside the ship—and they did not mention the blood pouring into the water regarding vessels. It must be that they mean it is permitted to slaughter on the back of a vessel that has an interior, for it does not appear as one collecting blood for avodah zarah since he is not slaughtering into it—and so is in Semag daf 141: permitted to slaughter on the back of a vessel, etc.—not like the Perishah who explained that he slaughters on the rim of the vessel and even though the blood goes into it, since he does not slaughter directly into it, it is permitted; and so it appears from the words of the Magen Avraham—rather as I explained.
