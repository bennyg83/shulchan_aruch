On top of vessels—the main thing according to Rashi's explanation that he slaughters on a sloped vessel so blood goes into the sea; and the second explanation as Kereti wrote is strained; and in any case as Kereti wrote on a ship it is permitted but not generally—for otherwise it is still difficult on Yom Tov to collect blood in a vessel and receive through the back; rather we learn that except on a ship—which in any case it is recognizable he slaughters for the ship's carpenter—it is forbidden.
Some slaughterers are careful, God forbid, to be stringent on this as I have seen several slaughterers do—and it is in the category of ways of the Emorites, since the matter is not from natural straw and intellect; and see the Ran in tractate Shabbat what he wrote on this—and it suffices not to protest against those who practice thus; but God forbid to be stringent on this—and let it be a wonder, etc.; and there is no place to extend, for then in the days of R' Yehuda HaChasid the time caused it, for then sorcerers multiplied with geese. And R' Eliezer of Germiza explained in his writings that many then refrained from eating any goose. But now there is no concern at all.
