Into vessels—see Kereti what he wrote in the name of Beit HaLevi and Peri Chadash; and in truth the language "one may not slaughter into a vessel" implies like Peri Chadash—for otherwise it should have said "one may not collect blood in a vessel," and it would sound like two matters; rather he was not particular only about slaughter, for this was common among nations in those days that they slaughtered into vessels to cast for their worship. But this was not common—that they collected after slaughter when the main leniency of slaughter already spilled to the ground, and afterward blood was not desirable for their worship and it was not common among them to collect it; and so was the inner service—to receive immediately from slaughtered blood, as explained in Gemara chapter 2 of Zevachim and Rambam laws of avodah zarah. Therefore the Sages did not decree. In truth one can be precise in what the Rosh wrote at the beginning of covering blood of a koy: one does not slaughter on Yom Tov, etc., and l'chatchila he should not slaughter and collect blood in a vessel and cover at night, for forbidden to slaughter in a vessel. And see below siman 28 and in Shach s.k. 10 (there)—and it is difficult: he should collect blood after he slaughtered from leniency and not slaughter into the vessel and cover, for not all blood is needed to cover—see siman 28 seif 15 (there). And he fulfills covering. And perhaps one can say: if so, he would be obligated not to slaughter until a vessel is ready to receive blood after slaughter—if so, since with this intent he slaughters, it is as slaughtering into the vessel, for the slaughter was for that and forbidden; unlike generally this is not a matter of slaughter at all, and because he will collect blood afterward the matter of slaughter is not worsened—this the Sages did not enact. And the main permission is that in our time all the above is not relevant, and they are lenient as written at the end of siman 12; but nevertheless one should be stringent initially to leave it for a gentile to receive.
