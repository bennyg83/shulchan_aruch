By day in a dark place. In the Gemara this is not found except at night; rather the Rashba innovated this law and compared it to night. It is difficult—for Rashi explained the reason at night l'chatchila not to slaughter: we are concerned lest he not slaughter the majority of the simanim and he not know. It appears his intent is: certainly if we see by day that he slaughtered the majority, it is certainly valid; and nevertheless the prohibition is only l'chatchila lest he forget during the time of night that he must inspect—and even if he slaughtered at the end of night the Rabbis did not distinguish. And Mahari Chai explained the reason: perhaps through convulsion the cut widened after slaughter and perhaps the majority was not slaughtered—and it is difficult, for if so b'dieved it should also be forbidden; and further, in siman 25 it is explained we are not concerned for this except as I explained is primary. If so, why is it forbidden by day in a dark place since he can immediately go to a place of light and see if he slaughtered the majority? One can say the Rashba follows his reason that he explains in Tosafot HaRosh: l'chatchila forbidden lest he make chaladah (hiding the knife); if so, the same applies by day in a dark place. It must be said according to him that nevertheless one can notice at night if he makes chaladah, and nevertheless it is valid b'dieved. And one can challenge the Rashba: let him slaughter l'chatchila and see if the feathers were cut—then certainly he did not make chaladah, like that of R' Yona who checked the arrow and slaughtered even l'chatchila; and according to what is written in Tosafot HaRosh, the short one still has concern lest he do derisah—end of his words.
