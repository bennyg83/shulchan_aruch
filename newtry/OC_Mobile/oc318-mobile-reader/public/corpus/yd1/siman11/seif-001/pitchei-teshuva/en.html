He should not slaughter. Magen Avraham and Shach—the reason. And see responsum Zichron Yitzchak siman 99 who wrote it appears to him another reason here: since l'chatchila one must bless the blessing of slaughter before slaughter, therefore he should not slaughter l'chatchila at night lest he not slaughter the majority and come to a blessing in vain (there). According to this, if he slaughtered a matter where a favorable outcome is born, as Rama wrote below siman 19 seif 1 in hagahah that he does not bless before slaughter—he may slaughter l'chatchila at night; and similarly if he slaughtered while still day and at night wants to slaughter without a blessing relying on the first blessing, the above concern does not apply—he is permitted to slaughter l'chatchila at night. But in truth this is not so—for if so, what did they challenge in Gemara Chullin daf 12b: one who slaughters b'dieved yes, l'chatchila no—and they challenged "one may always slaughter," etc.—perhaps there it deals with such a case? Or that nevertheless it is forbidden. And what Beit HaTalmid wrote in the name of Beit Yosef: and if he began, etc.—see in Beit Yosef there who wrote that even if it is clear to him he only slaughtered half the trachea and did not touch the esophagus—then if he stops he will not be invalidated for delay—nevertheless he finishes (there). Indeed in responsum Shevut Yaakov part 2 siman 61 he wrote: and regarding extinguishing the lamp in the middle of slaughter—even though he slaughtered at night without a lamp we hold his slaughter is valid—nevertheless through extinguishing the lamp the slaughterer is startled during slaughter and makes delay and renders it treifah (there).
