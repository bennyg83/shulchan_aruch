One who slaughters on Shabbat—the view of the later authorities, Taz and Shach: even if intentional, for at that time he became a mumar, his slaughter is not invalidated—see Peleti where I extended on this; for the Rambam also agrees to this explanation, only he holds that for any cutting he becomes a mumar to desecrate Shabbat, and at completion of slaughter they invalidate—for he is not fit for slaughter; only the Rambam establishes it in unintentional, and so Rashba—and I extended in Peleti to reconcile his words that they are not difficult from the Gemara; see there. And all this is one who desecrates in public; but in private he is not a mumar. And see Peleti what I wrote regarding intentional with warning, and nevertheless he transgressed and desecrated in public—it is called public. And see Peleti what I wrote that one should be stringent like the Rambam's view and forbid Shabbat slaughter when intentional.
