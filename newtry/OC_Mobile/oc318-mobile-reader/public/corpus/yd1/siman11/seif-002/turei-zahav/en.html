If he were intentional, etc. At first glance it implies that if intentional it is forbidden, for he is a mumar—and so Beit Yosef; if so, it must deal with public desecration as above siman 2. It is difficult—for the Tur should have written a stringency where he wrote unintentional; he should have written even intentional, only not in public, as the Ran wrote. In truth in this it appears the Tur validates even if intentional, for he is not a mumar for one time, as Tosafot in the first chapter of Chullin (daf 14) wrote. And that he only wrote unintentional is because we say in the Gemara that if intentional we penalize him like R' Yochanan the sandalmaker—forbidden forever, since we hold like R' Yochanan regarding cooking on Shabbat; and the Tur wrote simply "valid" implying we do not penalize him—therefore he wrote unintentional. And if you say: even for unintentional it is forbidden to him that day—one can say nevertheless his slaughter can be valid also for him on another day after Shabbat.
