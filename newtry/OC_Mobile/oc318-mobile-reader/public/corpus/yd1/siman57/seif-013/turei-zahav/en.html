Whether to be lenient or stringent. Perishah extended here to explain what is written to be lenient — attributing to what is prevalent — in vain, for the Rashba holds that if there is equal reason to attribute to prohibition or permission, we certainly follow leniency since most animals are kosher; only if permission is not prevalent and prohibition is prevalent do we go stringently. His proof from the incident of the goose in R' Ashi's house, etc., end Perishah. Bedek HaBayit disagrees there and holds even when not prevalent before us we attribute to leniency; this derusah is a different matter and we do not attribute wherever we can attribute to permission; this the Rav learned from Rava's words that he attributed to a dog even though a cat is more common for striking — all the more so here with the windpipe before us, even though a cat is not as common as a dog for rubbing; Bedek HaBayit and the Tur wrote examination is not needed except where blood bubbles; and if perforated even inside — kosher, end of his words. The explanation appears: difference between concern of hole and concern of derusah — for hole concern, examine only the torn place, no more; for derusah concern, examine opposite the entire cavity as below. Later he wrote (for) Ribam who is not concerned for internal organ perforation — even if we see the bird's skin perforated inside to an organ where minute perforation disqualifies, it is kosher since we are not concerned for derusah here when not prevalent; we attribute to prevalent permission. I saw one who wrote and labored in vain to explain differently.
