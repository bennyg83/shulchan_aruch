The Beit Yosef wrote in the name of the Rashba's novellae: there is a difference — for a thorn, even if it perforated into the cavity, it has examination; but for a hawk's perforation, once it perforated into the cavity and the flesh reddened, it has no examination. However, for the view that a thorn also has no examination, one may distinguish the opposite: a thorn has no examination at all, for a minute hole is not recognizable, but for a hawk one examines if it perforated into its cavity and reddened inside — treifah, and if not, kosher, end of his words. Maharshal wrote one must explain that therefore he holds a hawk's claw is not thin and sharp like a thorn or needle — otherwise why is it kosher without reddening of flesh inside; it could be treifah from a thorn that perforated into the cavity, not recognizable with a minute hole, end of his words. In my humble opinion, if so the Rashba need not make any practical difference, since a hawk's claw is not comparable to a thorn; rather the Rashba means: wherever it did not redden, we have proof it did not touch the organs, for if it touched it would certainly redden in that place, for whatever it touches it injects venom and reddens; therefore we have no concern perhaps it made a thorn-hole — wherever derisah of a hawk applies, it is more lenient regarding concern of a minute hole than a thorn, for the view that a thorn has no examination for a hole.
