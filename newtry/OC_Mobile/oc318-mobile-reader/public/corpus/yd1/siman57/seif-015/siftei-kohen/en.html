And even definite derusah, etc. It appears to me that the view of Tosafot, the Rosh, and their supporters, whom the Mechaber followed, is that if we only saw that it trampled, even if the place of trampling is not recognizable from outside, it is called definite derusah — not as Beit Chadash who extended at length in matters that are not seen. And I found support in the novellae of Maharam of Lublin at the end of his words, and it appears that initially Tosafot held this would be for Rashi a safek derusah, for otherwise why explain "and the place of trampling is not recognizable from outside"; but in the end, since they conclude that for this reason he explained so — because they hold that if recognizable from outside, examination is not needed except opposite that place — if so, this would also be for Rashi definite derusah, and with this Rav deals, who examines from the palm to the skull; and likewise explained the great gaon Maharil netivot. The words of Tosafot are not as the Bach who explained strangely "from the palm to the skull"; and likewise it appears in the Tur in what he wrote in the name of Ribam and the Rosh as he explained. And with this there is no difficulty from what the Beit Yosef wrote that the Rosh and Ran hold like Ribam, for the Rosh and Ran wrote that even if we only saw it trampled it is called definite derusah (for according to their view it has no examination); nevertheless, even if the place of trampling is recognizable from outside, it has examination. However, where we are concerned — such as when it is silent and they are trembling, and the like — one may not permit without examination. And wherever we are not concerned — such as when both are silent, and the like — examination is not needed at all. All this is the Mechaber's method and his supporters; examine well, for I shortened since we forbid in every case, as we are not expert, as below seif 18.
