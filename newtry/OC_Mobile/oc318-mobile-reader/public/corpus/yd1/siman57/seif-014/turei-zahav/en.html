They are concerned for it, etc. The Tur wrote they are concerned perhaps the lion trampled it and the claw slipped off and sat on its back, end of his words. This is not like seif 8 that venom is injected only after removing the claw — there the claw remains attached to the hand, but here when the claw slipped off and the hand is removed from there it injects venom. The Beit Yosef wrote it implies specifically when they saw a lion enter; but if they did not see it enter, we attribute that this claw was in the wall and the animal rubbed against the wall and it lodged in its back; and likewise the Rashba — double safek: perhaps it did not enter, and if it entered, perhaps this claw is not from the lion but rubbed on the wall, end of his words. And even though this is not a full double safek — for even on the first side that perhaps no lion entered, you must say this claw came from the wall — nevertheless we rely on the majority, as above, and we say there is good reason it came from the wall since we can say no lion was there at all; similar to what Darkei Moshe wrote.
And not only for this one alone, etc. This applies also when both are silent, as explained in the Beit Yosef.
But for all the cattle in the pen. There is great wonder here on the Beit Yosef's words who wrote: and it applies when a lion entered among cattle and a claw was found sitting on one's back — he adjoined that there in all cases we are concerned for this claw on the back, even where they can flee, etc., end of his words — honor to its place. Here it is difficult on his words, for the Gemara says Rav permits with a claw sitting on the back — we say it lodged in the wall and from there came to its back; Rav according to his reason does not concern for safek derusah. If so, this claw on the back has no more basis for concern than safek derusah — i.e., it is silent and they cry out. If you would say derusah concern is lighter than here — how could Rav say according to his reason, etc., and this is not my reason that there it is lighter than here; rather they are equal, and therefore for us who concern for safek derusah we also concern for a claw on the back that came from the lion, as the Beit Yosef wrote for this reason we do not hold like Rav with a claw. And why not say in safek derusah — it is silent, etc. — permitted in a wide place, as seif 9 writes specifically in a narrow place, and for a claw on the back learned from safek derusah — that one with the claw on its back would be treifah even in a wide place? And why not say also here: since it is in a wide place it certainly fled and the claw came from the wall, since this is learned from safek derusah — how can derusah law be stricter than itself — how can a geir stand on the ground and the convert in the heavens? In truth it appears clear that also the Rashba intended this, and his novelty is that the law of a claw on the back has the stringency of "it is silent and they cry" — i.e., safek derusah — and we do not attribute to the wall; for leniency of Rav from safek derusah reason does not help us, and its law is exactly like safek derusah. And so is explicit in Terumat HaDeshen HaAruch daf 64: he first wrote safek derusah with difference between narrow and wide, then this law of claw on back — since we concern for safek derusah we concern for this too; it is simple they are tied, and the claw law is not stricter. The Beit Yosef wrote proof for his words from the Rashba writing claw law after writing "it is silent and they cry" — I do not recognize any hint of proof; explicitly the Gemara says claw law was safek derusah. If so we have no reason to say it is stricter than other safek derusah; Rif and Rosh mentioned claw law due to safek derusah and gave no extra stringency. The Beit Yosef's words here are very puzzling. The essence: in a wide place, even the one with the claw on its back is permitted — the claw came from elsewhere; whatever we do not concern for due to safek derusah has Rav's law that we do not concern for that claw. And what is written: if three derusah are found, be stringent and forbid all even in a wide place, for the flock was established as derusah — it appears to me this is a gross error of that author who thought this like a dish where three worms were found forbidden as siman 84 seif 9; this is not comparable, for there we say the nature of this vegetable is to grow worms, not so here where each animal has a good presumption alone — what harm is one's prohibition to another?
