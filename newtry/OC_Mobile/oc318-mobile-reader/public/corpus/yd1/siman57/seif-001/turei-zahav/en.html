Derusah. Meaning one who has venom — a blow with its claws injects venom and burns in it.
Even in a large ox. Meaning that it is larger than a lion, for the mishnah states plainly, and a lion in a large animal implies even in an animal larger than it.
A wolf has no derisah in a large animal. This is the view of the Rif, Rambam, Rosh, Rashba, Ran, and Tur — not like Tosafot who are stringent with a wolf even in a large animal. Maharshal siman 70 ruled leniently like the Shulchan Aruch, only being stringent with a wolf that has derisah in small calves, as the Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Yerucham; and he wrote support for this from derisah of a hawk that has no derisah in large birds such as geese and chickens, yet we say it has derisah in small ones even of large species, end of his words. One who relies on the many early and late authorities who are lenient, as the Beit Yosef and Shulchan Aruch conclude, does not lose — a wolf has no derisah at all in a large animal.
Its law is like a thorn that perforated into the cavity. Its law was explained in siman 91.
Even if it is as large as a wolf. This is the Rashba's view in the Tur; his reason is that therefore the mishnah mentions trampling of a wolf — to exclude other impure beasts like a wolf; but above it, its law is like a wolf. And likewise regarding a lion — not like the Rosh who says a bear and leopard have no derisah at all.
And some are more stringent still, etc. This is the Or Zaru'a's view that the Beit Yosef brings in the name of Terumat HaDeshen — they are more stringent than the Rashba we mentioned regarding other impure beasts besides wolf and lion: they hold other impure beasts are stricter than a wolf, having derisah even in a large animal, but regarding the wolf itself they agree it has no derisah in a large animal, as the Beit Yosef wrote explicitly in his name. If so, what is written here "from a wolf and upward" means what is included in the Talmud's wording "from a wolf and upward" — other impure beasts; and since there is a dispute, it is permitted to sell to a gentile, as the Rama wrote at the end of this siman.
