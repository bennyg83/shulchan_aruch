Tur and Rashba wrote in this language: "and all this when we did not see it pursue and strike," end of his words — implying concern only with two grounds for concern: we saw it pursue and also struck; but if we saw one of them, they are not concerned. In the Shulchan Aruch here, since it only wrote "did not strike," it implies from the Ran in the Beit Yosef that for a strike alone they are concerned. If the Shulchan Aruch means Rashba and Tur require that we did not see neither pursuit nor strike, but for one of them they are concerned — the Shulchan Aruch should have written both and informed that if we saw pursuit alone or strike alone they are concerned; also it is strained to say Rashba and Ran disagree. It appears all striking is somewhat through pursuit, for one who strikes another wants first to save himself as much as he can, and the struck one pursues somewhat — it is obvious that what is not the manner of striking but standing together and placing a hand is mere play. Maharshal also wrote it is not considered striking unless from anger, such as pursuing; but he wrote when both are silent that daily cats strike chickens and we are not concerned and attribute it to play, and concludes nevertheless one should be concerned whenever it struck, even without pursuit, and likewise entering a chicken coop while silent and they cry out without knowing if it struck them, etc., end of his words. In my humble opinion there is no concern without our seeing it struck, even when it is silent and they cry out — what does it matter that there is fear on the chickens, since our cats are domesticated; Rashba and Ran explicitly permitted this by law, and one should not be stringent except when we saw striking through pursuit; with this it is forbidden even when both are silent, like seif 11; and even then there is permission to sell to a gentile, for there are two sefeikos; one who is stringent to kill first and then sell to a gentile — well done, as explained.
