One for whom a relative died. Even if the deceased is not laid before him, but burial is incumbent upon him — all laws of onen apply to him, as will be explained. Before burial. He is an onen and forbidden in all things an onen is forbidden; but after burial, even on the day he died, laws of onen do not apply to him — so in Semag and other poskim; so too Bach. He eats in another house. And if the deceased is not before him, he may eat in the house where he is. Perishah — and so Bach: "eats in another house" refers to when the deceased is laid before him, and there is concern of mocking the poor if he eats before him — and this is certainly forbidden even on Shabbat. He has no fellow's house. Such as when he resides among gentiles. Mordekhai and Bach. And even if he is in another city. It implies that even if he has people to attend to the deceased in his city, laws of onen still apply to him — this is Rosh's view who disagrees with Rabbenu Tam. But Bach ruled like Rabbenu Tam: relatives in another city have no onen law upon them unless the deceased has no mourning relatives in his city who are incumbent to bury him; but if there are relatives there incumbent to tend to him, those relatives in another city have no onen law upon them. He does not recline and eat. Meaning on the table or on the bed. If he wishes. Since he intends for a mitzvah — if he wishes he eats, and if he wishes he does not eat; it is not obligatory on a person to eat meat and drink wine on Shabbat — for they said "make your Shabbat profane rather than depend on others." In every obligatory matter they must act on Shabbat; but this is optional — if he wishes he eats meat and drinks wine, if not he refrains — end quote Terumat HaDeshen. And it implies blessings and prayer are obligatory on Shabbat and Yom Tov. And if he must darken, etc. See Orach Chaim siman 71 and siman 548 seif 4. And all the more so on second diaspora Yom Tov when he himself can bury. As explained in Orach Chaim siman 496 seif 2. Maharil wrote in the name of Maharash that regarding one who died on Shemini Atzeret at evening, the mourner may make Kiddush on the following Simchat Torah night since there is no aninut on Yom Tov. See Derishah who explained the reason. For us who hold like Rosh — likewise at night it is forbidden to make Kiddush. Some say, etc. And so the Mechaber wrote above siman 242 seif 27 and below siman 374 seif 10 without qualification.
