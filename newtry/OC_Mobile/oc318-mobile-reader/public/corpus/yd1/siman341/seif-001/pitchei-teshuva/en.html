One of his relatives died. See Be’er Heitev, seif katan 1. Tiferet LeMoshe writes that the following requires study: A child who died within thirty days of birth, for whom mourning is not observed, as well as an eight-month infant and miscarried fetuses, may the relative eat meat and drink wine before burial, since the obligation to bury the child rests upon him, or is he not forbidden, just as a kohen does not become impure for them? It appears that regarding a child who dies within thirty days one should rule stringently, since most infants are not nonviable, and the laws of mourning are treated leniently only because of doubt. These are his words. Whether such a person is obligated in all the mitzvot also requires study. I later saw that Birkei Yosef writes: If a son dies within thirty days of birth, the Yerushalmi at the end of the chapter Nose’in Al HaAnusah appears to indicate that the father is not subject to aninut.
