Whose relative died. See Baer Heitev, seif katan 1, and Tiferet LeMoshe. He questions whether, when an infant dies within thirty days of birth and no mourning is observed, or in the case of an eight-month fetus or other nonviable birth, the relatives are nevertheless forbidden meat and wine while burial remains their responsibility. Perhaps the prohibition applies because they must bury the deceased, or perhaps not, just as a kohen does not become impure for such a deceased. He inclines to stringency for an infant under thirty days because most infants are viable and the leniency concerns only mourning. Whether the onen is exempt from all mitzvot requires study. Birkei Yosef later cites Divrei Moshe, Yoreh Deah siman 83, that the Yerushalmi at the end of Perek Nose'in Al HaAnusah implies there is no aninut for a son who dies within thirty days.
