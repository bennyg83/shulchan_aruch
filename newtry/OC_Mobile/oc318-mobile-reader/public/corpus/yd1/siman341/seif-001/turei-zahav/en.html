He eats in another house. For it is like mocking the poor; so Rashi explained in Perek Mi Shemeto. The reason they did not forbid this also for another person on this ground is that specifically one who is obligated in his burial raises concern when he is not involved in the burial and sits eating in front of the dead.

All the more so on the second day of Yom Tov. This appears to refer to the daytime, when burial takes place. On the preceding night, after the first day of Yom Tov, the mourner is permitted as on the first day. Maharil records that when a death occurred on Shemini Atzeret, Maharash permitted the mourner to recite Kiddush on the night of Simchat Torah, because the laws of aninut do not apply at night in that circumstance. The precise practice depends upon whether burial is possible and upon the distinctions between the first and second festival days.
