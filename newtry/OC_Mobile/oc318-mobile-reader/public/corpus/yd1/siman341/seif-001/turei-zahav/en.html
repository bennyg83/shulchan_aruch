He eats in another house. This is because otherwise he appears to mock the deceased. Rashi explains this in Berachot. The same restriction is not imposed on an unrelated person because the special concern applies to one responsible for burial: he should be occupied with burying his dead rather than sitting and eating in front of the body.

All the more so on the second day of Yom Tov. It appears this means the daytime, when burial may take place. On the preceding night, namely Motzaei the first day of Yom Tov, the mourner is permitted like on the first day. Maharil, Hilchot Yom Tov, records that when a person died on Shemini Atzeret, Maharam Shatz ruled that the mourner may make Kiddush on the night of Simchat Torah, because there is no aninut on Yom Tov. It appears from the same reasoning that burial is not ordinarily performed at night.
