And he is exempt from all mitzvot. Beit Yehudah writes that an onen is exempt from searching for chametz, and another should search for him. Teshuvah MeAhavah records that in Prague the onen transfers his share to his partner; regarding chametz he should likewise give it to another, who then recites the blessing. If the other searches merely as his agent, whether a blessing is recited requires study. Noda BiYehudah, second edition, 67, rules that an onen during the Omer, where burial is far away and he will remain an onen all night and the next day, should count that night without a blessing and may bless on later nights. Shevut Yaakov, part 1, responsum 7, rules that one who began prayer or Shema and suddenly became an onen should finish, since the exemption is rabbinic. [Citation 100.]
