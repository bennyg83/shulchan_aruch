All the time, etc. The proof is from David, who washed, etc., in II Samuel 12; and mourning is learned from him at the beginning of chapter 3, 15b, as it says, "and he comforted," etc. This is the Rambam. See Tosafot in Ketubot 4a, end of s.v. bo'el.
(Likkut) All the time, etc.; and some say, etc. See Tosafot in Moed Katan 23b, s.v. ve-eino okhel, etc.
(Likkut) A mourner is forbidden, etc. This is Evel Rabbati chapter 11: a mourner, as long as the dead is lying, etc.
And all the more so, etc. For even on Shabbat it is forbidden, as written in seif 1.
(Likkut) And all the more so that it is forbidden, etc. The first Tanna and Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel disagree only regarding Shabbat, and even there the poskim ruled like the first Tanna, as above.
And some say, etc. That is, anything involving passive refraining that is a matter of joy.
(Likkut) And some say that it is forbidden, etc. As for what is written about David, "he washed and anointed," etc., that is different because a king is permitted to wash, as stated regarding Yom Kippur at the beginning of chapter 8 of Yoma: the king and the bride, etc. This is from Torat HaAdam; he gives another answer there, and in the name of the Raavad; see there.
(Likkut) And some say, etc. This is based on what is stated in Evel Rabbati chapter 8, that Rabbi Akiva removed his tefillin; the same applies to all these matters that are done by passive refraining. See the Rosh.
