(Siman 341, seif 2) But he should not recite the evening prayer, etc., since at night he was not obligated to pray before the burial. Tiferet LeMoshe challenges this from the Yerushalmi cited by Tosafot in Chagigah: David died on Shavuot, all Israel were onenim, and they offered their festival offerings the following day, implying that they possessed a compensatory obligation. Were an onen considered a person presently unfit for the mitzvah, there should have been no compensation. This presents no difficulty. There, they had already become obligated in the offerings when the day of Shavuot dawned, before David died. Indeed, for this reason they did not become tamei for David even though he was the nasi, as explained in Sha'ar HaMelech. They were therefore already subject to the obligation and could make it up on the following day. Here, by contrast, the person became an onen before nightfall and was already exempt from prayer before its time arrived. When the time of obligation subsequently arrives, he is not regarded as having been obligated, and therefore has no compensatory prayer.
