If the prayer time passes while he is exempt, perhaps he need not recite a compensatory prayer afterward, because he was exempt at the original time. This is not conclusive. An onen is legally exempt from the mitzvah itself, whereas one occupied with communal needs may still have an obligation once the impediment passes. The laws of missed prayer therefore depend upon whether the omission was through exemption, compulsion, negligence, or error, as explained in Orach Chaim. [Citation 5.] [Citation 93.] [Citation 108.]
He should recite havdalah after he is buried. For the time of havdalah extends until the end of Tuesday. What is written in the Tur, "the entire Shabbat," means what belongs to that Shabbat. See what I wrote in siman 396, that the rulings of the Shulchan Aruch contradict each other. In resolving the matter, Maharshal wrote in Responsa, siman 70, that if an onen has a son to circumcise and the deceased has not yet been buried, nevertheless he should circumcise the son in the synagogue before Aleinu as at other times. Afterward I found in the name of the Rama in his handwriting that the burial should be first, for otherwise he is an onen and cannot bless "to bring him into the covenant," since an onen is exempt from all mitzvot. It appears that one should be concerned for this if it is possible to bury him before people leave the synagogue in the morning, for we do not defer zealous performance of the mitzvah. But if it is impossible, the sandak should bless and circumcise him in the morning, for it is stated in siman 360 that when there is a corpse and a circumcision, the circumcision takes precedence.
