He eats in another house. Baer Heitev of Rabbi Maharit writes: 'It would be like mocking the impoverished. The reason this was not also forbidden to another person is that this concern applies specifically to one who is obligated to arrange the burial, for he neglects the burial and sits eating before the deceased. This is forbidden even on Shabbos.' These are presented as the words of Shach, but this is surprising, because he combined the words of Taz and Shach, who in truth disagree. According to Taz, who writes that the concern applies specifically to one obligated in the burial, it follows that on Shabbos, when he may not engage in burial, eating is permitted. According to Shach, who writes that it is forbidden even on Shabbos, he plainly maintains that it is also forbidden to another person. I subsequently found that Tiferes LeMoshe explicitly writes that according to Shach, even one who is not a relative may not eat in the presence of the deceased; the Baraisa mentions 'his deceased' only regarding the prohibition against meat and wine. As a practical matter, it appears proper to rule stringently like Shach.
