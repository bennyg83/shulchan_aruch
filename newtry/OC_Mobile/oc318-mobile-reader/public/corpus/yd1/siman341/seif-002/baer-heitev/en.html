Of the night. The Derishah wrote: from here it would seem that the same applies to one occupied with communal needs and the like during the time of prayer, who is exempt from praying as the Tur wrote in Orach Chayim siman 93; he need not make it up, because while occupied he was exempt, etc. The Taz disagrees and writes that this is only called an ones, and regarding an ones they explicitly said that afterward he prays two prayers, as stated in Orach Chayim siman 108. It is not comparable to mourning. Specifically where the exemption comes from another matter, and he himself could pray without any impediment but mourning exempts him, there is no makeup afterward. But where the exemption is because of preoccupation, that is called an ones, like an ill person who cannot pray, and for that they instituted makeup prayer. End quote. In Nekudot HaKesef he wrote that the Derishah's words are correct: specifically with an ones, where he is obligated by law but his compulsion exempts him, this is the law; but one occupied with communal needs is not obligated by law at all, because one occupied with a mitzvah, etc. See there.
He should recite Havdalah. Until the end of Tuesday, as stated in Orach Chayim 299:6. Rashal wrote in responsum that if an onen has a son to circumcise and the dead has not yet been buried, nevertheless he should circumcise the son in the synagogue before Aleinu, as at other times. The Taz wrote that he found in the name of Rama in manuscript that one should bury first, for otherwise he is an onen and cannot recite the berachah “to bring him into the covenant,” since an onen is exempt from all mitzvot. It appears that one should be concerned for this if it is possible to bury him before people leave the synagogue in the morning, for one does not delay zealous performance of the mitzvah. But if it is impossible, the sandak should recite the berachah and circumcise him in the morning, for in siman 360 it is stated that when there is a dead body and a milah, the milah comes first. End quote.
