Even if the wine was sealed with one seal. So wrote the Beit Yosef in the name of the Rashba, that wherever the non-Jew is not afraid, two seals are required. It appears to me that according to the Rama's ruling in siman 118, which is established for us, that after the fact one seal suffices, the same applies here. The Rashba follows his own view, for he holds everywhere that one seal is ineffective. One cannot say that even according to our ruling we should be stringent here and require two seals, because this resembles one who purifies a non-Jew's wine, whose law is stated in siman 130, where there is an additional stringency. For if so, even two seals would not suffice, and a guard would be required, as stated there. Rather, this case does not resemble one who purifies a non-Jew's wine, because here the wine itself belongs to the Jew, and it should have been permitted even without any seal, since the non-Jew would be afraid. However, since the non-Jew has a debt secured by it, he is not afraid, and it is comparable to a case where the Jew informed him that he would be absent for an extended time. According to our ruling, one seal suffices in such a case, as stated in siman 129, at the end of seif 1, regarding a substantial loss.
If the spigot is in his hand, the wine is forbidden. This implies that all the wine in the barrel is forbidden. The leniency applicable in a case of substantial loss, that a continuous stream is not considered a connection, does not apply here, for the reason I wrote in siman 124, seif 24, regarding one who inserted a knife into a barrel.
