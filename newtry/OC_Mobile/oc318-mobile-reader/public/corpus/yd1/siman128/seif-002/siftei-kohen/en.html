And even if the wine was sealed with one seal. It appears that this follows the view of the Rashba and the Mechaber below in siman 130. But according to what the Rama writes there in seif 2, that b'dieved one seal suffices even when wine is deposited with a non-Jew, all the more so it suffices here. The reason he states the law here without qualification in accordance with the Mechaber is that he relies on what he writes below in siman 130, which is the primary place for this law. Olat Tamid is difficult, for he copied the Mechaber's words without qualification. Perhaps he holds that here, since the wine was pledged to the non-Jew, it is treated more like the non-Jew's own property and is therefore worse, so that one seal does not suffice according to anyone. But Tosafos at the end of the chapter R' Yishmael, s.v. 'one who purifies,' explicitly compares this law to one who purifies the wine of a non-Jew, and so too the Gemara explicitly does at the end of the chapter R' Yishmael, Avodah Zarah, daf 60b. Accordingly, just as regarding one who purifies we rule at the beginning of siman 131 that one seal suffices b'dieved, the same applies here. Examine carefully. This is likewise explicit in the Ran at the end of the chapter R' Yishmael, that according to Rabbeinu Tam, who holds that one seal suffices, the same applies here. See there.
And even if the wine has no seal at all. And even if the barrels are open — Rashba there; and Bach wrote in seif 5: however, certainly specifically in a house open to the public thoroughfare where Israelites pass through the public domain — but otherwise he is not afraid; and see above siman 131.
