And if he would be apprehended as a thief. Meaning, if the Jew were to find him, he would be apprehended under the law as a thief by order of the ruler, and he is not a violent person who disregards the authorities.
Regarding entry, it is permitted for drinking. This is the view of Rabbeinu Yitzchak cited by the Tur. In the name of Rashi, however, the Tur cites that the matter depends on touching. The Beit Yosef has already written that Rashi did not state this. Therefore, the Maharshal emended Rashi's words as follows: If he would be apprehended as a thief for entering, the wine is permitted, even if he would not be apprehended as a thief for touching it. But if he would not be apprehended as a thief for entering, it is forbidden, even if he would be apprehended as a thief for touching it. Rashi explains the reason: since he entered without permission, he certainly entered to pour a libation. I, however, do not understand what he wrote, even if he would not be apprehended as a thief for touching it. How can such a case exist, in which he has permission to touch it? He cannot touch it unless he enters. It appears that this is what he means: even if he would not be apprehended for touching it were the wine situated in an exposed place, such as in the marketplace, and since he is also unafraid and enters there, it would be considered for him as though it were situated in an exposed place; nevertheless, now that it is in a concealed place, he is preoccupied and panicked by his entry, and he did not touch it.
