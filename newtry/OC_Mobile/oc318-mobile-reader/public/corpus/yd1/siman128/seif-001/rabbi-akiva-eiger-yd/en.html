(Siman 128, seif 2) A non-Jew who was found in the house. This implies even if he is not next to the wine but merely in the house, as the Beit Yosef wrote in the name of the Rashba. I am astonished, for this should be no worse than the case of a Jew who has arranged for a non-Jew to prepare his wine, in siman 131, where it is permitted when a Jew and a non-Jew reside there, even without a key and seal. See Tosafos at the end of the chapter Rabbi Yishmael. The Shach here, seif katan 6, explicitly compared it to the case of one who has a non-Jew prepare his wine. The Taz, seif katan 2, wrote that even two seals would not help. I am astonished, for there, when a Jew lives in the house, no key or seal is required at all. This requires very great study, unless we force the explanation that the case is one in which there is no plausible reason to attribute the non-Jew's entry into the house to any other purpose, so his entry itself creates a suspicious circumstance. This requires further study.
