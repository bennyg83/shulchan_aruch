It is permitted for drinking. See Baer Heitev and Ba’ei Chayei, Yoreh Deah responsum 173, concerning a Jewish maidservant who took the key to a cellar containing open wine barrels, entered with a non-Jew for immoral purposes, and locked the door behind them. The author considers whether this is not a case where the non-Jew could excuse his presence, since even if he claimed merely to have entered, he would fear punishment by the authorities. He concludes without a definite ruling. He also does not decisively permit the wine for drinking based on the incident of an invading troop, because Hagahot Ashri adopts the latter distinction between troops of that kingdom and others.
