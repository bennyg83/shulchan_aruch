(Siman 128, Shulchan Aruch, seif 2, Rama's gloss) Likewise, if he found the non-Jew holding the stopper, etc. See above siman 124, Taz, seif katan 33, who wrote that this law applies also to contemporary non-Jews. It is not considered contact through another object; rather, we are concerned that perhaps he touched the stream itself. The Shach at the end of seif katan 8 wrote, however, that according to us, since contemporary non-Jews are not worshippers of idolatry, the wine is permitted even for drinking; see there. According to his words, all the Rama's words in this gloss are unnecessary for practical halacha according to us, which is very difficult. It therefore appears that the Shach's ruling is certainly correct: we do not say that he definitely touched the stream, because it is possible that he did not touch it at all, and in this respect such contact is not regarded as something ordinarily expected. The Rama, however, is discussing a case where the barrel is open at the top in a manner that allows him to touch the wine from above. Ordinarily it is permitted because we say that he fears being caught like a thief and is afraid to touch it. But if the stopper is in his hand and we say that he himself removed the stopper, we see that he is not afraid, and therefore we are concerned that perhaps he touched the wine from above. The wine is forbidden even nowadays, since he is not afraid.
