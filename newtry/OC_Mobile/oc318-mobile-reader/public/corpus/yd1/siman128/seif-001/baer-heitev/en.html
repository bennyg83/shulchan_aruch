Who does not. Such as a ger toshav and Ishmaelites. Tur, Rashba, and Rambam. Perishah inferred that a ger toshav's touch is permitted for drinking, since otherwise we should suspect that he touched the wine. Shach rejects this, because the Mechaber ruled in siman 124:2 that such touch forbids drinking. Rather, as Rashi explains, since he does not worship idolatry we assume he neither touched nor libated. This applies only where there is no concern that he touched the wine to drink or for another benefit, as stated below in seif 4.
Entrust. Even in a city whose majority is Jewish. Bach writes that it is permitted for benefit only where it is known that he did not touch it. Otherwise it is forbidden even for benefit because we fear that he exchanged it for his own wine, which is certainly forbidden since he does not avoid non-Jewish touch. Shach.
Leave alone. Shach writes that this prohibition is initial only. After the fact it is permitted even for drinking where there is no concern that he touched it in order to drink, as below in seif 4.
