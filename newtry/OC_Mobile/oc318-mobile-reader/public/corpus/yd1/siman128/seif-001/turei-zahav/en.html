It is permitted to leave wine alone with him, etc. It appears that his intent is that there are two explanations of the distinction between leaving alone and depositing, which we find. One may explain that the term “leaving alone” applies specifically for a short time, while “depositing,” which is forbidden, means for a long time; this is the Tur’s view. But there is another explanation, and it is the implication of Rashi in the Gemara: “leaving alone” means that he leaves it alone with the gentile in the Jew’s domain, but does not appoint the gentile as a guard over it at all; he merely leaves it in the Jew’s house. The permission here is because the gentile has no prohibition that would lead him to exchange his wine for ours in our house. But depositing means in the gentile’s house, where other gentiles enter, and he is not particular if others touch it. Therefore the Shulchan Aruch was stringent to forbid both, and there is permission only in our house and for a short time. Although nowadays all gentiles are not idol worshippers, nevertheless here it is forbidden, for with regard to depositing with them ab initio, we treat them as idol worshippers, as the Rama writes afterward.
