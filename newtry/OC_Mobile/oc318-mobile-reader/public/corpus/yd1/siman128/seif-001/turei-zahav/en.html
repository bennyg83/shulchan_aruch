It is permitted to leave wine secluded with him, etc. It appears that his intent is as follows. There are two explanations regarding the distinction between leaving something secluded and entrusting it, which we find here. One can explain that the term leaving secluded applies specifically for a short time, whereas entrusting it, which is forbidden, refers to a long time; this is the view of the Tur. But there is another explanation, which is the implication of Rashi in the Gemara, that leaving it secluded means that he leaves it together with the non-Jew on Jewish premises, and he did not appoint the non-Jew as a custodian over it at all, but simply leaves it there in the Jew's house. The basis for permitting this is that the non-Jew has no reason to exchange it for his own wine in our house. Entrusting it, however, means leaving it in the non-Jew's house, where other non-Jews enter, and he is not particular if others touch it. Therefore, the Shulchan Aruch was stringent to forbid both cases, and there is no permission except in our houses and for a short time. Although nowadays all non-Jews are not worshippers of idolatry, nevertheless it is forbidden here, because regarding initially entrusting wine to them we treat them as worshippers of idolatry, as the Rama writes below.
