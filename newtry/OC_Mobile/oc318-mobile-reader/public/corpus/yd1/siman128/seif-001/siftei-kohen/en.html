But one does not deposit with them. And even in a city whose majority is Israel — Gemara.
Forbidden for drinking. This implies that benefit in all cases is permitted; and such appears in Beit Yosef; but Bach challenged him — it is only permitted in benefit when it is known he did not touch; otherwise it is forbidden even in benefit, as Tosafot wrote, that he is not concerned about the touch of a gentile; and likewise in the Rosh and the Tur, that the reason one who deposited with him is forbidden is because we suspect perhaps he exchanged it with his own, and his own is certainly forbidden since he is not concerned about the touch of a gentile.
One may not leave wine alone with them. In Rashba, even after the fact it is forbidden; so too Ateret Zekenim wrote that even after the fact it is forbidden for drinking. The Rav did not write so, apparently because he holds that Rashba follows his own view, that stam yeinam and contact of non-Jews nowadays are forbidden in benefit, as Beit Yosef brings above at the beginning of siman 123; for although non-Jews nowadays are not worshippers of avodah zarah, nevertheless since they sometimes pour libations, they are considered worshippers of avodah zarah. But for us, who permit their contact and stam yeinam in benefit because they are not worshippers of avodah zarah, the same applies here: if one left wine with him, it is permitted after the fact, like a ger toshav and Ishmaelites, though ab initio it is still forbidden. A proof is from what we hold below in seif 4 in the gloss, that nowadays even if a non-Jew entered a house containing a Jew's wine, and even locked it with a key, and even if he is not caught as a thief, it is permitted for drinking where there is no concern that he touched it in order to drink. There is no greater leaving-alone than this, and nevertheless it is permitted. It appears that Ateret Zekenim also holds this, and what he wrote that after the fact it is forbidden refers to a case where there is concern that he touched it for some benefit. So too it is explicit from Bach, siman 129 end of seif 9, that according to the one who permits below in seif 4, the same applies here; only he disagrees there with seif 4 and writes that in his humble opinion the primary view is like Rashba. In my humble opinion Rashba follows his own view, but we hold in simanim 123 and 124 and in many places that non-Jews nowadays are not worshippers of avodah zarah. Furthermore, perhaps Bach saw that the Rav in seif 4 derived his words from Hagahot Asheri, Perek HaSocher, and it was easy in his eyes to reject them before Rashba's words. But in truth the Tur in siman 129, Tosafot in the last chapter of Avodah Zarah in the name of Rashbam in the name of Rashi, Mordechai in the last chapter of Avodah Zarah, Agudah there, Semag, Semak, Hagahot Maimoniyot, and Isur VeHeter kelal 22 din 14 all wrote explicitly like the Rav.
