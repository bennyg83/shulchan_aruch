Who does not worship idolatry. Such as a ger toshav and Ishmaelites, as stated by the Tur, Rashba, and Rambam. The Perishah infers from here that contact by a ger toshav is permitted even for drinking, for otherwise we should be concerned that he touched the wine. This is astonishing, because it would contradict Rashba and the Mechaber above in siman 124, seif 2, who hold that contact by a ger toshav forbids the wine for drinking, while the Gemara explicitly permits leaving wine alone with him. Rather, the reason is as Rashi explains: since he does not worship idolatry, we do not presume that he will touch the wine in order to libate it. This applies specifically where there is no concern that he touched it in order to drink or derive some other benefit, as explained below in seif 4 in the Rama's gloss. Even though contemporary non-Jews are not treated as actual idolaters, where there is concern that he touched the wine for his own benefit, the wine remains prohibited.
But one does not deposit with them. And even in a city whose majority is Israel — Gemara.
Forbidden for drinking. This implies that benefit in all cases is permitted; and such appears in Beit Yosef; but Bach challenged him — it is only permitted in benefit when it is known he did not touch; otherwise it is forbidden even in benefit, as Tosafot wrote, that he is not concerned about the touch of a gentile; and likewise in the Rosh and the Tur, that the reason one who deposited with him is forbidden is because we suspect perhaps he exchanged it with his own, and his own is certainly forbidden since he is not concerned about the touch of a gentile.
One may not leave wine alone with them. According to Rashba it is forbidden even b'diavad, and the Levush writes likewise that even b'diavad it is forbidden for drinking. The Rama's omission of that qualification appears deliberate. Rashba follows his own position that ordinary non-Jewish wine and contact by contemporary non-Jews are forbidden even for benefit because, although they are not regular idolaters, they sometimes perform libations. According to our ruling that their contact and ordinary wine are permitted for benefit because they are not regarded as idolaters, leaving wine alone with them should likewise be permitted b'diavad, just as with a ger toshav or Ishmaelites, while remaining prohibited l'chatchilah. Proof is the Rama's ruling below in seif 4 that nowadays, even if a non-Jew enters a house containing a Jew's wine and locks it, and even where he would not be caught as a thief, the wine is permitted for drinking when there is no concern that he touched it for drinking. There can be no greater seclusion with the wine than that. Many authorities, including the Tur, Tosafos, Mordechai, Agudah, Semag, Semak, Hagahos Maimoniyos, and Issur VeHeter, support the Rama's position. See also below, siman 129, seif katan 14.
