Wine inside it. He states a novel ruling: even though the non-Jew also has his own wine there, or he jointly owns wine there with the Jew, nevertheless there are times when it is permitted, as he proceeds to explain.
With a lock on the inside. The Beit Yosef wrote in the name of a responsum of the Rashba that this applies specifically when it is known that they locked it from the inside. But if this is not known, even though there is a lock on the inside, we do not forbid it out of doubt. This applies by day, but at night we presume that they also locked it. Likewise, what he writes afterward, that when there are holes in the door it is permitted, also applies by day, but at night it is forbidden. So wrote the Rama at the end of siman 130.
Even sealed barrels. For he can evade the accusation and say, I was sleeping, and therefore I did not open the door for you.
Enough time to open it and reseal it. See the explanation of this in siman 129, seif 1.
