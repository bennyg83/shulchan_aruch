A house that belongs to a Jew, and there is wine of a non-Jew in it, and the non-Jew entered and locked the door behind him with a lock from inside, such that if the Jew comes to enter he cannot enter without the non-Jew’s knowledge — the wine is forbidden (Rosh). But if there were holes in the door such that the Jew can look and see from there all the barrels, they are permitted for drinking, even if they are open. And if not, that which he can see through there is permitted and the rest is forbidden in benefit, even sealed barrels, if he remained long enough that he could open and reseal and wipe (meaning: reseal and make another cover; and “wipe” means to wipe and dry), even if the Jew lived in that house.
