House, etc. In Rashi there s.v. this, etc., and the Rif states explicitly in the Gemara ; wine of an Israelite and a gentile, etc.:
(Likkut) A house, etc. This is the first incident there, daf 70a, concerning that house, etc., according to the reading, "wine of a Jew and a non-Jew," and Rashi explains it this way. The fourth incident there, concerning that house, etc., where R' Chisda said, "If he can offer an excuse," etc., concerns property belonging only to a Jew. In both cases, if the door was not locked, it is permitted, as the Gemara challenges there from the Baraisa, "If it was locked," etc., and as stated there, "He held the door," etc. In both cases, whatever is opposite the crack is permitted, as written in siman 129, seif 5, in the Rama's gloss. The difference is that in the second case, if the non-Jew would be caught as a thief, everything is permitted, unlike the first case, as Rashi explains there, s.v. "on this side," etc. This is what is written in seif 4, "a house," etc., regarding the entrance, in accordance with Rashi, s.v. "if there is," etc., and the Rosh there. The third incident there, concerning that inn, etc., appears to be in a place where the non-Jew had permission to enter. Therefore the Gemara says, "if he would be caught," etc., meaning caught for touching the wine, like the incident at the end of chapter 4 concerning that fortress, etc., and the incident in seif 5. End.
