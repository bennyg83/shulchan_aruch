That one may not lend a se’ah for a se’ah; it contains 5 se’ifim. It is forbidden to lend a se’ah for a se’ah, even if he did not set a time for repayment; and likewise, anything [lent in kind], except for a silver coin that goes out in circulation (then), for perhaps they will become more expensive, and it turns out that he gives him more than he lent to him — unless he makes it into money-value, such that if they become more expensive he will give him that money, and if he did not make it into money-value and they became more expensive, he gives him the money that they were worth at the time of the loan; and if they became cheaper, he gives him the se’ah that he lent to him. {Rama: There is one who wrote that nowadays a gold coin has the law of silver, and one may borrow gold for gold, and so they are accustomed to be lenient, and one should not protest them, for they have upon whom to rely (Piskei Mahari, siman 54). There is one who says that it is permitted to borrow a loaf of bread for a loaf of bread, like a silver coin, for since it is a small matter people are not particular with one another about it (the Tur and many poskim, and see Beit Yosef), and so they are accustomed to be lenient.}
