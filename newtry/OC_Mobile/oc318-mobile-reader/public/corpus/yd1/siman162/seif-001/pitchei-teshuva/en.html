From silver coin. See Responsa Darkei Noam, Yoreh De'ah, siman 24, at length. See also Responsa Radbaz, siman 470.
That he convert it to money. See Responsa Beit Efrayim, Yoreh De'ah, siman 43, who writes that this is permitted only if he explicitly stipulated that he would repay him with money. If he stipulated that the lender may choose to take either wheat or money, the condition is ineffective. On the contrary, it makes matters worse, for if the wheat later declined in price and he gives the lender money equal to its value at the time of the loan, it is ribbis ketzutzah, since this was stipulated initially. See there for his lengthy discussion.
If they declined in value, he gives him the seah. See Responsa Beit Efrayim there, who proves at length that he specifically takes wheat. It is forbidden to give him the money value that the wheat had at the time of the loan when it declined in value. That monetary payment is permitted only when the wheat rose in value, not when it declined, because of the prohibition of ribbis. This is unlike Machaneh Efrayim, siman 27, who was uncertain about this.
