If he does not clarify etc. And if it is written in the document that the lender shall be believed regarding all matters of the debt — the lender is believed — Hagahah Perishah.
At the time of the loan etc. It appears specifically if it is known that he lent him during cheap time, or the time of the loan is mentioned in the document — for then since there is a side of interest before us we must clarify the heter; and so it implies in the Rosh's responsum there, klal 108 siman 15; but if the lender claims “I lent it to you now,” or the document only states so-and-so owes so-and-so such-and-such seahs to be paid at such-and-such time, and the lender claims “you owe me these seahs because I acquired them from you by purchase and gave you their current value” — or he claims the price never rose, it was the same at the time of the loan — even though he then lent in prohibition since perhaps the price would rise — nevertheless now he is not taking interest; therefore the lender is believed; and see above siman 169 s.k. 78.
And the lender cannot etc. For the ability to transfer ownership to the borrower applies specifically if the borrower's mind is satisfied; but if the borrower's mind is not satisfied — such as when the borrower says he had no wheat and lent him in prohibition — it is of no avail, for one does not transfer ownership to a person against his will — there.
But a seah of wheat etc. And the same for all other two species — and so in Derishah; and it is obvious.
Even though they are at one price, etc. Ateret Zekenim wrote: even if the market price of both has been established. For although we said above in seif 3 that when the market price of wheat alone was established it is permitted, here, where he lends on millet, it is worse, etc. So too it implies in Baal HaTerumot, sha'ar 46, part 5, page 72b, even though he wrote proof from the Mishnah: one may not say to his fellow, weed with me and I will hoe with you, for weeding and hoeing are like something he has, and they are now at one price, yet it is forbidden because they are two kinds of labor and sometimes they are not equal; and it is forbidden, end quote. See below siman 173 s.k. 17. Rivash wrote in siman 396 regarding Reuven who borrowed a kor of wheat from Shimon, etc., until "and this one collects his money," end quote; Beit Yosef brings it, and so wrote Ateret Zekenim.
