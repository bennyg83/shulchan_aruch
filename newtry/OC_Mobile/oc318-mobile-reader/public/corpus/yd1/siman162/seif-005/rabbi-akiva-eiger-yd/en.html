(Seif 5) He lent him two se'ah of wheat and executed a note for him. The Tur cites a responsum of his father, the Rosh: If one lent another wheat and drew up a note obligating him for such-and-such se'ah, and the borrower cannot clarify the matter, the borrower swears, etc. A reason must be given why he is believed to claim against a note that it represents interest. It appears that this document does not have the status of a note collectible from purchasers, since the borrower obligated himself to give a se'ah according to its value at the time of payment, whether the price decreases or increases. The obligation is therefore unquantified both below and above, and cannot be collected from purchasers. Since it cannot be collected from purchasers, it is like a handwritten obligation, regarding which he is believed to say, 'I paid,' as stated in Choshen Mishpat, end of siman 69, because he is not concerned to leave it outstanding when it cannot be collected from encumbered property. He is therefore believed to claim that it involves interest by virtue of a migo that he could have claimed payment. This is straightforward. I later found support that in such a case it is not fit for collection from encumbered property in Urim VeTumim, beginning of siman 116.
