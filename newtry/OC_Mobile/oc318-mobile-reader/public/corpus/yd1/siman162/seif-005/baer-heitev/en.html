I transferred ownership. The Taz asks why this differs from prozbul in Choshen Mishpat siman 67 seif 22, where it works even not in his presence because they were lenient since it is rabbinic; here too seah beseah is only rabbinic, and a fortiori from prozbul. This requires study. In Nekudot HaKesef he writes that this is not difficult, for there we say one may acquire benefit for a person, even though some liability follows, namely that he becomes obligated to pay and shemittah does not cancel it; nevertheless we say it is a benefit because of neilat delet before borrowers, as the Ran writes there. That does not apply here. See there. Millet. The same applies to any other two species. The Levush writes that although above in seif 3 we say that if the market price for wheat alone has emerged it is permitted, here, where he lends upon millet, it is worse. See above siman 173. The Taz writes that one should not object from what is written in siman 163 seif 2, "give me wheat, for I wish to sell it and buy wine," which is permitted where he has it, for there it is in the manner of sale, and it is normal to sell in order to profit afterward. This is unlike a loan, where it is forbidden to profit in repayment of the loan.
