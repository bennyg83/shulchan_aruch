(Seif 3) There was for that kind fixed market price and known to both — permitted to borrow seah for seah. In my humble opinion lender of seah for seah relying on market price must deduct depreciation measure as law of fixing on market price as stated siman 175 seif 5 and CM siman 292 seif 12 regarding deposit; for lender must not profit at all — all the more from fixer who is sale path; certainly seah-for-seah loan on market price is not lighter than fixing on market price, for there is one who says ch. Eizehu Neshekh (73a) forbidden to lend on market price; whereas regarding fixing no one says — explicit mishnah there price went out they fix; if so when we conclude permitted — certainly not said to be lenient. And so plainly in Talmud end ch. Eizehu Neshekh (63a) regarding seah for seah: wheat that crackles in heat — lender must not profit; and so Rosh end ch. Eizehu Neshekh — see there; nevertheless if price did not go out — forbidden; and so Tosafot (62b) regarding they came as deposit — because he does not profit it is like deposit; and likewise for us when price went out permitted — like deposit; if so must give him depreciation like deposit; this seems clear and one who is lenient has proof; and ch. HaUmanin (ch. 2) regarding fruit loan Shavuot — implies one can profit there when he had little when he lent; and see Taz s.k. 160. And it seems plain: if he lent in summer and returned in rainy season — need not give depreciation because they improve; as stated CM in mentioned siman regarding deposit; however where he did not fix time — seems no need to deduct; and see Rambam ch. 10 Laws of Lending.
