And it is known to both of them. The Beit Yosef writes that it is sufficient that they know that a market price has been established, even if they do not know the amount of that price. He further writes that from the responsum of the Rosh at the end of this siman, seif 5, it appears that no knowledge is required at all, for the Rosh required only that one establish that the market price had been set or that the borrower possessed one se'ah, while knowledge of the price is not mentioned at all. The Bach writes that there the lender claims that he knew a market price had been established. It appears to me that although l'chatchila one should not lend until he knows that the market price has been established, nevertheless if he already lent and in fact the market price had been established, even though he did not know it, no prohibition was committed through this loan and the borrower is obligated to repay him. I likewise wrote this in seif katan 7.

Whenever he wishes. And then it is permitted even if he set a time — so is the consensus of the poskim and later authorities, unlike the Rambam; and the Rosh wrote: however if he says “I will not accept payment before the time” — it is forbidden to borrow at the market price, but if he has wheat even in this case one should not forbid — end; and Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe bring it; and it clearly implies from his words there that even if he does not say “I will not accept payment until the time of the price rise” but only “I will not accept until the time” — even if it is not known the price will rise then — it is forbidden, as it resembles “lend me a kor of wheat until the threshing floor”; and according to this what the Rav wrote “until the time of price rise” is not precise, rather it takes the language of Semak and the Kol Bo; or alternatively it teaches us that where the price rose, he is not obligated to pay more; and it seems to me that where he set a time because of concern the price may fall after the loan before the time — it is forbidden for all when he set a time even without saying “I will not accept until the repayment time,” since the poskim challenged the Rambam only on the grounds that setting the time is for the borrower’s benefit in that the lender cannot force him meanwhile — but if he comes to repay within his time, he repays; and in such a case the lender may say “I will not accept my debt before my time” — as Beit HaTerumah wrote in shaar 30, and the Tur in CM siman 74 seif 2, that if he set a time for the borrower and wants to repay within the time out of fear of currency devaluation, tax decrees, or embargoes — he cannot be forced to take his debt before his time, since one cannot be compelled to repay before time to the lender’s detriment and loss.
