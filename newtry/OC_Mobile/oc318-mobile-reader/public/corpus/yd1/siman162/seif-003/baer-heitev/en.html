The market price. As the Beit Yosef wrote, it suffices that they knew the market price had emerged, even if they did not know for how much it emerged. But from the responsum of the Rosh at the end of this siman it implies that no knowledge is required at all. It does not appear so to me: although at the outset one should not lend until the market price has emerged, nevertheless if he lent and the market price had emerged, even though he did not know, no prohibition was committed in this loan, and he must pay him; end quote, Shach. See above in s.k. 4 what I wrote there in the name of the Taz. The basic reason for the permissibility of the market price is stated in the Taz: since it is common to buy, it is comparable to saying, "lend me until my son comes." The Rosh wrote that even if the borrower has no money at all, this permissibility applies, for he can buy from the market on credit or borrow from his fellow. This is specifically with se'ah for se'ah, but if he lent him money, it is forbidden even though there is a market price. He further wrote that if the market price emerged elsewhere, even though it did not emerge in the surrounding places, we rely on that place; end quote. That he wishes. The Shach wrote: then it is permitted even if he set a time. The Rosh wrote: however, if he says, "I will not accept repayment before the time," it is forbidden to borrow on the market price; but if he has wheat, even in this one should not forbid.
The high price. Even if he does not say "until the time of the high price," but says only, "I will not accept until the time," although it is not known whether the price will rise, it is forbidden, for it resembles "lend me a kor of wheat until the threshing floor" (and what he wrote, "until the high price," is not literal). It appears to me that where he fixed a time because there is concern the price may decrease after the loan before the time, it is forbidden according to everyone when he fixed a time, even if he did not say, "I will not accept until the time of repayment"; end quote, Shach.
