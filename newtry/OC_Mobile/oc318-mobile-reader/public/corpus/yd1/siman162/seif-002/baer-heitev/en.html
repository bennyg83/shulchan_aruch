Many. For every seah borrowed, the borrower's existing seah is viewed as immediately transferred to the lender. Bach requires that the parties intend this arrangement; otherwise one may have intended interest. Shach understands this as an initial requirement only. After the fact, if the borrower owned even a little of the same species, repayment is permitted, even without such intent. Cash does not substitute for ownership of the same species. Taz agrees that initially both parties must know the permission. After the fact, actual ownership of the species suffices even if the lender did not know, because the Sages effect the transfer. Market-price permission differs: since the goods belong to others, both parties must know that a market price exists; otherwise, if wheat appreciates, the borrower may not repay in wheat.
They may lend him. Bach rules that the lender must first give or transfer ownership of a seah as a gift; merely lending it does not help, because a loan is given for expenditure. Levush agrees. Taz distinguishes: if the borrower already received one seah through a valid permitted loan, he may borrow many more against it. If he is now trying to create the permission, the first seah must be given as a gift.
