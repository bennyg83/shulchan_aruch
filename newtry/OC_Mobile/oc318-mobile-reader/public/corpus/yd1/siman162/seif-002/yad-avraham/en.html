(Ibid.) Unless he assigns it a monetary value, stipulating that if the produce rises in price he will give him that monetary amount. This requires study, for what does this teach us? Even if he did not assign it a monetary value, when the produce rises in price he must give the lender only the amount it was worth at the time of the loan, as stated below. On the contrary, the Tur and Shulchan Aruch should have taught the reverse: if he assigned it a monetary value, he gives that amount even if the produce later fell in price. Had he not assigned it a monetary value, it would be forbidden for the lender to accept the amount it was worth at the time of the loan after the produce had fallen in price, as the Tur and Shulchan Aruch state below. This is also explicit in the Gemara and Rashi at the beginning of folio 75, and in the Rif, Rosh, and Nimukei Yosef; see there. However, the purpose here is to teach when the loan itself is permitted: he must stipulate that even if the produce rises in price, he will pay the fixed monetary amount. Once he has done so, he may accept that amount even if the produce later falls in price.
