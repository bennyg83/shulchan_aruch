HaZahav we say Rav borrowed dinars from Rabbi Chiya's daughter; came before Rabbi Chiya — he said go pay her good and bad coins; we challenge: these are produce and it is like seah-for-seah loan which is forbidden; we answer Rav had dinars and it is as if saying wait until my son comes, etc.; N.Y. in name of Ran implies from here in all such cases even if not stated explicitly; reason: whatever is in his hand is permitted because from now those in his hand are acquired for lender and when appreciated they appreciated in his domain; since seah-for-seah ribbit is derabbanan they did not decree in such case — end quote. One should wonder what Ran teaches us — plainly permitted; Beit Yosef answered: teaches we need not specify intent to transfer drop against drop; if so ignorant lender would be forbidden only since derabbanan for any reason they were lenient — such is Beit Yosef's intent — end quote. It seems thus proven from Gemara's words needing to say it is as saying until son comes, etc.; is not enough what Rav answered that Rav had dinars; further I wonder Rav's intent going to judgment before Rabbi Chiya — did Rav not know one who has may lend seah for seah? Rather clear the incident: Rabbi Chiya's daughter did not know Rav had dinars and did not reveal at time of loan; nevertheless at time of loan neither Rav nor she had prohibition — Rav did not say lend dinar for dinar, rather lend dinar plainly — possibly he pays via heter; and language implies Rav borrowed dinars not dinar for dinar; deals afterward dinars appreciated and Rav thought even though truly he had — since she did not know forbidden for her to take this gold dinar as seah-for-seah law if appreciated; and there dinars appreciated and Rabbi Chiya answered pay good and bad in her domain — even though she did not know nevertheless sages fixed dinars in Rav's domain for her; and from leniencies they said here since ribbit prohibition is derabbanan; on this Ran wrote "learn from here," etc. Do not challenge from Rambam and Beit Yosef who bring and similarly below seif 3: market price existed and known to both — implies we require both's knowledge as Beit Yosef wrote: different there — cannot say acquired for lender since they belong to others — market people; if so no heter except they know market price exists — unlike "he has" where sages were lenient; and proven from Rambam's language ch. 10 Laws of Lending: he had nothing of that kind and market price not yet fixed or they did not know market price — forbidden to borrow seah for seah, etc.; behold he listed two things first then wrote they did not know market price — did not write or they did not know he has — implies in heter of "he has" no knowledge required; and possible even if borrower did not know clearly at time of loan it helps since truly he has and acquired per sages — such seems correct per Beit Yosef; Maharach wrote to disagree with Beit Yosef that we require both's knowledge also in "he has" as needed for market price; in my opinion as I wrote correct and clear — otherwise what novelty in Rav's case and Ran's explanation who wrote "learn from here," etc.; rather matter was as I wrote above; nevertheless seems if wishing to lend wheat for wheat via heter of "he has" — certainly lechatchilah both must know there is heter; and so in heter of market price; but distinction if he lent and did not know of heter: in heter from "he has" — permitted since acquired per sages; in heter from market price — forbidden to give wheat if appreciated — so appears to me main for halachah.
או ילוהו לו כו'. ב"י הביא בשם תלמידי הרשב"א דאם הלוהו סאה שאינו לוה עליה משום דסאה זו להוציאה לוה אותה וכתב על זה דלא נראה לו דאע"ג דלהוצאה ניתנה לו ילוה עליה שהרי כל חטים שביד האדם להוצאה עומדים ואפ"ה לוה עליהם עכ"ל והנראה לע"ד דס"ל לתלמידי רשב"א ודאי אם הלוה לו כבר סאה ופסק בדרך היתר ודאי יכול להלות עליה אפילו כמה סאין כיון שיש לו כבר סאה ברשותו ולא אסרי אלא אם בא עכשיו לעשות היתר שיוכל להלות בזה אין היתר אלא שיתן לו סאה אחת במתנה ונמצא דקרינן שפיר יש לו על אותה סאה אבל אם רוצה להלות לו עכשיו כדי שיוכל על ידי זה להלות הרבה סאין זה לא יועיל דכל עיקר ההיתר מחמת יש לו הוא דיכול להחזיק זה הסאה לשלמה לו אח"כ במה שילוה לו וזה אינו שייך כאן דהא אותו סאה גופה ניתנה להוצאה ויחזיר לו אחרת תחתיה ולא יחזירנה בעין אם כן אין כאן לומר שתהיה אותה סאה במקום תשלומין על אחרת ומ"ה כתב הרשב"א בתשובה הביאה ב"י בסמוך וז"ל וכדי לעשות בהיתר נותן סאה אחת חטים להקל כו' ולא כ' ילוה וכן סמ"ק וכל בו נקטו היתר נתינה ולא הלואה מטעם שאמרנו ועל כן כתב רשב"א גופיה הבאתיו ריש סי' קס"ג סעיף א' דאם היתה לו כבר הלואה מועלת להלות אח"כ הרבה והיינו בדרך שכתבתי תחלה שהיתה נעשית שלא לשם היתר עכשיו אלא היתה בהיתר בפני עצמה כן נראה לע"ד: **** ENGLISH **** Or they may lend him, etc. Beit Yosef brought in name of Rashba's students: if he lent seah he does not borrow on it because this seah was borrowed for spending; and wrote on this it does not seem to him — even though given for spending he may borrow on it, for all wheat in person's hand stands for spending and nevertheless he borrows on them — end quote. It seems to me Rashba's students certainly hold: if he already lent seah and fixed via heter — certainly can lend on it even many seahs since he already has seah in possession; they forbade only if he now comes to make heter that he can lend via this — no heter except give one seah as gift; and we read properly "he has" on that seah; but if wishes to lend now so that via this he can lend many seahs — this does not help, for entire heter from "he has" is he can hold this seah to repay what he lends; this does not apply here since that seah itself was given for spending and he returns another in its place — does not return in kind; if so cannot say that seah stands as payment for another; and therefore Rashba wrote in responsum Beit Yosef brings nearby: to make heter gives one seah wheat to ease, etc. — did not write "lend"; and so Semak and Kol Bo listed heter of giving not loan for reason we said; therefore Rashba himself I brought beginning siman 163 seif 1: if he already had loan placed to lend afterward much — i.e. via way I wrote initially that was done not for heter now but was in heter by itself — so appears to me.
