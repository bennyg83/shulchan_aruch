Taz explains that a sharecropper may borrow seed from the landowner seah for seah because the arrangement is not truly a loan; it is as though the landowner stipulated that he will first take one seah from his own field. This works when the condition is part of the sharecropper's initial entry onto the land. If he was already established and then borrows for his own need, he is like anyone else and it is forbidden. Where local custom requires the sharecropper to provide seed and the landowner may remove him if he fails, he may borrow even after entering, because his continued tenure is renewed upon fulfilling the condition. If local custom places seed responsibility on the landowner, but this sharecropper separately agreed before entry to provide it, the same applies. If he agreed only after entering, the landowner cannot remove him and the loan is forbidden, unless the later agreement expressly makes failure to provide seed grounds for removal.
