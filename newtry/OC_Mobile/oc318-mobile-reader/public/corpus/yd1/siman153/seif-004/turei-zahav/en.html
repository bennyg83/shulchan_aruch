Even though they are many and their wives are with them. In the Tur it is written regarding this: "And similarly among Jews it is permitted." The Beit Yosef wrote that this does not mean we require both conditions in the case of Jews — that they be many and also that their wives be with them — rather one of them suffices: if his wife is with him, even a single [Jew] is permitted; and if they are many, even without their wives it is permitted, as written in Even HaEzer, Siman 22. So far his words. And [even though] he wrote that one suffices, there is nonetheless a distinction between them: the leniency based on being many applies only to the upright, but not to the licentious. However, the second leniency — based on their wives being with them — applies to a Jew even if he is licentious, and even if there is only one of them. Therefore, when the Tur wrote "and similarly among Jews it is permitted," he meant among the licentious, analogous to non-Jews — which is why he states "many and their wives," meaning it applies when they are many with their wives present. But among non-Jews, these two conditions do not suffice. And so it is in the Gemara, where it says "except in [a group of] three [similar] to Jews" — with licentious Jews, is it then permitted? Therefore the Tur stated both positive conditions to teach that among non-Jews even these two conditions do not suffice. My father-in-law of blessed memory was wrong to disagree with the Beit Yosef here, for even though a licentious Jew is permitted with his wife, even without others — still, the Tur stated the case with "many" to teach us a fortiori that even so, among non-Jews this does not help.
