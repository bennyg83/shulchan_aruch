Even if they are many and their wives are with them. In the Tur he wrote on this: and in such a manner regarding a Jew it is permitted; and Beit Yosef wrote: it is not that we require regarding a Jew both — that they be many and also their wives with them — rather one of them suffices: if his wife is with him even one is permitted, and if they are many even if their wives are not with them it is permitted, as is written in Even HaEzer siman 22, end of his words. And that which he wrote that one suffices — nevertheless there is a distinction between them: regarding the heter that depends on their being many, it only helps for kesherim but not for prutzim; but the second heter because their wives are with them helps regarding a Jew even for a parutz, and even if there is only he alone; and if so, what the Tur wrote "and in such a manner regarding a Jew it is permitted" means regarding prutzim, similar to an idolater; therefore he took "many and their wives" etc., meaning that it helps with many if their wives are with them; and here regarding an idolater these two do not help; and so it is in the Gemara, that we say "but with three of his kind" — regarding Jewish prutzim would it be permitted? And therefore the Tur took both for a stringency regarding a Jew that does not help here; and in vain did my father-in-law of blessed memory dispute the Beit Yosef on this, for even though regarding a Jewish parutz it is permitted even without many when his wife is with him, nevertheless he took here the language of "many" for a greater novelty, that even so regarding an idolater it does not help.
