Without trousers etc. And the Bach challenged this, and this is his language: and I do not know from where he has this; and furthermore, if so, according to this reason even outside of four amos it would also be forbidden; yet in the Mordechai and Hagahos Maimoniyos they permit at a distance of four amos; rather certainly the reason is because of taharah, and there is no distinction between wearing trousers or not; rather within four amos it is forbidden even wearing trousers, and outside of four amos it is permitted even if he is not wearing trousers, end of his words. And in truth his words are correct, but he did not look in Darkei Moshe, where it is explained what compelled him to distinguish thus, and this is his language: and we are not accustomed to be stringent in this time; and it is possible that it is only forbidden in their days when they were accustomed to go without trousers, as the Agudah wrote in the chapter Makom SheNahagu; but nowadays we have no issue with it, end of his words. And nevertheless these words are not compelling; rather it appears to me that it is specifically in the time of the Talmud when they practiced taharah and perishus, as it is in the chapter Mefanin (Shabbat daf 127b) regarding Rabbi Yehoshua who immersed because a tzinora sprayed from the mouth of a matron onto his garments, and Rashi explained: spittle; and they decreed upon the nations that they be like zavim for all their matters, thus far (and so it is implied in Pirkei deRabbi Eliezer that the reason is because of taharah, as the Bach wrote). And now that tumah and taharah are not practiced among us and we are not careful about their spit and their midras, bathing with them is also permitted — so it appears to me. And see the Bach who also wrote that it is specifically in the time when tumah and taharah were practiced, except that afterward he concludes that according to the view of the Mordechai and Hagahos Maimoniyos it is forbidden even in this time, thus far. And it does not appear to me; it is possible that they speak only of a midas chasidus and for one who conducts himself in kedushah and taharah, but not for other ordinary people; and that is what the Mordechai and Agudah wrote in the chapter Af Al Pi, simply, in the name of R' Shimon bar Baruch, that it is permitted to bathe in a bathhouse of families of an idolater and we are not concerned because of taharah; and therefore the world is not careful about this now; and it seems that for this reason the Taz omitted this hagahah.
