In a place where the custom is to go to the bathhouse, and so forth. Rama follows Mordechai at the beginning of Perek Kol HaBasar. Mordechai writes that Rabban Gamliel bathed with a governor, from which it follows that if the Jew entered the bathhouse first and the gentile entered afterward, the Jew may continue bathing with him. But if the gentile entered first, the Jew may not bathe with him, as stated in Pirkei DeRabbi Eliezer. If the Jew distances himself four cubits, it is permitted, as we see regarding excrement, from which one distances four cubits and then prays; we do not say the entire house is considered four cubits. Rama was troubled that we are not careful about this nowadays and answered in Darkei Moshe that in their time gentiles went without undergarments and their nakedness was exposed, while nowadays they cover themselves. He therefore codified the law here this way. I am very surprised. His wording implies that if the Jew entered first, he may bathe with a gentile even when the gentile's nakedness is exposed. It is obvious that this is forbidden, because there is concern the Jew will see him and come to improper thoughts. Pesachim 51 says that two brothers may bathe together, and Rashi explains that we are not concerned that seeing one another naked will lead to homosexual conduct. From there it is clear that where there is actual concern for improper thoughts, such as exposed nakedness, there is a complete prohibition and the Jew must leave. This is unlike Rama's gloss. Although Agudah in Perek Makom Shenahagu writes that in the days of the Sages they bathed without undergarments and that nowadays, when people cover their nakedness, one may bathe even with his father, and Rama cites this in Even HaEzer siman 23, nevertheless they certainly refrained from staring at one another. Nowadays, when people may look at one another, it is plainly forbidden to bathe with anyone whose nakedness is uncovered, even another Jew. As for Rama's question why we do not observe Mordechai's restriction, I am surprised in the opposite direction. I examined Pirkei DeRabbi Eliezer, chapter 29, and it says that Avraham circumcised his servants so that they would not render him impure. If this were a continuing prohibition for us, we should prohibit even touching an uncircumcised gentile, and certainly eating and drinking with him at one table. Rather, Pirkei DeRabbi Eliezer speaks only within the framework of purity and impurity, and Mordechai likewise. We, who do not presently observe those purity laws, have no such bathhouse prohibition. Bathing together is permitted in every circumstance provided the nakedness is covered. If it is uncovered, the Jew must leave even if he entered first. This appears entirely clear to me. My father-in-law also concludes against Rama's gloss, but says that because bathhouse bathing was formally prohibited by a vote when purity laws were observed, another formal vote would be required to permit it. According to his logic, even touching would have been prohibited by that vote, because Pirkei DeRabbi Eliezer places them together. Rather, it is clear there was no independent prohibition to begin with; it was only a distancing measure connected to impurity and the pursuit of purity.
