In a place where the custom is to go to the bathhouse. Mordechai infers from Rabban Gamliel bathing with a governor that if a Jew entered first and a gentile later entered, bathing together was permitted, but if the gentile entered first it was forbidden, unless the Jew remained four cubits away. Rama explained that in earlier times gentiles went without trousers, unlike now when they cover themselves. This is difficult. Even if the Jew entered first, seeing exposed genitals creates forbidden thoughts, as Pesachim 51 indicates regarding brothers bathing together. A Jew must therefore leave whenever the other person is uncovered, Jew or gentile. The rule in Pirkei DeRabbi Eliezer concerns ritual impurity, not inherent prohibition. Since we no longer practice those purity laws, bathing together is permitted in all cases provided the genitals are covered. If they are exposed, the Jew must leave even if he entered first. Bach's suggestion that the old rule requires a formal court to repeal it would likewise prohibit touching gentiles, which is untenable. The original concern was only distancing from impurity.
