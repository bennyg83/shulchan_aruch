Suspected. Be'er Heitev in the name of Turei Zahav: an animal that was sodomized is permitted for eating. So too he wrote in responsum Beit Yaakov siman 118 — except he wrote that nevertheless ab initio it is forbidden to slaughter it in order to eat from it; rather sell it to gentiles. And in a place where gentiles do not eat an animal that was sodomized, it is forbidden to sell to them. Indeed if there is concern the Jew will transgress and slaughter and eat it, there is to permit selling it to a gentile. So too the law of an ox that gored a person and died — ab initio forbidden to slaughter it in order to eat from it, and post factum permitted; see there. And see responsum Yad Eliyahu siman 35, who disagrees with what he wrote that ab initio it is forbidden for a Jew to slaughter an animal sodomized by a gentile — it does not exist; he raised that it is permitted for eating and a mitzvah to slaughter it immediately, and not to sell it nor leave it alive at all. He also wrote one should not write on its hide a Torah scroll, tefillin, and mezuzot, and even straps one should not take from it; and even post factum if they wrote on it one scrapes to say one may not read in it and it requires genizah; see there. And see responsum Mekom Shmuel siman 91.
