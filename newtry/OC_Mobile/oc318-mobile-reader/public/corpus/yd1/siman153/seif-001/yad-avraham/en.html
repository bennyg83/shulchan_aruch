(Siman 153 in Shulchan Aruch seif 1) On bestiality. See Turei Zahav — it implies here is only ab initio prohibition because of lifnei iver, but post factum there is no concern of prohibition regarding permission to eat even when there are witnesses it was sodomized. It appears to me to answer this from what is taught in Sifrei parashat Re'eh: "still not multiplied upon whom a transgression was committed" — through two witnesses — "and still its judgment is not finished" — the Talmud says "ox, sheep, goats and kid goats you may eat" — it implies explicitly that even ab initio it is permitted to eat it before its judgment is finished. So too at the end of Chullin regarding goring. And this is not like what is written in responsum Beit Yaakov siman 118 that those liable for death are forbidden ab initio to slaughter and eat them even before their judgment is finished. And see responsum Tzitz Eliezer siman 46 — there it is in our time when there is no court, but when there was a court it was forbidden ab initio to slaughter it because it must be brought to court for judgment. And so if there are not two witnesses it is permitted at all times. And know that with four gentiles it is not liable for death at all, as the Rambam writes in Hilchot Issurei Bi'ah.
