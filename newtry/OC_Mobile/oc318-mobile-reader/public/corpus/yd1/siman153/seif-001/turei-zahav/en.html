For they are suspected of bestiality. A non-Jew is commanded regarding this because the verse says, 'They shall become one flesh,' excluding animals, whose flesh does not become one with man's. Leaving an animal with him therefore involves placing a stumbling block before the blind. The Gemara at the beginning of Avodah Zarah implies that the prohibition is this concern alone. Once the animal was already left and the transgression occurred, there is no resulting prohibition upon the animal merely from suspicion. The laws of an animal actually used for bestiality require testimony or a definite act. The various cases of purchase, entrusting, and seclusion must be distinguished as set out by the Rishonim.
