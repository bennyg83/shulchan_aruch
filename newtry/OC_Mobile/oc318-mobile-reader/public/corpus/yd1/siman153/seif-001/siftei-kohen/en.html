If exposure were the reason, it should be forbidden even beyond four cubits, whereas Mordechai and Hagahot Maimoniyot permit it beyond four cubits. The true reason, he says, is ritual purity, so within four cubits it is forbidden even with trousers and beyond four cubits permitted even without them. His argument is sound, though Darkei Moshe explains the basis for the distinction: in earlier times people commonly went without trousers, unlike today. Even so, the distinction is not compelling. In Talmudic times people observed purity practices, as with Rabbi Yehoshua immersing after gentile saliva fell on his garment. Now that ritual purity is not practiced and we are not careful regarding their saliva or trampling, bathing with them is permitted. Mordechai and Hagahot Maimoniyot may have spoken only as a pious practice for one who conducts himself in sanctity and purity, not for ordinary people. This also explains why the world is not careful and why Levush omitted this gloss. [Citation 10.] [Citation 50.] [Citation 100.] [Citation 410.]
