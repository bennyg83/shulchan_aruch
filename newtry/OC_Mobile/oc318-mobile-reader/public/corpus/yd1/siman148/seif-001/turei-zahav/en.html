To lend. A question applies to something that returns in kind, such as an object; and a loan applies to money, which is given for expenditure. And all that is mentioned here is that the gentile goes and acknowledges to his idol that Israel needed him; and in the Gemara (7a) they asked: because of profit — Rashi explained: he profits and goes and acknowledges idolatry; and Israel transgresses "he shall not listen at your mouth" — or perhaps because of lifnei iver, etc.; and Rashi explained: he sells him an animal and he offers it to idolatry — the gentile was also commanded regarding idolatry and he will not be punished through you. What difference does it make — he has an animal for the gentile: if because of profit he profits for him; and if because of lifnei iver — behold he has it anyway. And behold according to Rashi and the Rambam whom Shulchan Aruch cited here — that there is prohibition in selling and buying with a gentile — it is very wondrous, for there is a difference anyway: if because of profit it is forbidden even in something not fit for offering; and if because of lifnei iver there is prohibition only in what is fit for offering; and Tosafot already challenged at the beginning of the chapter in the name of R. Tam, and Mahari Abuhav explained R. Tam that in selling and buying the concept of "he goes and acknowledges to an idol" does not apply, as the Tur wrote in his name; and it must be according to Rashi and Rambam that this too can make such profit — rather it teaches a stringency that regarding an animal that is an offering there is also profit, namely that he has it for himself; and the essential question is whether there is concern he acknowledges idolatry in selling and buying — and it is found there is prohibition in everything because of profit; or there is no concern in selling and buying that he will acknowledge — and this is R. Tam's view; and the question was not resolved and we hold stringently that in selling and buying the concept of acknowledging an idol also applies, and one transgresses "he shall not listen at your mouth." And Mahari Chalav challenged: why did Rashi explain that in acknowledging an idol one transgresses "he shall not listen" — why is it not forbidden because of lifnei iver; and he resolved that in acknowledging there is no lifnei iver, for it is like bowing and kissing — the gentile is not warned about it since Israel too is not liable to death for it. And I wondered here: certainly Israel is liable to death for acknowledging idolatry, as stated in chapter Four Deaths (Sanhedrin 60a) — the mishnah: one who says "to you," etc.; and explicitly we say: whoever acknowledges idolatry is like one who denies the entire Torah. And the initial difficulty is not here, for it is explained there in the first chapter of Avodah Zarah (6a) that lifnei iver applies only where the gentile cannot do it except through Israel, like handing a cup of wine to a nazir when he is on opposite sides of the river and the nazir could not take the wine; but if it were possible for him and Israel only assists and brings it near — there is no lifnei iver; and as the Mordechai wrote at the beginning of Avodah Zarah, whom the Rama brought siman 151 regarding selling them things connected to their worship if they can buy elsewhere; and even to be stringent — what he brings there is rabbinic and not from the Torah; but because of "he shall not listen" there is nevertheless concern — for through him it is heard even though it would also be heard without him — nevertheless now it is heard at your mouth.
