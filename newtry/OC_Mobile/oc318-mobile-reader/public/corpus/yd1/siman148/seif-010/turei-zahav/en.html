Therefore it is good to precede him. Apparently this is difficult from what we find in R. Yehoshua ben Levi: no one preceded another in peace, not even a gentile in the market — what "even"? The essential law is so — one must precede so he need not repeat; and one can say there it deals with a gentile he never knew, as the language "in the market" hints; and it is not reasonable he would ask of his welfare and need to repeat; and even here he opens with his welfare because he feared lest the gentile happen to give him peace first; and here it deals with one he knows — and therefore it is by law. Again I saw in Semak siman 133: "and it is forbidden to repeat peace to them — and specifically peace, which is the name of the Holy One, blessed be He, or a blessing in divine language; but another blessing there is no concern" — end.
