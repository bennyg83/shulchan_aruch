And he should throw it in his face into a pit. In the Gemara it is thus in the incident where a heretic sent a dinar to R. Yehuda the Prince on his festival day and said: how shall I act — shall I give it to him? He goes and acknowledges that Israel needs him; shall I not give it to him? It is enmity. R. Yehoshua ben Levi said: take it and throw it in his face into a pit indirectly, so he does not understand you threw it intentionally; and since he sees it is lost he will not rejoice. And in the Rambam it is written: and if he fears enmity he takes it in his face and does not benefit from it; and it appears this "in his face" refers to afterwards — meaning he shows in his face that he does not benefit from it, namely he shows him an angry face at the time of accepting the gift so it appears from it he does not enjoy this; and he accepts it out of fear; and as stated in seif 9 that he should tell him in a soft tone, etc.; and this is a general remedy everywhere — for what is mentioned in the Gemara that he should throw it into a pit applies only when standing beside the pit; but if he goes to the pit the gentile will certainly sense his intent. And so one should explain the Tur's words that wrote "he accepts it and does not benefit from it" — meaning the gentile should not have benefit from what Israel accepts; whatever can be done in his face like this — that he is not happy — and now the Tur's language is settled: "he shall not benefit from it" — for if it referred to Israel he should have said "he shall not benefit from it"; rather certainly it refers to the gentile, that he should not benefit from the gift Israel accepts; and with this the Beit Yosef's wonder on the Rambam and Tur is resolved — why they did not mention as in the Gemara. But it appears that if it is impossible to act before the gentile so he loses it — it is permitted to accept and benefit from it; and proof from what the Rosh wrote here: "evidently if there were no evasion so there not be enmity he would take even though he goes and acknowledges"; and so we say in chapter One Who Is Saved that Rav Yosef permitted midwives with beer because of enmity; and so to raise a gentile from a pit, etc. — end. And behold he compares to those where there too is concern he goes and acknowledges idolatry when he comes out of the pit, as stated siman 124 "her husband is alive," etc.; and nevertheless the beer is permitted; and one can say: from where did the Rosh learn to explain thus — perhaps if he cannot evade he should not accept at all; one can say he deduced from that it says "take and throw it indirectly" and did not say "if you can throw it in his face take, and if not do not take" — rather certainly he takes in any case, only if possible afterwards to throw it in his face he should do so; and so at the end of this siman in the Tur who wrote permission for us to borrow and lend with them because of enmity — and we do not say it should be forbidden to benefit from it; rather as he explained — so it appears clear to me, and not as he understood the Tur's words that in every case benefit afterwards is forbidden:
