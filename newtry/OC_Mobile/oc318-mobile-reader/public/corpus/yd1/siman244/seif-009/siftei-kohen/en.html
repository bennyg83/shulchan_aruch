He does not stand, etc. This implies that it is forbidden to stand until the sage comes within four amos. If the point were only that he is not obligated, that was already explained above in seif 2. Although one stands for the nasi, head of the beit din, or one's primary teacher from the distance at which he can see them, and honor therefore exists even at a distance, there the obligation itself extends that far. For an ordinary sage, where there is no obligation at a distance, it would not be evident that he is standing in his honor and would instead appear that he stood for his own purpose. Bach.
Once he has passed before him, etc. The Rosh and Rashi read that he does not sit until the sage has passed four additional amos beyond him. Bach rules likewise.
As far as his eyes can see. Semak, siman 52, writes that for one's primary teacher the measure is the full range of his sight. The word used denotes 266 amos.
