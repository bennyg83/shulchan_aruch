He may waive — Beit HaTov of Mahariit; and see responsum Chavot Yair siman 202: they asked regarding a Torah scholar who knows to play several kinds of musical instruments — whether he may play a harp before bride and groom, or it is forbidden to degrade himself for honor of his Torah and he cannot waive his degradation; and he wrote that apparently this depends on the dispute between Rambam and Rosh regarding an elder for whom it is not his honor, brought in Choshen Mishpat siman 263 seif 3 — according to Rosh there, that even if he wants to be strict he may not, so too here; and he further wrote that in this all permit and it is a mitzvah, since everyone knows he has no reward for his toil and he acts for Heaven's sake — for Rosh's words apply only to a mitzvah between man and fellow, where Torah exempted the elder and it is not for his honor; unlike the general principle "whoever was occupied with his lost object and claims likewise regarding his fellow's" — Torah commanded only "love your neighbor as yourself," and if he wants to be strict he degrades his Torah to occupy with his own money which one should not degrade; but for mitzvot between Israel and the Omnipresent, all the more a time-caused mitzvah — there is no degradation of elder's or Torah's honor; and whoever conducts himself lightly for a lofty purpose and God's mitzvah, even if not literally a positive commandment but only kindness we learn from "walk in His ways," is praiseworthy and blessing comes upon him — see there.
