The elder takes precedence in every case. One must also say that this applies only when the elder is somewhat wise, as in the opening clause — and certainly so. We are also dealing with a case where the younger person is wiser than him, for otherwise it is obvious. So too it is stated explicitly in the Rosh. One should not raise the difficulty: if so, why was it necessary to state that where the elder is outstanding and the sage is not outstanding, the elder takes precedence — is that not a fortiori from this ruling? One can answer that it teaches us that even so, we require the elder to be somewhat wise. In the name of Maharshal. It is implied from the words of the Rashbam in the chapter of Yesh Nochalin regarding the daughters of Tzelafchad that when neither is outstanding, we follow the years — meaning the one who has more years, even if he is not an elder of seventy years. The reason the elder takes precedence in this case is that there is no diminishment to the sage who is not outstanding when they accord honor to the one opposite him on account of his years.
