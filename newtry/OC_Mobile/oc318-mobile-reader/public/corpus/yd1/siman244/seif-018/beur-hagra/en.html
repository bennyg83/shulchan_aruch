And if the Torah scholar, etc., and if the elderly person, etc. Since the Gemara says, “provided that he is distinguished,” and again, “provided that he is distinguished,” etc., it implies that when one is distinguished and the other is not, everything follows the distinguished person.
And if neither, etc. One cannot explain that the person is equally related to both of them and that both are distinguished. If so, why does the Gemara say, “provided that he is distinguished,” etc.? It should have stated that precise case. Rather, it is certain that in every instance priority follows one of the two, but we do not know which one. Rashbam writes that it is reasonable to follow the elderly person. See Baer HaGolah, who cites this in his name. The Rosh likewise writes all of this.
