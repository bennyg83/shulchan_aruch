Even if he is not, etc. This accords with Rabbi Yose HaGelili, for Issi ben Yehuda necessarily agrees with him. Since Issi expounds “old age” to include every elderly person, the term “elder” cannot refer only to an aged person, but must also bear the meaning assigned to it by Rabbi Yose HaGelili. We rule in accordance with Issi ben Yehuda, as do the Rosh and the Ran, and the Rambam likewise rules this way, unlike the Rif. [There is also clear proof in Ketubot 62b from the incident in which one rose before an elderly non-Jew.]
Even if he is not his teacher. This follows from the statement there on 33b, “First, because you are sages,” etc. Otherwise, their rising would have been proper regardless. Likewise, in Shabbat 31b, “Shall we rise before him,” etc., and in several other incidents. From this the Ran also proved that we rule in accordance with Rabbi Yose HaGelili. Otherwise, since we rule in accordance with Issi ben Yehuda, wisdom would have no practical relevance at all, and why would the Sages repeatedly require one to rise before a Torah scholar?
Only, etc. As explained above from the phrase “First,” etc.; see seif 8.
And likewise, etc. There, the ruling was issued in accordance with Issi ben Yehuda.
Meaning, etc. See chapter 5 of Avot.
Even, etc., provided, etc. See Tosafot, 32b, s.v. “elder,” etc. The Ran likewise writes this, for even with respect to his father he is not obligated in such a case, as stated in the second chapter of Yevamot (22b) and in Sanhedrin; see there.
