An elder, etc. The obligation to stand applies to one who is advanced in years, and also to a chacham, even if he is young, provided he is distinguished in wisdom. The Rama writes that one stands only for one who is outstanding relative to the people of his city, but this does not mean that he must be the greatest of the generation. Rather, anyone who is recognized as a talmid chacham and is greater than the one before him in Torah requires honor. The Ran and other poskim imply that the measure depends on the person standing: if the other is his superior in wisdom, he must rise before him. Nevertheless, for an ordinary elder who is not a chacham, the obligation is because of age, and the details differ. One should therefore be careful to honor both categories: the aged person because of "you shall rise before the aged," and the chacham because of "and honor the face of an elder," which Chazal expound as one who has acquired wisdom.
