Only that he is great, etc. And Tosafot wrote in the first chapter of Kiddushin (page 32 side 2) and Semag siman 32 that we require he be distinguished in wisdom; and the Beit Yosef brings them; and likewise the Rosh wrote there; but nevertheless this is not distinguished, for below seif 10 there he is distinguished in wisdom more than the other sages of his generation, and therefore his law is like a muvhak teacher, as the Beit Yosef wrote there in name of Tosafot chapter Ein Omdin who wrote the greatest of the generation was and distinguished in wisdom — here we mean he is distinguished in wisdom more than the rest of the people; and so appears in Tosafot and the Rosh there, for behold they did not write so except to answer zaken and old age — why need to clean and wise — we mean distinguished in wisdom; and if so he is distinguished more than the rest of the people — see there and examine; and with this it is well what the Tur wrote in name of the Rif, and not like the Bach who wrote the Rif disputes Semag, for the simple meaning of his words does not imply so, rather certainly as the commentators explained — examine; and what the Tur wrote in name of the Rambam (should read RaMaH, and so wrote the Perishah that so is in the old Tur on parchment, and so in Rabbi Yerucham part 2 chapter 5 in name of RaMaH) not like Beit Yosef and Bach who wrote it should be Ramban — again I found so in Beit HaGadol) one who is great in wisdom and fit to learn from him, etc. — deals also distinguished in wisdom from the rest of the people, and he is greater than ordinary people, for otherwise an ignoramus or one small in Torah would be obligated to stand before every person — and this is a matter without dawn; and furthermore the Torah did not obligate standing except before a sage; and it appears for this reason the Tur did not write at the beginning of the siman and in other places in this siman that the sage must be distinguished, for it is obvious he must be distinguished from the rest of the people — examine; and according to these words we explain the Rav's words — know that the Ran also wrote like the RaMaH (and likewise in Darkei Moshe the Ran's view is like the RaMaH that the Tur wrote below), and concludes: and it is possible that even when equal he is exempt, for he is not obligated to stand before a sage except one greater than him, etc. — evidently he deals that he is a sage distinguished more than the rest of the people, for otherwise it is obvious when equal he is exempt — and what "and it is possible," etc., for otherwise every person would be obligated to stand before his equal even if he is not a sage at all; rather certainly as we said, only here RaMaH disputes the Rif, for when not distinguished from the sages of the generation and he is not his teacher at all — it implies the Rif holds even greater than him he is exempt; and to RaMaH he is obligated, for fit to learn — he is like his teacher; however this is a teacher who is not muvhak; and so Rabbi Yerucham explicitly.
