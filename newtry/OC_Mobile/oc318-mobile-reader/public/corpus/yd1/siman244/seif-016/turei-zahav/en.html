Sons of scholars, etc. This is in the Tosefta at the end of tractate Horios. Its explanation appears based on what the Sages said in the chapter of the mitzvah of chalitzah, regarding the incident of R' Yishmael b'R' Yosi and Avdan — that initially R' Yishmael was stepping over the heads of the holy people, and stated the reason: "I am Yishmael the son of R' Yosi; I have come to learn Torah from Rebbi." Afterward a yevamah came before Rebbi, and he told Avdan: "Go out and examine her to see if she is an adult." After he went out, R' Yishmael said in the name of his father: this examination is unnecessary, for with a man we specifically require him to be an adult, but with a woman — whether adult or minor the chalitzah is valid. When Avdan returned, he was stepping over the heads of the holy people. R' Yishmael said: "Whoever is needed by the holy people may step over the heads of the holy people; whoever is not needed by the holy people should not step over the heads of the holy people," etc. It is evident from this that a sage himself may step over the heads of the holy people since they need his Torah — even when another great person is present there. This is the reason for R' Yishmael's stepping, etc. — to indicate by allusion that he too is a sage whom many people need, even though "I have come to learn from Rebbi." But Avdan did not have this distinction; his merit was that he went on Rebbi's mission for a mitzvah matter, and therefore he wanted to step over them. R' Yishmael told him that his mission had been for naught, since the woman did not need examination, and therefore he had no right to step. Rebbi then ruled regarding that matter there. Therefore the Tosefta stated that if the sons of scholars or their students are going on some errand — if that errand serves the public, they have the right to step over others. In this there is no distinction whether the errand was prior to their entering the study hall — they have the right to enter and step — or whether they were already there and are going out for this errand and returning — they have the right to step. What is stated afterward: "If he went out for his needs," etc. — implying that even a sage himself may not step unless he went out for his needs — refers to one who was already there and went out. The Gemara distinguishes that specifically when he went out for minor needs this is discussed, but if he went out for major needs, he is negligent since he should have checked himself beforehand. Rava said: nowadays since people are weak, even for major needs as well. In Tosafos in the chapter of the mitzvah of chalitzah (Yevamos 105), they asked from the passage in the first chapter of Sanhedrin: "a warning to a judge that he should not step over the heads of the holy people." They answered: this means he should not go out when the public does not need him. Alternatively, even when he went out for the public's needs, he should try to shorten his absence as much as possible, as we say "from where do we know that an elder should not trouble," etc. Thus far their words — this is what appears in these matters.
That he enter last. And although Rashi explained in these matters in the verse "your heads upon you" that the head should enter last; meaning he has permission to do so and the congregation is obligated to bear this from him; but he himself should refrain from doing so; an example of standing: although it is a mitzvah, nevertheless we say not to burden the congregation.
