Two Torah scholars, etc. This follows from the statement at the end of the second chapter of Bava Metzia concerning “Torah scholars in Babylonia,” etc. It applies specifically in Babylonia because they learn from one another, but otherwise it does not. As for the account in the second chapter of Shabbat (31b), “Shall we rise before him,” etc., although they were as great as he was, and likewise the passage at the end of the third chapter of Gittin, those refer to showing honor. This is why he writes, “rather,” etc.
And even the teacher, etc. Maharam acted this way, but strictly according to law he need not do so, as stated there in the name of the Jerusalem Talmud: Rabbi Elazar said, “The Torah does not rise before its son,” meaning that a teacher does not rise before his disciple. This also follows from the question posed there in Kiddushin: If one’s son is also his teacher, must the son rise before his father?
