And an ordinary fair, etc. It is obvious to me that this does not include a place where many non-Jews gather and say that their sins are forgiven there ; that is certainly for the sake of idolatry, and it is forbidden to do business with them there.
To their poor. The Derishah wrote: from here it appears that all the more so it is permitted to give to their poor in general when they go from door to door, without needing to say he is giving on account of the living God. The Bach and the Taz wrote that if one says it is for his deity it is forbidden.
That is known. Maharik wrote: specifically where [the proceeds] go from the pocket of a Jew to idolatry; but where there was a tax previously and they were not buying anything from it for idolatry, and then a lord arose and declared that the Jewish money would be given to idolatry ; but the Jews were not required to pay more on this account than what they had been paying before ; it is permitted, since nothing goes from the Jew's own pocket; it is from the pocket of the ruler whose tax it is.
