An ordinary fair, etc. It is obvious to me that this does not include a place where many non-Jews gather and say that their sins are forgiven there — that is certainly for the sake of the idol, and it is forbidden to do business with them there.
And further, it is obvious — for his deity. In the Tur it is written "for his deity" — and this is wondrous: since he said "his," it is certainly for his idolatry. The Rama acted properly in not copying this word "his," and so Mahari Chalav wrote that the word "his" in the Tur should be deleted. And it is written in the Derishah: from here it appears that all the more so it is permitted to give to their poor in general when they go from door to door, without needing to tell him he is giving on account of our living God.
