And even if they waive the toll for him, etc. So too the Tur; and in the Gemara it is in this language: R. Natan says — on the day the idolaters place the toll they proclaim and say: whoever takes a wreath and places it on his head in honor of idolatry — they will waive his toll; and if not — they will not waive his toll. A Jew found there — what should he do? If he places it — he is found benefiting; if he does not place it — he is found causing benefit. And Rashi: "he is found benefiting" — from the smell of the herbs of the wreath; and on "he is found causing" he explained: he gives toll. And Tosafot wrote: why did he not explain also in "he is found benefiting" that they waive his toll and he benefits — he is not called benefiting from idolatry since the money is his — end; and therefore it is wondrous on the Tur who wrote "he is found benefiting" — placing the toll, not like Rashi and Tosafot. And Mahari Chalav extended on this; and in my humble opinion it appears: specifically in that matter the Gemara took — that whoever places the wreath they waive his toll — and it is found he need not ask them to waive his toll; rather on his own he goes without toll, deceiving them that they think he does it for honor of idolatry while he steals their minds and does it for waiving the toll; therefore Tosafot properly wrote he does not benefit in waiving the toll since it is already his; and now he need not hold a favor to them for this — and this is similar to what the Rama wrote siman 157 seif 2, that it is permitted to use ambiguous language with two meanings; and see there siman 157 on what the Rama wrote that not in a place of danger it is forbidden — here it does not imply so, unlike in commerce at a gentile fair where he goes and asks them to waive his toll and they tell him privately that they waive his toll — therefore he properly benefits from it, for she does him a favor and he holds a favor to her for this; and this is precise in the Tur's language that wrote regarding waiving the toll — as appears to me. And it is written in Shelah: in a place where they waive the toll anonymously for every person, as they do today at our fairs where they grant freedom to every person in the market — it does not appear as benefiting from idolatry; and this is proof for what I distinguished here:
