If the road is designated for it — meaning there is no passing from that city. Meaning: he certainly goes to that forest where the idolatry festival is; but if one's route from that city of the festival and beyond is permitted, for people say: according to his route he goes to that city — and so it is explained in Rashi's explanation in the mishnah: "there is no road going out from that city to go to another city, and the conquered road from here to that city is designated for that city alone" — end. And this is simple: what Rashi took "from that city" means the city that has the festival; and so the Ran explicitly in this language — there is no road going out to that city with a lamed; and so wrote Perishah. But Mahari Chalav explained that this "from that city" that the Rama wrote means from the place one departs; and the essential distinction is whether the road going out from here is specifically to the city of the festival, or whether the road from here also belongs to another city — meaning it is possible he will not come to that city at all; and this is without precision, for we stand on the presumption that he certainly goes to that city of the festival, as the mishnah's language implies "what about going there, etc."; and so the Tur's language "forbidden to go there if the road is designated, etc." — rather the permission was that he goes from there to another city; and so in the Raavad's hasagot in explanation: "that if they go from there to another place it is permitted to pass through it to another place initially" — end. However, one should examine why the author of Shulchan Aruch wrote — and these are the Rambam's words — "one who goes from place to place" — why did he take it in such a case; but one who examines the Rambam will see it is explained that he has another explanation in this mishnah, where he wrote: "but if there is another road and he happened and went on it — permitted" — end; meaning: if he wants to go through the city of the festival to another place and there is no other road to his destination except through the city of the festival — it is forbidden for him to benefit from that city by walking through it; but if there is another road to his destination there is no prohibition to go through the city of the festival, for it is not benefit since he could have gone another way; and so in several places; and if so, also the Mechaber who wrote "forbidden to pass through it" — his intent is likewise that it is forbidden to benefit in his walking through there; and if so, the Rama should not have written on this "meaning there is no passing, etc." — for this applies according to Rashi's explanation:
