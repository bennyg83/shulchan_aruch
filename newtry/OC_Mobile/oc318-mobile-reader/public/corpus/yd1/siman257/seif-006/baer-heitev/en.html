After they removed him. Another version states that he is believed even after his removal. The Bach, Taz, and Shach reconcile the versions: if he made the statement immediately after being removed, he is believed. If he said nothing when removed and only spoke a day or two later, when the charity purse was no longer in his possession, he is not believed. The Shach concludes that if he made the statement at the time of removal, even if he did not collect his money until afterward, he receives it without an oath. If he is a trustworthy charity collector renowned for righteousness, one may rule that he is believed without an oath even after his removal and after the purse is no longer in his possession. See siman 50 and siman 136.
