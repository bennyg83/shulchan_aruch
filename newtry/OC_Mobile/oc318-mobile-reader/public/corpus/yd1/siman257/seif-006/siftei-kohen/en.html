But not, etc. This is based on Mordekhai and Maharam Tiktin's gloss to Mordekhai. Another version reads, 'even after they removed him,' and the Bach writes that this is the primary reading. Even according to that version, it may mean that although he had not previously stated that he had lent money to the charity fund, after they removed him he said, 'I lent such and such,' and is believed. Yet perhaps this applies only if he made the statement at the time of his removal. If he said nothing then and only one or two days later, when the charity purse was no longer in his possession, made the claim, he would no longer be believed. Still, the unqualified phrase 'even after they removed him' implies that he is believed in every circumstance, since no distinction is stated. For practical ruling, the matter requires study. Regarding a collector who is known to be upright and renowned for his righteousness, one may rule that he is believed without an oath even after removal and when the charity purse is no longer in his possession.
Not afterward, etc. This means where he said nothing at the time of his removal about having advanced money. But if he stated it when removed, then even if he did not collect repayment until afterward, he may collect without an oath. This also appears from the Bach. See there.
