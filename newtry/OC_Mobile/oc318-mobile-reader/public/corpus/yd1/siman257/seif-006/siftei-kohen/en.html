But not so, etc. From Mordekhai and Maharam of Tikotin gloss in Mordekhai that in the second version it states even after they removed him — and similarly Bach that this version is primary. Possibly even according to this version the explanation is: even though before this he said nothing that he lent to the charity fund, only after they removed him he said such and such I lent — he is believed; however specifically when he said so at the time they removed him, but if he said nothing at the time they removed him, only after a day or two when the charity fund is not in his hand he is no longer believed. But the wording "even after they removed him" without qualification implies that in every case he is believed since it did not distinguish; and for practical law it requires investigation. However regarding a fit gabbai renowned for his righteousness one may rule he is believed without an oath even after they removed him and the fund is not in his hand — end quote.
