This is incorrect. The conclusion, “to deposit it with his wife,” also implies that the reason is the principle that one who deposits property does so with the understanding that it may be entrusted to the custodian's wife and children. This too is incorrect. Maharik discusses a woman whose husband deposited a sum of money with her and authorized her to use the profits as she wished. She then deposited the principal with another person. Maharik ruled that she acted improperly, because her husband entrusted only her personally. He raised a difficulty from the incident at the end of the chapter HaChovel concerning money sent to Rav Yosef in Pumbedisa. Rav Yosef was the communal scholar there, and he deposited it with another person. The reason was that since the money had been sent relying upon Rav Yosef, he could do with it whatever he wished, and certainly could deposit it with another. One might therefore argue that the same applies here, since the donor explicitly made everything dependent upon the wife and delivered it relying upon her. The cases are not comparable. There, everything that arrived did so relying upon Rav Yosef. Here, although the deceased authorized his wife regarding the profits, he did not authorize her to act similarly with the principal. He wanted the principal to remain specifically in his wife's custody, even if the other person was exceptionally righteous. Since she deviated from his instruction, the appointment that she made for the other person is void. These are Maharik's words. It follows that there is no distinction between a wife and the communal scholar. Rather, if a person was entrusted with the principal and authorized to do whatever he wished with the profits, he has no power to deposit the principal with another person, only the profits. If, however, he was also authorized to do whatever he wished with the principal, he certainly may entrust it to anyone he chooses. The entire matter depends solely upon this. The words of the Shulchan Aruch here are therefore imprecise. The Levush followed it and wrote that the reason a communal scholar may deposit the money with another is that he is occupied and certainly will not personally attend to it. It is clear that he did not examine Maharik's responsum, siman 7, which is the source of this law.
