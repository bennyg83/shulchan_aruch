Nevertheless, in order to, etc. As stated in the Midrash and in Tanchuma at the beginning of Parashas Pekudei: another interpretation, 'A faithful man' refers to Moshe, who became treasurer over the work of the Mishkan, etc. Although Moshe was treasurer by himself, he called others and made the accounting through them, as it is stated, 'These are the reckonings of the Mishkan which were reckoned at Moshe's command.' It does not say 'which Moshe reckoned,' but 'which were reckoned at Moshe's command,' through Moshe, by the hand of Itamar, etc. Our Rabbis taught: one who entered to withdraw funds from the Temple treasury did not enter wearing a garment with a hem, etc., because a person must be innocent in the eyes of people just as he must be innocent before Hashem, as it is stated, 'and you shall be innocent,' etc. When the work of the Mishkan was completed, Moshe said to them, 'Come, and I will make an accounting before you.' Moshe said, 'These are the reckonings of the Mishkan: so much was spent on the Mishkan,' etc. At that moment all Israel was reconciled regarding the work of the Mishkan. What caused this? The fact that he sat and satisfied them. This is the meaning of 'These are the reckonings,' etc.
