(Siman 257 Taz note 6) He challenged the author for not being correct, for Mahariik's language there is plainly that he could entrust it to another hand, for he is a city partner — everything that comes is by his intent, etc.; and all the more R. Yosef could entrust the purse to whomever he wished, since he could do whatever he wanted with it — this is obvious. And you might say: so too — behold Perishah on one who gives explained that everything depends on her and by her intent he entrusted and gave; if so, she too can entrust to whomever she wishes, etc. — plainly they are not comparable, for there everything that came was by R. Yosef's intent, but here, although she was given authority over the profit from the hundred perutot, nevertheless over the principal she was not given authority at all; and we must say he does not want the deposit of a hundred perutot in another hand besides his wife — end. Behold explicitly he distinguishes in unspecified cases between his wife and a city partner: for a city partner, presumably his intent is to do with it whatever he wishes; but for his wife in unspecified cases we do not say so — this is the author's view, that he deals with unspecified cases; thereby there is a distinction between his wife and a city partner. And what is written in the conclusion "he may entrust to his wife" is a continuation of the language of the beginning, and the same applies to another hand, for Beit Yosef wrote explicitly that a city partner may entrust them to whomever he wishes; and so too Taz explicitly.
