Charity collectors who have no poor people to distribute to combine the coins with others, but not for themselves; and likewise if they need to sell what they collected from the daily distribution, they sell to others but not to themselves, because of suspicion. They do not reckon charity with charity collectors, nor hekdesh with treasurers, as it says, "And they did not reckon with the men into whose hand they delivered the money... for in faithfulness they dealt" (Rambam there): {Rama: Nevertheless, to be clean before the Lord and before Israel, it is good to give an accounting. (Tur.) And all this is with fit collectors; but one who is unfit, or was appointed in childhood and by presumption, must give an accounting — and the same applies to all public funds. (Mahariiv siman 173.) And when the public wishes, they may remove the collector and appoint another without suspicion — and the same for other funds. (Kol Bo.)}
