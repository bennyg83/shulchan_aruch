If charity collectors have no poor people to whom to distribute the funds, they may exchange the collected coins for larger denominations with others, but not for themselves. If they must sell food collected for the communal kitchen, they may sell it to others but not to themselves, because of suspicion. No accounting is demanded from trustworthy charity collectors or Temple treasurers, as it is written, “They did not reckon with the men into whose hands they delivered the money to give to the workers, for they acted faithfully.” Rama: Nevertheless, so that they remain clean before Hashem and Israel, it is proper that they provide an accounting. This applies to trustworthy collectors. One who is not trustworthy, or who assumed office through force and intimidation, must provide an accounting; the same applies to all public officers. When the community wishes, it may remove a collector and appoint another, and this does not imply suspicion. The same applies to other public appointees. See Mahariv, siman 173.
