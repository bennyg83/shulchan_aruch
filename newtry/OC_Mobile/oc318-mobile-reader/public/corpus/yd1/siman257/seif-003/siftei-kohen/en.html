And he leaves it until he finds poor people. He need not search for poor people, even if three festivals have passed. Rather, he waits until poor people present themselves. This is the view of the Ran and apparently also of the Bach.
He then transgresses immediately if poor people are present, etc. This does not mean that he must know that poor people are actually present. Once the charity collector demands payment, he becomes obligated immediately, for the collector may need to reimburse himself or others for money advanced for the poor. Thus he is obligated even if no poor people are presently there. The qualification excludes only a case where he knows that the collector will merely hold the money and has no present need for it, in which case he does not transgress. This is implied by the Mordekhai. See there and consider carefully.
He must inform the charity collector, etc. The Mordekhai writes in the first chapter of Bava Batra that one who pledged charity through the prayer leader in the synagogue does not transgress until the collector demands payment, since the collector already knows that he owes charity.
But if he said, 'I will give a sela,' etc. The Mordekhai writes in the first chapter of Bava Batra that one who vows to give to whichever poor people he chooses transgresses immediately, because poor people are present and he is able to give to them. He did not vow specifically to give through the charity collector. He cannot claim that he does not wish to give to these poor people but to others who will arrive later, for then he could exempt himself forever. The Beit Yosef and Darkhei Moshe cite this.
