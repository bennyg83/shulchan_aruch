And sets aside. He does not need to search after poor people even if three festivals passed; rather, he waits until poor people come his way. This is the view of the Ran and the Bach.
Are present. The Shach wrote: one should not say that he must know that poor people are present, for once the charity collector demands it from him he is obligated immediately, since perhaps the collector needs to repay himself or others for what they lent for the needs of the poor. Rather, it comes to exclude where he knows that the collector will leave it with him and has no need for it at all; then he does not transgress.
To inform. The Mordechai wrote: where one vowed through the cantor in synagogue to give charity, since the collector knows that he owes charity, he does not transgress until the collector demands it from him.
To so-and-so. Where he vowed to give to the poor, to whomever he wishes, he transgresses immediately, for poor people are present and he has the ability to give to them, since he did not vow in order to give to the collector. One should not say that he does not transgress because he can say, "I do not want these, but others who will come," for if so, he would exempt himself forever in this way; end quote, Mordechai.
