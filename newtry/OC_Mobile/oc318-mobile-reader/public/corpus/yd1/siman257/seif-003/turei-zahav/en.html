But any person may set it aside, etc. The Tur writes in the name of the Rambam that if no poor people are present, he should set the money aside and retain it until a poor person is found. If he stipulated that he would not give it until he found a poor person, he need not set it aside. The Tur challenged this; see there. It appears that the Rambam's intent is that when a person vows charity, his vow includes both giving and setting aside the money. Thus, if giving is presently impossible, he must at least fulfill the requirement of setting it aside. If, however, he stipulated that he would not give until he found a poor person, we assess his intent as seeking exemption from setting it aside as well. Otherwise, what purpose would the condition serve? It is obvious that when no poor person is present he cannot give it, for to whom would he give it? He must therefore have intended to exempt himself from setting it aside as well. This resolves the Tur's objection that the Rambam appears to hold that a condition is effective only when no poor people are present, which the Tur rejected. The Rambam likewise holds that a condition is effective even when poor people are present, such as when he stipulates that he will distribute the money gradually, a little at a time. That is obvious. The Rambam's novelty is that with the condition he describes, even setting the money aside is unnecessary, whereas without a condition it must be set aside. This appears to be the primary practical ruling.
