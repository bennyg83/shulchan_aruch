When there is a deficiency, etc. Yerushalmi, chapter 6 of Kesubos, halacha 5: “One who gives a marriage portion to an orphan girl,” etc. R' Chanina said: This teaches that we tell the trustees to borrow. He explains it where there is no money in the fund: they borrow up to fifty. But if there is money in the fund, they add. R' Yosi said: This teaches that we do not tell the trustees to borrow. He explains it where there is money in the fund; but if there is no money in the fund, they reduce it. This is like the case of a girl in the days of R' Ami. He said: Set it at the festival. R' Zeira said to him, etc.: Can the master of the festival endure? The view of R' Chanina follows R' Zeira, and that of R' Yosi follows R' Ami. It appears that R' Ami accepted R' Zeira's words.
When he finds it, etc., and he need not, etc. There, chapter 3 of Gittin, halacha 7: “One who lends,” etc. The members of R' Nechemiah's household borrowed coins from the community. A dispute came before the Rabbis. They said to him: Is every community wealthy? Is every community poor?
One who sets aside, etc., and only, etc., and there is no need to be concerned, etc. The Mishnah and Gemara in Gittin, at the end of chapter 3, daf 30a: “One who lends,” etc., and “he need not be concerned,” etc. There, daf 30b, it says, “the rich person became poor,” etc. But there it says that this applies only where he knows them personally or grants it to them through another, and Rambam ruled so; see Shach, seif katan 13. It further says there that if they die, he obtains permission from the heirs. However, Ramban wrote that this is only regarding a kohen or levi, but it does not help regarding a poor person, as stated in the Yerushalmi there: R' Chiyya bar Ukva said in the name of R' Yehoshua ben Levi: The Mishnah concerns heirs of kehunah and leviim, but regarding heirs of a poor person, a poor person has no inheritance. Bar Kappara taught: There is no person who does not come to this situation, if not he, then his son, etc. See Sefer HaTerumos, end of gate 65. The Gemara there also says that if he lent through a rabbinical court, he sets aside on the presumption of the poor of Israel. Tosafos there, s.v. “Alternatively,” says that this is only on the presumption of the poor of that city.
