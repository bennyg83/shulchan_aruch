The charity collector must borrow, etc. See above, siman 250, seif 2.
His tithes, etc. The same applies to other charity. So state the Sefer HaTerumot and other authorities.
And he lent the poor person from his own funds, etc. Meaning, he lent the poor person against the gifts that he expected later to receive from him, intending repayment from those gifts. He may therefore separate his tithe or charity on the poor person's behalf and regard it as though he gave it to him, after which the poor person repays the loan with it. He may consequently retain the funds for himself as repayment. If the poor person later dies or becomes wealthy, the lender need not pay, because the loan was not made on condition that the poor person repay from his own assets. Nor can he now separate the funds on the poor person's behalf, since he can no longer confer ownership upon him after his death or enrichment. He cannot separate them retroactively for the period when the recipient was alive and poor, because one may not separate tithes for property that has been lost.
He separates his own tithe. He must confer ownership of it upon the poor person through another individual and then collect his debt from him, thereby fulfilling the commandment, 'You shall surely give.' If he regularly gives to this poor person, the recipient is comparable to a recognized recipient of priestly or Levitical gifts, and such formal transfer is unnecessary. So write the Sefer HaTerumot, end of gate 85, the Bartenura at the end of chapter 3 of Gittin, and the Rambam, Laws of Tithe 6:6. At first glance this is difficult, for at the end of chapter Kol HaGet, Gittin 30a, Ulla explains that the mishnah follows Rabbi Yose, who holds that the Sages treated one who has not acquired as though he had acquired. According to this, no formal transfer should be necessary. Although the Gemara says that the other Amoraim did not answer like Ulla because they would not establish the mishnah according to an individual opinion, Tosafot explain that they merely preferred to establish the mishnah even according to the majority. For practical law, however, we follow Rabbi Yose, as Shmuel himself rules in the first chapter of Bava Metzia, page 12. Perhaps this is why the Rama states without qualification that one may separate on the poor person's behalf and does not say that ownership must first be conferred through another. It appears, however, that the Sefer HaTerumot, Bartenura, and Rambam follow the Rashba's second explanation: the Gemara means that Rabbi Yose himself did not apply the principle of treating one who has not acquired as though he had acquired in a case like this. Tosafot in the first chapter of Bava Metzia similarly explain that the law follows Rabbi Yose only in the case discussed there, not in the case in Gittin. See there.
Or if he became wealthy, etc. The Sefer HaTerumot writes there in the name of Ramban, citing the Yerushalmi, that if one lent money to a community, even if some members are wealthy or some later died, there are presumably still poor members. He may rely upon the poor among them. The same is implied by the glosses to Mordekhai in the first chapter of Bava Batra.
There is no concern that he may have become wealthy. Likewise, there is no concern that he may have died. Sefer HaTerumot there, based on the Gemara in Gittin 30a.
