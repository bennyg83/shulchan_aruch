Found in ha-meses. Be'er Heitev what he wrote that Bach and Shach and the acharonim equated the law of the kurkavan to the law of ha-meses, etc., and if not — kosher even when punctured from both sides — until here his language. And he intends to say according to the Mechaber's view in siman 48 seif 5; which is not so for us — even in ha-meses and beit hakosot treifah even without koret dam, as Rama wrote there. And nevertheless there is a practical difference even for us according to what Shach wrote there that in great loss one may declare kosher when there is no koret dam; if so, in the kurkavan too the law is thus. And see in responsum Toldot Yitzchak siman 5 who raised that in the kurkavan one should be stringent even in great loss, per Taz's view (and so is Peri Megadim's view in siman 48). However, this is specifically when it is known that the kurkavan was punctured from both sides only that there is no koret dam at all — not from within, not from without, not rust, not any mark; but if there is doubt whether the kurkavan was also punctured from the outside and it is impossible to check it — such as if it was lost after a needle was found in it, or it was cut in a way that it cannot be checked — one may declare kosher in great loss; see there where he elaborated the reason of the matter. And see what he wrote above siman 48 s.k. 8 in his name, and see there further in s.k. 13. [And see in responsum Chatam Sofer siman 51 regarding that they found in a goose's kurkavan stuck in the outer membrane half a needle, and the other half of the needle was stuck in the kurkavan's interior opposite the place of the outer insertion — but the inner membrane and also half the thickness of the outer until near the place of the outer half-needle was intact and fine, and no mark of koret dam or rust was found around the place of the needle. And he wrote: behold there are three laws in this. One: if a needle is found in half the outer membrane and koret dam opposite it inside and the flesh intact between the koret dam and the place of the needle such that certainly it went from inside to outside and healed — in this Peri Megadim wrote end of siman 48 that it is only a safek treifah, since the inner certainly healed — perhaps it healed before the needle punctured outward, and then the sealing of the membrane helps, and this is clear. Two: all this if there was no koret dam from outside; indeed if there was no koret dam and no rust from outside — it appears simple that one who is lenient in great loss does not lose, since in any case most poskim hold to be lenient even in the kurkavan when there is no koret dam from outside, for we lean that after slaughter it went out to the outside; and although we hold like Rama that even in ha-meses it is forbidden because we are not expert — all the more in the kurkavan; nevertheless here there is also that doubt mentioned above, that even if it went out to the outside while alive — perhaps it went out after the inner membrane healed; if so, one who is lenient does not lose. Three: when there is no koret dam inside — even if there is also none outside — nevertheless forbidden, for we lean that it came from outside; and now even if it entered into the kurkavan after slaughter because there is no koret dam at the puncture site — nevertheless before slaughter it was inside the body cavity, and we are concerned for inner organs punctured by thorn and needle. And all this is simple. And now in the case of the question: if we judge the half-needle that was stuck inside opposite the insertion of the needle from outside like koret dam inside — behold it is permitted without hesitation as above in section two; and if we do not judge it like koret dam — behold treifah as above in section three; and he elaborated a little on this and concluded: if this law had come before me I would not have instructed to permit — see there.]
