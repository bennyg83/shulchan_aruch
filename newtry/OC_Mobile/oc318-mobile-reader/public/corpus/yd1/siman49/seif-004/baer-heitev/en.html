To leniency. For it is a twofold doubt: perhaps punctured through illness, and perhaps one of the inner organs was not punctured. And Taz disputes this and rules to prohibition, for this is not a twofold doubt because it is a twofold doubt that cannot be reversed. And Peri Chadash wrote: even though it cannot be reversed it is called a twofold doubt — see there that he refutes Taz's proofs and rules like Shulchan Aruch to be lenient. And see there and in Sefer Nekudot HaKesef. Tur wrote that one must not remove the kereis; and Taz wrote: it appears to me that the law of flesh covering most of the kereis also does not apply here; and proof for this he found in Darkei Moshe: incident with a goose that had a wound in its belly like two large eggs, and they opened its belly and saw the membranes intact and declared it kosher even though the skin was torn; and nevertheless it appears that there is concern here as the sages are concerned in the esophagus siman 33 seif 8 — namely that there is concern that it was punctured by a thorn.
