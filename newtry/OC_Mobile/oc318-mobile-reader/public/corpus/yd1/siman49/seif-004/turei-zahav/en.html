We lean to leniency. In Beit Yosef so too in name of Ohel Mo'ed that he brought this also to be stringent, and Beit Yosef ruled to be lenient from the reason of a twofold doubt: perhaps it was done through illness or a thorn, and even if through a thorn — perhaps it did not make a puncture in one of the inner organs; and so too Rama in siman 51. And Darkei Moshe wrote: and I say — certainly if the doubt is because it is impossible to clarify whether it is through a thorn or through itself, then one may be lenient; but if it is possible to clarify only that the sage who came before him is not expert to see whether it is through a thorn or through illness — this is not called a doubt at all, and it is a single doubt and treifah, end of his words. And I have great astonishment on this, for Tosafot wrote and Rama brought them in siman 33 seif 4 regarding the esophagus that we require that it be known that it was done through illness; but if there is concern that it was punctured through a thorn — even if only the inner was punctured — treifah, for we are concerned perhaps the outer was also punctured, etc.; and why do we not also say there a twofold doubt through illness or thorn, and even if through a thorn — perhaps the outer was not punctured. And one may answer that there is a distinction between concern that perhaps it healed on the outside and concern that perhaps one of the inner organs was punctured; on the contrary it is a fortiori: in concern that perhaps it healed there is a dispute in the gemara whether we are concerned for it, and so among the poskim; but in concern that perhaps one of the inner organs was punctured — all agree that we are concerned; and if so all the more one may be lenient in siman 33 if you are lenient here. And therefore it appears to me as those who are stringent who bring Ohel Mo'ed; and there is a great reason here, for we do not say a twofold doubt, as written in Sefer Halichot Olam that we do not say a twofold doubt except where it can be reversed, like that which Rish Lakish interpreted in Ketubot: doubt whether she was unfaithful beneath him, doubt whether she was not beneath him, and even if beneath him — doubt whether by force doubt whether willingly — one can reverse: doubt willingly doubt by force, even if willingly doubt beneath him; and only in such a case do we say a twofold doubt. And here you cannot reverse the twofold doubt, for when you say doubt whether one of the organs was punctured, doubt whether it was not punctured, and even if punctured — again you have no doubt to be lenient; therefore it appears clear that one must be stringent here, even though I am not fit to dispute the great lights Beit Yosef and Rama; nevertheless the matters are clarified as written. In this siman Tur wrote that one must not remove the kereis, etc.; it appears to me that the law of flesh covering most of the kereis also does not apply here; and proof for this I found in Darkei Moshe who wrote: there was an incident with a goose that had a wound in its belly like two large eggs, and they opened its belly and saw that the membranes were intact and they declared it kosher even though the skin was torn, end of his words. And nevertheless it appears to me that there is concern here that we should be concerned as we are concerned in the esophagus siman 33 seif 8 — namely that there is concern that it was punctured by a thorn; and see immediately after this.
