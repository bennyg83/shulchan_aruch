Kosher. For the kurkavan rests calmly as it stands. Shach wrote: the same applies if both membranes of beit hakosot were punctured not opposite one another — kosher. (One found in a bowl a chicken cut into pieces, and in the kurkavan a needle was stuck and it was not discernible whether the needle punctured both membranes or only the inner or only the outer alone. And if only the outer — it would also be treifah because it pierced the body cavity; but the doubt was that perhaps only the inner alone was punctured — then it is kosher — and it was not discernible because the kurkavan was cooked. He forbade for that day the food in the bowl, for it is only a single doubt; but if the needle rests on the kurkavan and is not stuck and no rust is found on the needle — kosher, for it is a twofold doubt. And Peri Chadash disputes and says: even if there is rust there we lean, for rust comes from elsewhere — see there; and see Taz siman 41 s.k. 18; and see responsum Beit Yosef siman 61 and in responsum Chavot Binyamin question 48.)
