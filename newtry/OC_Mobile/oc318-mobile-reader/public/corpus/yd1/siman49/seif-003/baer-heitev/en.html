In ha-meses. Darkei Moshe wrote, and Shach brings it: it appears that specifically regarding puncture from within and not punctured entirely its law is like ha-meses and beit hakosot; but if punctured entirely — treifah even if no koret dam is found around the needle. And that which he omitted this in the hagahah is because he wrote above siman 48 seif 8 that even in beit hakosot one should practice prohibition when punctured from both sides even if there is no koret dam, for we are not expert; but granted in ha-meses and beit hakosot whose membrane is soft one can say that after slaughter something so slight was punctured. But the flesh of the kurkavan is thick — one does not say that perhaps it was punctured in such a brief moment; rather it was entirely punctured while alive. But if punctured from within to the outside it is simple that one must investigate whether there is koret dam outside opposite it — forbidden; and the same applies even if the needle merely rests inside it and there is koret dam from outside — forbidden, except that then one need not check from outside whether there is koret dam. And Bach and Peri Chadash and Shach and the acharonim equated the law of the kurkavan to the law of ha-meses and beit hakosot even regarding koret dam: if koret dam is found — treifah; if not — kosher, even when punctured from both sides. Radbaz wrote in his responsa siman 58: a needle found stuck in a goose's kurkavan passing above the diaphragm the measure of half a finger and not discernible except after cooking; and at the time of salting and rinsing and washing it was not discernible at all and they did not feel the needle. From the essence of the law it is permitted, for we lean that afterward the needle fell into the kurkavan and became stuck there. In practice one should be stringent, end of his words. And Peri Chadash wrote: in my opinion this is excessive stringency, since the needle was so long that it passed above the diaphragm half a finger — it is far that if it were there before cooking they would not feel it; therefore even in practice we instruct to be lenient. And Taz also wrote: and if punctured from within alone one must investigate whether there is koret dam from outside opposite it that is forbidden; and the same law applies even if it merely rests in its usual way inside it — except that then one need not check from outside whether there is koret dam there, since we have no companion for it inside — which is not so when stuck from within.
