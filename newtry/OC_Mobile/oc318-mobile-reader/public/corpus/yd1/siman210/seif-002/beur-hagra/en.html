And some say, etc. As in chapter 1 of Nedarim (8a) — "they ostracized him in a dream," etc.; and Rambam's text, except he only counts up to ten who know to read. The first reasoning holds it is not like there, for there it is a curse from heaven and requires drawing near; but one who swore — even awake we require mouth and heart equal — all the more in a dream where there is neither mouth nor heart. "And some say" — this is Rashba, who wrote for mere concern one should be stringent even in an oath in a dream; it appears to my humble opinion — if a woman swore with self-affliction, annulment by her husband on the day he hears within twenty-four hours applies, lest this oath — which is a mere stringency — be preferable to an oath while awake; and even though for requiring ten initially they were more stringent than awake where three suffice — nevertheless one should not learn stringency except what Magen Avraham wrote — the husband cannot annul since they swore him from heaven; and I found Maharal of Prague who answered practically like Rosh — no annulment at all.
However, etc. — so too Rashba, not like the Geonim; and his proof from what is written in chapter HaRo'eh (55b): one who sees a dream, etc., "they will treat him well before three." And all the more so according to Rambam's version that even for "they excommunicated him" it suffices thus, as written above siman 334 seif 35.
