Likewise Rosh — specifically "they ostracized him in a dream" the Gemara said requires annulment by ten men since they distanced him from heaven and requires drawing near; but one who swore — even awake we require mouth and heart equal — all the more in a dream where there is neither mouth nor heart. "And some say" — this is Rashba, who wrote for mere concern one should be stringent even in an oath in a dream; it appears to my humble opinion — if a woman swore with self-affliction, annulment by her husband on the day he hears within twenty-four hours applies, lest this oath — which is a mere stringency — be preferable to an oath while awake; and even though for requiring ten initially they were more stringent than awake where three suffice — nevertheless one should not learn stringency except what Magen Avraham wrote — the husband cannot annul since they swore him from heaven; and I found Maharal of Prague who answered practically like Rosh — no annulment at all.
