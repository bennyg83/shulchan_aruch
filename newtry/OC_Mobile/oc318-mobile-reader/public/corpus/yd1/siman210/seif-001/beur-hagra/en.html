He is permitted in both cases. As written in chapter 3 of Terumot: "one who intended to say, etc." — the Rosh; and see there [in the Rosh's responsa]; and so wrote Rambam: "one who intended to vow [to become] a nazirite and vowed [to bring] a sacrifice; [to bring] a sacrifice and vowed [to become] a nazirite; with a shevuah and vowed with a neder; or who vowed with a neder and swore — [and one who said] figs and said grapes — he is permitted in both cases and there is no vow here." And this is from the Tosefta, chapter 5 of Nedarim. Or barley bread. For that is also included, as written in siman 217, seif 19. Darkei Moshe.
And if he vowed, etc. His intent is to resolve the difficulty of the commentators from what is written in Nedarim 25a: "and even though we rule, etc." — and likewise we say there (25a): "he said to him: when one swears [he does so] on the understanding that, etc. — and we, etc." But there we say [this is] implied by the obligation of tzitzit, etc. — and only something remote from [the literal] meaning he is unable to claim; but when he explicitly says "on his understanding" then even regarding something within the [obvious] meaning he cannot say [otherwise] — see there and Shevuot 29a. And it is in such a case that Rambam speaks; his language in chapter 2 of the Laws of Oaths: "one who swore and said, etc. — 'and on your understanding I swear' — he cannot then claim 'such and such was in my heart.'" And so wrote Tosafot there, 26b, s.v. "he concluded — and had we said, etc." But in an unspecified [case] one may claim a condition [in one's heart]; as written in Masekhet Kallah: "R. Akiva would swear with his lips while nullifying in his heart." And all the more so, one who vows on his own can claim: "there was a condition in my heart" — as written in chapter 6 of the Tosefta of Taharot: "trusted on his own: how so? He said to him: 'you have vowed' — and he replied: 'there was a condition in my heart' — we listen to him." And so it is in Torat Kohanim (Parashat Vayikra): "he said to him: 'you are a nazirite, etc.'" (And see above, siman 232, seif katan 35.)
