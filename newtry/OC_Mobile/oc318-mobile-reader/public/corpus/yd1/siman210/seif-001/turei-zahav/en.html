And he uttered with his lips unspecified bread. Rosh wrote — regarding what we learn "one vows for cheremim and killings" (below siman 232 seif 14) — words of the heart count as words regarding an ones; it implies when not ones we do not follow words of the heart. He answered in Ramban's name — it is different there, for there is no error in his words since he intended in his heart to say with his mouth "fruits shall be forbidden forever"; rather he truly thinks only today — therefore his mouth is preferable, for words of the heart are not words. But here his utterance was in error, for in his heart he meant wheat bread — therefore his utterance is nothing, for by right his thought too should be nullified since he did not utter his thought; but rabbinically from the verse "everything that proceeds from his mouth" we follow his thought whenever his mouth does not contradict — wheat bread is also called bread — end quote. Thus appears Tur's explanation, who wrote "and he uttered with his lips unspecified bread" — meaning not intending to utter unspecified bread, for he concluded in his heart wheat bread — why should he conclude in his heart to say unspecified specifically; rather plainly he forgot to complete the word wheat. Tosafot raised this difficulty in chapter 3 of Shevuot (27) and answered in one answer — that "one vows for killings" deals with an am ha'aretz, and here with a Torah scholar; and this is what Tur wrote here "and some say" — specifically a Torah scholar, but an am ha'aretz — Rosh z"l did not say so — end quote; meaning as Ramban we mentioned, also in Mordechai in Raavyah's name wrote thus, and Beit Yosef brought it — except he wrote the Gemara's plain meaning does not appear so; and it is astonishing on him — if so how are Tosafot's and Rosh's difficulties we mentioned answered; and thus Rashal wrote — specifically if he erred in what he said unspecified bread; but if he intentionally says unspecified bread we follow his mouth and forbidden also in barley, and words of the heart are not words.
