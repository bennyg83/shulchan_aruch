Rosh wrote: the fact that the mishnah states "one may make vows to murderers and bandits" (below siman 232 seif 14) — where his words in the heart constitute his actual words vis-à-vis the coercer — implies that without coercion we do not follow words in the heart. He answered in the name of Ramban: that case is different, for there is no error in his words — he intended in his heart to say with his mouth "let produce be forbidden to you forever," but mentally intended specifically "only for today"; hence his verbal statement prevails, for words in the heart are not words. But here, where his verbal utterance was erroneous — having intended in his heart to say "wheat bread" — therefore his verbal utterance amounts to nothing; and by strict law even his intention would be voided since he did not verbalize his intention; except that from the verse "whatever he utters" we derive that we follow his intention whenever his verbal statement does not contradict it — since "wheat bread" is also called "bread" — end of quote. And so appears the intent of the Tur who wrote "and he verbalized 'bread in general'" — meaning he did not intend to verbalize "bread in general," for he had concluded in his heart to say "wheat bread"; and why would he conclude in his heart to verbalize specifically "in general"? Rather it is obvious that he forgot to complete the word "wheat." And Tosafot raised this very difficulty in chapter 3 of Shevuot (27) and answered in one interpretation that the case of "vowing to murderers" involves an ignorant person while here it is a Torah scholar; and this is what the Tur wrote: "and some say this applies specifically to a Torah scholar, but my father the Rosh did not write so" — meaning the Rosh follows Ramban's approach we mentioned. And Mordechai too wrote this in the name of Raaviah, cited by Beit Yosef — except that he wrote that the plain sense of the Talmud does not appear so; and this is puzzling, for how then would the difficulty of Tosafot and Rosh be resolved? And so Maharshal wrote: specifically if he erred in saying "bread in general"; but if he says "bread in general" with intention, we follow his verbal statement and he is forbidden even regarding barley — and words in the heart are not words.
