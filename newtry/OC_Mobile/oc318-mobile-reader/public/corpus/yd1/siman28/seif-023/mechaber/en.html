All things in which seeds that are sown in them grow — they are included as earth and one may cover with them; and if they do not grow, if they are called earth one covers with them; therefore one covers with manure, and with very fine sand until a potter need not crush it at all, and with lime, with clay, with brick, with earthenware body that was crushed, with powdered stones, with powdered potsherds, with fine flax tow, with wood shavings, with burned food or garments until they become earth, with charcoal and coal blue, with refuse of mills — but one does not cover with manure that is still moist, nor with sand a potter must crush, nor with flour, bran, or middlings, nor with powdered metal if not burned — except ground gold which is called earth as written "and dust of gold to him"; and ashes are called earth as written "and for the unclean they shall take of the dust of the burnt purification offering" (Rambam end Hilchot Shechitah, chapter 14, daf 88b).}
