And another saw it. In the Gemara they derive it from the verse "say to Bnei Yisrael" — a warning to all Israel; and Tur wrote a positive commandment incumbent on all Israel like other mitzvot. Taz wondered: we learn from a verse, and it appears one can say: the Torah placed the obligation other than on the slaughterer — meaning the owner of the animal, for the animal is his and likewise the blood; but to slaughter is for his benefit, and to impose obligation on others — who asked this of you that you slaughter and impose covering on us? Therefore Tur wrote this is in the category of other mitzvot; and since the verse teaches this in a particular derivation, all are equal — owner or other.
Another — if the slaughterer acquired the mitzvah of covering, can he honor another with this mitzvah? One should examine: Shach in Choshen Mishpat siman 382 wrote at the end s.k. 3 from Ra'osh that one who is a mohel in Eretz Yisrael may not give his son to another to circumcise and he himself must circumcise; I brought this because I saw people who could circumcise themselves yet honor another to circumcise their son — they nullify the command of Heaven. From this it appears in covering it is plain the slaughterer may not honor another with kisuy hadam. And what is plain to Shach is a doubt to Rama, who wrote in Yoreh De'ah siman 264 in Darkei Moshe in the name of Or Zaru'a: if the father is mohel it is forbidden to appoint another; and Darkei Moshe wrote: study is needed from other mitzvot where one may appoint an agent. We find in Bava Kamma (daf 140): a kohen acquires through service — this is the law of the offering; nevertheless it is explicit one may give another kohen to offer in his place. Terumah is his and incumbent on him to lift — nevertheless we derive agency from terumah "you" "also you"; and in Yevamot (daf 40) it implies regarding eating menachot: the mitzvah is for the serving kohen to eat, and he may not give to others not of his father's house. Therefore it appears an agent of a man is like himself and the sender receives reward as if he did it; regarding sin, the sender to commit sin — were it not for the reasoning "words of the master and words of the disciple — whose words are heard?" the sender would be liable as if he did it; so in a good measure regarding mitzvah he receives reward. Why hesitate to appoint an agent since his reward is not diminished? This applies when he appoints him as agent saying "do this as my agent" — plainly an agent of man is like himself; but if he honors him by way of honor in this mitzvah he does not receive reward, for the mitzvah was in his hand and he gave it to another gratuitously — agency does not apply; therefore regarding eating we do not say regarding sin "an agent of man is like himself," as explained there in the Gemara — a fortiori regarding mitzvah; and this is correct for halakhah: distinguish between way of honor and making him an agent.
