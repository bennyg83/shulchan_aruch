Obligated to cover. In the Gemara they derive from what is written "and say to Bnei Yisrael" — a warning to all Bnei Yisrael; and Tur concludes here: obligated to cover, for the mitzvah of covering is like other positive commandments incumbent on all Israel, only the slaughterer precedes everyone; therefore if another preceded and covered, he must give him ten gold pieces — wages for the blessing he prevented from him — end. And it is difficult: why did he need to give an additional reason beyond the derivation from the verse in the Gemara? Also the language "therefore" is not well settled — he should have said "and if another preceded," etc. It appears his intent: lest we err and say since the Torah said "and pour out and cover" — whoever pours covers — and afterward said a warning to all Israel, we must say the warning is when he is not there; but if he is there, another is not included in the warning; if so, if another wants to cover while the slaughterer is there, he does not bless, since he is not warned about this act; accordingly he has no obligation to pay ten gold — for one pays blessing wages only when he benefits and prevents his fellow from it; unlike here where he does not benefit, only prevents his fellow's benefit of the blessing and harms him generally. Therefore Tur wrote the mitzvah of covering is like other mitzvot — meaning: since the verse revealed a warning on all Israel, here it is like all mitzvot incumbent on all Israel even if he is there, only the slaughterer comes first in that obligation; therefore since in any case there is warning even when he is there, he blesses properly; and since he blesses and prevents his fellow the benefit of the blessing, he must give ten gold as above. Tur wrote: however they do not collect this nowadays, for it is like penalty laws, as explained in Choshen Mishpat siman 1; therefore Shulchan Aruch did not bring it; but I saw in Semak that he wrote: nevertheless he did not act well and must appease him.
