[Recites shehecheyanu, etc. Hagahot Minhagim that Darkei Moshe brought: covering is a mitzvah, as we say: in reward that Avraham said "and I am dust and ashes" his children merited two mitzvot — and one blesses shehecheyanu like tzitzit and sukkah, as explained in Hagahot Shechitot in the name of R' Yedidya of Shapiro — end of his words. And study is needed: regarding tzitzit we hold one does not bless shehecheyanu unless he acquired a new garment, because he acquired new utensils — as explained in Orach Chaim siman 223; but when making tzitzit on an old garment he does not bless shehecheyanu, as Ba'al HaItur, Mahari Abuhav, and Darkei Moshe in Orach Chaim siman 22 wrote; and so Shulchan Aruch and Levush there; and so appears in Tosafot, chapter Lulav and Aravah (Sukkah daf 46a), s.v. ha'oseh sukkah: the reason we do not bless shehecheyanu on tzitzit and tefillin is because shehecheyanu was enacted only for a mitzvah that has joy. And one can say regarding covering: since in reward for "I am dust and ashes" it is considered joy — on the contrary we say in chapter 25 (end of daf 88): Rava said in reward that Avraham said "and I am dust and ashes" his children merited two mitzvot — ashes of the red heifer and ashes of sotah; let us also count earth of kisuy hadam — there is no benefit in the mitzvah; and Rava said in reward "if from a shoelace to a sandal strap" they merited two mitzvot — tekhelet thread and tefillin strap — end; and so appears from Ran there: the reason we do not bless shehecheyanu on tefillin is because it does not come from time to time; and so Rashba in responsum 126. If so, all the more regarding covering. And it also appears: even for Rambam, chapter 1 of Hilchot Berachot, who blesses shehecheyanu on tzitzit and tefillin — it is because he wrote there: since they are mitzvot that are an acquisition for him — unlike covering. And I found in Rokeach siman 371: he wrote in the name of Rivash of Shapiro: any mitzvah incumbent on a person to do and he did not do it and is now beginning l'chatchila must bless shehecheyanu; and he brought proof from what we say in Menachot (daf 75): one standing and offering menachot in Jerusalem says shehecheyanu — I have become fit for service of the time — end; and perhaps on this they relied to bless on covering. But study is needed: if so, why do we not bless shehecheyanu on tzitzit, tefillin, and other mitzvot one begins? And likewise we do not bless shehecheyanu on kiddushin of a woman or her marriage, as Maharik wrote, shoresh 128; and likewise on mezuzah, as below siman 289. Rather, one must hold: even in a mitzvah one begins, one does not bless shehecheyanu unless its time is fixed — like Tosafot, Ran, and Rashba above; and our commentators explain that mishnah of offering menachot as Tosafot and Rashba in his chiddushim, chapter Keitzad Mevarchin: there were twenty-four watches and they did not renew more than twice a year; since they have a fixed time, one blesses shehecheyanu — end; if not so, not; and if so, the same regarding covering; and study is needed.]
