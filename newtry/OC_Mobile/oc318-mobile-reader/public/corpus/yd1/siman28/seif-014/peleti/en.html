Or bird — and Bach wrote bird upon behemah is exempt; and latter authorities all challenged him; the essence is from Tur who wrote in Ba'al HaItur: one who slaughtered bird or chayah and slaughtered behemah upon it — exempt from covering behemah; and afterward chayah — obligated to cover upon it. Beit Yosef wondered: behold the baraita is arranged at the beginning of chapter Kisuy HaDam: slaughtered chayah and afterward behemah — exempt; slaughtered behemah and afterward chayah — obligated. Therefore Bach explained Ba'al HaItur began with two — bird or chayah and afterward behemah — exempt; and in the obligation — behemah and afterward chayah obligated, and did not mention bird — implying with bird too exempt, because bird blood is minimal. This reason is not straight at all in the eyes of latter authorities, as Taz expanded. It appears to me Beit Yosef brought in Hagahot Maimoniyot: if he slaughtered a kosher hen and again slaughtered and it became neveilah — obligated to cover; and Beit Yosef wondered: behold it is chayah blood below and behemah blood above. These words are in the gloss to distinguish known cases to settle Beit Yosef's difficulty. Shach in Nekudot HaKesef wanted to say there it deals when he did not slaughter upon it, only mixed at the side — forced; also for Beit Yosef and Rama this was not hidden, only difficult — what does Maharam teach us? Plain: blood mixed with blood — obligated to cover. Therefore it appears Maharam's difficulty: why when slaughtering behemah above does he not cover for chayah blood below? If behemah blood interposes between chayah blood and earth — what of it? We rule in chapter Lulav HaGazul: min b'mino does not interpose; or chayah blood and behemah blood — although for mixtures essentially they are both blood, here for interposition in the mitzvah it must be min b'mino — both in the category of kisuy hadam; but if one is not in kisuy hadam, for interposition in the mitzvah it is not called his species — there is no species requiring covering, so how does it not interpose and it is not his species? Or who says he does not cover only because of interruption or interposition — only if they see him cover behemah blood as proper covering and bless, they will not distinguish chayah blood below, only think it is chayah and permit its milk. From these two reasons one must require covering in Maharam's case: if below is bird blood requiring covering and above bird blood not requiring covering — min b'mino, for his species requires covering, only due to neveilah he is exempt from min b'mino and does not interpose, and permitting its milk does not apply. Maharam's words are clear. Returning to Ba'al HaItur: in the Gemara at the beginning of Kisuy HaDam it mentioned earth below is me'akev, earth above — from the baraita slaughtered behemah and afterward chayah he covers, yet there is no earth below; and it answers: whatever is fit for mixing is not me'akev. One can understand: perhaps the baraita deals when there was earth below; if the baraita's language is slaughtered behemah and afterward chayah and it does not teach he placed earth on behemah blood — it implies earth does not interpose between behemah and chayah, unlike the reverse slaughtered chayah and afterward behemah where certainly no earth interposes, for it was already covered — only perhaps before slaughtering behemah he placed earth below and slaughtered behemah and afterward chayah, or the reverse. But what of behemah blood below near that earth interposing between chayah blood and earth? Only if we say all is min b'mino — what difference behemah or chayah, min b'mino does not interpose; and what interposes above in behemah blood is due to permitting its milk as above. But if min b'mino interposes, why need earth below? Behemah interposes; it must be there was no earth below, and the Gemara is precise that no earth below is not me'akev. Tosafot in Sukkah chapter Lulav HaGazul wrote: this "if min b'mino interposes" is uncertain — it should have been asked in chapter Behemah HaMekasheh — see there; rather Rava resolves for himself min b'mino does not interpose; and so the Gemara challenges well from chayah blood above that earth below is not needed, and why care if behemah blood interposes — and if it were min b'mino, resolve the question that min b'mino does not interpose — and so is the sugya throughout the Talmud; and the Gemara rejected "whatever is fit for mixing," etc. But since we rule like Rava that min b'mino does not interpose, the challenge and "let us drag it" are settled: what does dragging accomplish for earth below — behold there is earth below as written; and study is needed. With this, Ba'al HaItur's words that it deals when there is earth below are true; and what they challenged from the Gemara is no challenge, for the Gemara was unresolved and we say according to the conclusion. Behemah blood and chayah blood are one species; but behemah blood and bird blood are not one species — the nature of this is not like that in every matter of quantity and quality; if so, Ba'al HaItur deals when there is earth below according to the conclusion, therefore slaughtered behemah and afterward chayah he covers and need not drag, for there is earth below and no interposition; but bird above — what does covering help? There is no earth below; and if anyway there is interposition of behemah and chayah and bird — it is species where there is no mino, and it interposes; in any case dragging is needed to cover with earth; and Bach's words are correct — all on one reasoning; and study is needed.
