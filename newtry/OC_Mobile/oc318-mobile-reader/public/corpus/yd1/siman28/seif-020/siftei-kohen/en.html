[Until he checks, etc. And even though above siman 19 we say one blesses on shechitah before shechitah — that is because it is impossible otherwise, for all berachot one blesses before performing them, and we rely on the presumption that most animals are kosher, as below siman 81; but here, since it is possible to clarify, we clarify; and it is like above siman 1: we do not rely on presumption where we can clarify.]
[Such as when we are concerned perhaps damaged in the hide, etc. And as above siman 18 s.k. 1 and 11; and Bach, section of Maharai, and the Mechaber, inferred from what Tur wrote in siman 18: if the knife was found defective after shechitah — behold it is neveilah, and he did not write safek neveilah; he comes to teach we establish it in full neveilah to exempt from covering, and one could say safek treifah — cover without blessing since the safek arose in shechitah itself — end. And one may wonder: how can it enter the mind to uproot a positive commandment from the Torah to exempt from covering because the knife was found defective — there is only a safek? And perhaps in the spinal column itself it was damaged — for there is an authority, first chapter of Chullin (daf 10), who validates; and although we rule stringently, nevertheless there is only a safek; and as we say in the Talmud: we are concerned perhaps damaged in the hide; and so all poskim wrote, as in siman 18. And what he inferred from Tur's words is nothing — certainly Tur in siman 18 did not come for this, for there he does not deal at all with laws of covering; and even if his intent were this, he would not write it in hint; and so in several places: "this is neveilah" is safek neveilah; and the Mechaber himself wrote in siman 18 "this is neveilah." Also what he distinguished between safek treifah and safek in shechitah is not clear at all; and this is proven explicitly in the Talmud, chapter 25, daf 86, regarding shechitah of deaf-mute, imbecile, and minor: if not that most of their actions are defective, we would go stringently to require covering from safek — see there; and so Maharshal, chapter 25, siman 6, like the Mechaber; and so Darkei Moshe.]
[But something that, etc. It implies that for cutting the spinal column he must cover by law and must bless; and so appears in Terumat HaDeshen; and so Sefer Lechem, page 191; and so logically: as written in siman 24 s.k. 5, that stringency in cutting the spinal column is stringency without reason; and I proved there from Talmud and poskim that it is permitted — see there; therefore although we are stringent to eat, because they already practiced so, plainly he must bless — not like Atzei Erez and Bach who wrote cover without blessing.]
