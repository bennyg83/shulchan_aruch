[And others do not see them, etc. But when others see them — we say in the mishnah (daf 86a) he is obligated in covering; and it implies there in the Talmud he is obligated by law to cover; and so poskim; and so the law: among themselves they are exempt because most of their actions are defective; therefore when others see that they slaughtered well, since kosher even for eating, plainly they need covering and must bless; and so in Sefer Lechem, page 191, end of 191a — not like Atzei Erez who wrote he covers without blessing; however this is specifically when they are experts or know how to use their hands — then their shechitah is kosher b'dieved for all, as written in siman 1 s.k. 24; otherwise he covers without blessing; and see above siman 16 s.k. 21.]
[And exempt from covering. In the Talmud it implies he may not cover, since most of their actions are defective and it is like full neveilah; when he covers, people will say the shechitah was excellent and they will err and eat from their shechitah — so Tosafot there, s.v. sippa, etc.; and so Bach.]
