(Siman 28, Shach s.k. 10) And I will receive blood in a vessel. It is hard for me: in such a case forbidden because of nullification of a vessel from its place — see Shulchan Aruch Orach Chaim (siman 265); and perhaps one can say: he comes to teach even if he has a vessel attached to the ground — in such a case it would be fit to slaughter over this vessel, for it is not called nullification of vessel from its place, since anyway the vessel is attached and cannot be moved on Shabbat; see Minchat Ani (siman 336 s.k. 4) — proven he holds even pouring into attached vessel is nullification of vessel from its place — study needed. And perhaps one can say: because of mitzvah of covering it is permitted to nullify vessel from its place — see Rashi Shabbat (daf 155b) and Magen Avraham (siman 263); and still study needed: perhaps here it is worse, for at the time he transgresses nullification of vessel he has not yet fulfilled kisuy, and also safek obligation of covering; in my humble opinion: there when blood is covered there is no obligation to cover; and if it became uncovered again he must cover; if so, here too one can say: if he did not place earth below he is not obligated to cover; nevertheless if it became uncovered again he must cover; if so, "and he places" specifically — mitzvah of covering is specifically with placing; here too when he found loose earth and slaughtered upon it he did not fulfill kisuy; if uncovered, since blood is exposed, obligation to cover; and when he must drag blood and place earth below as proper covering — in the simple case: difference when slaughtering on loose earth whether must drag and place loose earth; whenever blood is exposed and obligated to cover — obligated in placing earth below; and exactly like "there was no earth below" must drag as in seif 7; likewise according to one view: when loose earth is by itself below, it is as if no earth at all below.
(Shach s.k. 16) For there is no rejection regarding mitzvot. This too is only safek that perhaps there is no rejection regarding mitzvot — cover without blessing.
