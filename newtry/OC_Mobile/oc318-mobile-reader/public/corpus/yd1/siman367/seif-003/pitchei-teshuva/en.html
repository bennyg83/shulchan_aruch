Of the deceased. See Responsa Maharit Tzahalon, in his novellae to Eizehu Neshech, p. 12, who writes that the prohibition against reading Torah or wearing tefillin within four cubits of a corpse as "mocking the poor" applies to one who had been obligated in these mitzvot while alive and upon death became free of them. But for a woman or minor, who were exempt during life, he argues that there is no such concern. See, however, Tzelach to Berachot 3b and 18a, who regards it as obvious that "mocking the poor" applies also beside the corpse of a minor or woman. See also Yeshuot Yaakov, Orach Chaim siman 71, seif katan 6.

See Responsa Noda BiYehudah, second edition, Orach Chaim, siman 109, concerning the custom in a time of distress of going to pray at the graves of tzaddikim, and whether it is proper to bring a Sefer Torah into the cemetery. He replied that the Talmud and poskim do not explicitly state whether merely holding a Sefer Torah is prohibited, though Rambam's wording in Hilchot Sefer Torah appears to prohibit it. In any case, it should certainly not be done. The Zohar in Parashat Acharei states that if, Heaven forbid, that Sefer Torah is missing even a single letter, great harm can result. Since we cannot be certain that our Torah scrolls are fully checked even for defective and plene spellings, one should not do this; see there.
