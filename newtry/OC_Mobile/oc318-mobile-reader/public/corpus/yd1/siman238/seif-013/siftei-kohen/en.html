And this applies only, etc. Ran writes: It is difficult. How can we say that if one swore, “I will not eat nine and ten,” he is liable only once, because since the first oath forbids nine, it certainly forbids ten; whereas if he swore, “I will not eat ten and nine,” he is liable twice, because the first oath forbade only ten and the second oath also forbade nine? The reverse should be true. If he said, “I will not eat nine and ten,” he should be liable twice, because the first oath did not prohibit the tenth loaf, whereas the second oath now prohibits it. We stated above in seif 10 that one who says, “I will not eat this loaf,” swears regarding each act of eating from it. Conversely, if he said, “I will not eat ten and nine,” he should be liable only once, because through the first oath he became forbidden regarding every act of eating from those loaves. One may answer that here he did not vow concerning designated loaves, but swore generally concerning a number of loaves. Therefore, when he says, “An oath that I will not eat nine and ten,” the oath concerning nine already forbids ten, for if he did not eat nine he certainly did not eat ten. He is therefore liable only once. But when he says, “I will not eat ten and nine,” the oath concerning ten did not forbid nine, since it forbids ten while permitting nine, and he is therefore liable twice. If, however, he vowed concerning designated loaves and said, “An oath that I will not eat nine and ten,” he is liable only once, for through the first oath he vowed concerning each act of eating from them. End quote. See Derishah.
