And this is only, etc. — Ran wrote: and it is difficult — how do we say oath "I will not eat nine and ten" he is only liable for one, because since he is forbidden in nine by the first oath, all the more forbidden in ten; and in oath "I will not eat ten and nine" we say he is liable for two, because from the first oath only ten was forbidden, and in the second oath even nine was forbidden — the opposite should be said: in "I will not eat nine and ten" liable for two, because by the first oath the bread was not forbidden, etc., the tenth; and from now forbidden, for we say (above seif 10) one who said not to eat this bread — on every eating and eating he swore. And in oath "I will not eat ten and nine" should be liable only once, for by the first oath he was forbidden on every eating and eating in them. One can say: here the case is where he did not vow on known loaves, but swore in general on a number of loaves; therefore in saying oath "I will not eat nine and ten" — from the oath on nine, ten is forbidden, for if he did not eat nine, certainly he did not eat ten; and therefore he is only liable for one. And in saying "I will not eat ten and nine" — from the oath on ten, nine was not forbidden, for ten he will not eat but nine he will eat; and therefore liable for two. But if he vowed on known loaves and said oath "I will not eat nine and ten" — he is only liable for one, for by the first oath he vowed on every eating and eating in them — end quote.
