He is not liable. It appears that there is a practical difference even nowadays regarding an ill person who may be fed neveilah, for he need not have his oath released. However, this applies when he swore while healthy, when neveilos and treifos were forbidden to him. But if he swore while ill, since he was then permitted to eat, the oath may take effect and should be released. Nevertheless, one could say that even in such a case the oath does not take effect, because he is permitted to eat due to danger to life. He is already under oath from Mount Sinai to fulfill the mitzvah of preserving life, and his oath seeks to negate that mitzvah. It therefore appears proper to be stringent and have the oath released.
And likewise, if he said, “An oath that I will eat less than a kezayis,” etc. The Rashba, in responsum 616, maintains that an oath does not take effect upon half a Torah measure. Tosafos likewise write at the end of Shevuos 23b that since half a measure is forbidden by Torah law, he is already under oath from Mount Sinai and incurs no liability for the oath. However, in the first case, where one swore not to eat any amount of neveilah or treifah, it also appears from Tosafos there that he is liable for the oath. See Bach. It appears that he should have the oath released and not transgress it. See below, siman 239, seif katan 19.
