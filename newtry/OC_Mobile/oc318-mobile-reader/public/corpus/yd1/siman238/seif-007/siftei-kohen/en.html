“Konam, my wife shall benefit from me,” etc. Meaning, that he shall not benefit from her. The same law applies to oaths. Rambam writes in chapter 5 of Hilchos Shevuos: If one swore that he would eat and then ate foods unfit for consumption, drank liquids unfit for drinking, or ate neveilos, treifos, and the like, he is exempt from an oath of expression, for he has fulfilled his undertaking to eat, since these items are considered significant by him. If he swore, “I did not eat,” and he had eaten items unfit for consumption, or neveilos or treifos, he is liable, for their consumption is considered eating, since they were already significant to him and he ate them. But regarding the future, if he swore that he would not eat and an incident occurred and he ate them, this is not considered eating. End quote. Beis Yosef cites this. It appears that all of this likewise applies to a konam when the vow was made dependent upon whether he eats or does not eat.
