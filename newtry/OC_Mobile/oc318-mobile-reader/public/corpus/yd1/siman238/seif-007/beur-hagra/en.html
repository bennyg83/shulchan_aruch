An oath that he shall not eat, or, etc. — Rif and Rambam; but according to Rashi's view specifically in unspecified, as written at the beginning "in unspecified," etc., "in specified," etc., except like Rava, etc.; but Rosh wrote that in all books the version is found like Rava, and do not read "except"; and further we learn in Rava: oath "I shall eat" and he ate dirt — exempt; and Rosh explained: meaning since you find them in "I shall eat" unspecified it suffices, and he is liable for the prohibition even in specified, as in Tosefta that Tosafot brought there dh "except oath I shall eat," etc.; and you might ask why exempt — behold we say at the beginning of the mishnah "oath I shall not eat" and he ate foods that are not, etc., that nevelah is not included — answer: one who swears "I shall not eat" does not intend except fit things, for unfit things one need not swear on; but "I shall eat" his intent is on everything he will eat — he fulfills through this like Rava who said, etc.; and even though if he swore "I shall not eat" he is exempt if he ate dirt, as written at the beginning "and he ate unfit foods" — dirt too is unfit, as Rava himself said there "oath I shall not eat," etc.; and this is Rif's and the Geonim's view, as there page 2; and R. A. to one who says to him, etc.; and there "and if it were so you would find," etc.; and Tosafot there dh "that," etc., strained themselves on this; and so there page 1 dh "except." However one may say, etc.; and Rosh (in his novellae) answered one answer; but according to the Geonim all is resolved.
