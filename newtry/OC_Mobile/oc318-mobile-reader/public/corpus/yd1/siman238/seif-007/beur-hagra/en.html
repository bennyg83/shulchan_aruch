An oath that I will not, etc., or, etc. This is the view of the Rif and Rambam. According to Rashi, however, the rule applies only where the oath was unspecified, as the Gemara says, "the first clause is unspecified, etc.; if he specified, etc.; rather, according to Rava, etc." The Ran writes that all reliable texts read, "It can be found according to Rava," without the word "rather." They also read in Rava's statement: "One who swore that he would eat and ate earth is exempt." The Ran explains that since the liability can apply where he said simply, "I will eat," that suffices to establish the prohibition, and he is liable even if he explicitly referred to forbidden foods. This is also stated in the Tosefta cited by Tosafos there, s.v. Ela: "One who swore that he would eat and then ate forbidden foods, such as piggul, nosar, or tamei meat, is exempt." One might ask why he is exempt, since the opening clause of the Mishnah says that one who swore not to eat and then ate inedible items is exempt, implying that neveilos are not included. The cases are not comparable. When one swears not to eat, he intends only edible things, for there is no need to swear against inedible things. When he swears that he will eat, however, his intention is satisfied by anything he eats; earth is excluded only by Rava's special ruling. Although one who swears not to eat is exempt if he eats earth, as stated in the opening clause, because earth is also inedible, this remains consistent with Rava's own ruling there. This is the approach of the Rif and the Geonim, and it is also implied there, 29b, in the statements, "according to the one who says, etc.," and "if so, the case can be found, etc." See Tosafos there, s.v. Shelo, and there, 29a, at the end of s.v. Ela, who were forced into strained explanations. The Ran in his novellae answers differently, but according to the Geonim everything is clear.
