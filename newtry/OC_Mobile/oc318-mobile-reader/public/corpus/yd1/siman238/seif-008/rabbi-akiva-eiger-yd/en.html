(Shach note 26) And so in responsum Maharam Mintz. And he deduced thus from Rashi's language: since a sage uproots the vow from its root; but in my humble view it is not proof, for Rosh wrote in Nedarim (8a) that one who swore to fulfill the mitzvah takes effect for the prohibition of "he shall not profane," but not for a sacrifice, for we exclude from "to do evil or good"; however this is specifically one who swore to fulfill a positive mitzvah like sitting in a sukkah and the like; but one who swore to fulfill a negative mitzvah such as not eating nevelah — certainly it does not take effect even for "he shall not profane," as explicitly in the sugya in Makkot (22a) where we challenge there and consider also an oath not to plow on a festival, and answer there the oath does not take effect because he is already sworn from Sinai; and there it deals entirely regarding lashes, and therefore the reason is from "a prohibition does not take effect on a prohibition" as generally; and automatically even if there is no prohibition; but one who swore to fulfill a positive mitzvah where there is no "a prohibition does not take effect on a prohibition" in this — Rosh wrote it takes effect for the prohibition of "he shall not profane"; and similarly Tosafot Shevuot (27a) on R. Yehuda ben Beteira who obligates one who swore to fulfill and did not fulfill — he holds one need not be both prohibition and affirmation; nevertheless he agrees regarding one who swore not to eat nevelah because of "a prohibition does not take effect"; see there; and likewise for us who hold like the first tanna that one who swore to fulfill and did not fulfill is exempt because there is no prohibition and affirmation — that is regarding sacrifice; but regarding lashes there is a distinction between fulfilling a positive mitzvah and fulfilling a negative mitzvah — and this is clear in Rosh's intent. Accordingly one may say that what Rashi wrote — the reason a sage uproots a vow from its root — is that Rava's language "if he was asked about the first, the second rose for him in its place" implies the second has full law of an oath; and thereby it is difficult — behold it is called "swore to fulfill the mitzvah" — the first oath, and there is no sacrifice [and especially why Rashi wrote in Makkot regarding oath not to plow that it does not take effect — we exclude from "to do evil or good" — it is clear to explain Rashi that also for the prohibition we exclude from "to do evil or good," not like Rosh above]; therefore the reason a sage uproots from its root is needed and it is not in the category of "swore to fulfill." And in truth if the husband annulled the first, she is not liable for a sacrifice on the second; but nevertheless for the prohibition of "he shall not profane," which we do not exclude by verse, and we come to judge only from "a prohibition does not take effect," one may say it is like generally that it hangs and stands — if the first falls, the second takes effect, as in Yevamot (32a); and even with husband's annulment the second takes effect for the prohibition since the first fell. And analyze well. Further it appears to me there is no proof from Rashi's words — one may say it implies the second rose for him in its place meaning if he ate before being asked about the first, he transgressed retroactively with this eating on the second, like the case of two nezirut; and even though the conclusion of the sugya is Rava, and he holds there if he ate it all he is not asked about it — nevertheless we need not ask regarding "oath I shall eat" "I shall not eat" — there is a difference if he ate an olive-bulk and was asked, he is liable retroactively on that olive because of the second; and even in "I shall not eat" "I shall not eat it" — if for one who ate an olive-bulk he was asked and afterward ate the remainder — if we say he did not transgress on the second with the first olive he ate before being asked, automatically he would not be liable for the eating after being asked, for he did not eat it all in prohibition; and on this Rashi came to give the reason a sage uproots from its root and he transgresses retroactively on the second; in such a case with husband's annulment it does not take effect; but what he ate after annulment one may say it takes effect as generally — when he finds an opening it takes effect; analyze.
