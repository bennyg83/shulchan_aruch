An oath that one will eat this loaf, and afterward he said an oath that one will not eat it—the first is a shevuat bitui and the second a shevuat shav, for he swore to nullify the mitzvah to transgress his oath, and he transgresses immediately even if he does not eat it; and if he eats it he fulfills his first oath and transgresses only the second. Therefore, it is better that he eat it so that he not transgress also the first. And if he said an oath that one will not eat this loaf, an oath that one will eat it—he transgresses the second immediately, and if he eats it he transgresses also the first; therefore it is better that he not eat it so that he not transgress also the first. A congregation that imposed a cherem on a matter and afterward imposed a cherem on its opposite—if they did not annul the first cherem before they imposed the second, the second is nothing. (See above siman 228 seif 35.)
