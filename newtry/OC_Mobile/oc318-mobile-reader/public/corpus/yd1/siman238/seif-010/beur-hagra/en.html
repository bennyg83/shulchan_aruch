An oath, etc., upon, etc. As stated there, 28a: since it takes effect with regard to, etc.
And it is prohibited, etc.; but it is prohibited, etc. As explained above in se'if 1.
Nevertheless, etc. The Gemara there says, in an alternative answer, that the case is where he said, "I will not eat it, etc." The Ramban explains that if he did not leave an olive's volume, he has already violated his oath and therefore can no longer petition for its annulment. This also appears from the Yerushalmi concerning the dispute between Rabbi Akiva and the Sages at the beginning of the chapter regarding one who ate any amount. It states there: "What practical difference is there between them? One who swore, 'I will not eat this loaf,' and ate all but the smallest amount. According to Rabbi Akiva he is exempt; according to the Sages he is liable." This means that he said, "I will not eat it." Ran.
An oath, etc. This is the view of the Rambam and Ran, and the Rosh likewise writes so there at the end of siman 5. The rule is like all other prohibitions, where even less than the prescribed measure is prohibited according to the Torah under Rabbi Yochanan's view in Yoma 74a. Although here the prohibition was created by his own oath, and one might argue that he did not intend to prohibit less than the stated measure, as indicated by the dispute concerning an unspecified oath but not an explicit one, nevertheless, since the Gemara in Yoma explains the prohibition by the possibility that the amounts may combine, the same reasoning applies here, unlike the Ran's statement in his novellae.
