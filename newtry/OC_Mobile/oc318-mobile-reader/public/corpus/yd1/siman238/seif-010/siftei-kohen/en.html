And also below seif 15 regarding oath "I will eat it" and part was burned — we say he need not eat the permitted, and it implies even prohibition there is none if he does not eat the permitted. And from Bach's words seif 8 it seems explained he compared it to half a measure forbidden from the Torah; and accordingly one can say: specifically in oath "I will not eat it" — forbidden even in half a measure; but in "I will eat it" he is only obligated to eat it all; but it is difficult from where to distinguish thus, for certainly one can say regarding half a measure: there since he forbade upon him in general we say — even though for lashes he is only lashed for an olive's worth, for prohibition even half a measure is forbidden from the Torah; but here he only swore not to eat it all. And it is like one who swore explicitly not to eat an olive's worth of bread — certainly not forbidden in half a measure. And so from Rashi's and Ran's language who wrote "I will not eat it" implies all of it, but part he did not forbid upon him, etc. And all the more so per Bach above seif 4: Tur's view that even one who swears not to eat less than an olive's worth of nevelah — the oath does not take effect since there is prohibition in half a measure — if so, if one who swears not to eat it, there is prohibition in half a measure, why when he afterward said "oath that I will not eat this bread" does the second take effect?
