“An oath that I will not eat it,” etc. The Rav writes that this applies whether he said, “I will not eat it,” or, “I will not eat this loaf.”
But it is forbidden even in any amount. The Tur writes this, but I do not know its source. Moreover, below in seif 15, regarding one who swore, “I will eat it,” and part of it was burned, we say that he need not eat what remains. This implies that there is not even a prohibition if he does not eat the remainder. The words of Bach in seif 8 appear to compare this to the Torah prohibition against half a measure. According to this, one could say that specifically where he swore, “I will not eat it,” even half a measure is forbidden, whereas where he swore, “I will eat it,” he undertook only to eat the entire loaf. Yet this requires study, for what is the basis for such a distinction? This is certainly unlike the law of half a measure. There, since he forbade the item upon himself without qualification, we say that although lashes apply only to a kezayis, even half a measure is forbidden by Torah law. Here, however, he swore only that he would not eat the whole loaf. This resembles one who explicitly swore not to eat a kezayis of bread, who is certainly not forbidden to eat less than that measure. This also appears from the wording of Rashi and Ran, who write that “I will not eat it” means the whole loaf, but that he did not forbid part of it upon himself. All the more so according to Bach above in seif 4, who understands the Tur to maintain that even an oath not to eat less than a kezayis of neveilah does not take effect because half a measure is already forbidden. If an oath not to eat the loaf also created a prohibition upon half a measure, why would a second oath, “I will not eat this loaf,” take effect afterward? This requires study.
Nevertheless, if he left over, etc. Sefer Toras Chaim, chapter 3 of Shevuos, folio 86c, writes that it appears to him that one is not liable until he eats the entire loaf.
