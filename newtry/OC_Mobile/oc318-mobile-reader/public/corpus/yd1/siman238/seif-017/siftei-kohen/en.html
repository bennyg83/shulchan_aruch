There is room to doubt, etc. From Rashba's responsum that Beit Yosef brought briefly — and it is in Rashba's responsum siman 302; and he compared there explicitly this law to oath "I will not eat it" — oath "I will not eat it" and he was asked about the first; only he doubted in this itself whether the second oath takes effect afterward or not, since it appears from Rif's words that he rejected Rava's view from oath "I will not eat" — oath "I will not eat" and he was asked about the first — the second takes effect. And so in responsum Maharal ibn Chaviv siman 137. And if so, it is a wonder on Mechaber — for below seif 21 and likewise above siman 228 seif 13 he ruled like Rava; and here wrote there is room to doubt. And it is possible Mechaber's view distinguishes: here is different since the second oath is against the first — and it is difficult. And one difficulty: in Choshen Mishpat siman 73 seif 5 he copied Rosh's responsum: one who swore a second oath to pay to void the first — if he finds an opening for the first oath and they release it to him, he may sell and uphold the second oath — end quote; implies afterward the second oath takes effect retroactively. And so Maharal ibn Chaviv there. And one can say in this: meaning he upholds the second oath on account of being obligated to pay, not on account of his oath; and needs investigation. And nevertheless for practical law it seems if he released the first, the second oath should also be released; and so Maharal ibn Chaviv there — and this is not like Atzei Levanon.
