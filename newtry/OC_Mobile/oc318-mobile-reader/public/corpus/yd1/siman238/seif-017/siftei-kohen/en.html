There is room for doubt, etc. This is based on a responsum of the Rashba cited briefly by Beis Yosef, found in Rashba, responsum 402. There he explicitly compares this law to one who said, “An oath that I will not eat it,” and again, “An oath that I will not eat it,” and then had the first oath released. He was uncertain whether the second oath then takes effect, because the Rif appears to reject Rava’s ruling from the case of one who said, “An oath that I will not eat,” repeated the oath, and then had the first released, where the second takes effect. This is also discussed in Responsa Mahari ibn Chaviv, siman 137. If so, it is difficult that the Mechaber below in seif 21, and above in siman 229, seif 13, rules in accordance with Rava, while here he writes that the matter is doubtful. Perhaps the Mechaber distinguishes this case because the second oath directly contradicts the first. This requires study. There is a further difficulty. In Choshen Mishpat, siman 73, seif 5, the Mechaber cites the responsum of Rosh regarding one who took a second oath to pay, thereby contradicting the first oath. If he finds grounds for releasing the first oath and it is released, he shall sell and fulfill the second oath. End quote. This implies that the second oath then takes effect retroactively. Mahari ibn Chaviv writes likewise. One may answer that he means only that the person must fulfill the second undertaking because he is independently obligated to pay, not because of the oath itself. This requires study. Nevertheless, as a matter of practical law, it appears that if the first oath was released, the second oath should also be released. Mahari ibn Chaviv rules likewise. This is unlike the Levush.
