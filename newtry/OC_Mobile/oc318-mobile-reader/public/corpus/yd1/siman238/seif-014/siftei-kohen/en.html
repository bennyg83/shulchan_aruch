Part of it, etc. Rosh writes at the end of chapter 3 of Shevuos: Rava said that if one swore, “I will not eat this loaf,” once he ate a kezayis from it he is liable; but if he said, “I will not eat it,” he is not liable until he eats all of it. Therefore, where he ate a kezayis he is liable because part of it is also included in the meaning of the oath. Nevertheless, the entire loaf is also included in the oath, and he swore regarding each act of eating from it, for his statement means, “An oath that I will not eat any act of eating from it.” The same law applies if one says, “An oath that I will eat this loaf.” Part of it is also included, yet he is obligated to eat the whole loaf. If part of it was burned, he must eat the remainder in order to fulfill his oath. End quote. Ran writes likewise. This resolves the difficulty raised by Tosafos at the end of chapter 3 of Shevuos 29b: Both are oaths of expression. “An oath that I will eat” means a kezayis, just as “an oath that I will not eat” above in seif 10; whereas “I will not eat it” means that he will not eat all of it. Perhaps one should distinguish between “I will eat” and “I will not eat.” End quote. In truth, there is no distinction between “I will eat” and “I will not eat.” Rather, “I will eat” also includes the entire loaf. This is the view of the Tur and Mechaber in seifim 10 and 16, unlike the gloss of the Derishah, which inserted the words of Tosafos into the Tur.
