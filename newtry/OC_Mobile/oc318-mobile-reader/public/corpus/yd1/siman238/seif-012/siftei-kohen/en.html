The second oath does not take effect. He vowed concerning nine and ten unspecified loaves. It is therefore impossible for him to eat ten loaves without those ten including nine upon which he had already sworn. Regarding any ten that he eats, we may say that nine of them were included in his first oath. Consequently, there are two oaths upon those nine, and an oath does not take effect upon an existing oath. This differs from where he said, “These nine,” and then swore not to eat ten unspecified loaves. Those ten unspecified loaves need not include the specific nine upon which he first swore. Thus, if he ate these nine and ten other loaves, he would certainly be liable twice. Therefore, if he eats these nine and one other loaf, that additional loaf can combine with nine unspecified loaves, for had he eaten another nine unspecified loaves he would be liable by reason of the later oath. Since the later oath takes effect upon that one unspecified loaf, it also takes effect upon these nine so that the one loaf combines with them, and he thereby violates two oaths, one concerning the ten and the earlier oath concerning these nine. This resembles one who swore not to eat figs and then swore not to eat figs and grapes, who is liable twice for the figs. It is possible that if he ate these nine and ten unspecified loaves, he would incur liability for three oaths, like the case of figs and grapes, where if he also ate the grapes he would certainly incur three liabilities, two for the figs and one for the grapes. Derishah.
