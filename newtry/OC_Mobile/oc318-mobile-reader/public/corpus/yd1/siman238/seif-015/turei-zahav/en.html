He need not eat the permitted. For on eating it all he swore, and he does not fulfill by eating part of it — so Rosh, Tur, and Ran. And from this it seems to learn: one who swore to his fellow to pay him a maneh on a certain day, and duress occurred that he did not have the full maneh — neither money nor worth money — on that day he set for him — exempt from the oath since he cannot fulfill as he swore. Further one can learn from this: one who swore to pay a maneh on a certain day, and if he transgresses the oath he gives a fine of ten gold pieces — and he paid half of what he owes — he gives the fine entirely, since the oath was on the full amount specifically. And if he said in this language: "oath that I will pay you a maneh on a certain day, and if I do not pay you the maneh I will give you the fine of ten gold pieces" — and he gave him part — he gives the fine according to the value of what he transgressed. And in Choshen Mishpat siman 73 seif 18 I brought clear proof for this from the case of the contractor 104: "if I do not plow for her," etc. — see there.
