Unless, etc., so that they acquire a reputation. The Bach wrote that from the Rambam and Tur it appears that this is specifically where they have much money and are exceedingly wealthy; but from the Semag it appears that even if they do not have much money, since they are included among the wealthy, one assesses them in order to give them a name, and this requires study; end quote. It appears to me that assessing them in order to give them a name means that it is permitted to do so, and the judge does not extract money unlawfully through this. This is also implied from the language of the Rambam and Tur, who wrote: "If the judge assessed them in order to give them a name, it is permitted." So too the Semag wrote, positive commandment 162: if the judge's intent is to give them importance and a name, it is permitted to assess them; end quote. This implies that he means that when his intent is not to give them a name, he extracts money unlawfully and commits a prohibition, even though the orphans rely on his words because they think they are obligated in this. When his intent is to give them a name, it is permitted. But if the orphans' guardian stands before us and says he does not want them to acquire a name, we do not force them. So it appears to me.
So that they acquire a reputation. The Bach wrote that this appears specifically with orphans who have no reputation, and now by assessing them for charity one gives them a name. But with orphans who are children of people with a good name, one does not assess them in order to give them greater importance; end quote.
And specifically, etc. Meaning specifically charity that has no fixed amount, or another mitzvah that has a fixed amount and can wait, etc.
And they had a fixed amount from their father, etc. It appears that this is specifically where they had a fixed amount from their father; otherwise, one should not establish a fixed amount for their relatives, for if not, they would always become obligated to give by means of that fixed amount. This is what we say, that they are exempt from giving charity even for redeeming captives, which has a fixed amount according to the captives as they are now. With this, the Bach's challenge against Mahari Mintz is removed.
And it will be a disgrace for the orphans, etc. At first glance this is difficult: even if they had no fixed amount, this should also apply, since it is permitted to assess them for their honor so that they acquire a name. Likewise, why did Mahari Mintz not mention that reason there, since he wrote there that it is a blemish, disgrace, and harm to them in several respects? Why did he need the reason that it had a fixed amount? According to what I wrote in s.k. 5, that for the sake of a reputation one does not force them, one may say that here, where they have a fixed amount, we do force them. Also according to what the Bach wrote, that from the Rambam and Tur it implies that for a reputation one assesses only the exceedingly wealthy, one may say that here, even if they are not very wealthy, one assesses them. But this is not implied from the master's words. Although in law it appears that here one assesses them in every case, even if they are not very wealthy, and forces them, nevertheless the master and Mahari Mintz in his responsum should have explained this. Rather, the primary view appears to be that specifically in order to give them a reputation one assesses them, because through this giving a good name is made for them. But when giving to their relatives so they not be disgraced, one does not assess them in a matter with no fixed amount, because no benefit is immediately made for them through this giving; rather, through it one removes the blemish that would later come to them. All this is unlike the Bach; see there.
