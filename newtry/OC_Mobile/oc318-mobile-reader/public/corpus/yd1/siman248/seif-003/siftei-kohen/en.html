Unless, etc., so that they acquire a reputation. The Bach writes that the Rambam and Tur appear to speak specifically when the orphans possess great wealth and are exceptionally rich, whereas the Semag appears to hold that even if they do not possess great wealth, once they are included among the wealthy, an assessment may be imposed upon them so that they acquire a reputation. This requires study. It appears to me that imposing an assessment upon them so that they acquire a reputation means only that doing so is permitted, and the judge is not thereby extracting money unlawfully. This is also implied by the wording of the Rambam and Tur, who write, 'If the judge assessed them in order that they acquire a reputation, it is permitted.' The Semag likewise writes in positive commandment 162: 'If the judge intends to accord them distinction and give them a reputation, he may assess them.' This implies that if his intention is not to give them a reputation, he extracts money unlawfully and commits a prohibition, even though the orphans rely upon his ruling, since they assume that they are obligated. If his intention is to give them a reputation, it is permitted. However, if the orphans' guardian stands before us and says that he does not wish them to acquire a reputation, they are not compelled. So it appears to me.
So that they acquire a reputation. The Bach writes that this apparently applies specifically to orphans who have no established reputation, and through the charitable assessment they now acquire one. But orphans who are children of people with an already established good name are not assessed in order to accord them still greater distinction.
And specifically, etc. Meaning, specifically charity that has no fixed amount, or another mitzvah that does have a fixed amount but can be delayed, etc.
And they had a fixed allowance from their father, etc. It appears that this applies specifically where their father had established a fixed allowance for those relatives. Otherwise, no fixed allowance should be created for their relatives, for if not, they would always become obligated through the creation of such an allowance. This is the meaning of the ruling that orphans are exempt from giving charity even for redeeming captives, although the current needs of the captives can be fixed and measured. This removes the Bach's objection to Mahari Mintz.
And it would be a disgrace to the orphans, etc. At first glance this is difficult, for even without a fixed allowance it should be permitted to assess them for their own honor, so that they acquire a reputation. It is also difficult why Mahari Mintz did not mention this reason, since he writes there that it would cause them blemish, disgrace, and harm in several respects. Why did he need the reason that there was a fixed allowance? According to what I wrote in seif katan 5, that they are not compelled merely to gain a reputation, one may answer that here, where there is an established allowance, they are compelled. Also, according to the Bach, who understands from the Rambam and Tur that an assessment for reputation is made only upon exceptionally wealthy orphans, one may say that here they are assessed even if they are not extremely wealthy. The Rama's words, however, do not imply this. Although as a matter of law it appears that here they are assessed in all circumstances, even if they are not extremely wealthy, nevertheless the Rama and Mahari Mintz should have explained this in the responsum. Rather, the primary explanation is that an assessment is made to create a reputation because this very gift directly gives them a good name. But when money is given to their relatives merely to prevent their humiliation, they are not assessed for something without a fixed amount, since this gift does not immediately confer a benefit upon the orphans; it only removes a blemish that may reach them afterward. All this is contrary to the Bach. See there.
