(Siman 248, seif 1) Every person is obligated to give charity, etc., and one who gives less than he ought may be compelled by beit din. Mishneh LaMelech, chapter 7 of Gifts to the Poor, implies that beit din is not obligated to compel, and that despite the prohibition it is merely discretionary. This does not appear to be the accepted ruling. Where there is a full negative commandment, beit din is warned and obligated to enforce even a positive commandment whose reward is stated. Proof may be brought from Choshen Mishpat 231 regarding honest measures, where beit din has an enforcement duty, although the positive commandment has an express reward. The case of returning collateral in Choshen Mishpat 97 is different because the prohibition is repaired by a positive commandment. Beit Hillel adds that if one offers charity to a poor person and the recipient refuses, the giver has fulfilled his obligation, similar to sending portions on Purim as discussed in Orach Chaim.
