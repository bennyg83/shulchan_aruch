(Siman 248, seif 1) Every person is obligated to give tzedakah, and so forth, and one who gives less than is fitting for him to give is compelled by beit din. The words of the Mishneh LaMelekh in chapter 7 of Hilkhot Matanot Aniyim imply that beit din is not obligated to compel him. Although a prohibition is involved, coercion is merely discretionary for beit din. It appears, however, that we do not rule this way. Wherever there is a full negative commandment, beit din is obligated to compel compliance, even when the associated positive commandment carries its reward alongside it. Proof may be brought from Choshen Mishpat, siman 231, concerning weights and measures, where beit din is obligated to act. There too the positive commandments carry their reward alongside them, as stated in the Yerushalmi cited by Hagahot Maimoniyot at the end of Hilkhot Genevah. Nevertheless, it states that beit din is charged with enforcement. We thus see that where a full negative commandment applies, beit din is warned and obligated to compel compliance. The ruling in Choshen Mishpat, siman 97, concerning return of collateral, that coercion is merely discretionary, is because that prohibition is not a full negative commandment, but one remedied by a positive commandment. See Beit Hillel, who writes that if a person wishes to give tzedakah to a poor person and the poor person refuses to accept it, the giver has fulfilled his obligation, as was stated regarding mishloach manot in Orach Chaim.
