Even a poor person who is supported by charity, etc. This applies when he has his sustenance independently. Otherwise, he is not obligated to give charity, as stated below in siman 251, seif 3. Nevertheless, he may receive support from charity if he lacks capital from whose profits he could support himself, as stated below in siman 253, seif 2.
Beit din compels, etc. This does not refer to a poor person, for a poor person is not obligated, as stated below in siman 253, seif 8. The Derishah likewise writes this. According to what I wrote immediately above, however, this proves nothing, for one may say that if he has his sustenance he is obligated, whereas below in siman 253 the discussion concerns one who lacks sustenance. This also resolves the Derishah's question, namely, what novelty is taught by saying that charity is accepted from him, since he is even obligated to give, as written here. The Derishah answered with difficulty, but according to what I wrote there is no difficulty, and this is clear.
They compel him. Although this is a positive commandment whose reward is stated alongside it, as it is written, 'so that He may bless you,' nevertheless, since it also includes the prohibitions 'you shall not harden' and 'you shall not close your hand,' coercion applies. So write the poskim.
In his presence, etc. The Bach appears to understand this as meaning even in his presence. They do not wait for him to give on his own, but once he refuses, they no longer heed him and descend upon his property even in his presence. This does not appear correct to me. Rather, it means specifically in his presence, excluding when he is absent, for then they do not descend upon his property until they notify him, if possible. This is no less than an ordinary debt, as explained in Choshen Mishpat, siman 106, and above in siman 245, seif 3, in the gloss.
