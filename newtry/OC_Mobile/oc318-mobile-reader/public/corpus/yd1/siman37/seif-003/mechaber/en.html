If it has two blisters adjacent to one another — forbidden, even if full of clear water; and if it is one and appears as two, they puncture it on one side — if they pour to one place it is one and kosher; if not, they are two and treifah. This is when there is mugla or water in them; but if they are tinnirim (hard growths) {Rama: The same applies to morsa on the lung — meaning the lung membrane covers it and it is not higher than lung flesh (Mahariu and other acharonim)} — even though adjacent — kosher. {Rama: And they are not called adjacent unless there is not between them the gap of two hairs after inflating the lung (Tosafot and Hagahot Ashiri, and see there) {Taz}: Provided that between them there is lung appearance (Machatzit HaShekel and Bedek HaBayit in the name of Mahara"k) {Beur HaGra} {Peleti}. Some permit even two adjacent blisters if the water in them is clear, sweet (Rashbam, Rivash, Mordechai, and see there, Mahariu) {Taz}: One may follow custom (it appears the Maharil was precise when he wrote in the slopes) or according to the matter of loss and time of pressure {Baer Hetev}. Bubbles in the slopes of the lung with clear water — their law is like adjacent (darkei emet from the poskim). A blister on a groove and wrinkle — treifah, for it is like two adjacent blisters {Shach} {Kereti} {Baer Hetev} (Machatzit HaShekel). Two blisters opposite one another between two onot — kosher. But a blister opposite tinnir in this manner — treifah, for the tinnir is hard and punctures the blister opposite it (Machatzit HaShekel) {Kereti}: Bubbles and tinnir adjacent to one another — kosher (darkei emet).}
