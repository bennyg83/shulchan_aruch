It was opened and lacking a sela — treifah. There are types of geese that were created with deficiency in their skull bone but the membrane was not pierced, and there is a sign on their head in the feathers that are somewhat high that they call "tufts," and some consider forbidding for two reasons: (1) that "as a sela" per Ri"v refers to a large ram, and from there on everything is according to proportion and size — and thus according to the proportion of a ram these geese, and all the more bar avza which is smaller — their measures are very small, until a hole with any deficiency renders them treifah as a proportionate deficiency like a prohibition; and there is room to examine, for I already wrote in siman 41 that the great ones dispute whether we measure from large rams and from it downward everything according to proportion, or all behemah and chayah, large and small, have one measure, only in a bird they measure according to the proportion of behemah; and according to those great ones, if so, in a small behemah whose measure is a sela — in a bird there is here the proportion of behemot in deficiency, and any deficiency does not help unless less than one-third of a sela and the like; but according to those who say that from a large ox the proportion begins, if so in the aforementioned bird any deficiency counts as a sela; and see below in siman 41 — the Shulchan Aruch is silent in this opinion; and in truth according to the first opinion it is more spacious, for this deficiency of a sela was stated regarding a person in deficiency of the skull to defile a tent — how could the proportion of a person and the proportion of a large ox be equal? We never heard this; and also if in the species of a person there is likewise proportion and a difference between large and small, like between an ox and a calf — if so, deficiency in the skull that does not defile would be a difference between a large person and a small person, and we do not find this anywhere; and if we come to the opinion that everything is measured according to the proportion of a large ox — therefore it must be that deficiency in the skull means deficiency removed by hand and not born lacking, for if in a large ox a sela — a one-day-old ox — any deficiency renders treifah, if so in one born lacking, behold it was then a one-day-old and rendered treifah by any deficiency — would the treifah return to kosher when it grows? If so, how could there be deficiency of a sela, and why does it teach "removed" and "as a sela" — let it teach "born with any deficiency"? And this is the intent of Tanna of Yerushalayim in siman 177 that he wrote: deficiency in the spine means removed by hand and not born lacking, for he included it with deficiency of the skull; and this discusses removed by hand; and at first glance it is difficult in itself — from where do we know? And according to what I wrote it is satisfactory, for Tanna of Yerushalayim holds like the Rosh that we measure with a large ox, and if so it is impossible in one born lacking; and examine this. The rule of the matter: from every side it appears in this type — born lacking — permitted; and see below siman 34 that wrote that the measure of prohibition is for every case less than half the majority of the width of the windpipe — if so it is obvious that a large sela from issur is at least its measure; and this is not in geese; and in particular this deficiency of the skull is not the agreement of all poskim, for many disagree and permit, especially if the upper membrane is intact — Baal HaItur, and the Rosh as the Tur testified in his name, and Rabbeinu Yonah. And in the words of Tosafot there is uncertainty, for that which the Gemara objects at the beginning of Elu Treifot on eighteen treifot — eight of them R' Yehudah — and to consider deficiency in the spine, for it teaches: how much deficiency in the spine? Beit Shammai say two vertebrae and Beit Hillel say one vertebra, and R' Yehudah said: Shmuel, and likewise for treifah — and Tosafot challenged: there too it says deficiency in the skull — Beit Shammai say like a drill and Beit Hillel say enough that it be removed from the living and it will die, which is a sela, and R' Yehudah said: Shmuel, and likewise for treifah — why does the Gemara object from deficiency in the spine and not from deficiency in the skull? And one may answer that R' Yehudah does not refer to the skull, for it is an opinion that deficiency of the skull is together with deficiency of the membrane, and a behemah is rendered treifah by piercing the membrane alone; and that which we teach in Eruvin that it counts two stringencies that contradict, such as spine and skull — it mentions skull incidentally to the spine, etc. This requires further study in Tosafot, for one must understand what they wrote in Tosafot: it is an opinion that deficiency of the skull is with deficiency of the membrane — for according to Tosafot in truth it is so, since they did not object on that; and if you say that Tosafot hold only in their opinion but in the conclusion deficiency of the skull alone without membrane renders treifah — one may say deficiency in spine and skull is one matter; besides that Tosafot there, s.v. hanakh, wrote: if they are not equal in measure we do not count them as one; even so Tosafot need not strain and say that skull mentioned in Eruvin is incidentally to spine — for according to the conclusion the law is one for skull as for spine. And it appears to me to resolve: at first glance there is room to resolve Tosafot's difficulty simply — for this is certainly received from the ancients: deficiency in the skull — enough that it be removed from the living and it will die; and in this statement "deficiency in the skull" there are two interpretations — whether they intended the skull alone or together with the membrane; and this matter was clarified from "if treifah — not alive": if treifah — not alive; and in the Mishnah it is said: deficiency of the membrane — treifah; and since it is not alive, behold this is "removed from the living and it will die"; if so, for what purpose is "deficiency in the skull" stated — the skull alone from here? Is it not from deficiency of the membrane alone that it is removed from the living and it will die? Therefore they must have intended deficiency of the skull alone; but if we say treifah is alive, then even though it is rendered treifah by piercing the membranes, nevertheless it is not "removed from the living and it will die"; and for impurity of tent it is not pure until it lacks so much that it be removed from the living, etc. — then it emerges from the law of a person; therefore we require both skull and membrane, and then the weakness prevails that it be removed from the living and it will die; if so, it is not relevant for us who hold treifah is not alive — deficiency of the skull in the bone alone renders treifah, and Shmuel properly said "and likewise for treifah" on the skull alone; and we say in Eruvin that there are two stringencies, etc.; but for the tanna of the house of R' Yehudah who holds treifah is alive — one may say as above: deficiency of the skull is with the membrane, for even though it is rendered treifah by the membrane, nevertheless it is not included in "removed from the living," etc.; if so, it is impossible for him to count it in the calculation of treifot, for he already counted piercing of the membrane; and this requires further study at first glance. And what there is to resolve appears: Tosafot's difficulty is from Eruvin: what does Shmuel teach us with "and likewise for treifah," since it teaches "removed from the living and it will die" — it is obvious it is treifah, for it dies; and to resolve: because Tosafot in Eruvin strained themselves, therefore they hold that Shmuel spoke for the one who says treifah is alive, and regarding impurity it is received in our hands that it must be so much that it be removed from the living and it will die, and then it is pure from tent impurity; if so, one may say the novelty to purify tent impurity requires the measure of a sela, for if it had so much deficiency it would certainly die; but for treifah we do not require its measure of a sela, for in truth it does not die, nevertheless it is treifah, for treifah is alive; therefore Shmuel said: it is not so, and likewise for treifot — the measure stated for impurity is the measure stated for treifah, and we require specifically a sela; and this type of treifah is stringent until even if it dies in it — even though generally treifah is alive — if so, Tosafot's difficulty from Eruvin is well, for the passage there is not for the one who says treifah is not alive, for the main statement of Shmuel follows the one who says treifah is alive; and examine this. And likewise I found in Niddah, daf 24, that Shmuel holds a fetus that has two backs and two spines — in a woman it is a viable offspring for impurity of birth, even though it is certainly treifah, for all extra is considered as removed; whereas regarding a sealed body we conclude for the one who says treifah is not alive that it is not impure for birth; therefore it must be that Shmuel holds treifah is alive; and examine (and see what I wrote below in siman 31). And henceforth the words of Tosafot that they wrote for their opinion are also satisfactory. It is difficult for Tosafot regarding Rabbeinu Tam who establishes this deficiency of the skull only in a person and not in other animals — for it teaches in the Mishnah: enough that it be removed from the living and it will die — the word "from the living" indicates all types of living creatures; otherwise it should have taught "from a person"; if so, how can we say according to Rabbeinu Tam that it discusses specifically a person? Only if we say treifah is alive, as the opinion of Shmuel and the tanna of the house of R' Yehudah; if so, deficiency of the membrane in a person is kosher and in behemah treifah, but nevertheless it is not included in "it will be removed from the living and it will die," for treifah is alive; but when the skull is also lacking, there are two adverse factors — then in all living creatures when removed it will die; and in a person, even though he has fortune, since we see that this weakness is so much that it causes death in animals — it is not relevant; and all this is for Shmuel and the tanna of the house of R' Yehudah; but for us who hold treifah is not alive — why should Rabbeinu Tam distinguish between person and behemah? For "from the living" is taught, which includes all types of animals; and the above answer is not relevant, for treifah is not alive; therefore one law for person and behemah; and for the tanna of the house of R' Yehudah it is not difficult, and to consider, etc., that he holds treifah is alive — properly there is a difference between person and behemah; and Tosafot properly wrote for their opinion that in the conclusion it does not apply. And likewise for the second answer in Tosafot that it discusses deficiency in the skull and piercing of the upper membrane — even for the one who says upper membrane alone in a person is different, for the lower membrane is hard — it is also difficult how the Mishnah teaches "removed from the living," implying in all animals, whereas in behemah piercing the upper membrane alone renders treifah; and it must also be as I wrote that treifah is alive; therefore for the matter of dying we require even in behemah both skull and membrane; and that which we say there in Eruvin "and likewise for treifah," etc. — all is for the one who says treifah is alive; but according to what we hold, treifah is not alive — we do not distinguish between person and behemah. And in truth, in Tosafot in other places it appears that Rabbeinu Tam truly holds thus to distinguish treifot of person from behemah; but in any case from the above words it appears the opposite, etc. And behold the testimony of the Tur is trustworthy as he testified in the name of his father the Rosh that he holds to permit deficiency in the skull; and the matter inclines thus, that he omitted this law; but nevertheless an answer is needed, for what they argued against him — that Rabbenu Chananel copied kesela like kesita — one may say as the commentators wrote that it refers to gluda; but the difficulty in his arguments against Rif who explains in a bird: what if it lacks like an issur, and the Rosh argued: if so, also in deficiency of the skull of a sela he should have asked in a bird what is the measure — from this it is explained that he holds deficiency of the skull for treifah was stated in behemah and bird; and in truth, were it not for the words of the Tur, I would have thought the Rosh holds like Tosafot in Eruvin that there is a distinction between removed by hand — then the weakness is greater that it is treifah "removed from the living," or because of illness — then it is kosher; therefore in the skull that lacks, that is, through illness and the like, the Rosh holds it is kosher; therefore he copied the Mishnah in its form and did not write that deficiency of a sela is treifah; but against Rif he argued that at least in removed by hand and lacking a sela it is treifah — he should have asked in a bird what the measure is; but my opinion was nullified in this against the Tur. And perhaps one may say not to attack Rif — the Rosh wrote this only to explain Rashi's words, for Rashi explained in a bird: what? — on a hole in the wing, and not like Rif who refers to deficiency like issur; and the Rosh explains that it is difficult for him regarding Rashi, for if so, also in deficiency of the skull he should have asked, for Rashi holds deficiency in the skull is treifah at kesela; but in truth the Rosh himself did not hold thus. And it is more plausible that certainly the Rosh holds: in lacking skull alone one cannot say treifah, as Tosafot [say from] actions every day — people live many years and the skull lacks; one may only say: in lacking skull and upper membrane — then treifah, as Tosafot wrote; but the language of the Mishnah does not bear this, for it teaches: how much deficiency in the skull, etc., and no mention of membrane at all — and who mentioned membrane? And behold Rif ruled: piercing membrane is treifah, for "pierced" means the lower membrane, for the upper does not raise and does not lower; and it requires investigation: even though it teaches plainly "pierced membrane," nevertheless the upper is not included and should not be counted, for it is nullified relative to the skull; if so, one may well say: "lacking skull" that it teaches means with upper membrane, and that it does not teach it — for it is nullified and ancillary to the skull, as it does not count it in the Mishnah of treifot and teaches plainly "pierced membrane," meaning the lower membrane, and no mention of upper membrane; but for the Rosh who holds we require piercing of two membranes — "pierced membrane" that the Mishnah teaches means upper and lower; if so, one cannot say that "lacking skull" in the Mishnah means combined with upper membrane — who mentioned membrane? And in these treifot membrane is mentioned — why was it not mentioned in Ohalot in the Mishnah? Therefore it must be that it did not intend in the Mishnah what it teaches — deficiency in the skull — for the membrane; and in truth it is not treifah in behemah; and accordingly the Rosh who argued against Rif argued according to his view, for according to his view he properly argued: he should have asked also in deficiency of skull in a bird; but according to the Rosh's view he does not hold there is treifah; but accordingly even for Rif it must be that he holds deficiency of skull alone is not until the upper membrane is pierced; and this requires further study. And behold Ri"v wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Tam: lacking skull and piercing upper membrane — treifah; and Beit Yosef challenged: but for Rabbeinu Tam piercing upper membrane alone is treifah; and one may say that which we say regarding a person that the lower membrane is hard — therefore we require the skull too, as Tosafot wrote to resolve Rabbeinu Tam's words, or as Rabbeinu Tam himself wrote that a person has fortune; nevertheless the novelty that Rabbeinu Tam ruled piercing the upper alone is treifah in behemah — from the force of two languages we go stringently; if so, there is a safek; therefore in a safek whether the upper membrane was pierced — permitted from a double safek: perhaps it was not pierced; and if you say it was pierced — perhaps the halachah is like the one who says we require the lower membrane, not so in lacking skull that by law is treifah even in a person — there is only one safek and treifah; and examine this. In any case I did not emerge from uncertainty, for great sages permit in deficiency of the skull, and one may join to the aforementioned practice. And reason (2) that they argued to forbid these geese — see what I wrote at the end of the siman.
That have deficiency — the later authorities; and in Tosafot Shach was uncertain in those that do not have deficiency whether they join to a majority; and I say: I did not understand this "majority" — whether they meant like the majority of the gullet that they explained the circumference of the windpipe even though it is only a crack in general and does not hold any quantity at all — this is impossible, for then the difficulty of the later ones below siman 34 would be difficult: from forbidden you can cut strips on how much more so majority of circumference, and all the more so from sela; if so, where there is deficiency is more lenient than where there is no deficiency, as Shach and Taz challenged below siman 34; and in this the answer of the later authorities between length and circumference does not apply — here there is no difference between length and width at all; and if they truly meant that there were so many cracks until in combination a majority of the quantity of the skull was cracked — this is included in the skull that was crushed, that is, that there are fractures and cracks, as the Arukh explicitly [says] regarding a majority; and even though one may distinguish in deficiency whether by force or by Scripture, as Rashba wrote in his novellae — we have to be stringent because of Torah law and not rely on such a weak distinction.
Deficiency — and if the whole between them joins, Beit Yosef and Shach disagree. And in my humble opinion it is decided from the words of the Rosh who argued against Rif that he explains in a bird: what if it lacks like an issur, and he challenged: if so, also in deficiency of skull at kesela he should have asked in a bird what is the measure; therefore the Rosh explained that the Gemara's question is on a hole in the wing, since the whole joins; and they also measure according to proportion when combining its measure; and it is still difficult: he should have asked also regarding deficiency of kesela if the whole joins and according to proportion its measure is combined; and what difference does it make that a sela is larger — the proportion also changes? Therefore it must be: since it is bone and hard and is not dented and is broken easily; and also according to many poskim treifot — deficiency of skull causes piercing of the membrane; if so, when there is a break there is no cause for piercing the membrane, and it is not comparable to the gullet which is deficiency on its own account.
That it is one-third tefach, etc. — the Gemara said (Chullin 50b): Rav Nachman [said]: kesela is like more than kesela; issur is like more than issur; and according to Rashi and most commentators his explanation is: a sela in the narrow sense — its law is like kesela in width; but according to what I wrote in siman 34, Rambam holds deficiency of gullet like issur in the narrow sense is kosher; and this is difficult on this, for they said: issur is like more than issur. And it appears to me that he explains "issur is like more than issur" — he explained what is mentioned in the words of the Mishnah: issur means spacious issur, that is, more than issur; and see what I wrote in siman 34; and according to his opinion, also in deficiency in the skull that teaches sela — the explanation is more than sela; and with this the Rambam in Hilchot Treifot regarding deficiency of sela in the skull wrote "deficiency of sela — treifah," and in Hilchot Tum'at Met, chapter 2, also wrote regarding deficiency from the skull "sela — pure"; whereas "sela in the narrow sense" — and the difficulty as above — for impurity he should be stringent and say it defiles until more than sela, as explained in the Gemara that every rabbinic measure is to be stringent; see Rashba's novellae there; but according to what I said that in truth the explanation is more than sela — for the Gemara there is nothing more to be stringent; if so, the matter falls away by itself — Shach's difficulty how the Shulchan Aruch gave the measure one-third tefach which is the measure of more than sela on sela, as explained below siman 48; for according to what I said, even in this the explanation is more than sela; and this requires further study. However, the language of the Shulchan Aruch and the opinion of most authors [is] sela in the narrow sense; if so, Shach's difficulty — but not on the Shulchan Aruch alone, only on the Rosh it is difficult, who holds regarding nikderah kesela: if pierced more than kesela — treifah — "if you stretch it, it stands at a tefach," meaning the cord that encircles the pot — when stretched it will be a tefach; it is found that the width of the sela is one-third tefach, end of his words — he opened with more than sela and concluded that the width of sela is one-third tefach. And see Ma'adanei Melekh who emended sela in place of pot — and this matter is not for the above; and it would have been a better emendation to emend in place of sela "more than sela." And so Rabbeinu Yeruham explicitly, as the Mechaber wrote: most of sela — one-third tefach that its circumference is a tefach; and there it is impossible to rely on error; and it would appear to say: what do they argue in the Gemara on this nikderah — whether the width of sela or more than sela? For in the Mishnah no measure is mentioned, only the measure of a tefach tear — if so, one comes and measures: if sela's diagonal is one-third tefach, then when stretched to a tefach in sela it is treifah, which is the tefach tear stated in the Mishnah; and if sela's diagonal is not one-third tefach, if so even in its circumference there is not a tefach — kosher, for we taught a tefach tear; and it requires more than sela that its diagonal be one-third tefach — what do the amoraim argue in a matter a child can clarify? Therefore it appears they argue in the Mishnah: it teaches in the large one "tefach," and it is as if it said "until a tefach"; see Tosafot, daf 54b, s.v. kesela: Mar holds thus — he explains in the large one: if much was torn — treifah; "until" — and "until" is not inclusive; if so, a tefach is kosher; and Mar holds from below upward: if a small tear up to a tefach — kosher; this tefach — treifah, for "until" is not inclusive; if so, in this they disagreed: Gavin holds a tefach is treifah, and even kesela in the narrow sense is treifah — its circumference is a tefach; and R' Chiyya bar Abba holds a tefach tear is kosher, therefore even a pot-sela is kosher; and in a narrow sense more than sela — then treifah; and it is not relevant and they did not argue in reality. And these are the words of the Rosh, who explains after he ruled more than sela in the words of Gavin: kesela — if you stretch it will be a tefach — it is found the width of sela is one-third tefach; and in this there is no dispute, only they disagree whether a tefach is treifah or kosher, and it teaches us that it is kosher; but this is to everyone that sela's diagonal is one-third tefach and its circumference a tefach. And these are the words of Ri"v who decided the width of sela is one-third tefach as above; nevertheless he ruled specifically more than sela — and that is as I wrote: a tefach tear is kosher; and this resolves properly the words of the Rosh and Mahariv; but the Shulchan Aruch who in this siman follows the Rosh and Ri"v and ruled that sela is one-third tefach, and in siman 48 followed and copied Rambam's words on more than sela that if this tear is stretched it will be a tefach tear — he instructs in explanation that less than this its tear is not a tefach; if so, it is difficult how he followed once the Rosh and once Rambam. And it also appears: certainly the above difficulty is strong — how would Gavin and R' Chiyya bar Abba argue in reality whether we measure the quantity of sela if its circumference is a tefach or less, and we taught a tefach tear — one may say the tefach tear itself is kosher with strain; and what there is to resolve is that Rashba in his novellae on this matter of Rav Nachman kesela more than kesela — he brought that on deficiency in the skull Rav Nachman speaks, sela in the narrow sense according to Rashi; and Rashba wrote: the line of the law is not rendered treifah except like more than sela — only to be stringent they said that even at sela it is treifah; see there; and it must be they were stringent lest people err and it be more, and they will think it is only like sela — therefore they were stringent even at sela to declare treifah. And one must give a reason to R' Chiyya bar Abba why they were not stringent in a tear in a vineyard likewise to declare treifah at sela like more than sela because of error; and it must be that this is not common and they did not decree, not so deficiency in the skull which is common, as Tosafot wrote at the beginning of Elu Treifot — many people, etc., and they decreed. And with this one may say the reason for the dispute of Gavin and R' Chiyya bar Abba: Gavin holds they decreed here too like deficiency in the skull, therefore he declares treifah even at sela in the narrow sense; and R' Chiyya bar Abba holds they did not decree and require specifically more than sela; and both agree on a tefach tear that it is treifah; and it must be: why for Gavin did they not decree on a tefach tear to declare treifah even in less than a tefach, like at sela? But the matter is: in selaim it is fit to be stringent, for it is known to every coin-maker that they do not preserve the mold of the coin so precisely on its parents that sometimes one will be wider than its fellow a little from the striking of the die in beating the silver, and there are wide selaim and there are not-wide and medium according to what happens in minting work; and indeed the measure of one-third tefach is a medium sela, and there is somewhat to fear that one will take a wide sela and more and declare kosher — and it is not according to law; therefore the Sages established as the measure sela in the narrow sense; if so, even if one takes a wide sela we have no concern, for what is fitting in truth to be more than sela. However, this is in selaim; but if we measure with a tefach tear, certainly each one can estimate with precision a tefach or less than a tefach, and there is no error in it; therefore they did not decree on it and left the law: a tefach which is more than sela — and they were not stringent in it at all, since there is no place for error; and it comes out according to everyone: if so, now that we have no selaim to measure with, we only need to estimate if the pot is round until its diagonal is one-third tefach — in its circumference a tefach; in this one may estimate even though in truth it is more than sela, as I wrote — for here there is no decree, for between a tefach, less than a tefach, and more than a tefach there is no place for error; if so, the words of the Shulchan Aruch are clear regarding deficiency of gullet: it requires only sela to be stringent, as I wrote, lest one err between sela and sela; but now that we have no sela and we estimate with the circumference of the pot — it is on the diagonal of the pot at one-third tefach; and even though this is the measure of sela and more than that — so is the line of the law, and there is a place to be stringent in the measure of tefach — there is none; and likewise "he erred" does not apply; therefore he declares treifah only according to the law; and the words of the Shulchan Aruch are binding and clear; and examine this. And behold another difficulty: Shach challenged — behold the rule: whatever has a width of a tefach has a circumference of three tefachim — it is not precise according to the geometers, only it is a tefach and one-seventh part of one-third tefach; a great difficulty at first glance. And to resolve the words of the ancients, etc.: for that which was received — a tefach tear of the bowels is treifah — was the received tradition regarding a pot whose diagonal is one-third tefach — treifah; and from it the Sages learned that a tefach tear is treifah — whatever has in its circumference a tefach, etc.; and no further difficulty: according to the geometers it is a tefach and one-seventh of one-third tefach — stringently it was not precise to declare treifah even in any tefach tear. Not so if the tradition had been on a tefach tear — then its diagonal even in less than one-third tefach would be treifah according to the geometers and measures — difficult, as Shach wrote.
And in waterfowl. Here I wrote above at the beginning of the siman regarding geese and bar oveza that have a hole in the skull of the head but their membrane is without flaw, which we debated because of deficiency of the skull; but the claim that our geese are like waterfowl and piercing of the membrane is not needed — in this everyone agrees, and the great ones of the generation already stood on this: the rabbi, author of Tzemach Tzedek, head of speakers to permit, and so many forbade; and I in my house erased to eat them, and it should only be like meat that a sage permitted. But to forbid for the public I did not know a clear reason to forbid, for Rashba who wrote in his responsum, siman 483, to forbid a thin hole, wrote: and even though they say it was created thus — many of behemot have thus; who knows? And many say it was not created thus but from the butting of oxen; if so, it implies here that everyone agrees that if created thus there is no doubt. And in truth to permit from all these living creatures — one baraita — it is hard for me to permit, for we are not expert how it would be common and in what manner until we say there is here a species departing from the rule of other geese and they have a different custom and nature — only this one, if we say to innovate a species with a new nature; but regarding these land geese — kosher in such a case — only water geese: their membranes are soft and they are rendered treifah by piercing the skull bone; and they said our goose is like a water goose. And that is because the majority are water geese. But nevertheless it is not decisive that there is no land goose species at all; see responsum of the Rosh, general rule 4; if so, since we see they live twelve months and reproduce — this is proof and testimony that those geese are not a water-goose species but land geese, and piercing the bone alone does not disqualify; therefore behold they are like other living creatures to live and reproduce; and if so, certainly treifah has no birth and living twelve months — this sign is because one may not change the rule. But here we judge whether it is a goose species that is rendered treifah or not; and since we see it reproduces and lives — clear proof that they are a land-goose species, and there is no contradiction to the rule that treifah is not alive. And the Sages already sought to bring proof from Iyov and the like to clarify the safek whether it is treifah; here too, since it is not clear to us that it is treifah — birth twelve months is a complete sign that it is among the kosher in birds that are not rendered treifah by piercing the bone alone. And this is clear. And also in the Gemara and poskim only "broken and pierced" is mentioned — one should be concerned for piercing the membrane, for the membrane is soft and leans on the bone, and when the bone is pierced it is as if the helper fell; but in what was created by nature, nature strengthens and adopts the membrane that it be healthy and firm and no weakness falls in it, and there is no breakage and piercing — only thus it was and thus it will persist; if so, with what would one declare treifah? And even though many hold regarding treifah to forbid also what was born lacking — nevertheless this is not treifah on its own account, only from concern that piercing of the membrane might occur; if so, "broken" was stated, but "born lacking" was not stated; and from where should we be concerned for piercing the membrane regarding what we see persisted in this bird and there is no mark of piercing — from where to declare treifah and innovate a law in the Gemara that teaches "broken" to say "born lacking"? And somewhat one may infer from what Mahari wrote in Sha'arim, and likewise Orach Chayyim, that he did not wish to clarify the matter of waterfowl, for also in land fowl we are not expert in examination — if so, there is no difference. And it is difficult: there is much difference if they lay eggs or live twelve months in land fowl — kosher, for here there is only concern of treifah; not so in waterfowl — there it is certainly treifah and living twelve months does not help, and there is much difference. Therefore one may say that he holds: even in waterfowl the explanation is: since its membrane is soft, one should be concerned when the bone breaks that afterward a small hole occurred in the soft membrane — but not certain; even in this, eggs and twelve months help; therefore there is no difference; and examine this. For this I wrote: the one who is lenient here did not lose and has great pillars to rely on.
