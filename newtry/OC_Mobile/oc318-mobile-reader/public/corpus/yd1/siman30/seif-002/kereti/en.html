Kesela — see Peleti: with what do we measure a sela — if in every behemah and chayah its measure is a sela, only in a bird they measure according to proportion, or they spoke only of large rams; and also a creature born lacking — there is room to be lenient; therefore in geese that sometimes have a hole in the skull and were created thus, it is possible that if they measure according to proportion of large rams, this would be a small measure; nevertheless one may be lenient, for in any case the great ones among the decisors did not rule this law; see Peleti.
Holes, etc. — our rabbi Beit Yosef's opinion: the whole between them joins, as in the gullet, siman 34; and Peri Chadash is lenient; and see Peleti that I made a summary for Peri Chadash.
That have deficiency — but those that do not have deficiency do not join to a majority, as in the gullet — Beit Yosef; and Peri Chadash agreed; and Shach was indecisive; and see Peleti, for I did not understand well their divisions and their uncertainty.
Kesela — even in the narrow sense, and not like the explanation of the Mishnah according to Rambam; see Peleti.
One-third tefach — and the great Shach challenged: a sela in the narrow sense is not so much, since more than a sela is thus its measure, below siman 48; and he also argued that the geometers said that this measure — whatever has in its circumference a tefach has a width and diagonal of one-third tefach — is not precise, only seven tefachim in the diagonal in its circumference twenty-two tefachim; and one must be precise stringently; and see what Peleti wrote in resolving this.
(Tefach) — a tefach is four thumb-breadths; and if so, one-third is a finger and one-third of a finger; and a finger is as Rambam wrote in Hilchot Sefer Torah, chapter 9, halachah 9: in length two thumb-breadths with spacing, and width seven medium thumb-breadths with strain.
In any amount, etc. — for its membrane is soft and when the bone breaks the membrane tears; and in geese that have a hole in the skull and not in the membrane, and they are designated — the feathers on their head are high in that place that they call in foreign tongue "tufts"; many permit and many forbid; and I expanded on this in Peleti and clarified the measure of prohibition that one must estimate in a bird according to the proportion of behemah; and I concluded: where there is no loss, one should be stringent unless the goose already lived twelve months or laid eggs — then the one who is lenient did not lose; and a God-fearing person should be stringent for himself.
In any amount in a bird — that is, when it did not live twelve months or lay eggs; and if on the skull there is swelling and after cutting the swelling it is whole — kosher.
