And all this, etc. In Torat Chatat there din 3 he brings the view of the poskim that blood of eggs is not forbidden d'oraisa at all, and the view of the poskim that blood of formation (rikum) is forbidden d'oraisa; and he concludes: and in Issur VeHeter general rule 42 it is written that if an egg was cooked or roasted, or one egg was beaten and blood was found in it and it is not known in which place the blood was found — they are accustomed to forbid it; however, if they are still raw and beaten in a bowl and mixed and blood is found and it is not recognizable in which place it was, it is written in Agudah that it is a rabbinic doubt and to be lenient, and such is our practice even if there are only two or three; but if it is recognizable that the blood was in the place of prohibition — they practice according to the responsum of the Rosh, end of his words. And it appears to me that the same applies if it was cooked (and afterward became mixed) with others — if we do not know that the blood was in the place of prohibition, one should permit the mixtures; for there is no distinction between cooked and raw regarding mixtures; and even though it is possible that through cooking it imparts flavor to others, unlike when it is raw — nevertheless it appears that one may rely on the words of those who are lenient. And we say that blood of eggs is d'rabbanan; and as long as we do not know that the blood was in the place that forbids the egg, one may be lenient from the doubt that perhaps it was not in the place that forbids the egg; and because of the drop of blood itself that is absorbed in the egg — there is no reason to forbid, for Rashba wrote in a responsum that blood of eggs forbids only when it is in its visible form, but it does not forbid its mixture — as will be explained shortly; even though wherever the egg is forbidden it forbids its mixture, as stated in the responsum of the Rosh — nevertheless in a case of doubt one may be lenient, end of his words; and so Darkei Moshe. And according to this are his words here in the hagahah — except that he explained more here in the hagahah the reason to be lenient in mixtures than regarding the egg itself; and that is: since in any case in mixtures one in two is nullified; and even though we are accustomed to permit even when there are only two eggs, as Torat Chatat and Darkei Moshe wrote — nevertheless it is called a mixture; and in mixtures we find that one in two is nullified; and moreover that even with two eggs it is permitted by law, as I will write shortly. And this that he concluded 'but if the egg alone is forbidden' — it appears clear: all the more so whatever became mixed, even only one in one, is permitted, because in mixtures we rely on the poskim that blood of eggs is d'rabbanan. And that which the Rav ruled anonymously like the Mechaber in seif 2 — 'if it is known that it is marukem ha'afroach one is liable' — that is because regarding the egg itself we say every doubt stringently; he left matters as they were. And with this is settled what Sefer Lechem challenged on the Rav on daf 179a — see there. And Pri Chadash wrote here words without attention and there is no need to respond to them, as the examiner will see; and Maharshal there and siman 120 wrote that we rule blood of eggs is d'oraisa; therefore where blood is found on the chelevon and it became mixed, whether raw or cooked — everything is forbidden; and where found on the chalmon not on the knot — one takes the egg with the chalmon and discards it and all the rest is permitted, for the essential law is like Ra'ah, end of his words; and so Bach at the end of seif 4 is stringent and wrote that according to what we rule, to be stringent like Ra'ah's view and like the Geonim's view — if so, in every case of mixture it is forbidden, as the Rosh wrote in his responsum, end of his words. And for practical halacha, the Rav's and Issur VeHeter's words appear — that even according to those who say blood of eggs is d'oraisa, one may be lenient in mixtures, for we rule from the Torah that min b'mino one in two is nullified and the Sages required sixty; and if it spilled and they cannot know whether there are sixty — it is permitted, for it is a rabbinic doubt and to be lenient, as below siman 98 seif 4 — if so, all the more so here where there is doubt whether there is any prohibition at all, for it is nullified in the majority. And even though from the Rav's words in Torat Chatat and Darkei Moshe it appears that he holds that according to those who say blood of eggs is d'oraisa it is a Torah doubt — the Rav followed the responsum of the Rosh, which implies thus; and that is because specifically when it spilled we say thus, since after it was nullified in the majority the prohibition regarding itself is rabbinic; but not where the doubt is in the prohibition itself and it comes to be rabbinic from another aspect — and for this reason we do not permit a doubt of a terefah animal that became mixed in a majority because of a rabbinic doubt to be lenient, as Darkei Moshe distinguished explicitly in siman 57 in such a case — nevertheless here, since Rambam, Semag, Smak, Agudah, Issur VeHeter, and Maharai in his gates hold that blood of eggs is only d'rabbanan — if so, even without a mixture it would be a rabbinic doubt and to be lenient; certainly one should consider it in such a case a rabbinic doubt through mixture according to everyone — examine. And even if only one became mixed with one, it appears permitted, for one may say that the permitted egg is more than the forbidden egg; and if so, it is a double doubt — doubt whether there is a forbidden egg here or not; and even if you say there is a prohibition here, perhaps the permitted egg is the majority; and if so, it is forbidden only d'rabbanan, and it is a rabbinic doubt and to be lenient. And in such a case Semak siman 205 wrote, and Beit Yosef brings it siman 83; and so Issur VeHeter general rule 13; and the Rav in Torat Chatat general rule 20 end of din 3 — fish brine where there is oiliness is forbidden from the Torah, and salted fish like herring are permitted because of a double doubt — whether they were salted with tamei fish or not; and even if you say they were salted, perhaps there was no fat in them and then their brine is forbidden only d'rabbanan — until here his words, and it is exactly like this case here — see there. And even when found on the chalmon and it became mixed, it is considered a doubt — perhaps we hold like Ra'ah and his supporters; and likewise when found on the chelevon and it became mixed — perhaps we hold like the Geonim and their supporters; and Bach himself holds that this is a valid double doubt — see there. And even though I wrote in end of siman 57 that in such a case there is no double doubt except regarding a terefah where there are in any case sides to permit — nevertheless here, since there is doubt of prohibition at all, it certainly appears to be a complete double doubt. And moreover, even according to those who say blood of eggs is d'oraisa, and the blood is in the place that forbids it — the egg is not certainly forbidden, but only from doubt that perhaps formation began, as Rashba and Maharshal wrote there — if so, from all these reasons the words of Issur VeHeter and the Rav appear; but what Maharshal was lenient about when found on the chalmon does not appear at all, and it is not the view of the Rosh and all the later authorities.
