That he discarded the chalmon, etc. Beit Yosef wrote that the concern of this law is perhaps the blood was in the place that forbids and came here through beating, and we go stringently — blood of eggs is from the Torah; and according to this, whoever holds blood of eggs is d'rabbanan — we go leniently, for perhaps it was not in the place that forbids. And Torat Chatat general rule 42 wrote that his words are incorrect — for even according to those who say d'rabbanan it is forbidden here, since blood was found on the chalmon we are stringent like Rif, that the chalmon is forbidden by law; only that if there is doubt whether on chalmon or chelevon — in that case Beit Yosef's words are correct, end of his words. Maharshal wrote: one should analyze — if they were beaten, how can he take the chalmon upon which the blood was found, for it is already beaten and mixed; certainly it deals after he began to beat them, he saw blood on the chalmon while it was still whole. And he wrote further that this is according to Rosh like Rashi; but according to Rif, who forbids without uprooting reason except because of the chalmon itself being forbidden. And I say further that if they were not beaten and blood was found on the chalmon not on the knot — he should take the egg with the chalmon and discard it and the rest is permitted, since according to Rashi and Ra'ah it is permitted and their words are essential — only that we are stringent like Rif; therefore we are not so concerned to be stringent in mixtures that a little of it mixed with other eggs; but when he already began to beat, even a little — forbidden, for it is a Torah doubt and one should be stringent, end of his words. One takes the kosher chalmonim, etc. For it is impossible to take an egg from its fellow without leaving some of the upper on the lower; therefore it is better that some of the permitted remain in the bowl with the forbidden, than that nothing forbidden remain with the permitted in the bowl — so Hagahot Mordechai wrote, and he resolves why we do not do the opposite:
Since in any case one in two is nullified. Meaning from the Torah one in two is nullified even wet in wet; only the Sages required sixty, as below siman 98; therefore here, when there is doubt whether it was in the place that forbids it, it is a rabbinic doubt and we go leniently. But if there was no mixture except one in one — this is not a rabbinic doubt, since from the Torah it is not nullified — if the blood were in the place that forbids, it would be a Torah prohibition; if so, it would be a Torah doubt and stringently; therefore Rama mentions one in two, etc. And one may say that even one in two is not nullified — below siman 111 — only that there it deals with an important matter where the Sages decreed it should not be nullified even in sixty; therefore it has no permission except in two mixtures — not so with a beaten egg, whose importance is diminished; therefore we go leniently in a rabbinic doubt. And even according to Maharshal, who ruled like Ra'ah that everywhere is forbidden as I mentioned in his name shortly — nevertheless a doubt whether it was in the place that forbids is relevant here; if we say the essential view is Rif's version, automatically in chelevon not on the knot there is permission; if we say Ra'ah's version, automatically on the chalmon there is permission; only that from the side of stringency, since we cannot decide, we forbid everywhere; but in truth according to the truth there is doubt whether there is prohibition here. And Maharshal himself ruled to be lenient in mixtures, as I mentioned in his name in this seif — that since according to Rashi and Ra'ah it is permitted and their words are essential, only that we are stringent like Rif, etc. — sixty is not required, and as below siman 98 regarding spilled gravy it is called a rabbinic doubt. Yet we still need to explain Rama's words here — for according to his words it is difficult why Rosh wrote in the responsum that Beit Yosef and Tur bring — since the thin chelevon mixes together and everything is forbidden — for there too it deals when it is not clear to us whether it came from chelevon or chalmon, as explained in the responsum Beit Yosef brings, that it is unknown whether it came from chelevon or chalmon, etc.; and if so, he should have permitted because of Rama's reason. And in truth this rationale of one in two being nullified does not help here to say that therefore it is a rabbinic doubt — since there is doubt whether it is in the place that forbids from the Torah, for whoever says blood of eggs is d'oraisa — it is as if it were certainly in the place that forbids, as we find in siman 110 seif 9: wherever the doubt is in its substance, it does not help afterward to say a double doubt through mixtures; and so Issur VeHeter general rule 27 din 10 — if the first mixture was only one in one and afterward a second mixture — all is forbidden, since in the first mixture it was not nullified from the Torah but was a Torah doubt; it does not help afterward for the second mixture to say one in two is nullified, as if definite prohibition fell there, since it is from the Torah if so. If so, here too this blood would be a Torah doubt and be as if it were certainly in the place that forbids, and afterward one in two being nullified does not help, for sixty from the rabbinic level is still required; and it is not comparable to spilled gravy in siman 98 seif 2 where we go leniently — for there there is certainly a Torah prohibition, only that it has remedy through nullification in the majority which helps from the Torah, only that d'rabbanan sixty is required; here we come to be lenient from the side of doubt of Torah prohibition — in such a case we say it is as definite prohibition; and this is a principle in many places — see what I wrote siman 107 seif 1 on Torat Chatat. And that Rama permitted here because one in two is nullified — is because he goes according to his view that Torat Chatat general rule 62 wrote: specifically when it did not become mixed we say stringently that perhaps it was in the place that forbids, for blood of eggs is d'oraisa; but if it became mixed we rely on that resolution of Tosafot that blood of eggs in every place is only d'rabbanan, since many later authorities hold thus — if so, here he ruled properly in mixtures to be lenient, since there is another leniency that one in two is nullified, and there is no Torah doubt of prohibition here, for we may say there is no Torah prohibition at all — unlike the egg alone, where we are stringent like one who says blood of eggs is d'oraisa; and so Issur VeHeter general rule 42 — therefore Rosh's responsum that forbids the mixture is not difficult at all, for he holds blood of eggs is d'oraisa, and therefore one in two being nullified does not help, as I mentioned. And Maharshal holds that even in mixtures we are stringent from the stringency of blood of eggs being d'oraisa, as I copied in his name above; and for practical halacha one should rule like Rama, as Issur VeHeter also wrote thus, that such is the practice — especially since many later authorities are lenient in every such doubt; and therefore I ruled in a case that came before me: a woman made dough kneaded with eggs, and after kneading a drop of blood was found in the shell of one of them, and there were many eggs from that mixture — and I permitted everything for Rama's reason that I mentioned, that it is as if there is only a rabbinic doubt, since even if it were in the place that forbids it, it would be nullified in the majority from the Torah (and likewise) here in doubt we go leniently. And they said to me that it became mixed in the dough and it is a type not in its type requiring sixty from the Torah, as below siman 98 — this is not so, for it also became mixed in kosher eggs, and we say: remove what is not its type as if it were not there, and its type is greater and nullifies it, as below siman 98 — therefore I permitted the aforementioned mixture, end of his words:
