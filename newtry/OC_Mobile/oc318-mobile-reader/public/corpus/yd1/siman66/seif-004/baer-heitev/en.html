And one leaves. Taz wrote the reason that it is impossible to take an egg from its fellow without leaving some of the upper on the lower; therefore it is better that some of the permitted remain in the bowl with the forbidden, than that nothing forbidden remain with the permitted that were removed from the bowl. And he wrote further in the name of Maharshal that if they were not beaten and blood was found on the chalmon not on the knot — he should take the egg with the chalmon and discard it, and the rest is permitted; but when he already began to beat, even a little — forbidden, for it is a Torah doubt and stringently:
The mixture. Shach wrote that it appears from Rama's words that if the egg alone is forbidden, it implies that if it became mixed, even one in one — permitted, because in mixtures we rely on poskim that blood of eggs is d'rabbanan — not like Bach, who wrote that for us, who hold blood of eggs is d'oraisa, it is a Torah doubt; and in my humble opinion it appears that even according to those who say it is from the Torah, one may be lenient in mixtures, for we rule from the Torah min b'mino one in two is nullified and the Sages required sixty; and if it spilled and they cannot know whether there are sixty — permitted, for it is a rabbinic doubt and to be lenient — all the more so here where there is doubt whether there is any prohibition, for it is nullified in the majority. And even though from the Rav's words in Darkei Moshe it appears he holds that according to those who say blood of eggs is d'oraisa it is a Torah doubt — and specifically when spilled we are lenient, since after nullification in the majority the prohibition regarding itself is rabbinic; but not where the doubt is in the prohibition itself and it comes rabbinically from another aspect — and for this reason we do not permit a doubt of a terefah mixed in a majority because of rabbinic doubt to be lenient, as Darkei Moshe wrote explicitly siman 57 — nevertheless here, since Rambam and many poskim hold blood of eggs is only d'rabbanan — if so, even without mixture it is a rabbinic doubt and to be lenient; certainly one should consider it through mixture a rabbinic doubt for everyone. And even if one became mixed with one — permitted, for one may say the permitted egg is more than the forbidden egg; and if so, it is a double doubt — doubt whether there is a forbidden egg here; and even if you say there is prohibition here, perhaps the permitted egg is the majority; and if so, it is forbidden only d'rabbanan, and it is a rabbinic doubt and to be lenient. And even when found on the chalmon and it became mixed, it is considered a doubt — perhaps we hold like Ra'ah and his supporters; and likewise when found on the chelevon and mixed — perhaps we hold like the Geonim and their supporters. And even though I wrote in end of siman 57 that in such a case there is no double doubt except regarding terefah where there are sides to permit — nevertheless here, since there is doubt of prohibition at all, it certainly appears to be a complete double doubt. And moreover, even according to those who say it is from the Torah, the egg is not certainly forbidden, only from doubt that perhaps formation began — if so, from all these reasons it appears one should be lenient, end of his words. And Taz wrote: a case came before me where a woman made dough kneaded from eggs, and after kneading a drop of blood was found in the shell of one, and there were many eggs from that mixture — and I permitted everything, for it is like a rabbinic doubt, since even if it were in the place that forbids it, it would be nullified in the majority from the Torah; and likewise in doubt we go leniently. And they said to me that it became mixed in the dough and it is a type not in its type requiring sixty from the Torah, as below siman 98 — this is not so, for it also became mixed in kosher eggs, and we say: remove what is not its type as if it were not there, and its type is greater and nullifies it, as below siman 98 — therefore I permitted, end of his words:
