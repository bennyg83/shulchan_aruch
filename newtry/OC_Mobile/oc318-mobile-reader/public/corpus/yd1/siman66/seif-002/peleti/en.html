One is liable for it; and all the later authorities challenged — for below siman 86 seif 8 the Mechaber copied Rambam's wording that fowl eggs, even if formation began, are forbidden only d'rabbanan; and Maharshal explained, and Shach commented that the blood that turned to flesh left its prohibition of 'clot' and became like dust; and Peri Chadash commented that there was no blood there, only flesh, and because of anger this is not so — for formation never begins as blood; and one cannot find flesh without blood inside, as nature knows. Nevertheless there is difficulty: what difference — if blood alone, obviously forbidden; but if afterward absorbed in flesh — even if the piece is full of blood, all the more so if it did not separate — permitted, nullified by the piece of flesh; and this case is similar — the blood was absorbed in flesh. And the Rav in Beit Yosef wrote that from Rambam it appears blood of eggs is d'rabbanan — meaning he had to mention that if blood of formation without flesh is forbidden from the Torah — we learn that he holds this too is d'rabbanan; but we do not learn from the law itself; if so, there is no contradiction in Shulchan Aruch, for in truth he wrote in this siman forbidden from the Torah — and why mention more — requires further study:
