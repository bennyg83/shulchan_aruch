And this is according to one resolution in Tosafot Perek Elu Treifot that Beit Yosef brings here — meaning that in every place it is forbidden d'rabbanan; but according to the second resolution there is a distinction, that blood on the knot is forbidden from the Torah, for it is considered a type of flesh since the embryo is formed from it; but in another place there is no prohibition, even d'rabbanan. And it is implied from Tur and Shulchan Aruch that they adopted the stringency of both resolutions. And in Beit Yosef it is written: but Rambam wrote regarding a tahor fowl's egg, etc., end of his words — it appears that he holds that Rambam disagrees with what Tur wrote, that one is liable if it is marukem ha'afroach — meaning from the Torah. And it is difficult, for Tur himself brings Rambam's words in siman 86; and likewise in Shulchan Aruch he brings in both places like Tur. And it appears to me that what he wrote here 'one is liable' refers to the blood, as Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Rashba that it is blood-flesh; but in siman 86 regarding Rambam he deals when there is no blood there, only formation of flesh — and that is d'rabbanan. And Maharshal brought that one should be stringent and say blood of eggs is d'oraisa, and a practical difference regarding doubt — until here. And nevertheless one should be stringent also like the first resolution and forbid the blood in every place d'rabbanan; but in Torat Chatat general rule 62 he wrote in the name of Hagahot Shaarei Dura and Semak and Maharai that it is a rabbinic doubt and to be lenient; and so Issur VeHeter general rule 42 in the name of Agudah — except that he wrote that specifically in a mixture we rely on this; and such is the essential ruling, as Rama also ruled in seif 4 — see what I wrote there:
