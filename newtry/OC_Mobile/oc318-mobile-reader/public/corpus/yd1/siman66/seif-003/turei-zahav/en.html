But if it is found in the chalmon, etc. Their dispute depends on the sugya in this matter. According to Rosh and Tur, it is stated in the Gemara: if blood is found upon it, one discards the blood and eats the rest. R' Yirmiyah said: and this is when it is found on its knot (Rashi: it is the rooster's seed tied at the head of the blunt end of the egg, and from there embryo formation begins; and since it is still there and has not spread through the rest of the egg, therefore one eats the rest; but if found on the chalmon — the entire egg is forbidden, as below). Dosetai taught: they taught only when found on its chelevon; but if found on the chalmon — the entire egg is forbidden, for 'he cast the flaw into the whole of it' — meaning the defect spread through the whole egg; and this regarding the chalmon that forbids the whole — means on the knot of the chalmon, even if it did not spread outside; and the reason that specifically on the knot of the chelevon and it did not spread outside one eats the rest, but if found on the knot of the chalmon we say that it was already on the knot of the chelevon, from where formation begins and spread outside and came to the knot of the chalmon which faces it; but if found on the chalmon not on the knot and it is not recognizable that it spread from the knot — the egg is permitted. And R' Yirmiyah and Dosetai do not argue: R' Yirmiyah teaches that in the chelevon itself there is a distinction — if the blood is on the knot the blood is forbidden and the egg permitted; if it spread outside, the egg too is forbidden; but not on the knot — whether in chelevon or chalmon the egg is permitted. And Dosetai teaches that on the knot of the chalmon it is as if it spread from the knot of the chelevon outside, and the entire egg is forbidden. This is Rashi's view, and it is one view that Rama brings here. But Rif and Rambam explain that Dosetai argues on R' Yirmiyah and holds that in the chalmon there is no distinction between knot or other place, and it is forbidden everywhere; and in the chelevon everywhere the blood is forbidden and the egg permitted — and this is Shulchan Aruch's view in this seif. And Rosh and Tur ruled to be stringent like both views — meaning in the chalmon the egg is forbidden everywhere, and in the chelevon too it is forbidden if the blood spread from the knot outward, and there is no permission for the egg except in chelevon not on the knot, or on its knot and it did not spread. And Rama wrote that our custom is to forbid the egg in every manner; and the reason is explained in Torat Chatat — that we are not expert which place is called the knot. But there is reason to forbid for another reason — there is a view of Ra'ah that the Gemara's version is reversed, that chelevon is worse; and he wrote that we know the embryo is formed from the chelevon and not from the chalmon — for if you break the egg while the embryo is still inside you will not find anything from the chelevon from which it was formed, and the chalmon remains whole; if so, Ra'ah would forbid everywhere — if found on chelevon, forbidden by his view; if found on chalmon, forbidden by Rif and Rambam's view.
