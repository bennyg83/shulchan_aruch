(3) Musk, which they call musk — it is from a chayah whose name is known, and it has a kind of hump on its neck where blood first collects like blood and afterward dries and becomes musk. Some say it is forbidden to eat because of concern for blood; some say permitted, for it is mere secretion; and even though its beginning was blood — afterward. Now we follow; and as Knesset HaGedolah elaborated in Hagahat Tur note 2 and concludes: for practical halacha the common people are accustomed to permit musk in eating, and daily practice is to throw musk into broth or sweets for good fragrance — see there. And likewise in the indexes at the end of Kol Bo Levi siman 106. And likewise Taz in Orach Chaim siman 216 s.k. 2; Peri Chadash in responsa Mayim Chayim siman 10 regarding Orach Chaim there note 2; Shvut Yaakov in this siman note 2; from Mechaber note 11; Zivchei Tzedek note 3; and see further what we elaborated in Orach Chaim siman 216 notes 21–22 — see there.
(2) A place where they place salt in nevelah blood, and if a person says to bring him salt made in permissibility from outside — it is forbidden in its visible form, such as to immerse an egg or radish in it; but permitted to put it in a cooked dish l'chatchila; and if there is something else in the salt along with the blood — it is 'this and this cause' and permitted even to eat the salt in its visible form; and all such cases we rely on a non-Jewish taste-tester. Ram"a of Panu siman 27; Knesset HaGedolah in Hagahat Tur note 1; Peri Chadash below siman 105 note 58; Lev HaPanim there note 58; in Levush there note 58; Shvut Yaakov note 1; end of this siman from Mechaber note 12; Zivchei Tzedek note 2; and see below in the hagahah to siman 134 and our words there.
(4) Nevertheless civet, which is the sweat of the civet cat — it appears to me forbidden to all for eating, for one cannot say it dried, for it remains moist; and accordingly one must be careful not to eat civet or that it was put in foods; and likewise forbidden to smell it and recite a blessing, as Shaarei Dura wrote: everything forbidden in eating — one does not smell or bless on it; and Knesset HaGedolah in Hagahat Tur in Orach Chaim there note 8; and likewise in the indexes at the end of Kol Bo Levi there. And Beit Din — Shvut Yaakov in this siman at the aforementioned note 4. However, what is written that everything forbidden in eating — one does not smell or bless on it — is not the poskim's view; rather even if forbidden in eating, one may bless and smell it, since it is not forbidden in benefit — as we wrote in our words in Orach Chaim there note 23 — see there.
(6) [Seif 3] If a speck of blood is found upon it, one discards the blood, etc. In the holy community of Hebron 5660 they practiced: if a speck of blood is found on the chalmon, they remove it with cotton wool; and if the soft membrane on the chalmon remains whole, they eat the egg — from Mechaber note 2; and he wrote this is according to Shulchan Aruch's ruling in this law that specifically when found in the chalmon it forbids the whole; therefore if the speck was removed and the membrane on the chalmon remained whole, it proves the blood was in the chelevon — discard the blood and the rest is permitted, as the Geonim wrote and Maran ruled. And likewise in note 4 — except he concluded: this is to instruct others; however a scrupulous person should be stringent for himself and forbid any egg in which blood is found in any manner — and so the wholesome ones practiced, and many — end of his words. And Beit Din — Zivchei Tzedek note 5 wrote: and we in our place, from the day we established our view, thus we practice for every questioner — to forbid in every case the entire egg, whether in chalmon or chelevon — end of his words; and likewise Ben Ish Chai, Parashat Tahorot note 5 — such is the custom in Baghdad — see there — to forbid in every manner. However, where they already practiced like the custom of Hebron and Shulchan Aruch's ruling — one does not treat them lightly; only a scrupulous person should be stringent for himself as mentioned; and it appears that also one who comes to ask in that place where they practice like Shulchan Aruch — one informs him that some are stringent, for perhaps he will separate himself if he is scrupulous. And see below note 14.
(7) In time of distress or b'dieved one may rely on the Geonim to be lenient when found in the chelevon outside the knot. Chessed David on the Tosefta chapter 9 of Terumot; Orach Chaim note 5; Zivchei Tzedek note 6; Ben Ish Chai there. And behold the matter of the knot is known — the chelevon has a knot at the blunt head, a matter grasped from the male's seed, recognizable to the eye as more tied; and likewise the chalmon has a knot — like a round crown the width of a large lentil; and according to the Geonim embryo formation is from the chalmon, therefore they are more stringent on the chalmon; and such is Maran's view. But Ra'ah and Rokeach hold embryo formation is from the chelevon, therefore they are more stringent on the chelevon. Ben Ish Chai there; and this is from Beit Yosef's words; and see below note 14.
(8) And Kol Bo wrote that when blood is found in an egg in the place that forbids it — even if it is a day-old egg it is forbidden, for the Sages did not distinguish. Beit Yosef; Torat Chesed general rule 62 end of din 1; and he wrote such is the custom — Shach s.k. 6; Pri Chadash note 6; and he concludes there — Shach: the later authorities' view appears that one should not distinguish; and it is implied he means that wherever blood is found in a day-old egg one should forbid. However Menachem Tziyoni on Torat Chesed there note 5 wrote: if found on the chelevon not at the place of the knot — one may permit in a day-old egg — not like Shach; and Beit Din — Pri Chadash there — wrote one may lean thus where there is financial loss in the matter.
(9) However, certainly when blood is found in a day-old egg it is only d'rabbanan, and a practical difference to be lenient in its doubt when it fell into liquid and one does not know if there are sixty, and similar cases. Pri Chadash there; Zivchei Tzedek note 7.
