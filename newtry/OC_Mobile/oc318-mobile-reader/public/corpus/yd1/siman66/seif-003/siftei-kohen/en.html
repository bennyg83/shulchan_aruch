Since there are poskim who hold that the place of the knot is at the blunt end and there are poskim who hold at the pointed end — if so, it has no permission except when found on the sides, and we are not expert as to how far the blunt end and pointed end extend; therefore the custom is to forbid it in every case — so wrote Torat Chatat general rule 62 end of din 1; and Bach and Lev Chayim daf 79b challenged the Rav on this, for certainly everyone is expert in this, for our eyes see that blood on the sides is far from the knot; rather the reason for the custom is according to the law, that we are concerned for the view of Ra'ah, who is more stringent on chelevon than on chalmon not on the knot, as Ran wrote on his name, and so Rokeach siman 367 wrote the version of the Jerusalem Talmud and an old version in our Gemara to be more stringent on chelevon than chalmon, end of his words (also in Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe they brought Ra'ah's and Rokeach's words briefly — see there).
