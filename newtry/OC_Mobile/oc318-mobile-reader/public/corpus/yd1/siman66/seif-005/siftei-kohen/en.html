It appears to me that it is permitted. For there are poskim who hold that there is no prohibition in this blood at all, even d'rabbanan; and even according to those who forbid it d'rabbanan — that is only not to eat it in its visible form because of mar'it ayin; but if it became mixed unintentionally — it is permitted; and so Rashba, etc., end of his words. And it is implied that if they mixed intentionally it is forbidden; and so Pri Chadash — and this is for him personally or for one for whom it was nullified on his account, because of a penalty, as below siman 99 seif 5:
Permitted. It is written in Sefer Lechem (daf 179b) that even according to the custom where they were accustomed to forbid the egg wherever blood is found — specifically when it is still visible and not when it became mixed, for they did not add a stringency upon a stringency, end of his words. And in truth the Rav's words in Darkei Moshe and Torat Chatat appear at first glance thus, since he ruled anonymously here like the Mechaber; but this is not a correct rationale (and the author of Lechem answered: the rationale is that since it is not visible they were not stringent; and this is what I said — they did not add a stringency; and even though by law it is forbidden, the law itself is because of the stringency of a doubt, end of his words) — for what of it that it became mixed? Even if it were visible and they discarded it, the egg would be forbidden; and all the more so according to what was explained, that we rule that the entire egg is forbidden by law in every case. And it appears more that the Rav did not rule anonymously here except in a place where they were not accustomed to forbid the egg ever; and there is a practical difference also in a place where they were accustomed to forbid the egg — if the blood itself became mixed in mixtures afterward, it is permitted.
