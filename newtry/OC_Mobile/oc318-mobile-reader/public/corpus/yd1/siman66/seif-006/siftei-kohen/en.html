And moreover, even if it were peeled — since the sauce in the pot does not rise above the cover, what is in the pot does not absorb from it, for it is no worse than a piece within the sauce when one did not stir and cover, end of Rashba's words that Beit Yosef brought; and the Rav in Torat Chatat end of general rule 63 wrote on this: it does not appear to permit by a peel, for the egg is forbidden because of itself and is not comparable to a piece that is forbidden only because of what is absorbed in it, and absorbed prohibition does not pass from piece to piece without sauce — until here his words. And it appears that even Rashba said only to permit what is in the pot; but the piece upon which the egg rests is forbidden the measure of removal, as below siman 92 and siman 105 seif 4; and this is what he meant: 'and moreover,' etc. — 'what is in the pot,' etc. — would be forbidden if so; if so, here when it is not peeled everything is permitted. And Rashba's wording in Beit Yosef is parsed more precisely thus — see there. And Maharshal wrote in Perek 9 end of siman 120 on Rashba's aforementioned words: all this is according to his view; but according to what we rule, that even roasted and hot into hot is considered like cooking and forbids up to sixty — here too it forbids up to sixty, end of his words. And from the Rav's words it appears that he agrees when it is not peeled; and it appears his reason is that even though we rule below siman 105 that even with a weak absorbed prohibition we measure sixty — nevertheless it was explained there in s.k. 17 that with absorbed prohibition, a weak flavor does not spread from piece to piece without sauce; and since the egg is within the peel it is considered absorbed prohibition (and Pri Chadash who copied Rashba's reason did not aim well, for we measure sixty). And Maharshal's words appear — for it is implied from Rashba's own words that if he would forbid up to sixty even where there is no sauce it would be forbidden and would not be considered absorbed prohibition what is within the peel; and so appears Issur VeHeter's view below shortly. And one must say that the word 'from the cover' in Rashba's words is a scribal error and should read 'above the egg,' for it deals when the egg is placed in a pot on the food; and so I found in a corrected text. But in Issur VeHeter general rule 42 din 6 he wrote: Rashba answered that in the cover of the hot food in which an egg was found with blood — it does not forbid the hot food except the measure of a peel, end of his words; and specifically when it did not touch the food itself; but if it touched it, sixty is required against the entire egg, as stated above, end of his words. And according to his words one must say that even though we rule below siman 92 seif 5 that a drop that fell on the pot opposite the sauce requires sixty — nevertheless here, since the egg is within the peel and the peel separates, it is like two hot pots touching one another — which is permitted below end of siman 92 (and accordingly it is proven that Issur VeHeter holds like Maharshal above shortly — examine).
