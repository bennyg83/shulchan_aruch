(6) [Seif 3] If a speck of blood is found upon it, one discards the blood, etc. In the holy community of Hebron 5660 they practiced: if a speck of blood is found on the chalmon, they remove it with cotton wool; and if the soft membrane on the chalmon remains whole, they eat the egg — from Mechaber note 2; and he wrote this is according to Shulchan Aruch's ruling in this law that specifically when found in the chalmon it forbids the whole; therefore if the speck was removed and the membrane on the chalmon remained whole, it proves the blood was in the chelevon — discard the blood and the rest is permitted, as the Geonim wrote and Maran ruled. And likewise in note 4 — except he concluded: this is to instruct others; however a scrupulous person should be stringent for himself and forbid any egg in which blood is found in any manner — and so the wholesome ones practiced, and many — end of his words. And Beit Din — Zivchei Tzedek note 5 wrote: and we in our place, from the day we established our view, thus we practice for every questioner — to forbid in every case the entire egg, whether in chalmon or chelevon — end of his words; and likewise Ben Ish Chai, Parashat Tahorot note 5 — such is the custom in Baghdad — see there — to forbid in every manner. However, where they already practiced like the custom of Hebron and Shulchan Aruch's ruling — one does not treat them lightly; only a scrupulous person should be stringent for himself as mentioned; and it appears that also one who comes to ask in that place where they practice like Shulchan Aruch — one informs him that some are stringent, for perhaps he will separate himself if he is scrupulous.
