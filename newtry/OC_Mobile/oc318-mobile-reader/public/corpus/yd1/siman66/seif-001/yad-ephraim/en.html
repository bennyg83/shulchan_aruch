Permitted to eat roasted eggs. And Maharachav wrote in Likutei Torah in Sha'ar HaMitzvot, Parashat Acharei, regarding a roasted egg — I saw my teacher that he would swallow it roasted without examining whether there is blood, and he was not concerned for the stringent view not to swallow a roasted egg; and he brought proof from the Talmud in many places regarding swallowing an egg alive or roasted, and the incident of R' Akiva who was fasting on erev Shabbos and they brought him a peeled egg and he swallowed it, and many such; and the reason — because it is a double doubt and d'rabbanan — end of his words; and Mahar Chasdav brought it and analyzed 'without examining' — implying there is examination; whereas in Hagahot Maimoniyot that he brought Beit Yosef, it implies there is no examination in roasting, as Maran wrote in this law; and Lechem wrote to distinguish between hard roasted eggs called 'ripidan,' where if there is blood it might be seen immediately — if so, no proof from the Talmud, for perhaps they examined; and it is forced to say his intent was that he did not command to empty them first, etc. (meaning as some write that some people empty into a bowl first and return to the shell, seal the hole, and roast — see there); and see there he also explained 'it is a double doubt d'rabbanan'; and concludes: in revelation it was revealed — our holy master the Ari said one need not be concerned for the stringency of people who write to empty first — see there. And see there he also wrote that certainly in the Rav's house, when making a dish or frying they would look if there is blood — emptying the whole egg from its shell to cook it, etc. — not so in swallowing — see there. And I did not understand the distinction, for certainly for making a dish, which is through mixture, one must be more stringent; therefore it certainly appears that after roasting it is far that examination would help, and that was the stringent view not to swallow a roasted egg; and if so, as Mahar Chasdav wrote, they should have examined and swallowed; and likewise proof from the Talmud.
And what 'without examining' means — that he was not concerned for the stringent view at all, and even as a stringency to examine lest something be recognizable after roasting — he did not do; therefore since he needed to swallow the egg after roasting, he was not concerned; but when they needed to cook or fry, where it is easy to check — it certainly appears he did as Mahar Chasdav wrote — see Tiferet Lemoshe, who wrote regarding an egg in the hen's innards in which blood is found — requires further study; and likewise Pri Chadash wrote: perhaps they decreed on it like a day-old egg:
