Strange eggs. Meaning that they are not from a male but rather that they are 'suspended from the ground' and are never fertilized, end of Tur and Rashi; and Rashi explained that then even if there is blood in them it is permitted. But the same applies to eggs that come from a male — they are permitted when there is no blood in them, even though they are strange; or when the blood is in a place that does not forbid it, in a place where leniency is practiced — and so Ran, and Beit Yosef brings it — see there:
It is permitted to eat them, etc. And he wrote in Shibbolei HaLeket in the name of the Geonim that if the eggs remained under the hen for three days they are forbidden because of revulsion, end of his words. And Beit Yosef wrote that this matter has no root in the Talmud, and I also did not see any posek who wrote thus; on the contrary, Rashi wrote that even if the hen sat on them for many days they are permitted, end of his words; and after him the Rav followed in Torat Chatat general rule 62 din 6. And I do not know why they rejected the Geonim's words without reason, for in the Talmud and all the poskim it states: strange eggs — 'a good soul shall eat them' (and Rashi and other commentators explain: one who is not fastidious and whose mind is not repelled by them); and therefore Rashi explained that even if the hen sat on them for many days they are permitted.
