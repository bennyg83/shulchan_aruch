Permitted to eat them. Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Shibbolei HaLeket in the name of the Geonim that if the egg remained under the hen three days — forbidden because of revulsion; and Beit Yosef wrote that Rashi contradicts, for it is written: a good soul shall eat them, even if it sat many days; and Shach wanted to distinguish between fastidious and not fastidious — and Peri Chadash spoke well, bringing from the Gemara Perek Makom SheNahagu that after three days eggs spoil and are unfit even for one who is not fastidious, as Rashi wrote there — within three days there is no great spoilage: a good soul shall eat them. But in truth the Geonim deal with eggs that are not 'suspended from the ground,' fit for laying and capable of slight formation — and the Gemara in Pesachim also deals with them: if suspended from the ground, why would the court inspect on the Festival; the rooster after three days because of loss of eggs — even if returned they are spoiled and will not succeed in hatching — a matter of desire; therefore in eggs fit for hatching the Gemara deals; and Rashi wrote explicitly on 'suspended from the ground' — even if it sat many days; and Beit Yosef who challenged Shibbolei HaLeket — that is regarding suspended from the ground — and he is correct; and in the Jerusalem Talmud Perek Makom SheNahagu it implies we require three days from above — see there:
