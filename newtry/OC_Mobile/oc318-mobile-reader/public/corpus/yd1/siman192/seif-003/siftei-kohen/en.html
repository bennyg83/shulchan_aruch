Even though she sat seven clean days. And even if she immersed; and so in Mordechai in the name of Rashbam.
When they arrange the marriage, etc. Bach wrote: the explanation is they postponed the marriage generally and did not agree at that time on which date the marriage would take place, but said when they would gather together after they have settled and made peace they would set the time for marriage — in this we are concerned perhaps she did not take it to heart to be careful in her bedikot, since she does not know the set time for marriage she is not concerned about those first seven clean days; but if the postponement from the first set time and the setting of the second time were all at the same session and they were occupied with that matter — there is no doubt she need not return to count seven clean days anew; and if she examined herself at that time and found herself pure — she is pure; and unlike Taz who was stringent here from his own reasoning without proof. However if through the quarrel the assembly broke up and at that session the bride agreed to marry another man and she entered the chuppah immediately that same day — the matter is obvious that this is a genuinely new proposal, and even if she examined herself and found herself pure she must count seven clean days again, for there is new desire for another man — end quote; and so Taz. And also in Maadanei Melech he copied Taz's latter words but not the former.
Even though she examined, etc. In responsa Masat Binyamin siman 5 he writes that this applies specifically where the chupah was delayed because they disagreed about the dowry or other needs of the marriage, so that she certainly still had concern for the chupah even if she herself did not know what was being delayed. But if the groom became sick, or the bride became sick, or the bride became niddah, even if she counted seven clean days and immersed before the postponed chupah, once she examined herself and found herself pure she need not examine again, for in that case her mind was not set on the chupah at all, as she thought the chupah could not take place because of the illness or because she was niddah.
