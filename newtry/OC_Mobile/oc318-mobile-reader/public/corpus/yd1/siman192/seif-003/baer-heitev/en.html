In responsa Masat Binyamin, regarding a bride who wore white and counted seven clean days with the wedding in mind, fixed for Rosh Chodesh Nisan, and when Rosh Chodesh Nisan arrived the groom did not come, and the next day a messenger from the groom came saying that some unavoidable delay had arisen on the road and he would arrive in a day or two, and they made the chupah two or three days after Rosh Chodesh Nisan: in such a case she certainly does not require another seven clean days, even if she did not examine herself on the intervening days, provided that seven days did not pass without examination. Since the wedding was not postponed in essence, but only delayed because of some unavoidable circumstance, she did not abandon reliance on the first seven clean days, and her mind relied on the groom coming after a day or two; she therefore paid attention to herself and kept in mind to remain clean so as not to enter concern of desire. Thus his words. The Taz disagrees and writes that the bride is certainly uncertain in her mind, for all roads are presumed dangerous, and perhaps there is danger and he will not come at all; after she learns that he is certainly coming, this is new desire. The Shach writes that in truth one should not be stringent in these laws, for the entire root of this law is rabbinic. Maharitz Tzahalon siman 67 ruled by the law that even if the wedding was delayed two or three days from the set time, there is no concern at all requiring a new count of seven clean days; see there, and see responsa Shevut Yaakov siman 75.
