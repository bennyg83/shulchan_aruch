That she counted. Even if she already immersed. Bach writes that if the wedding was postponed without setting a new date, she must count again because she no longer remained focused on the wedding. If at the same meeting the wedding was immediately rescheduled, she need not recount. If the match dissolved and she agreed to marry another man, this is a completely new claim and new desire, requiring seven new clean days. Taz adds that if she later returns to the first groom after agreeing to the second, she also requires a new count.
That she examined. Masat Binyamin permits relying on the original seven clean days where the groom was delayed by an unforeseen event and arrived a few days later, provided seven full days did not pass without examination. Taz disagrees because renewed certainty of his arrival creates new desire. Shach writes that one should not be overly stringent because the entire rule is rabbinic.
