Perhaps because of desire, etc. The Maggid Mishneh wrote in chapter 9 of Hilchos Issurei Bi'ah that this is a rabbinic matter, for the Sages were stringent through this decree; according to the Torah she is entirely permitted, for even if she saw blood without sensation she is not impure according to the Torah, all the more so in this case, where she did not even find a stain, etc. End quote. This resolves for me what is stated in the first chapter of Sotah regarding Tamar, “and she sat at the entrance of Einayim,” meaning that she gave attention to her words and told Yehudah, “I am pure from niddah.” We likewise find regarding Rus that she entered Boaz's bed after immersing. This is difficult, for they should have required seven clean days after agreeing together to marry one another. According to what I wrote, it is resolved, for this decree had not yet been enacted. However, this requires further study. Since it is rabbinic, where do we find that this decree was newly enacted? If it was through Rava's ruling that a woman who was asked to marry and consented must observe seven clean days, and Rava introduced this stringency in his time, there is a difficulty from the incident in the chapter HaCholetz. When Rav arrived in Darshis, he said, “Who wishes to be my wife for a day?” The Gemara challenges him from Rava's ruling that a woman who was asked to marry and consented must observe seven clean days. What is the challenge? This stringency was only introduced in Rava's time. It appears to me that when the Sages decreed concerning stains, as stated at the beginning of siman 190, and did not permit a woman merely because she had not sensed a flow, this decree was enacted as well; the fact that she felt no sensation does not help her. So it appears to me.
Furthermore, it appears that one can answer that regarding Tamar and Rus there was no concern that they might see blood because of desire. Immediately upon Yehudah's and Boaz's consent, respectively, they were together with them in bed. This is comparable to one who has bread in his basket, unlike the case of an ordinary bride.
And she does not require a hefsek, etc. The reason is that she is not presumed to have seen blood, since it is not certain that she saw.
