Some say that if she was pure, etc. In Toras HaBayis there, the author cites the view of those who prohibit, in accordance with the plain language of the Gemara, which says that if he had relations with her, supervision is unnecessary. Others permit, explaining that when the Gemara says “he had relations,” it means that he was fit to have relations. The author of Toras HaBayis ruled leniently. His proof is from a mu'ad ox that saw oxen three times and did not gore, thereby reverting to the status of an ordinary ox. This demonstrates that we do not say that its impulse merely rested at that time and will return today or tomorrow. The same should apply in this case. Nevertheless, one who is stringent is deserving of blessing. End quote. In my humble opinion, the case is not comparable to the proof. His proof from a mu'ad ox is not valid, for we rule at the end of the first chapter of Bava Kamma, 15a, that ordinary oxen have a presumption of being guarded, etc. Thus, every ox that is mu'ad to gore deviates from the nature of ordinary oxen. Therefore, whenever we observe that it saw oxen and did not gore, its deviation is nullified and it becomes like other oxen. Here, however, the ordinary human being has a powerful inclination and does not restrain himself, as Tosafos wrote in the first chapter of Kesubos, 9a, s.v. Mai lav, etc.: “Alternatively, since he secludes himself with her, he certainly has relations with her, for a person does not restrain himself. Although regarding a yevamah we say that he may restrain himself for 30 days, ordinarily a person cannot restrain himself even for one hour,” etc. End quote. Thus, this groom who did not have relations departed from the ordinary nature of the world. We do not say that because he deviated for one hour or several days he will continue that way forever. On the contrary, as long as we observe that he continues to deviate, we accept that fact; when we no longer observe it, the matter reverts to ordinary human nature, which characterizes the majority of people. The novelty extends no further than its particular case, and one cannot derive anything beyond it. Indeed, the Tosafos cited above provide proof to the contrary. Regarding a yevamah, a man may restrain himself for 30 days, but no longer. Although he established a pattern for 30 days by not having relations with her, we nevertheless say that afterward his inclination will overpower him and he will not restrain himself. The same applies here. The novelty extends no further than its particular case. Therefore, the view of those who prohibit appears correct to me, in accordance with the plain language of the Gemara, that permission exists only if he actually had relations with her. This is not merely a case in which one who is stringent is deserving of blessing. Rather, it is the actual law, and supervision is required.
