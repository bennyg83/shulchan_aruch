These seven days are counted, etc. All this is from the Rashba in Toras HaBayis. The Rashba wrote in Mishmeres HaBayis, folio 167a: "The custom of Israel is Torah. In all our communities they instituted that the festive Shabbos meals be prepared on the Shabbos before the wedding. This is considered the demand for marriage, and from that point these seven days are counted." End quote. His words follow the custom of entering the marriage on Friday evening and immersing at the onset of Shabbos, as explained in Even HaEzer, siman 63. It must be that as soon as Friday of the week before the wedding arrives and they begin preparing for the meal, that day is considered the day of the demand, for otherwise there would not be seven clean days. This is readily understood. Nowadays, when the usual practice is to immerse on Thursday night, the day of the demand must be considered two or three days before Shabbos, in accordance with what is stated at the beginning of Kesubos, that a person should exert himself for three days.
