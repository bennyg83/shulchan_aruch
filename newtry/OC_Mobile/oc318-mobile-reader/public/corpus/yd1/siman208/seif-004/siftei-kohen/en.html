One who transgressed niduy, etc. It is astonishing — below siman 334 s.k. 28 he wrote if he treated lightly his niduy and cherem he must observe prohibition like days he observed permissibility before they annul him — end quote. Beit Chadash there answered — here it deals when he transgressed intentionally because he cannot fulfill his vow, e.g. he vowed from grain and cannot live without grain and the like — considered somewhat like an ones; but in a matter he can fulfill but treats lightly his niduy, saying he does not care about niduy and cherem — they were stringent that he must observe prohibition — end quote. One cannot say thus, for from what is written in seif 2 at the end "and if there is an obstacle in the matter," etc., "he cannot be careful," etc.; and from giving the reason "because of niduy" which is rabbinic — it implies it deals with a matter he can fulfill, for otherwise even in a biblical vow we do not penalize to observe prohibition like the first days. Rather it appears to answer — this law is taken from Rivash in Beit Yosef: "One need not observe niduy at all even one hour, for we hold in rabbinic vows we do not penalize; and likewise matters a menudeh must observe are not biblical; but to annul for him something he swore and swore in niduy and cherem — a clear matter — we do not annul until he observes prohibition in that matter like days he observed permissibility," end quote — meaning if he treated lightly the laws of niduy, not observing what a menudeh must observe, there is only rabbinic; but if he accepted something in niduy or cherem and transgressed that matter — it is biblical, for niduy and cherem themselves are biblical; therefore we do not annul until he observes prohibition in that matter like days he observed permissibility. If so, in siman 334 s.k. 28 it deals like this end of Rivash — he treated lightly niduy and transgressed that for which he was ostracized, or accepted cherem or niduy — this is the true law.
