And he does not require release, etc. This is inferred from the latter clause, which states, 'and if they sought release,' etc. The Mishnah does not state this here, but only there, because that expression is more readily understood stringently than leniently, as the Ran writes there. See Tosafot, s.v. 'an unspecified expression,' etc. According to the Rambam, who holds that even one who explicitly associates his vow with something forbidden requires release when he is an unlearned person, as explained above in siman 205, there is no need to teach this law here, since release would be required in any event. The words of the Shulchan Aruch therefore require study. See the Shach, who presses to resolve this.
And if the majority, etc. In that case it is like one who vows by a cherem, etc., because the expression is more readily understood as prohibitive, as explained above. This accords with the Mishnah on 62b, 'everything follows,' etc.; and see there, 49a, 'one Sage according to,' etc.; and there, 52b and 53a. See the Shach.
