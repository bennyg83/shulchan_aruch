And if most people of the place, etc. — Beit Yosef challenged from what is written in siman 217: one who vows from oil — if they are uncertain between the two, both are forbidden even though most of their supply is from one of them; and he answered that there everyone is supplied from both, except that most of their supply is from one — therefore since everyone is supplied from both, both are forbidden — end. And his words are astounding, for if we heed majority here too we should heed majority, and we do not follow the minority of people's supply. And in truth one may further challenge this case here where we follow majority, and so in siman 280 seif 14: if the place he vowed in is a barley harvest we follow it, even leniently; and in siman 217 seif 3 he wrote regarding doubt what is called "pickled" that we do not follow majority in vows but say to be strict. And Maharach wrote in siman 280 to resolve that case in siman 208 regarding salted meat of consecrated animals that we follow majority because the majority call it "salted" unspecified; and so in siman 280 regarding harvest — the majority call wheat harvest and barley harvest "harvest" unspecified; but regarding oil in siman 217 where we do not follow majority, it deals where both are called "oil" unspecified except that the majority are supplied from one of them. And he omitted that case in siman 217 seif 3 regarding pickled where we say there: some people of the place call it thus and some do not call — we do not follow majority but this is a vow doubt, and every vow doubt is strict; behold this is the main strictness that we do not follow the majority's terminology. The challenge returns to its place; and it appears in my humble view that all is resolved: certainly wherever there is doubt in the forbidden item itself we go strict and do not follow majority — this is the vow strictness that whatever can enter prohibition is forbidden even if a minority in that name; but if the doubt is not in the forbidden item itself but he attached a sign, then we interpret that sign according to common majority language; and since he came to give a sign and explain his words, he certainly intended something clear, for a person does not put himself in doubt; therefore in that case in siman 217 regarding oil and also regarding pickled, which is the forbidden item itself, we go strict even after minority; not so here where he attached these fruits to salted meat, and so in siman 280 regarding harvest — he says this thing shall be forbidden to me until the harvest; behold the doubt is in what he attached the forbidden item to, and he came to clarify what he attached it to — then we say he intended something clear, namely the majority; and clear proof from Kiddushin chapter HaOmer (64a): one who has two groups of daughters and said "I betrothed my elder daughter" and does not know whether elder among elders or elder among younger — R. Yosi says all are permitted except the elder among elders, and we hold like him; we explain the reason there that a person does not put himself in doubt — Rashi: he would need to ask which she is; and we say there that regarding vows too it is so — behold whenever one comes to give a sign to his words, i.e. says "the elder," we say a person does not put himself in doubt; whereas if he said only "my daughter is betrothed" unspecified, all are in doubt and we do not say he certainly intended the elder since most of the world marry the elder first; and even though Rosh wrote in chapter HaIsh Mekadesh, etc., and Rabbenu Tam said perhaps we do not say they are not given to relations except "one of your daughters," but "your daughter is betrothed to me" implies specifically the elder — he did not say so for practical halachah — end; it implies for halachah without action he would hold even for "your daughter is betrothed to me" he intended the elder; but there that argument of Rabbenu Tam is strong, for a person does not marry the younger before the elder since Scripture says "so shall not be done in our place," etc., and it is as though he said explicitly "the elder," as Rosh wrote there in his name — if she is betrothed to him he certainly intended her; but in other places where we have no strong presumption before us to which he intended, we certainly go strict even after minority; but if he came to clarify his words and attached a sign, then we follow majority; and so this case here appears correct and clear in my humble view; and I saw others write very forced things on this — what I wrote is clear with Heaven's help.
