(Siman 205 seif 1.) And if he says his intention is like salted meat, etc., and even if he is an am ha'aretz. See Shach s.k. 2 what he wrote in Rambam's view — apparently he is stringent only in chapter 2 of Shevuot, law 13, when he does not know whether attaching to a forbidden matter counts as a vow or not; but here he knows attaching to a forbidden matter is not a vow, and said his intention was idolatry — not believed. It appears to Rabbi L in Shach's words in Rambam's intention — with this Beit Yosef's difficulty on Kesef Mishneh above Rambam there from the sugya of "ve'elu mutarim" is resolved — we learn there Ravina said thus is taught: "these are permitted like secular, like pig meat," etc.; and if "secular" were not taught, one would think inquiry is needed; who would attribute such intention, yet the end teaches — see there. Now how did Rambam rule there one who says like pig meat, if the speaker is an am ha'aretz needs inquiry, when the Gemara said one does not attribute such intention; and Kesef Mishneh was also astonished there on Rosh — see there. According to Shach's words one may say — investigate.
