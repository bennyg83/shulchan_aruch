Stringently. This applies where he does not explain his original intent but says only that he vowed as the Sages interpret the words. We then construe them stringently. Rosh and Rambam. Mabit writes that one who forgets whether he vowed or swore must observe both sets of stringencies. All the more so, uncertainty whether he swore at all is treated stringently.
An ignorant person. Bach requires formal annulment for an ignorant person, as at the beginning of siman 205. Shach distinguishes: there the person did not know whether invoking an intrinsically forbidden object creates a vow; here he knows it does not, and the phrase merely has two possible meanings. Once he explains his intent, no annulment is needed.
He is not. Hagahot Perishah limits the rule to an ignorant person and trusts a Torah scholar. Shach disagrees where local usage gives the expression only one ordinary meaning. Where the majority use an expression leniently, that usage may likewise govern. If the speaker explains that he intended the lenient meaning and the majority is not against him, we accept it; if he left without explanation, we rule stringently.
