To be stringent. That is, when he did not specify what was in his mind but merely says "so I vowed" — as the sages interpreted it, we interpret it stringently. So wrote the Rosh, and so is the view of the Rambam. And the responsum of Mabit wrote: one who forgot whether he vowed or swore, we impose on him the stringencies of both — the stringency of a vow and the stringency of an oath; and all the more so if he forgot whether he swore or not, he is forbidden due to doubt.
An ignoramus. Bach wrote that one should be stringent to require a query for an ignoramus, as in the beginning of siman 205. But Shach wrote that it seems the primary view of the author here is that we are only stringent there when he does not know whether vowing by something forbidden counts as a vow or not; but here he knows he is vowing by something forbidden — it is not a vow; it is only that he said simply "yayin nesech" and the expression carries two meanings, therefore when he specified he does not need a query — end quote.
It is not. The Hagahot Perishah wrote that this applies specifically to an ignoramus, but a Torah scholar is believed by kal va-chomer from below seif 2; and the plain reading of the poskim does not seem so — for only there is the Torah scholar believed since the expression carries two meanings but its plain sense is stringent, therefore an ignoramus is not believed; but here since the people of the place call ordinary non-Jewish wine simply "yayin nesech," its meaning is only one — a vow — and similarly to be lenient, e.g., where the majority call salted non-Jewish meat simply "salted meat" and similarly ordinary non-Jewish wine simply "yayin nesech," we follow the lenient reading. And Beit Yosef raised the difficulty that in siman 217 seif 17 we follow the minority for stringency — and it seems the primary ruling is as written in Perishah, that this case is different since he specified his words and said he intended non-Jewish yayin nesech, therefore we do not follow the stringent reading if the majority is not against him; but if he did not specify his words but merely vowed and left, we follow the stringent reading — and see below siman 220 seif 4 — end quote of Shach.
