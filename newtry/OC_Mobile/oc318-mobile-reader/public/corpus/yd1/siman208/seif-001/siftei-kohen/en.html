Unspecified vows are to be stringent, etc. — that is, when he did not specify what was in his heart but says “I vowed thus” — as the Sages explained, we interpret to be stringent; see above siman 236 s.k. 1, Rosh; and likewise Rambam chapter 2 of Laws of Vows, law 7; and likewise Rabbeinu Yerucham, after citing Rosh’s words wrote “and so Rambam wrote,” etc.; and it is astonishing about what Beit Yosef wrote that Rambam chapter 2 does not hold so. — In responsum of Mabit vol. 2 siman 256: one who forgot whether he vowed or swore, we impose upon him the stringencies of both vow and oath; all the more one who forgot whether he swore or not is forbidden from doubt — see there.
And even if he is an am ha'aretz. The Bach writes that this is astonishing, for this is correct only according to the Rosh and Tur. According to Rambam, whose view the Mechaber follows above at the beginning of siman 205, an am ha'aretz who attaches his vow to something prohibited requires a question to a sage, and the same should apply here. This requires study. He concludes that one should be stringent like Rambam and require a question here as well for an am ha'aretz. It appears to me, however, that the Mechaber holds that Rambam also agrees here. Rambam is stringent there only where the person does not know whether attaching a vow to something prohibited has the force of a vow. Here, however, he knows that attaching a vow to something prohibited does not create a vow; he merely said yayin nesech without qualification, which can mean either wine of idolatry or wine of the altar. As long as he does not explain his words we interpret them stringently, but once he explains that he intended idolatrous yayin nesech, which does not create a vow, he is believed. The point here is only to teach that no question is required because of a davar hanadur. Examine carefully.
And if most, etc. — it implies specifically majority, but we do not follow minority; and Beit Yosef challenged: below, siman 217 seif 17, we follow minority to be stringent; and he answered — there is different, since they draw supplies from both but most of their supply from one; but this is unclear, for there the reason is because doubtful vows are to be stringent. And furthermore, below siman 217 seif 3 Rambam and Mechaber wrote: if some people call it such and some call it such, we do not follow majority, etc. Rather it appears the main view is as Perishah wrote — here is different, since he explained his words and said "I intended yayin nesech"; therefore we do not go to be stringent if majority is not against him. But if he did not explain his words, only vowed and went, we go to be stringent; and see above siman 220 seif 14.
To salted meat of consecrated offerings, etc. — and the same to be lenient: when the majority call salted meat of idolatry “salted meat” unspecified, and likewise call yayin nesech of idolatry “yayin nesech” unspecified, we go to be lenient; and likewise in Rambam chapter 2.
He is not believed. Hagahos Prisha writes that this applies specifically to an am ha'aretz, but a talmid chacham is believed by an a fortiori inference from seif 2 below. The straightforward words of the poskim do not imply this. Specifically below the talmid chacham is believed because his expression can bear two meanings, although its plain meaning is stringent, and therefore an am ha'aretz is not believed. Here, however, since the people of the place call wine offered to Heaven yayin nesech, the expression has only one meaning, namely the language of a vow. Examine carefully.
