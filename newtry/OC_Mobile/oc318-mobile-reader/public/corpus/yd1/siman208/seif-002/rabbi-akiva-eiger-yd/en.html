And in a vow not to eat from such-and-such fruit for thirty days — he transgressed ten days, and four days before completion of thirty days comes to inquire about his vow — it appears to me he need not observe ten days in prohibition, since after thirty days there is no vow prohibition upon him; we only penalize him for the four days of his vow that he observe prohibition not to annul for him — and automatically forbidden to him by vow prohibition; the penalty only not to annul — and automatically he remains in his prohibition from the vow. And see what is written below (siman 238) in the name of teshuvah Sha'ar Efraim; according to his view, if we wait for his annulment until he observes prohibition four days, he will have no annulment at all for his vow afterward — one may say we do not penalize thus; and perhaps we penalize him not to annul until one hour before completion of the vow — doubtful for practical law. Afterward I saw in Yachin uBoaz (siman 58, 16d) "vehaTaam hashevi'i" — he wrote in the name of his ancestor the Tashbetz: one who accepted by oath or cherem to his fellow to do something for a time and the time passed without doing — if they annul him he is not permitted — see there. In my humble opinion, doubtful.
