One who vowed by cherem and said, “I did not vow except by the cherem of the sea” (meaning: a net with which they trap fish in the sea); “korban,” and said, “I did not vow except by korbanot of kings”; “Behold, my body is a korban,” and said, “I did not vow except by the bone I placed to be the subject of vow”; “Konam, my wife shall benefit me,” and said, “I did not vow except by my first wife whom I divorced” — if he is a Torah scholar, he is believed and does not need release. And if he is an am ha’aretz, he needs questioning, so that they not treat vows lightly. However, regret suffices, and it is not necessary to open a breach for him. And if he transgressed the vow — we do not force him to conduct prohibition like the days on which he transgressed the vow and treated them as permitted. But one who vowed a complete vow that is d’oraisa and transgressed it (intentionally) — we force him, and they do not release him unless he conducts prohibition like the days on which he transgressed and treated them as permitted. And if there is an obstacle in the matter — for example, he vowed regarding something he cannot be careful about, such as that he forbade upon himself all the fruits in the world except grain, and he transgressed — they release him immediately, and they do not force him to conduct prohibition like the days on which he transgressed, so that he not come to an obstacle. And there is one who says that nevertheless one should be stringent with him not to release him through regret but through a breach. And from this we learn regarding one who forbade upon himself meat and wine if he transgresses a certain sin, and he transgressed it and was not careful from meat and wine and comes to release his vow — that they release him immediately, and he does not need to conduct prohibition like the days on which he treated them as permitted, since there is concern of an obstacle in the matter. {Rama: However, if it appears to the sage that he can stand firm and will not transgress again, they do not release him until he conducts prohibition like the days on which he treated them as permitted (Mordechai, beginning of Shevuos Shetayim; and in the responsum of Ramban, siman 225).}
