And he completes his intercourse. Meaning with a living organ — so wrote the Tur; and this applies even though she saw blood at the time of that intercourse.
Her time to see arrived but she did not see. The Tur writes here: even a mature woman whose virginal blood has ceased — and Beit Yosef found it difficult: what is meant by "even a mature woman," the opposite would seem true — for a mature woman there is more reason to attribute it to menstrual blood since she has no virginal blood. But it seems no difficulty at all: the Tur's intent is to teach us two novel rulings. First — even if it is certainly virginal blood and not menstrual blood (e.g., her time had not arrived and she did not see), we are still stringent because one may come to confuse one type of blood with another. Then it further teaches: do not say that only where it is certain she sees virginal blood is it appropriate to be stringent on account of confusion — but if it is not clear that she saw at all, even virginal blood, why be stringent? We would say: since it is not yet clear to him that she saw, why impose needless stringency? Thus when she is mature and her virginal blood has ceased, he need not withdraw — to this the Tur says that even so one should be stringent, for perhaps she saw and he was unaware; and further, even if it is certain she did not see — i.e., she examined herself — we are still concerned lest semen covered it. The matter is clear, and not as my father-in-law z"l wrote that the order of the passages is reversed; rather the matters are as the halacha requires, in their proper sequence.
And semen covered it. That he needed here this and did not say as stated at the beginning of siman 193 regarding desire and she did not feel it — one can say because there she goes here and there and certainly not relevant to seek anywhere if blood came from her; not so here where she lies in bed and it is possible to see if there is blood in the sheet beneath her; therefore he needed to say perhaps it was covered with semen; and in the Perishah he strained himself in vain on this.
And she should not begin counting until the fifth day, etc. I found in the name of the Maharal of Prague: even though the world now customarily has the menstruant begin counting from the sixth day as explained in siman 196, nonetheless when she begins counting after the first intercourse of a virgin she begins from the fifth day, and he ruled thus in practical halacha — see siman 196 seif 11 what I wrote on this.
