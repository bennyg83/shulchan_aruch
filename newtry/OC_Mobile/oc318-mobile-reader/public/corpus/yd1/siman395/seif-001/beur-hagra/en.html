(Likkut) Since etc. since etc. Tosafot and the Rosh; but the Ramban disagreed with them and wrote that even part of the night counts, and what is stated (19b) “since the comforters stood up” — because if he wished he could sit the entire day, the right was his, and it was customary for comforters to come, and therefore it is forbidden since they are still consoling him; and what he cited as proof from what is stated 27a “and at the end of Shabbat even though etc.” — from there the proof is to the contrary, for if it entered your mind that the whole night is required, there would be no need to state it (see Ritva there); rather, since part of the night counts, one might think that one thing suffices — the removal of the shoe — therefore it informs us that one requires all the laws of the seven, even overturning the bed, even for one hour; and what they cited as proof from a shomeret yom (at the end of chapter 2 of Megillah) is also no proof, for there we derive from a zavah that their immersion is by day, and on the contrary the proof is from there to the contrary; see Rashi in Pesachim 81a s.v. alternatively one who sees etc.; and although according to the Rabbanan specifically by day — that is because they hold we do not say part of the day is like the whole, and she interrupts even if she saw at the end of the day and it was still within the days of her zivah, which is not the case according to Rabbi Yehudah; but Tosafot there s.v. alternatively explained according to their view, see there; and the Ramban wrote that Rabbenu Tam ruled accordingly in a time of need, see there (in Torat HaAdam) (end of note):
