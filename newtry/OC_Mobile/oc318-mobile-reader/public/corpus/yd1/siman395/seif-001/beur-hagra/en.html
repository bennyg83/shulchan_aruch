Once. Moed Katan 19b asks according to whom this ruling follows. (Likkut) Once. See Tosafot 21b. (Likkut) Once... once. Tosafot and Rosh require the entire night. Ramban disagrees and writes that even part of the night counts. The statement "once the comforters have arisen" is because the mourner may remain seated all day if he wishes, and comforters commonly continue coming, so the mourning situation remains active. Tosafot bring proof from the passage on 27a concerning Motzaei Shabbos, but Ramban argues that the passage actually proves the opposite: if the whole night were required, there would be no need for the statement. Since part of the night counts, one might have thought removing shoes alone would suffice, so it teaches that all seven-day restrictions, including overturning the bed, must be observed even briefly. Their proof from a shomeret yom is also not decisive because that law is derived from a zavah whose immersion is by day. Ramban writes that Rabbenu Tam himself ruled leniently in pressing circumstances. See Torat HaAdam.
