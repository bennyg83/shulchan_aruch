For part of the day is considered like the whole day. See the Tur regarding the dispute among the poskim whether one specifically needs part of the daytime, or whether part of the preceding night also suffices. The Mechaber follows Maharam of Rothenburg, who requires part of the day itself. Responsa Radbaz III, siman 559, rules that regarding Torah study and marital relations, which involve a mitzvah, we should follow the lenient authorities and treat part of the night as equivalent to the whole day. Regarding bathing, anointing, and other practices that do not involve a mitzvah, we follow the stringent authorities who require part of the daytime specifically. Nevertheless, for marital relations one should wait somewhat longer into the night than usual, so that some measure of mourning is also observed at night. See there, and the new Responsa Radbaz, siman 268.
After leaving the synagogue. See Responsa Givat Shaul, siman 70, concerning a mourner whose seventh day falls on Shabbat, whether he may put on a clean white shirt in the morning before Shacharit. The Taz, siman 402, seif katan 5, distinguishes between mourning practices requiring an affirmative act, such as removing shoes and overturning the bed, which require waiting until the consolers rise, and passive restrictions such as laundering and haircutting, for which sunrise suffices. According to that distinction, putting on a shirt, which is a passive restriction, would also become permitted at sunrise. Givat Shaul, however, argues that the Taz's distinction is difficult and concludes that even passive restrictions require waiting until the consolers rise. The same applies to wearing a clean white shirt, which remains forbidden until after the prayer service.
