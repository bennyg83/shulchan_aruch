To add. Even if that day falls on Shabbat, the law of twelve months applies to it; so it is in Terumat HaDeshen, siman 292. However, when the year is a leap year, and he is permitted to enter a feast hall after twelve months as written in siman 391 seif 2, he does not practice the law of twelve months on the day his father or mother died, even though he recites Kaddish and Barekhu and fasts that day; once he has ceased his mourning, he does not return to his mourning. So wrote the Bach. It appears that this applies specifically to other forms of mourning, but eating at a joyous banquet and feast is forbidden every year on the day his father or mother died, as the Rav wrote below at the end of siman 402; all the more so here. End quote from Shach. See what I wrote above in siman 391. However, even according to those who are lenient in this, as the Levush wrote and as I wrote above, nevertheless in the first year, even in a leap year, he should not eat at a joyous meal. The custom is that if his yahrzeit falls on Shabbat or Yom Tov, they wear a weekday tallit to make some small change; so is the custom every year, even after the first year. Regarding whether one completes a yahrzeit fast, see siman 402.
