To add the day on which he died, and so forth. Terumat HaDeshen, responsum 292, explains that Kaddish and Barechu are recited and one fasts on that day because it is the anniversary of the death; therefore certain mourning practices are observed. According to this reasoning, the same should apply every year forever on the yahrzeit. My father-in-law writes that in a leap year one may attend a festive meal after twelve months, as stated in siman 391. Consequently, the law of the twelve months would not return on the anniversary itself, even though one fasts, because once he attended a feast and interrupted his mourning, he does not resume it. His words are astonishing, for these practices apply every year on the yahrzeit. The Rama likewise writes in siman 402, seif 12, that one should never eat at a festive meal on the night of the yahrzeit. The Levush comments that perhaps this was the custom in the Rama’s locale, since in most places through which he traveled he did not see it practiced.
