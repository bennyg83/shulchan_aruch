In what case is this said? When there is place for suspicion; but when the matters indicate this is not from the field owner's knowledge but grew on their own, we do not obligate him to diminish. How so? Such as grain in which darnel and clover grew—and their seed is for human food—and species of grasses grew in it that this one ruins, and all similar cases. And how does one know clover was sown for human food? When it was sown in beds and has a border around it. And likewise a threshing floor place in which many species grew—we do not obligate to uproot, for it is known he does not want plants to grow in the threshing floor place; and if he removed part of them, we tell him uproot all except one species, for he revealed his intent that he wants the rest to remain.
