(297—11) Whether one plows, sows, pulls them with a wagon or stone as one, or drives them as one—even with voice he receives lashes. {Rama: A non-Jew driving kilayim—it is permitted for an Israelite to greet him or speak with him in a loud voice, for we are not concerned lest the animals go because of his voice—for in all prohibitions an unintended act is permitted if it is not a definite outcome (pesik reisha). But if an Israelite walks on the road with a non-Jew and an Israelite's load is on the non-Jew's wagon that he drives as kilayim, the Israelite may not walk near the wagon, for sometimes the wagon is pressed and he will shout at the animals to go quickly—and this is forbidden because of driving kilayim (Terumat HaDeshen siman 298).} But the one who harnesses is exempt until he pulls or until he drives. {Rama: And some say it is forbidden to drive them or pull them only when tied together (Tur in the name of the Rosh); therefore it is permitted for a rider to pull with a rope a dog beside a horse (there in the name of Avi HaEzri). And some disagree (there in the name of R' Efraim).}
