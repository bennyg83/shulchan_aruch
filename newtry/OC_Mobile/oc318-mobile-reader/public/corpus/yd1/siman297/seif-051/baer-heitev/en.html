He led them. Taz argues that mere leading without common labor or a shared load should not create liability. The case concerns joint work or carrying the same rope. He therefore forbids tying two different species of birds to one cord to prevent escape, because their movement causes them jointly to carry the cord.
Some disagree. Orchot Chaim forbids tying a hunting bird's tether to the horse's saddle because of mixed species. Taz similarly forbids sitting in a wagon pulled by a horse while a cow is also tied to it and protests butchers who do so. Derishah's defense that it is only temporary is rejected because a Torah prohibition does not depend on duration.
The wild ox. Shach questions Rama, because earlier he cites the view that a wild ox is a wild animal, in which case breeding it with a domestic ox is plainly forbidden.
A mule. Every mule comes specifically from a horse and donkey. Therefore, a mule whose mother was a donkey necessarily had a horse father and may be paired with a female mule of the same parentage. It may not be paired with a donkey, though lashes do not apply because perhaps paternal lineage is disregarded. Shach.
