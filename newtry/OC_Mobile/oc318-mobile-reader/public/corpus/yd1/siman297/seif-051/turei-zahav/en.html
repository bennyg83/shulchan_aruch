Or he drove them as one. This is Rambam's language; and ostensibly implies that for driving alone one is liable even if they have no work together or carrying anything; but it is not so, as explained in Tosafot and Rosh in chapter "with what animal" that Beit Yosef brought in this siman on what they said in the Gemara: and a person is permitted with all of them, etc. — and they challenged: even a person should be forbidden — challenge: what relevance has kilayim since he does not pull cart and wagon — for it deals with Shabbat; and he said: forbidden to drive even if he pulls nothing — for if so forbidden to move from its place ox of consecrated animals that is considered two species in last chapter of Makkot; and one could say: only when not plowing on weekday he speaks; and it deals when they pull something; or one could say carrying is considered what the ropes carry; and so proven in Yerushalmi that due to carrying the ropes there is liability — end quote; and if so also here where Rambam wrote "or he drove them as one" — meaning they do some work or carry the rope they carry together; also in explaining the mishnah chapter 8 of Kilayim Rambam wrote: one who drives in kilayim is liable — meaning there are wagons pulled by them; only Rambam holds even if kilayim are not tied together — forbidden when doing work together; and Rosh holds only when tied together; and so Tur concluded in the name of Avi HaEzri that there is no side of prohibition in driving until he does work with both together and via tying — end quote; only I saw Maharach Or Zaru'a wondered on Tur who says without work permitted even when tied together — this is not so, for tied together even without doing work in them — merely driving together — certainly forbidden per the mishnah chapter 8 of Kilayim: one who drives receives forty, etc.; and he extended to explain Tur's words that he speaks only via tying; and all this is not as explained from Tosafot, Rosh, and Rambam as I wrote. And since the prohibition is due to carrying the rope as we mentioned — it seems one should not tie two species of birds on one rope so they not escape — for when they shake themselves as birds move from place to place even when bound — then they carry the connecting thread they are tied with; and behold in two species of birds there is kilayim as stated end of (Marbeh) [the cow]; and the world does not know to be careful of this.
