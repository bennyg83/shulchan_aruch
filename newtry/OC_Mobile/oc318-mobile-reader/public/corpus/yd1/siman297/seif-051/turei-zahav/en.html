Or he drove them together. The Rambam does not mean that mere driving without any labor or burden creates liability. Tosafos and the Rosh explain that the animals must pull something, or at least jointly carry the connecting ropes. The Yerushalmi likewise attributes liability to the burden of the ropes. The Rambam's phrase therefore means that they perform some joint labor or carry the rope together, even if they are not tied to one another. The Rosh requires both joint labor and tying. Accordingly, one should not tie two different species of birds with one cord to prevent escape. When they move, they jointly carry the cord, and the prohibition of kilayim applies to birds as well. People commonly fail to observe this.
Therefore, it is permitted for the rider, etc. This is the Rosh's opinion. Nevertheless, one may not sit in a wagon to which a cow is tied while a horse pulls the wagon. The butchers who do this must be stopped. The fact that it is done only temporarily does not lessen a Torah prohibition, and the lenient gloss cited by the Derishah should not be relied upon.
