It is permitted to tell a non-Jew to sow for him, etc. At first glance this seems to permit a Jew to instruct any non-Jew, which is difficult. Regarding vineyard mixtures outside Eretz Yisrael, siman 296 permits this only through a minor non-Jew, and castration through a non-Jew is prohibited because instructing a non-Jew to violate a Torah prohibition remains unresolved and is treated stringently. The Rambam also writes that a Jew may not allow a non-Jew to graft his tree. It appears that the Rambam permits telling the non-Jew to sow for the non-Jew's own benefit, not for the Jew. Even though a non-Jew's acquisition does not remove the sanctity of Eretz Yisrael, and one might have thought that instructing him to sow his own field is like instructing him to sow Jewish land, the ruling teaches that this is permitted. So explains R' Yosef Korkos.
