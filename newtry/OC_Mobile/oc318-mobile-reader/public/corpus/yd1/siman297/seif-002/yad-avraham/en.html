(There seif 2) And forbidden to sustain in his field, but uproot them; and if he sustains them he is not lashed. Kesef Mishneh chapter 1 of Hilchot Kilayim law 3 wrote: so from Yerushalmi chapter 8 of Kilayim — per the rabbis against R' Akiva, sustaining kilayim also has prohibition even not via action — only not lashes. And Mishnah Lemelech challenged from sugya of Avodah Zarah (64a) that implies per rabbis permitted to sustain. And Tur Even HaEzer first chapter of Megillah also wrote so. And he strained because of this the mishnah that they would send agents to uproot kilayim — only follows R' Akiva who said one who sustains is lashed; per rabbis no prohibition at all. And I say: also our Talmud does not dispute Yerushalmi — per rabbis also there is prohibition to sustain kilayim; and if so the mishnah that they went out on kilayim is according to all; however this is kilayim the Israelite himself sowed in his field; but kilayim a gentile sowed in his field — even Yerushalmi agrees there is no issue of sustaining kilayim. Per this the sugya of Avodah Zarah is not difficult — it deals with gentile's kilayim; and this distinction brought in Birkei Yosef in the name of Ritva and in Sha'arei Melech in the name of Bnei David; and so in Merkevet HaMishnah chapter 8 of Shabbat. And so one may learn from Tosafot Bava Kamma (81a): even per rabbis against R' Akiva where he wants kilayim — the sages obligated him to uproot even if they grew by themselves, lest he be suspected he sowed them — perforce. In gentile's case suspicion does not apply. And perhaps also Tosafot in Avodah Zarah intended this distinction. And thereby the Taz's difficulties there are removed, as I wrote in my novellae. And similarly Radbaz explained at end of volume 2 siman 199 what Rambam wrote: permitted to tell a gentile to sow for him kilay zera'im — it deals in gentile's field even though seeds are Israel's and he wants them sustained — permitted, for "your field" is written.
Further: perhaps the reason Rambam and Shulchan Aruch ruled forbidden to sustain kilay zera'im and did not heed the sugya of Avodah Zarah — because Tosefta is explicit in Makkot: one who delays consecrated animals, leaves chametz on Pesach, sustains kilayim — transgresses negative commandment and is not lashed, for there is no action. This is the rule: every negative commandment with action — liable; without action — exempt — perforce. And per this necessarily the Avodah Zarah sugya speaks only of those rabbis against R' Akiva who permit sustaining and it is not everyone's view; and Rambam did not rely to rule like them, since there is also plain Tosefta that forbids. And further: it teaches regarding a fixed halacha: one who delays consecrated animals and leaves chametz — no one disputes; and particularly since Yerushalmi supports it. And with this also what Taz challenged from Sotah (43a) is answered: why not challenge the braita that excludes one who grafts kilayim — does not follow Hagahot Mordechai who said no prohibition here. And per what I wrote it is answered: although sugyot prove permitted to sustain — nevertheless we do not rule like them against plain Tosefta and Yerushalmi that forbid sustaining even not via action. And in Tosafot Rosh sensed Taz's difficulty from Sotah and answered: this prohibition is only due to marit ayin. And further wrote there: perhaps grafting kilay ilan discussed there is permitted to sustain — we derive tree grafting from animal crossbreeding that we taught regarding them permitted to raise and sustain.
And what Mishnah Lemelech challenged: why not lashed for sustaining even not via action — since possible to sustain via action, say he should be lashed also when not via action, as the Rav Hagahot wrote at end of Hilchot Sekirut — and similarly challenged Rambam in Hilchot Chametz — I wrote to settle in my work on Rambam Hilchot Chametz and Matzah and Hilchot Nezirut; and so one may settle here.
