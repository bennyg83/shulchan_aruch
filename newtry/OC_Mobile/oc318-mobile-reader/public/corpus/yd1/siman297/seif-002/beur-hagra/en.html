It is forbidden, etc. See there, seif 1: "One may not hoe," etc. The Tosefta at the end of chapter 2 of Kilayim and the Yerushalmi at the end of Orlah state: one may not work together with a non-Jew in matters of kilayim, whether kilayim of a vineyard or kilayim of seeds. In towns enclosed within Eretz Yisrael, however, such as Beit Anya, Ima, and their neighboring towns, one may work with them in kilayim. See the Yerushalmi there.
And permitted to say, etc.; Kesef Mishneh in the name of Ri Korki: "for him"; meaning for the gentile's need; the language does not imply so; and see previous seif 69; and if he said; but he holds there is no prohibition of speech to a gentile except in an Israelite's field, as written regarding castration in chapter 16 of Eben HaEzer; specifically his animal; but to plow in a gentile's field; even for Israel's need permitted; and he said "and forbidden to sustain in his field"; but in an Israelite's field even to sustain forbidden; and all the more to sow, for speech to sow is no less than sustain; and he learned from Bava Metzia 90a: "muzzle my cow"; and it implies in Israelite grain, as Tosafot s.v. "muzzle" wrote; and nevertheless specifically "my cow"; and the same here even for Israel's need.
(Likut) And permitted to say to a gentile, etc.; see siman 297 seif 4 and Orach Chayim siman 307 s.k. 1 (end).
And it is forbidden, etc.; Mishnah chapter 8 of Kilayim; and even according to the first tanna, as above; and although in Avodah Zarah page 64 it seems that according to the rabbis it is permitted; that is in a field of a non-Jew.
And if, etc.; like the first tanna and as above; and for what is written in seif 61: even per R' Akiva he is not lashed, as Yerushalmi writes; only in our Gemara Makkot it does not seem so; but nevertheless since we rule it is not "speak and act"; one is not lashed for it; this law is according to all.
