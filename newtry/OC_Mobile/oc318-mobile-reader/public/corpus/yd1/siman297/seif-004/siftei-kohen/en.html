If not that the gentile does, etc. — implies when the gentile does for his own benefit — permitted in prohibition even if it belongs to Israel; and so Hagahot Asheri chapter HaPo'alim: a matter the gentile is not warned about, such as muzzling — permitted to tell a gentile "muzzle my cow and thresh with it your grain" — but to thresh his grain — no — end quote; and so the Rav Hagahot at end of Hilchot Sekirut: there is no prohibition of speech to a gentile except in Israelite work; but in gentile work permitted — similar to Shabbat where even on Shabbat an Israelite may tell a gentile to do the gentile's own work (and see Orach Chayim siman 307); but Rashi chapter HaPo'alim 90a end and Tur Choshen Mishpat siman 338 wrote: even to thresh gentile grain — questionable and unresolved — stringently; and so Rosh there and so Tosafot and Agudah there in the name of R' Yitzchak; and Rosh wrote there: and similarly forbidden on Shabbat to tell a gentile whose sustenance is not upon you "take this meat and cook it for your need"; however they wrote there: even though permitted to tell a gentile "eat this carcass" — there if the Israelite did by hand what the gentile does by speech, i.e. puts carcass in his mouth — permitted, for Israel may put carcass in his mouth; but in muzzling forbidden to do what the gentile does by speech — end quote (and Rabbeinu Yerucham wrote Bnei Zion beginning chapter 6: forbidden to tell a gentile whose sustenance is not upon you "take this carcass meat and cook for your need" — but permitted to tell him "eat this carcass"; and so plain in Tosafot — end quote; and he did not mention Shabbat — and it is an error, for plainly even carcass there is no cooking prohibition). And ostensibly from their words the same regarding gentile's cow — forbidden since Israel would be forbidden to act thus; however Nissim Gaon wrote in chapter HaPo'alim: Israel who says to a gentile "muzzle my cow and thresh with it your grain" or "muzzle your cow and thresh with it my grain" — forbidden due to speech to a gentile, for he did prohibited work in something of Israel; and one could say this refers to that which is taught in Mekhilta: in gentile work there is no prohibition of speech to a gentile — different there since he does not act in Israel's property — end quote; and one could intend their words thus; and nevertheless it is explained from Tosafot, Agudah, Rosh, and Nissim Gaon: even if the gentile acted for his own benefit — forbidden in something of Israel — and not like Hagahot Mordechai who wrote the Rav; and so from Rambam chapter 17 of Hilchot Issurei Biah and the author in Even HaEzer siman 5: forbidden to tell a gentile to castrate our animal, etc. — implies even if he has no benefit — forbidden since it is Israel's animal.
