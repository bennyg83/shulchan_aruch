Forbidden to an Israelite. Shach; and see Likutei Peri Chadash Yoreh De'ah who challenged him [see Dagul MeRevavah and responsum Chatam Sofer siman 288 what he wrote on this].
To breed. See responsum VeShav HaKohen siman 36 who concluded that it is preferable to be strict ab initio not to give one's animal to a non-Jew if he knows he will breed it, even without explicit instruction, on account of lifnei iver — see there.
If not that the gentile does for his benefit. See Shach s.k. 4; and see responsum Chavot Yair siman 150 who wrote: an Israelite who has wood placed on the riverbank — it seems permitted to tell a gentile to take his animals to bring him wood to his house, even though he knows he has only horse and ox and will take both — for the Jew does not benefit from the body of the prohibition and the gentile is occupied in his work for his own need; and even if he says plainly "take your horse and ox and bring me wood or merchandise from such place" — and even though Shach wrote: if the thing itself belongs to Israel, even for his own need — forbidden; not comparable, for there the Israelite receives benefit in the prohibition itself — his animal was castrated or crossbred or muzzled; and his grain — not so here where his benefit is bringing his movables whether by oxen or horses or kilayim; and even though he commands take horse and ox because he knows he has no others — not called benefiting from the prohibition — end quote; and to my humble opinion his words require study.
