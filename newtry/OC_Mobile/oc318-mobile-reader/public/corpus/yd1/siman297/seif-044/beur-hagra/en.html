If not, etc. This applies specifically when the work belongs to the non-Jew, as Tosafot there, s.v. chasom, conclude. It is forbidden to say, "Here is my animal; muzzle it and thresh your grain," or, "Here is your animal; muzzle it and thresh my grain." The Rosh rules accordingly, as stated in Orach Chaim 307:21. Nimukei Yosef likewise writes that both formulations are forbidden. This is unlike the teaching in the Mechilta that no rabbinic prohibition of instructing a non-Jew applies to work belonging to the non-Jew, since there he is not acting with Jewish property. The clause does not refer to the Shulchan Aruch's statement that it is forbidden, for in that case the act is forbidden even with the non-Jew's property, since a descendant of Noach is commanded concerning it, as explained above.
