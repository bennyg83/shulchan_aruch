They are permitted. The Tosafot Yom Tov, on the Mishnah stating that a wild ox is a domesticated animal while R. Yose says it is an undomesticated animal, writes that the distinction concerns its fat and the requirement of covering its blood, but not mixed species, since any two distinct species are forbidden together. I do not understand his conclusion. The Yerushalmi, Rambam, Tur, Shulchan Aruch, and other poskim explicitly make the distinction relevant to mixed species. This is unlike two genuinely distinct species, because here they are fundamentally one species. The sources explain that the wild ox originated in settled territory, fled to the wilderness, and became wild. Rambam and the poskim therefore speak of a species having both a wild and a domesticated form. Rambam and the Bartenura mention fat and covering the blood because the Mishnah's wording, ‘the wild ox is a domesticated animal,’ implies relevance beyond mixed species. Otherwise it should simply have said that the wild ox is permitted with the ordinary ox. This is clear.
