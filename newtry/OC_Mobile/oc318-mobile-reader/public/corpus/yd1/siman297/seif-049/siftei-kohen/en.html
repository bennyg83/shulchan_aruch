If their mothers were of one species, it is permitted. This is where their fathers were also of one species, for example both fathers were horses. It is then permitted whichever principle is followed. If paternal lineage is disregarded, both offspring are regarded as donkeys through their mothers. If paternal lineage is considered, each offspring contains the same two elements. We do not say that the horse element of one uses the donkey element of the other and vice versa. Rather, we presume that like joins like, the horse element with the horse element and the donkey element with the donkey element.
Although their fathers were of one species. This requires study. It should seemingly have said: although their fathers were of two species, it is forbidden, and we do not say that each horse element joins the corresponding horse element and each donkey element joins the corresponding donkey element, since perhaps paternal lineage is disregarded while maternal lineage is certainly considered, making this an actual mixture. As written, where their fathers were of one species, it is forbidden even if paternal lineage is certainly considered, because one of the animals does not contain two corresponding elements. One must explain that the phrase means: although their fathers were of one species and outwardly the mixture appears less pronounced, it is nevertheless forbidden. This is strained.
Even with the species of its mother. Perhaps paternal lineage is considered, in which case the paternal element is being used with the maternal species, which contains no corresponding paternal element. There are nevertheless no lashes, because perhaps paternal lineage is disregarded. All the more so it is forbidden to mate the offspring with the species of its father, since maternal lineage is certainly considered, and in that case lashes are incurred.
How so, a mule, etc. A mule specifically comes from a horse and a donkey. Thus, where the mule's mother was a donkey, its father was necessarily a horse. It may therefore be mated with a female mule whose mother was likewise a donkey, because her father was also a horse. Each animal contains the same two elements. Consequently, when examination establishes that their mothers are of one species, they are permitted, because their fathers are likewise of one species, either horses or donkeys.
