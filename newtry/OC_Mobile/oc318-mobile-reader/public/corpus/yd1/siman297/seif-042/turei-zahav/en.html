Siman 297.
They would administer lashes of rebellion to him. This implies that his act is prohibited rabbinically, and this is what Tur means when he says, "there is nevertheless a prohibition." This is unlike Perishah, who writes that it is a Torah prohibition because he was troubled that, if only rabbinic, there is in any event already a prohibition against watching animals mate. That is no difficulty. Because of the concern of improper thoughts there are no lashes of rebellion; indeed, there is no formal prohibition there, but only a standard of holiness for one who wishes to sanctify himself. A proof is that a penitent who had transgressed adultery is warned in this regard not to gaze when animals or birds mate.
