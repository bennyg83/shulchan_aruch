IT MEANS THAT THE PETITIONER IS REJECTED AND THAT IT IS WRITTEN IN THE COLUMN, BUT IT IS FORBIDDEN TO SEE WHEN HE HARMS IT, AND IT IS NOT DIFFICULT FOR HIM TO DO SO BECAUSE OF THE PROHIBITION OF THE DESTRUCTION OF THE Hashem, IT IS FORBIDDEN TO SEE ONLY A HOLY FENCE THAT WANTS TO BE SANCTIFIED BY IT, AND THAT IT IS NOT NECESSARY FOR HIM TO DO SO
They would flog him with lashes of rebellion. This implies he transgresses a rabbinic prohibition — and this is what the Tur wrote: "but a prohibition there is"; and not like Perisha who wrote it is a biblical prohibition, reasoning that if it were only rabbinic, one already has a prohibition in any case against looking when animals engage with each other. But this is no difficulty at all — for the prohibition against forbidden thoughts involves no lashes of rebellion, since there is no prohibition there at all, only a fence of holiness for one who wishes to sanctify himself. And the proof is that for a penitent who transgressed adultery they warned him not to look when animals or birds mate with each other.
