If between the two species there was a pit, or fallow ground, or a stone fence (meaning: a fence of stones arranged one on another without mortar), or a path, or a fence ten handbreadths high, or a ditch ten handbreadths deep and four wide, or a tree providing shade on the ground, or a rock ten high and four wide—behold it is permitted to adjoin one side to these and the other species to the other side, since each of these separates between them—behold they appear separated one from another. In what case is this said? When he needs distancing or a separating matter when he sowed within his field. But if his field was sown with wheat, his friend is permitted to sow barley beside it, as it says "your field you shall not sow kilayim"—the prohibition is only when he sows his field as kilayim, for it does not say "the land shall not be sown kilayim." And not only that, but even if he sowed within his field barley near wheat and barley seed extended until it adjoined his friend's field sown with barley, behold it is permitted, because the barley in his field appears as the end of his friend's field.
