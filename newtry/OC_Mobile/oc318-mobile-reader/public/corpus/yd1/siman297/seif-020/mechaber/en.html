His field was sown with grain and he wishes to sow within it a row of vegetable—even a row of gourds whose leaves are long and intertwine—he distances between them six handbreadths. And if gourd leaves spread and entered the grain and intertwined with it, he uproots from the grain before the gourd until the leaves do not mix. And all the more so if he sowed a row of one species and a row of another species, it suffices that between them there be one furrow (meaning: the plowshare's groove is called gedudim and the height is called a furrow).
