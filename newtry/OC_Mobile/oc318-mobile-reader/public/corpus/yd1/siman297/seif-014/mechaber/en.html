There are among seeds species that will be one species for many forms because of variation of places and the work farmers do until they appear like two species; and even though they do not resemble one another, since they are one species they are not kilayim one with another. And there are among seeds two species that resemble one another and their form is close to being one form, and nevertheless since they are two species, behold these are forbidden one with another. How so? Garden cress with field cress, field endives with field endives, field leeks with field leeks, mountain coriander with coriander, mustard with Egyptian mustard, Egyptian gourd with pickled gourd (meaning: a bitter gourd species sweetened with hot ash)—they are not kilayim one with another. And likewise wheat with darnel, barley with wild barley, spelt with shifin, beans with sparrow, vetch with topach (meaning: vetch and topach are fine seed species—shaua in Arabic; and cucumber—Jerusalem Talmud: fruit coming from watermelon; kishut or from watermelon and topach; and some say it is the fruit called citron in vernacular; trubador is a cabbage species with small heads; beets are a vegetable species—boliti in vernacular; leeks are a beet species; nefusim are a cucumber species; laptan—Aruch: maroy in vernacular) and white beans with shaua, kishut with cucumber, cabbage with trubador, beets with leeks—they are not kilayim one with another. But cucumber with nefusim, mustard with laptan, Greek gourd with Egyptian gourd or with pickled gourd—even though they resemble one another, behold these are kilayim one with another. And likewise in a tree when there are two species resembling one another in leaves or in fruits—since they are two species, behold they are kilayim as explained in siman 295.
