He sold, etc.; but if, etc. This follows Rabbenu Chananel's explanation there, that the statement, 'when you have it,' etc., concerns a later transaction. See Hagahot Ashri there, s.v. Rabbenu Chananel, etc. What Hagahot Ashri wrote, 'provided that they acquired,' etc., means that he made a kinyan or executed a document, since otherwise it is permitted in any event because the undertaking has no legal effect. Terumat HaDeshen understood Rabbenu Chananel to refer only to a sincere verbal promise, while a kinyan would be forbidden. He therefore interpreted Hagahot Ashri's phrase, 'provided that they acquired,' etc., to mean that the buyer first acquired from the seller and afterward the buyer made the promise to him, as stated there in siman 303 and above, siman 169, seif 18, at the end of the gloss. This is incorrect. Rather, the law is as the Rashba wrote here. The phrase, 'provided that they acquired,' refers to the kinyan itself, lest one think that a mere promise effects acquisition as it does during the course of the sale.
