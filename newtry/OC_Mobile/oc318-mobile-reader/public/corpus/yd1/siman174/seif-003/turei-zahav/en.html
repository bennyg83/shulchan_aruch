חברים בטוב תתפשרו. before שנגמר המקח אומר כן וseif 4 דהfor אמר להו אתם חברים תתרצו ביניכם אבל אני איני קונה ע"מ קמ"ל דלא סמכא דעתי' ולא מכר but rather on דעת כן וכ"ש if שתק שליח ולא אמר כן דלא מכר but rather ע"מ כן שהרי התנה כן explicitly כ"כ ב"י בשם תלמידי Rashba:
And we extract from him the fruits he ate. The Tur wrote: "and the fruits he ate are avak ribbit and cannot be extracted in court" — end of quote. Beit Yosef raised a difficulty: for the Talmud explicitly concludes in this law that Ravina acted accordingly, calculated, and extracted the fruits — not like Rabba bar Rav Huna, etc. — thus it is outright interest; and Rif, Rambam, and also Rosh wrote similarly — that Ravina extracted the fruits because it was a mistaken waiver: he ate the fruits in error, believing it was a complete sale; but not because of avak ribbit. Beit Yosef answered that the Tur also intended this: from the side of interest one does not extract, but from the side of a mistaken waiver one does. Many challenged: if so, the Tur should have stated this explicitly — and this is not a valid challenge, for the Tur already wrote in siman 164 that whatever one ate due to a mistaken waiver is extracted; and here he came only to inform us that there is no prohibition of interest — and the practical difference is where a mistaken waiver does not apply, such as where the transaction was clearly known not to be a sale and yet he permitted him to eat: in that case one might have thought he is obligated to pay as outright interest — therefore it teaches us that it is only avak ribbit. And this seems correct to me. And my father-in-law of blessed memory wrote that the Tur disagrees with Rosh and rules not like Ravina but like the other amora'im in the Talmud who hold it is avak ribbit — and this is very puzzling: how could the Tur disagree with Rif, Rambam, and Rosh without even citing their view? Furthermore, in Kitzur Piskei HaRosh, chapter Ein Neshekh, it is ruled explicitly that it is avak ribbit — proving that Rosh too ruled this way; and rather Beit Yosef's interpretation is correct — and as I wrote here regarding the ruling: that in truth one extracts it based on the mistaken waiver, as most decisors hold, as Beit Yosef wrote.
