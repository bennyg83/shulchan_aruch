As friends, reach a fair compromise. He says this before the sale is completed. One might have thought that this is what he says to them: You are friends, so reach an agreement among yourselves, but I am not purchasing it subject to that condition. It therefore teaches us that his intent is not settled otherwise, and he sold it only with that understanding. All the more so, if the agent remained silent and did not say this, he sold it only with that understanding, since he expressly stipulated it. So wrote the Beit Yosef in the name of the students of the Rashba.
And the produce that he consumed is extracted from him. The Tur wrote, "And the produce that he consumed is the dust of interest, and it is not extracted through the courts." End quote. The Beit Yosef challenged this, for the Gemara explicitly concludes regarding this law that Ravina acted in practice, calculated, and extracted the produce, unlike Rabbah bar Rav Huna, etc. This demonstrates that it is fixed interest. So too wrote the Rif and the Rambam. The Rosh also wrote that Ravina extracted the produce because it was a waiver made in error, since he consumed the produce under the mistaken assumption that it was an absolute sale, but not because of interest, for it is the dust of interest. The Beit Yosef answered that the Tur likewise intended that it is not extracted on account of the law of interest, but it is extracted because it was a waiver made in error. Many challenged that, if so, the Tur should have written this. This is not a difficulty, for the Tur already wrote in siman 164 that whenever one consumed something because of a waiver made in error, it is extracted from him. Here he came only to teach that there is no prohibition of fixed interest. A practical difference arises where a waiver made in error does not apply, such as where he already knew that there was no sale, yet nevertheless allowed the other to consume the produce. One might have thought that he is obligated to pay because it is fixed interest. It therefore teaches us that it is only the dust of interest. This appears correct, as stated above. My father-in-law, of blessed memory, wrote that the Tur disagrees with the Rosh and rules not like Ravina, but like the other Amoraim in the Gemara who maintain that it is the dust of interest. This is exceedingly astonishing. How could the Tur disagree with the Rif, Rambam, and Rosh without citing at least their opinion? Furthermore, in the abridged rulings of the Rosh, in the chapter Eizehu Neshech, he rules explicitly that it is the dust of interest. We therefore infer that according to the Rosh as well, the law is as stated. Rather, the words of the Beit Yosef are primary. Accordingly, as ruled here as practical halacha, the produce is indeed extracted because of a waiver made in error, since most of the poskim maintain this, as the Beit Yosef wrote.
