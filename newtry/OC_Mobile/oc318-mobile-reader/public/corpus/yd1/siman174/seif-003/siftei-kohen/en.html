as Rosh wrote: some say mortgage without deduction — here though not fixed ribbit — nevertheless forgiveness in error, for he entered field as sale and sale is not sale — like one who leaves fellow to take object from his domain, or gave him thinking it was his and it is not his — end quote; and so Tosafot and other poskim Beit Yosef brought CM siman 207 seif 13 — meaning; but in mortgage borrower knew it was his and forgives; only Derishah and Sema CM siman 182 and Bach strained themselves per Tur's words that fruits he ate are avak ribbit and do not come out in court; and he did not explain nevertheless because of forgiveness in error we extract — implies complete forgiveness; and per this they strained to reconcile Rosh's words agreeing with Tur; their words do not seem correct; also wonder on Derishah: difficulty Rosh raised initially Ravina on Ravina should remain per Rosh's view — see there; rather seems as Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe: Tur too agrees because of forgiveness in error we extract; and what he did not explain thus — seems automatically implied since he wrote it is avak ribbit and does not come out in court — evidently does not come out in court because avak ribbit; and ribbit applies only with borrower's knowledge, e.g. seller initially said he forgives eating fruits; and so Raavad in Hasagot: Ravina extracted fruits not because of ribbit but because of robbery; but if he forgave and again claimed — they do not return to him, etc.; and likewise he wrote there on him; and what he wrote: but if he forgave, etc. — even in fixed ribbit forgiveness helps, etc.; even in fixed ribbit forgiveness helps when he already took (and as above siman 165 seif 5, etc.) — this is not challenge, for we find even when he forgave before eating fruits that he eat afterward; and as above siman 165 seif 5 — understand; and so essence for law we extract fruits he ate, for all poskim ruled like Ravina who acted and extracted fruits — unlike Bach; only some hold because of fixed ribbit and some because of forgiveness in error — see CM siman 207.
