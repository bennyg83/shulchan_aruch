And all the produce, etc. As stated there, 67a, in the incident involving that woman, etc.
And even, etc. See Tosafot, 104a, s.v. hayah, etc.; and likewise at the beginning of the chapter HaPo'alim, etc., since one who hires himself presumably does so in accordance with local custom. The same follows from the end of Bava Kamma. This is conclusive.
But if, etc. This accords with Rashi's explanation there and with the other commentators. The Rambam explained it in a different manner, as stated in Choshen Mishpat, siman 207, seif 7.
And if, when he said, etc. As stated there, 44a: it is itself, etc.
But every, etc. Ibid.
And even. For after the sale, even if the seller formalized it by a kinyan, it is permitted, as stated in seif 2.
And likewise if, etc. The Gemara there, 65b: Rav Safra taught, etc. Although it is stated there, 'and let the money be,' etc., the Nimukei Yosef wrote in the name of the Rashba that this is not literal, for that is presumed even if they did not expressly say so. See Hagahot Ashri there, s.v. vehacha, etc., who proves this from the rule that an earnest payment acquires against the entire purchase. Hagahot Maimoniyot likewise wrote this in the name of Rav Hai Gaon. This is also as stated there, 77b, regarding the reason that he wrote, etc.; see Hagahot Maimoniyot there.
