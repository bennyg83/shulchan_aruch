(There) Fixed ribbit. See Rivash (siman 305 and 364) brought in Beit Yosef here briefly: since possible house will be decided his retroactively as sale, e.g. he will not return money until limited time — and it seems one can say likewise here if he dies and heirs cannot return — as Tosafot wrote — therefore certainly cannot judge now as fixed ribbit as long as he did not return money and it was revealed sale void retroactively — then fixed ribbit; but beforehand only avak ribbit; and all he ate buyer cannot deduct — must first return all money and afterward we extract fruits he ate — see there well.
(Shach s.k. 3) And he brings them — Beit Yosef. In Beit Yosef he brought Nekudot Yosef's language in name of Rashba: not to say he must say explicitly thus — presumably law is so: all who took possession or acquired properly — lender's money on buyer is all that does not enter and exit money — end quote. And it seems plainly requires that it be known and clarified that money does not enter and exit — with this sale concludes; but before this there is concern perhaps money will enter and exit. Rama's language "and he waited for him" — possibly he includes in this language that he made condition of money or by saying let it be loan with you or money does not enter and exit; and Shach abbreviated his language. However per Ramban and Ran: money enters and exits — nevertheless he acquires against his money and buyer's hand is superior — he can say you acquired against my money — see at length in Sema (siman 199 s.k. 11): buyer permitted to eat fruits, for he can say I am certain I will hold sale for my money — entirely in his hand; but per Rashi and supporters who hold seller can void entire sale even against his money — here forbidden; and possibly Rashba and Raavad hold like Rashi — understand well.
