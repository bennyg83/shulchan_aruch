The purchaser may not consume the produce. This means from the time the doubt arose. What he writes, "even with a condition," appears to mean as follows: Even if he says, "I will later deduct it from the account," which is effective above in seif 5, it is not effective here. Since he concludes, "until it is known that the sale will stand," this implies that as long as it is not clear that the sale will stand, no condition is effective. Otherwise, he should have said, "until he says, I will deduct it from the account." One might ask how this differs from the case in seif 5, where a condition that he will deduct it from the account is effective. There is no difficulty. The author of this seif is the Nimukei Yosef in the name of the Rashba, and he also maintains above that a condition to deduct it from the account is ineffective. For he wrote there concerning the Mishnah, "If he sold him the field, etc.," that since the Gemara states that the produce is to be placed with a third party, and did not say that both of them may consume it on condition that they return it afterward, we infer that consuming it on condition to return it is forbidden. Otherwise, the Gemara should not have said that it is to be placed with a third party, for whenever the Sages say that something is to be placed with a third party, they mean specifically so, and there is no remedy except in that manner. Therefore, where he sold it but ultimately did not need the money, the purchaser may not consume the produce, etc. End quote. Had he maintained in the case of seif 5 that the remedy of deducting it from the account is effective, the difficulty would remain: Why did the Gemara say specifically that the produce is to be placed with a third party? Rather, he certainly maintains that this too is included in interest taken on condition that it be returned, and he does not accept the ruling in seif 5, which is the opinion of the Tur. It is astonishing that the Beit Yosef did not note this dispute, for he wrote that the words of the Tur accord with the words of the Nimukei Yosef in the name of the Rashba, whereas we have already demonstrated that this is not so. As to practical halacha, the law of this seif and that of seif 5 are certainly identical. If a condition to deduct it is effective there, it is likewise effective here, and conversely. The Nimukei Yosef there explicitly equated them. The only difference is that according to the Rashba, no condition is effective in either case, whereas according to the Tur, a condition to deduct it is effective in both. The Levush wrote: "However, it appears to me that if he consumes it with the intention of deducting it from the purchase price if the sale is reversed, it is permitted, for this is not comparable to what we stated in seif 4, since here there is only a doubt of interest." End quote. There is an error in his words. Why did he write that it is not comparable to seif 4, meaning what is written here in seif 5? In truth, there as well the condition is effective. It is possible that there is a scribal error, and it should read that even if he consumed it with the intention of paying him, which is forbidden in seif 4, etc. This certainly is not accepted according to all opinions. As practical halacha, since interest taken on condition that it be returned is a rabbinic matter, one may be lenient here as well. A condition that he will deduct it is effective in both cases, but a condition that he will pay him is not, as stated above.
