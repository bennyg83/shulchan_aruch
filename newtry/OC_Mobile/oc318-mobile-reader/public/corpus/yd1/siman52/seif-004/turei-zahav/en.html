Vegetation does not disqualify in liver, etc. Specifically in liver they said thus, but not in heart and gizzard — so in Torat HaBayit:
Of domestic chicken. This is Rashba's view in Tur; and he learned thus from Rav Ashi, who thought to declare treifah in extra lobe, and they said to him: all desert animals are thus — so too here. And I wonder at this ruling, for in any case it is not compelled that it is green; rather, if green is found we can hang that it is not from change but that it is like a desert bird, even though the majority of domestic ones are not thus; for otherwise why hang the permission on desert birds? Rather certainly most domestic ones are not thus, only that one found was hung on desert birds; and if so, it is difficult: in the next seif he wrote that if it fell into fire and is not before us to inspect — forbidden — this teaches you have no permission in falling into fire until the permission is clarified for you, and not in doubt; and this domestic bird — since it is doubtful whether it is green by nature like desert birds, or was red and turned yellow from fire and is as lost, for it cannot be inspected and you cannot permit it until it is clearly permitted. Granted, if we had no prohibition except from finding yellowing in it, we could well say that for this reason it did not leave its permission and we hang the yellowing on its nature like desert birds; unlike here, where it fell into fire and is forbidden until the permission is clarified, and here it cannot be clarified. And if we say Rashba does not have that of seif 5, it would be fine, and so it appears from his words in the long Torat HaBayit daf 51, that he brought a version that seems to him change disqualifies only if it is known it fell into fire, but in an unspecified case we hang the change on something else — namely, there are those who permit whom Rama brings in seif 6; and he challenged him: if so, also in falling into fire we could say thus, for majority is from the Torah and most animals are kosher, etc.; rather we hang vegetation on what is common, and that is through fire, end of his words — behold he follows majority in falling into fire if there is something to hang leniently on, namely whenever vegetation is not before us; if lost, it is obvious it is kosher from majority; but if not, it is difficult on the author of Shulchan Aruch who brought both to halacha; and it is possible to say Rashba follows his reason that he holds even when we do not know it fell into fire it is forbidden if it turned yellow, as Shulchan Aruch wrote in seif 6; if so, we can say here it deals when it is not known it fell into fire, only we want to forbid it because of the yellowing — what is in its species of desert birds helps; but if not, he should have explained that it deals without falling, and not conceal that it implies falling into fire; and furthermore we already proved in seif 1 that Rashba permits grass appearance even in certain falling into fire; if so, the same applies to this permission of desert birds, for for him there is no difference between fell and did not fall in this. And on what Rashba wrote that this green of liver is like grass — whereas like an egg does not forbid, for liver is green as an egg if fatty — from this there is no doubt at all, for all are thus; unlike that matter where permission depends on desert birds, which is against the majority of domestic ones, as we proved shortly — and certainly difficult as written; and it is reasonable as I wrote initially that Rashba does not have that of seif 5; and if so, for us this permission of desert birds fell in a pit: if in falling into fire it does not help him, for it is forbidden without inspection, and here inspection is impossible; and if it did not fall into fire, there is no prohibition in yellowing at all, as Rama wrote in seif 6 that one may rely on those who permit, as it appears to me — correct; and afterward I found Maharshal, who wrote on this permission of desert birds: and it is not clear, for if so from where do we learn to be lenient in what the Talmud did not distinguish; and furthermore, since change of appearances does not disqualify except in falling into fire — if so, since there is a defect before us, why do we need to hang on natural change to be lenient and say perhaps domestic ones also changed, end of his words — it seems he intended what I wrote:
