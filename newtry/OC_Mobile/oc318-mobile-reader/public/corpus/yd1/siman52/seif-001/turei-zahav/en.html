And they contracted. Meaning they contracted — from the expression "their intestines contracted"; and some explain the language of heat — so too Mordechai:
And they turned yellow like grass. Rashba likewise wrote that there are two types of vegetation: one, like grass — meaning the color of grass; and the second, like an egg — meaning gogel. And Rashba said it is reasonable that the vegetation that disqualifies in the liver is like grass, for the liver is green as an egg if it is fatty, end of his words; and Maharshal wrote: if it is known that it is thin, one should not hang on fat, etc.:
And the liver — the change does not forbid in it except opposite the intestines. So wrote Rashba and Tur; and in the Gemara it is asked of Rav Yosef, son of R' Yehudah bar Levi, from R' Yehudah bar Levi: the liver turned yellow opposite the intestinal children — what is the law? He said to him: treifah. And it should not be except removed. Rava said: since it turned yellow opposite the intestines, it is known that it fell into fire and its intestines contracted; and Rashba holds that the force of the fire that ruled in the liver there already prevailed even in the organ beneath it; therefore we say it is known that the intestines beneath the liver also contracted, only they are not yet visible, for it is not their nature to be revealed as quickly as the liver; and it is not specifically opposite the intestines, but the same applies in other places — there is prohibition in yellowing, namely at the place of bile; for bile that was perforated is treifah; therefore here, since the heat of the fire ruled and makes the place of the liver as if perforated there, it also makes the bile as if perforated there; and if the yellowing is at the place of its life-source, where there is no prohibition from another organ that would disqualify through perforation, but the prohibition is from the liver that is as if that place of life-source was removed — therefore there is no treifah in this unless the yellowing is over the entire place of its life-source; but in part of it — namely, that something remained whole like an olive — there is no prohibition, for it is no worse than other perforation or removal, etc.; in all the liver except these places there is no prohibition in liver that turned yellow. But Mordechai wrote: liver that turned yellow opposite intestines — treifah, and the measure of its greenness is in any amount; and even though the liver was perforated — kosher — nevertheless, since the liver turned yellow we are concerned also for heart and intestines even though it is not yet evident in them; and furthermore one should be concerned that perhaps ultimately all of it will be removed; and from here there is support for four Geonim who declare treifah in perforation of the liver's courtyard, for we are concerned that ultimately the entire liver will be removed, since we declare treifah in liver yellowing at the measure of perforation of any amount that they forbade its beginning because of its end, end of his words. And Beit Yosef wondered: if so, why did they say specifically opposite the intestines? And he answered that perhaps we hold that ultimately all of it will not be removed, only specifically in yellowing opposite the intestines; and this matter is a great strain — as if Mordechai was told this thing, that the reason is because ultimately all of it will be removed; and from this one must enter a strain that they know specifically opposite the intestines; and he also brings support for the Geonim, and there is no hint or allusion in the Talmud, for they forbade only because of intestinal children alone. And it appears to me that Mordechai holds there are two reasons for treifah here: one, that since we see a defect in the yellowing, certainly other organs near it were also scalded, even though not yet visible — nevertheless it is possible that their nature is not revealed as quickly as this scalded place; and the second reason, that even in that organ itself that was scalded in part, and we see that the remaining part was not scalded — since in this organ itself whose nature is to change and be revealed, and nevertheless it was revealed only in part — this teaches that in another place in that organ it was not yet scalded; nevertheless we are concerned that perhaps afterward it will be scalded, for this blow will cause it; and he learned this from Rava's words, who said it is known that it fell into fire — for why did he need to say this; he should have said briefly it is known the intestines contracted, for the questioners also asked about liver that fell into fire, that it is known; otherwise yellowing does not disqualify at all, as Mordechai himself wrote there; rather Rava means there are two prohibitions: one, it is known it fell into fire and forbidden because this will harm it even in a place where the blow has not yet ruled; and another reason, that the intestines already contracted since they are near it, only it is not their nature to be revealed as quickly as the liver. It turns out that for the first reason there is prohibition also from the liver, for we forbid it because of its end; and Mordechai's words are explained from what is stated in the Gemara according to this; and according to this there is prohibition in liver yellowing in every place from the first reason in Rava's words; and for practical law we have only the ruling of Shulchan Aruch, that we do not say at all that ultimately all of it will be removed, but we say only Mordechai's last reason alone, and that is Rashba's and Tur's view above, for the Gemara's language proves as their words, from the challenge "and it should not be except removed" — this teaches that from the liver there is no prohibition; and so Ran; and therefore there is prohibition only at the place of bile and opposite intestines even in any amount, and opposite the place of its life-source specifically in all of it, as Beit Yosef emended Tur's words "life-source," etc.; and from this is proof for what is written in siman 41 seif 2, that liver that melted not at the place of life-source and place of bile — kosher, and we do not say cast the spoon in all of it and ultimately all of it will be scalded; for even in falling into fire here, which is explicit treifah in the Talmud, we conclude the halacha here that there is no prohibition because ultimately all of it will be scalded — a fortiori there, which is doubtful treifah; and even according to Mordechai who forbids here in liver yellowing in every place because ultimately all of it will be scalded — one does not compare treifot to each other, for there is a great difference between a blow that comes through fire and other illnesses, for we did not find in the Gemara concern in them, as it appears to me:
And facing inward. Meaning: but the outer side that is opposite the ribs — even if it turned yellow there — there is no concern for intestines, since there is a separation between the liver's yellowing and them; and according to Mordechai above, treifah even on the outer side from the reason that ultimately all the liver will be scalded — only we do not hold like him:
We are not concerned for it. In Tur it is written in the name of Rashba: but if we see that it reddened, etc.; and Beit Yosef wrote that it should read that it turned yellow; and likewise in what he wrote afterward "red" should read green, and he did not write a reason for this; and it is simple that his reason is because Rashba and Ran wrote "perhaps it turned yellow" — kosher, etc., as Beit Yosef copied; and therefore Beit Yosef also wrote that Rosh holds like those who disagree, whom we mentioned. And there is great room to wonder here on Tur and Beit Yosef, for it is stated in Torat HaBayit HaAruch daf 51: I found among some commentators that since they did not count lung together with heart, gizzard, and liver, it implies specifically these; but lung — even if it turned yellow — kosher, it is mere smoke that entered it; and what we say "there is no lung for a bird to contract" — even if it turned yellow he speaks; and to say that vegetation does not disqualify in it at all is not clear in my eyes; on the contrary, if in heart they said thus, etc.; and what is stated "there is no lung for a bird to contract" — they stated the reason: since ribs protect it; and if we saw that it was crushed — how did they protect it? Rather they spoke only from the default that inspection is not needed, etc.; and they spoke in these only because they are common; but a bird's lung is not common to contract and inspection is not needed; but heart, liver, and gizzard need inspection from the default; and if the lung is found crushed or contracted — treifah. I still have to say that it is impossible to count lung with them, because vegetation includes two colors: green like grass, which is grass, and green like an egg, which is gogel; and if it turned yellow like an egg — even if it did not fall into fire — treifah; and if it turned yellow like grass — kosher, from R' Nasan; and it is common in lung; and whatever can be hung on a derivative we hang and are lenient, for most animals are kosher; and this supports the explanation I wrote above to support the Geonim's words; and this supports what I wrote there: and lung of a bird — when they said it is not to contract, this means inspection is not needed lest green like an egg be found; behold animal lung needs inspection, end of his words. Behold before us that for Rashba, in green grass there is no concern even if it fell into fire; and certainly one should not say this deals only when we did not see that it fell, but certainly we are concerned even in green grass, for he wrote above on that page: when heart, gizzard, and liver turned yellow — evidence for the Geonim who hold that even when it is not known that they fell into fire we are concerned in whatever we see that turned yellow; for if not, you would explain to hang the yellowing on another change even in falling — behold majority is from the Torah and most animals are kosher, and we hang vegetation on something else; rather it must be that all vegetation of heart, gizzard, liver, and redness of intestines is a distant matter not through fire, and therefore we do not hang except on what is common; and since so, even when we did not see that it fell into fire it is so, etc.; if so, for him there is no distinction between fell or did not fall in any vegetation; you could challenge: in grass vegetation there is a distinction; in gogel vegetation too one could distinguish; if so, we have no proof for the Geonim that they do not distinguish; and furthermore Rashba wrote at the end of his words that I copied: and lung of a bird — when they said it is not to contract, this means inspection is not needed lest green like an egg be found — it implies grass does not harm at all; and this deals when it certainly fell — for that which "there is no lung for a bird to contract" deals when it certainly fell, for he said there is no lung for a bird — not to fall and not to contract, meaning it fell from a height of ten handbreadths or fell into fire — we are not concerned for its lung, for ribs protect it from the blow of the fall. And further proof: Rashba's main dispute is from the force of what they said: since ribs protect it, and whatever we saw contracted — how did they protect it? And if it deals with grass vegetation, how is it possible to say we saw it contracted — behold grass vegetation is in any case kosher from R' Nasan without our seeing that it fell; if so, what proof do you have in falling to say you saw it contracted; on the contrary, hang it from R' Nasan. Rather it is simple that grass vegetation does not disqualify lung at all for Rashba, even in falling, certainly; and gogel vegetation disqualifies even when we did not see that it fell; if so, why does Rashba disagree with those who disagree? It must be said the disagreeing ones hold that even appearance of gogel is kosher even in certain falling; and even though it is stated in Piskei Halachot: green (like liver) treifah — that is in an animal, where they say it is a scald; but not in a bird, since ribs protect it no scald rules over it; and so the language of the disagreeing ones implies: "there is no lung for a bird," etc. — vegetation does not disqualify in it at all — implying all vegetation does not disqualify in it; and in this Rashba disagrees with them, that gogel vegetation disqualifies also in bird lung, even if we did not see that it fell; and since so, it is difficult what Beit Yosef emended in Tur "turned yellow," that Rashba disagrees with Rosh in its greenness, and Rosh is the disagreeing ones of Rashba — this is not so, for the disagreeing ones permit even in gogel, as we proved; and for Rosh it is obvious it is forbidden in appearance of gogel even when we did not see that it fell, for Tur did not distinguish above in disqualifying appearances in lung between animal and bird; and therefore what Rosh permits here is in appearance of grass, that we do not hang on contraction; if so, there is no dispute here, for Rashba also concedes grass is kosher, as we proved, etc.; and certainly this emendation is not correct in Tur; rather Tur's words are also difficult in what he wrote dispute in lung reddening, that appearance of redness is kosher like appearance of grass, even in complete redness; if so, for Rashba too it is kosher, for he did not forbid except appearance of gogel; and if I were not afraid I would say Tur wrote his words thus because he did not see Torat HaBayit HaAruch, where it is further written "I still have to say," etc., and he thought Rashba forbids also in appearance of grass and called it contracted, certainly; but in truth it is not so; and I can reconcile Tur's words another way: namely, when we see a clear sign that a change in lung was done now, such as there is contraction in another place and there the lung reddened from it — in this for Rashba treifah; and therefore Rashba properly said: whatever we saw contracted, we do not hang the redness on something else; and for Rosh also in this kosher, since ribs protect; and therefore Tur wrote here the language of reddening, implying through another matter that caused this redness, as I explained to reconcile his words; and still my mind is not at ease with these words as fitting, and this is one of the places in Tur that commentators of Tur's words did not notice at all. Tur wrote: behold even if all of it was removed — kosher; meaning, and an olive-bulk remained at the place of bile and place of life-source:
That ultimately it will perforate. This is Rashba's view; and even though we already wrote that for Rashba we do not judge because of its end — for otherwise he should have declared treifah in every place in the liver — nevertheless he properly said here that ultimately it will perforate, because it was already scalded — all its supports around the blow were scalded and it is treifah from the reason that whatever was scalded ultimately perforates; but what was not scalded we do not say will eventually be scalded:
