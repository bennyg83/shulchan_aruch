And they turned yellow, etc. — But if they reddened — even if they increased in redness — kosher; and likewise intestines whose way is to be green — if they increased in greenness — kosher. Ran:
Like grass. — But green as an egg (which is called gogel) is kosher in the liver, for the liver is green as an egg if it is fatty — Tur in the name of Rashba; and it appears that therefore even in a thin bird it is kosher, and so it appears from the Mechaber's words. But Maharshal in Piskei Halachot siman 96 wrote that in a thin bird it is forbidden; and it also appears from the Mechaber's words that the same applies to heart and gizzard — we do not declare treifah except for green like grass, as Ran brought in Beit Yosef in the name of Ra'ah. But Rashba in Torat HaBayit daf 51b wrote that in heart and gizzard even like the appearance of an egg is treifah, and so wrote Bach and so Maharshal there:
Opposite the intestines. — Even in any amount — certainly they were scalded, also the intestinal children; and even if they are before us and we see no defect in them — treifah, end of Tur:
And facing inward. — And its one head above — which is not opposite the intestines — and that is the thick head in it, the place where it hangs from it; and likewise the outer side that faces the ribs is not opposite the intestines; and even if it is found green on that side at its thin head — kosher, end of Rashba:
Opposite the bile. — For we think the force of the fire that ruled and turned yellow as if the bile was perforated. Rashba:
Or opposite any place of its life-source. — That is, that it turned yellow near the place of its life-source so that if the place of vegetation is removed, an olive-bulk will not remain at the place of its life-source; and so too Ran and Beit Yosef, and see above siman 41 s.k. 3:
We are not concerned for it, etc. — It implies even if it fell into fire and changed in appearance we hang the change on something else — Rosh and Rambam and other poskim. However, there are poskim who are stringent where it changed in appearance from falling into fire, and Bach is stringent even in great loss, and it is not clear; and perhaps there is a scribal error in his words, and so his language somewhat implies — see there:
Because its ribs protect it. — For the ribs of a bird do not lie on their breadth but on their edge, and their breadth protrudes inward, and the lung is hidden among them. Rashi:
And a spleen that turned yellow in its support. — An animal — treifah; such is Rashba's language, and Beit Yosef brings it; and it implies but in a bird kosher, as Rashba said and the Mechaber above siman 43, that perforation does not disqualify in a bird's spleen; and therefore the Mechaber omitted this law, because he wrote in seif 7 that treifot of falling into fire do not apply to an animal:
