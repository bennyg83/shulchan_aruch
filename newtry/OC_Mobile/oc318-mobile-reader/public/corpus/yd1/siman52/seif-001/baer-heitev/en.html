Like grass. Meaning the color of grass; but green as an egg, called gogel, is kosher in the liver, for the liver is green as an egg if it is fatty — Tur in the name of Rashba. And Shach wrote: and it appears that therefore even in a thin bird it is kosher; but Maharshal forbids in a thin bird. And it also appears from the Mechaber's words that the same applies to heart and gizzard — we do not declare treifah except in green like grass; but Rashba wrote that in heart and gizzard even like the appearance of an egg is treifah, and so Bach and Maharshal; and if intestines whose way is to be red increased in redness, and likewise those whose way is to be green increased in greenness — kosher. Ran:
The intestines. Even in any amount — certainly they were scalded, also the intestinal children; and even if they are before us and we see no defect in them — treifah, end of Tur:
Facing inward. Rashba wrote: and its one head above, which is not opposite the intestines, and that is the thick head in it, the place where it hangs from it; and likewise the outer side that faces the ribs is not opposite the intestines; and even if it is found green on that side at its thin head — kosher, end of his words:
Its life-source. Meaning that it turned yellow near the place of its life-source so that if the place of vegetation is removed, an olive-bulk will not remain at the place of its life-source; and see above siman 41:
Protect it. (That the ribs of a bird lie on their edge and the lung is hidden in them.) Shach wrote: it implies even if it fell into fire and changed in appearance we hang the change on something else — Rosh and Rambam and other poskim. However, there are poskim who are stringent where it changed in appearance from falling into fire, and Bach is stringent even in great loss, and it is not clear, end of his words. (And for practical law one should be concerned like Ran and Rashi to declare treifah when it turned yellow like grass from fire, for it appears their words are that we see ribs did not protect them; however in great loss one may rely on Rambam and Rosh who permit. Pri Chadash.)
In its support. Shach wrote: Rashba's language is "an animal — treifah," and it implies but in a bird kosher, as the Mechaber wrote above siman 43 that perforation does not disqualify in a bird's spleen; and therefore the Mechaber omitted this law, because he wrote in seif 7 that treifot of falling into fire do not apply to an animal. (And Pri Chadash wrote both in animal and bird — see there.)
