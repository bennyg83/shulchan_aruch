And if it is not before us, etc. These are Ran's words; and Beit Yosef wrote his proof from what we say in the Gemara: there is no lung for a bird to contract; and that one certainly speaks in an unspecified case; for where it certainly contracted — behold it is before us that ribs did not protect it; but if it fell into fire — inspection is not needed, and specifically lung whose ribs protect it; but other intestinal children of a bird, for nothing protects them — need inspection, end of his words. And accordingly, according to what we hold, even if we saw that the lung turned yellow — kosher, as Tur wrote in the name of Rosh, and so is Beit Yosef's conclusion as I copied above in seif 1; if so, that which they said there is no lung for a bird is even if we saw that it turned yellow, and Ran's proof is not proof; or we do not hold like him in forbidding without inspection, and Shulchan Aruch should not have brought Ran's view here, since in Beit Yosef and Shulchan Aruch seif 1 he ruled like Rosh as I mentioned; only it is possible that even though Ran's proof is not proof, nevertheless it is reasonable to rule thus, since there is a defect here that it fell into fire — we say there is certainly change until it is clarified there is no change, as I will explain shortly — proof is not needed, and therefore Ran's words are halacha; and Maharshal disagreed with these words of Ran and brings proof from what we taught in the mishnah: fell into fire and intestinal children contracted — and it did not teach fell into fire alone, as it taught "trampled in a wall," etc.; rather certainly that there is no prohibition in falling alone but specifically when intestinal children certainly contracted; but in falling into fire alone there is not yet a defect before us. And it seems to me his words are wondrous in this, for there is one explanation of "and they contracted," namely the language of heat, as written in seif 1 in the name of Mordechai; if so, from where do we know the intestines were heated when there is no change of appearance? Therefore it must be said the matter depends on measure — namely, that it remained in fire long enough for the measure of heating; if so, his proof is not proof at all, for the mishnah means: fell into fire and intestinal children contracted — meaning it remained there for that measure; but certainly we do not see any sign of contraction, only presumably it fell into fire and remained there; and this certainly has no reason that as soon as it falls into fire it should be forbidden, even if they immediately removed it; but if it remained for the measure that it would heat — certainly there is a defect before us and inspection is needed; and in this matter it suffices to uphold Ran's words without another proof:
