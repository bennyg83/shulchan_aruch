Were rendered valid. The Taz writes that this does not mean that they are presently fit for immersion, since each lacks forty se'ah after they were separated. Rather, the drawn water was purified through this contact and acquired the status of rainwater, because it was joined to forty se'ah. If rainwater is later added to the drawn water until it reaches forty se'ah, it is valid for immersion. The Shach writes that even if the middle mikveh was connected to the drawn one before being connected to the valid one, it was not invalidated, since both were also connected to the valid mikveh before the drawn water descended into them. This is the Rosh's ruling.
Pure. The Shach writes that this discusses a case where the water that overflowed returned to its place. Otherwise it is flowing water and the immersions were ineffective. It also discusses a case where none of the water was lost when it flowed over the edge, such as where the mikveh walls are high. Otherwise the immersions were certainly ineffective and the mikvaos were not validated, because they are deficient. It appears that return of the water is required only when the mikveh contains exactly forty se'ah. If it contains more, return is unnecessary, because forty se'ah remain even while some flows outward. It is not called flowing water when it leaves because of a person immersing. Even the authority who invalidates in seif 50 where water exits through a crack despite forty se'ah remaining agrees here, for there it leaves by itself outside the time of immersion, whereas here it leaves because of the immersion.
