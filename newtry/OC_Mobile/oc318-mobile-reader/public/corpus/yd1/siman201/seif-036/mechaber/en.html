A pipe that does not have 4 rims is not considered a vessel, and it is fit to bring by it water to the mikveh. If one carved into it one small hollow before setting it in place: if it is of wood, even if the hollow holds only the slightest amount, all of it becomes, through it, a vessel, and all the water that passes over it is considered drawn. If it is of earthenware, the carving disqualifies it only if it holds a revi’is. If pebbles or dirt fell into the hollow, it is not considered a sealing to nullify it from the law of receiving unless they are pressed tightly into it. A gutter that is narrow on both ends and wide in the middle is not considered “receiving” to disqualify the mikveh with it. {Rama: Therefore, it is permitted to make mikva’os through wooden pipes and wooden gutters that bring water from the river or another spring to the mikveh (Mordechai in Kiddushin in the name of R’ Shemaryah; and Ran; and a responsum of Rosh; and so is the opinion of Tur and Rambam), or roof pipes (Mahari”k, shoresh 54). We are not concerned that perhaps a hollow was formed in them due to dampness and they receive [water], for they are not called “receiving” even if there was a hollow in them, since a person did not make it intentionally (there, the reason of Rashi). And if the waters come to the pipe by means of vessels fixed in a wheel, and they are perforated in a way that is not called a vessel, it is permitted to immerse in them if there are 40 se’ah in the mikveh (Mordechai, Hilchos Niddah, in the name of Rokeach). But if there are not 40 se’ah in it, one does not immerse there, for this is not called a connection to the river through this (and see Orach Chayyim siman 159) (Beit Yosef at the end of this siman; and not like Rokeach).}
