Agudah, beginning of Mikvaot: from here it would appear to me that when they empty a mikveh and it was invalidated by three log of drawn water, if they remove from it a bit more than was in it when the three log fell in, it is valid; but I did not see my teachers practice so; and perhaps one can distinguish between when the water exited by itself and when they drew and removed from it; and I discussed before my teachers to validate it if they did not remove all the water and dried the mikveh with cloths or sand, or pierced the vessels drawing in it to remove it from the category of vessel ; see in Semag and Semak; and there are those who ruled that a spring is not invalidated by three log of drawn water, and they extended in proofs, and I did not see many practice so ; end of his words; and see above, seif 40, in the hagahah.
The mikveh was,. etc., "less than three log and they completed it," etc. ; here, even if the mikveh is very large, it is valid; for even though less than three log remained in it, nevertheless it was not invalidated, only not validated; and therefore we say the less than three log are as if they are not, and the rainwater afterward validates it; and so is Beit Yosef's intent also; and Bach in seif 27 wrote on this and did not examine, etc., and his words are astonishing.
They did not invalidate it,. etc. ; and specifically with valid water; but with impure water, whatever lacks from the mikveh more than a kortov ; which is the weight of a dinar ; Rambam wrote they invalidate it unless valid rainwater afterward increased upon it; however Ra'avad and Rosh argue that in every case less than three log does not invalidate the mikveh, as explained in Beit Yosef ; see there; and so appears the view of Tur and the Mechaber.
