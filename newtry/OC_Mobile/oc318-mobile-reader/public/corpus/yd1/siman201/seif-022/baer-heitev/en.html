The mikveh. Shach writes that this refers to a mikveh that holds only forty se'ah, so we assume that the incoming water pushes out the water already present. If it holds more, it is possible that the invalid water remains together with the new water. Agudah writes that from here it appeared to him that when cleaning a mikveh that became invalid through three log of drawn water, if one removes slightly more water than the amount present when the three log fell in, the mikveh should be valid. However, he had not seen his teachers practice this. Perhaps there is a distinction between water that leaves on its own and water that is drawn out manually. He therefore ruled before his teachers to validate only after all the water was removed and the mikveh dried with cloths or sand, or after the drawing vessels were perforated so that they lost the status of vessels. Some authorities ruled that a spring is not invalidated by three log of drawn water and gave lengthy proofs, but this was not the common practice. See also seif 40 in the gloss.
