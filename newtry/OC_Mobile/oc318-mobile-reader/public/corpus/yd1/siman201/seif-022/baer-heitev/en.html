The mikveh. The Shach wrote: it speaks of a mikveh that holds only forty se'ah, and therefore we say that the water entering it pushes out the water inside it. But if it held more than this, it is possible that the invalidated water remained there together with the water entering it. The Agudah wrote: from here it would appear to me that when they clean a mikveh and it was invalidated by three log of drawn water, if they remove from it a little more than what was in it at the time the three log fell into it, it is valid. But I did not see my teachers practice so. Perhaps one should distinguish between where the water left on its own and where they drew and removed it. I discussed before my teachers to validate it only if they removed all the water and dried the mikveh with sheets or with sand, or perforated the vessels with which they drew so as to nullify them from the status of vessels. Some ruled that a spring is not invalidated by three log of drawn water, and they discussed proofs at length, but I did not see the public practice so; end quote. See above seif 40 in the gloss.
Invalidated it. It is implied from the Mechaber's words that there is no distinction between fit water and tamei water: in every case, less than three log does not invalidate the mikveh, like the view of the Ra'avad and the Rash, unlike the Rambam, who distinguishes. Shach.
