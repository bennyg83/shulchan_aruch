Spring water, etc. Torat Kohanim, cited by Rashi and Tosafot in Shabbat there, in Bechorot, and in chapter 10 of Niddah. So too it appears from the Gemara in those places, from Mikvaot 1:7, "above them," concerning purification when collected, from chapter 5, mishnah 4, where Rabbi Yishmael says, "all," and from mishnah 5 there concerning flowing waters.
Even while flowing. Tosafot in Shabbat there, s.v. "perhaps," and all the more so, and Tosafot in Bechorot there, s.v. "perhaps."
But through, etc. From the aforementioned Torat Kohanim.
If the flowing waters, etc.; and if they increased, etc. Mikvaot 5:5, and the passages in Shabbat, Bechorot, and Niddah cited there. Rabbeinu Chananel, the Rambam, and the other authorities rule like Rav and like the father of Shmuel. Even according to Shmuel, since his own statements appear contradictory, the practical ruling is what he came to teach, as explained in Shabbat 54a. See Ran on Nedarim 40b, Rosh on Niddah there, siman 10, and Beer HaGolah.
Therefore, etc. This is how I explain the statement there that mats were placed in Tishrei. Even in Tishrei there is concern that the waters may increase, while in Nisan he used a different corrective measure. See Kesef Mishneh there. To me it appears that in Nisan, when the waters increased greatly, he could not restrain them with a mat. But Niddah there implies that the mat was a safeguard because of the harbor, as stated there, and the Rambam himself writes so in chapter 1, halachah 11.
But some permit, etc. See Tosafot in Shabbat there, s.v. "for he said," in Bechorot there, s.v. "there is not," and the Rosh and Ran in those passages.
Nevertheless, etc. The Ran in Nedarim there explains that with respect to them one does not say that the river is blessed from its own source.
But in a river, etc. According to the view cited in Kesef Mishneh. Meaning, even in a place where the river did not originally flow and it increased there because of rain. See Ran in Nedarim there, who writes that according to the Raavad, even the father of Shmuel and Rav spoke only of a place where it did not originally flow. He brings proof from the Tosefta cited by the Rash in Mikvaot 5:3 and 1:7, which states that it does not purify while flowing beyond, and from the mishnayot there that say it is like a mikveh in one respect and like a spring in another. The opening and closing clauses would otherwise conflict, so they must refer to different places. The Rambam disagrees; see the Ran there. According to Shmuel, however, who holds that a river is blessed from its own source, it is valid in all cases, as the Ran writes there, though see the testimony of Rabbi Elazar bar Tzadok, and so forth.
