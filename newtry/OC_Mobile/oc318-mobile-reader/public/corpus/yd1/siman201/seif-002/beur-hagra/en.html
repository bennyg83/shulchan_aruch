Spring water, etc. Toras Kohanim, cited by Rashi and Tosafos in Shabbos, Bechoros, and chapter 10 of Niddah. It is likewise evident from the Gemara in those places; from Mikvaos 1:7, "Above them is," etc., "to purify when collected"; from Mikvaos 5:4, where Rabbi Yose says, "All," etc.; and from Mishnah 5 there concerning flowing waters.
Even while flowing. Tosafos, Shabbos 65b, s.v. Shema, and Bechoros there, s.v. Shema.
But through, etc. From the Toras Kohanim cited above.
If the flowing water, etc.; and if it increased, etc. Mishnah 5 of chapter 5, and the passages in Shabbos, Bechoros, and Niddah. Rabbeinu Chananel, Rambam, and the other authorities rule like Rav and the father of Shmuel. Even Shmuel, although his statements appear contradictory, intended to teach practical halachah, as explained in Shabbos 54a. See the Ran, Nedarim 40b, the Rosh in Niddah, siman 10, and Be'er HaGolah.
Therefore, etc. This is how they explain the statement that mats were set up during Tishrei, because even then there was concern that rainwater might become the majority. In Nisan he used another remedy. See Kesef Mishneh. In my view, during Nisan, when the waters increased greatly, the mat could not restrain them. Niddah, however, implies that the mat was used because of the harbor, and the Rambam himself writes so in chapter 1, halachah 11.
Some permit, etc. See Tosafos, Shabbos there, s.v. De'amar; Bechoros there, s.v. Ein; the Rosh and Ran in those places.
Nevertheless, etc. The Ran in Nedarim explains that one cannot apply to them the principle that a river is increased from its source.
But in a river, etc. Kesef Mishneh, "according to the view," etc. This means even at a place where the river did not originally flow and expanded there because of rain. See the Ran in Nedarim, who writes that according to the Raavad, even Rav and the father of Shmuel disqualify it only at a place where it did not originally flow. He proves this from the Tosefta cited by the Rash in Mikvaos 5:3 and 1:7, which says that it purifies while flowing only up to a certain point, and from the Mishnayos there that say it is equal to a mikveh in one respect and to a spring in another. The beginning would otherwise contradict the end, so the law must vary by location. The Rambam disagrees. According to Shmuel, however, who holds that a river is increased from its source, it is valid in all circumstances, as the Ran writes, though he discusses Rabbi Elazar bar Tzadok's testimony. See siman 81.
