There is no distinction whether he squeezes his garment. At first glance this might imply that disqualification applies only if he intentionally squeezes it. If the water is pressed out unintentionally, perhaps it would not disqualify, since clothing is not a vessel and is full of openings. In truth this is incorrect. The ruling begins with the phrase "if they fell into it," implying an accidental occurrence, as the Gemara infers in other contexts from expressions such as "a knife fell and slaughtered." The later phrase about squeezing one's garment refers back to that framework. Moreover, the ruling first says there is no distinction whether he drew the water with a vessel, and there it is obvious that three log of fully drawn water disqualify even without intent. The same must therefore be true of water coming from his garment, since the two are stated together. This law is learned from the Tosefta cited by R. Shimshon in chapter 2 of Mikvaot: if one immersed a thick garment in the mikveh and three log flowed from it into the mikveh, the mikveh remains valid; if he lifted it out and the water then flowed from it, the water disqualifies. R. Shimon permits until he intends to detach the water. The Tosefta expressly says "three log flowed from it," which implies even automatically and without human intent. The same applies to one squeezing his garment. Likewise, the later ruling concerning one who throws water with cupped hands has the same meaning. This is stated explicitly below in seif 39: any water entering through human action, even if splashed with hands or feet, and even if a person merely passes through water and it splashes by itself from his feet into the mikveh, disqualifies it. This is Rashba's ruling in Torat HaBayit, cited by Beit Yosef below. The same is clear from Responsa Ramban, siman 231, cited here by Beit Yosef, quoting Raavad: any water brought into a mikveh by human action without hamshachah, even through a perforated vessel, disqualifies. He concludes that whenever water falls from a vessel while it is held by the person drawing it, it disqualifies the mikveh. A perforated vessel avoids disqualification only when water falls from it automatically without human handling. Thus the distinction is only between water falling by itself and water entering through human action. Within the latter category there is no distinction between intentional and unintentional entry. That responsum itself addresses someone cleaning a mikveh who is concerned that water will return to it and proposes making a hole in the bucket. The responsum rejects this: perforating the bucket does not help because the return still occurs through human action. When it says "whenever it falls from the hand of the person drawing it," it refers precisely to someone drawing water out in order to clean the mikveh. He gains nothing by perforating the bucket, even though he plainly does not want the escaping water to return to the mikveh. Nevertheless, it disqualifies because it enters through human action. This explicitly proves that the law does not depend upon the person's desire. My father-in-law cites this responsum but explains that disqualification applies only when the person wants the water to return, whereas if he does not want it, it is as though the water fell by itself. He did not examine the responsum itself closely enough, only Beit Yosef's excerpt. There is, however, something to examine in Beit Yosef's citation from Raavad's Ba'alei HaNefesh. One view says that whenever a person takes water and casts it into a mikveh, even with cupped hands, it disqualifies. That authority explains R. Yose's statement in the Tosefta that if he splashes three log with hands or feet into the mikveh it is invalid, while a troop passing from place to place and splashing water with their hands and feet does not invalidate. He distinguishes intentional splashing from accidental splashing. It is clear, however, that we do not rule like this distinction. Rashba and Ramban cite the view that any water entering through human action is disqualifying and yet do not require intent. Even completely unintended human-caused water disqualifies. This is the settled halachah below in seif 40 and should not be departed from. See the discussion immediately following.
