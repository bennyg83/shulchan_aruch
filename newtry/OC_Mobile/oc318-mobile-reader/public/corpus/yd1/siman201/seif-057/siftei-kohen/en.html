Provided he does not uproot, etc. for if the wave was uprooted it would not purify, for a mikveh does not purify in flowing waters except in ashboran; and if uprooted it is not ashboran. However, specifically a mikveh that does not flow but rainwater gathered in a pit; but a mikveh that flows live water ; we hold it purifies in flowing waters and in any quantity; even if the wave was uprooted it would purify vessels in any quantity, for vessels do not require forty se\'ah, and it is not similar to a wave uprooted from the sea, for we require forty se\'ah even for vessels (above, seif 5), because the sea is not considered like a spring at all to purify in flowing waters, but not to purify in any quantity as Perishah explained; but an actual spring that purifies vessels in any quantity, even if the wave was uprooted from the spring and fell on the vessel, purifies in any quantity ; it appears simple, end of Bach, this siman. And it is difficult on his words, for this law is taken from Rosh, end of Niddah, who wrote there: provided he does not uproot the wave, for then it would not purify if there are not forty se\'ah in it ; end of his words; and Beit Yosef brings it; and so Rabbenu Yerucham, nekudot 56, end of part 5. And therefore it must be that he deals with a spring, for if with a mikveh, even if there are forty se\'ah, what is it, since it is flowing when uprooted. And further, if with a mikveh, there is a difficulty from what he brought from Rosh from the mishnah of a wave uprooted with forty se\'ah that fell on a person and vessels ; they are pure; and he wrote: this anonymous statement is like Rabbi Yosi (who holds there is law of spring upon it and purifies in flowing waters), for a wave uprooted implies even from the sea; apparently it purifies in flowing waters ; end of his words. And if so, even for a wave uprooted from a mikveh he holds it purifies in flowing waters; rather certainly Rosh says here in a spring too one should not uproot, for then it will not purify if there are not forty se\'ah; and the reason is that it does not suffice in a spring in any quantity except in the body of the spring, but whatever is uprooted, although it purifies in flowing waters, nevertheless does not purify in any quantity when uprooted. And it is strained to say what Rosh wrote "if there are not forty se\'ah" refers to a person, for he did not mention a person there at all, rather a pipe placed in the cave opening; and although Rabbenu Yerucham wrote there: and so the law for immersion of a person ; certainly the law is for immersion of a person that one may immerse in a wave that has forty se\'ah; but to say Rosh deals specifically with a person, this is not implied; rather it appears that even for vessels, an uprooted wave from a spring requires forty se\'ah ; so it appears to me. And in Tosafot Yom Tov, end of chapter 7 of Mikvaot, he challenged Rosh: even if there are forty se\'ah, nevertheless it becomes through something that receives tumah; and so in Perishah, one can say so; however Rosh\'s view appears that it is not called becoming through something that receives tumah except when the water that becomes a mikveh passes through something that receives tumah; but here, although the person brings and conducts in the mikveh and through this the wave is uprooted, nevertheless the wave was uprooted from the mikveh itself, and at the place of its uprooting there are no human hands ; examine.
