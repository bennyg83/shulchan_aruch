Since the ruling here does not say "until the original volume has gone out," as seif 22 does, it implies that the water overflowing and leaving the pit is not all attributed to the original water as in seif 22. Rather, we calculate proportionally between the water originally in the pit and the new water entering it. Alternatively, perhaps we treat it as half and half, because the newly entering water is closer to the outlet and therefore more likely to leave than the original water. In seif 22, by contrast, we require the amount equal to the original volume to leave, plus a little more, meaning the full quantity of water that was in the pit before the three log of drawn water fell in, plus enough additional water to reduce the remaining three log by any amount. Although the overflowing water is mixed and contains both original invalid water and newly added valid water, once forty se'ah and a little more have flowed out the mikveh becomes valid, because the original disqualification was only caused by three log of drawn water. Here, by contrast, the entire mikveh originally consisted of drawn water. All this follows Raavad as cited by Beit Yosef, and Rama cites the same distinction nearby when he says, "specifically regarding three log," and so forth. Accordingly, the case in seif 21, where the only disqualification was the fall of drawn water, should have the same law as seif 22. However, Rambam as cited by Beit Yosef does not appear to understand it that way. We must therefore say that because there were originally only twenty se'ah of valid water, the situation is treated as though all the water were drawn.
