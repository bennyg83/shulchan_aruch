This is the language of R' Shimshon: "Even though the drawn water mixes with each of them, it does not invalidate gathering upon them — for they mixed by way of flowing, not by falling from a vessel into the valid waters; and what mixed from it with each one was nullified in the majority before descending into the mikveh; and drawn water that was nullified outside the mikveh does not invalidate, etc." — end of quote. This requires explanation regarding his statement that it "was nullified in the majority" — from where does he derive this? And in Perisha it is written that the water exits from each equally, and the drawn water that exits is divided — half one way and half the other — and that constitutes a majority. But he was not precise: for in the following seif as well, regarding two mikva'ot from the Tosefta which R' Shimshon cited, he also wrote: "and since they were nullified in the majority of the valid water before entering into the mikveh, as we explained in the mishnah" — end of quote. Thus he also wrote there that they were nullified in the majority. And it seems to me that R' Shimshon's intent is as follows: first he stated that drawn water coming into the valid mikveh by way of flowing does not invalidate; and if you should say the situation lacks the drawn-water leniency — such as where there are not three handbreadths — there is still a leniency, for the water that exits when one immerses returns to its place, and these drawn waters do likewise; and we do not say they exchanged with each other, for each one pushes the other back from entering; and thus the only concern is at the point where they touch each other — namely at their meeting point — where there is a slight mixture; and it is regarding that mixture that he said it was nullified in the majority of that stream. So it seems to me that this is R' Shimshon's correct intent — and there is no need to combine the second valid mikveh for this purpose.
