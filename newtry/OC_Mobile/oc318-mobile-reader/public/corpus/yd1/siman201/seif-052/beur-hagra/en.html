That which comes, etc. Mikvaot 6:7, according to the Rosh's explanation there and in his rulings, siman 15: drawn water, or, or. The Rash, however, mentions only the latter two cases, as written in seif 53. Therefore the Mechaber wrote "invalid" rather than "drawn," because regarding invalid water the Rash agrees, as stated in the gloss there, "specifically invalid," and so forth. That contact with a valid mikveh remedies drawn water is taught in Mikvaot 3:2, 6:3, and 6:8; regarding a deficient mikveh beside a complete one, see mishnayot 1 and 5 there; regarding two deficient mikvaot, see mishnah 3 there. This is specifically where the connecting opening is the width of the mouth of a wineskin, as in the aforementioned mishnayot, and all the more so where both are deficient.
And a stream, etc. The Rash at the end of chapter 5 of Taharot. One may also explain that in any event where the waters mingle through an opening, and so forth; then the passage is well explained. Tosafot likewise write in Chagigah 21b, s.v. "the thickness," in Yevamot 15a, s.v. "mixture," and in Gittin 16a, s.v. "the stream," unlike Rabbeinu Tam. See there.
Afterward, etc. Mikvaot 6:8 and 6:3.
It is permitted to dig, etc. As stated in Chagigah 22a, "for the earth," and so forth. Meaning, if one dug two or three pits, each lacking forty seah, they do not combine. This is also why separating them with a basket or wicker vessel does not help. But where a basket or wicker vessel is filled with utensils, since a complete mikveh surrounds them, it is different. This is the meaning of the Shulchan Aruch's words, "but if," and so forth. See Rash on Mikvaot 6:5. The statement in the chapter "Chomer" and so forth is different from a mikveh beside a river, because the river itself is a complete mikveh.
The same applies if he divided, etc. From the aforementioned Gemara.
