A spring. It purifies whether its water is flowing or collected, and it purifies vessels in any quantity. The Taz writes that this must refer to a spring that was already flowing, whose flow was now merely extended into a pool. If, however, the spring was initially stationary and was then drawn into a pool, or drawn water was added to it, although continuation through the spring is effective to validate the drawn water for immersion, it does not give the water the law of flowing spring water. It purifies only when collected, since the spring itself was not originally flowing. This appears clear to me.
A mikveh. A mikveh purifies only with forty seah and only when its water is collected. The Tur. The Shach writes that even if the water has not yet come to rest and is still flowing, once its connection to the spring has been interrupted it no longer has the status of a spring, but that of rainwater. The Perishah asks why this differs from immersion in rivers during the rainy season, which is disqualified in seif 2. There too the rainwater flows together with mikveh water, so why should the rainwater not have the law of mikveh water as it does here? The Shach answers that there the water is disqualified only for purification while flowing, but it is permitted when collected, since even if it were entirely rainwater it would be valid in that state.
It reverted. Nevertheless, it does not have the status of a spring in every respect. For a zav or a metzora it is not considered living spring water as it was initially, and living water is required for them. So writes the Beit Yosef. See simanim 30 and 80.
