It has the law of a spring. to purify both in flowing waters and in ashboran, and vessels in any amount.
To have for it the law of a mikveh.. it does not purify except in forty se'ah and in ashboran ; Tur. Maharik wrote, roots 115 and 196, that specifically where the water rested in the mikveh and already became ashboran; but where it has not yet rested and is still flowing, even though the spring was stopped, the law of a spring is upon them, for its vitality has not ceased since it flows; and proof from a wave that was uprooted (above, seif 5). And even though here the law of mikveh is upon them because the pool was full of water ; but if the pool was empty and filled from the spring, even though the spring was stopped, the law of a spring is upon it, end of his words. But the view of Ra'avad that he brought there is that in every case the law of mikveh is upon it; and so Rashba in Sha'ar HaMayim that Beit Yosef brought ; nevertheless the law of mikveh is upon it; and so implies the plain sense of the mishnah and poskim that there is no distinction; and so is proven in Perishah who explained Tur's words regarding an empty pool and an uprooted wave ; there is no proof, for that case is different, for there the wave uprooted itself and its vitality is great, and it also uprooted from the sea itself, and likewise its manner of flowing even though uprooted, unlike here. Also what Maharik brought there as proof ; otherwise it would be difficult for one who invalidates (below, seif 50) when they exit through a crack that mishnah of three mikvaot (below, seif 55), for there the water exited on the lip of the mikveh ; rather certainly there it deals with a spring that was stopped, end of his words; and Beit Yosef brings it below, beginning of daf 248 ; I do not recognize this proof for several reasons. One, that there it deals with three mikvaot that are springs that purify in flowing waters ; know that even for Maharik who establishes it regarding a spring, nevertheless he holds that in such a case it is a complete spring; if so, these three mikvaot are springs. And further, and this is primary: there there are no flowing waters at all, for flowing applies only when they flow by themselves; and likewise in a crack the water flows by itself through the crack and goes and does not return to flow; but those that exited on the lip of the mikveh through the power of the person immersing ; through this they were filled and exited on their lips, and when the immerser leaves they return to their first place ; certainly in such a case they are not flowing waters; and so is in responsum of Rivash, beginning of siman 292, explicitly, who brings there that mishnah of three mikvaot and wrote they are not called flowing waters even though the water will not remain in the place one immerses, rather it is called ashboran since it comes from the mikveh and to it they return, only it was diminished by necessity through a person or something else in the mikveh, etc., end of his words; and so too the Rav below, seif 50, even regarding a crack in a mikveh where they return there they are not called flowing waters. And further, there the mikvaot mix with each other through the water that exited on their lips, and there is no flowing here but mixture of mikvaot; therefore the essence is that when the spring was stopped there is no spring upon it even in flowing waters, rather it has the law of trickling waters.
The law of a mikveh, etc. Perishah challenged, seif 21: what is different from immersion in rivers during rains, which R' Yochanan and Rambam and his supporters invalidate (above, s.2) ; there too they are drawn along with the mikveh water; why should the rainwater not have the law of mikveh as here, etc.. And his answer is strained and remote; but it is not difficult at all, for there it is invalid only to purify through their flowing; but in ashboran it is permitted, for it would be at most like all rainwater ; and this is simple.
