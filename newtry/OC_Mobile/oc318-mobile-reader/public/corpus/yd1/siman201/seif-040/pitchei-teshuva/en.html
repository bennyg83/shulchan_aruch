One may rely upon the lenient authorities. See Responsa Mishkenot Yaakov, siman 45, concerning mikvaot made by digging deeply into the ground until water rises naturally. When such mikvaot are cleaned, must the vessel used to draw out the water be perforated with an opening the size of a shfoferet hanod, or may these mikvaot be treated as springs that are not invalidated by drawn water at all? He argues at great length for stringency for two reasons. First, the rule that a spring is not invalidated by drawn water is itself not clear and undisputed. Many major Rishonim hold that even a spring is invalidated by drawn water for human immersion when it contains less than forty se'ah. One should not be lenient against them except in great necessity, and even then only after the fact where an unperforated vessel introduced three log of drawn water. If most of the mikveh was created this way, there is certainly no basis for leniency even after the fact. Second, there is doubt whether these excavated mikvaot have the status of springs at all. The Tosefta, and Rambam accordingly, rule that one who digs beside a sea or river has the law of a mikveh, not a spring. If so, many claimed leniencies regarding drawn water in our mikvaot collapse, because who can determine which pit has an independent spring source and which is fed underground from a river, especially in countries situated among rivers and marshes where natural springs are uncommon? Although Shulchan Aruch and Rama below in seif 52 appear explicitly to contradict this, their words there require great study. For practical halachah, he writes, we should follow Rambam and the Tosefta. He concludes that regarding mikvaot near a river, whenever there is any doubt whether the underground water derives from the river rather than an independent spring source, no halachic authority should rely on spring leniencies to permit three log of drawn water, and certainly not a majority of drawn water, even after the fact. This is a possible karet prohibition. He later found that Responsa Maharit, part 2, also inclines to stringency in these matters.
