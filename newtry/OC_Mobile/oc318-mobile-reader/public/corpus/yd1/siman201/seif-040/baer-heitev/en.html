Initially. The Taz writes that this implies that if the opening is the size of a wineskin spout, one may bring the water even initially. This is difficult, because the preceding seif states that anything brought through human action invalidates even when no vessel is involved. It must therefore discuss a vessel not emptied by a person holding it. Rather, it is set beside the mikveh and water flows from it into the mikveh. By strict law this is permitted even if the hole in its bottom is minimal, but initially it should not be done unless the bottom opening is the size of a wineskin spout.
He shall pierce it. The Taz asks how a hole helps, since the water still comes to the mikveh through human action, which invalidates even without a vessel. He answers that once the bucket is pierced, there is no concern from human action. Since its water continuously flows through the hole into the well, it is viewed as never having been disconnected and remains joined. Although the Rama writes at the end of seif 39 that such a stream does not join the mikveh to a river, that case concerns treating water below in the river as though raised into the mikveh to supply forty se'ah, which would require the principle of extending upward. Here, however, we regard the stream as remaining below and never becoming drawn water, which is the principle of extending downward, and that principle applies. Accordingly, even if a person draws with a vessel pierced to the size of a wineskin spout and pours the water into the mikveh by hamshachah, the entire mikveh may be made this way initially. After the fact, even a minute hole is sufficient if water flows through it, provided it is at least the size of a lentil.
To pierce. The practice was to make a hole large enough to admit liquid. This is the Bach.

To plug. It must be plugged in a manner that leaves none of the original three log of invalid water. The Bach writes that it may even be plugged with cloth susceptible to impurity. Even according to the authority who forbids this in seif 50, here the plug serves only to dry the mikveh. This differs from the Taz, who holds that the authority who forbids there also forbids here. Maharshal agrees with the Bach.
