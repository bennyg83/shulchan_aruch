But if there is. Any pressure in the matter, to the point that it will lead to delay and the woman will be unable to immerse until she waits and her immersion is postponed, it is not proper at all that she delay immersing, etc. Maharik there; and so wrote the Atzei Zahav.
One may rely, etc. In the Shulchan Aruch, both here and below, Maharik roots 55 and 56 are cited. In truth, Maharik is more stringent even in a doubtful case, and where there is doubt he wrote there to rely on the lenient authorities if there is difficulty in the matter; see there. Rather, the master wrote his words according to the view of most poskim and the Beit Yosef, who are lenient. Similar to what Maharik wrote regarding a doubt, the master wrote regarding a certainty; and in a doubtful case he permitted entirely after the fact, like the view of the Semak and most poskim. Maharik himself also wrote so in root 55, except that afterward, in root 56, he was stringent; see there.
