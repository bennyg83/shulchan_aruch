(Supplement) In mikveh water. Living water is required only for a zav, as stated in the Tosefta at the beginning of chapter 3 of Zavim: a zav is more stringent than a zavah, for a zav requires living water whereas a zavah does not. Rashi and Tosafos cite this in Shabbos 65b, Tosafos in Bechoros 55b, the Rash in chapter 4 of Zavim, and the other authorities. The Tosefta in chapter 1 of Megillah likewise states that the only difference between a zav and a zavah is that a zav requires living water. The Rosh cites it in chapter 10, seif 8. (End of supplement.)
Or a spring. Toras Kohanim: "Only a spring, a cistern, or a gathering of water," etc.
That contains, etc. Toras Kohanim states that a spring purifies in any quantity, whereas a mikveh requires forty se'ah. Rashi and Tosafos cite this in Shabbos 65b and in Bechoros, and it is evident from other sources as well. Mishnah Mikvaos 1 states that it is equal to a spring in purifying in any quantity, and at the beginning of chapter 5 it states that a spring purifies in any quantity. The Raavad rules accordingly. See Tosafos, Pesachim 17b, s.v. Ela, where Ri says otherwise. The Rosh, in the name of Ri, writes that this applies to utensils only, but a person requires forty se'ah, because of Tosafos' question in Pesachim. The Rash likewise writes in chapter 5, Mishnah 2, though he notes that the matter is astonishing. Ri's proof is not at all clear to me. Can drawn water not travel through underground channels? Many streams of water enter a pit without filling it because the water passes through veins in the ground. Naturalists record many cases in which fish were found in enclosed pits, and others in which fish marked with silver rings and placed in such pits were later found in rivers. The Rashba, at the beginning of gate 7, cites other proofs in the name of Rabbeinu Tam, but they too are not conclusive. He writes that the Raavad and Rambam disagree, yet concludes that one should be stringent in a Torah-law matter.
(Supplement) Or a spring, etc. See Toras HaBayis, which cites Rabbeinu Tam's proof from Mishnah 5:6, concerning a wave. This has already been answered: the Mishnah speaks of forty se'ah so that a person can be covered by it. See Taz, seif katan 6. It teaches that his entire body must be covered at once within the wave. On the contrary, that source proves the opposite, that a spring purifies a person even while flowing, and by the same reasoning it purifies in any quantity. The Rivash explains that a spring purifies while flowing, unlike a mikveh, because descending water does not form a connection, whereas a spring purifies in any quantity. The Shach's comments there, seif katan 20, are not clear. Rabbeinu Tam also brings proof from Yoma 78a, "When it reaches the entrance of Beit David," but this is difficult to understand. Could one not immerse in it before then, when the entire spring is connected? Rather, the point is that the water must be deep enough to cover the person. (End of supplement.)
