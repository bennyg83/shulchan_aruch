Supplement: In mikveh water. Living water is required only for a zav, as stated in Tosefta, beginning of chapter 3 of Zavim: a zav is more stringent than a zavah in that a zav requires living water, whereas a zavah does not. Rashi and Tosafot cite this in Shabbat 65b, Tosafot in Bechorot 55b, the Rash in chapter 4 of Zavim, and other authorities. Likewise, Tosefta, chapter 1 of Megillah, states that the only difference between a zav and a zavah is that a zav requires living water, and the Rosh cites it in chapter 10, siman 8. End of supplement.
Or a spring. Torat Kohanim: "but a spring and a pit, a gathering of water," and so forth.
That contains, etc. Torat Kohanim there states that a spring purifies with any quantity, whereas a mikveh requires forty seah. Rashi and Tosafot cite this in Shabbat 65b and in Bechorot, and so too in Mikvaot 1:7, which equates it to a spring in purifying with any quantity, and at the beginning of chapter 5, where it states that a spring purifies with any quantity. The Raavad rules likewise. See Tosafot, Pesachim 17b, s.v. "rather," where Rabbeinu Yitzchak writes otherwise. The Rosh in the name of Rabbeinu Yitzchak writes that this applies only to utensils, but a person requires forty seah, based on the difficulty of Tosafot in Pesachim. The Rash likewise writes in chapter 5, mishnah 2, "if we were to say," but adds, "nevertheless this is surprising." To me, Rabbeinu Yitzchak's proof is not understood at all. Is it impossible for drawn water to travel through underground channels? Many streams of water flow into a pit without filling it because the water travels through channels in the earth. Naturalists have written that fish have several times been found in pools of collected water, and that fish marked with silver rings were placed in such pools and later found in rivers, together with many similar incidents. The Rashba, at the beginning of Gate 7, brings other proofs in the name of Rabbeinu Tam, but they too are not conclusive. He writes there that the Raavad and Rambam disagree, though he concludes that one should be stringent in a Torah prohibition.
Supplement: Or a spring, etc. See Torat HaBayit in the name of Rabbeinu Tam, who adduces a proof from Mikvaot 5:6 concerning a wave. This has already been answered, since the forty seah mentioned there is the quantity needed for a person to be covered by it; see Taz, seif katan 6. The novelty is that his entire body must be covered at once within the wave. Indeed, that very source proves the opposite, that a flowing spring purifies even a person, and likewise with any quantity. As the Rivash writes, the reason a spring purifies while flowing, unlike a mikveh, is that a descending slope is not considered a connection, whereas a spring purifies with any quantity. The Shach's words there, seif katan 20, are not clear. Rabbeinu Tam further brings a proof from Yoma 78a, "when it reaches the entrance of the beit din," but this is difficult to understand. Could one not immerse there earlier, since the entire spring is connected as one? Rather, the point is that there must be enough water to cover the body. End of supplement.
