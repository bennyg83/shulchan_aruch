"With the water of a mikveh," etc. — that is, rainwater gathered in a pit; and even though they are not mayim chayim, for mayim chayim are required only for a zav (as it is written of him, "mayim chayim"), but not for a niddah or a zavah. Tur and the poskim. But wells that flow are a complete spring (for every purpose), as Maharik wrote and Beit Yosef in the name of Terumat HaDeshen, and in responsum of Ramban siman 231 — see there.
"Or a spring," etc. Beit Yosef wrote that it implies that if the water is drawn along — such as a spring that flows and nowhere along it has forty se'ah — but when one combines all the water from beginning to end they are forty se'ah, one may immerse anywhere in it, for the body of the person immersing is covered by it, whether a person or a vessel — end of his words; and he brought proofs, and Darkei Moshe brings it; and it is straightforward.
"Forty se'ah," etc. — but if it lacks any amount whatsoever, it is invalid — Beit Yosef; and it is simple. And even a small body that is covered in less than forty se'ah — tevilah does not take effect in it until there are forty se'ah — Tur and the poskim.
"And it is not so high," etc. — see above, siman 198, seifim 36–37, and below, seif 66.
"Kosher" — when among all of it there are forty se'ah.
"One thousand eight hundred forty-eight fingers," etc. — so too in Beit Yosef; and Beit Yosef wrote one thousand and four hundred seventy-two fingers, and that calculation is directed at an amah of six tefachim, which is twenty-four fingers — an amah by amah, that is twenty-four fingers by twenty-four fingers, is nine hundred seventy-six fingers; and in height three amot, which is seventy-two fingers — seventy-two times nine hundred seventy-six comes to one thousand four hundred seventy-two. And here the Mechaber states according to what Rashba wrote, that we require a "laughing" amah, which is more than the "sad" amah by half a finger — an amah by amah is twenty-four and a half fingers by twenty-four and a half fingers, which is six hundred four fingers and a quarter; and in height three amot, which is seventy-three and a half fingers — seventy-three and a half times six hundred four and a quarter comes to one thousand eight hundred forty-eight and a half finger; less than half a quarter of a finger — examine this. And his words in Beit Yosef do not contradict what he states here in Shulchan Aruch, for in Beit Yosef he deals with "laughing" fingers, which are one thousand four hundred seventy-two, and therefore he did not mention the half finger; but here he deals with "sad" fingers, which are one thousand eight hundred forty-eight; but nevertheless all amounts to the measure of forty se'ah, neither less nor more, as we say at the end of chapter Arvei Pesachim (109): "A revi'it by Torah — two fingers by two fingers in the height of two fingers and a half finger and a fifth of a finger," etc., as we learned: "Mikveh waters — an amah by an amah in height three amot," and the Sages estimated forty se'ah, etc.; and the amah and fingers mentioned there deal with "laughing" ones, which are forty se'ah. And in Magen Avraham, daf 54 side b, he wrote that in Beit Yosef he did not examine precisely to calculate also the extra half finger in the "laughing" amah; and do not wonder, for thus we find in Rashi and Rashbam, and also Tosafot and Rif in chapter Arvei Pesachim — when they all stood to count and number the measure of a revi'it cup that they derived from the calculation of the mikveh of forty se'ah, you will not find in any one of their calculations that they paid attention to the half finger in the extra measure; but I can say that there, since the ultimate purpose of their calculation was to find a revi'it cup, which is only d'rabbanan, therefore they need not be concerned for the extra finger — end of his words. And it is astonishing, for behold it says "a revi'it by Torah," etc.; and furthermore, according to his words that here we require an extra half finger, if so there would be more than two thousand two hundred sixty-six fingers, which amounts to two se'ah and a half and a log and a half of a log approximately; if so the measure of a mikveh would be forty-two se'ah and a half log — yet in the Gemara and all poskim only forty se'ah is mentioned. And likewise in the first chapter of Eruvin (daf 14 side b) we say: "The sea that Solomon made held one hundred fifty mikvaot of purity," as it is written, "It would contain two thousand bat"; and a bat is three se'ah — if it were so, it would not even have one hundred mikvaot. Rather, take this principle in your hand: the amot and fingers in the Gemara and poskim on this matter deal with "laughing" ones; and on this Rashba wrote that they are an extra half finger over the "sad" ones, and when you make them "sad" they amount to one thousand eight hundred forty-eight fingers, as the Mechaber wrote. Therefore we calculate everything as "sad," and we require one thousand eight hundred forty-eight fingers; and so agreed the Gaon, may his light shine. And all this is clear. And see in the responsum of Mabit, part 2, siman 143, daf 68 side 9; and in the responsum of Mahariv, siman 75, he wrote that the length of two shoes of an average person is an amah — see there; and see above, siman 472, I wrote in the name of Rambam how much is a thumbbreadth.
