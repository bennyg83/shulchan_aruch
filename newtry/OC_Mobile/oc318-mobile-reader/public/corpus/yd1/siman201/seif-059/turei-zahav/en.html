As though it were connected to the spring. It becomes valid through contact with the spring. The Beit Yosef writes that this is unlike drawing water from a spring into a vessel, discussed in seif 8, where connection to the spring is ineffective. There, the receptacle is a vessel with a cavity, and the water is invalidated as drawn water. Here, however, it has no receptacle, and although it can contract impurity, the contact is effective. I do not understand this distinction. Once the connection constitutes valid contact, it is immaterial that the water is drawn, because all drawn water is purified through contact. Above, in seif 8, I wrote that there is no difficulty, because the ruling there is based on a rabbinic decree. The Beit Yosef himself writes similarly at the end of this siman, in the passage beginning, “If one immerses a pot in it,” that according to the Rambam, connection to a spring is effective even when the mikvah is inside a vessel, whereas according to the Rosh he writes that it is ineffective. In my humble opinion, even according to the Rosh it is effective as a matter of law, as I wrote in seif 8. [Citation 12.]
