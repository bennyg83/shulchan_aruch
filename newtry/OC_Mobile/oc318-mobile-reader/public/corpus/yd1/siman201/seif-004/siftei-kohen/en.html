"Unless," etc. — and Bach wrote that this is specifically when locked with a key; but when not locked, since idolaters can enter into it and take water from there for all their needs and return and fill it, we do not believe the idolater even if the mikveh is complete, for we are concerned lest he return and fill it, as Tur wrote — end of his words; and it implies that we are not concerned that they took water from there and exchanged it, except when he benefits from the exchange (as above, siman 118, seif 10) — such as when the water in the mikveh is foul and he fears he will lose his wage that women will not immerse in it, as stated in responsum of Rosh; or as Beit Yosef wrote, lest they sprinkled in order to dilute it from the dirty water and filled it with clean water to wash their bodies — end of his words; but if not so, we are not concerned lest he intended to cause stumbling; and so too Beit Yosef.
There is always in the mikveh twenty-one se’ah. The Mechaber’s intent is not because of doubtful rabbinic law to be lenient, for he himself holds below, seif 53, that even all drawn water is only d’rabbanan; rather he deals with a locked mikveh where one cannot bring water into it except by drawing along through the roof; and therefore when there are twenty-one se’ah in it, it is not invalidated by drawn water since they were drawn along, as below, seif 44; and so explicitly in responsum of Rosh, and Beit Yosef brings it. And the Rav follows his method below, seif 46, that drawing along helps only on the ground; therefore here through the roof, drawing along is invalid, and therefore he needed the reason of doubtful rabbinic law to be lenient; and therefore he required it to be known that there are always twenty-one se’ah there.
