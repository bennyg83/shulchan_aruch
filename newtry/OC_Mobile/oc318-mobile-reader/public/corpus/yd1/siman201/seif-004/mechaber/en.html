A mikveh that belongs to a non-Jew, and he receives payment from him — one may not trust the non-Jew regarding it, unless there are (always) 21 se’ah in the mikveh. {Rama: Since most of it is kosher, its safek is treated leniently. [If] a mikveh was lacking before us, and one came and found it full (Beit Yosef in the name of a responsum of Rashbatz), and we do not know who filled it — if its majority had already been with validity, meaning that there were 21 se’ah in it, we go leniently; but if its majority had not been with validity, now there is a safek d’oraisa whether one must be concerned that a non-Jew filled it in order to bathe in it, [and it is] invalid (so it appears in the Tosefta). And if not — for certainly a Jew filled it in order to immerse in it, [and it is] kosher (Rashbatz there), for most who are found by a mikveh and act to make it kosher are experts, and certainly he filled it with validity. And all the more so if the Jew is before us and says that he filled it with validity, for he is believed (Mahari”k, shoresh 115; and not like Rashbatz), since it is in his power to repair it; and in such a case, one witness is believed regarding prohibitions (there; see Darkei Moshe), as above siman 127. And see further below at the end of the siman regarding the law of a safek of drawn [water].}
