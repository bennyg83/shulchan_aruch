Its doubt. The Shach wrote: the Mechaber's intent is not because it is a rabbinic doubt and therefore lenient, for he himself holds below in seif 23 that even entirely drawn water is only rabbinically invalid. Rather, he speaks of a locked mikveh into which water can be introduced only by hamshachah through the roof. Therefore, since it has twenty-one se'ah, it is not invalidated by drawn water, because the water was drawn through hamshachah, as below in seif 44. The Rav follows his own view below in seif 46, that hamshachah helps only over the ground; therefore here, on a roof, hamshachah invalidates, so he needed the reason that a rabbinic doubt is lenient. The Taz likewise wrote that one should not believe a non-Jew even in a matter that is a rabbinic doubt, all the more so here where the non-Jew benefits. Therefore what Shulchan Aruch writes here, that there is concern only if there are not twenty-one se'ah, is imprecise. The Bach likewise wrote that this applies specifically where it is locked with a key. But if it is not locked, since the non-Jew can enter it, take water from it, and then refill it, he is not believed even if the mikveh is full, for we are concerned that he may refill it. The Shach wrote: it implies that we are not concerned that he removed water and exchanged it unless he benefits from the exchange, as above in siman 118 seif 10, such as where the mikveh water is foul and he fears losing his wage, or he wants to empty it of dirty water and refill it with clean water for washing their bodies. Otherwise, we are not concerned that he intended to cause failure. End quote. Maharibal II, siman 69, wrote: this law applies only to a rainwater mikveh. But in a mikveh of living water that flows from the ground, even if at times the spring dries up and only a revi'it remains, one need not be concerned for drawn water, for drawn water does not invalidate a spring. See there for distinctions. See Maharit, Yoreh De'ah siman 19, Mishbit Milchamot siman 12, and Pleitat Beit Yosef siman 5. Knesset HaGedolah wrote in the name of his teacher that if the key was in Jewish hands, but that day the door suddenly broke, there is no concern that on that day he sprinkled the mikveh and filled it, for he is afraid; see there.
