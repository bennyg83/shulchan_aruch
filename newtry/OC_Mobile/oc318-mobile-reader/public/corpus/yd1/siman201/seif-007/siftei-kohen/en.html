"And he pierced it," etc. — specifically in its base we require that it cannot receive any water at all; but a hole from the side, even like the opening of a wineskin, and slightly higher than the base, and it receives from the hole and below a bit of water — is invalid, as explained in the mishnah and poskim; and so below, seif 40.
"The opening that purifies it from tumah." And this is like removing an olive, which is less than the opening of a wineskin — Beit Yosef; and below, seif 40, it is explained how much that is. And know that there, nearby, Rambam wrote: a trough in rock does not invalidate the mikveh because it is not a vessel; but a vessel attached to rock invalidates the mikveh even if attached with mortar — if pierced from below or from the side like the opening of a wineskin it is valid and does not invalidate the mikveh — end of his words. And Beit Yosef distinguished: that which requires (here) [there] the opening of a wineskin and does not suffice with like removing an olive — is because there the vessel was attached to rock while still whole, unlike here where it was pierced and afterward set — end of his words. Accordingly, what our rabbi and one authority wrote is not precise, for Rosh and Tur deal with setting it whole; and it is possible that when pierced and afterward set he concedes to Rambam's law. But it appears our rabbi holds like the other version in Beit Yosef, that there it deals with all stones, similar to a trough, that one cannot estimate in them a measure of taharah since they do not receive tumah; or alternatively, a trough in rock is different, etc. — see there; and so implies Beit Yosef himself, who holds those versions are primary, for he afterward wrote on Tur: and if like Rambam he holds, behold he holds that in every case one immerses in it like removing an olive, whereas Tur requires the opening of a wineskin for a plain cistern even in every case — therefore, etc.; and what is the difficulty? One can say Tur deals when he set it whole. Rather certainly there is no distinction — examine. Nevertheless this requires further study, for it implies our author holds that any opening at all does not suffice by law; and below, beginning of seif 40, he wrote: a vessel pierced in its base, even any amount, is not considered a vessel to invalidate the mikveh; and it is possible his words here are according to what he wrote there afterward. Nevertheless one should not be lenient l'chatchila to make a mikveh in such a vessel; and it means only that it requires an opening that purifies it — and this is strained.
"Behold this is valid." — even to immerse in it.
"With mortar and building," etc. — meaning: two things are required — one, that it be pierced with an opening that purifies it; and furthermore it must be set in the ground, or there must be proof that it is a matter of building and not a matter of a vessel. Therefore if they plugged with mortar and building — meaning with stones and the like, like building — it is not considered a vessel at all, and therefore it does not invalidate the mikveh — meaning the water inside is not considered drawn water; and the same — it is valid to immerse in it. But when sealed only with mortar or gypsum alone, since building is not done with these alone without stones or pebbles, the vessel-status still applies and it invalidates the mikveh; and all the more so it is forbidden to immerse in it. After this it says: even though they did not set it in the ground — meaning buried in the ground — and also did not build, but only placed it on the ground and plastered — it is valid, even if placed on mortar and gypsum, since the mortar and gypsum on the ground stand like the ground, support of water; and whatever plasters the sides with clay is considered as set and valid — Beit Yosef. But Rosh, Rabbeinu Shimshon, and Rashba have another approach in explaining mortar and gypsum, and Beit Yosef brings them; and Taz wrote below, seif 40 — see there.
