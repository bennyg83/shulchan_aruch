One who bought a large vessel, such as a large barrel or a large kneading-trough, and bored into it a hole that purifies it (and some say that we require a hole like the tube of a leather flask (Tur, and Rosh in a responsum), and so one should be stringent), and set it into the ground and made it a mikveh — this is kosher. And likewise, if he plugged the hole with lime and with building, it does not disqualify, and the waters gathered in it are a kosher mikveh. If he sealed it with lime or with gypsum, it still disqualifies the mikveh until he sets it into the ground or builds [around it]. And if he led it on top of the ground and on top of the lime, and plastered with clay from the sides, this is kosher. {Rama: It is permitted to make it on the roof, provided that it is not within a vessel or a single stone that one hollowed out and afterward set [in place]; but joining many stones is not called a vessel (responsum of Rashba, siman 400).}
