That renders it pure. Meaning, through this hole it loses the legal status of a vessel and can no longer contract impurity. The required size is one that allows an olive to pass through, which is smaller than the opening of a wineskin tube. Taz. This applies specifically when the hole is in its bottom. If it was perforated at the side, even if the hole is the size of a wineskin tube, but it is slightly above the bottom and the vessel can retain some water below the hole, it is invalid, as stated below in seif 40, indexed here as citation 400. The Shach writes that this requires study, for it appears that the Mechaber maintains that, as a matter of law, even the smallest hole is insufficient. Yet at the beginning of seif 40 he writes that a vessel perforated in its bottom, even by the smallest hole, is no longer considered a vessel so as to invalidate the mikvah. Perhaps his words here follow what he writes there afterward, that nevertheless one should not initially construct a mikvah with such a vessel, meaning that initially a hole large enough to render it pure is required. This explanation is strained. End of quotation.
