One may rely upon the lenient authorities. See Responsa Mishkenos Yaakov, siman 45, concerning mikvaos formed by digging deeply into the ground until naturally flowing water is found. When they wish to clean them, must the vessel used to draw out the water be perforated with an opening the size of a wineskin spout, or may one say that these mikvaos have the status of a spring and are not invalidated by drawn water at all? He rules at great length stringently for two reasons. First, the principle that a spring is not invalidated by drawn water is itself neither clear nor simple. Many leading early authorities maintain that even a spring is invalidated by drawn water for immersion of a person when it contains less than forty seah. One should not rule leniently against them, except in a case of great necessity, where after the fact one may rely on the lenient view if the vessel was not perforated and three log of drawn water fell in. If, however, most of the mikveh was made in this manner, there is certainly no basis for leniency under any circumstances, even after the fact. Second, there is reason to doubt whether the mikvaos under discussion have the status of a spring at all. The Tosefta states, and Rambam rules, that one who digs alongside the sea or a river has the status of a mikveh, not a spring. Consequently, all grounds for permitting drawn water in our mikvaos collapse, for who can determine which mikveh is an independently flowing well and which derives from a river, especially in regions situated among rivers and marshes where independent springs are not found at all? Although the Shulchan Aruch and Rama below, seif 52, appear explicitly to contradict this, their words there require great study. In practice, we have only the ruling of Rambam and the Tosefta. He concludes that regarding mikvaos near a river, whenever there is reason to suspect that the spring has no independent source and that its water is drawn underground from the river, no halachic authority may employ any leniency applicable to a spring, whether by failing to invalidate it through three log or, all the more so, through a majority of drawn water, even after the fact. This is a possible prohibition carrying kareis, and one who rules leniently has no sound basis. He subsequently found that Maharit, part 2, likewise rules stringently in all these matters. See there.
