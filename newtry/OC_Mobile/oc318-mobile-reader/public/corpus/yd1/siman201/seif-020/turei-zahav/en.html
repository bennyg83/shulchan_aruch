It has the law of a spring. The Tur writes that it has every law of a spring, and it is obvious that it purifies even while flowing. The Mishnah in chapter 5 of Mikvaot states: If a flowing spring was increased and extended, it remains as it was. If it was standing and one increased it and extended it, it is equivalent to a mikvah in that it purifies only when gathered, and to a spring in that one may immerse in it with any quantity of water. We thus see a distinction. When the spring was already flowing, an addition of rainwater or drawn water retains the law of a spring in every respect. When the spring was originally standing and not flowing at all, after the addition it has the law of a mikvah with regard to requiring gathered water. Accordingly, the ruling here that one extended a spring into a pool and that the pool has every law of a spring must refer to a spring that was already flowing, and whose flow was merely extended farther into the pool. If, however, the spring originally stood still and was then extended into the pool, or if drawn water was added to it, although extension is effective to purify the drawn water so that one may immerse in it, it does not acquire the law of flowing water. It purifies only when gathered, because the spring itself was not flowing initially. This appears clear to me. [Citation 80.]
