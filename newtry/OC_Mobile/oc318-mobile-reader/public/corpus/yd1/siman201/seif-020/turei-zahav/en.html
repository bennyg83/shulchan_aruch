It has the law of a spring. Tur writes that it has all the laws of a spring, and it is obvious that it purifies even while flowing. The Mishnah in chapter 5 of Mikvaot states: if a flowing spring was increased with other water and extended, it remains as it was; if it was standing and one increased it and caused it to flow, it is like a mikveh with respect to requiring gathered water, but like a spring in that immersion in any amount is valid. Thus there is a distinction between an already flowing spring, where added rainwater or drawn water assumes the law of the spring for every purpose, and a spring that was originally standing and not flowing. In the latter case, after adding water and making it flow, it has the law of a mikveh regarding the requirement that the water be gathered and not flowing. Accordingly, the present law, where one extends a spring into a pool and the pool has all the laws of a spring, must refer to a spring that was already flowing and whose flow is merely extended into the pool. But if the spring was originally standing and one then caused it to flow into the pool, or increased it with drawn water, although extending the water can make the drawn water valid for immersion, it does not give the added flow the law of flowing spring water. Since the spring itself was not flowing at the outset, it purifies only when gathered. This appears entirely clear to me.
