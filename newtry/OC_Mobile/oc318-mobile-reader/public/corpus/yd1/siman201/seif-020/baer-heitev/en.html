In its invalid state. This discusses a channel containing less than forty se'ah, for otherwise the pit would be purified through contact. This is the Shach.
Remained. The Shach writes that the overflowing water is not all presumed to be the original water, meaning the exact amount formerly in the pit. Rather, the outgoing water is apportioned according to the ratio between the water originally in the pit and the water entering it. Thus, if the channel water is greater, for example, the pit contains forty log, the channel water one hundred twenty log, and eighty log flow out, we calculate that three parts, sixty log, came from the channel and one part, twenty log, came from the pit, leaving twenty log of the pit water. The Raavad writes that one should calculate half and half, which in that case is lenient. If the pit water is greater, proportional calculation would say that sixty log of pit water flowed out and only sixty remained, whereas half-and-half calculation would say that only forty log flowed out and eighty remained. It appears that in either direction we rule stringently. One might ask why here we calculate proportionally, whereas later we presume that all invalid water floats out first. There, all the water was initially valid and became invalid only through three log of drawn water. Strict law would require only proportional calculation until three log were removed, but stringency requires removal of the full volume, meaning forty se'ah. We therefore view only the invalidated water as having left, without proportionally including the valid rainwater that entered afterward. Here, however, all the water initially in the pit was drawn, so the fact that an amount equal to the original contents left does not establish that all original water left. We always say that channel water also left proportionally.
