Remained. Shach writes that the water that rises and exits is not all treated as the original water that filled the pit. Rather, we calculate proportionally between the original pit water and the water entering from the channel. For example, if the pit contained forty log and the channel water was one hundred twenty log, and eighty log then flowed out, we assume that three quarters of what exited came from the channel, namely sixty log, and one quarter from the pit, namely twenty log. Thus twenty log of the original pit water remain. Ra'avad, however, writes that we estimate half and half. When the pit water is the majority, this can produce the opposite practical result. It appears that in each direction we should rule stringently. One may ask why here we calculate proportionally, whereas below we regard the invalid water as though it exits first. There, the existing water was valid and only three log of drawn water invalidated it. By strict law proportional calculation would suffice until three log had left, but as a stringency we require removal of the full amount. Here, however, all the original pit water was drawn water, so even when an amount equal to what originally stood in the pit has flowed out, we cannot assume that all the original water left. We must always calculate proportionally, because some of the channel water also flowed out. Shach.
