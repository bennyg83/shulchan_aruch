Valid,. etc. ; Bach wrote: this is specifically according to the one who said above, seif 40, that a spring is not invalidated by drawing; but for us who are stringent there, the same here ; end of his words. And for now there is another reason here, as Rosh wrote (from whom this law is derived) in a responsum, general principle 31, halachah 11, and so it is: because most of the strength of the water is from its place of springing; and likewise a live-water well, its springing is from roots spreading here and there under the ground; and also in time of heat and drought when their roots are cut off and their water cannot fill the well, nevertheless the moisture of the roots and water remain in them, only they are little and insufficient to fill the well; nevertheless when water comes upon them and they add water from themselves, it is found that drawing was purified through increase and drawing ; end of his words; and Beit Yosef brings it. And if so, the first reason Rosh wrote there is not needed: and further, a known matter that those roots cannot remain without any water at all, and since they have some water they have the law of a spring, and a spring is not invalidated by drawing ; if so. Also in Rosh's responsum it appears clear that the first reason is primary and the whole responsum is built on this reason; and it appears that even one who says drawn water that was entirely drawn is invalid concedes here for two reasons stated; and even one who invalidates drawing in a spring, even if he also holds drawn water that was entirely drawn is invalid ; still, one can say he concedes here since there are two aspects for leniency: drawn water that was drawn and a spring; and so appears the view of the Rav and Taz, that here for all it is valid.
