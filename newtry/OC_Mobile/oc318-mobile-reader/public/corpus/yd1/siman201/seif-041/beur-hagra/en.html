One who leaves, etc. This is the Tur's wording. He understands the passage to mean that at the time clouds gather, the water is considered intentionally left, while when they disperse it is considered forgotten, even if he did not actually forget it. The dispute concerns water gathered while the clouds were forming, and all agree that he did not forget it. This is strained. Rashi explains that all cases involve actual forgetting. The Rambam has another approach; see his commentary. He rules like Rabbi Meir, whereas the Tur rules like Rabbi Yose, as codified here, because the halachah follows Rabbi Yose against Rabbi Meir. The Rambam's reason is that the Gemara's discussion follows Rabbi Meir. It appears that the Tannaim follow their respective positions elsewhere. See Tosafos 14b, s.v. Ela, where Ri explains that the Mishnah separately counts liquids. According to Rabbi Meir, however, even the capacity to impart impurity to foods is rabbinic, as stated in Pesachim 15b and 16a, so these are counted as one. Rabbi Yose follows his own view that imparting impurity to foods is biblical. The Rambam likewise follows his own ruling there like Rabbi Meir, because the anonymous Mishnah in chapter 4 of Taharos accords with him. The Rambam, however, does not accept Tosafos' explanation, as seen in chapter 8, halachah 10. In any event, his words there are exceedingly difficult, as the Raavad and Tosafos note. See siman 164.
