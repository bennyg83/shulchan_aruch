One who leaves. This is Tur's language. He understands the teaching as follows: at the time the clouds gather, the person is considered to have intentionally left the vessels, while when the clouds disperse it is called forgetting, even if he did not actually forget them. The dispute is therefore where the clouds had gathered, although he had not forgotten the vessels. This is forced. Rashi explains the entire passage as involving actual forgetfulness. Rambam has a different approach; see his commentary. He rules like R. Meir, whereas Tur rules like R. Yehudah, as the Shulchan Aruch does here, because in a dispute between R. Meir and R. Yehudah the halachah follows R. Yehudah. Rambam's reason is that the Gemara's discussion follows R. Meir, as seen from its statements there. This appears connected to their broader positions. See Tosafot there 14b, who explain that liquids were counted separately. According to R. Meir, however, who holds that even impurity imparted to food by such liquids is rabbinic, the two matters are effectively one category, whereas R. Yose follows his own view that impurity of foods in this case is biblical. Rambam likewise follows his own ruling there like R. Meir based on an anonymous Mishnah in chapter 4 of Taharot. Rambam does not accept Tosafot's explanation, however, as appears in chapter 8, halachah 10. In any event, his words there are very difficult, as Raavad and Tosafot note.
