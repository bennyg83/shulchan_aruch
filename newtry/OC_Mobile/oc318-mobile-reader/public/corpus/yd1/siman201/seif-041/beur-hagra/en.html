**One who leaves, etc.** The language of the Tur; and he holds that this is what it means: at the time of the binding of clouds, this is “leaving,” and at the time of dispersing it is called “forgetting,” even if he did not forget them at all; and they dispute when they became bound, etc.; and all of this is when he did not forget them — and this is forced. But Rashi explained there that all of it is when he forgot them. And the Rambam has a different approach — see there in the commentary — and he ruled like Rabbi Meir; but the Tur ruled like Rabbi Yehudah, and as written here in the Shulchan Aruch: [between] Rabbi Meir and Rabbi Yehudah, the halacha is like Rabbi Yehudah. And the Rambam’s reason is that the sugya of the Gemara there follows Rabbi Meir, for it says “and the other, etc.,” and we say there on daf 17b: if so, according to Rabbi Meir, etc. — foods and vessels, etc.; and it appears that each follows his own method. And see Tosafot there, daf 14b, s.v. ella, etc.: Rabbi Yitzchak said, etc. — rather he mentioned liquids (etc.), and therefore he counts them as two; but according to Rabbi Meir, who holds that even to render foods tamei is d'rabbanan, as written in Pesachim 15b–16a, if so he counts them as one; and Rabbi Yose according to his view, who said that to render foods tamei is d'oraisa, as written there. And the Rambam follows his method, for he also ruled there like Rabbi Meir, since the anonymous mishnah in chapter 4 (of Taharot) is according to him; but the Rambam does not hold that [point] of the aforementioned Tosafot, as he wrote in chapter 8, law 10; and even without this, his words there are very puzzling, as Raavad and Tosafot wrote.
