A mikveh that has 40 se’ah, and a spring of any amount — one may draw all that he wants and place into it, and they are kosher even though they are more than the waters that were in it initially. (And there is no difference whether the spring preceded the drawn [waters] or not, as was explained; and see above se’if 40, for there are those who disagree) (Beit Yosef in the name of a responsum of Ramban, siman 231). But as long as there is not 40 se’ah in the mikveh — even if it is lacking only the slightest amount — if 3 logim of drawn water fell into it, they disqualified it, whether one drew in a vessel, or whether one wrung out his garment and lifted it and the waters in it fell from many places (Rambam perek 5). And likewise, one who pours from a flask and pours from many places into it, or threw [the water] with his handfuls — and even if [the waters] fell into it from 2 vessels or from 3, a little from this and a little from that, they combine. In what case is this said? When he begins from the second vessel before the first vessel ceased. But if the first ceased before the second began, they do not combine. And if the second began before the first ceased, this is specifically from 3 vessels, but from 4 they do not combine. In what case is it said that from 4 they do not combine? When from the outset his intention was not to give all 3 logim; but if from the outset his intention was to give all 3 logim, even if he gave only little by little from many vessels until he completed 3 logim, it is invalid. {Rama: And if he passed 3 logim with his hands upon the ground, or they spurted from the legs of an animal, it is kosher (Tur). And some say that the legs of an animal are like the legs of a person, and in both cases it is kosher only when he passed [the water] upon the ground, for this is like “hamshachah,” but not when it spurted (Rashi, perek 2 of Mikvaos, and Rambam perek 5). And see below in this siman, se’if 39.}
