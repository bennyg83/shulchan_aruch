"And a spring," etc. — or a spring in any quantity. And Beit Yosef wrote in the name of responsum of Ramban, siman 231, that a spring in any quantity, even if standing, such as these mikvaot whose water is from spring sources — even though its water is little, it is not invalidated by increase of drawn water; and so implies from the words of other poskim.

"Even though they are many," etc. — and Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Rambam and Rosh: however, from the spring the law of flowing waters was nullified, rather it purifies only in ashboran; and so Bach for Tur's view, and so Darkei Moshe for Tur's view, and he brings it in Hagahat Drishah, seif 25; and the Rav already explained this in seif 11. And nevertheless it is clear that the law of a spring is upon it for immersion of vessels in any quantity, and the Mechaber did not explain this, that it is a spring for immersion of vessels in any quantity; and so in several places in this siman, such as seifim 2, 5, 10, 11, and other places, he should have explained and did not — because since tumah and taharah are not practiced among us, immersion of vessels nowadays is not relevant; and for vessels taken from idolaters, in truth we hold that forty se'ah are also required like a person, as explained at the beginning of siman 120 — see there.
"And see above, seif 40," etc. — meaning that there it is explained that there are disputants: in a spring of any quantity that does not have forty se'ah, drawn water invalidates it — see there.
"Three log," etc. — and both at the beginning (meaning there was drawn water at first in the pit and afterward mikveh-water) and at the end (drawn water fell after mikveh-water) it is not invalidated except by three log — so too Beit Yosef in the name of the poskim; and so ruled the acharonim.
"The language 'he wrings his garment and lifted it,'" etc. — meaning the mikveh lacked forty se'ah then; when he immerses and lifts his garment, the water in his garment becomes drawn and invalidates the lacking mikveh if there are three log in it; but when he did not lift the garment, since the water in the garment comes from this mikveh and is still connected to it, it did not become drawn.
"He wrings his garment," etc. — Beit Yosef in the name of responsum of Ramban, siman 231, in the name of Ra'avad: the reason is since the water descends to the mikveh from a person's hands. According to this he answered that above, seif 7, in a vessel pierced in its base it does not invalidate the mikveh, and a garment here is like a pierced vessel; and he wrote: and since so, even in a pierced vessel, whatever fell from a person's hand drawing to the mikveh invalidates the mikveh; and they only said it does not invalidate when they fall by themselves from a pierced vessel to the mikveh, for since they exited through its piercing from the category of vessel and fall by themselves to the mikveh, it is valid — end of his words. And Bach wrote, siman 221: and it appears to me from precision of his language that specifically when the person intends to draw to cast into the mikveh there it invalidates; but if they fell from his hand unintentionally, it is as if they fell by themselves not through a person and they do not invalidate, etc. According to this he strained to reconcile that of one who removes mud to the sides, etc.; but to me it appears the opposite from precision of his language: whatever comes from a person's hand, even unintentionally, invalidates; and specifically when they fell by themselves from a pierced vessel to the mikveh is it valid; and so appears from Ba'alei Nefesh to Ra'avad, daf 69 side a, and the case of removing mud fits its plain sense — see there; and Beit Yosef brings it below nearby. But it is difficult, for Tur and the Rav and other poskim wrote below, end of seif 40, that one who draws from a mikveh and fears lest they return from the vessel with which they draw water — three log to another mikveh after forty se'ah were lacking and will invalidate it — pierce the vessel in its base and then they are not considered drawn water; and what of it — in the end through a person, even by itself, is invalid? One can say it is different there, since he draws water from there, if so he certainly does not want the water to fall there, for on the contrary he draws them from there and it is by itself — examine. Taz wrote: some say specifically when they flow by themselves from the garment to the mikveh they are not called drawn; but if he squeezed them in his hands, even though the garment is still partly in the mikveh, they are drawn and invalid, etc. And in Ma'adanei Melech, daf 307, he wrote he did not know who these "some say" are; and he understood he intended for Ramban in the name of Ra'avad above that Beit Yosef brings at the end of daf 237; and therefore he challenged him, for their view is opposite; and it appears to me he intended for Rokeach that Beit Yosef brings below, this siman, daf 246, beginning of side 4, and so it is: and it implies their essence is that he lifted it from inside it; but Rokeach wrote "lifted from inside it" meaning squeezed with hands from it — end of his words; it appears from his words that even though he did not lift it, since water exited through squeezing, they invalidate the mikveh — end of his words, Beit Yosef; and see below, seif 63.
