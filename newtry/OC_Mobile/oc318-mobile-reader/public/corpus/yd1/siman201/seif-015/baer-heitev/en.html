And a spring. Meaning, even a minimal amount of spring water, though standing still, such as mikvaot whose water comes from spring sources, may have drawn water added to it and remain valid. This is specifically when collected and standing. Regarding purification through flowing water, however, it does not have the law of a spring, because initially the spring itself stood still and did not flow. Taz. Shach cites R. Yerucham that if the water thrown into the spring then flowed into an empty, deficient mikveh, some authorities also permit immersion there, implying that others prohibit it, and one should therefore be stringent. This applies specifically to an empty deficient mikveh. If even a small amount remained, and twenty-one se'ah or more remained there, the drawn water does not invalidate it according to anyone, because a majority of the required measure may be completed through hamshachah.
Many. Rambam and Rosh write that the added water loses the law of flowing spring water and purifies only when standing. Nevertheless, it still has the law of spring water for immersing vessels in even a minimal amount. The Mechaber does not spell out this law because laws of ritual impurity and purity are not practiced by us today, so immersion of ordinary vessels for that purpose is not relevant. As to vessels acquired from gentiles, we rule that they too require forty se'ah, like a person, as stated at the beginning of siman 120. Shach.
