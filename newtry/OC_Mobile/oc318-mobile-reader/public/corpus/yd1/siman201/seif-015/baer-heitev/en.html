And a spring. This means even a spring of any quantity, though it is stationary, such as mikvaos whose water comes from spring sources. The statement that one may draw water and that they remain valid applies only when the water is gathered. Regarding flowing water, it certainly does not retain the status of a spring, since the spring was originally stationary and did not flow. This is the Taz. The Shach cites Rabbeinu Yerucham that if water poured into a spring then flowed into an empty, deficient mikveh, some permit immersion in it as well, while others apparently forbid it. It is therefore proper to be stringent. This applies specifically where the deficient mikveh was completely empty. If it lacked even the smallest amount or more but still contained twenty-one se'ah, the drawn water does not invalidate it according to any opinion, because a majority of a mikveh may be completed through hamshachah.
Abundant. The Rambam and Rosh write that it nevertheless loses the spring's law of purifying while flowing and purifies only when gathered. It is still considered a spring for immersion of utensils in any quantity. The Mechaber did not state this because laws of ritual purity do not presently apply, and thus such immersion of utensils is not relevant today. Utensils purchased from non-Jews, however, require forty se'ah just as a person does, as stated at the beginning of siman 120. This is the Shach.
Disagree. They maintain that drawn water invalidates a spring of any quantity when it contains less than forty se'ah.
His garment. The Shach explains that when he immerses his garment and then raises it, the water in the garment becomes drawn water and invalidates a deficient mikveh if it contains three log. If he does not raise the garment, the water within it came from this mikveh and remains connected to it, so it does not become drawn water. This applies even when the water was squeezed out unintentionally, whenever it came through human action. The Taz agrees, unlike the Bach, who requires intent. One cannot object from seif 40, where one draining a mikveh who fears that three log might return from the vessel after the mikveh falls below forty se'ah is instructed to pierce the vessel's bottom, thereby preventing the water from being considered drawn. Although it still returns through human action, that case is different because he is removing the water and clearly does not want it to fall back. It therefore returns of itself. The Levush cites an opinion that only water flowing by itself from the garment is not considered drawn, but if he squeezes it by hand, even while part of the garment remains in the mikveh, it is drawn water and invalidates it. The Maadanei Melech writes that he does not know who this opinion is. It appears to me that it is derived from the Rokeach, who writes, "he squeezed it by hand." His words indicate that even though the garment was not raised, water removed through squeezing invalidates the mikveh.
Combine. Rashi writes that at least one log must come from each vessel; less than a log does not combine.
From four. Even if the water from all of them fell in together, since no vessel supplied a full log.
Ground. He did not lift the water from the ground, but caused it to flow along the ground from one place to another until the mikveh, or it came from the feet of an animal even without flowing along the ground. The Taz writes that the Rama's statement, "In both cases it is valid only if he conveyed it," discusses a mikveh made entirely in this manner.
