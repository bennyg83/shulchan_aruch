Drawn waters do not disqualify the mikveh with 3 logim until they fall from the vessel into the mikveh; but if the drawn waters were dragged outside the mikveh and were drawn and descended into the mikveh, they do not disqualify the mikveh until they are half and half. But if the kosher [waters] were the majority, the mikveh is kosher. How so? A mikveh that has 20 se’ah and something of kosher water, and one was filling and drawing outside the mikveh, and the waters are drawn and descend into the mikveh — whether they were drawn over the ground or within a gutter or the like, from things that do not disqualify the mikveh — it is kosher, even if he completed it to 1,000 se’ah, for the drawn [waters] that were drawn [into it] are kosher if there was there a majority of 40 se’ah from the kosher [waters]. And likewise, a roof on whose top there were 20 se’ah and something of rainwater, and he filled with water carried on the shoulder and placed into it less than 20 [se’ah], so that all is found invalid, and he opened the pipe and all were drawn to one place — this is a kosher mikveh, for the drawn [waters] that were drawn [into it] are all kosher, since there is there a majority from the kosher [waters]. {Rama: But if one first drew invalid waters, and afterward brought upon them a majority of kosher waters, it does not help (Beit Yosef inferred from the words of Rambam).}
