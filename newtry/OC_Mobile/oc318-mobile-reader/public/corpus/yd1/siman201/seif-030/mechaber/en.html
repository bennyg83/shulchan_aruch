Drawing disqualifies only with water. But snow, hail, frost, salt, and mud that is somewhat thick — even if it has softness such that one can pour it from vessel to vessel — drawing does not disqualify them; for if one drew from these into a mikveh that is lacking, they do not disqualify it. Moreover, even if he made the entire mikveh from snow or frost or hail, which he brought in a vessel, and made from them a mikveh, it is kosher. {Rama: And when estimating the measure of the mikveh with snow, he should first crush its hollow space (Beit Yosef in the name of Ra’avad and Rosh, and so wrote Rambam), and then it is permitted to immerse in it as it is (Mordechai, end of perek Bameh Tomnin, in the name of R’ Shemaryah). And there are those who are stringent to immerse in all these only after they melted and became water (there in Mordechai in the name of R’ Simchah and R’ Aharon of Bohemia), and it is good to be stringent l’chatchila (Beit Yosef; and see Orach Chayyim siman 160).}
