One who comes to draw waters to a mikveh must ensure that it is not by means of something that receives tumah. For example, rainwater that he wants to draw to another place to make a mikveh: he should not hold a board in his hand and pass them over it; rather, he should place the board on the ground and remove his hand from there before the waters pass over it. And it is forbidden to draw waters to a mikveh with a metal gutter, for it receives tumah, and this is when the waters fall directly from something that receives tumah into the mikveh. But if they fall on its outer lip and are drawn into it; or if he attaches to the mouth of the gutter a small pipe of lead a small pipe of wood or of earthenware, and the waters pour from it into the mikveh, it is kosher. And if the metal gutter is connected to the ground, even if it pours directly into the mikveh it is kosher, for it does not receive tumah since it is nullified to the ground. (And there is no difference whether it is buried under the ground or not) (Beit Yosef wrote that so it appears from a responsum of Rosh and a responsum of Ramban and Rashba, and Mordechai in the name of Ra’em, and Rashi in the name of his teacher.)
