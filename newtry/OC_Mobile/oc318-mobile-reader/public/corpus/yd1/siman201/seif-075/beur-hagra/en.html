Some are lenient, etc. See Rash, chapter 10, mishnah 4, where a Tosefta teaches that if one rubbed or immersed in hot water he is pure, as stated above in siman 195, seif 45. See Tosafot, Niddah 66b, s.v. "if." According to Rabbeinu Chananel's explanation, however, there is no proof, for he explains it differently. The first opinion was forced to interpret the Tosefta as referring to the hot springs of Tiberias, which is strained, especially since the passage begins, "if his feet were in a mikveh," and so forth. Their proof is from Taanit 13a; see Tosafot there, s.v. "immersion." It follows that the reason is because the waters are drawn. The Gemara there likewise says, "they are drawn waters," except that this explanation is very forced. Although immersion in hot water can technically be found in such a manner, it is remote to say that the Tanna who states, "all who require immersion immerse," came to teach specifically such a case. In contrast, the case described by Tosafot is very simple.
And in the hot springs of Tiberias, etc. The Gemara there and Chullin 106a.
And afterward, etc.; but, etc. From Shabbat 14a, concerning one who immerses his head and most of his body, and the explanation there that originally they said, "not these purify but," and so forth. That does not apply to sweating. Others have already disagreed, since the decree there applies only to terumah, rendering him impure for terumah by rabbinic law, while his immersion remains effective even for terumah. If several days passed he does not require a new sunset. The same applies all the more so for her husband, whose status is ordinary food, as explained in Chullin 31a regarding intent, which is among the higher standards the Sages imposed for terumah, as explained in Chagigah chapter 2.
