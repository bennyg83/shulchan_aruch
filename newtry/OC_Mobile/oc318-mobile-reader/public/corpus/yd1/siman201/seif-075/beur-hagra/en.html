Some are lenient, etc. See Rash, Mikvaos 10:4, who cites the Tosefta: if one rubbed or immersed in hot water, he is pure. This is as stated above, siman 195, seif 45. See Tosafos, Niddah 66b, s.v. Im. According to Rabbeinu Chananel's explanation, however, there is no proof. The first opinion was forced to explain the Tosefta as referring to the hot springs of Tiberias, which is strained and does not fit its opening words concerning feet in a mikveh. Their proof is from Taanis 13a. See Tosafos there, s.v. Tevilah, which shows that the concern is drawn water, as the Gemara itself asks, "Are they not drawn water?" The alternative explanation is extremely strained. Although immersion in hot water can occur in such a way, it is remote to say that the Tanna's statement, "All who require immersion immerse," comes to teach such an unusual case. Tosafos' case is much simpler.
And in the hot springs of Tiberias, etc. The Gemara there and Chullin 106a.
Afterward, etc.; but, etc. From Shabbos 14a, concerning one who places his head, etc. Initially they said, "These do not purify, but," etc. That does not apply to sweating. Others have already disagreed, explaining that the decree there concerns terumah only, rendering him rabbinically impure for terumah. His immersion nevertheless counts even for terumah, so that after several days he does not require another sunset. Certainly it counts for relations with her husband, since ordinary food is treated less stringently, as explained in Chullin 31a concerning intent, which is one of the additional standards the Sages imposed for terumah, as stated in Chagigah chapter 2.
