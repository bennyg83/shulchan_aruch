Shach writes that even if the stream was not interrupted, it has the law of a mikveh, because passing it over the backs of vessels is considered as though the spring flow had been interrupted. This applies specifically where the entire spring flows over the vessels. If part flows along the edges of the vessels, it retains the law of a spring, all the more so than the case above in seif 8 where it flows into the vessels themselves. Taz agrees. Tosafot Yom Tov, chapter 5, Mishnah 2, writes that the case must also be one where, even without these vessels, the water would have passed there. Otherwise the water would be invalid even as a mikveh because its formation must occur through something not susceptible to impurity. Hagahot Perishah writes that since the source is a spring it is permitted in every case. However, below in seif 49 the Mechaber cites an opinion that does not distinguish in this way.
