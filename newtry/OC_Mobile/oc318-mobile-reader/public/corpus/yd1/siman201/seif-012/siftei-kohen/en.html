It returned to be,. etc. ; it is written in Tosafot Yom Tov, chapter 5, mishnah 2: it appears to me that here too it deals when without these vessels the water would pass; for if not so, say thus: they would be invalidated even for a mikveh because their formation requires taharah, as Rosh wrote (below, seif 35) regarding setting up handles ; end of his words; and I did not know from Rosh's words proof that Rosh said so only for rainwater, but not for a spring that was drawn, as below, seif 49; and so too in Hagahat Perishah, seif 40, that here in every case it is permitted since it is a spring; however below, seif 49, the Mechaber brought: there is one who does not distinguish in this.
Provided he does not immerse,. etc. ; for we decree on account of inside the vessel ; Rosh. And above, siman 198, Tur and Beit Yosef brought in the name of Ra'avad and other poskim that specifically with vessels that receive tumah does this decree apply, unlike earthenware and the like that do not receive tumah; and Beit Yosef challenged there Tur for writing that Rosh, may he rest in peace, did not distinguish in this. And it appears to me that Tur holds according to Rosh that Rabbi Yosi decreed only l'chatchila but not b'dieved, and even for earthenware he decreed l'chatchila lest one come to immerse inside the vessel; for if Rosh held to distinguish thus he should have written that Rabbi Yosi's reason is since it is a vessel that receives tumah he decreed on account of its inside; and from the fact that the plain statement of Rabbi Yosi is "we decree on account of inside the vessel," apparently he decreed for all vessels; nevertheless this is specifically l'chatchila, but b'dieved it is permitted, for we did not forbid in the Gemara (and above, siman 198, 131) even in a valley except because of modesty ; apparently b'dieved is permitted. Also in Rava's statement Rosh wrote: a woman should not stand on a vessel or a wooden depression and immerse because of modesty, and omitted earthenware; and if Rosh held to distinguish he should not have omitted it, from which we learn as Ra'avad wrote that specifically for earthenware there is no decree of bathhouses; rather certainly he does not distinguish, and Rabbi Yosi deals with l'chatchila ; and therefore Tur wrote here plainly "provided he does not immerse on the backs of vessels," implying even earthenware, and this is l'chatchila. And accordingly it would appear to be lenient b'dieved for all vessels; but since the view of Ra'avad, Rashba, Ran, and Beit Yosef above, siman 198, is to forbid even b'dieved on other backs of vessels, and so explained above, siman 198, seif 31, one should not be lenient; but for earthenware one may be lenient b'dieved, for both according to the one view and the other view it is permitted, as stated.
