As a man whom his mother comforts. “So shall I comfort you, and in Jerusalem you shall be comforted,” followed by the blessing.
Who comforts Zion through the rebuilding of Jerusalem. One should not say “Who comforts mourners and rebuilds Jerusalem,” because a blessing may not conclude with two separate themes. At first glance the same concern should apply to “Who comforts Zion and rebuilds Jerusalem,” since Berakhot 49a treats “Who saves Israel and rebuilds Jerusalem” as two conclusions. Yet all prayer books conclude the Tisha BAv blessing, “Who comforts Zion and rebuilds Jerusalem.” Evidently comforting Zion and rebuilding Jerusalem constitute one theme. [Citation 260.]
