His wife. But she, whose husband died, mourns only thirty days; she is merely forbidden to become betrothed or marry until after three months because of distinction of pregnancy, as written in Even HaEzer siman 13; end quote, Tur. The Mordechai wrote that the reason a woman need not wait three festivals is that a woman is content with anything, and she forgets the love of the first after thirty days. If it happened that she married within thirty days of mourning for other relatives, she may not have intercourse; this is the Rambam's view according to the Bach's explanation of his words, see there; end quote, Shach.
With other. Likewise, all other mourning practices apply only thirty days, as with other relatives. Shach.
Important. The Agudah ruled that they are considered like festivals even for this matter (and in Nekudat HaKesef he wrote that all the poskim disagree with him, and so is the view of all the later authorities, and their reasoning is sound; end quote). Nevertheless, it appears that according to the custom Rama wrote, that many are lenient and a person of spirit should be concerned for himself, if three festivals passed together with Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur, he may rely on the author of the Agudah. The reason for this is not because of added mourning, but so that the first wife be forgotten from his mind when he is with the second, and through the joy of the festivals he forgets the first. Therefore there is one who wants to distinguish: if he had a wife who was sick for a long time, or they had enmity and he wanted to divorce her and she died, then certainly the love of the first was immediately forgotten from his heart; unlike if she was always with him in love and became sick in the ordinary way and died, where the love of the first is fixed in his heart and is not forgotten until after three festivals. But it does not appear right to make such a distinction on our own, when it is not mentioned by the great poskim, and the later authorities also do not mention it, except that in Teshuvot Maimoniyot he analyzes and discusses this at length; see there in Hilchot Semachot. One should not move from the words of Rama. See Responsa Chavat Yair, question 4, regarding an incident that occurred there, where he was stringent; and see Responsa Ra'anach II, question 4.
To betroth. The Bach wrote that this follows the view of the Mechaber, who permits in seif 1 to betroth on the day of death; but we do not hold so. Rather, after seven days he is immediately permitted to betroth and marry her.
