And the same applies to other mourning. See Chatam Sofer responsa siman 348, who concludes that one who was permitted to marry within thirty days according to what is explained in the Shulchan Aruch is plainly permitted to launder and wear laundered garments. According to most poskim, there is no legal prohibition in this, and although we are accustomed to be stringent like the Riva, as explained in Shulchan Aruch siman 389 and the Shach there s.k. 2, here one should not be stringent, for that would lead to leniency during his wedding days. All the more so regarding the prohibition of washing, which according to everyone is only a stringency. Regarding haircutting too, it is obvious that on the wedding day itself he may cut his hair and take what is needed. But on the other days, if he did not shave on the wedding day, he may not shave on the other days. If he did shave that day, then one may permit him on the remaining days of the wedding celebration to shave as well. This is the wording at the end of the responsum there, but from his discussion there it is clear that he meant only trimming the beard as practiced in his country, which causes significant distress. This applies if both are not mourners; but if she too is a mourner, only laundering and washing were permitted, not haircutting. He further wrote that in any case the marriage does not cancel the decree of thirty from him. Rather, after the wedding days he returns to complete the remaining days of his mourning, and the wedding days count. It is not comparable to one who buried his dead before a festival, where the festival cancels it entirely, for there the reason is stated in aggadah that the festival cancels the Heavenly judgment from the soul. That applies to a public festival, but here, on his personal Yom Tov, the judgment is not canceled from the soul. Although the mitzvah of joy overrides mourning, once the days of joy have passed he returns to complete it; see there.
