Three festivals. Not because of additional mourning, but so that the first wife is forgotten from his mind while he is with the second — through the joy of the festivals he forgets the first.
And Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur are not like the festivals. The Agudah ruled that they are considered like festivals even for this matter.
It is written in the Tur: a woman whose husband died does not mourn for more than thirty days. Thus far his words. Regarding when she may remarry — it is explained in Even HaEzer siman 13. The women practice mourning a full year for their husbands; I do not know their support or proof for this. Perhaps they do it for their children, who mourn for twelve months. The reason a woman need not wait three festivals before remarrying, as a man must — is that a woman is content with any husband. So wrote the Mordechai.
He is permitted to betroth immediately and take her in after seven. He follows his own reasoning, having ruled in seif 1 like the Rambam that in all cases betrothal is permitted immediately. But according to what the Rama ruled there — that even betrothal is forbidden — here too betrothal would be forbidden until after seven days. It is surprising that the Rama wrote nothing here at all.
