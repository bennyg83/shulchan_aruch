Three festivals. See Dagul MeRevavah, who wrote that Shemini Atzeret is considered a festival in its own right for this matter as well; see there. See Chatam Sofer responsa siman 350, where his father-in-law, the Gaon Rabbi Akiva Eiger z"l, wrote to him that this does not appear so from the Rosh, chapter Elu Megalchin siman 95; see there. See there further regarding one whose wife became ill on Erev Pesach and died on the third day of chol ha-moed Pesach, and he wants to marry on the twentieth of Adar II, after more than twelve months have passed but not three festivals. The view of his father-in-law, the Gaon mentioned above, was to permit, and he z"l discussed the matter at length and agreed, because there are many grounds for leniency; see there. In the preceding responsum there, he concluded to permit a great rabbi whose wife died to marry after two festivals, since although his daughter and daughter-in-law were in his home, they were not accustomed to managing his home according to the honor of a rabbi and Torah scholar like him, and according to the honor of the distinguished visitors who came early to his door. But the rabbanit proposed to him had managed the home of her first husband in a large community. Together with the fact that the widow in question was the daughter of a great Torah scholar and the widow of a great Torah scholar, and waves had passed over her head, etc., and it is a great mitzvah to gladden the widow's heart and free her soul from confinement, it is plainly permitted to marry her after two festivals; see there.
