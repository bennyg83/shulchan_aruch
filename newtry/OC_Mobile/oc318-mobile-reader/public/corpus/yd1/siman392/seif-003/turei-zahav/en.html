One who has not fulfilled, etc. In the Tur it is stated in this language: there was an incident with a man who was betrothed, and the brother of the betrothed woman died close to the wedding, after the guests had already been invited. R' Tam permitted the wedding to take place within thirty days of mourning, because of his obligation to procreate — since he was betrothed to this woman and if he does not marry her, he will not marry another. However, if he was not betrothed, even though he desires this woman more than another, it is forbidden. Similarly my master father the Rosh ruled in practice regarding a man who was betrothed and had prepared all the wedding needs, and the sister of the betrothed woman died, and he had not fulfilled the mitzvah of procreation — he permitted him to marry and be with her after seven days, since there would be a financial loss if he does not marry. But without a loss, he said he would not have permitted. Thus far his words. This permissive ruling is novel — for even though she is a mourner and is not obligated in the mitzvah of procreation, nevertheless he permitted her on his account, since he would fulfill the mitzvah of procreation. This is not found explicitly in the Gemara as a permissive ruling; rather, the incident that occurred was close to the wedding, and the Rosh did not rely solely on R' Tam's leniency, but rather because of the loss of the meal, which he combined with this. Therefore, in the incident involving R' Tam, no mention was made of preparing wedding needs, as was mentioned in the Rosh's ruling. This resolves what my father-in-law z"l asked — why the Tur needed to repeat the substance of the incident in the Rosh's ruling, rather than writing briefly "and so too my master father z"l ruled in practice because of the loss" — for there is a difference in language in the Rosh's ruling. In Even HaEzer siman 14, the Tur wrote simply: one who betrothed a woman and a mourning occurred, etc. — and made no mention there of the loss of the meal. This is because as a matter of law it is so, but in practice the leniency is only because of the loss of the meal, as per the Rosh. So too the Shulchan Aruch ruled here. What is stated here "he is permitted to take her in and be with her after seven" — this "taking her in" is also specifically after seven, for one cannot say that because of the loss it is permitted to take her in even within seven, since the Rosh wrote in the chapter Eilu Megalchin in the name of R' Tam that even where there is financial loss, they did not permit marrying within seven, even for one who has no children. They only permitted within seven for the father of the groom and the mother of the bride specifically. Certainly since she is a mourner and not obligated in procreation — it is surely as I wrote. The Perishah elaborated on this but did not examine the words of R' Tam that the Rosh wrote in his name, as I have mentioned.
He is permitted to take her in because she has compassion. Meaning: only entry under the chuppah was permitted, but not to be with her until after thirty days — in this case, since there is no mitzvah of procreation here.
