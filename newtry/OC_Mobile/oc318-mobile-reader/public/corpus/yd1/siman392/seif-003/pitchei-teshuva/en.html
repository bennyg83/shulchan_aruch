They are lenient. See Chinnukh Beit Yehudah responsa siman 90, who wrote that the Rama's intent is that people rely on the leniency of the Ritzba mentioned above when he has no daughter, etc. About this he wrote that a baal nefesh should be concerned for himself not to rely on that leniency, for all the early authorities explained the phrase "he has no one to serve him" plainly, meaning that he cannot hire servants. The Rama also wrote carefully, "and they marry within three festivals," meaning that although they are lenient like the Ritzba, they are nevertheless stringent to wait two festivals like Rabbi Yehudah. The Ritzba himself joined grounds for leniency specifically after two festivals. One who explains the Rama's words to mean that the custom is to be lenient even without the grounds newly proposed by the Ritzba is revealing a face in the Torah not according to halachah. Heaven forbid that the Rama z"l would uproot an explicit Talmudic law and write that a baal nefesh should be concerned for himself as though it were merely pious conduct; see there at length. See Shevut Yaakov II siman 100, who was asked whether the Rama's intent is specifically where the above grounds for permission exist, or in every case. He wrote that although Chinnukh Beit Yehudah responsa was very stringent in this, we see that the world is accustomed to be lenient in every case. He copied there the grounds for permission written by Terumat HaDeshen, and further wrote that one can suggest another reason for permission: every day licentiousness grows, and sinners have multiplied; therefore, to guard him from sin, it is better not to delay. This too is the Rama's intent. "A baal nefesh should be concerned for himself" means if he is able to restrain himself from sin. He further wrote that since the Rama wrote "should be concerned for himself," it implies that he himself should be concerned to be stringent; but if he already prepared himself for kiddushin and marriage, the decisor should not prevent arranging the kiddushin, unlike what one decisor thought. He further wrote that we count from the day of death, and one should not be stringent at all in a distant report; see there.
