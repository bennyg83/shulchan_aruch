Wine used for libation, etc. There is no object here whose prohibition was created by a vow.
Some permit. This is the opinion of Rosh cited by Tur here at the end of the siman. Beit Yosef cites another responsum of Rosh: "One who said, 'My wine shall be forbidden to all Israel if I perform a particular act,' and then performed it, has not rendered the wine forbidden. He cannot prohibit it unless he uses the term konam or links it to an object prohibited by a vow." This is surprising, for Tur wrote at the beginning of siman 204 that language of prohibition also constitutes a vow. It is likewise explicit in siman 224 that a person may prohibit something to another person using language of prohibition. Perhaps Rosh holds that language of prohibition is effective only with respect to oneself, but not with respect to others. This is also implied by the verse, "To bind a prohibition upon his soul." It appears that this responsum is not authoritative, and Tur therefore did not cite it.
