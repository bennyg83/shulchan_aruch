Yayin nesech. — meaning he says explicitly "like idolatry wine," for if not one may say he intended yayin nesech in a sacred sense, as below beginning of siman 208 — Perishah. And it appears to me: because below, siman 208 s.k. 4, it will be explained that if the people of the place call idolatry wine simply "yayin nesech" we go to be lenient; therefore the Rosh and Tur wrote plainly "yayin nesech," since the standard yayin nesech called thus now is idolatry wine; so implies in Hagahot Perishah below, beginning of siman 208.
He is not forbidden thereby. Since he did not attach to something vowed. Beit Chadash wrote, and one may challenge the wording of the Rosh who wrote in general principle 12 law [4] — one who said "my wine shall be forbidden upon all of Israel if I do such-and-such," and he did it, is not forbidden, as he wrote from one who says "this loaf is forbidden upon me," which is also a vow even though he did not attach to something vowed but said simply "forbidden." And here too he only said simply "my wine shall be forbidden upon all of Israel" — one may say it is different there, since he said "this loaf upon me" it is language of a vow; and here too he is not forbidden unless he said in language of konam "my wine upon all of Israel if I do such-and-such." But "my wine shall be forbidden upon all of Israel" — this is not language of a vow. However, from the Tur's wording it appears the Rosh only permitted because he mentioned yayin nesech, and it requires investigation — up to here his wording — meaning the Tur wrote thus: I saw in teshuvah of the Rosh z"l — one who says "my wine, yayin nesech, if I do such-and-such," and he did it, is not forbidden since he did not attach to something vowed, up to here his wording — it appears that for this reason he is not forbidden, since he said yayin nesech which is a forbidden matter; but if he said "my wine shall be forbidden" even though he did not mention "upon me," he is forbidden. And it seems to me that even though if he said "this loaf is forbidden upon me" it is also a vow — that is because it is written "to prohibit a prohibition upon his soul" — but to forbid upon all of Israel he cannot forbid unless with konam; and therefore the Tur wrote specifically because he mentioned yayin nesech he is permitted, for if not he would be forbidden regarding himself. And this law is in teshuvah of the Rosh there, law 5. It appears to me that in teshuvah of the Rosh law 4 it deals where his intention was in what he said "my wine shall be forbidden" and it shall be yayin nesech — and this appears to be the main point.
