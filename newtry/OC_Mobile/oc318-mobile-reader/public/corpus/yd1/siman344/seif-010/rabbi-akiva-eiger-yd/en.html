(Siman 344, seif 10 in the gloss) But if he commanded that mourning not be observed. The source of this ruling is in Mahariv (siman 7), copied in Darkei Moshe, who wrote: but within thirty days — since mourning is also observed for other deceased and it is supported by a verse — it has not been clarified to me whether mourning is for the honor of the living or for the honor of the dead. For certainly mourning itself cannot be waived — for if so you would abolish all laws of mourning. Clearly his primary reasoning is not from the conclusion that "you would abolish all laws of mourning" — for what would be wrong if it is abolished in a case where the deceased commanded not to mourn, just as with twelve months of mourning where Mahariv concedes one follows the command? Rather his primary reasoning is from his doubt whether mourning is for the honor of the living or for the honor of the dead. Given this, I am surprised — for this is a dispute among the authorities: regarding one who takes his own life, in the baraita of Evel Rabbati (beginning of ch. 2) it states that whatever is the honor of the living, one tends to it; whatever is not the honor of the living, one does not tend to it. Rambam's view (ch. 1 of Laws of Mourning) is that one does not mourn him. See Kesef Mishneh there, who holds that Rambam considers mourning as honor of the dead. [It implies from Rambam that even for mourning on the first day — which is biblical according to Rambam — the verse's reason is for honor of the dead; requires further study.] But Ramban in Torat HaAdam holds one does mourn him, for it is honor of the living. So it is a dispute among the major authorities. Since we rule definitively (at the beginning of siman 345) like Rambam — that one who takes his own life deliberately is not mourned — it follows that we hold definitively that mourning is the honor of the dead. Or we rule from doubt to be lenient regarding mourning. In any case, we should rule that a command not to mourn takes effect — and Rama should not have cited Mahariv's words as law. I saw in Responsa Ranach (siman 50) who wrote on another matter definitively that mourning for seven and thirty days is honor of the living — without noticing that it is a dispute between Rambam and Ramban; requires further study as to the law. See Responsa Shevut Yaakov (part 2, siman 102).
