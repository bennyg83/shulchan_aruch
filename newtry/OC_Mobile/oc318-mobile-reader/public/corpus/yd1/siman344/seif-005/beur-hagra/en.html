Within four amot. Beyond four amot it is in any event permitted, as stated in Yerushalmi Berachot chapter 3 and Nazir chapter 7: R. Yonah and R. Yose the Galilean said in the name of R. Yose ben Chanina that one may not discuss halachot before the bier of the deceased. Yet R. Yochanan asked R. Yannai before the bier of R. Shimon ben Yehotzadak whether one who consecrated his burnt offering to Temple maintenance, etc., and he answered him. The response must have been when he was distant, or while they were taking the bier to the row. Likewise, R. Yirmiyah asked R. Azaryah before the bier of R. Shimon son of R. Yitzchak; when distant he answered, but when near he did not. Torat HaAdam cites this. Rosh's text at the end of Berachot 3:6, 'and it speaks beyond four amot,' must read 'within four amot,' unlike Rabbenu Yerucham. See Tur and Beit Yosef. Four amot is the measure stated at the beginning of chapter 3 there: within four amot, etc., for the master said, etc. End of supplement. [Citation references: 5, 52, 74.]
