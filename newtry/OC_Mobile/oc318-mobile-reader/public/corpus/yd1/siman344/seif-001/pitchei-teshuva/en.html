We heed his instruction. See Baer Heitev of Rabbi Maharit, citing Responsa Beit Yaakov, siman 83, that when the leading sage of the generation dies after directing that he not be eulogized, one who is lenient and disregards this instruction has not acted improperly. Responsa Teshuvah MeAhavah, part 1, siman 174, discusses this at length. He cites the Noda BiYehudah, who said while eulogizing the Pnei Yehoshua that although the deceased had directed that he not be eulogized, one does not heed such a command from a master of the stature of the Pnei Yehoshua. After extensive discussion, however, he concludes that he has no clear permission to disregard the deceased’s instruction and eulogize one who said, “Do not eulogize me.” See also siman 207. Beit Yaakov further writes that if two Torah scholars in one city hated one another and one died, the eulogy may not be diminished out of concern for the honor of the surviving scholar. Responsa Even Shoham, siman 72, rules that if one directed that his son not recite Kaddish for him, the instruction is obeyed. The source index also preserves the number 4 and the citation 50.
