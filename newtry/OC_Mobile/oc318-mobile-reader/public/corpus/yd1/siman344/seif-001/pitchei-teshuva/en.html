We heed his instruction. See Baer Heitev of Rabbi Maharit, who writes in the name of Responsa Beit Yaakov, siman 83, that if the leading sage of the generation died after instructing that he not be eulogized, one who is lenient and does not obey this scholar's instruction has not lost, meaning there is basis to eulogize him. See Responsa Teshuvah MeAhavah I, siman 174, who discusses this at length and cites the Noda BiYehudah as having said, when eulogizing the Pnei Yehoshua, that although he had directed that he not be eulogized, one does not heed such an instruction from a master of such stature. After extensive discussion, however, he concludes that he has no clear license to disregard the deceased's words and eulogize him where he expressly said, "Do not eulogize me." See also there, siman 207. Beit Yaakov further writes that where two Torah scholars in one city hated one another and one of them died, it is forbidden to diminish the eulogy out of concern for the honor of the other scholar. See also Responsa Even Shoham, siman 72, that one who instructed that his son not recite Kaddish for him is obeyed.
