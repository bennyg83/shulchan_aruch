But if he instructed. See Iyun Yaakov, by the author of Shevut Yaakov, Sanhedrin 47, who disagrees with Mahari Weil and Rama. In his view the same applies to the seven days of mourning and to keriah: if the deceased directed that they not be observed, his instruction is heeded, since these practices too are for the honor of the dead. This is why there is no mourning for one who deliberately took his own life. He writes that he ruled this way in practice, contrary to Rama. The same position is repeated in Responsa Shevut Yaakov II, siman 102. See, however, Responsa Teshuvah MeAhavah I, end of siman 207, who challenges him and concludes that one should not rely upon this view to be lenient.
