(Likkut) Some say, etc. That is, regarding metal vessels. This is the view of Rabbeinu Tam, that we do not say that vessels touching a corpse become like the corpse itself except specifically with metal vessels, as Tosafot wrote in Nazir 54b, s.v. u-meshani, and the Rash in the first chapter of Ohalot. This is also the view of Rashi in Pesachim 14b and elsewhere, and of the Raavad in chapter 5 of Hilkhot Tum'at Met. But the Ri Migash, the Ri, the Rambam, the Ramban, and the rest of the poskim disagree. Their view is that the same applies to all vessels and garments except earthenware vessels, which never become an av ha-tumah. The proof is from the Mishnah at the beginning of Ohalot, which speaks generally of four, etc., and in all of them says "vessels" without qualification. Moreover, that vessels touching a person become like him is not learned from "a sword slain," but from "you shall wash your garments," etc., as stated in the Sifrei; only, in other cases of garment tumah this applies specifically while touching the person, whereas here even after separation. It must therefore apply to all vessels, and the same applies to the opening clause. At the beginning of Chullin, the first chapter of Pesachim 14 and 19b, and certainly Shabbat 101b, see Tosafot there, and in the eighth chapter of Pesachim, we do not read "a sword is like one slain," because it is not learned from "a sword slain," and it is not needed there. See Mishneh LaMelekh, chapter 5 of Hilkhot Tum'at Met. So too the Rash wrote at the beginning of Ohalot. The Sifrei likewise says: we have learned vessels and a person; from where do we know vessels through a person, etc.? This shows that it applies to all vessels, as above. The Ri further brought proof from the Tosefta in Kelim chapter 6 regarding a skin-bottle, etc.; see Tosafot and the Rash there. Although Rabbeinu Tam forced the text, the plain meaning of the Mishnah and baraita accords with the Rambam. As for the law here, Rabbeinu Tam's view in Tosafot in Nazir above is that a nazir shaves for a sword that touched a corpse, and that is why the Gemara there did not establish the case as metal vessels, as Tosafot wrote there. If so, a kohen is likewise warned against it, as stated in the Tosefta, Makkot chapter 3, and Semachot chapter 4: any tumah for which a nazir shaves, a kohen is warned against, etc. This is also proof for his view that it applies specifically to metal, from the Gemara's question, "and these," etc. See Tosafot there, who leave the Ri Migash's view requiring study. The Ramban in Bava Batra 20a, however, answered that sprinkling is needed only for one who touches or overshadows the corpse itself, for that is the implication of the verses; and so our master wrote in his Likkutim. According to Rabbeinu Tam, it would also be forbidden to enter a tent in which such a vessel is present, for his view there is that it also transmits tumah in a tent, as written in Tosafot there, and in Bava Batra chapter 2, 20a, s.v. be-chavit, in Chullin 104a, s.v. challat, and in Shabbat chapter 1, 17a, and elsewhere. This is not the Rambam's view; see Evel Rabbati at the beginning of Kelim. But the other poskim disagree with him: the nazir does not shave and the kohen is not warned for it. This is the implication of the Tosefta's language, though it remains at least prohibited; see the Beit Yosef and Sefer HaEshkol. As stated in the Tosefta of Ohalot, brought by Tosafot there and the Rash in the first chapter of Ohalot: anything that conveys seven-day tumah from a corpse, etc.; vessels through vessels, etc. When is this said? Regarding terumah and kodashim. But a nazir shaves only for the corpse itself, and one is liable for tumah of the Temple and its sacred things only for the corpse itself. So too at the end of chapter 7 of Nazir, Rabbi Yehoshua stated the general rule: all, etc. The Rambam wrote likewise in chapter 7 of Hilkhot Nezirut. But regarding tumah of the Temple and its sacred things, in chapter 5 of Hilkhot Tum'at Met he ruled that one who touches a corpse and one who touches him are liable, but from that point onward they are exempt; this follows Rabbi Yochanan in the Yerushalmi, Nazir chapter 7.
