Two who made, etc. Yerushalmi chapter 3, brought by the Rash in chapter 4 mishnah 1, the Rosh siman 11. The Raavad wondered at this, and the Rash there also wrote, “however this is astonishing,” etc., and explained that they had separated challah beforehand, meaning that they did not initially intend to divide. But the Rosh, Rambam, and Tur wrote according to its plain sense. See siman 324 seif 2 in the gloss. There too his words are vague: “with intent to divide them and he divided them,” etc., which is the Tur's language; but it is specifically with intent to divide among many people, as above, and as stated in the Yerushalmi above: “combination for challah,” etc., when they are, etc.; “one who brings in loaves,” etc. This is as above, and so are the words of the Tur and the Rav here in the gloss, “and the same applies,” etc.
For they already had, etc. Meaning that this is not comparable to what is written in seif 1, two doughs, etc., nor to seif 5, “therefore,” etc.
But if, etc. Tur, as stated regarding a baker: although he made it to divide and did divide them, since his intent was that if he would not find, etc. The Tur's words are clearer, for he wrote: but if he did not make them, etc., he is obligated even if he did not add to each one, since it had become obligated, etc.
