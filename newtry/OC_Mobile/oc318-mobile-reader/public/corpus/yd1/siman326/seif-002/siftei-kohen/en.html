Even if he intends to divide it into small portions, each below the measure requiring challah, the dough is obligated, because if it is not sold in portions he will bake it as bread and it will then require challah. The Bach rejects the practice in which a baker sells portions of one large dough and each purchaser separates challah with a blessing. Since the baker's original dough already became obligated, he must separate once for the entire dough, and multiplying blessings is unnecessary. He records that his teachers regarded the contrary practice as a full prohibition. Masat Binyamin, responsum 1, defends the custom of women buying a measure of dough from a Jewish baker and each separating challah, in order to preserve the special practice of women taking challah before Shabbat, but his reasoning is not compelling. He agrees that at weddings, when one large loaf is prepared from a single kneading, only one challah may be separated, and the women should be prevented from each separating separately. This is plainly correct, and I saw my learned mother and other righteous women take care in this matter.
But an ordinary person who makes dough in order to divide it while still dough. If he intends to distribute it among many people so that no portion contains the required measure, it is exempt. If he does not intend to divide it while still dough, then even if he intends to divide it after baking, it is obligated.
