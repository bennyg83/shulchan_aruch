To divide it. See Baer Heitev. The Gaon of Lissa writes in his siddur that although the Taz and Shach imply here that one challah separation exempts all dough kneaded together even after it is divided into separate doughs, nevertheless if one explicitly intends that the separation exempt only one dough and not the other, it does not exempt the second dough. Challah, like terumah, is separated by intent, and where one intends not to exempt something, it remains obligated. The reason one may not divide a large dough in order to make several separations is therefore that doing so creates unnecessary blessings, since one could intend to exempt all of it with one blessing. See his Mekor Chaim, siman 457, Biurim, seif katan 2, and Responsa Beit Ephraim, Orach Chaim, siman 44.
If one intends to divide it while still dough, it is exempt. Levush, Derishah, and Bach write that this applies only where one intends to divide it among many people. Dividing it for oneself is not considered "making it in order to divide." They use this to justify the common practice of women on Friday to knead only one challah-measure of dough, separate challah with a blessing, and then divide it into small loaves, each below the challah measure, because the division is not among many people. Responsa Beit Ephraim, Yoreh Deah, siman 69, writes that Tosafot in Berachot 38 and other authorities explicitly reject this distinction. Tiferet LeMoshe had already raised the same difficulty. Maadanei Melech in Hilchot Challah offers a different distinction: only where one divides the dough so as not to bake all of it now, but for separate meals, such as morning and evening, is it exempt; where one divides it merely into separate pieces all to be baked immediately, it remains obligated. Tiferet LeMoshe rejects this distinction as well, since Tosafot do not imply it. Beit Ephraim instead justifies the custom by distinguishing a permanent division from a temporary one. Only if one intends to divide the dough in a manner that will never later be recombined is it exempt, regardless of whether there is one owner or many. But if one intends the pieces to be joined at baking, removing them and placing them into one basket, the division made while they were dough is not destined to endure and is not considered "made in order to divide."
