All this is where neither contains the required amount. Meaning that where neither dough contains the challah measure, they do not combine if the owner is particular that they remain distinct, even through contact or adhesion. But where each dough independently contains the challah measure, one may separate from one for the other through contact and adhesion. Although at the end of siman 325 we ruled that where both are before him one may separate from one for the other without adhesion or even basket combination, here is different because he is particular that they remain distinct. Terumat HaDeshen, siman 190, makes this clear from the case of one pure dough and one impure dough, where because the owner is particular they require a strip of dough joining them. Therefore, the statement elsewhere that where one is particular, even adhesion does not combine, concerns two sub-minimum doughs, because we cannot treat them as one dough to create the obligation. But where each already contains the measure, adhesion is sufficient to permit separation from one for the other. Rama here relied on the opening of the siman, which speaks of doughs that touched or adhered. Levush erred badly by saying that adhesion is needed only when each lacks the challah measure, while if each contains the measure no contact is needed provided both are before him. He confused this case with the prior siman and failed to notice that here the owner is particular. In this case, if neither has the measure, adhesion does not create one obligated dough; if each has the measure, adhesion does help for separation. There is no doubt he did not examine the source carefully.
