(Siman 326, in the Taz §1) He disputed the Atereth Zekenim. In my humble opinion the words of the Atereth Zekenim appear correct, true, and clear, and Terumat HaDeshen's view is likewise so, as I will explain. Since several novel rulings and true laws will be clarified through his words, I am compelled to extend somewhat. What the Taz wished to say — that when one is particular [about mixing, such as with] saffron and the like, "biting" is required for the purpose of [separation being deemed] from the surrounded (mokuaf) — is not precise. For even regarding a pure and impure dough, the Mishnah at the end of chapter 1 of Challah states: "he places less than a ke-beitzah in the middle, etc." — implying that touching suffices and biting is not required. So too it is proven explicitly in the Talmud in Sotah 30a that this Mishnah speaks of touching. So too it is explicit in Tosafot, chapter Elu Ovrin (Pesachim 46b), and in Sefer HaTerumah §62, and in the beginning of chapter 2 of Challah, Mishnah 4, and in Terumat HaDeshen §169 — that touching alone suffices for an impure and pure dough, and biting is not required. So too it is clearly implied in the Asher Ri (Rosh's rulings on Challah) and in other decisors. It is possible that the requirement of biting is where they are not in a basket, whereas the Mishnah and decisors that say touching suffices refers to when they are in a basket. But from the plain meaning of his words it is implied that the Mishnah about two doughs speaks of biting, and it is further implied from his plain words that touching does not suffice even in a basket — and this is incorrect. Furthermore it appears that here even touching, and even combination by basket, is not required — for if you do not say this, but rather that since one is particular [about mixing] the basket combination does not suffice for the purpose of surrounded [separation] when each dough has the minimum measure, then the Tur and Shulchan Aruch are difficult, as they wrote without qualification at the end of siman 325 that when each has the minimum measure, even basket combination is not needed, only that both are before him — and they did not distinguish between one who is particular or not, just as they immediately distinguished afterwards in siman 326 regarding combination. They should have written that this applies only when one is not particular, but when particular, even basket combination does not suffice. And all the more so would the Rema be difficult, who wrote here in siman 326 without qualification that when one of them has the minimum measure, one separates from one on behalf of the other — and did not clarify that this requires touching or biting specifically. And so all the later authorities copied his words without qualification, and not one thought to clarify this. And likewise difficult is Rabbenu Yeruham, Niv 21 §3, who ruled there in the name of R. Yitzchak bar Yehudah and Rivva that for surrounded separation, not even a basket is needed — and he does not distinguish between one who is particular and one who is not; then later for combination he does distinguish explicitly. It is likewise clearly implied that the Rambam's view is that he only distinguished regarding "particular" in the context of combination, not surrounded. Thus certainly all these authorities hold that for surrounded separation there is no distinction between one who is particular and one who is not. There is good reason for this: specifically for combination should one distinguish — since the primary obligation is that we regard them as if they were a single combined dough, and he is particular about combining them together. But for surrounded separation, where one separates from one on behalf of the other, they need not be combined together. This is also explicitly the view of R. Eliezer of Metz in Sefer Yere'im §163, cited by the Mordechai in chapter Elu Ovrin there. So too is the view of Tosafot in chapter 1 of Niddah 7a, and so in Sefer HaTerumah §81, and Rabbenu Shimshon in his commentary on Mishnayot in chapter 2 of Challah, Mishnah 4 — in their first resolution — and so wrote the Agudah in chapter Elu Ovrin in the name of some Geonim. Thus all these authorities hold that for surrounded separation there is no distinction between one who is particular and one who is not. And for combination, everyone agrees there is a distinction between one who is particular and one who is not, as in the Mishnah at the beginning of chapter 4 of Challah and in the Yerushalmi, Rashbam, and all decisors. Furthermore it appears to me that even the one who distinguishes even in surrounded separation regarding one who is particular — this applies only to [a case of] purity and impurity, where being particular is very strong, unlike other cases. For the need to distinguish was only in order to resolve the case of a pure and impure dough — and one may say it applies only there, but not generally. It appears to me clearly that the Rosh's view is likewise so, from his statement: "pure and impure are different, as stated in the Yerushalmi of this chapter — a matter about which one is particular regarding mixing: a basket does not combine [them]; a pure and impure dough they treated like a matter about which one is particular regarding mixing, etc." — there he is particular about mixing to an extreme degree, because the moment they touch, even if they do not become mixed, the pure dough becomes impure. Therefore specifically in impure and pure cases one may say touching is required, since without doubt they are not like ones that are touching. This too is the implication of the language of Sefer HaTerumah §82 in the second resolution, which wrote: "since he is particular about their touching due to impurity, basket combination does not help." So too the Yerushalmi of tractate Challah implies: "a matter about which one is particular regarding mixing" — and this is why the Rosh said "pure and impure are different, as stated in the Yerushalmi" — meaning, specifically since it is pure and impure [that] it is different. And therefore the Tur, Shulchan Aruch, Rabbenu Yeruham, and other later authorities follow the Rosh's approach, and yet they did not distinguish here — since the impurity and purity rule does not apply in our times. However, the Raf in his glosses on the Semak §245 wrote regarding surrounded: "provided he is not particular, etc." — and did not mention impure dough. It seems to me he means analogously to an impure dough — i.e., where one is very particular even about touching. And the decisors who bring proof from a pure and impure dough for the case of surrounded separation hold that even in the impure and pure case — where one is particular about their touching — no distinction applies regarding surrounded separation. It appears to me clearly that the view of Terumat HaDeshen §189 is likewise so, and this is the flow of his argument: Question — a woman kneaded two doughs, in one she added saffron and in the other she did not, and neither has the minimum measure for challah. May they be combined through biting in order to take challah from them, or not? Answer — it would appear at first glance from the words of the Geonim that they may be combined through biting, since the marginal glosses on Tosafot in chapter 1 of Niddah, the Semag, Sefer HaTerumah (second resolution), the Asher Ri in the laws of challah, and the Mordechai in chapter Elu Ovrin resolve the problem of why basket combination does not suffice for a pure and impure dough for surrounded separation — only touching is needed — because one is particular about mixing, etc. Now it follows from this resolution that joining by touching, and certainly by biting, is effective. End quote — meaning: granted that there [with impure and pure] it is worse, since it is impurity and purity and one is particular even about touching, as I explained — nonetheless it follows a fortiori that there touching or biting is effective. The Terumat HaDeshen initially thought that surrounded separation and combination are equal, and thus since touching suffices for surrounded separation even when one is particular, the same would apply for combination. Then he wrote: "However, from the rulings of the Rosh and from the Tur it is stated explicitly regarding combination that even biting does not help — even when one is [the coarser bread] and the other is fine bread. And it follows a fortiori that there [with impurity and purity], where one is particular even about touching, touching is effective — and certainly regarding one who is particular about coarse and fine bread and the like. Rather certainly one must say that specifically for combination does touching and biting not suffice, but for surrounded separation touching is more effective than combination." Therefore regarding a pure and impure dough, touching is effective even though one is particular — but for one who is particular in ordinary cases such as coarse and fine bread or saffron, for surrounded separation no touching is even needed. Thus the question was about combination, not surrounded — and touching does not help for combination. Afterwards Terumat HaDeshen cites the words of R. Eliezer of Metz and Rashbam in the first resolution, and Tosafot in chapter 1 of Niddah mentioned above, who do not distinguish even for pure and impure regarding surrounded separation between one who is particular and one who is not, and he deliberates there and concludes that for his question, biting does not help. This then is the flow of Terumat HaDeshen's argument there — he only came to discuss for his question (which was about combination) whether it helps; but for surrounded separation with one who is particular about saffron he did not write that biting is required. This accords with what I explained. In any case, whatever the conclusion, it is difficult regarding what Terumat HaDeshen wrote there at the end: "Now in our case, where he is certainly particular about mixing — since the saffron is not sufficient in appearance and taste for both doughs — and the majority of opinions hold there is a distinction, etc." Why did he write "the majority of opinions"? For in his own case all opinions agree — that for combination there is a distinction between one who is particular and one who is not, as the Mishnah and Yerushalmi at the beginning of chapter 4 of Challah state — and opinions do not disagree except regarding surrounded. But for combination, all agree. Thus the words of the Atereth Zekenim stand properly — and this is why the Rema wrote without qualification: "But if one of them has the minimum measure, he separates from one on behalf of the other, etc." — and all of this is clear. However what is difficult is the law of surrounded separation itself even when one is not particular, as ruled in the Shulchan Aruch at the end of siman 325 following the Tur and Rosh — that neither touching nor basket combination is needed. This is based on what the Beit Yosef rejected: the words of Sefer HaTerumah §62 in favor of the words of the Rosh and Rashba in his laws of challah and the Rambam. In my humble opinion this requires further study — for the Semag, Positive Commandment 141 (folio 212d) in the name of R. Yitzchak, holds that for surrounded separation a basket is needed without touching. So too wrote Tosafot in chapter Elu Ovrin in the name of R. Yitzchak. Even though they wrote there in his name that even touching is required — so too the view of Tosafot in chapter 1 of Niddah, and so the view of the Rokeach §329 — which contradicts what the Semag wrote in the name of R. Yitzchak (and one must say these are two different R. Yitzchaks) — nonetheless from all of them it is clear that at minimum a basket is required. So too wrote the Raf in the glosses on the Semak §245. And in the responsa of the Maimonides [school] regarding the laws of Zeraim it is written like Sefer HaTerumah. Thus in fact all these authorities disagree with the Rosh — and they are numerous. The proof the Rosh brought from jars of wine, and from five people at a threshing floor, is not a proof — for dough is different, as the Rashbam wrote in chapter 2 of Ma'aser Sheni. And what the Beit Yosef wrote, that the Rambam's view is similarly implied, I do not know any such implication — in fact one may say he holds like R. Yitzchak in Tosafot in chapter Elu Ovrin, that even touching is required, and he does not distinguish between pure and impure dough. And he holds that what the Mishnah states "a pure and impure dough" comes to teach us that even in [a case involving] impurity it is permitted in this way — alternatively, to teach that it is permitted to cause impurity to unconsecrated produce. Therefore the Rambam wrote at the end of chapter 7 of Bikkurim: "It is permitted to cause impurity to unconsecrated produce in the Land of Israel. Therefore, two doughs — one impure and one pure — he takes the amount of challah for both from the dough from which challah was not yet separated, places it in the middle adjacent to the pure dough, and draws from the impure toward the pure, in order to separate from the surrounded." End quote. Even though one may say that specifically in the case of the impure dough he requires touching — nonetheless, who can prove this? Furthermore, even if generally he does not require touching, perhaps he requires basket combination. And in truth the plain meaning of his words implies that for separated surrounded separation there is no distinction between pure and impure — and in all cases touching in the dough is required. And what the Rambam wrote in chapter 3 of Terumot, law 18, regarding two storage bins in one house or two barrels — he separates from one on behalf of the other — this too is no proof, as I wrote above, for dough is different. However, Rabbenu Yeruham wrote in the name of R. Yitzchak bar Yehudah and Rivva like the Rosh. And so too is written in the gloss on the responsa of the Maimonides [school] there in the name of Ritzhba. And so too the apparent view of the Ran at the end of chapter Elu Ovrin. And so too the view of the Bartenura in chapter 2 of Challah, Mishnah 4. Nonetheless it has been clarified from Tosafot in chapter Elu Ovrin in the name of R. Yitzchak that basket combination when there is not the minimum measure in one dough means they touch in the basket — so too wrote the Semag in the name of R. Yitzchak, and so too the Raf in the glosses on the Semak, and so it is written in Sefer HaTerumah §82, and so the Rashbam wrote in his commentary on the Mishnah in chapter 2 of Challah, Mishnah 4 — in the first resolution — and so wrote the Bartenura there, and so too the Rosh wrote in his laws of challah §4. Therefore what the Beit Yosef wrote about the Tur — from the words of the Semag in the name of R. Yitzchak it appears that basket combination does not help unless they are touching — is not precise. It appears from his language that according to the Tur, touching is not needed — but this is not so, for then the Tur would contradict the Rosh and all the decisors mentioned above. Rather, from what the Tur wrote: "if they are not stuck together (i.e., bitten) and they are in a basket, the basket combines them" — and he did not state a fortiori that even if they are not touching at all the basket combines them — this implies he means specifically: when they are not stuck together (i.e., bitten) but are touching each other, then the basket combines them. And perhaps this is also what the Beit Yosef means from the Semag's words in R. Yitzchak's name — it is stated more explicitly there than in the Tur's words — but it is strained. And furthermore, if so, he should not have written this only in the name of the Semag in the name of R. Yitzchak alone, since it is clear from the Rosh and all the decisors mentioned above. The conclusion of all this: two doughs, neither of which has the minimum measure, but together they have it, and one is not particular about them mixing — the manner in which basket combination helps requires that they touch each other in the basket. If each has the minimum measure and one wishes to separate from one on behalf of the other, neither touching nor a basket is needed — only that both are before him. Some dispute this and require a basket without touching. For surrounded separation, there is no distinction whether he is particular about the doughs mixing or not — except if he is very particular even about their touching, such as when one is pure and one is impure and the like. And specifically for dough — but for other things, not even a basket is needed for surrounded separation.
