They touched one another, and so forth. As explained in the preceding siman. See the Rash at the beginning of chapter 4.
Ordinarily, and so forth; if, and so forth. All these distinctions are in the Yerushalmi cited by the Rash.
If both were, and so forth. The Mishnah there.
So that they not touch, and so forth, and not mix. This wording is imprecise. Even where the owners are not particular, if the doughs are destined to be divided before completion they do not combine, even if initially made together. The Rash and Rosh state that neither contact nor basket combination helps where the owners are particular or the dough is destined for division.
But if there is, and so forth. Actual contact is required, as at the end of chapter 2 and the end of the preceding siman. Terumat HaDeshen writes likewise. [444]
