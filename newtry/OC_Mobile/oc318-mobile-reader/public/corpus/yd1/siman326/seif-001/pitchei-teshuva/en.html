The Gaon of Lissa writes in his prayer book that although Taz and Shach here imply that one separation of challah exempts the entire dough kneaded together, even after it is divided into separate doughs, nevertheless, if one expressly intends that the separation exempt only one dough and not the other, it does not exempt even the dough kneaded together with it. Challah is like terumah, which takes effect through thought, and when one intends not to exempt other produce, it does not exempt it. The prohibition against dividing a large dough is because doing so causes an unnecessary blessing, since one could intend to exempt all the portions with a single blessing. See there. See also his Mekor Chaim, siman 457, Biurim, seif katan 2, and Responsa Beis Ephraim, Orach Chaim, siman 44, cited in Shaarei Teshuvah to Orach Chaim 457, seif katan 3.
