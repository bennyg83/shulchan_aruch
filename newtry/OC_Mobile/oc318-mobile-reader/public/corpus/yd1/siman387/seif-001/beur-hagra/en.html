(Likkut) In overturning, etc. See the Rosh, siman 125: but if he overturned, etc., for the obligation is not, etc.; all the more so, etc. And as stated there 21a: perhaps it is a matter, etc.; but it was taught, etc. In Evel Rabbati chapter 11: if he was sleeping on a bed, on a bench, or on a platform, he is obligated in overturning the bed. One who says, "I will not overturn; I will sleep on a bench," is not heeded. In the Yerushalmi, chapter 3: if he said, "I will not overturn the bed; I will sleep on a bench," he is not heeded, because he said, "I will not overturn the bed." But if he said, "I will overturn the bed," he is heeded. Does the Mishnah not say otherwise: when they give him the mourner's meal, all the people recline on the ground and he reclines on the bench? That is regarding a kohen gadol, but not an ordinary person. It answers: that is in public, but not in private. And they do not act that way, meaning that even in public they sit on the bench and are not careful about this. Nevertheless, we learn from here that in private, whenever he overturned the bed, he may sleep on a bench, and all the more so on the ground. This is the wording of Torat HaAdam, and the words of the Tur and Shulchan Aruch require study.
(Likkut) But all, etc. See Tosafot 27a, s.v. min, etc.
(Likkut) But all day, etc.; and likewise, etc. The Gemara 25b, brought by the Rif and Rosh there, says: Rabbi Yitzchak said in the name of Rav, the comforters, etc., s.k. 45. The Ramban's text in Torat HaAdam reads: from where do we know that a mourner may not sit on a bed, but only on the ground? As it says, "and they sat with him on the ground." And Rabbi Yitzchak said in the name of Rav: from where do we know regarding comforters, etc.? So the Tur writes. All this is absent from our Gemara because the printers omitted it.
