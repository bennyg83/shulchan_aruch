(Likkut) Regarding overturning the bed. See Rosh, siman 125. If he overturned the bed, and so forth, because the obligation is not itself to sleep upon an overturned bed. The Gemara on 21a likewise asks whether this is merely a practical matter. Evel Rabbati chapter 11 says that one sleeping on a bed, bench, or platform remains obligated in overturning the bed, and if he says, "I will not overturn the bed; I will sleep on a bench," we do not listen to him. The Yerushalmi similarly says that if he merely refuses to overturn the bed and proposes sleeping on a bench, we do not listen, but if he says he will overturn the bed and nevertheless sleep on a bench, we do listen. The Mishnah's statement that at the mourner's meal everyone sits on the ground while he sits on a bench concerns a kohen gadol. The Yerushalmi distinguishes public and private circumstances and notes that in practice they did not observe this stringency publicly. We nevertheless learn that privately, once he has overturned the bed, he may sleep on a bench, and certainly on the ground. Torat HaAdam. The wording of Tur and Shulchan Aruch requires study.
(Likkut) But throughout. See Tosafot 27a, s.v. min. (Likkut) But throughout the day... and likewise. The Gemara 25b, cited by Rif and Rosh, states in the name of R. Yochanan that comforters are not permitted, and so forth. Ramban's text in Torat HaAdam reads: from where do we know that a mourner may not sit on a bed but only on the ground? From "and they sat with him upon the ground." It further states regarding those who comfort mourners. Tur cites this. These passages are absent from our printed Gemara, apparently omitted by printers.
