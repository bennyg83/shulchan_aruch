In the world. The Shach wrote: it implies that even regarding other relatives the law is so. Likewise regarding greeting and regarding marriage there is no distinction at all between father and mother and other relatives. The Bach did not explain so, and it is not clear. End quote.
Thirty. The Taz wrote: one should wonder at the Rama, for in Responsa Maharil it implies that after seven it is permitted. I later saw in Darkhei Moshe that he decided by his own reasoning to prohibit until after thirty. It is obvious that this refers to one's father and mother, for with other relatives it is obvious that after thirty it is permitted. One cannot say that he comes to teach the prohibition within thirty, for then he should have said, “it is forbidden all thirty.” Rather, with other relatives even within thirty it is permitted after seven days. This is astonishing, for the Rosh explicitly wrote in chapter Eilu Megalchin that within thirty other relatives are the same as father and mother. Nevertheless, one should not be lenient within thirty for father and mother against the Rama's decision. End quote. In Nekudot HaKesef he wrote that one who examines Responsa Maharil, siman 123, will see plainly that he does not permit within thirty for father and mother; see there.
