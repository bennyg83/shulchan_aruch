A mezuzah-post from here, etc. The requirement is that the doorway have ten handbreadths of vertical height from the ground without counting the curved portion. According to Rambam, once the arch begins it is no longer part of the ten-handbreadth vertical posts. According to Rashi, the curved part can count toward the ten-handbreadth height so long as the opening remains four handbreadths wide; only where the width narrows below four is it treated as the arch rather than the post. The Tur's wording initially sounds like Rambam because he requires two doorposts, but later he makes liability depend on whether the post reaches ten handbreadths before the opening narrows below four, which sounds like Rashi. It appears the entire passage follows Rashi; the reference to two posts merely teaches the novelty that even with recognizable side-posts the doorway is exempt if the qualifying height is under ten. In practice, where there are ten vertical handbreadths and then a long curved section that remains four handbreadths wide, there is a difficulty where to place the mezuzah. Placing it where the straight side ends follows Rambam, but according to Rashi and the Tur the upper curved portion may still count toward the doorway height, so that position could fall below the upper third and be invalid even after the fact. The matter therefore requires care.
