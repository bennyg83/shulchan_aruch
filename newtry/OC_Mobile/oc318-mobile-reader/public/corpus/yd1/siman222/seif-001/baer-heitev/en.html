Provided that. Shach writes that if he merely said, 'This money is given to you so that you may eat and dress,' but did not add, 'on condition that your husband has no authority over it,' the husband acquires it and benefit is forbidden. Rashi explains that where a father may validly give to his daughter, the husband may benefit from her because he is benefiting from his wife's property.
His father-in-law. Taz asks why the father-in-law may not simply give a gift to his daughter, since the son-in-law will surely avoid prohibited benefit. He answers that the husband acquires through his wife's receipt, as though he received it himself. Therefore even if the husband also prohibited benefit from his father-in-law's property, a gift to the wife does not help. The husband's own vow can be annulled, so it provides no reliable barrier. This is unlike Perishah.
By saying. Since he does not state explicitly for what purpose he gives it, the transfer is an unrestricted gift, and the condition that her husband have no authority does not help. In Even HaEzer 85 the Mechaber rules like the first view. Bach writes that in vows one follows the stringent side of the dispute, while in monetary law the possessor retains the property and the claimant bears the burden of proof. Shach.
