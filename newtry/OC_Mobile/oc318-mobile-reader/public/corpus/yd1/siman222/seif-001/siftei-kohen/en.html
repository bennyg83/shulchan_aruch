To give his daughter money. If he wishes to provide her with food, however, he need not say anything to her, because her husband acquires no rights in it. One may feed the wife and children of a person who is prohibited from receiving benefit from him, as stated above in siman 221, seif 2, and cited by the later authorities.
Or for what you will wear, etc. Perishah, se'if katan 3, writes: “This requires study if he merely said to her, ‘This money is given to you on condition that you eat and dress with it,’ but did not say, ‘on condition that your husband has no rights in it’”; this concludes his words. It appears clear to me from the language of the Mishnah and the poskim that whenever he did not tell her, “on condition that your husband has no rights in it,” the husband certainly acquires it and it is forbidden. This is also explicit in Rosh's commentary to Nedarim 88a.
And some say that even, etc. Since he does not specify the purpose for which he gave it to her, it is a complete gift. The condition that her husband has no rights in it is then ineffective, because the husband has already acquired it, as a wife's hand is like her husband's hand. In Even HaEzer, siman 85, seif 11, the Mechaber rules without qualification in accordance with the first view and does not cite the second view. Bach writes that this appears to be a dispute among the leading authorities. Regarding vows, one rules stringently. In monetary law, one rules leniently for the defendant and stringently for the claimant, and the burden of proof rests upon the person seeking to take property from its possessor.
