Six onot. And each onah is twelve hours.
Lest she discharge. And she will not feel.
Until day five. For until that day we are concerned every day lest she discharge, or lest she discharge all three days.
From hour to hour. Meaning seventy-two hours.
And it negates. Meaning, even though six onot were completed at beginning of night four after her relations on motzaei Shabbat, nevertheless since she discharged at beginning of night four before time of her relations on motzaei Shabbat — if so it negates that day, namely all of day four, and she begins counting from day five.
Until day six, etc. And Maharshal wrote in responsum siman 82: per this custom, if she saw after Maariv on day one while it is still day, she can wear clean garments at the end of day five and count from day six, and there is no doubt in the matter (and from his words it appears that even though she also prayed Maariv); but per the custom of places of Taz, who wear whites on day (1) [4] of their sight and begin counting from day five — difficult in the matter, etc., and it is not like what Mahar"i wrote in responsum that a woman cannot stop in purity after the congregation prayed Maariv, for here is different since the cause came from Heaven, and it is not relevant to say she should have preceded or delayed, unlike hefsek taharah, etc.; and nevertheless it appears sufficient for us here that we are stringent where she already prayed Maariv and made it already night; but where she did not pray, even if the congregation already prayed Maariv, we are not so stringent to consider it night, since the cause came from Heaven, etc. end quote; and it appears this follows Mahar"i's words above seif 1; but per my example s.k. 4, that Maharil's and the Rav's view in hefsek taharah is to be lenient even if she prayed, and all the more here — a fortiori.
Without having relations. For the Sages did not distinguish and decree lest she had relations as if she had relations; and even though having relations itself is a decree — concern of bein hashemashot is a simple concern and the Sages were very concerned in many places. Tashbetz. And Bach wrote: however this is specifically when her husband is in the city; but when her husband is not in the city one may be lenient, for immediately when she stops in purity near bein hashemashos she counts from the morrow; and even though in Tashbetz it appears from the language of the question that he asked to be stringent even when her husband is not in the city — nevertheless from the language of the answer that he did not mention being stringent in this, it appears somewhat one may be lenient thus end quote; and his words do not appear correct, for it is known the questions in Tashbetz were made by Mahar"i the author of the responsa himself and not that others asked him as in his rulings; and further if so why did he not answer from this question immediately — the matter is simple since in the question he equated explicitly her husband is not in the city to without having relations — if so also in the answer it is so; and also in responsum Mahari"k siman 35 he wrote women are stringent even when their husbands are not in the city; and so is the settled custom in these lands. And greater than this they practice that even a bride does not count seven clean days until day five of her sight, even though the groom has not yet come to the city; even though per my view there is no reason to be stringent at all, nor is it mentioned in any early or late posek — nevertheless let it be only among permitted matters where others practiced prohibition, etc.; and nevertheless in time of pressure one may permit a bride that immediately when she stops in purity she counts seven clean days — for thus is better than the custom that they marry while niddah, which Rambam ruled she should not marry at all until pure; and even though we are not meticulous so, as the Rav wrote Even HaEzer end siman 61 — nevertheless certainly where possible in purity is better. So it appears to me.
And every woman who sees a stain, etc. It appears regarding a stain one may be lenient where she did not have relations, to count from the morrow of finding the stain, for this not having relations itself is a distant decree; and also Beit Yosef wrote in his place they did not practice this decree; therefore one may say also Tashbetz himself, who wrote to be stringent, meant regarding sight but not rabbinic stains, for we follow lenient approach in many places, since he did not mention stain at all; and possibly for this the Rav did not write this; and some wrote now, etc., after law of stain only after law of sight; but Taz wrote some wrote that every woman, even seeing a stain like mustard (it appears to emend: or a drop of blood like mustard, but a stain requires a gris, and further as above siman 190), whether she had relations or not — must wait, etc.; and so is the custom.
Must wait five days, etc. Meaning from the beginning; and because of the reason of Sha"z; but if within her counting days or after five days she was corrupted and returned and saw blood or stain — she stops that day in purity and counts from the morrow; and this is simple.
