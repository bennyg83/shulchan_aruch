Onot. And each onah is twelve hours.
From her having intercourse. For until that day we are concerned every day lest she discharge, or lest she discharge all three days; and from above, of the six onot it is seventy-two hours.
Within. Meaning, even though six onot were completed at the beginning of night four after her relations, nevertheless since she discharged at the beginning of night four before the time of her relations, as stated — if so it negates that day, namely all of day four, and she begins counting from day five end quote Shach.
To add. Taz wrote, and so in siman 196 in name of Maharal of Prague, that a bride after relations of mitzvah can count from day five for her relations; and his reason appears: since here there is no niddah blood, only hymeneal blood, they were not stringent regarding relations during bein hashemashot; but if she truly had relations of mitzvah during bein hashemashot, it appears certainly we consider her as if she had relations on the night after that bein hashemashot, even regarding a bride — so if the bride had relations on the night of relations of mitzvah she counts from day five; and if she had relations during bein hashemashot at the end of day one she counts from day six, as stated; and Maharshal wrote that if she saw after Maariv on day one while it is still day, she can wear clean garments at the end of day five and count from day six, and there is no doubt in the matter end quote; and from his words it appears that even though she also prayed Maariv. Shach.
Stain. Meaning, a pure woman who finds a stain — her law is like an actual sight also for this; but a woman who wore whites and found a stain, and even an actual sight within the days of clean days — she need not wait six onot but counts anew seven clean days from the day she can. Taz. And Shach wrote that it appears regarding a stain one may be lenient where she did not have relations, to count from the morrow of finding the stain, for this case of not having relations itself is a distant decree; therefore one should be stringent only regarding actual sight but not regarding rabbinic stains, for we follow a lenient approach in many places (and see responsum Sha'y part 2 question 77, who ruled regarding a woman who immersed and her husband was not in the city on the night of her immersion and she found a stain — he rules for that day and counts from the morrow, even though the custom is to be stringent; nevertheless in such a case, where she did not have relations and her husband is not in the city, lean toward leniency in stains; and so responsum Pnei Yehoshua siman 12 end quote); and possibly for this the Rav did not write this; and some wrote now etc. after the law of stain only after the law of sight; but nevertheless what Bach wishes, to be lenient even in actual sight when the husband is not in the city — his words do not appear correct, and so is the settled custom in these lands to be stringent even when the husband is not in the city; and greater than this, the custom that even a bride does not count seven clean days until day five of her sight, even though the groom has not yet come to the city; and even though per my view there is no reason to be stringent at all, nor is it mentioned in any early or late posek — nevertheless let it be only among permitted matters where others practiced prohibition; and nevertheless in a time of pressure one may permit a bride that immediately when she stops in purity she counts seven clean days — for thus is better than the custom that they marry while she is niddah, which Rambam ruled she should not marry at all until pure; and even though we are not meticulous so, as written in Even HaEzer siman 61 — nevertheless certainly where possible in purity is better; and Taz wrote that it appears to be lenient regarding a widow who declared niddah near her chuppah, and even after the chuppah before she secluded with her groom she does not wait at all, for in this certainly there is no reason to decree lest she had relations as if she had relations, since she has not yet entered complete relations with him — why decree such? end quote.
