Semen does not putrefy, etc. Explanation: and after it putrefies, even though she discharges it, behold she is like one discharging mere water; therefore they required she wait six complete onot — meaning each onah is measure of day or night, namely twelve hours; and therefore if she had relations on night of relations of mitzvah, for example two hours at night, and begins counting seven clean days from day four — behold six onot were not completed until night four two hours into it; and if so how can she count day four as a clean day, for perhaps she will discharge in those two hours at beginning of night and semen is still not putrefied; and every clean day must be entirely clean both at night and on the day within it — therefore she should not begin counting except from day five; all who had relations on the night after Shabbat, except there is one more stringency: namely if she had relations on night of relations of mitzvah — if we permit her to count from day five, she may err if she has relations on the first day during its bein hashemashot and she thinks it is still day — it turns out per this her vessels: six onot of hers at end of day four and the night after it would be for beginning clean days, since still day, and we count hour for hour, and she begins counting from the fifth day; but in truth it was then night and her six onot were not completed until beginning of night of day five; and perhaps she will discharge Sha"z at that beginning of night — it turns out she must count from day six, for day five is not clearly clean from discharge of Sha"z at its beginning; therefore the later authorities were stringent regarding every woman who has relations on whatever day — we consider her as if she had relations on the day after it; therefore if she had relations on night of relations of mitzvah she counts from day six as if she had relations at end of day one during bein hashemashot, when she is compelled to count from day six from force of doubt; and they placed this stringency upon her for having relations all that first day, even beginning of its night; and I already wrote siman 193 in name of Maharal of Prague that a bride after relations of mitzvah can count from day five for her relations — his intent to say we do not decree upon her lest she have relations that day at its end, namely during bein hashemashot; and his reason appears: since here there is no niddah blood, only hymeneal blood, they were not stringent because of relations during bein hashemashot; but if she truly had relations of mitzvah during bein hashemashot, it appears certainly we consider her as if she had relations on the night after that bein hashemashot, even regarding a bride — it turns out if the bride had relations on night of relations of mitzvah she counts from day five; and if she had relations during bein hashemashot at end of day one she counts from day six, per my humble opinion.
And there is no reason in the matter. Moharach z"l brought a reason for the matter in name of Rosh of Austria: since niddah from Torah immerses after seven days of niddah even if they were not clean days, only she requires seven clean days lest she be in zivah days — and in early days they practiced immersing twice: once after seven from Torah and second after seven clean days; and they remembered that matter and also practiced waiting fourteen days end quote; and Rama wrote well that one who prepares himself prepares for the mitzvah, and such reasons are not sufficient to delay immersion of mitzvah.
There is no distinction between whether she had relations, etc. Beit Yosef did not consider this stringency except if she did not have relations she does not wait at all; but Rama fulfills the custom to be stringent in this; and Moharach z"l wrote it appears to him to be lenient when her husband is not in the city, that one should not be so stringent; and it escaped him responsum Mahari"k shoresh 35, who wrote explicitly they practiced to be stringent even when her husband is not in the city — who will dispute the custom? Except it appears to me there is one leniency in this (for a widow) [for a bride] who declared niddah near her chuppah, and even after her chuppah before she secluded with her groom she does not wait at all — for in this certainly there is no reason to decree lest she had relations as if she had relations, since she has not yet entered complete relations with him — why decree such? So it appears to me.
Even a stain. Meaning, a pure woman who finds a stain — her law is like an actual sight also for this; but a woman who wore whites and found a stain, and even an actual sight within the days of clean days — she need not wait six onot but counts anew seven clean days from the day she can.
