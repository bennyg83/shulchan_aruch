Even at end of the seventh day, etc. And likewise on day eight — forever she must sit seven clean days near her immersion — and this is simple.
Some say in three, etc. Thus wrote D"M in Mordechai: if she counts within seven clean days, it is reasonable from three days onward stains are pure if there is something to attribute to wound, bruise, or bird, and she does not forbid; but she must know with certainty that source blood stopped completely — therefore the first three days of counting clean days must be completely pure (and in Mordechai there it states from Sefer HaTaharah — I abbreviated) end quote; and so in Hagahot Maimoniyot chapter 9 from Fathers of Impurity; and it appears that not attributing to wound in the first three days is specifically regarding a wound not known to discharge blood — we attribute other stains as above siman 187 (seif 5), and so from Mordechai's language that it deals with this; but regarding a wound known to discharge blood, it appears we attribute even in the first three days, for even regarding actual sight we attribute above siman 187 to a wound discharging blood — all the more rabbinic stains; and one may say actual sight is different, for it is beginning of sight, therefore we attribute to wound and say her opening is still closed and she did not see; but within her counting her opening is already open — we do not attribute to wound at all — this is not so, for behold I wrote above siman 187 that some hold at her veset time we attribute to wound, since vestot are rabbinic; if so, stains too, which are rabbinic, we attribute to wound; and even for one stringent at veset time, that is because there is one who says vestot are from Torah; but stains, which are rabbinic, all attribute to a wound known to discharge blood, as above, to lean lenient, because other poskim did not distinguish between a stain seen during counting days and other stains; if so, it implies he holds even within three days we attribute a stain wherever there is something to attribute; and so above siman 190 (seif 41) in name of Beit Yosef in name of Rashba, that we attribute a stain even during her counting days; and possibly Sefer HaTaharah and Mordechai were stringent only as a general stringency, for behold Rashba wrote in Teshuvot siman 249: the reason we do not attribute in the first three days is since it is possible to be careful, etc.; and if it were forbidden by law, it would not be relevant to distinguish because one can be careful — rather certainly they were stringent only because one can be careful; but where impossible to be careful, one should not be stringent; and therefore he permitted a stain smaller than a gris, and further we attribute to a louse — so it appears one should not be stringent regarding a wound known to discharge blood end quote D"M; and all his words contradict what he wrote, and it appears to me we do not attribute, etc., and so is Mordechai's language, etc.; on the contrary, the simple meaning of Mordechai's words, which conclude for her, implies he deals with every wound; and further, behold even if she was occupied with bird blood we do not attribute, even though blood is before us — all the more a wound discharging blood; and for the Rav one must distinguish, for bird blood is from another place, unlike a wound on her body where impossible to be careful — and this is not the simple meaning; and further, behold these are words of Sefer HaTaharah and Hagahot Maimoniyot, and the simple meaning of Sefer HaTaharah and Hagahot Maimoniyot implies even from a wound known to discharge blood we do not attribute, for they wrote: if she has a wound in that place she attributes her stain to it even if she does not know whether it discharges blood or not, etc.; and if she counts seven clean days, etc. — therefore the first three days of counting must be clean end quote; and since they wrote at the beginning "even if she does not know her wound discharges blood," it implies all the more when she knows; and if so, since the beginning deals in all cases, the end also deals in all cases; and further, since the poskim attributed the reason that she must know with certainty source blood stopped, it implies in the first three days when she does not know with certainty her source blood stopped, in every case we do not attribute to wound — and so it appears explicitly in Beit Yosef siman 190 in name of Orchot Chayyim; and thus wrote Rambam: a woman afflicted with boils — it is simple her stains are pure, for since boils and sores are in her certainly discharge blood and stain her cloths and garment, and even at her veset time, and all the more during counting days we attribute; and Hagahot Alfasi and Hagahot Maimoniyot commented on him: after three for counting, but within three we do not attribute end quote; and these words of Rambam and Alfasi are in Tashbetz siman 481 — therefore even though certainly they discharge blood, we do not attribute within three; and one may say what the Rav wrote to distinguish between opening closed or open is certainly correct, and so it appears from what the poskim wrote that she must know with certainty source blood stopped; and what he wrote this is not so, for behold I wrote above there are those who hold, etc. — it is not a contradiction, for nevertheless there it is not known her opening is open, unlike here; and further, behold there we hold at veset time we do not attribute to her wound even if known to discharge blood, as written there s.k. 26; and what the Rav wrote is because there is one who holds vestot are from Torah, etc. — certainly not so, for certainly Rashbag, who holds vestot are rabbinic, also holds thus from the proof that otherwise she would never be tamei, as written there in that s.k.; and more correct for the Rav to say that even one stringent there, that is because if not so she would never be tamei, unlike here; but all this is not the simple meaning — rather the opposite is reasonable: here, since her opening is open, all require she know with certainty source blood stopped; and what he wrote thus it appears to lean lenient because other poskim did not distinguish, etc. — this is not worth being lenient, for in many places we find such, even where some poskim are lenient and other poskim were silent — we say learn the silent from the explicit, all the more here where the explicit ones are stringent; and further, who is greater than Sefer HaTaharah and Mordechai and Hagahot Maimoniyot, and all the more Alfasi and Orchot Chayyim and Tashbetz agree to their words; and so Agudah chapter Tinoket siman 39; and what he wrote and so above siman 190 in name of Rashba, etc. — one may say Rashba deals after three and Rashba's words are not better than Rambam's words, who wrote during counting days we attribute to be lenient, and Alfasi explained after three; and even if Rashba disputes this he is alone against all these great ones, especially since they are stringent; and what he wrote and possibly they were stringent only as a general stringency, for behold Rashba wrote in Teshuvot siman 249, etc. — I do not know what proof this is, for Tashbetz did not write there except smaller than a gris, and further we attribute to a louse since it is very common and impossible to be careful — if so there is no woman who can count seven clean days, unlike a wound; but certainly the reason is because she must certainly know source blood stopped; again I found in Bach end siman 190, who wrote regarding a woman afflicted with boils: since blood always issues from the boils on her body and stains her cloths and garment, one may attribute even in the first three days, for otherwise she cannot count seven clean days ever; and one may say similarly for a bruise on her body discharging blood; and even so we do not attribute in the first three days to bruise A, which was covered with plaster that does not discharge blood except sometimes — similar to a wound in that place, for Sefer HaTaharah wrote sometimes it discharges blood; but one afflicted with boils, that every hour blood issues from her, is like food-blood that we attribute even in the first three days, etc.; and in any case everything depends on the nature of the boils — examine end quote; it is clear from his words one may be lenient only when full of boils and sores and blood always issues from her; if not so, even if known her wound discharges blood one may not be lenient; and so in Sefer Ephi Rabrevi, who copied all the matters in this siman — only this law that the Rav wrote regarding a wound discharging blood we attribute even in first three days he omitted, only wrote anonymously that one does not attribute a stain like a gris, and nothing else in the first three days end quote.
