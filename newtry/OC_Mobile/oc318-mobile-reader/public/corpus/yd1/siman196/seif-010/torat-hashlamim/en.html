One does not attribute it to be lenient. The reason: within the first three days she still has doubt whether her source blood stopped; unlike after three — so Mordechai and Hagahot Maimoniyot in name of Sefer HaTaharah; and per this it appears for a woman occupied with stains or who has bruises and wounds (meaning she does not know it discharges blood) and fears she cannot count seven clean days cleanly without stain — for us, where women do not count seven clean days until after day five from relations, as explained seif 11 — it appears there is a remedy: immediately when source blood stopped on day one or two of her sight she checks herself well as is customary for counting seven clean days; and after day five from relations when she begins counting seven clean days, already three days of stopped source blood have passed and she attributes her stain to be lenient — a fortiori; and similarly ruled responsum Tzitz Eliezer siman 65 in words of the questioner, who brings clear proofs; and even though author of Tzemach Tzedek there objects to be stringent — that is where she checked not intentionally but for honor of Shabbat and concerned perhaps she did not check very well; unlike if she checked intentionally — see there.
And you know it discharges blood. Shach elaborated to challenge this law and wrote one may be lenient only if the wound always discharges blood; and in truth his words are not compelled at all; and regarding stains we hear to be lenient as explained above siman 190; and so responsum Tzitz Eliezer siman 65: thus wrote one who is lenient when she found a stain within three of seven clean days to attribute even for any reason does not lose so much, since in any case many are lenient in stains within three end quote; and it appears if she found a stain within first three days of seven clean days she must return and count seven clean days anew; and then even if she saw a stain within three of the second seven clean days she attributes to be lenient, since already the first three days passed and the second time she did not see from her body; and if you say it was from her body — her opening was not presumed open from sight of the stain; and further it is a doubt within a doubt — doubt whether the first stain was from her body, and if you say from her body, perhaps the second stain was not from her body — examine; and see Taz, who wrote that per the Rav one may not attribute to a wound unless impossible to be careful not to soil herself; but if possible to be careful and she did not do so — one does not attribute.
