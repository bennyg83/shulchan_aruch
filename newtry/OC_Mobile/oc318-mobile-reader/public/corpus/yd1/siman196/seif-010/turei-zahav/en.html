Must be consecutive, etc. In Beit Yosef it is written that from this there is no practical difference except for Talmudic law, where there was distinction between niddah and zivah; but nowadays, after R. Zeira's stringency that every woman who sees even a drop like mustard must sit seven clean days, there is no practical difference from this law; for even if she does not negate, behold she must sit seven clean days because of the sight now — and this is a great stretch that Tur would write law according to the Talmud also in Shulchan Aruch; Beit Yosef and Rama wrote, and therefore there is some practical difference from it; and it appears to me there is practical difference even for us: per what we hold, a woman who has a veset need not check at all, as written beginning siman 187 — the reason is then she is in presumption of purity, unlike regarding seven clean days — we hold here lechatchila she needs checking, the reason being she is in presumption of impurity until the seven clean days emerge; therefore he informs us here presumption of impurity is upon her until seven consecutive are completed; lest you say at end of seven clean days behold she returns to presumption of purity and need not check even lechatchila — he informs us even if she sees at the very end of the seven she negates everything; if so still her original presumption is upon her and needs checking lechatchila also at that time, per my humble opinion.
But less than a gris, etc. In Beit Yosef in name of Tashbetz wrote thus: but in less than that she need not attribute it to bruise or wound, only to food-blood — certainly we attribute forever, for otherwise no woman can become pure, as stated chapter HaRo'eh: there is no woman upon whom there are not several drops of food-blood end quote; and Rama wrote in D"M: that not attributing to bruise is certainly a general stringency, for if by law what do we care whether impossible to purify or not; and he proved from law we attribute even in first three days, for even regarding actual sight we attribute to wound she has in that place; and he concludes there: so it appears to me for halachah; but practically one should be stringent lechatchila regarding passing wound in few days, for also this is called possible to be careful end quote; and nevertheless he wrote here to permit regarding a wound she knows with certainty discharges blood; and it appears what he brought in D"M proof from actual sight is not proof, for there she has initial presumption of purity and you come to defile her — therefore we attribute to wound; unlike here where she is already tamei and you need to purify her — therefore you need clear presumption for purity to remove her from previous presumption of impurity; therefore we require three completely clean days — then there is presumption for purity in three days; but in less than a gris impossible in the world to be careful in this — the Sages did not decree, for this matter is only rabbinic, since she does not feel blood issuing, as above; and likewise that case of one afflicted with boils that I copied end siman 190 — impossible to be careful from it; and so it appears regarding a wound she has on her body — whenever she can be careful not to soil herself from blood and did not do so, she may not attribute to that wound; unlike a wound she cannot guard properly — she attributes; and Rama's words here in Shulchan Aruch also intend this. And Moharach z"l copied in name of Bedek HaBayit on Beit Yosef, who wrote on that case of one afflicted with boils in name of Alfasi: in first three days one does not attribute to boils; and Moharach z"l concludes everything depends on the boils — namely per the manner I wrote.
