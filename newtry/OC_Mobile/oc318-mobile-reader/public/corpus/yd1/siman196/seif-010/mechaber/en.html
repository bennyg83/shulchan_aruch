The seven nekiyim must be consecutive, that she should not see blood in them; for if she saw blood, even at the end of the seventh day, it nullified all the days, and she needs to stop in purity and return to count seven nekiyim. {Rama: Some say that in the first three days of the counting days, if she found a ketem, one does not attribute it to be lenient as one attributes other ketamot; for the first three days must be completely clean (Mordechai laws of Niddah, and in Hagahot Maimoniyot chapter 9 of Avot HaTumah, and Mordechai and Terumas HaDeshen siman 249 and Agur chapter Tinoket and Orchot Chayim and Rokeach). But afterward its law is like any other ketem, and so they practice. And specifically a ketem that is more than a gris and more; but less than a gris and more — one attributes it to an itch even in the first three days (Terumas HaDeshen there). And the same if she had a wound on her body and she knows that it emits blood — one attributes it to it even if more than a gris and more (the reasoning of the Rav, and so the language of Mordechai implies); except that one is not lenient in the first three days to attribute to a wound that is not known to emit blood, or to other matters to which we attribute a ketem, as explained above siman 190.}
