It negated. Taz wrote that even for us, who are stringent even when seeing a drop of blood like mustard, she needs seven clean days — what is the practical difference? The Mechaber wrote this law of negation; and it appears there is practical difference also now: per what we hold, a woman who has a veset need not check at all, as written beginning siman 187, for she is in presumption of purity; not so regarding seven clean days — we hold here that lechatchila she needs checking, for she is in presumption of impurity until completion of the seven clean days; therefore he informs us here that presumption of impurity is upon her until seven consecutive days are completed; lest you say that when the seven clean days end she returns to presumption of purity and need not check even lechatchila — he informs us that even if she sees at the very end of the seven she negates everything; if so, her original presumption is still upon her and she needs checking lechatchila also at that time.

Shach disagrees with Rama on this law and ruled that even if she knows with certainty that her wound discharges blood, nevertheless she may not attribute it in the first three days of seven clean days; and so from Bach's words — one may be lenient only specifically when she is full of boils and blood always issues from her, for then we say if she does not attribute to it she cannot ever count seven clean days; but otherwise, even if it is known her wound discharges blood, one may not be lenient end quote — he elaborates with many proofs; and Taz wrote that one should understand Rama's words as dealing with a wound she cannot guard properly — then she attributes; but whenever she can guard and did not do so, she may not attribute at all end quote (see responsum Tzitz Eliezer siman 65 and Avnei Nezer siman 79).
