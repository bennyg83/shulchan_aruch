Siman 61 in Shulchan Aruch siman 1. And they consider this bediavad; per my humble opinion I say, to resolve Tosafot's question on Shach siman 375: why regarding mourning that day did not count — because here if we are stringent to consider night, sometimes there is also a leniency, as explained s.k. 89: a woman established a veset three times and is not concerned further until uprooted three times, and all stains she attributes to be lenient; and likewise if uprooted three times she is no longer concerned; and it is clear it must be specifically in one onah — equal day or night; and if so, if a woman sees twice at night on twentieth of the month and on third saw on Yom Tov while still a long day after the congregation prayed Maariv — if we consider it night, this woman establishes on the tenth a veset and need not be concerned for any other veset; and similarly if she establishes veset by day and uprooted it twice at night and on third saw by day after Maariv prayer — if we consider it night it is uprooting and she is permitted even in that onah she was accustomed to before, for uprooting as required by law; and in this matter there would be a great leniency; and therefore they consider it day, for in truth it is still a long day, unlike mourning where we are properly stringent because no leniency emerges — a fortiori; and one may reject that also regarding mourning a leniency is relevant for distant hearing — if it is night it is after thirty days.
Seif 1 in Shulchan Aruch siman 1 in the Hagahah. Since the congregation already made it night; and per my humble opinion I rely on what R. Yehudah said in the mishnah, that she must check from minchah and upward; and Beit Yosef already wondered: the day is still long two and a half hours — what is this great measure from night that he mentioned? Indeed R. Yehudah according to his reasoning holds minchah until plag minchah; and see Rashi to Berakhot, who holds from there his law is like night for all matters; and if so hefsek taharah does not help; and therefore he gave time from minchah and upward; and per this they already said in Berakhot the halachah was not stated whether like R. Yehudah or like the Sages — one who acts like this acts, and one who acts like that acts; if so the congregation who prayed Maariv while still day took the essence like R. Yehudah, that it is night — if so how can she stop in purity? What for R. Yehudah does not help — examine.
Seif 2 there in the Hagahah. Even if the congregation accepted Shabbat — see Shach s.k. 4, who inferred from the Agur who wrote twenty-four hours, that even for those who are lenient it is only until twenty-four hours, etc. end quote; and I did not understand his intent: if he intended in summer on a long day, after the sixth hour it is all night — he exaggerates the measure, for the day is still long; and Agur brought proof from counting of an apostate, and there it is reversed that the day should be long even twenty hours — nevertheless he counts only at night from emergence of stars; and Agur wrote twenty-four hours and did not write twelve hours, for then there would be room to say he did not intend fixed times; but he wrote twenty-four hours; and if the day is long the night is short — never are night and day more than twenty-four hours, whether in winter or summer, for when the day is short the night is long and when the night is short the day is long — ultimately they are twenty-four hours; therefore Agur gave twenty-four hours; and if so it is simple he means until end of day; and Shach's words are astonishing.
