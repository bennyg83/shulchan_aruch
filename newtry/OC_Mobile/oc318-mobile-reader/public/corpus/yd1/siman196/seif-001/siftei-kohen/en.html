In order that she stop in purity — bein hashemashos.
Only in the morning, etc. — meaning from day two of her sight onward, as below seif 2.
Since the congregation already made it, etc. — even though she has not yet prayed Maariv. Tashbetz there.
Even if the congregation accepted Shabbat. For that addition does not relate to niddah matters and requires twenty-four hours; and so it states in chapter HaNa'arah (Ketubot page 47a) in Tosafot s.v. demasar lah on Shabbatot and Yom Tov; and there is proof from counting the omer that we do not consider night even on erev Shabbat, for tosefet Shabbat is from Torah; and likewise eating matzah on Pesach and sukkah end quote — the Agur in name of Maharil; from his words and from all these proofs it appears that even though she also prayed and accepted Shabbat it is permitted (and so is the view of one of the great ones in a responsum that Tashbetz brought there, that even if she prayed Maariv it is permitted — see there in Tashbetz); and per this, what the Rav wrote that even if the congregation accepted Shabbat, etc. — even if the congregation also accepted Shabbat and she too — the Gaon Ammo"z"l did not write so in his responsum, rather wrote for the Rav that if the prayer is Maariv one may not be lenient even bediavad, as below siman 375 end quote (and so is Maharshal's view in responsum siman 72 that I cited below s.k. 19) — it is clear from the Agur I cited that even for those who are lenient it is only until twenty-four hours; and if so, when the day is so long we consider day and night equal, as for example siman 184 s.k. 7); but after that, for all it is forbidden.
And some women, etc., within her counting days, etc. Bach wrote: from his words it appears specifically within her counting days; but at beginning of her count if she saw blood after Barchu, even though the day is still long, she does not stop in purity that day but on the morrow — and this is not so; rather even at beginning of her count she stops in purity even if she saw blood near evening, etc.; and per my humble opinion also per the Rav, even if she saw blood near evening she stops before Barchu at beginning of her count; and this means: even if she stopped before Barchu we do not say since she stopped before Barchu therefore after Barchu is considered night for her; rather if she was corrupted afterward she stops even after Barchu; and this is what the Rav wrote "and bediavad one need not be concerned"; and if she sees blood until near evening, it is obvious there is no greater bediavad than this and she begins to stop after Barchu; and what he wrote "and they practice lechatchila to be careful" — means where possible, such as when she does not see blood before Barchu — a fortiori.
