Seif 5 there in the Hagahah. Some say in the first three days — I am astonished at the body of this law: is it not clear in the Gemara that if she checked herself on the second for sight and found no blood, and did not check again until day eight and found tamei — we do not defile retroactively, for we establish her in presumption of purity, and she was not presumed for three days stopping; learn from this that with one checking and not finding she was presumed; and further, if so you presume her in presumption of seeing — that which they say therefore for Rabbi she is not pure, she attributes observing one day against one day, for they are equivalent; and Rashi explained she too is in presumption of purity; and difficult — behold she was not presumed to stop three days; consider yourself: she the pure one can attribute to stain, wound, and observing one day against one day — impossible to attribute; and how are they equivalent — behold she is in presumption of impurity so much that I attribute to her more than a wound; and how did you pair that she attributes to wound — she cannot attribute for Rabbi; and even Rashbag, who disagrees, from reason of attributing the mishap to the defective, but not from the above reason; and it would appear to say Mordechai and Sefer HaTaharah deal with one who saw three days, therefore she needs three days she did not see to remove presumption; but if she saw only two days she does not need, and hefsek suffices — all the more; but his language does not imply so; and also if she checked herself on day four, why also do we presume presumption of purity? And in the Gemara it appears she negates presumption of purity on day four from day three, two; and therefore it must be that certainly per what I stated siman 50 regarding doubtful life — presumption is not relevant since there is presumption of prohibition; even though in slaughter it was permitted, nevertheless we do not say presumption; and likewise here — behold otherwise tamei; and even though in this there would be permission in this counting, nevertheless there is no presumption; and if so all stains one should be stringent; only we say since blood should not come from a woman we attribute to stain; but this one who was not presumed three days — still common in her — we attribute to something common, as above siman 153 in Shach; and if so Rama"ch wrote well regarding a wound discharging blood: more common than blood from source; and behold she is permitted; and behold it is without sensation, and it is a rabbinic doubt for leniency, since both are common; and in vain the later authorities were stringent regarding her wound discharging blood, for the above law contradicts.
