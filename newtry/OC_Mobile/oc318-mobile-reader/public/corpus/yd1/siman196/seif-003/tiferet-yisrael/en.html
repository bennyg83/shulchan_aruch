Even if the congregation accepted Shabbat — see Shach s.k. 4, who inferred from the Agur who wrote twenty-four hours, that even for those who are lenient it is only until twenty-four hours, etc. end quote; and I did not understand his intent: if he intended in summer on a long day, after the sixth hour it is all night — he exaggerates the measure, for the day is still long; and Agur brought proof from counting of an apostate, and there it is reversed that the day should be long even twenty hours — nevertheless he counts only at night from emergence of stars; and Agur wrote twenty-four hours and did not write twelve hours, for then there would be room to say he did not intend fixed times; but he wrote twenty-four hours; and if the day is long the night is short — never are night and day more than twenty-four hours, whether in winter or summer, for when the day is short the night is long and when the night is short the day is long — ultimately they are twenty-four hours; therefore Agur gave twenty-four hours; and if so it is simple he means until end of day; and Shach's words are astonishing.
