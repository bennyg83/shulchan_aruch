See responsum Me'il Tzedakah siman 63, who wrote regarding a woman who found a stain during days of whiteness, and in the evening stopped in purity anew and wore whites again — and her friends told her the next day that for the stain too she must wait anew five days — and she returned and wore her garment stained with several stains; however she wore a white apron tied below, clean from yesterday's whiteness; and after two days she learned she need not wait anew five days — and asked if she may count seven clean days from her first hefsek taharah, for she claims she sold the stains on her garment which are from days of her first impurity; and to prove her words she said the apron is still clean, and had the stain been anew it would be found on the apron. And behold this question divides into several aspects. First: whether she may count the first days that fell at the beginning per her view. Second: even if those are considered pure, if she wore soiled garments — do we say those stains are from days of her niddah? Third: even if we do not attribute on this, do we believe her that she sold them bediavad; and what does it add that the lower apron is clean — this raises doubt one: since she presumed herself impure she should not count those days, and now there is no practical difference in the later doubts; but if she did not presume herself impure, those two days count for her since she sold the stains bediavad as from days of her niddah; and even if she has no bediavad in them, they count for her from the proof that on the apron she needs to find; but if there is no such proof and also no bediavad in them, we do not attribute they are from days of niddah, since it is in the first three days end quote.
