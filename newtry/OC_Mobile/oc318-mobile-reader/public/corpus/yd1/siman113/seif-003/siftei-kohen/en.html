Pastida. Of fish. So it is among the poskim. And Mahara of Tikotin wrote in the margin of Mordechai, chapter of Avodah Zarah: those fats of meat or of goose — usually they are already rendered, and there is no [prohibition of] bishul akum, for there is no cooking after cooking — end of his words. And if a non-Jew baked pastida with raw fat of meat, its law is like fat of fish, and it is straightforward:
Even for one who practices leniency with bread of a non-Jew. And this is with pastida of a non-Jew; but pastida of a Jew that gentiles baked — they did not practice leniency in any place, as above in siman 112, small note 16. And the same applies: you find this law with pastida of a Jew that a non-Jew baked and made it fit with a chip, which helps for bread as above in siman 112, seif 11, and does not help for bishul akum according to the Mechaber in seif 7; but according to what the Rav wrote there, that even for cooking of a non-Jew a chip helps — you do not find this law except with pastida of a non-Jew; and so R' Peretz in Semak, siman 207:
