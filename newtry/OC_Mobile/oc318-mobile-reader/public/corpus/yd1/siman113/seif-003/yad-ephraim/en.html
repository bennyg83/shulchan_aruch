And smoked. Avodat HaTaharah; and in responsa Shevut Yaakov, volume 3, siman 74: a non-Jew who gave a Jew a gift of smoked fish — there is no concern of prohibition because they were smoked on his stove on which he cooks his foods, as we are not concerned for the smoking of a Jew to say concern that meat was smoked over milk cooking, as written in Terumat HaDeshen siman 13, since the hand is not scalded by steam — even though there is room to distinguish, etc. And that which they buy small fish called piketig that are smoked — one could say an oven has a special place for smoke; not so a non-Jew homeowner who smokes on his stove. Nevertheless, there is some proof from what they permit smoked [fish] because of bishul akum and they did not mention concern of steam, for we do not maintain a prohibition; also gentiles are careful not to smoke near a dish lest steam rise from the dish. And nevertheless, to distance from the smell of prohibition — lest he hung it to smoke with other meat or non-kosher fish and touched the prohibition itself — one should rinse the smoked meat again three times in alternating waters; and such a case is explained in Orach Chayim siman 447 regarding dried meat — see there.
