Heating the oven (meaning: heating, heating up, and kindling — Onkelos: veyahon shagrin) is not effective except for bread; but for other cooked foods, heating the oven and lighting the fire do not raise or lower [the prohibition] — only placing [the food] specifically. Therefore, one who wishes to cook in a pan in a non-Jew's oven must have a Jew place the pan into the oven in the place fit for it to cook. {Rama: And there are disputants who hold that lighting the fire or stoking coals is effective for cooking as for bread, and so is the practice (glosses of ShaDa, Arukh general rule 43, and Mordechai, chapter of Avodah Zarah). And even stoking without intention is effective and helps (from the implication of Maharai in glosses of ShaDa and Kol Bo). And some say that even if a Jew did not stoke and did not throw a chip there, but only the non-Jew lit the fire from a Jew's fire — it is permitted (Issur ve-Heter there in the name of Maharam).}
