Or as relish, etc. — so the Tur; and it appears he means that even though kings do not dip bread in it, since it rises on their table as relish — that is, to conclude a meal — it is forbidden because of bishul akum. Peri Chadash, note 3. However, he disagrees on this and holds that things that come to conclude a meal that are not made to dip bread in them have no [prohibition of] bishul akum — see there. But Chochmat Adam, klal 66, note 1, wrote that a thing that comes to conclude a meal has [the prohibition of] bishul akum. And so Arukh HaShulchan, note 7: the view of the Tur and Shulchan Aruch is that when the Sages said "to dip bread in it," it is not specifically so, and it is the way of the matter — they said that an unimportant thing does not rise on the king's table to dip bread in it; but the essence depends on a thing of importance that rises on the king's table even if eaten by itself and not with bread; and he concluded that for practical law, since the Tur and Shulchan Aruch ruled to forbid, so we conclude.
