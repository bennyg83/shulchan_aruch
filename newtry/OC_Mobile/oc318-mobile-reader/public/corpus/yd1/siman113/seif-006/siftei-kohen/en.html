And even if it would not, etc. It appears difficult, for it is against the Gemara in Avodah Zarah (daf 38 side 1), and Beit Yosef brings that we say: if the non-Jew did not turn it over in it, it would not be cooked — it is bishul akum and forbidden; and so among the poskim and Beit Yosef; and also in Issur ve-Heter, general rule 43, halachah 9: if a Jew placed meat on coals where it was not fit to cook and roast, and a non-Jew came and turned it over and it cooked — permitted — end of his words; requires study. And it appears that Issur ve-Heter and the Rav hold that although by Gemara law it is forbidden in such a case, nevertheless also stoking and a chip are forbidden by Gemara law, for it would not cook without the aid of the non-Jew, and nevertheless we permit — only because we establish that just as a chip helps above in siman 112, seif 10, so too for bishul akum, as the Rif wrote in Semak and Issur ve-Heter, general rule 43. And if so, just as a chip for bread is not permitted by Gemara law, as the poskim wrote in siman 112, but we rely on what is written in Hilchot Minhagim between the people of Eretz Yisrael and Babylonia that it helps, and "leave Israel to be unwitting," etc., as the Rosh and Tur wrote in siman 112 — so too for bishul akum; and so the Ramban whom the Ran brought, chapter of Avodah Zarah — see there. And accordingly, that which the Rav did not emend anything in seif 10 against the Mechaber's words is because he relied on what he wrote before; and it is possible to say that here we deal where the Jew placed the coals, therefore even though it would not cook without the aid of the non-Jew it is permitted, for it is like lighting the fire in the gloss of seif 7 that is permitted — the reason being since the Jew made the flame. And below seif 10 deals where the non-Jew placed the coals, and therefore even though the Jew placed the meat on the coals, since it would not cook without the aid of the non-Jew, it is forbidden; but in Issur ve-Heter and Turei Chayim there it appears permitted in every case — see there; and also here it appears it is only permitted because the Jew placed the meat on the coals — investigate. And nevertheless for practical law requires study, since the Gemara states explicitly that where a Jew placed meat on coals and a non-Jew came and turned it over, it is forbidden if it would not cook without the aid of the non-Jew, and so the poskim — except that Issur ve-Heter and the Rav are lenient in this, and because they compare them to a chip and stoking; and one may say there is a dispute regarding a chip and stoking, as the Ran explicitly distinguishes there, chapter of Avodah Zarah — see there.
