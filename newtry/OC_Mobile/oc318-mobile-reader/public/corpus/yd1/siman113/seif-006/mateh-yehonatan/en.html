(Siman 113 in the Shulchan Aruch, seif 6 in the gloss.) And even if it would not cook, etc. See Taz, small note 6, and Shach, small note 8, in what they challenge — that this ruling is against the Gemara. And understand: that which the Gemara says that if it would not cook without the aid of gentiles it is forbidden — where a Jew does not do a complete action, but only the Jew places it on the fire and a non-Jew comes and turns it over — if so, the Jew does nothing and it is as if the non-Jew placed it on the fire from the outset without any aid of a Jew; not so when a Jew does a complete action, such as stirring it in such a case — the aid of a non-Jew forbids nothing.
