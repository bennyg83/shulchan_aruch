5) And even herbs and mushrooms have [the prohibition of] bishul akum, for they rise as relish. Issur ve-Heter there. Torat Chatat there. Darkei Moshe there. Shach there. Knesset HaGedolah there. Chochmat Adam, klal 66, note 4. However, Peri Chadash at the end of note 3 disagrees and wrote that herbs and mushrooms do not rise on the king's table and there is no [prohibition of] bishul akum; and so agreed Arukh HaShulchan, note 10. And Peri Toar, note 7, wrote that in our places herbs and mushrooms rise on the king's table and are for improvement, as Issur ve-Heter's view; and what Peri Chadash rejected from the Gemara is no proof, for nowadays they do rise — and everything is according to the place. And so Minchat Yitzchak in Sulam at the end of Torat Chatat, general rule 71, halachah 16. And so Birkei Yosef in Shivhei Yosef, note 9, in the name of Radak, that everything is according to place and time. And so Chochmat Adam there. And see below, note 10 and note 11.
6) Chestnuts (called in Arabic kastana) that a gentile picked are permitted, for they are eaten as they are when raw, even though most of their eating is by roasting and cooking. Birkei Yosef there, note 8, in the name of Radak. However, Peri Chadash, note 22, brought a dispute whether they are eaten raw; and therefore we follow this according to place and time, as written in the previous note: if most of that place eat them raw, there is no [prohibition of] bishul akum; if not, there is bishul akum.
7) There. Or as relish, etc. — so the Tur; and it appears he means that even though kings do not dip bread in it, since it rises on their table as relish — that is, to conclude a meal — it is forbidden because of bishul akum. Peri Chadash, note 3. However, he disagrees on this and holds that things that come to conclude a meal that are not made to dip bread in them have no [prohibition of] bishul akum — see there. But Chochmat Adam, klal 66, note 1, wrote that a thing that comes to conclude a meal has [the prohibition of] bishul akum. And so Arukh HaShulchan, note 7: the view of the Tur and Shulchan Aruch is that when the Sages said "to dip bread in it," it is not specifically so, and it is the way of the matter — they said that an unimportant thing does not rise on the king's table to dip bread in it; but the essence depends on a thing of importance that rises on the king's table even if eaten by itself and not with bread; and he concluded that for practical law, since the Tur and Shulchan Aruch ruled to forbid, so we conclude.
