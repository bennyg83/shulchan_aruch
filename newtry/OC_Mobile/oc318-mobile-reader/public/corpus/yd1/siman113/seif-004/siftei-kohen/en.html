With our maidservants. Those acquired by us — for the labor of a gentile's slave is a Jew's, and he is warned about it on Shabbos from the Torah, and he is not included among [gentiles] — end of his words, responsum of the Ramban whom Beit Yosef brought, and Darkei Moshe; and with those maidservants the Mechaber deals. But not with our maidservants in these lands, who are not acquired by us but only hired for a year, and we are not warned about them on Shabbos, as explained in Orach Chayim siman 344; and so the Bach. And accordingly, what the Rav wrote that b'dieved one may rely on those who permit — this does not speak of our maidservants. And one must say that he refers to the maidservants the Mechaber deals with, or that one may rely on those who permit bishul akum in a Jewish home — and this is the view of R' Avraham whom the Tur brought; and so in Darkei Moshe and Turei Chayim, general rule 71, halachah 17, in the name of Issur ve-Heter — and this is in Issur ve-Heter, general rule 43, halachah 13. Or the Rav was not precise in this, for the essence of the matter relies on the reason that it is impossible that one of the household will not stoke a little. And see in Or Torah of Maharshal, siman 87, who brings the responsum of the Ramban, siman 249: some of our teachers permit with maidservants because the prohibition of bishul akum applies only to one who acts willingly, because of the decree of feasts; but these maidservants and slaves of ours act under compulsion, whether they wish or not — there is no closeness of mind; nevertheless their words are not clear and we do not rely on this, and we practice prohibition even b'dieved — end of his words; and so in the responsum of the Rashba, siman 68. And accordingly one may say that the reasoning of the one who permits whom the Mechaber wrote is for this reason, and therefore the Rav wrote that one may rely on them b'dieved; except that in Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe this reason is not mentioned.
