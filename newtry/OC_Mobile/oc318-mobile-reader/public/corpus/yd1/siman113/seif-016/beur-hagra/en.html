Vessels, etc. As with all prohibitions of their [rabbinic] decrees that forbid their mixture — such as the oil of the gid, as written above siman 65 seif 9, and in siman 100 seif 2, and udder in siman 90, and broth of non-kosher fish in siman 83; and we say in Chullin (99b) that according to R' Yochanan it forbids nearly two hundred, and according to the Tanna Kamma until 495, as written in chapter 10 of Terumot; and we say in Chullin (98a): do not treat lightly, etc.; and likewise terumah of chutz la'aretz — according to Tosafot it forbids up to 101; and other prohibitions.
And some say it is not needed. For the decree of feasts does not apply in such a case; and it appears there is proof for his words from siman 112 seif 14; but it is not so, for regarding ipinadeh he forbids there seif 6, and here seif 16; and so the Rashba brought proof from ipinadeh that he forbids because of feasts.

And even, etc., if it is, etc. As written in Yerushalmi, first chapter of Terumot: it teaches, R' Chalifta ben Shaul: a pot in which terumah was cooked — one purges it in boiling water three times and that suffices. R' Ba said: and one does not learn from it regarding nevelah. R' Yosi challenged before R' Ba: terumah [entails] death penalty and nevelah [entails] lashes — and you say thus? He said to him: as those who say they accepted the tithes upon themselves of their own accord. Meaning: since it is rabbinic, they were lenient even though the Torah testified about the twenty-four [priestly gifts], etc. R' Yusti ben Shunam asked before R' Mana: we learned: if one pressed and squeezed it, behold it is terumah — and you say thus? Meaning: thus it is clear they were not lenient in it even in a slight matter. He said to him: here it is purged by fire and departs. Meaning: this is not so much of a leniency. And the Rashba wrote: and the same applies to all prohibitions of their decrees; but in prohibitions of their decrees that have no Torah root, like terumah nowadays, [one may be lenient]; but in prohibitions that have a Torah root, no — for blood that was salted is forbidden only rabbinically, as written in chapter 23 of Menachot (21b), and nevertheless R' Ami broke that bowl in Pesachim (111b); and therefore "because," etc.; and see Nekudot HaKesef in Bava Batra, chapter 8a.
