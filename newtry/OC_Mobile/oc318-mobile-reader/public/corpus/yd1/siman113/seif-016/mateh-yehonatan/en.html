(There, seif 16.) Vessels in which a non-Jew cooked, etc. And some are stringent that it is not necessary, etc. They challenge this from above, siman 112 seif 6, where the Mechaber wrote regarding ipinadeh that a non-Jew baked — that it is forbidden even though there is only absorbed [taste]; and if so, it is proven that absorbed [taste] is forbidden in a kli rishon, and he brought no distinction in this. And it appears one could say that it is not comparable to above, for there there is absorption of the dish in the bread itself, and such a case is what there is room to be concerned for; not so where there is only absorption in the pot — if so, the dish cooked afterward in this pot has only absorption of absorption, and for so much they did not decree because of bishul akum. However, the Tur in this siman includes the law of absorption in the dish itself and absorption in the pot in one stroke, and it appears he has no such distinction. And one must say that certainly there is room to dispute such a case; and that which we included them is in min b'mino; but in a different species where the taste is recognizable, certainly in such a case he forbids because of bishul akum — investigate. But the implication of the Tur's words does not appear thus, for behold he wrote explicitly: the same applies to vegetables cooked with meat — investigate.
