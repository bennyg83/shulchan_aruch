1) [Seif 1] A food that is not eaten as it is when raw, etc. — and the reason is that the essence of the decree is because of [intermarriage] feasts, and a thing that is not so important — a person does not invite his friend for it. Taz, small note 1. Lechem HaPanim, note 1; Binyamin Zeev, note 1. And according to this reason, the cooking of an apostate is permitted, for there is no [concern of] feasts; except that there is another reason explained in Beit Yosef, lest he feed him forbidden things; and accordingly, the cooking of an apostate is also forbidden, for he is like a denier of the entire Torah, and lest he feed him forbidden things — Sefer Tiferet Moshe. And accordingly, the same applies to an apostate who desecrates Shabbos in public, or [denies] the entire Torah except for two [commandments], whose law is like a non-Jew, as above siman 2 seif 5, and below siman 119 — his cooking is likewise forbidden. Pit'hei Teshuvah, note 1. And it is found that this matter is disputed according to the reasons; and if so, since it is a rabbinic matter, it appears that b'dieved one should not forbid. 10) A food eaten as it is when raw, and one person cannot eat it except roasted or cooked — his view is nullified before all people, since most of the world eat it as it is when raw, and it is permitted for him too; and likewise the reverse: if the majority do not eat it as it is when raw, even though this person eats it as it is when raw, there is [the prohibition of] bishul akum, and for him too it is forbidden.
