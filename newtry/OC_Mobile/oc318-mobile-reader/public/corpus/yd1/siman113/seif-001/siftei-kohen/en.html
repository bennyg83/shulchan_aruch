And also rises on the king's table, etc. Since the Mechaber did not stipulate that it must be changed from its natural state by fire, it is clear that he holds that even if it was not changed, it is forbidden — and like the view of the Rambam whom he brought in Beit Yosef; and so the view of the Rav in Darkei Moshe and in Turei Chayim, general rule 71, halachah 9; and this is his view in the gloss, seif 2, that he only permitted the peas because they do not rise on the king's table, and not because they were not changed from their nature, as written in Darkei Moshe and Turei Chayim there. But in truth the essence of the Gemara appears (at the end of Avodah Zarah daf 37) like the Ran and Rashi, that it is not forbidden because of bishul akum except when changed from its nature by fire; and so in Hagahot Alfasi, chapter of Avodah Zarah; and so appears the view of the Rif, as the Ran wrote; and so appears the view of Raavan, siman 53. However, the view of other poskim appears that one should not distinguish in this:
Rises on the king's table.
