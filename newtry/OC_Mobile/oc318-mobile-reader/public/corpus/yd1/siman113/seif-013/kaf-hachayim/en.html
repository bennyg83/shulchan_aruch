13) Lupines have no [prohibition of] bishul akum. Rambam, chapter 17 of Hilchot Maachalot Assurot, halachah 15. Peri Chadash, note 2. And because of gentile vessels (meaning: that it was cooked in their intestines) it is a dispute, and one should be stringent. Maharal, siman 138. Maharikash in glosses on the Shulchan Aruch. However, what he wrote "and because of gentile vessels," etc. — see below siman 122, seif 6, and in the gloss that wrote that if he cooked for him, it is permitted to buy from him l'chatchila — see there. However, Rabbeinu HaAri, z"l, in Sha'ar HaMitzvot, parashat Vayelech, wrote that one must be careful not to eat lupines boiled by gentiles for seasoning; and although by law they are permitted because they do not rise on the king's table, nevertheless he is not inferior to that man (see Moed Katan daf 12 side 2) to whom Eliyahu, z"l, did not appear because he drank water that his Aramean brother gave him — end of his words. However, what he wrote "by law they are permitted," etc. — this is if he cooked in a Jewish vessel or in his vessel to sell to a non-Jew; but if he cooked in his vessel to sell to a Jew, by law it is forbidden because of gentile vessels, as will be explained in our words there — see there.
