And permitted, etc. As written 37b: wheat that one made into parched grain is permitted, and the same applies to these; and as Rambam wrote that a person does not invite his friend for parched grain — and therefore it is not, etc.
(Collection) And permitted, etc., and likewise, etc. Gemara 37b, 38b: the baraita, etc., and parched grain; and likewise of legumes, as written, the latter, etc., in Talfachi, etc. (until here).
(Collection) And permitted, etc., that they are not, etc. Rambam; and in the Gemara (37b) they said because they were not changed, etc.; and Rambam holds that for the conclusion that it is not derived from a verse, therefore one must say because of this reason; but the Ran wrote that the above reason that it was not changed remains, since they relied on a verse — we require a similarity to the verse (and in the end of chapter 4 of Shabbos and chapter 2 of Moed Katan — requires study); and so Rashi 35b, s.v. vehachamin, etc., and s.v. vekalyot, etc.; and the Ran wrote: except that I have not seen among the early authorities who explained that everything that was not changed by fire is permitted (until here).
And all fruits, etc. As written there 38b in oil, even though they were at first olives. Darkei Moshe.
