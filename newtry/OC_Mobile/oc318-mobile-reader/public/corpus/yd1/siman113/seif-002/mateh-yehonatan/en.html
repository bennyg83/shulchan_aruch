(There, seif 8.) A Jew placed a pot on the fire, etc. See Shach, small note 12. And behold, the proof of the Bach is that one must say that if a non-Jew removed it, then there is no [prohibition] of bishul akum in it; for otherwise it would be difficult — that which we say in the chapter of Avodah Zarah, daf 38 side 1, there: for example, he placed it on a tile and the non-Jew took it and placed it in the oven, etc. And it is impossible according to the Rashba's view; if so, it should have stated a greater novelty that even if the non-Jew himself removed it and returned and placed it in the oven — or that it is not forbidden thereby. And one must say that the Rashba holds that the Gemara did not descend to teach us only the law itself, that where the end of the cooking comes through a non-Jew it is forbidden, and it only used the language of placing on a tile; but in truth it is a dispute, and even if the non-Jew alone removed it, it is likewise forbidden. And the proof of the Rashba is from what it says in the first chapter of Avodah Zarah, daf 12: I did not go to draw water from your spring, and I saw a Jew and a non-Jew who cooked two pots, etc., and the Sages were not concerned because of bishul akum. And the Rashba explained that there is room to be concerned lest the non-Jew removed the pot and returned it on the fire; and from now one must say that even if the non-Jew alone removed and returned it, it is likewise forbidden. And the Bach must say that he explains like Rashi, that all the concern is lest he come to cook l'chatchila without aid.
[Gloss: And from the words of the Rashba in Torat HaBayit, house 3, gate 7, it appears that he does not explain thus; for if so, he should have brought from there that we are not concerned lest a non-Jew removed it, and he need not have brought from that in chapter 2 of a Jew who placed, etc. Investigate. End of gloss of the great rabbi, our teacher R' Shlomo HaKohen, may his light shine, of Vilna.]
