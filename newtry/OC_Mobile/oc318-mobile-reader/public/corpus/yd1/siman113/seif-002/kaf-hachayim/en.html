8) A rice dish has [the prohibition of] bishul akum. Peri Chadash there, and note 5. Lechem HaPanim, note 5. Binyamin Zeev, at the end of note 5. Chochmat Adam there, note 2. Zivchei Tzedek, note 5.
11) And if there are places that eat them raw and places that do not eat them raw — each follows his place. Peri Chadash there. Sulam there. Lechem HaPanim there. Binyamin Zeev there. Birkei Yosef there. Chochmat Adam there. Arukh HaShulchan there. Zivchei Tzedek, note 9.
12) And an important person must be stringent with bishul akum even with a thing eaten as it is when raw, as we say in Shabbos daf 51 side 1. Maharikash in glosses on the Shulchan Aruch; and so Shach below, siman 152, small note 2. However, Knesset HaGedolah in Hagahot Beit Yosef in this siman, note 14, lengthy on this and wrote that even an important person need not be stringent in this. And so Peri Chadash at the end of note 3. Lechem HaPanim, note 2. Peri Toar below, siman 114, note 70. Erech HaShulchan, note 3. Derech Emunah. Arukh HaShulchan, note 11. However, Rav Mazmor le-David in this siman wrote that for practical law an important person should be stringent, since the Rif and Rosh copied there that statement of R' Ami as well — end of his words. And so from the words of the Ari, z"l, written in the note afterward that one should be stringent. And so the view of Birkei Yosef in Shivhei Yosef, note 4. And see further in our words in Orach Chayim, siman 257, note 34.
14) Milk that a gentile cooked and made into cheese has no [concern of] bishul akum. Knesset HaGedolah in Hagahot Beit Yosef, note 26. Binyamin Zeev, note 5.
15) Cheese has no [prohibition of] bishul akum, since it is eaten as it is when raw; and even a person who does not eat cheese raw — if it was cooked by a non-Jew, it is permitted for him, for his view is nullified before all people. Maharikash in the responsa called Ohalei Yaakov, siman 58. Birkei Yosef, note 1; and so above, note 10, in the name of several poskim — see there.
