A non-Jew who cooked and did not intend for cooking is permitted. How so? A non-Jew who kindled a fire in a marsh to pass through the reeds and locusts were cooked in it — behold, these are permitted, even in a place where they are served on a king's table. And likewise if he singed the head to pass the hair — it is permitted to eat the tips of the ears that were roasted at the time of the singeing. But if he intended for cooking — for example, he kindled the oven to cook in it and there was meat in it at first and it was roasted — even though he did not intend for this meat, since he did not know of it, it is forbidden.
