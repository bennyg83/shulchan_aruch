To evade the tax. This means a tax that is theft even according to dina demalchuta; but what is according to dina demalchuta is certainly forbidden to steal, since he leased the right from the king. The Tur permits here because it is a davar she'eino mitkaven; the Mishnah that forbids follows R. Yehudah, who forbids unintended acts, while we rule like R. Shimon, who permits. It resembles clothing sellers below. In siman 300 the Beit Yosef cites Rabbenu Yerucham's question on the Rosh, who forbids wrapping kilayim on one's hand while leading animals: why is that not a davar she'eino mitkaven like here? One cannot answer that there it is forbidden because it is psik reisha, since he must warm himself when wrapping it, for all the more so here he wears it outright, and yet the Rosh and Tur permit. It appears to me that although Pesachim concludes that according to R. Shimon it is permitted even where he could separate from the prohibition and does not, provided he does not intend benefit, as proven from clothing sellers, this applies only where the prohibited object already exists and he does not avoid it temporarily. But one may not make kilayim in order to bring it upon himself permanently, relying that he does not intend wearing but only leading animals. Since it is made for that purpose forever, that is its primary bringing upon himself and is called a manner of wearing. Therefore animal ropes made from the start to wrap on his hand during leading are forbidden to make of kilayim. This differs from clothing sellers and similar cases, where the garments were not originally made to be upon him at the time of sale; that use is temporary. This is proven in the Gemara's language regarding animal ropes, "provided he does not wrap and tie": it mentions wrapping before tying to teach that the tying serves the wrapping, since everyone who ties animal ropes intends afterward to wrap them on his hand while leading. This seems very correct in reasoning and proof.
