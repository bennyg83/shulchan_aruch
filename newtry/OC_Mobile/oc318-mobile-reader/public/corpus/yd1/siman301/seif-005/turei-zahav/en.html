To steal the tax. Meaning a tax that is gezelah even according to dina d'malchuta; but that which is according to dina d'malchuta is certainly forbidden to steal, since he hired it from the king. And in the Tur it is written here to permit, and the reason is that it is a davar she'eino mitkaven. And that which they forbade it in the Mishnah is according to Rabbi Yehudah, who holds that a davar she'eino mitkaven is forbidden; but we hold like Rabbi Shimon, who holds it is permitted. And it is similar to sellers of garments nearby, which is permitted. And in siman 300 at the end, the Beit Yosef brought the words of Rabbeinu Yerucham, who asked on what the Rosh wrote there, that it is forbidden to wrap kilayim on his hand when driving the animals — for this is a davar she'eino mitkaven like here. And one cannot say that therefore it is forbidden there because it is a psik reisha, for it is impossible that he not be warmed when he wraps — for a fortiori here, where he actually wears it, and even so it is permitted according to the Rosh and Tur. And it appears to me to answer: even though we conclude in Perek Kol Sha'ah (Pesachim daf 26) that according to Rabbi Shimon it is permitted when it is possible to separate from the issur, and he does not separate, except that he does not intend to benefit from it, and they bring a proof from this of sellers of clothing here, that it is possible to do like the modest who sling it over, etc., and he does not do so, and even so it is permitted — nevertheless, that is only where the issur is already done, except that this one does not separate from it for the time being; but certainly he should not make an issur of kilayim to put it on always on the reliance that he will not intend to wear it except for driving the animals, since it is made for this forever, this is the main putting-on of it, and it is called a manner of wearing. For this reason, regarding those ropes of animals that from the beginning of their making are made so that he wrap them on his hand at the time of driving the animals, as is the way of every person in this, that they be upon him with a firm knot — it is certainly forbidden to make them of kilayim. Which is not so regarding those sellers of garments and the like, which from the outset are not made to be upon him at the time of their sale, for this is only temporary, and the main purpose of their making is not for this; therefore it is permitted when not intending. And this matter is compelled from the Gemara, that they said regarding kilayim of ropes above, "provided that he not wrap and tie" — for it was difficult: the Talmud should have preceded the tying, for it precedes the wrapping; rather, perforce we take it to instruct that the tying is for the need of the wrapping, and therefore it preceded to it the wrapping, which is the purpose of the tying, for whoever ties the ropes of an animal, his intention is that afterwards he will always wrap them on his hands at the time of driving. This is what appears to him very correct from the side of reason and from the side of the proof. And many wearied themselves to find an answer to this difficulty, and what appears to my humble opinion clear I have written according to the view of the Tur.
