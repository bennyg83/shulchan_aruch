And likewise clothing sellers. See the Taz s.k. 7 and Shach s.k. 8, who explain why this is not difficult from the previous seif, where even to evade the tax is forbidden. See responsa Chavot Yair siman 188. See also Merkavat HaMishneh, chapter 1 of Hilchot Shabbat and chapter 10 of Hilchot Kilayim, law 18, who challenges the Taz and Chavot Yair and writes that the Shach's words are also strained. He explains that a davar she'eino mitkaven is permitted even when the prohibited act is actually done, for where coins scattered before avodah zarah, it is forbidden only because of marit ayin even though he actually bows, and there is no greater psik reisha. The main principle is that it is considered psik reisha when he intends a permitted result that cannot be accomplished without the prohibited act, as here where he intends to wear in order to evade the tax and cannot wear without warming himself; his intent to wear is therefore like intent to warm. Likewise, placing a donkey-saddle on his shoulders to carry dung is a psik reisha. By contrast, clothing sellers and tailors do not intend to bring it upon themselves at all, only to carry, show, or sew; since their intent is only that work, it is davar she'eino mitkaven, like gathering coins before avodah zarah, except that there marit ayin applies, while here everyone knows he is occupied with his work. See there at length.
