Permitted. The Shach writes that in all the cases the Rav mentioned it is permitted even rabbinically. However, it appears that this is only where he does not touch them.
And some say. The Shach writes that this requires study: what is the meaning of "some say," since no one disputes that rabbinic kilayim may be sat upon when hard, and even wearing hard items is permitted. The Taz writes that the Perishah explains that a women's cap of samet, with felt sewn between the samet, is permitted. According to the Rama's statement that lechatchilah one should not make it of kilayim, it would have been proper to protest against the craftsmen, and all the more according to the stringent view in seif 2. However, the Rashba already wrote in a similar case: leave them; better they be inadvertent, etc. It also appears from the Rama that there is no prohibition to sit in wagons whose canopy has linen fabric sewn to wool fabric, called paklit. But this is difficult: why is it different from a curtain by the doorway in seif 11, which involves kilayim because the attendant sometimes warms himself with it? Here too the side of the paklit warms the one sitting next to it in cold weather, and this is a full prohibition, unlike a tub-cover, where he does not touch it at all. This is especially so when many sit there and one is pressed to that side. I have heard that some men of practice avoid sitting in such wagons, though most people are lenient. It seems they rely on distancing themselves from touching those sides, and if he sometimes touches them it is a davar she'eino mitkaven. Still, warning is needed as much as possible.
