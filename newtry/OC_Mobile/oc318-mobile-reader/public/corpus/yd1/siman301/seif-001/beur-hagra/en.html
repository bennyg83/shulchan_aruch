And likewise it is permitted, etc. Even rabbinically. This is from the Yerushalmi at the end of the chapter, brought by the Rash there: tents are not subject to kilayim.
And likewise to spread, etc. This follows from what is stated at the end of the first chapter of Yom Tov: "only with a curtain," and the Gemara asks, etc. If otherwise, it would be forbidden in any case, since it retains heat and protects from sun and rain. Although we learned regarding sellers, etc., "provided," etc., and tailors, etc., there the issue is that it is a manner of wearing and bringing upon oneself, as the Mordechai writes. So too what is written in seif 12, "one should not take," etc.; this is why he says that it is not forbidden, etc.
When is this said, etc. From the Gemara there and the beginning of the seventh chapter of Yoma.
But pillows, etc. From the Mishnah there.
But pillows and cushions that are hard, etc. However, Tosafot, Mordechai, Hagahot Mordechai of Shabbat, and Semak did not write the reason because they are hard, but because wrapping does not apply to them, and likewise with a donkey-saddle.
When is this said, when they are, etc. Yerushalmi there, brought by the Rash, Rosh, and other poskim. The Rambam omitted it because of our Gemara, which says only "with hard ones," etc., and does not distinguish. Tosafot already explained there that the case concerns rabbinic kilayim, proving from the beginning of Arakhin, and they wrote likewise in Shabbat and Semak. This follows their view that felt is forbidden only rabbinically, as in the sugya in Niddah. This is why he writes, "and some say all this," etc. But it is not exact, for according to the Rambam it is permitted in every case, and what he wrote in seif 2, "all hard garments," etc., and then "some are stringent," means according to the Rambam, who holds felt is forbidden by Torah law.
Nevertheless, etc. Because sometimes he removes what is inside them and covers himself with them. This is from there, and it implies that the case concerns Torah-level kilayim.
