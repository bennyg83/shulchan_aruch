See responsa Panim Meirot, part 2, siman 112, who discusses this at length and concludes that one may sit on pillows and cushions sewn with kilayim that are placed inside coaches for sitting. The Shulchan Aruch forbids filled pillows only like the pillows on which we sleep, which bend around the sides of a person. But pillows placed in a wagon and tightly stuffed with hair, so that they cannot bend around the sides, raise no concern even if they are soft to sit on. All the more so one may sit on chairs sewn with kilayim where the cushion is attached to the wood with nails and cannot in any way come around the sides of a person. Likewise one may sit in coaches even though their sides contain sha'atnez, since this is neither the manner of wearing nor of bringing upon oneself. One should be stringent only not to sit against the coach curtain if it is kilayim. Similarly, if embroidered fabrics are fixed to walls above, below, and at the sides with nails, one may sit next to them and lean on them. Although the Magen Avraham at the end of siman 638 writes not to hang sha'atnez in a sukkah unless it is higher than human use, there he speaks of items hanging like curtains and not fixed to the wall; fixed items are not a concern.
