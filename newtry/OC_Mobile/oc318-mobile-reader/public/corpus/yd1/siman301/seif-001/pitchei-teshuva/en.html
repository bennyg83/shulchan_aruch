See Responsa Panim Meirot, part 2, siman 112, which discusses this at length and concludes that it is permitted to sit on cushions and pillows sewn with kilayim that are placed inside coaches for sitting. The Shulchan Aruch's prohibition regarding filled cushions refers to cushions like those upon which we lie, which bend up along a person's sides. But cushions placed in a wagon and packed tightly with hair so that they cannot bend upward on the sides present no concern, even if they are soft to sit upon. Certainly one may sit on chairs sewn with kilayim where the cushion is attached to the wood with nails and cannot possibly come up along the sides of the body. Likewise one may sit in coaches even when the side panels contain shaatnez, since this is neither a manner of wearing nor of placing upon the body. One should be stringent only about sitting directly against the curtain of a coach when the curtain itself is kilayim. The same applies if embroidered fabrics are fixed to walls by nails above, below, and at the sides: one may sit near them and lean against them. Although Magen Avraham, end of siman 638, writes that shaatnez should not be hung in a sukkah unless it is above the range of human use, there he refers to material hung like a curtain and not fixed to the wall. Where it is fixed, there is no concern. See there.
Regarding a bed. Meaning a bed of ropes that sags beneath a person, causing the sides of the pillow to rise around him. But a bed of hard wooden boards does not sag so. Thus writes the Rash in the final chapter of Kilayim, and Tosafot Yom Tov there. To me it appears that the same concern applies even to a wooden bed if the pillow is wide and the bed narrow, so that when the pillow reaches the bed's sides it bends downward there and rises around the person.
