To spread over him as a tent, etc. Although it protects him from sun or cold, it is permitted because this is not a manner of wearing. This differs from seif 6, where protection from the sun is forbidden because it is in a manner of wearing.
And rabbinically it is forbidden, etc. Since the Rama and Tur permit making a tent with what is permitted by Torah law, it sounds that rabbinically it is forbidden even as a tent, lest he sometimes cover himself with that tent if it is near him above or at his sides. But this is difficult, for then with spreading it over a tub he should also have said that it is permitted only by Torah law, since the Rama grouped them together. That case is permitted even rabbinically, as the Mordechai in the first chapter of Beitzah writes that Rabbenu Yoel permitted it. Rather, tent and wagon permissions are even rabbinic. The Tur relied on the fact that in the latter clause he mentioned rabbinic prohibition regarding bedding underneath, so the other permissions remain as Torah law permits. Therefore there is no prohibition to sit in wagons whose canopy is sewn from linen and wool, called paklit. Still, it is difficult why this differs from a doorway curtain in seif 11, which has kilayim because the attendant sometimes warms himself with it. Here too, the side of the paklit warms those seated beside it in cold weather, and that is a real prohibition, unlike the tub-cover, where one does not touch it. I have heard that some men of practice avoid those wagons, though most are lenient. They appear to rely on distancing themselves from touching the sides; and since there is no Torah-level kilayim here, as it is not shua, tavui, and nuz together, care not to touch suffices. If he sometimes touches it, it is a davar she'eino mitkaven, but warning is still needed as much as possible.
Placed upon a bed. Meaning a rope bed.
It is permitted to sit on them in every way. Meaning even though it bends beneath him and resembles wearing. The same applies to actual wearing, which is permitted with hard felt, as the Rama himself writes immediately afterward, and so the Rosh wrote explicitly, brought by the Beit Yosef. The Perishah writes that according to this a women's samet cap with felt sewn between the samet is permitted. According to the Rama's statement that lechatchilah one should not make it of kilayim, one should have protested against the craftsmen, and all the more according to the stringent view in seif 2, namely the Rambam, who holds that shua alone is Torah-level as written in siman 300. But the Rashba already wrote in a similar case, brought by the Beit Yosef: leave them; better they be inadvertent, etc.
