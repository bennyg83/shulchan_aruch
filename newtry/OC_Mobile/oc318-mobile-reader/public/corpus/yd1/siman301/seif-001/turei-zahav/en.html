He has to be called Ahl. It is permitted that it is not a way of evacuating it from the warm or cold, because it is not a way of evacuating such negotiations in the section and that it is forbidden to protect against the warm, because it is a way to wear:
And from the words of the Soferim it is forbidden etc. Since the Rama wrote, and likewise in the Tur he permitted making an ohel with a d'oraisa heter, it implies that d'rabbanan it is forbidden even with a tent, lest he sometimes come to cover himself with that tent if it is close to him above or at the sides. But it is difficult: if so, also regarding spreading over the vat he should have said that it is permitted specifically from the Torah, for the Rama cut them with one stroke. And this is permitted even d'rabbanan, for it is written in the Mordechai, first chapter of Beitzah, that Rabbeinu Yoel permitted it. Therefore it must be that the heter of the ohel and the wagon is permitted even d'rabbanan. And the Tur relied [on this], since he wrote in the seifa the d'rabbanan prohibition regarding a mattress beneath him, automatically the other permissions remain according to Torah law, since this reason of the prohibition does not apply there. This appears to me clear. And if so, there is no prohibition to sit on those wagons that have a tent sewn of a linen garment with a wool garment, and they call it paklit. But it is difficult from what he wrote regarding a curtain over the doorway in seif 11, that there is kilayim in it, because the shamash is sometimes warmed by it; and likewise here, the sides of this paklit, one who sits next to them is warmed by them in cold weather, and it is a complete prohibition. And it is not similar to a vat, for there that covering which covers the vat does not touch at all, which is not so here at the sides of the wagon, especially when many sit there and one is pushed next to the sides of the paklit. And in truth I have heard that some people of deed are careful not to sit in those wagons, but most of the world practice a heter regarding it. And it appears that they rely on distancing themselves from touching those sides. And even though it is a difficult thing to be careful about this, nevertheless, since there is no kilayim d'oraisa here, for it is not shua, tavui, and nuz together, therefore it is sufficient to be careful not to touch this; and even if he sometimes touches it, it is called something he does not intend and is permitted. But in any case a warning is required in this as much as possible. So it appears to me.

It is permitted to sit upon them in any manner. Meaning even though it bends under him and it is like wearing, and the same law applies to actual wearing, which is permitted with hard felts, as the Rama himself wrote immediately afterward. And so the Asheri wrote explicitly, and the Beit Yosef brought it. And the Prisha wrote: and according to this, regarding a women's hat of velvet (samut) in which they sew felt between the velvet, it is permitted; see there. And according to what the Rama wrote, that l'chatchila one should not make it kilayim, it would be proper to protest against the craftsmen in this; and all the more so according to those who are stringent, as the Rama wrote in seif 2, namely the Rambam, who holds that shua alone is d'oraisa, as he wrote in siman 300. But the Rashba already wrote — and the Beit Yosef brought it — regarding similar matters: leave them be; it is better that they be shogegim etc.
