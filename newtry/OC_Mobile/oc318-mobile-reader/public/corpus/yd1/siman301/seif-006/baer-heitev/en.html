In their normal manner. The Taz explains: although while sewing the garment rests on their knees as tailors normally do, it is permitted because a davar she'eino mitkaven is permitted. This contradicts the nearby ruling that one may not wear kilayim to evade the tax, which is also a davar she'eino mitkaven, as stated explicitly at the beginning of HaGozel Batra. One must distinguish: with the tax, he benefits from the wearing, because without the wearing he would have to pay the tax; that benefit is like the benefit of anyone else who wears kilayim. But clothing sellers do not depend on wearing it for the sale; they do so only to ease their load or show its size, and it could be done another way. Since he does not intend benefit, it is permitted. The careful reader of the Gemara on 113 will find difficulty, because even regarding the tax it is still called davar she'eino mitkaven. One must say that this was said only according to the questioner; according to the answerer it is considered intended, yet it is permitted to evade the tax when the tax has no fixed measure.
