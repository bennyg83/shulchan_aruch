And likewise clothing sellers sell in their normal manner. For he does not wear it in the manner of clothing. But in seif 5 he wears it in the manner of clothing; this is how the Beit Yosef distinguishes according to the Rambam. This is difficult to me: what difference does actual wearing make if it is a davar she'eino mitkaven? Rather, it seems that in seif 5, since he intentionally wears it as clothing in order to be exempt from the tax, because tax is not taken from clothing, this is considered intended, for his main intent is that it be in the manner of clothing. Here his intent is only that people see and buy. This sets the Mishnah well according to the Rambam, for it is strained to say that two mishnayot in one chapter dispute each other. However, it is difficult according to the permissive view in seif 6: if even actual wearing without intent is permitted, why is it forbidden in seif 8 to place a donkey-saddle on one's shoulder to carry dung? That too is a davar she'eino mitkaven, and the permissive authorities also forbid the saddle for carrying dung, as explicit in the Rosh, Tur, Rabbenu Yerucham, and Ateret Zekenim. It appears that carrying dung is not a davar she'eino mitkaven, for he takes the saddle only so his clothes should not become dirty, intending benefit by protecting his clothes. It resembles the rule below in seif 12 that one may not take a hot egg with a kilayim cloth. This is unlike the Ateret Zekenim, who considered it a davar she'eino mitkaven. Examine carefully.
