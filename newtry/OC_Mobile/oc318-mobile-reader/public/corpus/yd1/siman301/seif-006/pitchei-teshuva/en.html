And likewise sellers of clothing. See Taz subparagraph 7 and Shach subparagraph 8, what they wrote to resolve that it not be a difficulty against what he wrote in the previous seif, that even to evade the tax it is forbidden. And see in the teshuva of Chavos Yair, and siman 188, what he wrote on this. And see in the book Merkavat HaMishneh, chapter 1 of Hilchot Shabbat and chapter 10 of Hilchot Kilayim, law 18, who challenged the Taz and the Chavos Yair, and wrote that the words of the Shach as well are forced. And he, of blessed memory, wrote that certainly something one does not intend is permitted even though he does the actual prohibition; for if one's coins were scattered before avodah zarah, it is forbidden only because of mar'it ayin, even though he is actually bowing, and there is no psik reisha greater than this. Rather the main reasoning that it is not considered psik reisha is when he intends some permitted matter, and it is impossible to do this permitted matter that he intends unless he does a forbidden matter — as in our case, where he intends to wear in order to evade the tax, and it is impossible to wear without becoming warmed; therefore what he intends to wear is considered as if he intends to warm, and it is psik reisha. And likewise one who places a saddle-cloth upon his shoulders to take out a load upon it is considered psik reisha. Which is not so regarding sellers and sewers of clothing, whose intent is not at all to place them upon themselves, but only to carry and transport — it is not considered psik reisha even though he actually places it on his shoulder, since his intent is only to transport, and it is possible to transport it in his hand and on a stick so that he not become warmed. And similarly regarding those who carry a corpse and an animal, whose intent is only to transport. And the same law applies to sewers of clothing, whose intent is only to sew, and it is utterly impossible to sew without placing it on his knees; therefore even if he places it on his knees it is considered something he does not intend, similar to if his coins were scattered before avodah zarah, which would be considered something he does not intend regarding bowing, since it is possible to gather without bowing — except that there it is forbidden because of mar'it ayin, which is not so regarding sellers and sewers of clothing, where there is no mar'it ayin, for all know that he is occupied with his work and does not intend.
