If it has, etc. In the Yerushalmi there: this was said where he intends it as clothing, etc.; the Rash and Rosh bring it there, and the Rosh explains similarly.
If it has, etc. The Tur's wording is difficult to understand. If he intends, it is forbidden anyway, like hand towels, taking a hot egg with a cloth, and many similar cases, even if it is not made like a garment. If he does not intend, it is always permitted, as with clothing sellers. The Rosh explains the Yerushalmi as defining how one can intend a towel for clothing: in some places their towels have a head-opening and one wears it like a garment. This requires great study. Protecting his clothes from hair is not considered benefit; only protecting his body from sun and rain is benefit. The Shach's words at the end of s.k. 5 and the Taz's words in s.k. 5 are not correct, and the Taz wrongly challenged the Perishah. The Shach's question from carrying dung can be answered: anything sometimes used for benefit is always forbidden, as with hand towels, book towels, and a sponge, and as in seif 9 in the gloss. Even the first tanna disputes only because that use is rare. This is why it says not to place the saddle, etc., even, etc.: the first clause is not because of carrying dung but for benefit, and it says that although underneath him is permitted even for benefit, on top of him is forbidden, and not only that but even to carry, etc.
