If it has a head-opening, etc. The reason is that this cloth was originally made not as a garment but to protect from hair dirt during haircutting. Therefore, if it has no head-opening, it does not have the name of a garment, and now he does not warm himself with it, so it is permitted. But if it has a receptacle/opening, it has the name of clothing and was initially made as clothing, meaning it is soft; even if he benefits only by protection from dirt, it is forbidden. The rule is that anything soft and wearable has kilayim even without warmth, for the Torah did not write warmth but "it shall not come upon you," and the Yerushalmi expounds that there be no bodily benefit. Items not worn on the body, such as hand towels, are forbidden because sometimes they warm the hands; all the more so a real garment placed on him or his shoulder to protect from rain is certainly Torah-forbidden.
In the Derishah it is written in the name of Maharshal, quoting Rabbenu Chananel, etc. I am astonished why he brings this, since we do not rule that way, for seif 3 explicitly permits against his words.
