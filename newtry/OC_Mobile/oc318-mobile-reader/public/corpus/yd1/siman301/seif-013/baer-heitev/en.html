Kilayim. The Taz writes that the Derishah asked: above in seif 3 a large garment with kilayim on one side is forbidden even on the other side, so why here may the rest of the foot benefit from kilayim at the heel? He answers that this is not difficult. Only with a soft garment did they say it is forbidden even without warmth; something not a garment is forbidden only when it warms. Here it has no garment-name, since leather is underneath and only opposite the heel is wool. If it warmed, it would be forbidden because of warmth, but since there is no warmth and it is not a garment, it is permitted. This also explains the Rama's wording about a shoe that is kilayim and has no heel: it resembles our pantoffel slippers, where around the heel there is no shoe leather at all. Therefore they permitted putting kilayim under the shoe leather above the foot or beneath where the foot stands, because the kilayim in the shoe does not have the name of a garment. If there were also garment around the heel, it would be forbidden as clothing. Thus people go out in pantoffels with fabric inside on all sides, even if a non-Jew sewed them with linen thread. But if this was done in a shoe that has leather around the heel too, and fabric sewn there, it is forbidden as a kilayim garment, because the fabric sewn inside is considered like an independent garment without the shoe leather. Socks with linen threads require removing the thread before wearing. A curtain called parhang made in houses may not be made of kilayim. Beit Hillel.
