Ardilarin, etc. The Tur writes in the name of the Geonim that people wore them under their shoes, placing processed goat leather underneath their soles, and opposite the heel some made it of wool; there is no kilayim because the heel is hard and does not warm. The Derishah asked from seif 3, where a large garment with kilayim on one side is forbidden even on the other side. In my humble opinion this is not difficult: only with a wearable garment did they say that soft material is forbidden even without warmth. Something not a garment is forbidden only where it warms. Here it is not called a garment, since leather lies below and only opposite the heel is wool. If it warmed it would be forbidden because of warmth, but since there is no warmth and it is not a garment, it is permitted. This also explains the Rambam's wording, copied by the Rama, about a kilayim shoe without a heel. It appears to be like our pantoffel slippers, where around the heel there is no shoe leather at all; therefore they allowed kilayim under the upper shoe leather and beneath the foot, because the kilayim in the shoe is not a garment. If there were fabric around the heel too, it would be forbidden as clothing. This is why people go out with pantoffels that have fabric inside on all sides, even if sewn by a non-Jew with linen thread. But if this is done in a shoe whose leather also surrounds the heel, and fabric is sewn there, it is forbidden as a kilayim garment, for the inner fabric is viewed as an independent garment without the shoe leather. The Bach's wording here contains a slip of the pen, for he wrote the opposite, that even without a heel it is forbidden, whereas it is permitted.
