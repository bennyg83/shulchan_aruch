With regard to forbidden relations involving blood relatives, etc. Meaning, this is the law governing converts: If, while still a non-Jew, he violated the law binding upon him and married his mother or his maternal sister, and they then converted, they are separated permanently. Were beit din not to protest, although he had violated his law while a non-Jew, he would nevertheless say, 'We have passed from a more stringent sanctity, etc., for previously I acted while it was forbidden and now it has become permitted to me.' If he had married another forbidden relative while a non-Jew, even a maternal relative, and he and his wife converted together, since the prohibition is only because of the rabbinic decree applicable to Jews, lest confusion result, and he was already married to her, they are not separated permanently. They are separated only for three months, to distinguish between seed conceived in sanctity and seed conceived outside sanctity, as stated below in seif 9.
