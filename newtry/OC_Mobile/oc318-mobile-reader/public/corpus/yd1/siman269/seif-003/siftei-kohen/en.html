A non-Jew is forbidden to maternal relatives, etc. Meaning, if he was not already married to a maternal relative and then converted, he is forbidden to marry any maternal relative, as will be explained. The Atteres Zahav writes: In this case it appears to me that even if he married her, he must divorce her, since he married her in violation of Jewish law, which is more stringent. See below, seif 5.
Some prohibit his father's wife. From Tosafos and the Rosh it appears that one should also be stringent regarding his father's sister, in accordance with the opinion that descendants of Noah are forbidden to her. The Bach rules stringently in accordance with both views.
But if his brother married her while he was a non-Jew, she is permitted. She is not considered his brother's wife, because his brother's marriage did not create kiddushin while he was a non-Jew.
