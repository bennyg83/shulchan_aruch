A non-Jew is forbidden to [marry] his mother's relatives, etc. Meaning: if he was not already married to a relative of his mother and then converted — it is forbidden for him to marry any of his mother's relatives, as will be explained. And the Levush wrote regarding this: it appears to me that even if he married [such a woman] he must divorce her, since he married in violation [of the prohibition]; for Jewish law is more stringent. End of his words. See below seif 5.
And some forbid [marriage to] his father's wife. And from the words of Tosafot and Rosh it appears one should also be stringent regarding his father's sister, according to the opinion that it is forbidden for Noahides; and so Bach ruled according to both of their views.
But if his brother married her when he was a non-Jew, she is permitted. For she is not his brother's wife — he had no legal bond with her, since he married her while he was a non-Jew.
