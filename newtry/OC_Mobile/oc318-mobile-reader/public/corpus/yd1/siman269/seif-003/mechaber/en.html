A gentile is forbidden in his mother's other relatives after he converted by rabbinic decree; and he is permitted in his father's other relatives even though he knows certainly that this is his father's relative — such as twins, for it is a clear matter that this one's father is that one's father; even so they did not decree on his father's other relatives; therefore a convert may marry his father's brother's wife, his father's brother's wife, his father's wife, and his son's wife — even though she married his brother or his father's brother or his son after they converted; and likewise his mother's sister from her father, his sister from his father, and his daughter who converted are permitted to him: {Rama: (And some forbid his father's wife) (Tur in Beit HaGadol).} But he may not marry neither his maternal sister nor his mother's sister from her mother, nor his maternal brother's wife who married his maternal brother after she converted; but if his brother married her when he was still a gentile — behold she is permitted to him.
