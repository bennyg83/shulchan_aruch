How, etc. Behold in chapter 9 of Laws of Kings he wrote six forbidden relations for Bnei Noach: mother, father's wife, another's wife, his sister from mother, male, and animal — as said "therefore a man leaves his father" — this is father's wife, "and his mother" as implied, "and cleave" — not to male, "to his wife" — not to another's wife, "and they shall become one flesh" — to exclude animal; and it says "my sister daughter of my father but not daughter of my mother she shall be to me for a wife" — these are R' Akiva's words in Sanhedrin 58a; however his sister is permitted to R' Akiva as written seif 2; and Rashi there s.v. teisham also etc. — only that he ruled like the rabbis there 57b Tosefta Kiddushin: R' Yehudah, all forbidden relations etc. and the Sages, many etc.; and it appears "many" is not missing text but "there is no Israelite court" etc. — this is what the end explains "came" etc. and they are only his sister and a betrothed maiden (examine what about a betrothed maiden) — his sister from verse "therefore leave" which for R' Akiva needs forcing, and rabbis establish as plain meaning; and in Tosefta Kiddushin and "a man" etc. "kindness" lest you say Cain married his sister — it teaches kindness; the world from its beginning was only created with kindness, as written 58b "teach" — why etc., and not like the rejection there, and not like Rashi there 57b s.v. harbeh etc.; and likewise Ramban — only that he wrote all those from mother's side are forbidden to rabbis, and there is no difference between Ramban and Rambam except mother's sister from mother; as Ramban wrote. Twenty-two forbidden relations: four not from kinship — male, animal, another's wife, niddah — all forbidden to gentile except niddah which they lack; eighteen from kinship as in chapter 1 of Yevamot 15 — six forbidden relations etc., behold twenty-one; and brother's wife counted three times — from father, mother, and not in his world; and his sister twice — from father and mother; behold seven are his own kinship and seven from his wife's side and four of wife's relatives — seven a husband forbids on his wife; behold in Bnei Noach seven from his wife's side are all permitted, for gentile has no marriage; and likewise four of wife's relatives all permitted except father's wife which is derived for R' Akiva; but for R' Eliezer all are permitted; and seven from himself — those from father's side are all permitted as in Yevamot chapter 1 (98a) — gentile has no father, therefore they said there 58b gentile is permitted with his daughter and a fortiori daughter's daughter and son's daughter; and father's sister for R' Akiva from same reason, but for R' Eliezer derived from verse; but mother's sister Ramban holds forbidden even to R' Akiva, as he wrote because it is written lemor — derived all from mother's side; but Rambam holds they are not forbidden even to rabbis except mother and sister as explicit, and like R' Meir according to R' Akiva, and per R' Meir and Sages only regarding his sister; and in scripture explanation as in Gemara above; and he challenges there 57b and R' Meir holds all etc., and Ramban explains the challenge applies only to the end where it says Bnei Noach are not warned etc. and here teaches his sister etc., but not from father's wife he does not challenge, for rabbis did not decree other father's prohibitions on converts, unlike Rashi there s.v. veika detani etc.; and proof from that he challenges only R' Meir, and if like Rashi's explanation it would be difficult also for rabbis since rabbis do not explain thus, and they answer: there is no difficulty — this is R' Meir etc.; and likewise the baraita is not like rabbis who explain only regarding his sister, since perforce like R' Akiva from his admission regarding capital cases of court for R' Meir; and in the dispute of Rav Ashi and R' Shimon in Yevamot 97b he ruled like Rav Acha bar Yaakov and like the first version, not like Rif — his reason as written 98a Rava said what rabbis said etc., implying even rabbinically gentile has no father; and he says two twin brothers etc., and likewise all else with father's side permitted ab initio — father's sister from mother, mother's sister from father, his sister from father; and although in the baraita there all are forbidden, they already established it like R' Eliezer because father's sister is forbidden to Bnei Noach according to him, but for R' Akiva and rabbis all with father's side entirely permitted; and likewise Ramban and Rashba; but mother's sister from mother forbidden to convert from "since" — although permitted to Bnei Noach from "gentile has no father" etc., implying if he has one; and likewise maternal brothers forbidden as above; and father's side from marriage permitted even father's wife per this reasoning — only brother from mother as above; and like his own kinship and as written "what, not" etc. "no" etc., and it applies only to brother's wife alone, not as Raavad who applies to all forbidden from marriage, for he counts there wife of father's brother who certainly even if she married when he was convert. Ramban. And further distinction between baraita and rabbis: for baraita mother's sister from mother he removes, but for rabbis he maintains like father's sister from father for R' Meir who is only because of confusion, and likewise mother's sister from mother for R' Akiva and rabbis, and all the more brother's wife from mother — this is Rambam's method and what he wrote "if he was married" to the rest. (Likut) As written above, the rule for Rambam: for Bnei Noach forbidden mother and sister from mother and father's wife and the rest entirely permitted like R' Meir according to R' Meir. And for a convert father's side entirely permitted whether from himself or marriage — therefore permitted even father's wife; in this he is lighter than gentile, but in this stricter, for all from mother's side forbidden whether from himself or marriage — therefore forbidden mother and sister from mother from himself, and from wife forbidden sister from wife's mother, daughter of wife, daughter of her daughter, mother-in-law, and mother of mother-in-law, as written in seif 8 "one who marries" etc. "and likewise" etc. "and likewise" to those and as Tosafot and Ramban wrote; and from marriage forbidden brother's wife from mother — all nine forbidden relations. And Ramban's method for a convert like Rambam except for gentile he wrote per this reasoning forbidden in all from mother's side and permitted from father's and marriage — why need a general rule, only those specified: father's sister from mother for R' Eliezer and father's wife for R' Akiva; except for R' Meir according to R' Akiva forbidden kin of mother are only court death, and for R' Eliezer and rabbis also karet.
