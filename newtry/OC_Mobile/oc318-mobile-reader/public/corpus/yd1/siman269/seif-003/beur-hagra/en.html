A non-Jew is forbidden, etc. As Rava states there: “That which the Sages said,” etc. This applies both to his own relatives and to relatives connected through the wife of a relative, as the Mechaber proceeds to explain.
Even though, etc., such as, etc. This follows Rabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov and the first version of the Gemara.
Therefore, etc. This is stated in the baraisa there.
And likewise a sister, etc. This does not follow the baraisa, but rather the Sages, as explained above.
And some prohibit, etc. This follows Rashi's explanation cited above, according to which the Gemara also challenges from the case of a father's wife and answers, “This is Rabbi Meir,” etc. See Tosafos in Yevamos there, s.v. “His father's wife,” etc.; “However,” etc. The Tur likewise rules this way, and it is proper to be stringent in accordance with both opinions. In all of this, there is no distinction between one who was conceived before conversion and born after conversion, one who was both conceived and born before conversion, or one son who was conceived before conversion, etc. Ramban agrees with this, and Rambam and Shulchan Aruch likewise state the law without qualification. See Tosafos there, s.v. “He married,” etc. (Collected note) And some prohibit a father's wife. This is taken from the Tur, as stated above. The Rav, however, did not examine the matter carefully here, for the Tur disagrees extensively with Rambam. Since the Tur also rules like Rabbi Eliezer, he prohibits one's sister, father's sister, and mother's paternal sister, as stated in the baraisa there. The paternal side is lenient only in relationships created through marriage, such as the wife of a paternal brother, and similarly the paternal sister of a female convert's husband, as Tosafos write and as explained above. Furthermore, one who married his sister, father's sister, or mother's maternal sister must divorce her, as stated there. He must likewise divorce his father's wife, because the Tur rules in accordance with both opinions. In addition, he rules like Rabbi Shimon that a relative connected through both the father and the mother is forbidden just like one connected through the mother alone, unlike Rambam, who writes concerning two twin brothers, etc. (End of collected note.)
But, etc. Every relation based exclusively on the maternal side is forbidden, just as the wife of his maternal brother is forbidden, as explained above. Nevertheless, it appears that his sister is forbidden even when she shares both his father and mother, for she is already forbidden to a ben Noach. This is derived from the verse, “And indeed she is my sister,” etc.; “but,” etc.
