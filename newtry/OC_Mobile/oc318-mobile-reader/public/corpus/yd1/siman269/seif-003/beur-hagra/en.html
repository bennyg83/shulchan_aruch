How, etc. In chapter 9 of Hilchos Melachim, Rambam writes that six forbidden relations apply to a ben Noach: his mother, his father's wife, a married woman, his maternal sister, a male, and an animal. This is derived from the verse, "Therefore a man shall leave his father," meaning his father's wife, "and his mother," literally; "and shall cleave," excluding a male; "to his wife," excluding another man's wife; "and they shall become one flesh," excluding an animal. It is also stated, "She is indeed my sister, the daughter of my father, but not the daughter of my mother, and she became my wife." These are the words of R' Akiva in Sanhedrin, daf 55a. Yet according to R' Akiva his sister is permitted, as stated there on daf 55b; see Rashi there, s.v. "come and hear," etc. Rambam, however, rules like the Sages on daf 57b and like the Toras Kohanim according to R' Yishmael: "Every forbidden relation," etc. The text appears not to read "many," but rather: there are relations for which a Jewish beit din does not execute, etc., namely those explained in the continuation, including his sister and a betrothed maiden. The matter of a betrothed maiden requires study. His sister is derived from "Therefore he shall leave," etc. According to R' Akiva the verse must be read less literally, while the Sages interpret it according to its plain meaning. In Toras Kohanim, Parashas Kedoshim, it is taught regarding "it is a disgraceful act": perhaps you will say that Kayin married his sister; Scripture therefore calls it an act of kindness, for at the beginning of the world it was permitted only because the world could not otherwise be established. This accords with Sanhedrin, daf 55b, "Come and hear, why," etc., and not with the Gemara's rejection there, nor with Rashi's explanation on daf 57b. Ramban likewise writes this, except that he holds that according to the Sages all maternal relatives are forbidden. The only dispute between Ramban and Rambam is therefore concerning the mother's sister through the mother, as explained by Ramban. There are twenty-two forbidden relations in all: four not based on kinship, namely a male, an animal, a married woman, and a niddah. All apply to a non-Jew except niddah. Eighteen are based on kinship, corresponding to the fifteen women and six forbidden relations listed in the first chapter of Yevamos, with the wife of one's brother counted in three forms and one's sister in two forms. Seven are one's own relatives, seven are relatives through one's wife, and four are wives of relatives whose husbands create the prohibition. For a ben Noach, the seven relatives through one's wife are all permitted because marital kinship does not create such relationships for non-Jews. Likewise, the four wives of relatives are all permitted except the father's wife, which R' Akiva derives from the verse, whereas according to R' Eliezer all are permitted. Of the seven direct blood relatives, those related through the father are all permitted, as stated in Yevamos, chapter 11, daf 98a, because a non-Jew has no halachic paternal relationship. Therefore the Gemara on daf 58b states that a non-Jew may marry his daughter, and all the more so his son's daughter and daughter's daughter. The same applies to the father's sister according to R' Akiva, whereas R' Eliezer derives a prohibition from the verse. Ramban nevertheless holds that the mother's sister is forbidden even according to R' Akiva, because the word "saying" extends the prohibition to maternal relatives. Rambam, however, holds that even according to the Sages only the mother and maternal sister, which are explicitly stated, are forbidden, as in the view of R' Meir according to R' Akiva. R' Meir and the Sages disagree only concerning his sister and the interpretation of the verse. The Gemara on daf 57b asks, "Does R' Meir hold," etc. Ramban explains that the question concerns only the latter clause, which says that a ben Noach is not warned, etc., while here the Baraisa includes his sister. It does not ask from the father's wife because the Sages did not decree other paternal relatives upon converts. This differs from Rashi's explanation there. Proof is that the Gemara challenges specifically according to R' Meir; according to Rashi the same difficulty would apply to the Sages, who do not disagree on that point. It answers that one source follows R' Meir, etc., and the Baraisa likewise cannot accord with the Sages except concerning the sister. In the dispute of R' Eliezer ben Yaakov and R' Shimon in Yevamos, daf 97b, Rambam rules like Rav Acha bar Yaakov and the first version, unlike the Rif. His reason is the statement on daf 98a: "This that the Sages said," etc., which implies that even rabbinically a convert has no paternal relationship. The Gemara says that twin brothers, etc., and therefore any kinship containing a paternal side is permitted l'chatchila: the father's sister through the mother, the mother's sister through the father, and a paternal sister. Although the Baraisa there forbids all of these, it has already been established according to R' Eliezer, who holds the father's sister is forbidden to a ben Noach. According to R' Akiva and the Sages, any relation containing a paternal side is entirely permitted. Ramban and Rashba likewise write this. A mother's sister through the mother, however, is rabbinically forbidden to a convert even though she is permitted to a ben Noach, because the Gemara's statement that a non-Jew has no father implies that maternal kinship does exist. The same applies to maternal brothers. Likewise, a paternal relationship created through marriage is permitted, even the father's wife according to all opinions, except for the wife of his maternal brother, as explained. The Gemara's statement, "What, is it not," etc., concerns only the wife of his brother, unlike the Ra'avad's explanation that it applies to all relations created through marriage. For the Gemara also counts the wife of his father's brother, who is certainly permitted even if that marriage occurred after conversion. Ramban. There is an additional distinction between the Baraisa and the Sages: according to the Baraisa, the mother's sister through the mother must be divorced, whereas according to the Sages he may retain her, like the father's sister through the father according to R' Meir, because the prohibition is only to prevent confusion. The same applies to the mother's sister through the mother according to R' Akiva and the Sages, and all the more so to the wife of his maternal brother. This is Rambam's approach, and this is the meaning of his words, "If he was married," etc., regarding the other relations, etc.
(Likkut) See what is written there. The general rule according to Rambam is as follows: for a ben Noach, his mother, maternal sister, and father's wife are forbidden, while all other relatives are entirely permitted, in accordance with the Sages who disagree with R' Meir. For a convert, paternal relationships are entirely permitted, whether direct blood relatives or relatives through marriage. He may therefore even marry his father's former wife, and in this respect a convert is more lenient than a non-Jew. On the other hand, every maternal relationship is forbidden to a convert, whether through direct blood relationship or through marriage. Thus, among his own relatives he is forbidden to his mother and his mother's sister through the mother. Among his wife's relatives he is forbidden to his wife's maternal sister, his wife's daughter and granddaughter, his mother-in-law, and his wife's maternal grandmother, as written in seif 5, "one who marries," etc.; the same applies to the corresponding cases, as Tosafos and Ramban write. Through marriage he is also forbidden to the wife of his maternal brother. These are nine forbidden relations in all. Ramban's approach regarding a convert agrees with Rambam, but regarding a non-Jew he holds that according to all opinions all maternal relatives are forbidden and all paternal relatives are permitted. Relations created through marriage do not apply at all except those explicitly specified: the father's sister through the mother according to R' Eliezer, and the father's wife according to R' Akiva. According to R' Meir in accordance with R' Akiva, only maternal relations punishable by death are forbidden, whereas according to R' Eliezer and the Sages even those punishable by karet are forbidden. Rashi has another approach; see Ramban's discussion. End.
