Some say, etc. The language of the Ran: from what we say "but a spring, etc." it implies that even in a place of danger it is forbidden, even if no person sees him — and this is included in "in all cases we heal, etc." However, a city that has idolatry within it, where it is forbidden to go there because it appears he goes to idolatry — it is reasonable that in a place of danger it is permitted; and it is also possible that even regarding a spring it is permitted in a place of danger. And that which we say there is a variant — rather that one may come to danger if he does not drink now, lest he die of thirst before finding water — his words are strained; and so the Tur wrote here explicitly "even whether there is a variant or not he drinks"; and so the Rashba, whom the Tur brought in siman 149. And the Rashba wrote: and because of this reason of suspicion, etc., and even for a life-threatening need it is forbidden to enter there, as we say regarding a spring, etc. — all this unlike the words of the Ran; and so we say in Sanhedrin 74a "granted, etc."; and even though companionship with them is rabbinic according to the Ramban — end of passage. Chiya bar Abba opened "the proud have hidden a trap, etc. Master of the world, a trap, etc." (end).
And there are lenient, etc. As we said 61b "one might think even if it was worshipped, etc." — it implies specifically if it was worshipped; and his proof is wondrous, for from there it implies the opposite; and Tosafot there on "and Rava, etc." — one can say as he said, etc.; and so on Shabbat 72b on "Rava, etc."; and so regarding a spring there — even though it is known he stays to drink, nevertheless it is forbidden; and in Turei Zahav he strained to distinguish between them, and his words are very strained; and see Orach Chaim siman 113 seif 8.
