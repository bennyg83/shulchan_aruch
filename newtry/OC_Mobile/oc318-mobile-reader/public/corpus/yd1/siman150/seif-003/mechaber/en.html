Faces that spout water in the presence of elilim: he should not place his mouth on their mouth and drink, because it appears as if he is kissing elilim. {Rama: There are those who say that anything that is forbidden only due to mar’it ayin, such as in this law, if there is danger in the matter — for example, if he will die if he does not drink — it is permitted to drink, and there is no concern of “he should be killed and not transgress” (Ran, first chapter of Avodah Zarah). Ministers or priests who have a form of idolatry on their garments, or who carry a form of the sun before them as the way of the nobles: it is forbidden to bow to them or to remove one’s hat before them, except in a manner that does not appear so — such as if his money was scattered, or if he rises before them prior to their coming, and likewise he removes the hat and bows prior to their coming (Terumat HaDeshen siman 196). And there are those who are lenient in the matter, since it is known that even the non-Jews do not remove the hat or bow to the form of the sun, but only to the minister (ibid. in the name of R' Yosef of Fano and Mahariv), and it is good to be stringent like the first reasoning.}
