One, etc. Mishnah at the beginning of chapter 4 of Nazir and Gemara, Shevuos 29b.
Or an expression, etc., such as answering, etc. As stated there, 36a, that an affirmative response constitutes acceptance of the matter. For this reason it is treated as though he himself uttered it. Tosefta, chapter 3 of Nazir: if one says to his fellow, "I am a nazir, and you?" and the fellow says yes, both are forbidden; if he says no, only the first is forbidden and his fellow is permitted. See there. "But," etc. See Shach, and the beginning of chapter 5 of Nedarim 46a, "is he then forbidden," etc.; and chapter 7 of Kesubos 70a concerning one who vows to deprive his wife, etc. If her acceptance were required, the Gemara could not ask there, since he is obligated to support her, etc. Likewise at the beginning of chapter 2 of Nedarim 15b; moreover, if so, she herself caused the prohibition, etc.
