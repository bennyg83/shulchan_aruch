(Seif 10) Bone broke in place of junction and bound and healed well — some permit if flesh appearance on fracture did not change; and for us treifah; specifically bird; but in animal we are expert even if broken and bound; specifically when bound; but if still broken — forbid even in animal, Shach. One may analyze: if still broken without concern for junction — treifah because of broken bone if skin and flesh do not cover majority; and for us not expert in any treifah matter as written siman 55 seif 5 in hagahah; it appears here he deals where no defect in skin and flesh outside, as Mechaber wrote if flesh appearance did not change — then kosher even for us in great loss as hagahah there; and if because concern for junction — treifah even in great loss. Such acharonim answered here what Shach meant: all that bound, even if fracture pieces do not adhere — kosher in animal. Difficult: otherwise treifah because of broken bone; per what I said — if skin and flesh outside whole we do not require fracture pieces to adhere, even lying one on another — kosher even without great loss per acharonim there siman 55 s.k. 15; responsa Chacham Tzvi siman 58; acharonim wrote even if defect in skin and flesh and not well bound, do not rebuke one who is lenient in great loss.
And know that in a case of great need, Mahari Weil validated even in the place of the tzomet hagidin, like the permissive opinion. So too it appears from the glosses of Shaarei Dura. In Noda BiYehudah siman 20 he wrote that according to the one who permits here, he also permits where it became joined after breaking near the body, for we are not concerned for the sinews when it rejoined, as with the tzomet hagidin. See there, where he was lenient regarding salting, permitting with only a peel according to Raavyah. See there, siman 21, regarding a doubt whether it is the place of the tzomet hagidin, and it rejoined but was not known whether the skin and flesh were intact, and after cooking it was found that he does not know whether the flesh had been whole. It implies that because of the bone break alone there is certainly no reason to render it treifah in such a case. Regarding a break found after eating, where it was not known whether the skin and flesh were intact, and the matter requires that the skin and flesh have been whole but this was not checked initially, the Noda BiYehudah, second edition, Yoreh Deah siman 23, wrote that it appears one should forbid even the utensils that can be kashered, except after twenty-four hours, even though it is a rabbinic doubt, because he is stringent with a davar sheyesh lo matirin, and a utensil that can be kashered is considered a davar sheyesh lo matirin. Earthenware utensils, which cannot be kashered by hagalah, he permits after twenty-four hours like a doubt in a rabbinic matter, since there is doubt whether the flesh was whole. According to his words this requires study, for it is a rabbinic doubt that comes through a chain, unless it was found only after twenty-four hours, when it had become rabbinic. But I have not seen the practice to be stringent in this; we assume it was whole. It appears that this depends on the words of the Shach in siman 50, whether we say "once slaughtered, it was permitted" in such a case.
