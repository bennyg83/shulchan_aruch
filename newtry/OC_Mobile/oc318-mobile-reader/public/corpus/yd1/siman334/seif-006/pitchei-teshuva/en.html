That they not circumcise his sons. Birkei Yosef cites Makom Shmuel as explaining that Maharam's gloss teaches that even if the father is himself a mohel, the community can decree that he may not circumcise his own sons. Birkei Yosef questions this. Perhaps such a decree may not be imposed, just as one may not decree that he refrain from prayer, Shema, or tefillin. There is no proof from Knesset HaGedolah's derivation from a responsum of Ramban that one may decree that a person not recite Kaddish for his father. Kaddish is comparable to zimmun and communal prayer: since the mitzvah requires others, three for zimmun and ten for Kaddish and Kedushah, the ban can isolate him from communal participation and deny him a share in the required Jewish quorum. But something he can perform entirely by himself, without joining others, such as circumcising his own son, may not be included in the decree. See there.
