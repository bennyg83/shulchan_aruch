One who disgraces a sage—even in words and even after death—if there are witnesses to the matter, bet din imposes nidui on him publicly and they release him when he repents; but if the sage is alive they do not release him until the sage whom they imposed nidui for his sake is willing (nevertheless if he did what is required and the sage does not wish to forgive, they release his nidui) (Beit Yosef). And likewise the sage himself imposes nidui for his honor on an am haaretz who treated with contempt against him, and he needs neither witnesses nor warning, and they do not release him until the sage is willing; and if the sage died, three come and release him. {Rama: And see above end of siman 243 (in Hagahah) whether they judge nowadays the law of Torah scholar. One who calls a sage informer or traitor—there is no greater disgrace than this and they impose nidui for this (Maharik root 180).}
