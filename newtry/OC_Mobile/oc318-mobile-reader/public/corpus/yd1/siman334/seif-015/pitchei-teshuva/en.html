But not with respect to other sages. See Baer Heitev in the name of Shach, who disagrees. This is difficult, for if one placed under a ban by his teacher is automatically under a ban with respect to all sages, why does the mishnah specifically teach that one placed under a ban by his teacher is under a ban with respect to his student? It would apply to all sages. I found in Responsa Radbaz, new edition, siman 378, that he too concludes that one under a ban imposed by his teacher is under a ban with respect to all sages. He explains that the mishnah's statement about the student is needed even where the student later became wiser and greater than his teacher. From this it follows that the reverse rule, that one under a ban imposed by a student is not under a ban with respect to his teacher, also applies even if the student has become greater than the teacher. This requires study.
