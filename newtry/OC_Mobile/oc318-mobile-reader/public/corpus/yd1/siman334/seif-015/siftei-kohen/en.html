But he is not under nidui to the other sages. Even if they are lesser than he. This is the Beit Yosef and Darkhei Moshe; see s.k. 30, what I wrote on this.
But he is under nidui to the rest of the people who are not sages, etc. This follows the implication of the Beit Yosef and is from the Rambam's words. But in truth, the plain meaning of the Talmud and poskim implies that he is not under nidui only to his teacher, but to the rest of the world he is under nidui, even to sages like himself. So wrote Rabbi Chasdai, the author of the Kuntresim, and the Rivash, that he is under nidui even to sages like himself, as long as they are not greater than he; the Beit Yosef brings them. He wrote only that from the Rambam's phrase "every person" it implies that he is under nidui only to the rest of the people and not to students, even if they are lesser than he, since they too are students. For practice, we cannot be more stringent than what is implied from the Rambam's words. In my humble opinion, this inference is not enough to explain "all the people" as excluding sages, since the plain meaning of the Talmud and poskim does not imply so. The great authorities above wrote explicitly that even sages like him must observe nidui toward him. The Tur also wrote that his nidui is nidui for every other person. So too it is implied in Moed Katan 17a: one under nidui to a student is not under nidui to the teacher; to the teacher he is not under nidui, but to everyone else he is under nidui, implying even to sages like himself except his teacher. Rashi too wrote at the end of 16a that he is not under nidui to his teacher, but to everyone else he is under nidui. So too Rabbeinu Yerucham, netiv 14 part 1, wrote that he is not under nidui to the teacher, but to the whole world he is under nidui. It appears that the Rambam's view is also so. According to this, all the more so when the teacher placed him under nidui, he is under nidui to the other sages. However, it seems this applies specifically where the student is a Torah scholar worthy of nidui being imposed for his honor, as below in seif 18.
It is insolence. On the part of the student, and the student himself requires nidui.
And his students cannot stand in his decrees, etc. The Rav also wrote this above at the end of siman 228 regarding a communal decree.
