For absolution. See above siman 228 seif 20 and siman 238 seif 20. It requires study, for the Mechaber's words appear to contradict each other; see there, and it is clear. It appears to me to answer that the Mechaber's statement here, that any beit din that attends to such a request is liable to shamta, refers to a matter where he has not yet practiced prohibition for as many days as he practiced permissibility, as above in siman 208, and so it is in the Gemara. But afterward, even if he transgressed deliberately, he may request absolution. All this concerns a matter to which practicing prohibition applies. But in a matter where that does not apply, such as one who swore not to eat a loaf and then transgressed and ate it, they release him immediately. That is the case in simanim 228 and 238. This distinction is explicit in siman 208 seif 5; see there.
