They placed him under a ban in a dream. See Responsa Radbaz, part 1, siman 100, who writes that the same applies if other people dreamed that they were placing him under a ban; he also requires release. Tashbetz, siman 329, likewise writes this.
It has no release. See Responsa Rashbash, siman 396, who was asked about an entire community placed under a ban in a dream. Who releases them, members of that community or another community? He concludes that one who is himself under a ban may apparently release others from their bans. Therefore, ten distinguished members of the community should first sit together and release the community. Even according to Ramban and the Rosh, cited among the dissenting opinions above in siman 234, seif 29, that one husband cannot annul the vows of two wives simultaneously, that restriction is specific to his wives because Scripture says "he shall annul her," implying her and not her companion. For unrelated persons, many may be released at once. After those ten have released the community, ten others should stand and release the first ten. If there are twenty qualified people this is straightforward. If there are not twenty, the ten who released the community can themselves be released one at a time: one leaves the group, a tenth person joins the remaining nine, and they release the one who left. Then another leaves and the one already released rejoins the nine, and this continues until all ten have been released.
