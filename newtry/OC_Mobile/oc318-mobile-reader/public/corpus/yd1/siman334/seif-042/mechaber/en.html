A sage elder in wisdom or head of bet din who turned wicked—they do not impose nidui on him in public ever unless he acted like Yerovam ben Nevat and his companions; but when he sinned other sins they flog him privately; and likewise any Torah scholar who became liable for nidui—it is forbidden for bet din to rush and impose nidui on him quickly, but they flee and slip away from him; and pious among sages would boast they never counted to impose nidui on a Torah scholar, even though they count to flog him if he became liable for lashes or makat mardut; and if his conduct is hateful, such as engaging in books of heretics and drinking musical kinds, or his fellows are ashamed of him and Heaven name is profaned through him—they place him in shamta.
