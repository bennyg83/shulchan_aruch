They are permitted. See responsum Chinuch Beit Yosef siman 77, in the name of his grandfather of blessed memory, regarding one who was under cherem, a son was born to him before the cherem, and because he was ill his milah was deferred until he recovered, and in the meantime the father was caught in nidui and Beit Din placed him under cherem not to circumcise his son. One sage wrote there that specifically before the son was born they may be stringent on the father not to circumcise his sons, for the obligation has not yet taken effect, but not after he was born; and likewise if they decreed that they would not bury a dead relative for him, specifically when that person was still alive at the time of the cherem. And he of blessed memory disputed him, and his view is that specifically regarding a dead person one distinguishes between whether he died before the nidui or afterward, but regarding milah there is no distinction. And see there in the gloss of his grandson, the printer of that book, who wrote that perhaps specifically in this case they decreed so, since Heaven delayed him, for he was ill and his milah was deferred; therefore we say, since it was deferred, it remains deferred; but to defer the milah from the eighth day, no; see there.
