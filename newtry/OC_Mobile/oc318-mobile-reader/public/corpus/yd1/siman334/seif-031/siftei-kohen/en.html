Even if he set, etc. Even though the time has not yet arrived. They said that one cannot release it until it takes effect only regarding a vow, but nidui is more lenient in release, for the one who placed under nidui may release himself, as below in seif 33. So wrote the Rosh in a responsum and the Atteret Zahav. The Beit Yosef wrote that even if others placed him under nidui, the law is the same, and so the Bach ruled. This is astonishing, for above in siman 228 seif 17, the Mechaber, Atteret Zahav, and Bach ruled that the same applies to one who placed himself under nidui conditionally: they do not release him until it takes effect, like a vow. This is the view of the Rashba. Likewise in the Bach to siman 228, on the Rashba's words, he wrote: the Rosh wrote in a responsum that nidui made conditionally may be released even though it has not yet taken effect, and this requires study. Regarding halachah, although the Beit Yosef and Bach here ruled like the Rosh and unlike the Mordechai in chapter HaOmanin, it appears that the Rashba's words escaped them. Furthermore, they themselves ruled in siman 228 like the Rashba. Therefore there is no proof from their words, and we return to the rule that the halachah follows the many against the individual: the Rosh is an individual against the Rashba and Mordechai, and this is also in the Rivash at the end of siman 272. So too it appears from the Rivash brought by the Beit Yosef in siman 228, p. 268a, who wrote regarding one who accepted a cherem upon himself to do a certain thing within a year: it is like one who swore that from now until a certain time he will do a certain act, for it takes effect now until the time passes, etc. This is also explicitly proven from his responsa, simanim 185 and 282, where he wrote: your doubt about releasing the cherem is because the time of payment had not yet arrived at the time of release, and one does not release a vow until it takes effect; the same applies to cherem and oath. There is no concern in this case, because that was said only regarding an individual who vows, but not regarding communal enactment cherem, etc.; it is as though they explicitly stipulated this at the time of the vow and cherem, etc. This appears primary.
