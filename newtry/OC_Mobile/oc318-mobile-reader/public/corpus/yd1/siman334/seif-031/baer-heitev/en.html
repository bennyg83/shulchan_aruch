Arrived. The Shach wrote that in the Beit Yosef it implies that even if others placed him under niddui, the law is so. This is surprising, for above in siman 228 seif 17, the Mechaber, Levush, and Bach ruled that the same applies to one who places himself under niddui conditionally: we do not release him until it takes effect, like a vow. As a matter of halachah this appears primary, and so ruled the Taz. The Shach wrote that from the Rivash's words it appears that this was said only about an individual who vows, but not regarding communal enactment bans; it is as though they explicitly stipulated so at the time of the vow and cherem, and so appears primary; end quote.
