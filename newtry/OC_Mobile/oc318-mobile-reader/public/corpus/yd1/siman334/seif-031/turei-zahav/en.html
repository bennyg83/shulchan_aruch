Although the appointed time has not yet arrived, etc. Although a vow cannot be annulled before it takes effect, nidui is more lenient, since a person may release himself from it and does not require another person to release him. Thus writes the Rosh. The Beit Yosef cites the Mordechai as holding that nidui is like a vow, but rules that the Rosh's view is primary, and so rules here in the Shulchan Aruch. It is surprising, however, that in siman 228, seif 17, the Shulchan Aruch rules like the Mordechai. The Rivash, cited there by the Beit Yosef, writes that one who accepted a cherem upon himself to perform a certain act within a year is like one who swore that he would perform the act between now and the specified date, so that the oath takes effect immediately. This implies that he agrees with the Mordechai that nidui is like an oath. It would therefore be proper to establish the halachah accordingly. See 228.
