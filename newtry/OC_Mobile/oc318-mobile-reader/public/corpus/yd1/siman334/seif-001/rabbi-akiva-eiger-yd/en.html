(Siman 334, Shulchan Aruch, seif 1) But our form of excommunication. This applies specifically to niddui. A cherem, even according to our practice, lasts thirty days. Beit Yosef in the name of Rosh.
(Taz, end of s.k. 1) And see what I wrote at the end of this siman. This proof appears in Binyamin Ze'ev responsa simanim 287 and 400. Chavot Yair responsa siman 141 concluded primarily like the Rama's view, and Shemesh Tzedakah responsa siman 68 agreed with this.
(Shulchan Aruch seif 37) He is obligated to place him under nidui. In Rama responsa siman 132 note 4, he wrote that nowadays, when people are accustomed to this, it is like one who says it is permitted and is considered within inadvertence. From this came the custom not to place him under nidui; see Semag at the end of siman 241. Nevertheless, one who is stringent to place him under nidui and immediately release him, may blessing come upon him.
