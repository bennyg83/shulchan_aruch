(Siman 334, Shulchan Aruch seif 1) But our nidui. Specifically nidui. A cherem, even nowadays, lasts thirty days. Beit Yosef in the name of Rosh.
(Taz, end of seif katan 1) See what I wrote at the end of this siman. This proof appears in Responsa Binyamin Ze'ev, simanim 287 and 400. Responsa Chavot Yair, siman 141, rules primarily like Rama, and Responsa Shemesh Tzedakah, siman 68, agrees.
(Shulchan Aruch, seif 37) He is obligated to place him under nidui. Responsa Rama, siman 132, letter 4, writes that nowadays, since people are accustomed to such conduct, it is treated like one who mistakenly believes the act is permitted and is therefore considered inadvertent. This is the basis of the custom not to place him under nidui. Nevertheless, one who is stringent and places him under nidui and then immediately releases him is praiseworthy.
