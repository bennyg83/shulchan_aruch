Immediately. This implies even without warning. However, in the Beit Yosef it implies that even for a prohibited matter, the practice was not to impose niddui except after warning. Shach.
From thirty. The Beit Yosef wrote in the name of the kuntresim that the practical difference is if a sage placed him under niddui for his honor and went away: according to them, they do not release him until after thirty days, and according to us, seven days; for since he went away, presumably he is agreeable that they release him after this time; end quote. Meaning, one should not explain that the court lacks power to reduce the niddui to less than thirty days, for that is not so, as below in seif 6. Shach.
To be concerned. The Taz wrote that one should not act in practice according to this gloss, and he brings several proofs and demonstrations for this; see there. (In Nekudat HaKesef he wrote that all his proofs can be rejected, since it is the law of the court that they impose niddui upon him; one should not be concerned, for otherwise the law of Israel would be nullified; end quote.)
