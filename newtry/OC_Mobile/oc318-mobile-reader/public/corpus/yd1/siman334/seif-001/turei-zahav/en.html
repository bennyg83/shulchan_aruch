This matter is very puzzling to me: how shall we be concerned for the prohibition that this one is doing, and bring him to bad culture, Heaven forfend, of which all who come to her will not return? And behold, the source of this law is in the Piskei Maharai siman 138, and this is his language: And a proof from what I found in a compilation that proved from that which is said in the Agudah, perek HaNosei, that Rabbeinu HaKadosh said at the time of his passing by way of prophecy regarding Rav Acha bar Yoshiyah, that they placed shamta on those who piled fruit in a kiln and they desecrated Shabbat and apostatized because they had been placed in shamta; and it is difficult, what does the Talmud teach us with this matter, and Rabbeinu HaKadosh himself, for what halacha did he prophesy this thing, except to teach us that even if it appears to the chachamim that because they place nidui on someone, on that account he will go out to bad culture, they should not refrain because of this from doing according to halacha, end of his words. And what he wrote in the name of perek HaNosei is a scribal error; rather this prophecy of Rebbi is in perek Asarah Yuchasin (Kiddushin daf 72). And it appears to my humble opinion that from this there is no proof at all, for there it implies that Rabbeinu HaKadosh came to warn the hearers that they should know there are pesulim in Bavel, as he said there much: that there is a place where all are mamzerim, and there is a place where there are two brothers who exchange their wives with one another; and regarding this he also said that there is a place in Bavel that they have turned away today from after the Omnipresent, blessed is He, for Rav Acha placed shamta on them etc., and therefore one should distance from them. And if you say: behold, that which is well-known needs no proof, and it is not similar to the other places that he warned that there are mamzerim, who are not known, which is not so in this case that he converted, and certainly even without this they would separate from them — to this I would say to you that all the more so there is no difficulty, for Rebbi's intent was to prove that prophecy spoke through him in the other things that he said, even though they were not clear in the eyes of the hearers, and they will bring proof from this matter, that they will see that he knows in Eretz Yisrael what is done today in Bavel regarding those whom Rav Acha placed in shamta, and this is impossible except by way of prophecy; from this they will know that also in the other things the spirit of Hashem spoke through him, as above, the intent of this prophecy is clear. And I have to bring a proof to the contrary from an explicit Talmud, first perek of Kiddushin (daf 20), that one who was sold to idolatry itself, that is, to chop wood for it and other needs, not for the sake of divinity, and the Torah said that one must redeem him; for the Tanna of the school of Rabbi Yishmael: since this one went and became a priest to avodah zarah, I would say, throw a stone after the fallen, meaning he should have no redemption; the verse teaches he shall have redemption. And behold there is an a fortiori here: just as there, where he already became a priest to avodah zarah, and even so the Torah warned with a positive commandment that they are obligated to redeem him, a fortiori in this case where he has not yet begun any avodah zarah at all, it is obvious that we need to be careful to be at least in sit and do not act, and not to do an act to bring them to avodah zarah; certainly this is a matter that it is not fitting to the heart to leave it. And a further proof from what the Gaon our teacher Rabbi Yehudah Mintz of blessed memory wrote in teshuva siman 5, and this is his language: And when I saw that it is impossible to save two souls of Israel from going out to bad culture, Heaven forfend, I said to permit a wet-nurse of one's fellow etc. And he brings a proof from perek HaSholeach (Gittin daf 38): that woman with whom people were doing a prohibition; Abaye said, were it not that Shmuel said whoever frees his slave transgresses a positive commandment etc.; see there. And if so, a fortiori in this that one should not do an act from which there is concern, as above; it is clear that one should not do in practice according to this gloss, even though it is from a halacha from a holy place, the author of the Agudah of blessed memory; and in our Agudah this is not found at all (1).
