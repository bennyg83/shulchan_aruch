Even if there is concern, etc. See Baer Heitev. Responsa Tashbetz, part 2, siman 47, agrees with Nekudot HaKesef, and so write Responsa Shemesh Tzedakah, Yoreh Deah, siman 48; Chavat Yair, siman 141; Sheilat Yaavetz, part 1, end of siman 79; and Responsa Radbaz, new edition, siman 187. Radbaz concludes that the leader of the generation must nevertheless proceed deliberately in such matters, because people are not all alike and transgressions are not all alike, and all his actions should be for the sake of Heaven. Responsa Chatam Sofer, siman 322, likewise rules this way and raises a further question: where there is concern that the offender will convert and take his young children with him, should the innocent children be considered? He distinguishes between one who holds heretical beliefs and is highly likely to teach them to his children, in which case the children too will become part of those described as "all who enter her do not return," and concern for them does not override communal discipline; and an ordinary recalcitrant or sinner, where it is not clear that one may ignore the danger to his children. He therefore writes that members of the cursed sect that believed in Shabbetai Tzvi, may his name be blotted out, if they do not repent, may be pursued together with their sons and daughters, but regarding an ordinary offender the matter remains one requiring study and careful deliberation.
