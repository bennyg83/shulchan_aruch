One who imposed nidui on his fellow improperly and the other said to him on the contrary—the nidui of the second is nidui; and this is when that second is a great man (even if not ordained) (Maharik root 158), or when he is unknown who he is for we fear perhaps he is greater than the imposer; but if the imposer improperly is a Torah scholar and the other is not a Torah scholar—not. {Rama: And likewise a student against his rabbi cannot say on the contrary unless he took permission from bet din (Maharik root 170). One who said to his fellow do not do this thing under nidui and this one says to him on the contrary—even though he acts improperly, nevertheless the second nidui is also nothing since he did not impose nidui generally but only told him the thing should not be done under nidui (Beit Yosef in the name of Rivash responsum).}
