And the other is not a Torah scholar. Even if he never learned before the one under nidui. This is Maharik there. At first glance this is difficult, for Maharik wrote there that this is specifically according to the view that the one placed under nidui must himself place the other under nidui; but according to the Rambam, who holds that the court becomes involved to place him under nidui, there is no difference between a Torah scholar and an ignoramus: since this person is not liable to nidui, the court must place the one who placed him under nidui under nidui. These are his words, and the Beit Yosef brings them. If so, below in seif 43 the Mechaber copies the Rambam's language. Perhaps the Mechaber disagrees with Maharik on this point; rather, he holds like the Ramakh's explanation of the Rambam below, that it speaks specifically of one who placed a Torah scholar under nidui, and the Beit Yosef brings this. Alternatively, he holds like the Tashbetz below in s.k. 86, and here it speaks of one who is not accustomed to doing this.
And the same applies to a student, etc. Maharik wrote there that from the words of Maharam's responsum it appears that if the rabbi was negligent and placed him under nidui in a matter where he should have suspected that the person was not liable to nidui, then if the student said "on the contrary," his nidui is effective. The Beit Yosef and Darkhei Moshe bring this; see Maharik himself, who discusses it at length.
Nevertheless the second nidui is also nothing, etc. Even according to the Rambam, who holds that the court becomes involved and places him under nidui, here they do not become involved. This is the Rivash there.
