The Beit Yosef asks why the Tur says this after already ruling that one who remains in nidui through two periods of thirty days is placed under cherem. It cannot mean that beit din may impose cherem before nidui, since the Tur shortly afterward says that cherem is imposed only after he has remained obstinate twice. Some answer that this passage concerns one who requests release from the ban. Otherwise, how could the Tur say that when the period arrives they release him immediately, when another thirty days are required? The novelty would then be that if he has not retracted, beit din may not only maintain nidui but may even impose cherem despite his request. This does not fit the wording. It appears to me that the Tur first taught the general structure and then explained it. Initially he described possible punishments of nidui: distancing him for up to sixty days and then placing him under cherem. One might think these stages are mandatory for everyone placed in nidui, with one necessarily following the other. He therefore clarifies that these details are discretionary. Beit din may vary the duration and need not insist upon thirty days, and cherem is likewise not obligatory but depends upon their judgment. This also resolves the question on seif 10, which repeats that he is not counted in a group of three. The purpose is to teach that these measures lie within the discretion of beit din and are not invariably required.
