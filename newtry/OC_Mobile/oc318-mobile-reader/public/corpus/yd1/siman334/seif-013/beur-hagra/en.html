(Likkut) And even if he did not, etc. So the Raavad explained there according to one explanation, and this is explicit in the Gemara in Shevuot 41a, as written in Choshen Mishpat siman 87 seif 9. The reason we flog him is because he keeps the ban upon himself, as stated in Yevamot chapter 5, 52a, and in the first chapter of Kiddushin. However, there it implies that this applies even if he does not request that they release him, and likewise there in siman 98 seif 5. Although they said there ninety days, that nidui is for the future, so that he will come to court and pay or set a time, and every moment he is subject to the nidui. This is unlike nidui for the past, as in the case in Shevuot, where the nidui is not for payment, as stated there, that they should seize him by his garment, etc., but because he refused to swear. Yet from the cases of Rabbi Eliezer in Sanhedrin 68a and Akavya in Eduyot chapter 5 it appears that it is specifically when he requests release. So too in the Yerushalmi at the beginning of chapter 3 of Moed Katan, brought by the Rosh there: the one under nidui whom they released, etc.; rather, in what case are we dealing, etc. However, there it implies that they do not release him at all before thirty days, and we do not rule so, as written in Moed Katan 16a and, according to the Rif, in every case: tut is forbidden, etc.
