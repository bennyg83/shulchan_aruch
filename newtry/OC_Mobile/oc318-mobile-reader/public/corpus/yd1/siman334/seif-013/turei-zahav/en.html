That which we say, etc. This is the view of the Raavad in the Asheri and Tur. We will explain his words with the help of Heaven, and it will become clear that what the Rama wrote, “and within thirty days, etc.,” is not precise, although he follows the words of the Kuntresim brought by the Beit Yosef. The Sages taught: no nidui is less than thirty days. The Raavad asked from what is stated in the Gemara, that Abbaye holds that in monetary matters we say “tot is forbidden and tot is permitted,” meaning that the court can remove him from the cherem even immediately if he appeased his litigant, but for disrespectful conduct thirty days specifically are required. The Rif ruled not like Abbaye, but that even with disrespectful conduct, if he appeased the other party, even immediately that suffices. If so, what is the case of the baraita, “no nidui is less than thirty days”? If he appeased the other party, we release him immediately; and if he did not appease him, after thirty days do we release him? One may answer that it speaks where he appeased the other party, and this is what it means: initially they do not place him in nidui for less than thirty days, but if he retracts, they release him immediately, etc. Alternatively, it speaks where he did not appease him, and nevertheless after thirty days they release him, for he has already received his judgment, provided that he comes to the court and asks them to release him, etc. The Tur and Shulchan Aruch ruled like both answers leniently, namely: if he appeased him, they release him even within thirty days; and after thirty days, even if he did not appease him, provided he asked the court to release him. Now, regarding the second leniency the Rambam disagrees and holds that they never release him until he appeases the sage whom he degraded, as the Beit Yosef wrote. But regarding the first leniency too, the Beit Yosef wrote in the name of the Kuntresim that the Rosh disagrees and holds like Abbaye, that for disrespectful conduct specifically thirty days are required, unlike the Rif. The Beit Yosef wondered whence the author of the Kuntres could say this. I say that certainly that author of the Kuntres was not precise, for in Responsa HaRosh, kelal 21, he explicitly ruled like the Rif, that there is no difference between money and disrespectful conduct: if he appeased the other party, it suffices even immediately. Also in Perek Elu Megalchin he brings the Raavad’s words and does not disagree with them. How then can we say that he disagrees with them? Therefore it is clear that what the Rama wrote, “there are those who say,” namely the view of the Rosh, that within thirty days appeasement and retraction do not help, is not correct. The Rif, Rambam, Raavad, and Rosh all hold this point, and no one disagrees with the first leniency we mentioned.
And the Beit Yosef wanted to uphold the Kuntres’s view from what is stated at the beginning of Elu Megalchin, on the moed, where it counts among those permitted to shave one under nidui whom the sages released. Meaning, he could not shave before the festival because one under nidui is forbidden in haircutting. The Yerushalmi, brought by the Rosh, asks: What are we dealing with? If they released him before the festival, he should shave, meaning why did he not shave before the festival? And if they did not release him before the festival, he should not shave, meaning he should not shave on the festival, for he was not forced before the festival, since he should have appeased the other party so they would release him. Rather, it speaks where they released him before the festival and his thirtieth day fell on the festival, for no nidui is less than thirty days. From this we hear that the Rosh, who brought this Yerushalmi, holds that no nidui is less than thirty days, unlike the Rif and Raavad, who do not require thirty. This is something the mind cannot tolerate. The Tur too wrote here like the Rif and Raavad, yet in Orach Chaim siman 531 he wrote specifically that his thirtieth day fell on the festival, as the Yerushalmi says, because no nidui is less than thirty. Likewise the Rosh’s words would contradict each other in the very same chapter, Perek Elu Megalchin, for at the beginning of the chapter he brings this Yerushalmi, and shortly afterward he writes like the Rif and Raavad. The Beit Yosef answered there that the Rosh explains the Yerushalmi as follows: what Abbaye said, that for disrespectful conduct thirty days are needed, means that within thirty days release is not relevant at all, even for after thirty days. Regarding this the Rif says that the halachah is not so; in any event release within thirty days is effective for after thirty days, but nevertheless thirty days are required according to all. I am astonished that a holy mouth would say this, for the Rif and Rosh brought proof that the halachah is not like Abbaye from the incidents of Reish Lakish and Rav Huna, who placed a woman in shamta for uttering the Divine Name from her mouth, etc., and there they did not wait thirty days at all. It seems the master Beit Yosef did not examine this sufficiently. I saw my teacher and father-in-law, of blessed memory, write in Orach Chaim to answer that in Orach Chaim it speaks where he did not appease the other party, but only appeased the court to remove him from the cherem, which helps even within thirty days so that he be permitted after thirty days; therefore thirty days are specifically required, and after thirty days he shaves even on the festival. This path is even more astonishing, for if so it should certainly be forbidden to shave on the festival, since he could have appeased his litigant and then it would have been permitted to shave immediately before the festival; therefore he should be forbidden to shave on the festival, as the Yerushalmi asked. We do not find that asking the court matters except after thirty days, when he has already endured his judgment, as the Tur wrote here in the name of the Raavad; but within thirty days, for the purpose of being able to shave after thirty days during the festival, this never was and never existed.
What appears to me in this is as follows. One should ask against the Yerushalmi and the Tur, who ruled like it, requiring that they released him before the festival: why does this matter? The thirty days were not completed until the festival, so why should he not be permitted to shave even if they released him only on the festival? Why should he have had to seek release before the festival, since the nidui still continued upon him? Tosafot at the beginning of Perek Elu Megalchin also brought this Yerushalmi in this language: In the Yerushalmi it asks, “But he was negligent by not coming before the moed before the sages to have them release him,” and answers that the thirty days of his nidui ended during the moed, for no nidui is less than thirty days; end quote. Thus they did not actually require release before the festival. So too it is proven from our Gemara in Perek Elu Megalchin, where Rava said: does it teach “whom the sages released”? It teaches “for whom the sages released,” meaning he went and appeased his litigant. Rashi explains: during the festival, the sages of that generation released him. If so, why did the Tur rule that they released him before the festival? Our Talmud is primary. Furthermore, I must be precise in the Tur’s language in Orach Chaim, where he wrote: “And likewise one under nidui who could not shave earlier, for one under nidui is forbidden in haircutting, such as where they released him before the festival and his thirtieth day fell on the festival, so he could not shave earlier because no nidui is less than thirty days. But if he remained in nidui because he did not ask them to release him, and on the festival he asked them to release him, he is not forced and cannot shave”; end quote. This is difficult: it implies that in the latter clause too it was impossible to leave the nidui before the festival as in the first clause, only that in the latter clause he did not ask earlier. Why then does he conclude that he is not forced? He is still forced because the thirty days had not ended. Therefore it appears to me that it is simple according to all that the baraita, “no nidui is less than thirty days,” is explained in two ways. If the nidui depends on time, such as where it is impossible to appease him because the sage whom he degraded went away, or because of other unavoidable circumstances, or because the sages saw fit to be stringent with him and leave him in nidui for some period, as written below in seif 40, then we say plainly: no nidui is less than thirty days. But if it depends on appeasing the sage, then we are not concerned with thirty days, except that at the outset, when imposing the nidui, they say that the nidui shall be for thirty days; when he retracts, they release him immediately. This itself is the debate in the Yerushalmi. The questioner thought that the nidui depends on appeasement, and therefore asked: why may he shave on the festival? If they released him earlier, he should have shaved then; and if they did not release him earlier because he did not appease, it is difficult, for he was negligent by not appeasing, since the matter depends on appeasement. The Yerushalmi answers that this nidui did not depend on appeasement but on a period of thirty days, as I mentioned. Therefore even if they released him before the festival he is called forced, and all the more so if they did not release him until the festival. Thus what the Yerushalmi says in its answer, that they released him before the festival, is a greater novelty, as I wrote. This is exactly the Tur’s words. This is what he means: likewise, the one under nidui who could not shave, etc., meaning because he specifically needs thirty days and appeasement does not help, may shave on the festival because his thirtieth day fell on the festival. What he wrote, “such as where they released him before the festival and the thirtieth day fell, etc.,” the word “such as” does not refer to “they released him before the festival,” but to what follows, that his thirtieth day fell on the festival; that is the precise condition. But what he wrote, “they released him before the festival,” is not precise; he mentioned it as an even greater novelty, as I explained regarding the Yerushalmi. So too wherever you find this language, that they released him before the festival, it is for the greater novelty. Therefore the Tur properly concludes: “But if he remained in nidui and did not ask,” meaning if the nidui depends only on appeasement and he remained in nidui and did not seek to appease before the festival, then he is certainly not forced. It follows that all faces are equal: wherever it depends on appeasement, appeasement suffices even immediately, and all the people will come to their place in peace. The Rama’s words here, that retraction within thirty days does not help, follow no one, and no one is concerned for them. So it appears to me.
