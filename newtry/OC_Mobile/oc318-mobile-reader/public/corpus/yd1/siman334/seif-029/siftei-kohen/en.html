Some say that post facto it is released. The requirement to release him only in his presence is due to suspicion: if he does not know he was released and others cease treating him as excommunicated, he may suspect them and later treat bans lightly. If the original ban was imposed in his absence, this concern is absent, because he may assume it was also released in his absence. Accordingly, where he was informed and no suspicion exists, release in his absence is valid even initially; and if he was already released, it is effective even if he was not informed. Ran. Rosh, however, cites an opinion that even post facto it is ineffective, and implies that all agree it should not initially be done merely by notifying him.
