One who treats lightly a matter of the Sages. See the Shach and his difficulty with the Kesef Mishneh. See Responsa Knesset Yechezkel, Orach Chaim, end of siman 19, who writes, with due respect to the Shach, that he did not understand the Kesef Mishneh's question; see there.
In one matter of the words of the Sages. See Responsa Perach Mateh Aharon II, siman 57, regarding one who cursed a Torah scholar and his Torah. He writes that even if the scholar has not yet attained the stature for which one would place another under a ban on his account, nevertheless the offender deserves severe punishment and a substantial fine, since he impugned the honor of the Torah; see there.
Of the words of the Sages. See the Shach, and Responsa Panim Meirot I, siman 90, by my ancestor. See there, where he writes that one who has incurred a ban and wishes to pay a fine is not thereby exempt from the ban as a matter of strict law. Nevertheless, everything depends upon the judgment of the beit din. If they see that he sincerely repents, it is proper to be lenient, since they are not obligated to place him under a public ban for this; see there. Accordingly, it appears that where the matter is one for which they are obligated to place him under a public ban, as in seif 47, they cannot be lenient with him.
One who desecrates the second day of Yom Tov. See Responsa Mahari HaLevi, siman 11, concerning a scholar who permitted setting sail on the second day of Yom Tov in the Diaspora, and whether he is placed under a ban as one who treats the law lightly; see there.
One who calculates years. See the Shach, and what I wrote above, siman 246, seif katan 4, in the name of Responsa Chavot Yair.
