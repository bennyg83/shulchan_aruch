One who disgraces a sage. This is one who is fit to rule and labors in Torah, as the Beit Yosef wrote in the name of the Kuntresim.
One who disgraces an agent of the court. See Choshen Mishpat at the end of siman 8.
One who belittles one matter of rabbinic law. The Ran asked that in some places we say that one who violates a rabbinic matter is lashed, but not placed under shamta. He answered that in a matter whose whole essence is rabbinic, where uprooting it uproots the entire mitzvah, we place him under shamta. But where the essence is from the Torah and he violates what the Sages prohibited, we give him lashes of rebellion. The Kesef Mishneh wrote about this: it is difficult to me, for netilat yadayim has no Torah root except through asmachta. Therefore it seems to me that one who questions a rabbinic matter is more severe than one who merely violates a rabbinic matter; therefore, although the violator receives only lashes of rebellion, one who questions or belittles it is liable to nidui. I do not know what was difficult to him. On the contrary, since netilat yadayim has no Torah root except by asmachta, we place him under nidui because by uprooting it he uproots the entire mitzvah. So wrote the Ran explicitly in chapter Makom Shenahagu: in a matter whose essence is rabbinic, where uprooting it uproots the whole mitzvah, we place him under shamta, like this, and as we also say, whom did they place under nidui? Rabbi Elazar ben Chatzar, who questioned netilat yadayim, etc. According to the Kesef Mishneh's answer, the Ran's question still remains: why do we place under shamta one who desecrates the second day of the diaspora festival, one who works on Erev Pesach after midday, and one who sees a dead person and does not escort him below in siman 343? Therefore one must say like the Ran.

Sixteen: one who calculates, etc. It appears that this is specifically where he calculates and fixes the calendar by sight as they were accustomed to fix it in Eretz Yisrael when the Temple stood. But to calculate seasons and constellations as is practiced nowadays, on the contrary, is a mitzvah, as we say in Shabbat 75: anyone who knows how to calculate seasons and constellations and does not calculate, about him the verse says, "they do not regard the work of Hashem," etc.

One who places under nidui someone who is not liable to nidui. If the person comes to court to complain that he placed him under nidui unlawfully, the court places him under nidui. This is the language of the Atteret Zahav, from the Beit Yosef. It implies that as long as he does not come to court to complain, the court does not involve itself to place him under nidui. So wrote the author of Migdal Oz at the end of chapter 7 of Hilkhot Talmud Torah. In Kesef Mishneh it is written that the Tashbetz in a responsum answered in the name of Rabbi Shem Tov that here it speaks of one who is accustomed to doing this, in which case the court involves itself to place him under nidui. This implies that when he is accustomed to it, even if no one complains, the court involves itself to place him under nidui.
