They are permitted. See Chinnukh Beit Yehudah responsa siman 77 in the name of his grandfather z"l, regarding one who was under cherem and had a son born before the cherem; because the child was ill, his circumcision was delayed until he recovered. In the meantime, the father was caught in nidui and the court placed him under cherem that he not circumcise his son. One sage wrote there that only before the child was born may they impose stringency on the father not to circumcise his sons, for the obligation has not yet taken effect; but not after the child was born. Likewise, if they decreed that his dead should not be buried, this applies only if the person was still alive at the time of the cherem. He z"l disagreed, and his view is that only with the dead is there room to distinguish whether the death occurred before the nidui or afterward; but with circumcision there is no distinction. See there in the gloss of his grandson, the printer of that book, who wrote that perhaps they decreed this only in that particular case, since Heaven had delayed him because the child was ill and his circumcision was postponed. Therefore we say: since it was postponed, it remains postponed. But to postpone circumcision from the eighth day, no. See there.
