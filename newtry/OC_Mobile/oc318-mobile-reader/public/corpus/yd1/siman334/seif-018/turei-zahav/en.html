Even if the rabbi promised to appease him afterward. This means that the rabbi had already placed him under nidui, and the person under nidui then promised to appease him. The promise is ineffective until the appeasement is actually made. If, however, he promises before the nidui, the rabbi should not place him under nidui even though the appeasement has not yet been made. The Levush explains the Rama to mean that even if the person promises explicitly to appease the rabbi only after the nidui is lifted, the promise is ineffective and the nidui is not lifted until he actually appeases him. According to that interpretation, an unqualified promise to appease him would suffice. In my opinion this is incorrect. The Rama's word 'afterward' means after the nidui was imposed, and even an unqualified promise does not suffice until the appeasement is actually made.
