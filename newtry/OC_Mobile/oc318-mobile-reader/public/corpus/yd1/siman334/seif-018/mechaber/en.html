A rabbi by power of the ruler and not a rabbi in Torah could not impose nidui for his honor. {Rama: And all the more one who practices dominion or rabbinic status from his wealth or lineage cannot impose nidui for his honor (Beit Yosef in the name of Kontresin). And specifically they impose nidui for honor of the rabbi, but not cherem (Beit Yosef and Rivash siman 173) unless he persists in dispute excessively (there in Rivash). And that which they say a rabbi who imposed nidui for his honor is menudeh to his students—this is if the menudeh did not repent before he imposed nidui on him; even if the rabbi promised afterward to appease him it does not help until he does so; but if before he was imposed nidui the rabbi said to appease and he did not heed and he imposed nidui, he is not menudeh at all to his students, and immediately when he says he will fix what he corrupted as the sages tell him, nidui is not upon him (Maharik root 169). A community that enacted that no person may impose nidui for his honor—their enactment is nothing; and if a rabbi stood and imposed nidui for his honor, his nidui is nidui (Rashba responsum siman 400).}
