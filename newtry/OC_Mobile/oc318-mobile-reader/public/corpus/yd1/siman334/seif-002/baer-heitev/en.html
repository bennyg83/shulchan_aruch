His four amot. The Shach wrote that these four amot are four amot in every direction, with him in the middle, making eight amot. They also take effect even in a public domain, alleyway, or ownerless field, but not in another house.
They join for zimun. Specifically where he was placed in nidui because of a transgression, but not where he was placed in nidui because of money. So wrote Responsa Rashba.
Explicitly. Where they did not specify in the nidui that he should not be included in a minyan. Taz.
To distance. This refers to those who came into his four amot; but those who were there first and he came to them do not need to distance themselves, as below in the gloss. Shach.
The sandal. So he ruled in Orach Chayim 554:17 and below in siman 382:4. The Shach wrote: this is astonishing, for in Beit Yosef here he ruled that wearing shoes is permitted, and so wrote Rivash at the end of siman 173 in the name of Rashba. Such is the custom. He also need not tear, wrap his head, or overturn the bed like a mourner, and he is permitted in washing, anointing, marital relations, tefillin, and greeting. This is the consensus of most poskim. End quote.
Standing. It requires study whether one who is walking establishes four amot. Shach.
