If an animal dragged him or bandits killed him, and on Shabbos he despaired of seeking — since private matters apply on Shabbos, it counts for him as one day: {Rama: If the thirtieth day of mourning fell on Shabbos and the twenty-ninth on the eve of Shabbos, it is permitted for him to bathe on the eve of Shabbos even in places where they practice not to bathe all thirty, for since by law it is permitted after seven but they practiced to be stringent all thirty — in this manner it is permitted because of the honor of Shabbos (Hagahos Maimoniyos in the name of Maharam chapter 10 and chapter 6, Laws of Mourning); and the same applies to anything similar, such as to return to his place on Friday night and to wear Shabbos garments, for these too are only a mere custom (so it appears from the words of Maharash), as explained above siman 389 and 390; and even though one could distinguish between bathing that is forbidden on Shabbos and these matters that he can do on Shabbos — nevertheless it does not appear to me to distinguish in this.}
