The Written Torah, etc. This implies the entire Tanach, and Bach rules accordingly. He writes that the widespread practice of not teaching children Tanach is improper. I say that Jewish custom has the force of Torah. Tosafot at the end of the first chapter of Kiddushin, Semag, positive commandment 12, in the name of Rabbeinu Chananel, Hagahos Maimoniyos, and Rabbeinu Peretz in his gloss to Semak, siman 105, all write that we rely on the statement in Sanhedrin that the Babylonian Talmud incorporates Scripture, Mishnah, and Gemara. Therefore one is not required to divide his years into thirds for Scripture, and is not obligated to teach his son Scripture separately once he has taught him Gemara. This also appears from the Tur below, siman 246, from Rabbeinu Yerucham at the beginning of nesiv 2, and explicitly from Rama below, siman 246, seif 4.
He is not obligated to teach him for payment, etc., but if possible, etc. All of a person's sustenance is fixed for him from Rosh Hashanah, except expenditures for Shabbos and Yom Tov and expenditures for his children's Torah study. If he adds to these, additional provision is granted to him, as stated in Beitzah, folio 16.
