He is obligated, etc., regarding his son. Maharshal writes that the same applies to one's son's son, and Perishah cites him. Kesef Mishneh, at the beginning of chapter 1 of Hilchos Talmud Torah, also writes that perhaps one must hire a teacher for his son's son, and likewise for later descendants through his son. He is uncertain whether a daughter's son takes precedence over another person's son, because perhaps precedence applies only where one had an obligation toward the child's father. Regarding a daughter, where that rationale does not apply, perhaps her son does not take precedence. It appears to me that he does take precedence over another person's son.
If not, the court descends to his property, etc. As explained in Choshen Mishpat, siman 106, regarding a creditor.
