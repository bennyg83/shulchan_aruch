His neighbors cannot, etc. See Choshen Mishpat, siman 197, seif 3.
Another sage may, etc. Even if the first sage is older than he is. This is implied by the rulings of Mahari, and Darkei Moshe writes likewise.
Even if he somewhat diminishes his livelihood. For example, by reducing his income from officiating at weddings and kiddushin. The rulings of Mahari state that if the concern is loss of livelihood because the leaders receive fees for gittin, chalitzah, administering oaths to women, blessings at betrothals and weddings, and similar matters, we are already embarrassed by these forms of compensation and labor to find permission for most of them. How, then, can we treat them as an established livelihood that others may not enter? This implies that where the compensation is clearly permitted, another may not deprive him of it. This is why Rama says that he may somewhat diminish his livelihood. All this is the strict law, but local custom may allow an objection.
But if he comes, etc. This means specifically where the other sage comes to reside there permanently. If he comes only temporarily as a guest, he may not diminish the first sage's livelihood.
