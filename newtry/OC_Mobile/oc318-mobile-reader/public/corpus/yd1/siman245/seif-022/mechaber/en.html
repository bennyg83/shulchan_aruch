One of the courtyard residents or alley residents who wished to become a teacher of children — his neighbors cannot protest against him; and likewise a teacher of children to whom a fellow came and opened a house to teach children beside him so others should come to him, or so the children with this one should come to that one — they cannot protest against him, as it says, "The Lord desired for His righteousness' sake to make the Torah great and glorious": {Rama: A rabbi who sits in a city and teaches the many — another sage may come and teach there too, even if it diminishes somewhat the livelihood of the first — such as where the community accepted the first upon themselves as rabbi and he takes a fee from them for this; even so the second may come to dwell there and hold rabbinical office in every matter like the first, if he is great and fit for this (Mahariv siman 151, Mahari in his rulings siman 158, see there responsa of gedolim). But if a visiting sage comes to the city, he may not diminish the fee of the rabbi dwelling there by making weddings and kiddushin and taking the fee that comes from them, since it is the rabbi's fee there; but it is permitted to make the wedding and give the fee to the established rabbi; and likewise he may judge between litigants of the city who come before him to judge, lest the city rabbi not be acceptable to them; but he may not rule on prohibition and permission or preach to conduct dominion in his fellow's place (Maharik shoresh 170, Maharad in responsum 22). One who was established as rabbi in a city — even if he established himself in some dominion, they do not remove him from greatness even though another greater than he came (Rivash siman 271). Even his son and son's son forever take precedence over others, as long as they fill their fathers' place in fear of Heaven and are somewhat wise (Rambam, chapter 1 of Laws of Kings); and in a place where the custom is to accept a rabbi for a fixed time, or to choose whom they wish — the authority is in their hands (Kol Bo); but all whom the community accepted upon themselves, and all the more if done with the ruler's consent — no great one in the world may dominate him and remove him (there in Rivash).}
