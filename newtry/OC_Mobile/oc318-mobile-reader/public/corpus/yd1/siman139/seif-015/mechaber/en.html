Books of non-Jews that are in the hand of a Jew: there is one who says that there is no prohibition to sell them. {Rama: And there are those who say that it is forbidden to sell them to any non-Jew if they are books that are used to sing with them for idolatry (Beit Yosef in the name of Semahot Gedolot and Semag; and Tosafos, first chapter of Avodah Zarah, daf 14; and Hagahot Maimoni; and Kol Bo). And there are those who say that specifically to the priests it is forbidden to sell, but not to other non-Jews (Piskei Mahari, siman 27), and one who is stringent — blessing will come upon him. And even to sell them parchment and ink with which to write their religious books, some are stringent to forbid it (Hagahot Ashiri, first chapter of Avodah Zarah; and Piskei Mahari, siman 112).} And there is one who says that it is forbidden to lend for the purpose of building a house of elilim of non-Jews, or for its ornaments, or for its appurtenances, and all the more so to sell it appurtenances such as incense-pans; and one who refrains will succeed. And one should not bind the books of non-Jews, except for the books of judges and scribes. And if he is concerned due to enmity, whatever he can evade, he should evade (and see below siman 151).
