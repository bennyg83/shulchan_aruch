Since he extinguished them for his own need, etc. This implies that selling or pledging the candles does not help if he extinguished them with the intention of relighting them, and all the more so if they went out by themselves. Selling or pledging helps only because it demonstrates that he extinguished them for his own need and not in order to relight them. Even the authority who holds in seif 12 that selling or pledging does not constitute nullification concedes here that it is effective, since the sale serves only as evidence that the extinguishing was done with no intention to relight them. This is the first opinion cited by the Tur. Afterwards, the Tur cites the opinion of Rabbeinu Yonah, who is lenient in two respects, because there are two possible acts of nullification: first, extinguishing; second, selling or pledging. Therefore, he states that even if we know with certainty that the candles were extinguished in order to relight them, they are permitted, because the act of extinguishing nullifies them. Likewise, even if they went out by themselves, they are permitted if he sold or pledged them, because the sale itself constitutes nullification. In the Beit Yosef's explanation of Rabbeinu Yonah's words, it is written that giving the candles to a Jew is not equivalent to selling them. This is astonishing, for the Beit Yosef himself immediately afterwards cites the Ran, who writes, 'Therefore, if they gave them or sold them, etc.' It appears straightforward that when the Beit Yosef wrote that giving them to a Jew is ineffective, he meant that they were not given to him as a gift, but merely entrusted to him for safekeeping, so the owner's name did not cease to be associated with them. If they were given as an outright gift, however, it is obvious that this is equivalent to a sale, for the rationale is the same. So too ruled my father-in-law, of blessed memory. In siman 146, seif 8, the Rama ruled, based upon the Beit Yosef, that regarding all accessories of idolatry, pledging or selling them constitutes nullification. See our comments there.
