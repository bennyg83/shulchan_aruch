An idol belonging to a Jew cannot be nullified. For it is written, 'You shall not plant for yourself an asheirah beside the altar.' Just as an altar requires burial, so too an idol belonging to a Jew requires burial. Regarding an idol belonging to a non-Jew, however, it is written, 'the carved images of their gods,' implying that he can invalidate his own god.
And its offering cannot be nullified. For it is written, 'the sacrifices of the dead.' Just as a dead person can never be nullified, so too an idolatrous offering can never be nullified. It appears to me that the reason an offering is more severe than the idol itself is that the idol and its adornments depend upon their wholeness. As long as the idol is whole, he worships it and maintains its adornment. Once it is damaged, however, this intention is nullified. This is not so with an offering, for even a small and insignificant item may be offered before it. Consequently, its prohibition does not lapse when it is damaged.
