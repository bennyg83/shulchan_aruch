An idol of a Jew has no nullification. For it is written, “You shall not plant for yourself an asherah near the altar”; just as the altar requires burial, so too the idol requires burial. But regarding that of a gentile, it is written “the idols of their gods,” implying that he can invalidate his god.
And its offering has no nullification. For it is written “sacrifices of the dead”; just as a dead person has no nullification forever, so too idolatry. It appears to me that the reason is that an offering is worse than the idol itself. The idol itself and its adornments depend on its wholeness, for as long as it is whole he worships it, and likewise maintains it as adornment; but if it is invalidated, that thought is nullified from it. This is not so with an offering, for even a small and insignificant thing is offered before it, and therefore the prohibition does not depart if it becomes invalidated.
