Even if there is no manner, etc. And Beit Yosef and the Rambam ruled like the Rabbanan — end his language. And thus the Rambam: chapter 3 of Hilkhot Avodat Kochavim, law 4 — if he slaughtered a locust before it he is exempt unless its worship is in such a manner; and likewise if he slaughtered before it a blemished animal he is exempt unless its worship is in such a manner. An idol that they worship with a stick — if he broke a stick before it he is liable and it becomes forbidden; if he threw a stick before it he is liable and it is not forbidden — end his language. And it is astonishing regarding the Mechaber that he did not mention the Rambam's opinion at all — for it is not his way to omit the Rambam's opinion. And it appears he follows his reasoning in Kesef Mishneh there, who forced himself greatly to align the Rambam's words here — see there. But in truth the Rambam's words are clear and rise exactly as Tosafot wrote; and he holds that since he challenges from the braita on locust, evidently he holds the braita deals with worship in such a manner — for otherwise "let Rav say I, who say even per the Rabbanan"; and that which they exempt is when its worship is not in such a manner. And if so, whence does Shas know — perhaps here too the braita deals when its worship is not in such a manner? Rather, certainly Shas holds that if its worship is not in such a manner, R. Yehudah is not liable — for he is not less than a blemished animal, where a verse derives exemption there; and likewise it is clear in Tosafot's words there (daf 51). And now the Rambam too ruled like R. Yehudah (as the Raavad and Tur ruled, unlike Migdol Oz and Beit Yosef and where they wrote he ruled like the Rabbanan) — except that where its worship is not in such a manner, for R. Yehudah too he is exempt like a blemished animal; and this is why the Rambam paired locust and blemished animal together. And all this is clear, and such is Tosafot's opinion there: where there is no manner of worshipping it with a locust at all, all exempt; and likewise is the opinion of the poskim like R' Yitzchak that the Ran brought — exempt and not forbidden; and he wrote that the Ramban agreed to this, see there.
