If he slaughtered, etc. Tur and perek like R' Yitzchak, and like the Raavad's position who wrote that Rav Yehudah and R' Nachman hold like R' Yitzchak and like the Tanna of Siftei — and not like the Gemara's rejection that the Tanna of Siftei is not like anyone; and R. Yochanan who said "whence for one who slaughters, etc." holds like the Chachamim; and the halachah is like Rav Yehudah, for R' Nachman and R' Yitzchak hold like him; and he explains: even if there is no manner, etc. — as written above; and as Rashi explains in the passage "if he slaughtered, etc." — except that he explains that even with a stick one need not pass with a stick, but it is a stringency: even though they worship it with a stick, nevertheless throwing is exempt. And with this the difficulty of Tosafot there in the passage "liable but" on Rashi is resolved. But the Tur's words are difficult like Tosafot's question on Rashi. And the Rambam ruled like the Tanna of Siftei and ruled like the Chachamim and like R. Yochanan and explained like Rav Nachman; and he omitted R' Yitzchak, who holds that R' Yitzchak and R' Nachman disagree. And the Ramban holds that Rav and R. Yochanan disagree, but he ruled like R. Yochanan and per the Gemara's establishment — not that all agree, etc. — and Rav's position is absent; and Rabbenu Nissim elaborated there. (Extract) If he slaughtered, etc. — Rashi and Tosafot's position: R' Nachman and Rav Yehudah and R. Yochanan do not disagree; and the rejection from above "not all, etc." does not exist; and the halachah is like R. Yehudah regarding locust, and the Tanna of Siftei as well (end).
