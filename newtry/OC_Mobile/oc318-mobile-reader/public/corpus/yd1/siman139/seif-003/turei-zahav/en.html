Such as fatty meat. This is how the text should read. The Perishah explains that this meat includes the flesh of wild animals and birds, which is called an offering and is forbidden because it belongs to the general category of animal flesh. End quote. I do not know the basis or proof for this, for the Sages explicitly said that it must be something similar to what is offered upon the altar, and neither a wild animal nor a bird is offered inside the Temple. From where do we know that the matter depends merely upon belonging to the same general category?
A scattering that breaks apart. Rashi explains that it resembles the sprinkling of blood inside the Temple, which is not cast as one connected object but breaks apart and falls in droplets. This excludes throwing a stick. Breaking a stick, however, resembles slaughter, which breaks the animal's neck.
He is liable, but it is not forbidden. The Ran writes that regarding liability, he becomes liable through either one of two grounds. Either the act resembles service performed inside the Temple, which is derived from the verse, 'One who sacrifices to gods shall be destroyed,' or it is the customary manner of worshiping that idol, which is derived from the verse, 'How did these nations serve their gods?' Regarding the prohibition of an idolatrous offering, however, it is forbidden only when the act resembles service performed inside the Temple, as derived from the verse, 'the fat of their sacrifices.'
