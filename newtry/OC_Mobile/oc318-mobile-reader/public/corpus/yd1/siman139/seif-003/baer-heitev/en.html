An offering. Beit Yosef writes that according to Rambam, anything found in the place where idolatry is worshipped is forbidden, even if it is not a type offered upon the altar. Candles brought into the place of worship are therefore offerings and cannot be nullified. Bach approves this and writes that a conscientious person should be stringent regarding all objects found in a house of idolatry, because perhaps they entered the place of worship. Taz explains that the meat mentioned here means animal meat specifically, not meat of a wild animal or bird.
That breaks apart. Rashi explains that this resembles the sprinkling of blood in the Temple, which is not a continuous mass but breaks into droplets. Throwing a stick is therefore excluded, while breaking a stick resembles slaughter because the neck of the animal is broken.
Liable. Shach writes that according to the Tur, regarding liability, even if this idol is not normally worshipped with a stick, he is liable for breaking it, though the stick itself does not become forbidden.
Threw it. This applies where throwing is not its normal mode of worship. If its worship is by throwing, he is liable, although the object thrown does not become forbidden. Ran.
