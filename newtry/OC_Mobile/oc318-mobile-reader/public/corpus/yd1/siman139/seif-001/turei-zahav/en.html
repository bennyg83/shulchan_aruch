That belonging to a non-Jew is forbidden immediately. For it is written, 'You shall burn the carved images of their gods in fire,' which teaches that once he carved it, it became a god for them. Regarding an idol belonging to a Jew, however, it is written, 'and places it in secret,' and he is not liable until he performs the acts that are done in secret. It appears obvious to me that even a Jew who is an apostate to idolatry is called a Jew, and his idol is not forbidden until he worships it, for although he sinned, he remains a Jew, as is stated in many places. The same applies regarding nullification, for he cannot nullify an idol, just as a Jew cannot, as Rashi explicitly wrote, cited by the Beit Yosef in siman 145, s.v. 'he plastered it, etc.' This is unlike what the Derishah wrote in a gloss to siman 145, that a Jew who is an apostate to idolatry has the status of a non-Jew. We derive the law here from verses that distinguish between a non-Jew and a Jew, and one may not expound the rationale of the verses to alter this law. Rather, they are decrees of the King, and a Jew does not render an idol forbidden until he worships it.
