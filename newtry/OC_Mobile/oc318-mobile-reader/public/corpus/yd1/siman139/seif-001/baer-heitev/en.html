A Jew. Taz writes that even an apostate Jew is still called a Jew and his act does not prohibit until he actually worships the object, for although he sinned he remains a Jew. Likewise, he cannot nullify idolatry, just as an ordinary Jew cannot. This is unlike Hagahot Derishah in siman 145, which treats an apostate Jew like a non-Jew. Nekudot HaKesef writes that Rambam, chapter 2 of Hilchot Avodah Zarah, appears to support the Derishah.
Becomes forbidden. If a non-Jew erected another brick belonging to a Jew and bowed to it, the owner's other brick is not forbidden, for perhaps he desired only the one he erected. That erected brick is forbidden and can never be nullified. Hagahot Ashri. The Gemara leaves unresolved the case of an egg whose erection is not recognizable, and therefore we rule stringently. Rambam rules likewise. See siman 146. Shach.
