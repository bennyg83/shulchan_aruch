It is, etc., whether, etc. This is clear from several places, as will be stated below.
And its appurtenances, etc. There on 52a Rav Huna asks, etc.: ‘If we say,’ etc., ‘rather,’ etc.
We rule like Rabbi Akiva, as above. The same applies to its ornaments, for they are included among its appurtenances. This is why the Gemara challenges from appurtenances, etc., and does not answer by distinguishing ornaments.
And its offering, etc. Meaning its offering is also not forbidden immediately, as written below seif 2, and as Rashi there explains in the passage "for sacred offerings, etc."
And he made, etc. See seif 3.
A Jew, etc. The Gemara there, 46a and 53b.
