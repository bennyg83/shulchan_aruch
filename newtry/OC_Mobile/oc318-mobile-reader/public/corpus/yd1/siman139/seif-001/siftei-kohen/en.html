A Jew who stood up a brick to bow to it and did not bow to it, and a gentile came and bowed to it — it is forbidden and cannot be nullified; but if a gentile stood up another brick from the Jew's property and bowed to it — it is not forbidden, for perhaps he only desired the one he himself stood up — end Hagahot Ashiri there, cited by Darchei Moshe; and likewise Tosafot chapter R' Yishmael (AZ 53b). And in Shas, chapter Kol HaTzlamim (AZ 46), it is a question regarding a brick where its standing-up is not evident, and it remains undecided — and we are strict; and so ruled Rambam chapter 8 of Hilkhot Avodat Kochavim; and see below siman 146.
