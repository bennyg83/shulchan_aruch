Challah outside Eretz Yisrael, etc. Specifically challah outside the Land, whose essence is only rabbinic. But challah of Eretz Yisrael, even nowadays, although it is only rabbinic, since its root is Torah law, is not included in this, as explained in the previous siman. Specifically regarding challah outside the Land did they rule leniently in this way. But with other rabbinic prohibitions one always requires sixty, and it is forbidden to nullify them ab initio, as above at the end of siman 99. See the Beit Yosef at the end of this siman, who brings the Rashba's words distinguishing this way.
Because it does not render a mixture forbidden, etc. In the Talmud, chapter Kol HaBasar 104b, we say that we do not decree placing it on the table because of eating, since challah outside the Land is only rabbinic. Both reasons are needed. The reason that it does not render a mixture forbidden is needed to explain why we are not concerned lest the challah become mixed into the food. The reason that it is only rabbinic is needed so that we not decree placing it on the table because of eating. This rejects what the Beit Yosef wrote, that from the Rosh's statement that we do not decree placing because of eating, it is proven that he holds like the Ri; this is not so, for the rule that we do not decree placing because of eating is an explicit Talmudic statement. Rather, it is as I explained. I later found support in Rabbeinu Shimshon and in Ma'adanei Melekh, p. 125b. Nevertheless, it appears that this applies specifically to challah, since bread commonly mixes in, and therefore, were it not that it does not render a mixture forbidden, we would decree. But with other prohibitions we are not concerned lest they mix, as explained above in siman 88 s.k. 2, that one may eat at one table kosher meat with a non-Jew eating neveilah meat, and likewise with other prohibitions. This resolves why we do not also permit because of the mixture itself on the ground that it is only rabbinic, saying that just as we do not decree placing because of eating since it is only rabbinic, so too we should not decree because of mixture since it is only rabbinic. Rather, with bread, mixture is common and there is more reason to decree. Examine carefully. In any case, one could also answer that we do not compare decrees to one another, as Tosafot wrote there, s.v. u-mina teimra, etc.; see there.
And some disagree, etc. In Ma'adanei Melekh at the end of p. 125 he challenged the Tur, Beit Yosef, and Rav, saying that the Rosh's view is on the contrary like the Rambam, that it is nullified in a majority even for non-kohanim. He inferred this from the Rosh's conclusion, where he wrote that it appears to him that the second challah of the Mishnah is what the Yerushalmi refers to when it says one gives it to an am ha'aretz kohen, for they were lenient with it since it is only for recognition, and they were also lenient to permit it to an Israelite through nullification in a majority. He therefore wrote that one who relies on permitting it by a majority loses nothing. I do not understand his final intent. On the contrary, from here it is proven that the Rosh's conclusion is like the Ri, for he wrote that the Yerushalmi refers to the second challah of the Mishnah. The flow of his words is as follows: first he brought the Rambam and Ri, then the Ramban's answer that our Talmud's statement that it renders a mixture forbidden means half and half, without yet deciding. Then he brought the Rosh's words, which disagree with the Yerushalmi's view that one gives it to an am ha'aretz kohen. Then he wrote his own conclusion on all this: the second challah of the Mishnah, where they would give two challot, one for the fire, which is the primary rabbinic challah and was burned, and they did not permit eating it in tumah, while the second challah was eaten by a kohen and was only for recognition, as the Rosh explicitly wrote just before, is what the Yerushalmi meant by giving it to an am ha'aretz kohen, since it is only for recognition. With that challah they were lenient to permit it to an Israelite through nullification in a majority. The passage in Bekhorot speaks even regarding an Israelite. Thus the Ramban's answer is no longer needed; the statement that a Jew's challah renders a mixture forbidden refers to the first challah outside the Land. This is the flow of the Rosh's words, and so it is implied in Tosafot at the beginning of Kol HaBasar and in the Agudah, that the leniency that it does not render a mixture forbidden applies to the second challah outside the Land, not the first. Since for us only one challah is separated, it has the law of the first challah, as is proven many times in the Rosh. All this is clear. Therefore the Rav was certainly correct in ruling that nowadays challah is not nullified for non-kohanim except in one hundred and one, since nowadays we separate only one challah, as explained in the previous siman. The Tur's words are also correct in what I wrote, that the Rosh's conclusion is like the Ri; otherwise his words cannot be settled. What the Beit Yosef wrote, because the Rosh wrote that we do not decree placing, is certainly incorrect, as I wrote above in s.k. 2. Maharshal in chapter Kol HaBasar siman 3 wrote that the Rosh brought the Ri's opinion last, but this is astonishing, for afterward he brought the Ramban, who contradicts the Ri; rather, it is as I explained. It is slightly difficult that he should have explained that the second challah is nullified in a majority. It appears that since the Rosh wrote that nowadays only one challah is separated, and the Tur too wrote above that where there is an adult kohen who immersed from keri only one challah is separated, etc., he stated the matter plainly. But if there is a difficulty, it is in the opposite direction against the Tur: why did he write that every challah that is rabbinic may be given even to an am ha'aretz kohen? This implies even where only one challah is separated, such as where there is a minor kohen or an adult who immersed from keri. So too it seems from the Beit Yosef and other later authorities that this is the Tur's view. But this is not so according to the Rosh, who wrote that specifically the second challah outside the Land is given to a kohen because it is only for recognition. If so, where only one challah is separated it is not given to an am ha'aretz kohen. One must say that the Tur in this respect is against the Rosh's view, and holds that challah outside the Land in every case may be given to an am ha'aretz kohen, but for non-kohanim it is forbidden until one hundred and one. So too is the view of the Rokeach, siman 359, that it is given to an am ha'aretz kohen in every case and forbidden to non-kohanim until one hundred and one. However, the practical law requires study, since it has been explained that the Rosh's view is that it is not given to an am ha'aretz kohen, and this is also Tosafot's view in chapter Kol HaBasar 104b, that one gives it to an am ha'aretz kohen only where there is no chaver kohen, or where there is a chaver kohen who is wealthy and the am ha'aretz kohen is poor. This is also Rabbeinu Shimshon's view and Rabbi Ovadiah of Bartenura's view in Challah chapter 4, and so Maharshal ruled in chapter Kol HaBasar siman 3.
That it renders its mixture forbidden, etc. Sefer HaTerumah wrote that one must be careful that a kohen not eat it in a bowl with hot broth, lest he forbid a non-kohen from eating from that bowl. The Tur brings this. The Bach wrote that according to this, when challah falls into a dish and the pot does not contain enough to nullify the challah, the dish should be sold or given to a kohen where there is a majority of non-challah, and the utensil requires kashering; if it is earthenware, it must be broken. He wrote this in passing, for certainly the earthenware utensil too is kashered by hagalah three times regarding challah outside the Land, as stated in the Yerushalmi, Baal HaItur, Rashba, and Tur above in siman 121, since it has no Torah root. So too the Mechaber wrote above at the end of siman 114 regarding bishul akum, and the same applies to challah, for they have one law, as stated in the Rashba; all the more so here, where there is a majority of non-challah.
And if it again became mixed, etc. He used the language "if it again became mixed" to exclude a case where one did not separate challah at all. In that case, even if the dough does not contain one hundred and one against the challah, it is permitted in every respect, as below, for one may eat and continue eating and afterward separate, as the Darkhei Moshe wrote. Mahari Weil wrote in responsum siman 48 regarding dough that was cooked without challah having been taken, that one should still take it, and should take a little so that the rest of the dough has one hundred and one against the challah and the dough not be forbidden by the challah. This requires study, for we say that with challah outside the Land one may eat and then separate, and we do not distinguish whether the bread contains one hundred and one against the challah or not. It appears that since challah outside the Land is only rabbinic, we do not rule stringently that it forbids retroactively, but only from now on if it became mixed after the challah was separated. Therefore it seems to me that even according to those who are stringent that it is nullified only in one hundred and one, that is if it became mixed after it was separated, since the name challah had already rested upon it. But where the name challah never rested upon it, one should not be stringent. All the more so according to the Rambam, who holds that even ab initio one may mix it half and half in such a case. So it appears to me. It further appears to me that even according to Mahari Weil, it is obvious that if there was sixty in the water in which the dough was cooked, it is permitted because of the rule: remove its own kind as if it were absent, and the different kind overpowers and nullifies it. If so, he can take a little such that the water has sixty against it, and it is permitted. These are the words of the Darkhei Moshe, and so the Bach ruled below at the end of siman 324.
If he did not eat the dough, etc. The Darkhei Moshe writes: it seems to me that this applies specifically in such a case, where the dough still exists and he can take another challah. But if the dough has already been eaten, he cannot ask release for it, for if so he would retroactively have eaten tevel. Although even now, if he does not ask release, he retroactively ate challah forbidden to non-kohanim, nevertheless since according to many poskim it is nullified in the majority, it is permitted; but tevel is forbidden according to everyone.
And he releases it like a vow, etc. The reason it is not forbidden even where there is one hundred and one except through release, because it is a davar she-yesh lo matirin, as above siman 102 seif 4 in the gloss, is that here, with vows of consecration, since it is not a mitzvah to seek release except under pressure, as above siman 203 seif 3, it is not considered a davar she-yesh lo matirin. So we say in Nedarim 59a, and so wrote the Ran there and at the beginning of 58.
For one may ask release for consecrations. As above siman 258, and likewise for terumot and maasrot, as below siman 331 seif 48. If so, this separation, where he said "let it be challah," is no better than a vow; one may release him from what he said, "let it be challah," and he then separates another.
And it is permitted to nullify it in a majority and eat it, etc. In this case even those who disagree concede. Specifically regarding a non-kohen eating it is one hundred and one required; but for a kohen eating it during his tumah, they agree that a majority suffices, as stated explicitly in the Asheri, Tur, and poskim; see there.
To nullify it in a majority. This implies that at least a majority is required. Although above even half and half suffices, that is after the fact. To mix it ab initio requires a majority. The Beit Yosef answered this way according to the Rambam. In truth, however, this is not compelling, for the Rambam wrote specifically regarding terumah that one nullifies it in a majority, and one can say he distinguishes between terumah and challah: with challah outside the Land half and half suffices, but not with terumah outside the Land. So it must be according to the Tur, who wrote according to the Rambam that challah can be mixed even ab initio half and half, showing he does not hold like the Beit Yosef. One must say he holds as I explained. According to this, it appears that for non-kohanim to eat it, one may not mix it ab initio to nullify it even in a majority; and according to the view that forbids even after the fact until one hundred and one, one may not mix it ab initio even in one hundred and one, even for a kohen to eat in tumah. The Ramban and other poskim hold that one may not nullify ab initio because we do not nullify prohibitions ab initio. Although the Mechaber ruled leniently here, nevertheless regarding eating by non-kohanim, since the Mechaber did not explicitly rule leniently, one should not nullify it ab initio. This is also the view of Rabbeinu Shimshon and the Rashba, and so appears to be the Bach's view in seif 4. However, if one did so intentionally, it appears that although above in siman 99 s.k. 18 it is explained that we penalize him, here it is permitted, since in any case some poskim permit mixing it even ab initio.
And there is no issue of a kohen, etc. Meaning that although generally we rule it is forbidden for a kohen to assist at the threshing floors, with challah outside the Land they were lenient in this. The Beit Yosef inferred from Rashi and the Tur that although the owner may give it to a kohen who assists him, it is nevertheless forbidden for the kohen to assist in order that they give him terumah outside the Land.
And specifically, etc. One does not separate challah from another dough when it is not min hamukaf; rather, one must place them together.
