And permit it. Shach writes that even if there are one hundred and one times the amount, we do not prohibit it as a davar sheyesh lo matirin on the ground that a sage could annul the consecration. In vows of consecration there is no mitzvah to seek annulment except under pressing circumstances, so it is not treated as a davar sheyesh lo matirin. The Gemara in Nedarim says so. If dough from which challah was not separated was cooked, one may still separate challah afterward. One need not leave so much dough that the challah will be less than one part in one hundred and one of the mixture. We are stringent regarding challah outside Eretz Yisrael, which is rabbinic, only where challah was first separated and afterward became mixed back in. Where it never acquired the status of challah before the mixture, we do not apply that stringency. This is why Rama says, "if it returned and became mixed," meaning after separation, excluding a case where it had not yet been separated. It further appears to me that if the water in which the dough was cooked contains sixty times the challah, it is permitted under the rule that one disregards the like-kind component and the unlike-kind component overwhelms and nullifies it. He may therefore take a small amount such that the water contains sixty times it. Darkei Moshe, Bach, and Shach.
