Creates meduma. The Shach wrote: it appears that specifically with challah, since it is bread and mixtures are common with it, we would have decreed if not for the rule that it does not create meduma. But with other prohibitions we are not concerned lest they become mixed, as I wrote in siman 88; see there.
That it prohibits. Sefer HaTerumah wrote that one must be careful that a kohen not eat it in a bowl with hot soup, lest a zar become forbidden to eat from that bowl. The Bach wrote: according to this, one must be careful that if challah fell into a cooked dish and the pot does not have enough to nullify the challah, the cooked dish should be sold or given to a kohen where there is a majority of non-challah, and the utensil requires kashering; if it is earthenware it must be broken. End quote. The Shach wrote against him that he wrote this in passing, for certainly earthenware too is kashered by three hagalot for challah of the diaspora, as above in siman 121, since it has no Torah root; all the more so here, where there is a majority of non-challah. The Taz disagrees with the Bach's core law and wrote that it has already been explained that we do not give challah to a kohen nowadays. I do not know why they were stringent here and did not rely on leniency in a rabbinic matter to nullify in a majority. End quote.
And release. The Shach wrote: why is it not forbidden even with one hundred and one because it is a davar she-yesh lo matirin through a sage's release? Here, with vows of consecration, since there is no mitzvah to ask release except under duress, it is not considered a davar she-yesh lo matirin; so we say in Nedarim. If a dough from which challah was not taken was cooked, one should still take challah, and need not leave so much that the challah is not more than one part in one hundred and one against the dough. We are not stringent with challah of the diaspora, which is rabbinic, unless it became mixed after it was separated. But in such a case, where the name challah never rested on it, we are not stringent. Therefore the Rama used the wording “if it again became mixed,” meaning after separation, excluding where he had not yet separated. End quote. It further appears to me that if the water in which the dough was cooked has sixty against it, it is permitted because one removes its own kind as if absent and the non-identical kind overwhelms and nullifies it. If so, he can take a small amount until the water has sixty against it, and it is permitted. Darkhei Moshe, Bach, Shach.
His tumah. In this, even those who disagree concede. Specifically regarding a zar eating it one requires one hundred and one, but for a kohen in his days of tumah they agree that a majority is enough. It appears that to feed it to zarim one may not lechatchilah mix it to nullify in a majority. Likewise, according to the one who forbids even after the fact until one hundred and one, one may not lechatchilah mix it to nullify in one hundred and one. However, if he did so deliberately, although above in siman 99 we rule that we fine him, here it is permitted, since in any event some poskim permit mixing even lechatchilah. End quote from Shach.
Adjacent. Rather, one must bring them together. Shach.
