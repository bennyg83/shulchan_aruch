(Siman 323, Taz seif katan 2) He elaborated. He elaborated with arguments that have no substance. The matter is simple. When the Mishnah says the produce should rot, it states the legal consequence, not the available corrective procedure, and refers to one who does not wish to seek annulment. Nevertheless, the Gemara initially considered it a davar sheyesh lo matirin because in principle one can seek annulment, and therefore had to answer that there is no mitzvah to seek annulment. Taz further asks why terumah is not considered a davar sheyesh lo matirin if one can create the permission without loss by asking for annulment, as ruled in siman 102. Why does he attach this question to Semag? He should have asked it directly on the Gemara. Clearly there is no difficulty. Since there is no mitzvah to seek annulment of terumah, a person is not expected to do so, because consecrations are annulled only under pressure, as explained above in siman 213, seif 3. I wrote the same in Shach, seif katan 7. Likewise, the liability of karet applies where he does not annul the designation and persists. Taz says the same question will later be brought in the name of Raavad and Tosafot in Gittin, but later he cites nothing from Raavad. Nor is the Tosafot passage relevant. Tosafot there merely ask whether the warning is uncertain because the nazir might later seek annulment of his nezirus. Taz's statement that Rosh's words are puzzling, because otherwise there could never be lashes for a nazir or death for one who eats terumah, is likewise no difficulty. Those laws concern one who does not annul the status. His further claimed proof from Tosafot is also incorrect, as I explain below.
