(Siman 323, Taz s.k. 2) He elaborated with words that have no substance. The matter is simple: when the Mishnah teaches “they must rot,” it states the law, not the remedy, and it speaks where he does not wish to seek release. Nevertheless, it would have wanted to say that it is a davar she-yesh lo matirin, since in any case release by a sage is possible, and therefore it had to answer that there is no mitzvah to seek release. What he further wrote, that he is astonished by the conclusion in Perek HaNoder that terumah is not a davar she-yesh lo matirin because there is no mitzvah to ask release from it, asking how this differs from every davar she-yesh lo matirin where we rule in siman 102 that if the permissive act is in his power without loss it is a davar she-yesh lo matirin, etc., is itself astonishing. How does this question depend on the words of the Semag? He should have asked this against the body of the Talmud itself. Rather, it is certainly not difficult at all, because with terumah, since there is no mitzvah, one will not come to ask release for it, for one seeks release from consecrations only under pressure, as above in siman 213 seif 3, and so the Shach wrote in s.k. 7. Likewise, the liability of karet applies where he does not release it and remains in his rebellion. What he wrote, “and this question we will bring below in the name of the Raavad and in the name of Tosafot in Gittin,” is astonishing, for below nearby he mentions nothing in the name of the Raavad. Also what he brings nearby in the name of Tosafot in Gittin has no relevance here, for Tosafot ask that it is at least an uncertain warning, since perhaps he will ask release from his nezirut. What he wrote further, that the words of the Rosh are also astonishing, for according to this you would never find lashes for a nazir or death for one who eats terumah, etc., all this is not difficult at all, for it speaks where he does not seek release. Likewise, what he wrote, “and so it is further proven from Tosafot, etc.,” is not so, as I wrote nearby.
