Challah of the diaspora that is permitted, etc. Meaning the first challah of the preceding siman. It is the Mishnah there, according to the explanation that everything refers to the first challah, as written in the preceding siman.
Because, etc.; and even, etc. In the Yerushalmi: Rabbi Abbahu ruled in Botzrah that it requires a majority. Rabbi Yonah said: this teaches that it rises with less than one hundred and does not prohibit as one in one hundred and one. Rabbi Zeira said: the Mishnah says so, even one with one, for we learned that it is eaten with a zar at the table. So too we say in Bekhorot 27a, according to Rashi's explanation there s.v. “nullified,” etc. Although there it says specifically with a majority, that is to mix it lechatchilah; but if it mixed on its own, as when it returned, etc., even equal with equal. But the Rashba wrote that the halakhah does not follow Rabbi Zeira because of the Gemara above, and specifically a majority is required.
And some disagree, etc. Tosafot there s.v. “terumat,” etc., explain that there it concerns a kohen in the days of his tumah, and the Yerushalmi case concerns the second challah. See Tosafot, Menachot 67a s.v. “and his terumah,” etc. But the Rashba ruled as above and wrote that the Menachot case, where it creates meduma, concerns equal amounts, and according to Rabbi Zeira, less than one, etc.
Until one hundred, etc.; and not of its kind, etc. Like terumah, as stated in the Yerushalmi, Nedarim chapter 6: this was Rabbi Shimon's rule in the name of Rabbi Yehoshua: anything that has permitted status forthcoming, etc.; and anything that has no permitted status forthcoming, such as terumah, etc. The Ran brought it there 52a in brief, s.v. “and it is difficult,” etc. The Mishnah in Orlah chapter 2 says to be stringent regarding its own kind, and to be stringent and lenient, etc. In Chullin chapter Kol HaBasar: a pot in which terumah was cooked, etc.
(Likkut) And not of its kind, until sixty. As stated in the Yerushalmi in Nedarim, brought by the Ran there 52a. So too we say in the Gemara Chullin chapter 7, 97a, and chapter 8: a pot in which terumah was cooked, etc., by taste; and there 99a, Abaye said to him, etc., is it not sixty? Although it answers there, no, one hundred, that is according to the view that all prohibitions require one hundred. Torat HaBayit. See Tosafot there s.v. “ein,” etc.; it appears unnecessary, etc.
And if it again, etc. As stated in Eruvin 30b and Nedarim 59a.
Unless, etc. See Shach.
For one may request release, etc. Meaning even for consecrations, as stated in Arakhin 29a.
And it is permitted to nullify it, etc. There: Rabbah would nullify, etc.
And one may, etc. Mishnah chapter 4: it is given to any kohen. The Yerushalmi says about it: whether to a chaver kohen or to an am haaretz kohen.
That it is forbidden. Meaning that it is permitted to a kohen with terumah of the diaspora. Rashi and the Tur did not explain so, but that an am haaretz can give it to them.
And it must, etc. Tosafot, Yom Tov 9a s.v. “rolled.” It must be large, etc. So wrote the Rosh and the other poskim. Meaning as stated: one who says “all my grain is terumah” or “all my dough,” etc., because it is said “reishit,” etc., as stated in Chullin 136b. But in Bekhorot there s.v. “he eats,” Tosafot did not write so; see there. The Rosh in the first chapter of Yom Tov wrote that since it says “and afterward he separates,” and not “he leaves over enough for challah,” it implies unlike Rashi. Nevertheless, adjacent separation is not required, as “he eats and continues,” etc., and as he writes. This is specifically, etc.
And specifically, etc. Tosafot, Niddah 7a s.v. “and joins.” It is correct, etc., although we say, etc.
But challah, etc. From what is stated there, “challah of the diaspora,” etc.; and furthermore, it may be taken only from adjacent dough, as he writes. See the Rosh in the first chapter of Yom Tov there.
Lechatchilah. But after the fact it is fine, as stated at the beginning of chapter 2 of Terumot.
And all, etc. In Sifrei, that it is like terumah for every matter, derived from “like the terumah of the threshing floor, so shall you lift,” etc. This is what is stated at the end of Challah chapter 1: challah and terumah, etc.
