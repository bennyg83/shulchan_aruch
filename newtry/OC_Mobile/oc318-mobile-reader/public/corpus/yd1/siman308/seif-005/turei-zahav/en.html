If it is not an important item, it is nullified in the majority. Rashi wrote at the end of Temurah: If you ask from what we say in tractate Niddah, that a garment into which kilayim was woven may not be sold to a gentile, why is that thread not nullified in the majority? This is not a challenge, for with kilayim we do not say that it is nullified in the majority, since the essence of the prohibition is through mixture, and nullification in the majority does not apply to it; end quote. This is like the words of Tosafot that I brought above in siman 299 seif 1. One should not ask why the Tur wrote in siman 303 that with rabbinic kilayim, if one thread was removed, etc., and why it is not nullified in the majority, since it is rabbinic. One may say that regarding nullification in the majority, the Sages treated it like Torah law, that anything present before us forbids as it does by Torah law, for whatever the Sages enacted, they enacted in the pattern of Torah law. This is unlike where one item was removed and perhaps is no longer in the world, where they were lenient. So it appears to me.
