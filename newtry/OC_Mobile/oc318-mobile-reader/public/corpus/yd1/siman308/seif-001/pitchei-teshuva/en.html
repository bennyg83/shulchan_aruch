And work. See Responsa Shevut Yaakov, part 1, siman 73, who writes that once a firstborn fetus has put out its head, it is forbidden to make the mother perform work because this weakens the fetus, unlike Responsa Beit Yaakov, siman 7. To my mind there is a great difficulty here: why did the Taz not cite the baraita in Moed Katan 12a that similarly one may not mate a firstborn animal or disqualified consecrated animals? Rashi explains that this is considered making it perform work, and the verse says, "You shall not work with the firstborn of your ox." The Rambam likewise rules in Hilchot Meilah 1:9, and so does the Rosh there. The Mishneh LaMelech's words there are surprising, since a firstborn is male, so how can he write that the prohibition applies only where the animal is mounted? Pri Megadim, Orach Chaim, siman 536, and the gloss Kisei DeHarsana to Besamim Rosh likewise note this difficulty. See also Responsa Chatam Sofer, end of siman 302, concerning whether one may send a firstborn out to graze together with other animals. In Responsa Mahari Weil, siman 204, the questioner's words imply that it is forbidden because it is virtually certain that it will mount cows, making the act equivalent to intentional use. The Chatam Sofer does not give a fully decisive leniency there, but concludes that one who gives it to a shepherd, warning him not to allow it to mount cows and providing that the shepherd will lose his wages if he does, has not lost by relying on the lenient view. In siman 304 he states more clearly that there is no prohibition, since the prohibition was stated only where one deliberately places it among cows for breeding, but merely allowing it to graze among other animals is permitted. In siman 305 he again concludes that it is fully permitted, since even regarding the more stringent case of a cow, if one places it in a team merely to nurse and it later threshes on its own, this is permitted. He further writes that a small cord or strap tied to it as a marker so that people will not inadvertently use it is neither labor nor an excessive burden, because even with the red heifer anything done for its own need is permitted, all the more so here. It appears that even on Shabbat it may go out with such a marker in our karmelit.
