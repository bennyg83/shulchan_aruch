And work. See Responsa Shevut Yaakov, part 1, siman 73, who writes that if a firstborn-producing animal has begun giving birth and the fetus's head has emerged, it is forbidden to work the mother because of the weakening of the fetus, unlike Responsa Beit Yaakov, siman 7. I am greatly surprised that Taz did not cite the baraita in Moed Katan 12 stating similarly that one may not cause a firstborn or disqualified sacred animal to mate, because this is considered work, and the verse says, "You shall not work with the firstborn of your ox." Rashi explains this there, and Rambam, Hilchot Me'ilah chapter 1, halachah 9, and Rosh rule likewise. Mishneh LaMelech there is puzzling, because a firstborn is male, so how could he write that the prohibition applies only to an animal being mounted? Pri Megadim, Orach Chayyim siman 536, and Responsa Besamim Rosh with the gloss Kisei DeHarsena already note this. Responsa Chatam Sofer, at the end of siman 302, discusses whether a firstborn may be sent to pasture together with ordinary animals. Responsa Mahari Weil, siman 204, implies prohibition because it is nearly certain that the male will mount cows and this is effectively intentional. Chatam Sofer does not give an absolute leniency there, but concludes that one who gives it to a shepherd, instructs him not to let it mount cows, and conditions the shepherd's wages on this may rely on the lenient view. In siman 304 he writes more decisively that there is no prohibition unless one explicitly places it among animals for breeding; merely allowing it to graze among other animals is permitted. In siman 305 he likewise concludes that it is fully permitted, proving this from the more stringent law of a red heifer: if it was placed with the team only to nurse and later threshed by itself, there is no problem. He also explains Mishneh LaMelech there. He further writes regarding a small cord or strap tied to the firstborn as a marker so people not mistakenly use it: this is neither prohibited work nor an extra burden. Even with a red heifer anything serving its own need is permitted, all the more so here. It appears even on Shabbos one may let it go out with such a marker in our karmelit.
