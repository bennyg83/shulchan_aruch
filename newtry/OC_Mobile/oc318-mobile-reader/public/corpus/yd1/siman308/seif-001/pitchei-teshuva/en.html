And work. See Shevut Yaakov I siman 73, who wrote that with an animal giving birth for the first time, once the fetus has put out its head it is forbidden to work with the mother because of weakening the fetus, unlike Beit Yaakov responsa siman 7; see there. In my humble opinion, there is a great astonishment here: why did the Taz not bring the law of the baraita in Moed Katan 12: similarly, one may not mate a bekhor or disqualified consecrated animals? See there. Rashi explains that he is doing work with it, and it is written, "You shall not work with the bekhor of your ox." So too the Rambam z"l wrote in Hilkhot Me'ilah chapter 1 law 9, and the Rosh there. Know that the words of the Mishneh LaMelekh there are astonishing, for a bekhor is male, so how could he write that the prohibition applies only to a female that is mounted? See there. I found that Peri Megadim in Orach Chayim siman 536 and Besamim Rosh in the gloss Kisei DeHarsena there noted this. See Chatam Sofer responsa at the end of siman 302, regarding whether it is permitted to send a bekhor out to pasture with other animals. In Mahari Weil responsa siman 204, in the questioner's words, it is explicit that this is forbidden, because it is close to certain and practically impossible that it will not mount the cows, making it as though done intentionally, etc. See there, where he did not write a clear permission. Nevertheless, he concludes that one who permits giving it to a shepherd and informing him not to let it mount the cows, and that he will lose his wages because of this, loses nothing. However, in siman 304 there he wrote more clearly that there is no prohibition in the matter, for we found a prohibition only against expressly bringing it into the mating herd; but simply letting it graze among the other animals is not a problem. Also in siman 305 there he concludes that it is fully permitted, for even regarding a cow, where the law is stricter, if he brought it into the team so it would nurse and afterward it threshed on its own, we have no issue. See there, where he explains the above words of the Mishneh LaMelekh very well. He also wrote regarding a small cord or small strap that people want to tie on it as a sign so that they not stumble with it: it seems to me that this is neither work nor an extra burden, for even with a cow, anything for its need is permitted, all the more so here. It appears that even on Shabbat it may go out with it in our karmelit; see there.
