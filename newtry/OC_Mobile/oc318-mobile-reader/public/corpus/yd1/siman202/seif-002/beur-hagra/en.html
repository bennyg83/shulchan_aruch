Anything that is ordinarily, etc. This is the Mishnah at the end of chapter 9. The same applies to a person, for the law is derived there from the verse, ‘his flesh,’ etc.
Blackening, etc. See the Rosh on Mishnah 2 of chapter 9, concerning the ruling, ‘One should not immerse the kettle,’ etc., and see Rambam in his commentary there. The Rosh writes there in explanation of Rambam’s view, etc. It is likewise reasonable that the Mishnah does not refer to blackening, as stated here. The plain meaning of the Mishnah does not accord with Rambam’s interpretation, nor do the words of the Tosefta. The Rash cites the Tosefta on chapter 10, Mishnah 4: ‘If his feet were covered with dust,’ etc.; ‘a kettle,’ etc. One should therefore not rule leniently.
