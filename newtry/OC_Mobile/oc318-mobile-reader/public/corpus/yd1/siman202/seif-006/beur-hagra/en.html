A utensil, etc. This is Mishnah 1 there. See there.
If its opening is somewhat narrow. So writes Beit Yosef. It appears that the Tosefta cited by the Rash on Mishnah 6 of chapter 7 was difficult for him: ‘If he immersed in it,’ etc.; ‘How so?’ etc., as cited above in siman 201, seif 63. This is incorrect, however, because there the case is one in which he tilts it slightly to the sides. It is well known that even when the opening is wide, the water does not enter properly unless the utensil is tilted. See Taz.
It is permitted, etc. At the beginning of the third chapter of Chagigah, the Gemara explains that the Sages imposed this decree only for sacred food, but not for terumah, and certainly not for ordinary food.
And he should take care, etc. This is the wording of Mordechai at the end of Avodah Zarah: ‘They also ruled that a person must take care when immersing a glass vessel filled with water by placing it in a bucket filled with water, while the bucket is submerged in a cistern, not to rest the vessel on its base. Its weight may cause an interposition beneath it, as stated in the chapter Chomer BaKodesh, but rather,’ etc. Although the Gemara there states that this concern applies specifically to sacred food, but not to terumah, and certainly not to ordinary food, and according to Rava, whose ruling we follow, it does not apply even to sacred food, as explained there, ‘What difference is there between Rava’s view,’ etc., that applies only to light utensils. See Rashi there, s.v. lechaber. Regarding the weight of the utensil, see above, siman 120, seif 2, and Taz there. See also what Rabbeinu wrote there in seif katan 7.
Therefore, one should not, etc. This is self-evident, for this is worse than the handles of utensils that are not intended to remain attached, discussed in seif 3.
