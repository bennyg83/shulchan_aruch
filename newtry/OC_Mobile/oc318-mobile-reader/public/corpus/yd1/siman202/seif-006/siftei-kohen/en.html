Somewhat narrow, etc. — and Bach wrote that even if its mouth is wide, its immersion does not take effect.
If there is, etc. — that is, when the outer one is pure; but when the outer one also needs immersion, even if its mouth is not like its belly it is permitted, for since the immersion takes effect for the outer one it also takes effect for the inner one — as above, siman 201, seif 9.
He should not stick knives in, etc. — rather he should tie them with a thread and the like, in a loose matter — Or Zarua, general 58, din 97.
It is heavy, etc. This is the language of the Issur VeHeter: if one filled vessels and only afterward placed them into the cistern, the water in them is heavy and presses against the walls of the vessel, and the water of the cistern does not enter properly. But if the vessels were empty and he placed them into the cistern and they filled there, it is permitted. Nevertheless, one should not place many vessels one on top of another, for the upper ones press on the lower ones. If they are placed side by side in a bucket, it is permitted.
