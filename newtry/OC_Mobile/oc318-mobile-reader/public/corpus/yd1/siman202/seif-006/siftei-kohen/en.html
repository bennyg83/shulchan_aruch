Somewhat narrow, etc. — and Bach wrote that even if its mouth is wide, its immersion does not take effect.
If there is, etc. — that is, when the outer one is pure; but when the outer one also needs immersion, even if its mouth is not like its belly it is permitted, for since the immersion takes effect for the outer one it also takes effect for the inner one — as above, siman 201, seif 9.
He should not stick knives in, etc. — rather he should tie them with a thread and the like, in a loose matter — Or Zarua, general 58, din 97.
It is heavy, etc. Orchos HaChayyim there, law 95, writes: this applies specifically to vessels that were already filled, because when a vessel is full its weight presses upon its sides and creates a chatzitzah. But with an empty vessel there is no concern, because it is impossible that water will not enter there. Nevertheless, one should be careful not to place many vessels inside one vessel to immerse them together, even when they are empty, unless they are arranged so that one does not rest upon another, such as a vessel standing on its base or on its mouth. But placing many vessels side by side in a bucket is permitted l'chatchila, even if in the well they fall upon one another, because as they roll this way and that the water reaches the surface of every vessel. End.
