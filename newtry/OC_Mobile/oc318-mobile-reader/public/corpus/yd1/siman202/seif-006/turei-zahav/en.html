If its opening is somewhat narrow, etc. In the Mishnah it is stated without qualification that one may not immerse a utensil through its opening. The Rash explains: Any utensil that, when first inserted into the water, is inverted upon its opening, the water will never enter it, even if he inserts the entire utensil into the water, end quote. The Beit Yosef innovated, based on his own reasoning, that with a wide utensil the water enters it, and therefore he distinguished here in the Shulchan Aruch between a narrow opening and a wide one. My revered father-in-law, of blessed memory, disagreed with him and said that it is tested and proven that with every utensil, even a wide one, the water does not enter as far as the inner bottom of the utensil because of the air inside it. The truth is indeed so. However, with a wide utensil that does not have a large cavity, such as bowls and the like, the water does enter them, and in this regard the words of the Beit Yosef are correct. Accordingly, what is written in siman 201, seif 63, is readily understood: If one immerses a large pot and other utensils in a mikveh of precisely the required volume, he should immerse them through their openings. Yet here it is written that immersion through the opening is not a valid immersion. According to what I have written, this is resolved, for the large pot may be very wide. One may also say that above it did not mean literally through its opening, but rather that, while it is in the water, he tilts it somewhat.
Therefore, he should not thrust the knives, etc. This is from the words of the Mordechai at the end of Avodah Zarah. I cited it in siman 120, seif 2, and in siman 198, seif 28, and there I explained the law of an interposition caused by the weight of the utensil.
