(Collected) It is forbidden, etc. The language of the Rambam: one who dresses his fellow in kilayim — if the wearer was b'mezid, the wearer is lashed and the one who dresses transgresses because of "and before a blind person you shall not place a stumbling block"; and if the wearer did not know that the garment is kilayim and the one who dresses was b'mezid, the one who dresses is lashed and the wearer is exempt. And this is the language of the Tosefta chapter 3 of Makkot: one who dresses his fellow in kilayim and one who makes a nazir tamei, even though the one who dresses is b'mezid and the one who makes tamei is b'mezid, is liable; if both of them were shogeg, they are exempt. And therefore there is a scribal error, and thus it should read: when the one who dresses is b'mezid and the one who makes tamei is b'mezid, the one who dresses and the one who makes tamei are liable; if the wearer was b'mezid and the one who became tamei was b'mezid, the wearer and the one who became tamei are liable; if both of them were shogeg, both are exempt. But regarding one who rounds [the corners of the head] and one who destroys [the beard] and one who makes a bald spot and one who makes a scratch and a tattoo, it is not so: when both are b'mezid, both are liable, as is written in the Tosefta there, and as is written above in siman 180. And one can say here that only the wearer and the one who became tamei were commanded (etc.). (And see in the Bach, who also has the girsa in the Tosefta as above, and see there the reason of the matter. And according to the view of the Rambam in chapter 5 of Hilchot Nezirut and chapter 3 of Hilchot Avel he does not have the girsa "and one who makes a nazir tamei," but rather the girsa is "and one who makes a kohen tamei." Understand this.) (Collected) It is forbidden, etc. The language of the Rambam at the end of Hilchot Kilayim: one who dresses his fellow in kilayim — if the wearer was b'mezid, the wearer is lashed and the one who dresses transgresses because of "and before a blind person" [you shall] not place a stumbling block; and if the wearer did not know that the garment is kilayim and the one who dresses was b'mezid, the one who dresses is lashed and the wearer is exempt. And the Rosh wondered at him in his responsum to the Rashba, and see Kesef Mishneh there, and see above siman 180 seif 11, where he wrote that if both of them are b'mezid, both are lashed, and so it is in the Tosefta there, and as is written there, see there (etc.).
