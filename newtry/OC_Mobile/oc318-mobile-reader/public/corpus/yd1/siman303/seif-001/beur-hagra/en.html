He does not remove it, etc.; and if, etc. Meaning that even when he discovers the kilayim in his own garment, the law is the same, in accordance with our text, "in his garment," and as stated at the end of chapter 3 of Menachot.
Likewise in the study hall, etc. Yerushalmi, final chapter of Kilayim, cited by the Rash there on mishnah 4. He writes, "and in the chapter," and perhaps, and so forth. The Rosh writes similarly there and in Niddah. He further writes in his commentary: "To me it appears that this applies specifically when he himself finds kilayim in his garment, but a person who sees it in his fellow's garment," and so forth. He writes similarly in Niddah in his final answer, in accordance with the reading "in his garment."
