(Siman 303, seif 1, in the gloss) Some say that if the wearer was unaware of the prohibition, one need not tell him while he is in the marketplace. Because of human dignity, one should remain silent and not cause him to desist from an inadvertent transgression. In Responsa Noda BiYehudah, Orach Chaim, siman 35, he proves that where failure to separate the person from the prohibition would cause him to transgress throughout his life, all authorities agree that one may not remain silent on account of human dignity, even when the transgression is inadvertent. However, in Shaagat Aryeh, siman 58, he rules in accordance with the Rambam that one may never remain silent on account of human dignity. The act that the other person performs as a result of one’s silence and failure to stop him is itself regarded as an act attributable to that failure to intervene. I have likewise demonstrated this in my novellae to Shabbat 40 and in my novellae to Sanhedrin 73. The Rosh, however, follows his own position, for he does not accept this reasoning, as is evident from what he writes in Shabbat at the beginning of 3a.
