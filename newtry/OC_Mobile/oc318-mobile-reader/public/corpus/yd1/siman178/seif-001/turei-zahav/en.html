Nor shall he grow the locks of his head. These are the words of the Rambam. It appears that this prohibition applies even without any shaving at all. That is, in many countries the way of the non-Jewish warriors is to grow their hair very long and not shave themselves at all; a Jew shall not do so. This too is included in the practice of those who grow a bloris, which the Sages prohibited. Although afterward the Rambam wrote another explanation of those who grow a bloris, namely that they leave the hair in the middle, nevertheless the underlying reason is one and the same. The general principle is that a Jew must distinguish himself from them. What difference is there between this form and that form, since the countries differ in their customs in this regard? The Beit Yosef wrote that what the Rambam wrote afterward, "nor shall he shave the sides," is an explanation of the opening statement, "nor shall he grow, etc." This is very strained, and so too wrote the Perishah and my father-in-law, of blessed memory.
And he shall leave the loose hair behind him. This is the correct reading, and so it appears in the Rambam, chapter 11 of Hilchos Avodah Zarah.
Nor shall he build places, etc. The Ra'avad wrote concerning this: "I do not know what this means, whether it means that he shall not make forms as they do, or that he shall not place a sign by which to gather the multitude, as they do"; end quote. Therefore, both practices should be prohibited.
We burn for kings. Meaning, their utensils, as an honor to them, so that another person should not use them.
