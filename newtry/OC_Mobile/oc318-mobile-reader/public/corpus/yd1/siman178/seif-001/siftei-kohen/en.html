And he shall not grow his forelock. That is, he shall not shave on the sides etc. — so wrote Eitan HaEzrachi, following what Beit Yosef wrote that "he shall not shave" etc., meaning he shall not shave his forelock. But Bach disagreed and wrote it is a matter unto itself: "he shall not grow his forelock" means those who grow their hair for adornment and beauty like maidens do — as a manner of haughtiness and arrogance; and likewise Perishah.
Of idol worship etc. But those built for their council to confer about their matters and for law — there is no prohibition to build in the style of those halls — Bach; and see there.
Red garments etc. For it is not the way of modest people to dress in red, and the color black is the way of modesty and humility — as we say: one whose evil inclination overcomes him should dress in black and wrap himself in black, etc.; and furthermore there is a tradition in our hands to be particular about red garments for our people — Maharik there.
But a matter, etc. See Maharik, who elaborated on this and brought several proofs. One proof he brought is from Rashi in Perek Kol HaBasar, Chullin 107, regarding the first waters that caused one to eat pork. Rashi explained that there was a Jewish shopkeeper who sold slaughtered meat to Jews and neveilah to non-Jews; a Jew came to eat and did not wash his hands, and he fed him pork. This shows that a Jew need not be distinguished by his clothing in any event, end of his words. My father the Gaon z"l said that this is astonishing, for this incident that Rashi wrote appears in Midrash Rabbah, Parashat Balak, and it states there that it was during a time of persecution, etc., until he said to him privately, "I am Jewish"; see there. It is clear that they worshipped Hashem secretly, and netilat yadayim was the sign for the shopkeeper, not another sign. In a time of persecution it is certainly permitted to change one's clothing so they will not recognize him as a Jew, as Maharik himself wrote there, and as explained above in siman 157 seif 2. This requires study.
