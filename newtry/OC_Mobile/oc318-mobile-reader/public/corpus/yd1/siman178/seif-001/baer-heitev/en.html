His head. Bach explains that this means growing the hair for adornment and beauty, in the manner of young unmarried women, as an expression of vanity and pride. Taz writes that in many countries gentile warriors customarily let their hair grow very long and do not cut it at all, and a Jew should not do so. This too is included in the prohibition against growing a blorit. Regarding the phrase "he shall not leave the hair," the word "from behind" is missing, as it appears in Rambam.
Places. Ra'avad writes: "I do not know what this means, whether he may not make forms like those they make, or whether he may not erect a sign used to gather the public in the manner they do." Taz writes that both should therefore be prohibited. Bach, however, writes that structures built as meeting halls where they gather to deliberate over their affairs or for judgment may be built in the same architectural form as their halls.
