His head. The Bach explained that this refers to growing long hair for beauty and elegance, like young women do — in a manner of arrogance and pride. The Taz wrote that this refers to the practice of warriors in many lands who grow their hair exceedingly long and never shave themselves at all — a Jew must not do so, and this is also included in the category of "growing a forelock" that the Sages prohibited. Regarding "he shall not let his forelock grow," the word "from behind" is missing, and so it is in Maimonides.
Places. The Ra'avad wrote about this: "I do not know what this is — whether it means not to make the same shapes as they make, or not to place a sign for assembling crowds as they do." So far his words. The Taz wrote: therefore both should be prohibited. The Bach wrote: but structures such as those built as a meeting hall for [non-Jews] to deliberate their affairs and for judgment — there is no prohibition to build them in the likeness of those palaces.
Of the kings. That is, vessels used for their service — on account of their honor, so that no one else may use them.
