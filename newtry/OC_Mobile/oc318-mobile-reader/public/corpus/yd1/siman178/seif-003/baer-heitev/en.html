He shall bring. Shach writes that it is specifically forbidden to bring the item there and place it next to the pieces of bread, because of the prohibition of "those who set a table for Gad." But if it was already there, one need not remove it. This is also implied by Rashi and is stated in Orach Chayyim, end of siman 180. As for the pieces of bread themselves, there is a mitzvah to leave them so that food will be ready for a poor person.
A prohibition. Shach writes that the custom in these lands to make a meal on the night before the infant's circumcision, called a vach nacht, is not prohibited. The prohibition applies specifically to placing various foods on a table and leaving them there, which resembles setting a table for Gad. A meal itself is permitted. Taz writes that Levush, based on this reason, prohibits the custom of some to set a whole loaf on the table for the entire Friday night, because it spread from the practice of setting a table for Gad. Taz disagrees. In his view, the concern applies specifically on the night before a circumcision, where the practice is associated with the infant's mazal, and Gad means fortune. On Friday night, however, leaving whole loaves for lechem mishneh is done for the honor of Shabbos. Indeed, even before Shabbos, as soon as the loaves are removed from the oven, they are placed on the table in honor of Shabbos. There is therefore no prohibition.
