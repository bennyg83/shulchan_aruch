He shall not bring a whole loaf. This is because it is stated, "Those who set a table for Gad," meaning, for the sake of idolatry. However, there is a mitzvah to leave pieces of bread alone, so that food will be ready for a poor person.
There are places, etc. The Levush wrote that for this reason one should prohibit the custom practiced by some people to set the table on Shabbos nights with a whole loaf throughout the night, for it spread from the statute of "those who set a table for Gad." This does not appear correct in my humble opinion. Specifically on the night before a circumcision, when they do this for the infant's fortune, it is related to "those who set a table for Gad," because Gad denotes fortune. This is not so on Shabbos nights, when they leave the bread of lechem mishneh in honor of Shabbos, whose honor is expressed through lechem mishneh. This has nothing to do with fortune, and there is no prohibition at all. Indeed, even before Shabbos, immediately when the loaves are removed from the oven, they place them on the table in honor of Shabbos.
