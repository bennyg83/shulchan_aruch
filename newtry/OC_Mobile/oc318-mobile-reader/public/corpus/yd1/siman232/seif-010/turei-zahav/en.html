Because he thought it was prohibited, etc. This implies that he did not say so explicitly, but now states that at the time of the vow he thought the act was forbidden. It is difficult why the Shulchan Aruch concludes that the oath did not take effect, implying that no release is needed. How does this differ from seif 6, where one must state explicitly the reason for the vow, namely, “because she stole my purse,” and his private intention does not suffice? According to what I wrote in seif 7, here too he must have said at the time of the vow that he was vowing because he thought the act was forbidden. If he merely vowed not to do a certain act and afterward says that he thought it was prohibited, release is required. So it appears to me.
