If he said: Konam, etc. As stated in Shevuot 29a concerning one who says, etc. Such a declaration is not a vow at all, as stated in Nedarim 15a: 'Do we leave him,' etc.; see Rosh there, s.v. mi shavkinan, etc. This resolves Tosafot's difficulty in Shevuot there, s.v. be'omer. The statement in Nedarim 24b, 'the produce shall be forbidden,' etc., is specifically because it was an exaggeration; otherwise it would be forbidden, as the Yerushalmi cited by the Rosh states, that when he insists upon the literal truth of his words, the vow is binding. See above, siman 239, seif 1. This is certainly so according to the version, 'exaggerated oaths are forbidden.' The Rosh there, citing Rabbeinu Yitzchak, answers that there he did not say 'all.' The Gemara in Shevuot could likewise have answered: 'an oath of falsehood,' etc., namely, 'an oath that I will not eat all the produce'; rather, it taught this formulation to exclude the Gemara's question there and in Nedarim: 'perhaps this person,' etc. This is why the ruling here specifies, 'If he said: Konam, all,' and similarly, etc. Likewise, the statement in Nedarim 28a, 'one who says: the produce shall be forbidden,' etc., also refers to a case where he did not say 'all.' The words 'all produce' in the continuation there should be omitted, as should the word 'all' in Gittin 35a. Tosafot there, s.v. venoderet, also write that 'all' is not part of the text. Their view, however, is that he nevertheless transgresses immediately, as with a vain oath. See below, beginning of siman 215.
But if, etc. Tosafot in Ketubot 71a, s.v. shelo, derive this from the end of Nedarim, where it states, 'let it become forbidden,' etc. The Hagahot Ashri there writes likewise. The Rosh, however, writes that even in such a case the vow does not take effect. The Mordechai in Shevuot remains uncertain about the matter.
And likewise if, etc. As stated at the end of chapter 3 of Bava Batra: 'it is possible through produce,' etc.
