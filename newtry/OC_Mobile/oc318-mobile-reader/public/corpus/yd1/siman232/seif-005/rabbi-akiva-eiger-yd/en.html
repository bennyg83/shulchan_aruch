It is not a vow. If he limited it to one or two days, it is a valid vow. See Tosafot and Maharsha regarding a woman who said that all produce should be forbidden until her husband divorces her.
Everything depends upon the word 'all.' This is also evident from Tosafot in Ketubot.
Even in such a case it is a vain vow. This is true where the prohibition itself is impossible to observe. But if the impossible matter is only the condition, while the prohibited act can be avoided, the vow may take effect. Tumim rules otherwise, but in my opinion that is incorrect.
