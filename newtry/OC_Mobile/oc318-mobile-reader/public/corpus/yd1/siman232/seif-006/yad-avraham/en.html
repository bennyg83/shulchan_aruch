(Siman 232, seif 6 in the gloss) One who says, 'If so-and-so was present at this event, I will give one thousand zuz to charity,' and he was there, is exempt because it was an erroneous vow. Beit Hillel cites Masat Binyamin that two people who wagered against one another with a charitable penalty are exempt, and this was ruled in practice. This must be distinguished from an asmachta, which is effective for charity as stated in siman 258 and Choshen Mishpat 207. Masat Binyamin 60 explains that an asmachta is not based on factual error, but on incomplete intent to transfer, and charity requires no acquisition. In a wager, however, each party acts under the mistaken belief that the law or fact supports him.
