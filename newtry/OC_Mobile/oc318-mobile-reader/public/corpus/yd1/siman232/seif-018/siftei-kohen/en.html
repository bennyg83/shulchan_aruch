He is forbidden to overturn their ruling. This is not included among coercive vows, because they did not force him to accept their decision. It is unlike one who vows to tax collectors while intending inwardly that the vow apply only today. In this case, even if there was some coercion, once he accepted the ban or excommunication without qualification it takes effect if he refuses their ruling. Rashbatz means that even an inward limitation to 'today' does not help where no coercion is apparent. Maharam of Lublin, responsum 11, explains that Rashbatz meant only that the coercion was not directed at the oath itself and that he had not intended 'today.' This does not appear from Rashbatz's words. Another responsum of Rashbatz cited by the Beit Yosef, concerning Elkanah and Peninnah, indicates that even one coerced into the vow or oath itself must formulate some condition in his heart; otherwise he must fulfill it. Maharam's proof from Maharik is also inconclusive. Although he cites Maharadakh as distinguishing between the cases, the practical ruling requires study, for the authorities imply that there is no distinction. See above, seif 14.
