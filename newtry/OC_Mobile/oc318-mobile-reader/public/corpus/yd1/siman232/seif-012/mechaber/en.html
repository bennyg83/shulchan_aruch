What are vows under compulsion? His fellow forbade him to eat at his home and he became ill, or his son became ill, or a river detained him—these are vows of compulsion. {Rama: And if compulsion befell him and it was possible to remove it by giving much money, it is called compulsion. (Rivash siman 286.) And similarly if the governor commanded him not to fulfill his oath and vow, even if the penalty is only money, it is called compulsion. (There, and Beit Yosef in the name of responsum of Rashba.) Nevertheless, if with little effort they can strive to remove it, it is not called compulsion. (There and responsum of Rashba.) And see above siman 228 seif 47 regarding the law of a cherem that the ruler decrees to transgress. One who swore or vowed to do a matter on a certain day and was compelled—for example, he swore to pay and when the time arrived he had nothing with which to pay—he is exempt from his oath. And if he can fulfill it afterward, his law is as if he transgressed his oath intentionally. (Beit Yosef siman 228 273d in the name of responsum of Rashba.) And its law was already explained above siman 228 seif 41. And if when the time to pay arrived the governor compelled the lender to extend time for the borrower, or acquitted him entirely—the oath remains. (Beit Yosef in the name of responsum of Rashba and Ramban siman 246.) And according to all opinions he must pay him afterward. And see in Choshen Mishpat siman 73 these laws. A congregation that imposed a cherem for five years—[*it has already been stated that matters of congregation and cherem are void now by law of the kingdom*]—and set a time when the cherem would begin, and at that time they were under compulsion or in error and did not begin to fulfill the vow—they must fulfill it afterward, for it is not void because of this. (Responsum of Rosh kelal 7.) One who swore or vowed to do a matter within a year or on a certain day should do it immediately, or at dawn when that day arrives, lest he forget afterward and not do it and transgress his vow and oath. And if he did not do it immediately because he said, "I still have leisure to do it," and forgot or was compelled afterward and did not do it—some say it is called compulsion. (Ran chapter Keitzad Haregel.) And some say it is not called compulsion. (Chidushei Agudah chapter Mi she-Achazo.) And it is not called compulsion in an oath unless some harm would have come to him if he had not sworn; but if no good would have come to him and he swore so that good would come to him from this, it is not called compelled in an oath. (Maharik shoresh 567 and Piskei Maharai siman 73.) Whoever is compelled in an oath, even though he said "according to the intent of the many" or "according to the intent of the Omnipresent," it is nothing. (Beit Yosef in the name of Rashbatz.) A person is believed to say his oath was under compulsion or on such-and-such a condition. (There.) And this applies specifically before he transgressed his oath; but if he already transgressed, he is not believed to exempt himself from lashes. (In siman 228 274a in Beit Yosef in the name of Rashba.) And similarly one who confessed before the many, or a document was written for him and he confessed in it that he swore, and afterward says he did not swear and gives a reason why he said he swore—he is believed; and all the more so if the truth is that he did not swear, for there is no oath prohibition upon him because of his confession. (Terumat HaDeshen siman 326.)}
