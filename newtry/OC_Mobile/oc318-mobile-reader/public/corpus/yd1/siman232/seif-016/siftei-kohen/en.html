Except for his dwelling house, etc. One should not say that if he lost his outstanding debts, then even if he owns land and movable property he never swore with the intention of selling them, because he assumed that he would pay from the debts owed to him. It is common for a person to sell land and possessions to marry off his daughter. A person's home and basic household utensils, however, are not sold even from a poor person who lives on charity. He is therefore not required to sell them to fulfill his oath, for he certainly did not swear with the intention of sleeping in the street and dying of hunger.
They lived in one city, etc. This requires study. Responsa Ramban states that Shimon may not remove his daughter from her place even if the husband came from another place. All the more so here, where both lived in the same place. The Beit Yosef and Darchei Moshe cite it in this form. See Even HaEzer, siman 75.
