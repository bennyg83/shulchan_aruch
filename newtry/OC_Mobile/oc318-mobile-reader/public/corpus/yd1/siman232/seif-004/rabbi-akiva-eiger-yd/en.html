It is enough that he said 'a snake.' Rabbi Akiva Eiger questions this. If the expression 'like the beam of an olive press' is interpreted as describing the snake's flattened shape rather than its size, why does the Gemara ask that the Mishnah should have stated 'flattened'? That very interpretation is the novelty.
Accordingly, even if he saw no snake. Rashba, Tosafot, and Rosh understand the case as exaggeration based upon seeing an unusually shaped snake. According to them, he must at least have seen an unusual snake. A statement about a flying camel may likewise require some real basis for exaggeration. Levush rules that he must have seen a flattened snake.
