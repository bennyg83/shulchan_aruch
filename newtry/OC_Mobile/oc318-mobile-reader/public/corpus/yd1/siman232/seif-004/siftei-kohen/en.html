Vows of exaggeration, etc. The Ran writes that there are two types of exaggerated vows. One involves a conventional exaggeration, such as saying that he saw people as numerous as those who left Egypt. The other involves something to which exaggeration does not ordinarily apply, such as a snake with fissures on its back like the beam of an olive press. The latter is also permitted, because we assess that he did not intend to create a prohibition. Had that been his intent, he would not have made the prohibition depend on an impossible condition. Rather, he intended only an absurd expression. The Ran further writes that when one makes produce forbidden upon himself if an exaggerated statement is untrue, such as saying, 'All produce shall be forbidden to me by oath or konam if I did not see on the road people as numerous as those who left Egypt,' yet he did not see enough people for such an exaggeration to be plausible, the produce is forbidden. The Beit Yosef questions this, for there is no fixed number at which exaggeration begins. A person might see one hundred people and describe them hyperbolically as those who left Egypt. He cites the Tosefta, which treats as exaggerated vows statements such as, 'If I was not counted for a Pesach offering whose tail weighed ten litrin,' or, 'If I did not drink wine whose log was worth a gold dinar.' This presents no difficulty. The Ran means that when he saw a large crowd and wished to emphasize its size, it is reasonable to say that he spoke hyperbolically. If he saw only a few people, however, the statement cannot reasonably be treated as exaggeration, and he intended the condition literally. The Tosefta is similar, for a person may speak hyperbolically about an unusually heavy tail or exceptionally fine wine. This is also implied by the Ran's concluding words that the Mishnah's case applies specifically where he saw a large number of people who could plausibly be described by exaggeration as those who left Egypt. Otherwise, he spoke literally and the produce should be forbidden.
