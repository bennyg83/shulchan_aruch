Or one who swears. Meaning that he may even swear an actual falsehood. The Yerushalmi states: they initially wished to say that this applies to vows but not to oaths; then they found a teaching of Rabbi Yishmael on the verse, 'You shall not swear falsely by My Name,' that one may swear to murderers, bandits, and tax collectors. It must mean even in such a case, for otherwise this is already explicit in the Mishnah. See Shach.
Or to swear. See the Yerushalmi cited above.
All the produce. See above, seif 5, where Tosafot, the Rosh, and the other authorities do not read the word 'all.'
If I am not, etc. The Tur writes this to explain the case of murderers. This is unnecessary, however, because the Rosh explains that even murderers generally intend only to seize money. As stated above in seif 12 in the gloss, where there is danger to life he need not nullify the oath in his heart.
Provided that, etc. See Ran there, s.v. heichi neder, regarding the wording, etc., meaning, etc.
And specifically, etc.; but, etc. The principle that unspoken intentions are ineffective applies specifically against the words actually uttered. The responsa of Hagahot Maimoniyot and Mordechai cited above, however, imply that even regarding a communal ban, he must nullify it in his heart; see there.
And all this, etc.; therefore, etc. As stated in siman 228, seif 20; see there. The same occurred with the Gibeonites, as explained there. The Rosh likewise writes this in the final chapter of Bava Kamma, section 10, and in responsa, rule 9, as stated below.
Nevertheless, etc. As stated in the final chapter of Yoma, 84a.
Coercers, etc.; and he should nullify, etc.; and likewise, etc.; provided that, etc. All these precautions are required initially merely to remove doubt, as Hagahot Maimoniyot writes there, because in a case of bodily coercion no mental nullification is required, as explained above.
And they shall compensate the injured party, etc. As stated in Bava Kamma 60b. See Tosafot there, s.v. mahu, etc.
