One who vows or swears to a compeller—it is not a vow or oath; therefore one may vow to murderers and tax collectors. If he is a tax collector standing without the king's command, or who comes to take from him more than his allotment, he may vow or swear in order to be released from him, and say, "All fruits in the world shall be forbidden to me if I do not go to the king's house to be released from death," or "if what I bring from the king's house is not to be released from the tax," and think in his heart, "They shall be forbidden to me only today"—even though he utters it without specification, and we hold that matters in the heart are not matters, regarding compulsion it is permitted. (Provided he does not utter with his mouth a matter that explicitly contradicts what is in his heart.) (In Tosafot Nedarim and chapter HaGozel Batra and many other decisors.) And even if the compeller did not ask him to vow and he vows by himself, or added to the vow more than was requested—for example, if he said to him, "Say, Konam you shall benefit from your wife if it is not as you say," and he says, "Konam, my wife and children shall benefit from me;" or if he asked him to vow and he swore—it is nothing, for all that he does he does only because of compulsion, and to strengthen his words against the compeller. {Rama: And this applies specifically when he swore to him with his mouth; then he must nullify in his heart with some condition and in a manner that does not contradict what he uttered with his mouth. But compellers who commanded to impose a cherem on some matter not according to law—that cherem is nothing, and even nullification in the heart is not required. (Hagahot Maimonides chapter 2 of Hilchot Shevuot in the name of the Geonim, and similarly Rabbeinu Yerucham.) And all this applies only when it is possible to transgress his oath and vow without the compellers knowing; but if the compellers know, it is forbidden because of desecration of the Name; and therefore Tzidkiyahu was punished for transgressing his oath to Nevuchadnetzar, even though it was under compulsion. (Responsum of Rosh kelal 9 and responsum of Maharash siman 49 and Mahariv siman 53.) Nevertheless, if he immediately informed the compeller when he left the compulsion that he had a condition in his heart and therefore would transgress his oath—there is no concern, since he was compelled from the outset. (So it is implied in chapter 2 of Yoma.) Nevertheless, everything is according to the need of the matter. (Opinion.) Compellers who commanded to swear to testify about someone whether he had relations with a gentile woman in order to punish him with death—it is called an oath of compulsion. (Beit Yosef in the name of Rosh kelal 8.) And one may annul it in his heart. And similarly if Reuven deposited money with Shimon and the gentile governor compelled to impose a cherem on whoever knows of Reuven's money if the gentile wishes to take Reuven's money under compulsion not according to law—the cherem is nothing; and similarly the depositor may swear he has nothing of Reuven's. (All in Beit Yosef in the name of responsum of Rosh kelal 8 and 9.) Provided there is no desecration of the Name in the matter. (There in the responsum.) As explained regarding two Jews who struck one another and the governor decreed after testimony that if they punish the striker more than his law permits, it is called compulsion; and if they cannot evade and must testify, they testify and pay the victim his damage and do not save themselves with their fellow's money. (Chidushei Agudah chapter HaGozel Batra and there Hagahah in Mordechai in the name of Maharam.) And see below siman 239 more on these laws.}
