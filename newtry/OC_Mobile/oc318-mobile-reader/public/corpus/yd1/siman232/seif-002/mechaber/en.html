What are vows of urging? If one was selling an item to his fellow and said, "Konam be upon me is this loaf if I diminish from a sela," and the buyer said, "Konam is this loaf upon me if I add beyond a shekel," and they both agreed on three dinars (or on a little less or a little more) (Ran)—neither of them is forbidden, for such is the way of merchants to vow or to swear in order to urge one another, and he does not have in his heart to make a real vow. (And some say: even though we say they intended to urge, nevertheless the seller cannot give it for a shekel and the buyer cannot buy it for a sela.) (There, in the name of Ye'iri.) (And some are lenient.) (There.) And this applies specifically when they do not stand by their words but both agreed on three dinars; but if they did not agree except that this one at a sela and that one at a shekel and the transaction was voided, they certainly intended a full vow and the vow takes effect, and whoever transgresses his vow—behold, he has profaned his words. And if one stood by his words and the other did not stand by his, since it was voided for one, it is voided also for the other. And all this applies when unspecified; but if they say they intended a full vow, it is a vow. And if the seller said, "Konam if I diminish more than a sela," or the buyer said, "Konam if I add to you a little less than a shekel," and they agreed on three dinars—this is a vow, for there is no way to urge when they differentiate so much, and they intended a full vow. {Rama: If one vowed regarding two who wish to buy from him and said to one of them, "I intended a full vow," and to the other, "for urging"—since the vow is void regarding one, it is void regarding the other as well, because it is called one vow for both of them. (Beit Yosef in the name of Ran in explanation of the Yerushalmi)}
