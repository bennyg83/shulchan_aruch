And if the seller says, etc. If I add a little for you, etc., this is not the ordinary manner of urging a transaction when the parties are so far apart. Only when the difference between them is half a shekel in an item worth three dinarim is it customary to vow merely as inducement. When they are farther apart, this is not so. Thus the Rosh and the other commentators explain the Gemara's question: one says, 'More than a sela,' and the other says, 'Less than a shekel.' The matter remains unresolved and must be treated stringently. It appears to me that the issue is as follows. Since he was precise and said, 'More than a sela,' his intent may be that in any event he will not accept less than a sela. Perhaps only the word 'more' is inducement, while a sela is a genuine vow. Or perhaps the entire statement is inducement. The same applies to 'less than a shekel.' This is implied by the Gemara's response that the cases are not comparable, for here there is uncertainty whether he meant less than a sela and more than a shekel. Accordingly, even when they are not so far apart, such as when one says more than a sela and the other says less than three dinarim, one should be stringent.
