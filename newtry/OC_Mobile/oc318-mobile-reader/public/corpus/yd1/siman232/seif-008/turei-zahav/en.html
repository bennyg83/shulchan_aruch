He changed his words. The Ran on Nedarim 26a explains the reason for this required change. Some people, when they know that their father is among those present, are particular to say, “So-and-so and so-and-so are forbidden, but my father is permitted,” and not, “All of you are forbidden except my father,” because they do not wish their father to be included in the collective wording even for a moment, namely, after saying “all of you” and before completing “except my father.” Other people are particular in the opposite manner. When their father is present, they prefer to say, “All of you except my father,” rather than, “So-and-so and so-and-so are forbidden and my father is permitted,” because one who begins listing individuals may appear as though he will eventually include his father too. They therefore choose collective wording so that they can immediately add “except my father.” Consequently, whenever he changes his wording there is a reason for that change. From the Ran’s statement that when he says “all of you” his father is included for the moment before he says “except my father,” I learned that in the formula after Birkas HaMazon, where people say, “May He bless all of us together, members of the covenant,” intending to exclude non-Jews who are present, one should not say it that way. Otherwise, those who are not members of the covenant are at least included in “all of us together” until he concludes “members of the covenant.” Rather, he should say, “May He bless us, members of the covenant, all together.” So it appears to me.
