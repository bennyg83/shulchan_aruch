That oath has no effect. This is so even if he did not nullify it in his heart. Mahari Weil writes at the end of responsum 53 that this applies specifically when he was coerced to give something. If he swore not to disclose a matter, however, the ban takes effect. See there.
All the more so, etc. It requires study whether one may be lenient where the coercion is a threat of death, which is clear and absolute. It may be that because of the coercion he fully resolves to transfer or obligate himself, as Mahari, siman 73, and Mahari Weil, responsum 53, write. This also appears from Responsa Maharam, Prague edition, siman 938, cited in Teshuvot Maimoniyot and Mordechai, chapter HaNizakin. Darchei Moshe concludes that it is nevertheless proper to be stringent and obtain release from the ban.
