Yet because he did not stipulate, if he is prevented within that period he conclusively intended the bill of divorce to take effect even if some accident delayed him. But generally, if he is prevented by accident, he is exempt. These are Rashi's words. This language implies to me that where a ferry exists and it commonly happens that it is unavailable on this side, we infer from his failure to stipulate that he intended to cross even if the ferry ceased operating. Since he undertook to cross despite that foreseeable impediment, even if a different accident occurred he would not be exempt, for had he contemplated it he would certainly have obligated himself. What difference would there be to him between this accident and another? The general rule applies where there is no ferry and no presently foreseeable common accident; there a plea of unavoidable accident remains available. This requires practical study. Reference 100.
