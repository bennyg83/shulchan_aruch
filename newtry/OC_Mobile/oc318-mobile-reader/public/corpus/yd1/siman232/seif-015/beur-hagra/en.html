One who, etc. Even a coerced gift is legally ineffective. This is unlike Gittin 55b, which states that because of the coercion he resolves and transfers ownership when they threaten to kill him, for there the captors actually held power to kill and had already killed others. Here, however, it may be merely an empty threat, as stated in Shevuot 46a. Moreover, Shevuot 26a states: 'a person under an oath,' excluding one under compulsion. The same applies here, as stated in Nedarim 27a from the verse concerning a violated maiden, 'you shall do nothing to the maiden.' This applies even if he did not nullify the oath in his heart, as explained above.
And certainly, etc. This is contrary to the rulings of Mahari and Mahari Weil. No proof can be brought from Tzidkiyahu, as explained in the gloss to seif 14. The responsum of Hagahot Maimoniyot cited above, however, appears to support their view.
