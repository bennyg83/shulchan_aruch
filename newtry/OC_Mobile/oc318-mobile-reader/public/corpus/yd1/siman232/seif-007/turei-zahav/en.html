Even though he did not specify, etc. This means that when making the vow he did not mention that he was prohibiting them because they were not his relatives. It might therefore be thought that he intended to prohibit even his father, just as in seif 6 we required him to say explicitly, “because she stole my purse.” The Ran explains the distinction. Above, he did intend to impose a vow upon his wife, so there was no error in the essence of the vow. Here, however, he never intended to impose a vow upon this person at all, and the vow is therefore void automatically. Based on this reasoning, I derived a ruling in a case that came before me. A person thought that the next day was Thursday and on the preceding day accepted a fast for “tomorrow.” He later discovered that the next day was actually Wednesday. Does he require release? At first glance this resembles the case of one’s father in this seif. Even though he did not say at the time, “I accept a fast tomorrow, Thursday,” since he never intended Wednesday, there is an error in the essence of the vow and it should be void. However, one may distinguish. Here, the Shulchan Aruch writes that circumstances demonstrate that a person does not prohibit his fruits to his father, implying that the error must be apparent to everyone. In our case, many people commonly fast on other weekdays as well. His error is not apparent to others, and his private mistaken intention is insufficient. It resembles seif 6, where one must say explicitly that she stole his purse, and his private intention alone does not suffice. Therefore, one who vowed to fast requires release. So it appears to me. See what I wrote in seif 10. It further appears from this seif that even if, after recognizing his mistake, he says nothing at all, the entire vow is permitted. He need not alter the language of the vow as in the following seif. There, he still seeks to prohibit the others, and therefore he must alter his wording. Here, he says nothing further. Just as it is permitted regarding his father, the entire vow is permitted, for no vow exists at all.
