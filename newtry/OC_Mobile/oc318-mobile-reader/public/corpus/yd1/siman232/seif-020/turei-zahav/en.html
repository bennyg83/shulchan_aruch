Did you vow for any reason other than to honor me, etc. The Gemara on 63b asks that even without this reason it should not be a vow, for it is a vow of urging like the case in seif 6. It answers that it is not a vow of urging because the person said, “Am I a dog, that I should benefit from you while you do not benefit from me?” The Rosh explains that since he wants the other person to accept a substantial gift, presumably he also receives much benefit from him. Likewise, in the latter case, where the recipient says, “It is as though I received it,” the Gemara explains that the giver says, “Am I a king, that I should benefit you while you do not benefit me?” The Rosh writes that presumably he provides him with much benefit, since he demands a substantial gift from him. Thus, this law applies specifically where the parties regularly benefit one another, as the Rosh states. Although the Tur and Shulchan Aruch did not mention it, that is because ordinarily one who makes such a vow has that type of relationship, as implied by the Rosh’s language. The Levush overlooked the Gemara’s words and raised a difficulty from his own reasoning, resolving it by distinguishing between accepting a gift and inviting someone to eat. That distinction is contrary to the Gemara cited above.
