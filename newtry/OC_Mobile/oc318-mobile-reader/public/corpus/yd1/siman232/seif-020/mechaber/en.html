And furthermore, there is a vow that does not require annulment—such as if he said to his fellow, "Konam that I derive benefit from you if you do not take from me such-and-such"—he can release his vow without a sage, by saying to him, "Did you vow only to honor me? And it is my honor that you not take from me"—even if the forbidding party does not say explicitly that such was his intent, but from the unspecified formulation. And similarly if he said to him, "Konam that you derive benefit from me if you do not give me such-and-such"—he has release without asking a sage, by saying to him, "Behold, it is as if I received." {Rama: And see above siman 228 seif 39. Some say that specifically regarding fulfillment of an act he can say, "It is as if you did;" but regarding nullification of an act—for example, if he says, "Konam, my property is yours if you go to such-and-such a place"—he cannot say to him, "It is as if you did not go." (Ran in the name of Rashba.) And some say there is no distinction. (Beit Yosef in the name of Ran chapter 4 of Nedarim, Terumat HaDeshen siman 311, and R' Levi ibn Chaviv siman 337.) And every condition that is for the benefit of the one who vowed can say, "Behold, for me it is as if the condition was fulfilled." One who said, "Konam, you shall benefit from me if you do not give my son such-and-such"—if the son relies on his father's table, then the father intends for his benefit and can say, "It is as if I received;" and if not, the father cannot say so, only the son. (Also there in the name of Ran.)}
