If the dough does not contain sixty times the volume of the sourdough, one may separate challah for it from another dough that is definitely obligated, because this is not separating from an obligated item for an exempt one: by Torah law the sourdough is not nullified, since for this issue sourdough and dough are treated as unlike types because taste is determinative. If, however, the dough contains sixty against the sourdough, then although the sourdough still leavens the mixture and remains significant rabbinically, by Torah law it is nullified. One may therefore not separate for it from a different fully obligated dough, because that would be from obligated to exempt. The solution is to make a small dough that itself is below the minimum challah measure, join it to this mixture, and separate from the small dough for the whole. Regarding the Shach's statement, cited in Baer Heitev, seif katan 14, that the same law applies when one dough becomes mixed with another, Levushei Serad cites Shaar HaMayim, siman 7, who asks how separating from elsewhere can help, since that too would be from obligated to exempt and there is no taste of one dough in the other. He answers that the Shach refers to a case where the obligated dough is the majority or where the two are equal. Levushei Serad says that reading this into the Shach is difficult; rather, the Shach was discussing separation from within the mixture. As practical law, however, the distinction is correct: if the obligated dough is the majority or the two are equal, one may separate from another obligated dough, but if the exempt dough is the majority one may do so only by adding a small subminimum dough as described above. He further records several cases where a fermented drink called borscht had been soured with sourdough that was tevel, and the sourdough dissolved and was no longer recognizable. He ruled that if there was not sixty in the borscht against the sourdough, one should separate challah from an obligated dough for both that dough and the borscht. If there was sixty against the sourdough, one may not separate from elsewhere because by Torah law that would be from obligated to exempt, while separation from within the borscht itself is impossible. The borscht is therefore forbidden and its vessel requires kashering. Even if it is earthenware, three rounds of hagalah are effective in this specific case. If, however, one happens to have dough from which challah was already separated that later became leavened by obligated sourdough, and there is sixty in the dough against that sourdough, one may separate from that dough for itself and for the borscht, thereby permitting the borscht and its vessel. One may not deliberately prepare such a dough for this purpose. See there at length for the reasoning.
