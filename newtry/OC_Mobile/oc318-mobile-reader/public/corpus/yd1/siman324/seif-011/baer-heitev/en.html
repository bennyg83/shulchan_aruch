In the leaven. Since the leaven is not nullified and renders the entire dough tevel, therefore if he separates [challah] from elsewhere, it suffices to separate only according to the measure of the leaven alone. But if he has no flour from elsewhere and must separate from the dough itself — since he is not separating from a place that is obligated in challah in its own right, but only because the dough absorbed the flavor from the leaven and became entirely obligated — he must separate the full measure from the entire dough, for we follow the act of separation. The Tur wrote: if he wishes, he may bring a quarter-kav from his [other] flour and mix it with this dough and separate from it. The Maharshal wrote that it is not necessary to mix it with the dough — he may even place it beside the dough if he wishes. End quote of the Taz. The Shakh wrote that the same applies if a dough that is not from leaven and from which challah was not yet separated became mixed into a dough from which challah had already been separated — the law is the same. It also suffices in these laws when they are adjacent even if they are not touching, and there is likewise no combining by vessel, as in siman 325 below. End quote.
Exempt. For example, after Passover when one takes the leaven of non-Jews and puts it in the dough — one may separate from within the dough itself, and we are not concerned that the exempt leaven happened to come into his hand; rather, we say the entire [dough] became obligated through the mixing. One must be careful after Passover to take challah in a larger amount than the measure of the non-Jew's leaven. End quote of the Taz. The Shakh wrote that one should be stringent as written by the Maharil — that Maharam Schulz would instruct to knead two doughs: one with the non-Jew's leaven and one of the proper measure without leaven, and to bring the doughs together and take challah from the one that was not leavened with the non-Jew's leaven on behalf of both. End quote.
Another [dough]. That is to say, in another dough he should leave some leaven for other doughs, and we are not concerned that perhaps in the first dough he took only the exempt leaven, and likewise now in the second dough perhaps only that exempt leaven came to his hand — for we are not so concerned that the same exempt [portion] came to his hand twice in exactly the same way. End quote of the Shakh.
