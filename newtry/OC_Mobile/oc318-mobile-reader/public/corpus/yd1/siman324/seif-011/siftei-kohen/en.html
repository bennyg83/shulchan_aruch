He placed leaven [in the dough], etc. The same applies if a dough that is not from leaven and from which challah was not yet separated became mixed into a dough from which challah had already been separated — the law is the same, for the reasoning that tevel forbids in any amount likewise applies here. And in fact here it is more lenient to separate from within the dough itself, for there is blending (bilah) in a liquid substance, and in the measure of challah that he separates it is impossible that it not contain some of the dough from which challah was not yet separated — and challah has no minimum measure from the Torah. But regarding leaven, Terumat HaDeshen, siman 190, writes that the Rosh agrees there is no blending, and accordingly one must say that the reason the Rosh permits separating from within the dough when leaven [is involved] is specifically for leaven from which challah was not yet separated — as is explicitly stated there — and the reason is as written there: that tevel forbids in any amount within its own species and the entire [dough] becomes obligated. Accordingly, for a non-Jew's leaven mixed in a dough, one may not separate from within according to the Rosh, since there is no blending and one must be concerned that he might come upon the non-Jew's exempt leaven and separate from the exempt on behalf of the obligated. And in this case one cannot say the entire [dough] became obligated [as tevel], since the non-Jew's leaven is not tevel that would forbid in any amount. However the Tur cited the Rif's ruling that even for a non-Jew's leaven the law is the same — implying he holds the Rosh also agrees there is blending even in leaven, as written in Terumat HaDeshen there in the name of the Semak. This is the view of the author (Mechaber), who wrote "and the same applies if the leaven is exempt, etc." Accordingly, the Rosh and the Mechaber did not need the reasoning that the entire [dough] became tevel — since even without it, it would be permitted because there is blending in a liquid substance. Yet possibly the reason of blending in liquid applies only when the main dough is obligated — for it is impossible that he not separate some of the obligated [portion], and challah has no minimum measure from the Torah. But when a small amount of un-separated leaven was placed in a dough, it is possible that he might happen to separate only from the main dough that had already had challah taken, and not from the leaven at all — so the blending reason does not permit it here, and therefore the additional reason is needed: that tevel forbids in any amount. The Perisha wrote: "He placed leaven from a dough from which challah was not yet separated — it appears this refers specifically to leaven; but a dough, as long as it does not have the minimum measure for challah, does not become obligated." And the same applies if the leaven is exempt, etc. That is to say, the leaven of a non-Jew — as was explained — or leaven from which challah had already been taken mixed into a dough from which challah had not yet been taken. The Maharil wrote in the laws of Pesach: when Maharam Schulz was asked — after Pesach, regarding a dough into which they placed the non-Jew's leaven — how to separate challah from it, he instructed them to separate challah corresponding to the leaven and a little extra, for he said that if they did not take more, one must be concerned that all of what he separates was from the non-Jew's [portion] and he is thus separating from the exempt on behalf of the obligated. He said that afterwards, when they leaven another dough with the leaven taken from this dough, then one need only separate a little for challah, etc. — it is then like a double doubt. And when they always take leaven from the dough to leaven another dough, they should take it before they separate challah, so that it not be exempt from [its inclusion in] what was already separated — for afterwards, when separating challah from the second dough, it would be from the exempt on behalf of the obligated. He said that some are accustomed not to separate even a little challah from the second dough leavened with what was taken from this first dough — but here too they take [challah] corresponding to the entire amount that was leavened, plus a little extra. And Maharam Schulz would instruct his wife, the Rabbanit, to knead two doughs: one with the non-Jew's leaven and one of the measure of challah without leaven, and to bring the doughs together and take challah only from the one that was not leavened with the non-Jew's leaven on behalf of both. End quote. Even though the Mechaber's view differs, as written above in §18, nonetheless one should be stringent. The Tashbetz is also stringent.
