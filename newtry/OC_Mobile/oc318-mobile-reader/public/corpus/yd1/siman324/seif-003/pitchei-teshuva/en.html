[See the Levushei Serad, siman 117, who writes that this applies in one of two ways: if there is not sixty times the leaven in the dough, then one is permitted to separate [challah] for it from another dough that is obligated in challah — for this is not separating from an obligated item on an exempt item, since even biblically the [leaven] was not nullified, as leaven and dough are treated as different species for this purpose because we follow the flavor. But if there is sixty times the leaven in the dough — even though it causes leavening and is therefore nullified biblically — it is forbidden to separate for it from another obligated dough, as this would be separating from an obligated item on an exempt item, unless he makes a small dough that does not have the minimum measure for challah and combines it with the mixture and separates from the small dough on behalf of everything. See there. Regarding what the Shakh wrote — cited in the Baer Heitev, §14 — that the same applies if a dough became mixed with another dough, the law being the same as leaven in dough, the Levushei Serad writes there in the name of Sha'ar HaMayim, siman 7, that he questioned this: how does separating from elsewhere help here? It is separating from an obligated item on an exempt item, and there is no [transfer of] flavor in dough. He answers that the Shakh is referring to a case where the obligated dough is the majority, or they are equal. The Levushei Serad himself writes that this reading of the Shakh is difficult; rather, the Shakh is speaking of separating from within itself. But as a matter of law, his words are correct: if the obligated dough is the majority or both are equal, one may separate from another obligated dough. But if the exempt dough is the majority, one cannot separate from elsewhere — only in the manner of making a small dough as explained above. He further writes there that a case came before him several times where people leavened with a leaven that was tevel — a drink called borscht — and the leaven dissolved in it and was no longer discernible. He ruled that if the borscht does not have sixty times the leaven, one separates challah from an obligated dough on behalf of [the borscht] and the rest. But if there is sixty times the leaven [in the borscht], one cannot separate from elsewhere, as it would be separating from an obligated item on an exempt item — for the leavening agent [once nullified] is only rabbinically [obligated], and separating from within itself is certainly impossible. Therefore the borscht is forbidden, and the vessel requires immersion in boiling water (hagalah), and even if it is a kli sheini, immersion in boiling water three times suffices. However, if it happens that a dough from which challah was already separated had been leavened with a leaven that was obligated [in challah], and there is sixty times the leaven in the dough, then one separates from this dough on behalf of itself and the borscht, and the borscht becomes permitted and so does the vessel. But one may not deliberately make such a dough. See there at length for the reason.]
