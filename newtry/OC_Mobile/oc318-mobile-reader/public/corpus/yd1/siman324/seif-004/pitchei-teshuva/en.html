From another dough. See Be'er Heitev in the name of the Taz, who wrote that one may not separate from the pastry dough itself. See Dagul MeRevavah, who disagrees with him. The Gaon of Lissa also wrote in his siddur, in the laws of challah, that the Taz's words are astonishing, for the taste of meat absorbed in the pastry dough does not harm the dough with respect to challah; taste does not nullify the substance. See Levushei Serad on this. Regarding what he further wrote, that it is proper to conduct oneself, etc., see Rav Meshulam responsa siman 2, who disagrees. See Peri Megadim siman 92, Siftei Da'at s.k. 10, who wrote of an incident in one community where they made lechem mishneh for Shabbat, took from that dough and put it as dried bread into a pot, and did not take challah. On Shabbat morning they ate all the lechem mishneh and also the dried bread, and only the meat and broth cooked with the dried bread remained. What is the law? I answered that certainly, if some of the dried bread remained, he could leave a little until after Shabbat, because with challah outside Eretz Yisrael one may eat first and then separate. Here, where none remained at all, nevertheless one may permit it, for the dried bread did not become neveilah, since while it existed no prohibition rested upon it, as he could have separated, etc.; see there. See Levushei Serad's explanation of his words.
