From another dough. See Baer Heitev in the name of Taz, who wrote that from the pashtida dough itself, no; and see Dagul Mirvavah, who disputes him. [Also the Gaon of Lisa, in his siddur, Hilchot Challah, wrote on the aforementioned Taz that it is puzzling, for the taste of meat absorbed in the pashtida dough does not harm the dough regarding challah, since taste does not nullify the substance; and see Sefer Levushei Serad, what he wrote on this.] And what he further wrote, "and it is proper to practice, etc.," see responsum Rav Meshulam siman 2, who disputes this. [And see Peri Megadim siman 92, Siftei Da'at s.k. 10, who wrote: an incident occurred in one community, they made lechem mishneh in honor of Shabbat, and they took from that dough and placed it as a toasted loaf in a pot and did not take challah; and on Shabbat morning they ate all the lechem mishneh, including the toasted loaf, and nothing remained except the meat and gravy cooked with the toasted loaf: what is its law? And I answered: certainly if some of the toasted loaf remained, one may leave a bit until after Shabbat, for challah of chutz la'aretz one eats and afterward separates; and here, where nothing remained at all, nevertheless one should permit, for the toasted loaf did not become nevelah, since at the time it existed there was no issur upon it, for one could still separate, etc.; see there. And see Sefer Levushei Serad, what he wrote in explaining his words.]
