A dough of five quarters. Maharshal writes that a small cake certainly combines with the dough just as a small dough combines with a large one; see Derishah.
One who bakes a meat pie. The novelty is that the meat flavor is treated as substance because taste is like the essence by Torah law, so separation from another dough can cover the pie dough. It cannot be separated from the meat-flavored dough itself for ordinary dough. Mahariv 48 addresses dough cooked before challah was separated. Since all of it was tevel, ordinary nullification does not apply. He nevertheless permits separating after cooking by leaving more than one hundred times the challah portion, relying on retroactive designation in rabbinic law and on the rule that outside Eretz Yisrael one may eat and separate later. Bach objects, but Mahariv is correct: until separation, the kohen's portion is mixed throughout and can impart prohibited flavor. The cooked foreleg in Chullin proves that even a priestly portion designated only after cooking would prohibit without nullification. Likewise here, later separation works only through retroactive designation. One who forgot challah should therefore leave enough that the baked dough contains one hundred and one times the eventual challah portion, then separate a very small amount. [Citation 54.]
