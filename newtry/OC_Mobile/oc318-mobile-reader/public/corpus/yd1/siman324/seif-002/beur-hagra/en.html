In the case of D.C. etc. Jerusalem Talmud there; see Tosafot in Pesachim 35a and in Menahot 70b s.v. "tana" etc., and the later opinion; and one may further say that when there is [a majority], etc. So wrote the Rashbam at the beginning of Kilayim, the Rosh, and other decisors.
And the spelt combines, etc. As stated in Pesachim and in Menahot there and there, that spelt is also a species of wheat, and certainly of the species of barley, as Tosafot wrote there — this is why they combine with wheat and barley as stated in the Mishnah, and likewise with oats and rye, since they are species of barley.
Rye combines, etc. In the Mishnah there, R. Yochanan ben Nuri says the rest, etc. And the Rashbam and the Rosh wrote that in the Yerushalmi it says that the first tanna disputes — that rye and oats do not combine with each other — and the law follows the first tanna. The Rashbam there cites the Yerushalmi: "What about its remainder? Rav Huna said, etc." But regarding spelt there is no dispute — it combines with both. And this is what was stated above: "The spelt combines, etc."
And according to the Rambam, etc. He holds that the first tanna does not dispute [R. Yochanan ben Nuri], and so he ruled in his commentary — that the law follows R. Yochanan ben Nuri. The Kesef Mishneh explains the Yerushalmi according to his view. A clear proof for the Rambam's position comes from the Gemara cited above, which states that oats and rye are species of barley — showing they are one species. The Yerushalmi cannot be reconciled according to the Rashbam and Rosh, who say that rye combines with wheat, while the Mishnah says "wheat, etc." Moreover our Gemara disagrees, as noted. The principle is that there is no practical difference between the Rambam and the Rosh except regarding whether rye and oats combine with each other specifically. See Bedek HaBayit — but his words are strained.
Mixed, etc. Yerushalmi, cited by the Rashbam at the beginning of Chapter 4 there: "There is a tanna who teaches that all species, etc." Even though the Rashbam wrote "this is puzzling, etc., and one must say, etc." — so too is the view of the Ra'avad. But the Rosh wrote according to his original view and according to the plain meaning, as he wrote at the outset: that the time of obligation had already arrived, etc. This is also the view of the Rambam. See siman 326, seif 4. In any case the law does not follow that tanna, but follows our tanna — whether leniently or stringently, they do not combine. As stated above in the Shulchan Aruch. The Rosh.
(Likkut) Mixed, etc. The Rashbam there concludes: "However it is puzzling — when one adds to what is already obligated, why can it not combine to obligate the addition? As stated, etc. One must say that the case here is where he separated the dough and afterwards added. And so too, etc." And one can likewise explain the words of the Rosh there (end of quote).
