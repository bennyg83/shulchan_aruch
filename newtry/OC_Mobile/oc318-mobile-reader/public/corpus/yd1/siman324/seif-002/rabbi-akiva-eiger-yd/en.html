In the continuation of the responsum: For behold, the leaven of a non-Jew. I find this puzzling: since by force of tevel — which forbids in any amount — the whole [dough] is considered obligated [in challah], how much more so where the main dough itself is tevel, that it is obvious the dough is not nullified by the leaven. So too wrote the Rosh in his responsa (Kelal 2, §3), in these words: "Those who leaven [their dough] with the leaven of a non-Jew may separate [challah] from another [dough], as in the case we learned: one who takes leaven, etc. — and we do not say this is like separating from an obligated [item] on an exempt [item], because leaven serves for flavor and is not nullified in any amount — all the more so where the main dough is fully obligated [in challah], it is not nullified by the exempt leaven, even though it serves for flavor; nonetheless, the dough is the primary [component] relative to the leaven." End quote. If so, the same applies to separating from the same dough [i.e., from within itself] — there is no distinction between tevel leaven and a tevel dough. Consider this well.
