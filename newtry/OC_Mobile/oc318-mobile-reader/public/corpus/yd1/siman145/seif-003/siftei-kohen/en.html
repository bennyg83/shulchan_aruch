The house itself etc. For otherwise it would be appurtenances of idolatry which are not forbidden until used, as above beginning of siman 139.
It was built for human habitation and a gentile plastered and whitewashed it. As I wrote in s.k. 8.
The Israelite takes etc. But a gentile — with any amount that he takes it suffices and constitutes nullification; and specifically idolatry of a gentile — but that of Israel has no nullification and he takes all that he added — Rashi and the Ran.
And the new addition is forbidden for benefit. It has no nullification ever if it is of Israel; and if it is of a gentile — until the gentile nullifies it. Tur; and already explained in the previous s.k.
Into the house. And the house itself he did not intend to worship.
He removed it. Even if he intends to return it; and likewise even if an Israelite removed it, permitted, since it was not designated for the idol but only temporarily — Tur and Ran in the name of Rashi.
He removed it from there etc. Beit Yosef wrote: and it is simple in my eyes — what helps by removing not to re-enter applies when a gentile removed it; but if an Israelite removed it, it does not help to nullify from this house the law of idol appurtenances — end; and Darkei Moshe brought it; and so the Magen Avraham; and so in Tur and Ran.
And regarding Israel etc. Meaning: if the house is appurtenances of an Israelite's idol, nullification never helps, as above beginning of siman 139 — an Israelite's idol and its appurtenances have no nullification.
