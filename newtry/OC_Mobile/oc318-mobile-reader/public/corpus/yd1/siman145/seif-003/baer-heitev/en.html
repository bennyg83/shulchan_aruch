That he built it. Turei Zahav and Siftei Kohen (Sh"akh) wrote: that is when a non-Jew built it; but if a Jew built it, it is not forbidden until one bows to it. In the Perishah's glosses he wrote that the law of an apostate Jew is like that of a non-Jew; the Taz disagrees and writes that his law is like that of a full Jew in this matter, and for all punishments in the Torah, as written at the beginning of siman 139. (And in the above siman I wrote in the name of Nekudot HaKesef that he agrees with the Perishah's gloss — see there.)
Takes [what was renewed]. Meaning: a Jew takes what was renewed; but for a non-Jew, taking any amount suffices for nullification. This applies specifically to idolatry of a non-Jew; but idolatry of a Jew has no nullification — and he removes everything that was renewed. Rashi and the Ran.
To worship it. The Siftei Kohen wrote: and what was renewed has no nullification ever if it belongs to a Jew; but if it belongs to a non-Jew, [it remains prohibited] until the non-Jew nullifies it.
The house. The house itself was not intended for worship.
Removed it. Even if he intends to return it; and even if a Jew removed it, it is permitted — since it was not dedicated to idolatry permanently, only temporarily. Rashi.
Nullification. Beit Yosef wrote and it is obvious: this applies specifically when a non-Jew removed it; but if a Jew removed it, it does not avail to nullify the status of accessories of idolatry from this house. — So far the Beit Yosef. And what the author wrote "and with a Jew" means: if the house is used as an accessory of Israel's idolatry, nullification is not effective ever, as stated above at the beginning of siman 139. Siftei Kohen.
