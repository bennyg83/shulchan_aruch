And even stones etc. This is the Rambam's (and Tur's) opinion; and the Ran wondered on him, since there is a dispute it should be a Torah doubt to be stringent; and the Ramah ruled so; and the Beit Chadash wrote a resolution for the Rambam's words, and his words are very forced (and so in Kesef Mishneh he wrote a resolution that does not appear). And therefore it would appear to be stringent; and further, R. Yerucham 17:4 wrote plainly: one who bows to stones of a mountain that crumbled is forbidden, for their law is not like a mountain — end; and so the Raavad in hasagot. But per my humble opinion the Rambam's opinion is clear: in the Gemara (47a) it is doubtful whether the sons of R. Chiya — meaning Chizkiyah — permit and R. Yochanan forbids, or the reverse; in the Jerusalem Talmud chapter "all images" halacha 6 it is stated explicitly that Chizkiyah permits and R. Yochanan forbids. And if so, we do not abandon what is explicit in the Jerusalem Talmud because of what is doubtful in our Gemara; and further, R. Yochanan himself compiled the Jerusalem Talmud — and if so, R. Yochanan himself says he forbids. And if so the matter is clear that we rule like Chizkiyah who permits. I shortened on this — investigate (and I again found so in Sefer Torat Chaim):
And springs of many. The Magen Avraham wrote: we do not hold except the Gemara's language (47a) "and likewise a river and spring of an individual" — since the reason is because it is attached, it is not forbidden except regarding libations, and there is a difference between many and individual; and his words are correct according to the Gemara there, and not like the Perishah's hagahah who wrote "and not like the Ramah"; and also what the Perishah wrote regarding an individual — "perhaps he dug for an idol" — difficulty. However, what the Magen Avraham wrote that in detached there is a difference between many and individual does not appear to me, for in the Gemara it is proven that detached, even of many, are forbidden because "perhaps he is bowing" — otherwise what does it challenge? And R. Yochanan said in the name of Rashbi "water of many is not forbidden" — and perhaps this is for an ordinary person, and in detached; and it was asked: perhaps for libations they are forbidden even in attached? Rather certainly detached, even of many, are forbidden for an ordinary person; and now Rashbi certainly deals with attached and for libations; for if for an ordinary person, even of an individual they are not forbidden since they are attached — investigate. And it is possible for this that the Rambam and Tur wrote "springs of many" to inform that even of many are not permitted for an ordinary person except springs that are attached:
