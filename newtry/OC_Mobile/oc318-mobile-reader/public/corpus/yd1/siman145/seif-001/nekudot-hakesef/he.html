נבעי מארעי' ודו"ק: **** ENGLISH **** (Siman 145 in Taz s.k. 3) And it is difficult etc. It is not difficult at all; and as the Magen Avraham wrote, and as Shach wrote in s.k. 2, that they did not specifically mention "of many" — or they mentioned "of many" to inform us that even of many are not permitted except in springs; and what appears from his words that we rule regarding many even in detached is permitted — does not exist, for if so great wonder why the Rif, Rosh, and all other decisors omitted this law, and not one of them mentioned it except the Raavad in hasagot chapter 8 Laws of Avodah Zarah who wrote so — that water of many is not forbidden — and there is no decisor who mentioned this; on the contrary, from the Rambam's, Tur's, and Shulchan Aruch's words it appears the opposite. Rather certainly it appears to him that we rule like Resh Lakish, who argues there in chapter R. Yishmael on R. Yochanan; and he holds that water of many is forbidden in detached, and the reason is that although everywhere we rule like R. Yochanan against Resh Lakish, here we rule like Resh Lakish because the Gemara leans his way. And I will explain one thing: what does the Gemara challenge in chapter R. Yishmael — "this of an individual is forbidden" — let it be derived that they are attached? Rather it comes to inform us that water of many, even for libations, is not forbidden; and as stated in chapter "all images" (Avodah Zarah 47) that this of Rashbi deals even with libations; and for raising it is forbidden in attached, as stated in the sugya of chapter "all images" there; and so all decisors — therefore for an individual they are forbidden for raising. And further: why does it challenge so in chapter R. Yishmael regarding "and R. Yochanan leaned to his reasoning" etc., and not challenge so on R. Shimon ben Yochai's words themselves in chapter "all images" — the main place of this law? Rather certainly it challenges only R. Yochanan; for if chapter "all images" were difficult for us, as I explained that "this of an individual is forbidden" means libations and water of many is not forbidden — deals with attached and for libations; but in detached, even water of many is forbidden even for an ordinary person, like Resh Lakish. But R. Yochanan deals with an ordinary person and says water of many is not forbidden — plainly he holds that this of Rashbi is not about libations, for for libations they are forbidden; rather it deals with an ordinary person. And if so it challenges well: "let it be derived that they are attached"; and it is also possible that it seemed to the challenger to establish the work of Resh Lakish and R. Yochanan in detached, meaning that it does not also challenge Resh Lakish himself — where does he hold it is forbidden? For they are attached. But the baraita of Rashbi does not seem to him to be established so, for it teaches plainly; and also plainly water is attached. Therefore we, who see above in the sugya of chapter "all images" that it challenges the set Gemara regarding libations — "and Rashbi said water of many is not forbidden" — therefore the Gemara rules like Resh Lakish (and what Shach wrote in s.k. 2 "or also in attached" is a textual error — investigate). And also from what it challenges there "and a bowl for an ordinary person too" it is proven so; and as I wrote in s.k. 2. And further, since we see that it challenges in chapter R. Yishmael "let it be derived that they are attached" and answers with forced variants — plainly we rule like Resh Lakish that detached water, even of many, is forbidden even for an ordinary person; and this of Rashbi deals specifically with libations and attached. And what the Gemara says in chapter R. Yishmael "and R. Yochanan leaned to his reasoning" — meaning we hear R. Yochanan said so; but we do not hear Resh Lakish said so; but in truth Resh Lakish establishes this of Rashbi in attached and for libations; and therefore (not) did any decisor write that water of many is not forbidden in detached for an ordinary person; and therefore the Rambam wrote chapter 4 Laws of Issurei Mizbeach: "One who bows to a mountain — although it is permitted for benefit, behold its stones are forbidden for the altar; and one who bows to a spring flowing in his land — behold its water is invalid for libations" — end; behold he was precise "in his land" to exclude of many that are not forbidden in attached for libations. And in Laws of Avodah Zarah he did not distinguish this; on the contrary it appears plainly from his words there that even of many is permitted only because attached — see there. And further it appears to me to say that the decisors hold Rashbi's reason that water of many is not forbidden is because a person does not forbid something that is not his; but of an individual is forbidden when the individual worships them. And if so, this law does not depend on water, for the dispute is at the end of chapter HaShochet in all matters; and the decisors wrote it as explained in the Tur and Beit Yosef above siman 4 and this siman — meaning the Rambam wrote "to a spring flowing in his land" — meaning according to the Gemara "flowing from his land" — meaning specifically his land; but on another's land, even of an individual, they are not forbidden because a person does not forbid something that is not his. And this answer appears clear to me, for thus it appears from the Rambam and Gemara that it says "flowing from his land" — investigate.
