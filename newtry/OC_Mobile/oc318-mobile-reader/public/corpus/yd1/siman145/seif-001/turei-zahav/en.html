He has no right hand. It is written by their gods on the mountains, not the mountains of Hashem:
And he is the mountain stones that have been paved. P. and did not go completely because they did not return such a person as a matter of money and the D. seems to have been completely broken, but not done by a man dea in the R.R. DNA, which is a water that is derived from their source by wave:
And likewise rivers and springs of many. So too the Rambam and Tur. And it is difficult, for in chapter R. Yishmael (Avodah Zarah 59) we challenge: why did R. Yochanan say in the name of R. Shimon ben Yochai "water of many is not forbidden" — this of an individual is forbidden; let it be derived that they are attached? It is not needed — for the gentile struck them with his hand, meaning and detached them — end; if it were of an individual they would be forbidden. And if so, here dealing with attached, the decisors are challenged with the Gemara's difficulties — "this of an individual is also not forbidden"; and so the Levush challenged from reasoning: attached has no prohibition even for an individual, like a mountain. And in the Perishah he wrote regarding this that for many 45:1 it is permitted because we do not say they dug for idolatry; but of an individual we say he dug for idolatry and it is forbidden even attached, since he acted upon it. And he was not precise in the Gemara I brought, for it explicitly challenges that of an individual is also permitted as long as it is attached. And further: why is it forbidden through action — it does not appear to me at all that although they said in 57 "he dug pits" etc. they forbade through action — there it is different, for he bows to the pit which is his handiwork; not so if he bows to the water that comes by itself, although they come because of the digging. Nevertheless in what he bowed to, he did not himself act upon it; and this is proven from what cannot be found in the Gemara I brought that an individual is forbidden except by striking with his hand and making them detached. And further it is explained in Tosafot page 47, who gave the reason: water flowing by itself, and they are of many, are permitted; and of an individual forbidden; and on this it challenges "but they are attached" and answers that he detached them with his hand. And in order to answer the above difficulty, it appears to me: since in chapter "all images" (Avodah Zarah 47) Rabbah asked — one who bows to a spring, its water for libations etc. — it appears there in the conclusion that even for the altar water of Rabbi is not forbidden, but of an individual is forbidden even though attached, since it is for the altar; and as we said above in what Rava resolved for Rami bar Chama — there is worship in attached regarding the altar. Therefore the decisors wrote that springs of many are not forbidden at all, even for the altar; and one should not say "behold even when not attached they are permitted for the altar since they are of many", for the altar applies only to living water brought for libations. And further, the main law came to permit attached, but incidentally it informs us that we find this permission even for the altar, such as when they are of Rabbi. So it appears to me, forced, to settle their words; and I wonder on the Beit Yosef who sensed this.
