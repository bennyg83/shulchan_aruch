Its plating is forbidden, as it is stated, "You shall not covet the silver and gold that is upon them."
The accessories of a mountain are treated like its plating. The prohibition of accessories of idolatry is derived in chapter R' Yishmael, Avodah Zarah 52a, from the verse, "You shall utterly destroy all the places where the nations served their gods upon the mountains." If the word "places" does not apply to the mountains themselves, because mountains do not become prohibited, as the verse says "their gods upon the mountains," but not "the mountains are their gods," apply it instead to the utensils used there. We thus learn that even with a mountain, the utensils used in its service become prohibited. Offerings brought to a mountain, however, do not become prohibited. Tosafos there explain that the prohibition of offerings to idolatry is derived in chapter Kol HaBasar from the verse, "They ate the sacrifices of the dead," and that verse was written concerning Peor, which was detached from the ground.
