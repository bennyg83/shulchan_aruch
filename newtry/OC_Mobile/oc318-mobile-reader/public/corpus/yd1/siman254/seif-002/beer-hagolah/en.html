The incident of Ifra Hormiz, mother of King Shapur, who sent four hundred dinars to Rava. Bava Batra 10b explains that charity from the nations is not accepted publicly, based on the verse, “When its harvest dries, it shall be broken,” meaning that when their merit is exhausted and the moisture of their charitable deeds dries, they will be broken.
Ibid. 8a, where she sent a purse of dinars to Rav Yosef and instructed that it be used for a great mitzvah. Tosafot there and Rashi at the beginning of 11a explain that since she specified a great mitzvah, the funds could not be diverted to non-Jewish poor, because one may not deceive anyone, even a non-Jew. Money generally distributed to the poor involves no deception, since non-Jews know that Jews ordinarily support them as taught in Gittin 61a. The Rama cites this ruling as one opinion because the Beit Yosef understood that other authorities did not distinguish the cases and held that Rav Yosef redeemed non-Jewish captives with the money.
This ruling is also derived there.
