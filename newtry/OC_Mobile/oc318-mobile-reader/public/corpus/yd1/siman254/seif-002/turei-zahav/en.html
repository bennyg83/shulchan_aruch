And it shall be given to poor gentiles — but not to poor Israelites, because it is written "in the withering of her harvest they shall be broken" — meaning when the moist merit in gentiles' hands dries up and their charity withers, then they shall be broken. It is difficult: at the beginning they permitted receiving in private and we do not care that the gentile gains merit thereby; and Derishah challenged this and answered that an individual who receives, since he benefits, is permitted; but a gabbai who has no benefit may not cause merit for gentiles for others' benefit — and this is astounding, for how do we permit him to prolong exile for his benefit? It appears to me that "in the withering of her harvest" applies only when the gentile intends specifically for poor Israelites — thereby Israel's name is exalted and there is great merit, as here and like the incident of the mother of King Shapur in the Gemara who sent to divide for Israel; and although Rashi wrote she also knew they sustain poor gentiles with Israel, for otherwise there would be theft of mind as we cite below — nevertheless the main point was for poor Israel, and therefore they were concerned about "withering of harvest"; not so at the beginning where the gentile does not intend specifically on Israel but from his nature is merciful to all who stretch out a hand — this is not great merit and there is no "withering of harvest" — so appears main. And they give to poor gentiles in secret. One may say on what I wrote that the gentile's main intent was for Israel: here he gives everything to poor gentiles and it would be theft of mind — one may answer that theft of mind applies only when the giver intends to give to Israel and not to gentiles; giving to gentiles passes through the gentile's mind who was given a burden to give to gentiles; but where he gives nothing to Israel, there is no theft of mind nor such prohibition.
And some say one should do with them, etc. — I have not reached the end of Rama's view; his words are astounding, for in the Gemara first chapter of Bava Batra there are two incidents: one on page 10 — the mother of King Shapur sent four hundred zuz to Rava, he accepted them for peace of the kingdom, and divided them for poor gentiles — this is the seif Tur and Shulchan Aruch mention; and also on page 8 — Hormiz sent a purse of dinars to R. Yosef for a great mitzvah, and R. Yosef gave them for redeeming captives, which is a great mitzvah; and Rashi and Tosafot wrote in R. Yosef's incident he did not heed "withering of harvest" because he sent for redeeming captives and could not divide them — therefore he had to give to captive Israelites, for if to captive gentiles he would transgress theft of mind; but regarding Rava who was sent money and could divide for poor gentiles, there is no theft of mind, for they too know Israelites sustain poor gentiles with poor Israel. It follows that if sent for captives one must fulfill the giver's statement because of theft of mind; but money divided for the poor one gives to poor gentiles; and Beit Yosef wrote that since poskim did not divide on this, learn that R. Yosef too divided for gentile captives — meaning "withering of harvest" applies everywhere even for captives; and so Tosafot for one answer on page 8. Therefore Rama's words that one should do as the ruler commanded apply when sent for captives or a great mitzvah like redeeming captives; but money sent for charity one gives to poor gentiles. And in the incident of Darva and Hormiz creation of a company — but for captives.
And in Perishah I saw the entire Beit Yosef does not bring to you that if the gentile says stars in a manner that does not belong to gentiles, he says because of theft of mind; and from any difficult place he is against disobeying verses Beit Yosef wrote, who is here to steal the mind — and there is a Taz.
