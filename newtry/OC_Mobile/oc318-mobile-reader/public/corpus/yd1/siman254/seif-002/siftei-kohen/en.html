And some say one should do with it, etc. The second opinion also concedes that if [the ruler] sent them without specification, it should be given to poor non-Jews by default, as stated in the Talmud chapter 1 of Bava Batra end of folio 10 — only they hold that if the ruler commanded thus and so, one should do so, because it is forbidden to deceive people, even a non-Jew's understanding, as stated above end of siman 65 and in Choshen Mishpat beginning of siman 227, where they wrote regarding why the mother of King Shapur sent 400 dinars to Rava Rabbah: Rav Yosef accepted them for the needs of Israel and it is not possible to distribute to poor non-Jews because it is forbidden to deceive people even non-Jews. But money distributed to the poor generally has no deception in it, for they too know that Jews are accustomed to supporting poor non-Jews together with poor Jews — end quote. And the first opinion makes no such distinction. And Bah ruled like this second opinion and wrote it is forbidden to redeem non-Jewish captives with such funds, because of deception and because non-Jews we lower and do not raise:
One accepts from them. As stated below siman 259 seif 2. The reason is that it is like a sacrifice — for sacrifices are accepted from non-Jews. And in Hagahot Asheri, chapter 1 of Bava Batra, a reason is given: because charity atones — therefore one does not accept from them; but vows and freewill offerings do not come to atone:
