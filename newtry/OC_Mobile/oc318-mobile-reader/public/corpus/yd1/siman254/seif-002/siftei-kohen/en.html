And it shall be given to poor gentiles, etc. It should not be given to poor Jews, because Scripture states, "When its harvest withers, it shall be broken." This means that when the merit possessed by the gentiles is exhausted, and the vitality of their charitable deeds dries up, they will be broken.
And some say that it should be used, etc. These authorities also agree that if the ruler sent the money without specifying its use, it should ordinarily be given to poor gentiles, as stated at the end of Bava Batra 10b. They maintain, however, that if the ruler instructed that it be used for a particular purpose, his instruction must be followed, because it is forbidden to mislead people, even a gentile, as stated above at the end of siman 65 and in Choshen Mishpat at the beginning of siman 228. They explain that when the mother of King Shapur sent four hundred dinars for a great mitzvah, Rav Yosef accepted them for the redemption of Jewish captives. He could not divert them to poor gentiles, because doing so would mislead the donor. Money sent generally for distribution to the poor does not involve deception, since gentiles also know that Jews customarily support poor gentiles together with poor Jews. The first view does not make this distinction. The Bach rules in accordance with the latter authorities and writes that the funds may not be used to redeem gentile captives, both because this would mislead the donor and because of the law concerning hostile gentiles that one does not raise them from danger.
He may accept from them. This is as stated below in siman 259, seif 2. The reason is that such a donation resembles an offering, and offerings are accepted from gentiles. The Hagahos Ashri in the first chapter of Bava Batra gives another reason: charity brings atonement, and therefore it is not accepted from them, whereas vows and voluntary gifts are not brought for atonement.
But not from an apostate, etc. In Responsa Mabit, part 2, siman 214, page 174, it appears that donations are accepted from an apostate. He writes there that although the verse, "When a person from among you brings an offering," excludes an apostate, this applies to an offering and matters comparable to it. A dedication for the poor or a dedication to a synagogue, as in the case discussed there, may be accepted. See there.
