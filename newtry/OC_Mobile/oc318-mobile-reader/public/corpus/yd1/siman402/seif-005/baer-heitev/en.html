**One.** Meaning one hour; but if he heard tidings on Shabbat when it is the twenty-ninth — then he observes seven and thirty after Shabbat. Maharshal. The reason is that he does not observe mourning after Shabbat even though he began on Shabbat and it is the thirtieth day — since he does not mourn full mourning on that Shabbat but only private matters, therefore there is no mourning decree after it — so Beit Yosef in the name of Rashba. Ramban wrote that on that Shabbat and festival private matters are forbidden all that day and we do not say here "part of the day is like the whole day" since he has an obligation for the day after of one hour — how can it be said that mourning stops and returns to him? — so Taz. Shach wrote that from here Bach ruled that if the seventh day of mourning falls on Shabbat he must sit on the ground on Motza'ei Shabbat and observe mourning one hour — for here even though private matters apply on Shabbat, he is not released thereby and must do on Motza'ei Shabbat an act recognizable as mourning — so he ruled. In my humble opinion I do not agree in general that here, since the Sages obligated observing mourning properly at least one hour, and on Shabbat mourning is not observed except in part — therefore this person never observed mourning law properly even one hour; therefore he is obligated on Motza'ei Shabbat or Motza'ei festival to observe mourning one hour. But one who observed seven days of mourning and within the seven Shabbat fell — why should he be obligated in mourning on Motza'ei Shabbat? And even if he was negligent on some days and did not observe mourning — we ruled above siman 397 that he need not complete; and all the more Shabbat when he observed mourning as required — so he wrote. Taz also challenged him and wrote that this is nothing but astonishing words, and those people who do so are not relied upon. Perhaps their error came from what they saw in seif 6 regarding distant tidings, that on Motza'ei Shabbat and festival one observes mourning one hour, and they thought that on every Shabbat of mourning they do so — but that is a complete error, for there the main obligation on Motza'ei Shabbat is because of distant tidings, which is not the case here — end.
