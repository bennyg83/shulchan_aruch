**To lie.** It appears one should say language that bears two faces — meaning it is not explained explicitly in his words that he died, but the language bears toward life and death; proof from R' Chiyya who asked Rav Abba "alive?" He said "mother is alive"; Tosafot explained that in wonder he said "until you ask me about father, ask me about mother" — and from this R' Chiyya understood that his father died, for he did not say explicitly that he died. And the Taz wrote likewise.
**The Binyaminster.** Levush challenged this — for the reason we fast is danger, bad luck on the day father or mother died; if so there is no distinction and one should always fast on day of death — so he wrote. So too responsa Mahari Brin brought in Bach; also responsa Meshech Benjamin challenged Rabbenu's words — are not two brothers dwelling in one city, one at father's death and the other not in the city when father died, and before burial the second also came — then one would do yahrzeit on death day and the second the next day, and there is concern of lo titgodedu and resembling mockery and absurdity, and we require "her ways are ways of pleasantness" — end. Also from other Acharonim it implies one always fasts death day not burial day. In responsa Maharam Benet he wrote specifically from second year onward; but in first year they always do yahrzeit from burial day, for otherwise sometimes he would not complete twelve-month mourning — e.g., died erev Shabbat near Shabbat with no time to bury until Sunday; if yahrzeit on death day mourning also stops that day since world practice not to mourn past yahrzeit — which is certainly improper since mourning begins from burial and must complete to that day — end. We never heard nor saw to distinguish yahrzeit years and say that in first year one does from burial day and afterward from death day; also from all Acharonim who wrote plainly to do on death day implies first year too. Regarding his difficulty it seems to me that in any case it is difficult — sometimes burial day is far from death day, e.g., they brought him elsewhere to bury; mourning begins only burial day as above siman 375 and yahrzeit begins death day. It is strained to say for twelve-month mourning we follow death day; also strained if such case occurs to do yahrzeit before twelve months. So too Sefer Tahara implies always yahrzeit at completion of mourning. By law even on yahrzeit day itself need not observe mourning since twelve months completed — only world practice not to cancel twelve-month mourning law that whole day; perhaps because they say Kaddish and Barchu and fast they observe mourning matters that day — but by law not required. Also relevant: one who fasts and says Kaddish and Barchu on death day and dies that day and is buried next day — mourning always counted from burial day — so Sefer Tahara. Accordingly it appears one should always fast death day; but if death day is three or four days or more from burial, in first year fast on burial day so yahrzeit at completion of mourning since impossible otherwise; and since uncommon matter one may rely in such case on Maharam Benet to do on burial day; from second year onward fast death day. But one whose father died and was buried the day after — fast death day even first year as above — examine. So Shach. But Taz ruled always yahrzeit only on death day even if far from burial — and what of us if he did not mourn twelve months if impossible; none concern themselves — end.
**The first.** Shach wrote in OC siman 568 Rabbi concludes; however some are strict to fast both; so Maharshal — they practice thus — Bach brought — end.
**The second.** And likewise if they died in leap year in first Adar — fast in leap year in first Adar; so Maharshal there that plainly if died on first or second Adar one fasts thus in leap years. Until here Shach's language.
