**Counts.** Taz wrote that in Tur it is written after this that R' Yechiel wrote that Shabbat does not count for him at the beginning of the count but he begins to count on day one, etc., and Beit Yosef ruled like the first view. Nevertheless I brought the words of R' Yechiel to learn from him that if he died on Sunday — that is, Shabbat is the seventh day of mourning — that one need not observe prohibition in private matters except for part of the day, i.e., in the morning at sunrise. One might object: but we say in siman 395 regarding "part of the day like the whole day" of the seventh day that it depends specifically on the comforters standing beside him and sunrise does not suffice as on the thirtieth day. This is no difficulty at all — for wherever they said "part like the whole," that part must be from the same matter for which he is obligated on the other days, such as in counting seven where he must do an act of removing a sandal and the like; and there they say he must on that part of the seventh day do the act obligated upon him, and they gave a boundary until the time the comforters rise from beside him — it is fitting to be in active performance of what is incumbent upon him to observe mourning at that time; not so for the law of thirty where there is no obligation of active performance but only passive — namely prohibition of laundering and haircut; here sunrise suffices since he already observed prohibition passively. Therefore one who heard distant tidings who observes one hour must do an act specifically at that hour as in close tidings seven days. Therefore when the seventh day falls on Shabbat, part suffices — for even on the whole Shabbat he does not do an act recognizable as mourning, only something private applies, and it is passive, and part of it suffices which is exactly like the thirtieth day as explained clearly. Not like Bach who wants to disagree with R' Yechiel and say we do not apply "part like whole" on Shabbat, and wants to forbid that mourner whose seventh falls on Shabbat from reading Torah even at Minchah — which is not so; even in the morning there is permission to read Torah for him — only since on a weekday he waits until after Shacharit, on Shabbat too he should do so; but at Minchah there is complete permission to read for him per R' Yechiel, who holds there is no full seventh-day law upon him, and none disagree. There is also proof from what is written in seif 5 in the name of Ramban that all that Shabbat private matters are forbidden — because he still has mourning obligation on Motza'ei Shabbat. It implies that otherwise he is not forbidden the whole Shabbat but only part like the whole — and that is certainly the essence. Shach too disagrees with Bach here and wrote that this is how Talmud and poskim language implies — one should not depart to split hairs — end.
