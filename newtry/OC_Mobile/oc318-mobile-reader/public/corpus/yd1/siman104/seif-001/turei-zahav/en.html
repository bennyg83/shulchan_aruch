And specifically in sheretz, etc. Beit David wrote the reason: lest something like a lentil remain, which is the measure of its tumah, since from the beginning of its creation it was like a lentil it is called beriah and is not nullified — end; and it seems they brought this to distinguish from what Tur wrote: if cut in a way one cannot clarify and remove it, all is forbidden — clear; for Rama we hold a crushed beriah is nullified, and so in Derishah in the name of Rashal: and even though above siman 101 where issur was crushed it is nullified — sheretz is different, for eating issur is like a lentil like its tumah, and we fear a lentil of sheretz was absorbed; see Rashi in Avodah Zarah daf 69 — end; and he wrote on this that we did not need to distinguish for this, for when cut beriah deals with its kind in its kind and issur is not recognizable within heter; unlike here in thick food where one cannot clarify — nevertheless issur is recognizable among heter — all forbid; only we must distinguish from Beit Yosef: if melted completely, etc., and siman 109 wrote from Terumat HaDeshen: even in not its kind, however nullified in sixty even a kezayit; therefore distinguish other issurim from sheretz — end; and Maharach wrote here like these Rashal words.) I wondered greatly why they thought this — what Tur wrote "if cut," etc. is per the Gemara Avodah Zarah daf 69: mouse fell into vinegar — Rav Hillel told Rav Ashi, there was an incident in Rav Kahana's house and Rav Kahana forbade; he said: it crumbled; Rashi: fine pieces; sheretz issur is like a lentil like its tumah, fear a piece of sheretz was absorbed with vinegar; at first glance it implies he was particular about lentil measure — impossible to say, for half a measure is forbidden d'oraisa, even half a lentil or any amount — we only need a lentil for lashes liability like kezayit of nevelah; and so from Tosafot who challenged: how do we eat bee honey when bee legs are mixed in honey — obvious one who eats honey mixed with bee legs who does not eat lentil measure of legs is not liable; and from their conclusion: what we say limbs have no measure, even less than kezayit from a corpse and less than a lentil from sheretz — learn it deals with less than lentil measure. I also saw Ran in chapter HaSocher challenges Rashi: why lentil — in other issurim too concern for less than kezayit; he challenged further; and Beit David wrote another explanation; and Rashi is not specific to sheratzim but cited the smallest issur most stringent — liable even for lashes on eating; not like Rashal and Rama that sheretz is different; and what Rama wrote in Beit David that if there is like a lentil it is called beriah — I wonder greatly on his holy mouth to say so, for in beriah laws we said explicitly siman 100 regarding ant: if part crushed, beriah importance is nullified; and Beit Yosef siman 101: if part crushed from it, importance nullified; and Beit Yosef siman 101 a fortiori here cut into fine pieces as Tur wrote; and so Or Zarua general rule 25 law 2: like lentil of eight sheratzim, crushed sheretz, etc., even though liable on them, nullified at their measure — end. What Derishah wrote: here issur is recognizable but cannot be clarified — so is true, similar to Beit Yosef siman 115 for Rambam on non-Jewish butter: issur recognizable — the cream — but cannot be clarified, therefore not nullified — see there. It appears Tur here did not come to make sheretz stricter than other issur; he only taught mouse laws in beer and vinegar, and detailed if mixed; what is forbidden when cut is not because of beriah importance but lest a thin piece of sheretz was absorbed in beer or vinegar; Beit Yosef intended this too with half measure forbidden d'oraisa even less than lentil; Tur's explanation: if sheretz melted completely until like liquid of beer and vinegar — measure is in sixty, wet in wet; if cut and not melted — issur substance remains, mixed in liquid, not wet in wet or dry in dry, for all nullification is because unrecognizable — unlike here recognizable but cannot be clarified — therefore sixty does not help here; unlike here where issur was not uprooted and not nullified at all, neither in sixty as wet in wet nor in majority as dry in dry; properly forbidden if one swallows it b'ein — therefore all forbidden here; this is the explanation of "crumbled" in the Gemara; there is no distinction between sheretz and other issurim; the law stated here applies to all issurim if mixed, recognizable, and cannot be clarified — so is clear to me; this hagahah has no place to stand for now, except still to challenge Rashba who wrote on forbidden butter Rambam gave reason because cream does not mix in butter so its minority does not nullify — Rashba challenged as we cite siman 115: even if not mixed it is nullified in minority; if so why is mouse different in beer or vinegar — here too cream in butter is recognizable and dry in wet and should nullify since cannot separate — it seems to me regarding mouse too concern lest one eat the issur piece by itself; granted dry in dry issur becomes heter through majority by Torah decree; not so here issur does not become heter, only taste in butter is permitted because cannot separate issur — that is the basis of heter; but whenever cream is somewhat recognizable, throw it away and do not eat it except as part of butter — therefore permitted; but this does not apply to mouse, lest one eat issur by itself.)
