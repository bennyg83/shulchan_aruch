Whether it was cut, etc.; and if cut, etc.; and specifically, etc. Rashi 69a s.v. "crumbled," etc.; and as stated in Me'ilah 16b; Tosafot and Ran challenged why this was needed — we hold half a measure is forbidden d'oraisa; and furthermore, strain it in a sieve and it should be permitted. Shulchan Aruch says: and if cut, etc., and it is in a case, etc. — this answers Ran's second question; and even with sixty, for the Gemara means it does not forbid at all because of noten ta'am lifgam; nevertheless they forbade if cut. On Ran's first question, the hagahah says: and specifically, etc., as Rashal and Shach wrote — the bones themselves are not nullified even in a majority while standing alone, called dry in dry; rather we come because of beriah; and even a crushed beriah is nullified here since its measure is a lentil it is like a beriah. They were challenged by Taz and Peri Chadash: even if very large, as long as it is not whole it is not called beriah; only the issur is not nullified while recognizable, as Yerushalmi chapter 2 of Orlah; and if the heter is recognizable, one does not nullify the issur, as Terumot chapter 4 — how fifty figs, etc.; therefore nullification is only wet in wet or dry in dry. Shulchan Aruch: whether it melted completely, etc. — wet in wet; and if cut, etc. — dry in wet, meaning in thick food like thick gravy, still wet since not standing alone, and even in a thousand not nullified since by touch in the dish one senses the issur — recognizable issur [but Rashi's view as Tosafot Zevachim 72a s.v. "and let them be nullified," etc. — whatever is forbidden in less than a kezayit is beriah at its issur measure like gid; no question from ant since specifically separated, etc., and it is like a whole ant; Rashi is correct; and Tosafot there, even though in Makot they did not write so but like Rosh, Tosafot in Zevachim are more correct; and one can say Rashi: here a limb is forbidden even in a thousand; and Tosafot in Me'ilah].
