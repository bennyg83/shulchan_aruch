Field mouse. Shach wrote: it appears from the Mechaber's words that a field mouse certainly imparts beneficial taste in beer and vinegar; and it is possible that even in wine, oil, and the like it does not spoil. But in the Gemara it is proven that even though it is served on kings' tables, we are uncertain whether it is noten ta'am lifgam in mixtures.
Issurim. Beit David wrote: the reason is that with a sheretz we are concerned lest something the size of a lentil remain, which is the measure of its tumah and it is a beriah and is not nullified; and Taz challenged him at length and refuted his words, concluding that in truth there is no distinction between sheretz and other issurim. What is forbidden when cut into pieces is not because of beriah importance but because we fear a thin piece was absorbed in the beer or vinegar; this deals with a recognizable issur that cannot be clarified — in that case sixty does not help, for sixty helps only when the issur is not recognizable. So too for other issurim; and so is the view of Rashal and Bach (see Kenesset HaGedolah who refutes Taz; see Peri Chadash who expanded).
