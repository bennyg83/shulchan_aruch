(Shulchan Aruch siman 104 seif 1) Mouse in beer, etc. Shach s.k. 1: it appears from the Mechaber that field mouse, etc., is obvious that it imparts beneficial taste in beer, etc.; but in the Gemara it is proven that even though it is served on kings' tables we are uncertain whether noten ta'am lifgam in a mixture — see there. At first glance it is incomprehensible: the Gemara says a mouse fell into a barrel of beer and Rav forbade that beer; the rabbis told R' Shimon; shall we say Rav holds noten ta'am lifgam is forbidden? See there. Rava said: the halachah is noten ta'am lifgam is permitted, and a mouse in beer — we do not know why Rav forbade. If because Rav holds noten ta'am lifgam is forbidden and we do not rule like him; if because Rav holds noten ta'am lifgam is permitted and a mouse in beer imparts beneficial taste — then it seems obvious the sugya is a city mouse, and we are uncertain whether it imparts beneficial taste or spoils; but for a field mouse we are not uncertain at all. One can say Shach explains the sugya: certainly for a city mouse we are not uncertain whether it spoils, but we are uncertain for a field mouse; and "this in the field, this in the city" means: since R' Shimon generally holds noten ta'am lifgam is permitted, here it is a novelty; they challenge: it is served on kings' tables — if so there is no novelty; if field mouse in beer is noten ta'am lifgam, why forbid? Therefore: since there is a mouse that is repulsive and nevertheless the Torah forbade, noten ta'am lifgam is forbidden for a mouse; so a field mouse in beer, even if noten ta'am lifgam, is forbidden — clear.
