Field mouse, etc. Shach wondered: whence for Shulchan Aruch to say field mouse certainly imparts beneficial taste in beer and vinegar — in the Gemara we only say that since it is served on kings' tables it is not repulsive; but what does that have to do with noten ta'am lifgam? One can say the Beit Yosef wondered why the Gemara brought this pilpul: whether field mouse is repulsive or served on kings' tables — for the law there is no difference, for a mouse itself is forbidden, and for emission too there is no difference, for even city mouse if it fell where it imparts beneficial taste it is forbidden, and in the field likewise one eats taste whether it imparts beneficial taste or spoils; therefore we are uncertain whether it imparts beneficial taste or spoils in itself — if in substance it spoils, one can say its emission's taste also spoils; this is the safek in the Gemara whether it imparts beneficial taste; if like its substance so its emission, then field mouse that in substance is praised, its taste too has no safek that it is highly praised; therefore the Gemara brought it and his words are correct.
And if it was cut, etc. See acharonim who expanded and investigated; the essence is Shach and Kenesset HaGedolah: certainly it is not beriah as Or Zarua wrote explicitly that sheretz like a lentil is stricter than kezayit of nevelah that is not beriah; however the reason seems as Shach and Kenesset HaGedolah wrote: those fine pieces stand alone and cannot be removed because of the thickness of the beer, so it is dry in dry. According to what I raised below siman 109 in the words of Beit David and Maharshal: dry in dry, not its kind, kezayit within the time of eating a half-loaf — the reason: if one ate them all he would come to lashes, for an olive of issur mixed in six olives; but if mixed in more, one cannot come to lashes — if one ate a hundred olives there is still no clear olive of issur; and if one eats more he already exceeds the time of eating a half-loaf and there is no combination for lashes; if so sheretz in food the size of a kezayit — lashes measure is like a lentil even if mixed in a thousand olives, if one ate them all he would come to lashes since within the time of eating a hundred olives one would eat a lentil of sheretz by itself and there would be lashes liability; what difference to me nevelah olive within six olives that within eating a half-loaf one reaches lashes liability, and what difference sheretz in a hundred that within eating a half-loaf one reaches lashes liability — therefore certainly it is not nullified dry in not its kind; if so. These matters are clear to that approach; what to investigate for that approach see below siman 109; but here the law is true law for that approach without doubt. And regarding whether it is beriah: know Rambam and Rashba disagree on the law of separated limbs of sheratzim that impart tumah, the eight sheratzim in the Torah; Rambam chapter 2 of Forbidden Foods: after their death, one who eats a limb from them nevertheless its measure is like a lentil like eating from sheretz; there is no distinction between in their quantity or limbs; and so seems Tosafot Avodah Zarah regarding bee legs; therefore a limb from them is not called beriah, for it needs measure like sheretz; one cannot say "do not eat" between large and small. But Rashba Avodah Zarah 104b wrote explicitly: the eight sheratzim that impart tumah with tumah of a lentil — their eating for lashes is also like a lentil; and so the law for their limbs that impart tumah in any amount — their eating prohibition is likewise, etc. — he holds one is liable for eating in any amount; if so this is included in "you shall not eat of them" large or small, since there is no measure for eating; and if one divides, its name "whole limb" no longer applies, and this is a full beriah like a limb from the living; on the contrary, for living limb at least a kezayit is required, only a majority of flesh suffices; here no measure at all. However Rashba wrote explicitly it is not called beriah for not being nullified; and possibly even a limb from the living Rashba did not count among beriah that is not nullified, neither in Terumah nor in his novellae; and possibly also not called forbidden from the beginning of its creation, for in life we require measure as explained in the Gemara; there in Avodah Zarah (though one can question: this issur from not eating sheretz was in life too, only for lashes there was no measure — nevertheless issur was immediate; it is not like nevelah of impure fowl whose nevelah issur did not apply from life) — therefore for this reason Rashba did not count it beriah; at least for Rosh who does not require forbidden from the beginning of creation, certainly it is beriah; if so in this mouse that dissolved, certainly one tiny whole limb has beriah law and is not nullified in a thousand; and truly Rashi's words that eating is like its tumah like a lentil do not teach this — one can push that it is likewise for limbs that have no measure; and Rashi cited eating and tumah as one law, and requires study.
