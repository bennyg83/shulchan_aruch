Field mouse, etc. It appears from the Mechaber that a field mouse is obvious that it imparts beneficial taste in beer and vinegar since it is served on kings' tables (and possibly therefore even in wine, oil, and the like it does not spoil); but in the Gemara (Avodah Zarah daf 68b) it is proven that even though it is served on kings' tables we are uncertain whether noten ta'am lifgam in mixtures — see there; and even though one can push and reconcile, nevertheless it does not appear so to Rif, Rosh, Tur, Rambam, Ra'avan, and other poskim who did not mention field mouse at all but wrote simply "mouse" — end.
Or in vinegar if it did not remain inside twenty-four hours, etc. See above siman 105 s.k. 2 from this.
And specifically in sheretz there is concern, etc. Know that we learn in the last chapter of Avodah Zarah (beginning daf 69): they asked — a mouse fell into vinegar, what? (meaning whether a mouse imparts beneficial taste in vinegar); Rav Hillel told Rav Ashi: there was an incident in Rav Kahana's house and Rav Kahana forbade it; he told him: it crumbled. Rashi explained: it was cut into fine small pieces; and sheretz's measure is like a lentil like its tumah, and we fear a piece of sheretz was absorbed with the vinegar; and so Ra'avan, Mordechai, and Agudah there; and so Or Zarua general rule 32; but Ran wrote on Rashi: wonder why this was needed — in other issurim too there is concern for less than a kezayit, for we hold half a measure is forbidden d'oraisa; and further: strain it and it should be permitted, etc. — end; and to reconcile: certainly in other issurim, since it is noten ta'am lifgam, there is no concern for taste; and also for substance there is none, for it is nullified in majority since it is not recognizable (and see above siman 103 s.k. 3 what is written about dry in dry — and even though not its kind dry we hold sixty, that is because if cooked it would impart taste, which is not so here where it is noten ta'am lifgam); and what is written "strain and permit" — one can say as Tur and Beit Yosef wrote: cut in a way one cannot clarify in a sieve, such as vinegar mixed in thick food; therefore sheretz, since its measure is like a lentil, is not nullified, for it is like beriah, as Or Zarua wrote there; and so appears Tur's view who did not write the law of cut into fine pieces and cannot clarify except here regarding mouse and not other issurim; and no posek omitted to write so for other issurim — this is the Rav's and Bach's view; and from Beit Yosef's words it appears even less than a lentil; and so for other issurim not nullified even in more than sixty whenever it cannot be strained; and so Maharshal Pesachim siman 49; and for now as I wrote; and it seems clear to me Ran did not write so except according to his understanding that it deals where one can strain — but for the law he agrees if one cannot strain it is literally dry in wet, for we hold everywhere we follow majority as written "follow the majority"; know Rashba in Terumot daf 110b wrote like Ran; and in responsum siman 101 wrote: a pot of soup into which a gnat fell — if a limb was cut from it or even whole and crushed and its form lost, it is like other issur and is nullified — end; Beit Yosef brings this in siman 66 — apparently whatever cannot be strained is nullified.
