BeHaT wrote regarding inverted nuns and see responsum Ma'il Tzedakah siman 29 who wrote his view — that all who are more than two inverted from letters within the verses themselves — behold the adder diminishes from letters of Torah, for two we heard and all written more — behold Torah lacks those inverted letters, for inverted nun is not in the category of letters — see there. And see in responsum Lechem Todah from the Gaon Mahari Bassan z"l siman 28 his view is not so — see there he wrote lean upright for inverting the plain kaf in the verse "to guard the way of the tree of life" — he raised one may not drag and correct what scribes already did per tradition mouth to mouth — see there at length. And see responsum of Maharshal siman 73 who elaborated greatly on inverted nuns and wrote there he searched and examined in Torah scrolls and saw twelve forms regarding nuns; and explained there which form is essential, which is valid after the fact without need to correct, which is invalid even after the fact and must erase and correct, and method of their correction — see there. And see responsum Noda BiYehudah Yoreh De'ah siman 74 who disputes Maharshal and raised scattered nuns are independent signs — one before "and it was when traveling" and one after the end of parashat "and it was when traveling" in the space between parshiot; and so one should not diminish parashah space — after end of "from the camp" which is before "and it was when traveling" leave in middle of line space for nine letters, then the scattered nun, then space of two or three letters and begin "and it was when traveling" on that line as closed parashah law; and after end of "thousands of Israel" leave space like two letters, then scattered nun, then nine letters' space on that line, and at head of second line begin parashat "and the people," etc. — thus one exits all concerns and form of scattered nuns as Or Torah wrote. And see there further he also disputes what Maharshal wrote there — if scribe made extra nun between parshiot the Torah scroll is invalid, for even one extra letter invalidates — this does not exist and one does not invalidate the scroll because of extra letter where it stands independently and does not ruin the word — see there. And see responsum Beit Efrayim Yoreh De'ah siman 64 who rejected his proof; nevertheless he raised the law is true regarding extra letter independently one does not invalidate; nevertheless certainly one must correct and erase it from there, but the Torah scroll is not invalidated thereby; but extra word is invalid, and if found in middle of reading they take out another — see there. And what BeHaT wrote in name of Shach — one invalidates if "cursed be he who lies" was written in a closed parashah — see Teshuvat MeTz siman 90 there who wrote one does not invalidate in this, and as found so it shall be; but ab initio the scribe should not write there closed; and likewise word "dachah" — as found so it shall be, and ab initio he should write with hei (and likewise in responsum Teshuvah MeAhavah part 1 siman 71 — see there he elaborated on this). Further wrote regarding "and all the days of Noah" found written "and they were" — one does not take out another on that Shabbat and the reader reads "and it was" — see there. And see responsum Beit Efrayim there regarding word "patsua dakha" found written with alef — if the Torah scroll is not yet complete, only the error is in the sheet, or even in the Torah scroll but other errors are still found so it is certainly invalid through it; and if he wants to correct this error to erase the alef and write hei in its place — permission is in his hand; specifically if the Torah scroll is valid one may not remove it from validity to erase and correct for this alone. And see there further regarding "ki tevet" at end of Bereishit — must be missing without vav; and he wrote he saw in newly printed work it should be full vav and write "tovot" — he writes all scribes and books err; but he erred greatly, heaven forbid to do so; and if found one who wrote full must correct and write missing — see there.
