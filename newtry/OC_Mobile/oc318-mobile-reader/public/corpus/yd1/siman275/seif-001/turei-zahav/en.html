If he made an open section, etc. All the more so, if he left the space of a section in a place where no section belongs, the scroll is invalid. See what I wrote in seif 2.
It should be placed in genizah. It cannot be corrected without erasing words and rewriting them in smaller script to create the necessary space, which lacks beauty. This is the Rambam's view. The alternative opinion, that of the Rashba, holds that the prohibition against reducing the script applies only initially. After the error occurred, one may write smaller letters to repair it.
The scribes customarily, etc. When rewriting, the scribe should make the new letters resemble the surrounding letters as closely as possible, so the scroll will not appear mottled. This appears to be the Rosh's intent. Correction is effective when the script can be matched, though in practice this is often difficult.
