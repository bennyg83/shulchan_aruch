He rendered it a closed section, etc. Likewise, if he left a break and blank space where there is no section, or wrote continuously and failed to leave a space where a section begins, the scroll is invalid. This is stated by the Rambam in chapter 8 of Hilchot Sefer Torah. The Bach writes that, according to the Tur, if one left blank space where none was required, the scroll is not invalid after the fact; only an open section made closed, or a closed section made open, is invalid. His words are not compelling.
Some say that it may be corrected, etc. This is provided that he does not compress the writing in order to create the space between one section and the next, as the Tur states in the name of the Rosh. Nevertheless, after the fact, the scroll is not invalid if he erased before or after the section and thereby compressed the writing. See the Bach, and see below at the end of siman 279.
For he may not cut out the Divine Name and make holes in the sheet. At first glance, this appears to mean that it is forbidden because one may not make holes in the sheet, and the Levush also appears to understand it this way. This cannot be correct. Were that the reason, why would the prohibition apply specifically when he encounters a Divine Name? It should likewise be forbidden to cut out other words, because making holes in the sheet is disgraceful. Moreover, the Rosh writes in responsa, klal 3, siman 7, 'Heaven forbid that one cut out the Divine Names,' which indicates that the prohibition is due to the honor of the Name. Maharal ibn Chaviv likewise writes in responsum 2 that the Rosh certainly did not prohibit this because the Torah scroll would remain invalid afterward. Although the questioner was concerned about that, as his wording indicates, the Rosh plainly was not; rather, in his opinion, the Rosh prohibited it out of respect for the sacred Name. It is also difficult to explain the Rama's words, 'and make holes in the sheet,' as excluding removal of the thin upper layer of parchment. Since the Rosh, and likewise the Rama, prohibited the act out of respect for the Name, it is certainly forbidden to remove that thin layer, for this appears even more disgraceful and approaches erasure. Maharal ibn Chaviv also writes this. It therefore appears that the Rama wrote 'and make holes in the sheet' to exclude a case in which the Name was originally written on a patch attached over a hole. In that case, one may remove the patch together with the Name. The prohibition applies specifically when one cuts the Name from the sheet in a manner that creates a hole, because cutting out the Name is disgraceful. When it was written on a patch, however, the patch may be removed when necessary for a repair. The words of Rabbi Yehudah son of the Rosh, cited by the Beit Yosef at the end of siman 276, must be understood similarly: 'A sheet contained two Divine Names, one of which was superfluous, and they cut it out in a manner that left a hole there. It would have been preferable to remove it rather than cut out the Divine Names, and my father, of blessed memory, wrote likewise.' Why did he specify that a hole remained, unless the explanation is as I have stated? Consider this carefully.
