Nine letters. This means the space occupied by the word 'asher' three times, as the Rambam writes. The Beit Yosef states that perhaps a section is not invalid if he left less space than the three words 'asher, asher, asher,' provided that the space still equals nine small letters. If there is not enough room even for nine small letters, however, it may indeed be invalid, for the received rule is that anything less than a space of nine letters is not considered a section break. Nevertheless, some authorities maintain that a space of three letters is sufficient. See the Derishah's discussion of this matter.
He should not depart from the Rambam's ruling in this matter, etc. The Rambam writes at the beginning of chapter 8 of Hilchot Sefer Torah: An open section has two forms. If the preceding section ends in the middle of a line, one leaves the remainder of that line blank and begins the open section at the beginning of the next line. This applies when the blank space remaining on the line equals nine letters. If only a small space remains, or if the preceding section ends at the end of the line, one leaves the entire next line unwritten and begins the open section at the beginning of the third line. A closed section has three forms. If the preceding section ends in the middle of a line, one leaves the required space and begins writing at the end of that same line at least one word from the beginning of the closed section, so that the blank space lies in the middle. If insufficient room remains on the line both to leave the required space and to write one word at its end, one leaves the entire remainder of the line blank, leaves a small space at the beginning of the next line, and begins the closed section in the middle of that second line. If the preceding section ends at the end of a line, one leaves the required blank space at the beginning of the next line and begins the closed section in the middle of that line. Thus, an open section always begins at the beginning of a line, and a closed section always begins in the middle of a line. End quote. The Levush writes regarding the Rambam's second form of an open section, where only a small space remains on the line or the previous section ended at the line's end, that the same applies if this occurs on the bottom line of a sheet. One leaves the top line of the next sheet blank and begins writing on its second line, so that a full blank line separates the sections. According to the Rosh, however, this constitutes a closed section. See further in the Tur and Beit Yosef for the Rosh's view and those of the other authorities concerning what they define as open and closed sections. See also chapter 8 of the Rambam's Hilchot Sefer Torah, where he explains the open and closed sections at length. The Tur and other authorities also discuss another form called a 'set section.' I have not prolonged the discussion, because the accepted practice is already known among scribes, who know and study the proper method. See responsum 37 of the Maharshal.
