The Rambam. The Taz wrote that there are two disputes between the Rambam and the Rosh. One: if he leaves an empty space at the beginning of the line equal to nine letters and begins the paragraph in the middle of the line, this is closed according to the Rambam and open according to the Rosh. Second: if he finishes at the end of a line, or there is empty space but not the measure of nine letters, and afterward he leaves one entire line blank and begins the paragraph at the start of the following line, this is open according to the Rambam and closed according to the Rosh. The rule is that according to the Rambam only a paragraph beginning at the start of a line is open, and only one beginning in the middle of the line or at its end is closed; end quote. The Levush wrote that according to the Rambam it appears to me that the same applies if this occurred on the bottom line at the end of the page, where only a little space remains, or he finished at the end of the line: he leaves the upper line at the top of the next page blank and begins writing on the second line, so that he leaves an empty line between them. See Responsa Maharshal siman 37, and the Rambam, chapter 8 of Hilchot Sefer Torah, where he discusses at length the closed and open paragraphs. Written. The Shach wrote: nevertheless, it implies that this is specifically when a Sefer Torah is found that was not written according to the Rambam's words, for one may attribute and say that it was written according to some great authority. But if it is known to be a scribe's error, that he erred and changed from the Rambam's order in a place where no posek disagrees with him, it appears that it is invalid even after the fact. He further wrote that he was asked about a Sefer Torah in which, in Parashat Tzav, "vayedaber Hashem" of "kol chelev" should have been open, as it is in every Sefer Torah, but it was not open, and they wanted to invalidate it. He answered that it is clear that a precise scribe wrote it according to the Rambam, who did not count this as open at all in his count of all open paragraphs in the Torah. Certainly this Sefer Torah is fit, straight, clear, and the Torah of Hashem is pure; end quote.
