Nine. Beit Yosef suggests that less than the space of nine small letters may invalidate, although some authorities accept three letters after the fact. Taz rules that the Shulchan Aruch's measure of nine, accepted by most authorities, is controlling.
Letters. Meaning the space occupied by three repetitions of the word 'asher.'
Rambam. Taz identifies two disputes between Rambam and Rosh. First, blank space for nine letters at the beginning of a line followed by the new section in midline is closed according to Rambam but open according to Rosh. Second, finishing a line with insufficient remaining space, leaving the next line entirely blank, and beginning at the following line is open according to Rambam but closed according to Rosh. For Rambam, an open section begins only at the start of a line, while a closed section begins in its middle or end.
Written. Shach permits relying on a Torah scroll that differs from Rambam's section layout when it may have been written according to a recognized authority. But where the variance is known to be a scribal error in a place with no dissenting view, the scroll is invalid even after the fact. He also validates a scroll whose section at 'all fat' differs from common copies because Rambam does not list it as open.
