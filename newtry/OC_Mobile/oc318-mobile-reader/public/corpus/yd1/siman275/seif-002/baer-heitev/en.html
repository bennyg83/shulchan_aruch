Rambam. And Taz wrote there are two disputes between Rambam and Rosh: one — if one leaves empty space at the beginning of the line for nine letters and begins the parashah in the middle of the line — this is closed for Rambam and open for Rosh. Second — if he finishes at end of line or there is empty space but not nine letters' worth, afterward he leaves one whole line empty and begins the parashah on the line after it at its beginning — this is open for Rambam and closed for Rosh. The rule for Rambam: it is open only what begins at the start of the line, and closed only what begins in the middle or end of the line — end quote. And Levush wrote: per Rambam's view it appears the same if it happened on the bottom line at the end of the page where little remains, or he finished at end of line — he leaves the top line on the next page empty and begins writing on the second; automatically he leaves empty between them a line. And see responsum of Maharshal siman 37; and in Rambam chapter 8 of Laws of a Torah Scroll he elaborated on closed and open parshiot.
Written. Shach wrote: nevertheless it implies specifically when a Torah scroll is found not written per Rambam's words — one may attribute and say it was written per some great one; but if known it is a scribe's error who erred and changed from Rambam's order where no posek disputes him — it appears invalid even after the fact. And he wrote further: he was asked regarding the Torah scroll that should be in parashat Tzav "and the Lord spoke" that "all chelev" is open as in every Torah scroll and it is not open — they wanted to invalidate it; he answered the matter is clear this is an accurate scroll written per Rambam who did not count this open one at all in his count of all open parshiot in the Torah — certainly this Torah scroll is valid, upright, and clear — Torah of the Lord is pure — end quote.
