Regarding the form of open and closed there is a dispute between the Rambam and the Rosh; and one who fears Heaven should satisfy all of them and do as follows: for an open parashah he finishes the preceding parashah in the middle of a line and leaves at its end a space of nine letters, and begins to write at the head of the line beneath it; and for a closed parashah he always finishes the preceding parashah at the beginning of a line, leaves a space of nine letters, and begins to write at the end of that same line. And he should not change to make another form, neither for open nor for closed—and thus it is valid both for the Rambam and for the Rosh. {Rama: And if he cannot aim thus, he should not deviate from the Rambam's words, for the Acharonim relied on him and wrote that his words are primary (Mordechai in the name of a certain sage). Nevertheless, if a Sefer Torah is found not written according to his words, one does not declare it invalid, for there are great authorities with other views in this (R' Yitzchak Minz siman 8).}
