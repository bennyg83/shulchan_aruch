The Derishah cites opinions requiring only three even for an open section. For practical halacha, however, we follow the Shulchan Aruch, whose measure is supported by most authorities.
One should not depart from the Rambam's words. The Rambam and Rosh disagree in two cases. First, when nine letters of blank space are left at the beginning of a line and the next section begins in the middle, the Rambam regards it as closed, while the Rosh regards it as open. Second, when the prior section ends at the end of a line, or leaves less than nine letters, and an entire blank line is then left before the next section begins at the start of the following line, the Rambam regards it as open, while the Rosh regards it as closed. According to the Rambam, an open section begins only at the start of a line, while a closed section begins in the middle or at the end of a line. The arrangement in seif 1 is valid according to both.
The Tur further writes: 'In Tikkun Soferim there is also a parashah called sedurah: one writes a line to its midpoint or one-third point and begins on the line beneath opposite the blank space above. It appears that if one deviated from this, the scroll is not invalid, since this form is not stated explicitly in the Gemara.' The clear meaning is that some sections which according to our Talmud are classified as open or closed are classified by Tikkun Soferim as sedurah and arranged in the manner described. The Tur says that if the scribe did not follow that special form but instead made them open or closed, the scroll remains valid because the category of sedurah is not mentioned in the Gemara. I write this because my father-in-law understood Tikkun Soferim to require a paragraph break where according to our tradition there is no break at all, and from the Tur's statement inferred that leaving a paragraph-sized blank where no paragraph belongs does not invalidate. That cannot be correct. Leaving a paragraph break where none belongs is an obvious invalidation and did not need separate mention. The Baraisa in chapter HaBoneh concerning an open section made closed or the reverse presupposes the locations of the sections; it does not license inventing a new section. Rambam likewise explicitly invalidates a scroll where a blank was left in a place where there should be none.
