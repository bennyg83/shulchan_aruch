He should leave a space of nine letters and begin, etc. This differs from the Tur, who assigns three letters to a closed section and nine to an open section. The Derishah cites opinions requiring only three even for an open section. For practical halacha, however, we follow the Shulchan Aruch, whose measure is supported by most authorities.
One should not depart from the Rambam's words. The Rambam and Rosh disagree in two cases. First, when nine letters of blank space are left at the beginning of a line and the next section begins in the middle, the Rambam regards it as closed, while the Rosh regards it as open. Second, when the prior section ends at the end of a line, or leaves less than nine letters, and an entire blank line is then left before the next section begins at the start of the following line, the Rambam regards it as open, while the Rosh regards it as closed. According to the Rambam, an open section begins only at the start of a line, while a closed section begins in the middle or at the end of a line. The arrangement in seif 1 is valid according to both.
