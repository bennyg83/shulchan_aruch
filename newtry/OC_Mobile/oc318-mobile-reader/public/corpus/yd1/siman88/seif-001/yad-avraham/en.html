(siman 88 seif 1) Even meat of wild animal and fowl — forbidden to place it on a table where one eats cheese, lest they come to eat them together. There is difficulty — this reason is not in the Gemara chapter Kol HaBasar — it would be decree on a decree, for eating fowl itself is rabbinic as written siman 87 — shall we stand and decree decree on decree placing fowl because of eating it? Rather the conclusion is we decree placing fowl because of placing domestic animal in a first boiling vessel which is Torah prohibition — and so Rashba in Torat HaBayit and Ra'avad and Tosefta — see there; and what Taz wrote here is not understood — see there and Taz siman 98. And it appears to me one who examines the Gemara and Rashi there will see regarding domestic animal placing and eating are one — placing is eating that they will come to eat together; for otherwise even per the conclusion it would be decree on a decree (see Lechem Mishneh — his words require study) — they decreed placing fowl because of placing domestic animal; and placing domestic animal itself is a decree because of eating it; there is no saying placing domestic animal in first vessel is itself Torah prohibition by cooking in basar b'chalav; therefore we decree placing fowl because of placing domestic animal. This is not — for per Mechaber siman 87 seif 4 they did not decree any decree because of cooking as Shach wrote there — end; rather one must say placing is eating — when they came to decree on eating they saw that if they do not decree on placing there will be no fulfillment of the eating decree — and all is only one decree. And similarly prohibition in many places. See Maggid Mishneh chapter 9 Laws of Forbidden Foods halachah 4 — appears he intended what I said, unlike Lechem Mishneh; and also unlike Tur HaChadash there. And since we merited the ruling that placing and eating are one regarding domestic animal — the same for fowl; and we need not further say we decree placing fowl because of placing and eating domestic animal — rather placing fowl is its eating — and this is precise in Shulchan Aruch's language: forbidden to place lest they come to eat together. It follows there is no decree on a decree — they decreed placing and eating fowl because of placing and eating domestic animal; and therefore Taz may have intended this.
