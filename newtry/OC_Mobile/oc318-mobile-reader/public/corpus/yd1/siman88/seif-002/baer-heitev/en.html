Those who know each other — who are not ashamed of each other and each will eat from his fellow's (it appears even one person sitting at the table with no one else — nevertheless forbidden lest he come to eat them from forgetfulness — Beit Yosef). And Shach wrote: it appears specifically meat is forbidden to place on a table where one eats milk or the reverse, because people do not refrain since each is permitted by itself; but permitted to place nevelah meat on the table where one eats kosher meat; however forbidden to place bread of prohibition on the table as meat and milk — the reason because a person is accustomed to it; and see siman 221 and Orach Chaim siman 440.
Heker. Shach wrote: from Tur in Orach Chaim 173 and from Mechaber here it implies even if they have one expense the heker helps — and so poskim (in Beit Yaakov siman 12 he permitted a Jew to sit with a gentile who eats treifot — we are not concerned lest he feed him to trap him; but one who eats meat is forbidden to sit with one who eats cheese, for there is concern that since he knows him he eats cheese he will not know he ate meat and will hand him food and he will eat — end of his words).
That they drink. Shach and Bach ruled if they drink from it, it is not a heker even though it is not customary on the table — and Beit Yosef and Rav are primary.
Lamp. Shach wrote: meaning when it is not customary for it to be there such as by day and the like; but if customary at night in that place it is not a heker (for every table needs a lamp).
Sticks. Therefore even if they are at two tables and even two guests — forbidden — and so Orach Chaim.
