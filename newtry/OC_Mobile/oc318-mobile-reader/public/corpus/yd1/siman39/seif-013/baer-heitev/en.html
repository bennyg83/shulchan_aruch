With alacrity. And Maharshal wrote: what the Mechaber wrote to insert his hand with alacrity, etc. — on the contrary, in all versions of the laws of examination it says "he shall insert his hand gently"; and so: when he comes to examine he shall cut in the courtyard of the liver and be careful to cut gently and tilt his hand toward the ribs so he does not cut toward the body cavity; and when he inserts his hand inside he shall move it gently between the arugot and the ribs — end.
Ordinary [lung]. And Shach wrote: however after reduction one must examine with saliva or lukewarm water to see if it does not bubble (acharonim):
With force. And Darkei Moshe wrote that one should not press with dirt and sand, and one who does so is as though feeding treifot to Israel; and Maharshal wrote: I heard that in some communities that are not people of Torah they take a thick rag and press with it — such people certainly feed treifot to Israel; and it is fitting to warn slaughterers not to press so forcefully, but to roll between the fingers; and Taz wrote: I also saw that some examiners are not careful to press gently and rely on inflation of the lung — and they feed treifot; for certainly in every complete sircha inflation does not help — end:
Treifah. And the reason is because it is not in order:
For sircha. And Taz wrote: nevertheless one should not press and rub sircha that is attached emerging from a boil or from a tinnir, as stated siman 37 seif 2 and in this siman seif 9 (and likewise in every case of "two for ri'uta"):
Abundant [loss]. And Bach wrote: and they practice to permit even not in a place of abundant loss; and "let Israel be better off as unwitting sinners" etc. (and likewise a thief whose [lung] became sircha-adherent to his pouch — they press as is customary) (Pithei Teshuva) (and Shelah wrote daf 74, and so: "And you, children of Israel, I command you not to eat meat declared kosher by reduction in a place where sircha would render treifah by law, and do not buy meat from the slaughterer until you investigate this; and residents of the Land of Israel and all the dispersed of Israel dwelling in the kingdom of Turkey declare treifah" — end).
Reduction (miyuchak). Bach wrote: it appears to me specifically in tender calves — meaning within its year and it is nursing, etc.; but kids and lambs — even though it is not nursing, as long as it is called by the name kid and lamb in human language, its law is not to press them (Shach). And see Peri Chadash: a slaughterer disagrees — he slaughtered and examined and declared kosher four calves; and in the calf he examined first, sircha was afterward found by another examiner; and when he asked the slaughterer he admitted it was an error in his hand that he did not feel the sircha — the other calves he examined afterward should be declared treifah (Lev HaKohanim in the name of Tzemach Tzedek, see there; and see Teshuvot Einayim Yeshayim question 24 that it appears he sided to be lenient). Pithei Teshuva wrote: some places do not press kids and lambs until 15 Av, and in some places until Rosh Hashanah. (And in Beit Yosef he also wrote until Rosh Hashanah they are judged as small ones, and from then on they press as with large ones):
