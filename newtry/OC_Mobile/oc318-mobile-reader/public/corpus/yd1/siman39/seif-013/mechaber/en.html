There is one who says that the examiner should insert his hand into the animal swiftly without slackness of hands; and if any sircha separated when the examiner inserts his hand — he removes the lung outside and examines it; if it is sircha he will find its head in the lung or on the wall, and if not — it is moisture and kosher. And one may not be lenient in this except in an animal of Israel, and one does not rely on this leniency except with an examiner who is fit and fears Heaven greatly. {Rama: And there are those who permit rubbing sirchot and pressing them, and they say that sircha — if a person presses it all day — will not detach; therefore wherever it is pressed they rely to be lenient and say it is not sircha but mere moisture (Mahariu and Kol Bo). And even though this is a great leniency, already all the inhabitants of these countries practiced thus and one may not protest against them since they have upon what to rely; nevertheless the examiner must be God-fearing, who knows to beware to press gently so it will not detach by force. And there are places that do not practice pressing and rubbing attachment cords of the rose if they became sircha'd to another place — and wherever it becomes sircha'd — treifah (so I heard, and so it appears from the language of the R"tz ibn Chiya in Beit Yosef). And there are places that practiced to be lenient also in this; but to me it appears like the first reasoning — not to press boroda and in all sirchot that are not in order, since sircha not in order is mentioned in the Gemara and no one disputes it — one may not rely on the words of those who are lenient regarding pressing and rubbing (and so is implied from the language of Mahariu that there is no dispute). But the custom in our city is to rub and press all sirchot, and there is no distinction between sircha and sircha; and it is proper to be concerned for what I wrote if not in great loss. Furthermore they practiced in our city to declare treifah all sirchot of kids and lambs (found written) and tender calves, and not to press them at all, for there is a received tradition in this to declare treifah because the sircha is still soft and detaches through pressing.}
