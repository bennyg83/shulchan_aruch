So Rashal: we did not receive thus; on the contrary, in all versions of examination laws it is written that he should insert his hand gently; and such is the language: when he comes to examine he should cut in the courtyard of the liver and beware to cut gently and tilt his hand toward the ribs so as not to cut toward the body cavity, etc.; and when he inserts his hand inside he should lead it gently between the arugot and ribs, etc.; and so it appears from Rosh's language, who wrote "gently," etc.
To beware to press, etc. So Rashal: indeed I heard that in some communities that are not sons of Torah they take a thick rag and press with it — such people certainly feed treifot to Israel; and it is fitting to warn slaughterers not to press so forcefully, but to roll between the fingers and somewhat minimally — end. And I also saw that some examiners are not careful to press gently and rely on inflation of the lung — and they feed treifot; for certainly in every complete sircha inflation does not help.
And there is no distinction between sircha and sircha. It appears nevertheless one should not press and rub sircha that is adhered emerging from a boil or from a tinnir, as stated siman 37 seif 2 and this siman seif 9.
