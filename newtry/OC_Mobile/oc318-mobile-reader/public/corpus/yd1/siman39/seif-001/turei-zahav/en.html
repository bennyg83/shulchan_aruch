And they also practiced to inflate, etc. In Beit Yosef he brought here the words of Mordechai in the name of BeHag that one must remove the lung, and if he did not do so it is a doubt of treifah. And Darkei Moshe challenged: behold Beit Yosef brought in siman 35 in the name of Rosh in the name of BeHag that if the lung was wrapped like a red egg — kosher, and this is when it does not emit wind — meaning it requires examination; if so, his words contradict his words here — that here he requires in every animal to remove the lung and inflate it; and he extended in forced answers. And Moharach z"l also forced himself to say that BeHag deals when after he inserts his hand inside and finds ri'uta — then he removes the lung. And it appears to me to answer: BeHag holds that we do not rely on majority in a place where we can clarify, as Mordechai wrote at the beginning of Chullin in the name of R' Yitzchak Levi — and we mentioned this above several times; nevertheless, whenever the lung is before us and we can examine it — we do not rely on majority, and for him it is a doubt of treifah if he does not examine by inflation; but if it was lost he concedes we rely on majority in animals whose lung was not punctured. But regarding a lung that was wrapped — above, since there is ri'uta, it left the category of majority, and there is no kosher in the world except after clarification that it did not emit wind; therefore he wrote there "and this is when it does not emit wind" — meaning that this matter will be clear to us, and afterward it will be permitted even if the lung was lost, as explained — clear is the view of the hagahah. But Rambam and other poskim hold that even when it is before us, examination inside suffices if there are no sirchot — and no more. And what Rama brought in seif 2 "there are those who are stringent if it was lost," etc., and a source reference was marked there showing this is BeHag's view — is a mistake; rather for BeHag it is kosher whenever there is no ri'uta and it was lost. But "there are those who are stringent" — is R' Yitzchak Levi and Ra'avan; and even though in Beit Yosef it appears he did not decide thus — in my humble opinion I wrote this.
