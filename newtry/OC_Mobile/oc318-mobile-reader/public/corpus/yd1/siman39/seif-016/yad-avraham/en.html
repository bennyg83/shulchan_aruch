(There seif 16) The examiner said it was not in order and another contradicts him — they establish the animal in the presumption of permissibility. One must say this deals when the lung was lost and it is impossible to clarify; but if it were possible to clarify, we are concerned for the words of the one who forbids and clarification is required. And as Tosafos wrote, chapter HaOmer (daf 65b), s.v. nitme'u (and on daf 66), s.v. shalach. And likewise the hagahah, siman 127 seif 3. And the same law applies regarding a boil on the lower part, as the hagahah wrote above siman 37 seif 4. And see Mahari Weil, klal 20 s.k. 19. And see Shach siman 83 s.k. 11: where one could say "from whence did it come?" — here there is a general rule that clarification is not required; however, according to one view it is worse. And Ritva in Eruvin (daf 59) wrote: even in d'rabbanan, where it is possible to establish the matter, we do not rely on establishing lekula unless there is great effort — we judge as though it cannot be established. I have proof that in a safek of law clarification is not required: we hold that in d'rabbanan we act and afterward raise objections. And where there is a safek, clarification is not required; and likewise we rely on majority — only on presumption we do not rely. And so in pesakim of Mahari and in She'erit Menachem, Laws of Mikvaot. With this one may explain what was challenged on the Rambam, chapter 12 of Laws of Bikkurim — see there. And he wrote in She'erit Menachem it was safek 1 in d'rabbanan. However, he wrote in Beit Avraham: if it is about to become clear on its own, such as an animal that limps — it is forbidden to eat its milk even where there is safek that perhaps the limb did not fall off, and you will say it fell off, perhaps the tendons were not consumed. However, in responsa Ginat Shoshanim he wrote to permit. And likewise in responsa Tur HaEven he permits even when certainly the tendons were consumed, for it is a balanced safek and the animal itself has presumption of permissibility, for it was not born in this safek; and also in Beit Avraham itself he retracted below. However, what Beit Avraham brought as proof from the Rosh, chapter 2 of Avodah Zarah — it slipped his attention, for he already rejected this in Mahari Weil. Also in She'erit Menachem he concludes that clarification is never required. And see Noda B'Yehuda who distinguishes regarding safek where there was presumption of prohibition: if the presumption was always, such as presumption of an animal in its lifetime that never had presumption of permissibility — clarification is required. But a prohibition that had presumption of permissibility — safek helps to remove from presumption of prohibition and establish presumption of permissibility as originally, without clarification — such as presumption of a limb, and as Hagahot Maimoniyot regarding three sefeikot.
