They declare it kosher. It appears this deals when the butcher explicitly says he found treifot in the lung; but if he said merely treifah he can say he found some hole in the courtyard of the liver that the second did not find, for the examiner is not obligated to examine afterward whether there is a hole in the courtyard of the liver — as we will mention siman 41 with Heaven's help. In this certainly one may not declare kosher; and even if he says "I searched the courtyard of the liver and did not see a hole there" — "I did not see" is not proof, for perhaps the first saw what this one did not see — some small hole. And so is proven the matter of the incident in responsum of Rivash siman 497: they declared the animal kosher because the first attributed the treifot to the lung and thought it was entirely adhered, but in truth it was not open there at all.
