There is no testimony here, and it is like an animal, etc. And the Bach wrote: and I wonder why the Rama did not write in the hagahah that for us, who are stringent regarding a lost lung, in these two laws too one should be stringent; and so ruled Maharshal, first chapter of Chullin, siman 21 — end of his words (also in Darkei Moshe the Rama brought the words of Rabbenu Shimshon, stam). And in truth Maharshal ruled thus. But it appears to be the Rama's view: for perforce we, who are stringent regarding a lost lung, it is certainly d'rabbanan — for examination of the lung is d'rabbanan, as all the poskim wrote. And since he did not act according to the Sages' enactment, they forbade even b'dieved; for if not so, he would not fulfill the Sages' enactment. But when one says it was examined properly and was kosher, and one says it was treifah — in either case it was examined and he did the enactment of the Sages; only this one says kosher and this one says treifah. If so, it is one against one, and we establish the animal in the presumption of permissibility — for certainly all agree it was slaughtered and stands in full presumption of permissibility, and every doubt after slaughter we lean leniently. Only that the Sages required examining the lung because it is liable to become treifah; and from this they forbade even b'dieved when it was not examined. But by law it is full permission — as I demonstrated in my book with many clear proofs and demonstrations; and as written in s.k. 8. And the Bach himself likewise at the beginning of this siman. If so, when the lung is before us and one says in order and one says not in order, and the like — certainly it stands in the presumption of kosher (and in any case there is no difficulty, for the majority of poskim and the Mechaber hold that a lost lung is kosher, and their reasoning is primary in many places in the Talmud, as in my book; and the Rav in the hagahah and the Bach relied on them in a case of great loss. Therefore, even though they are stringent where there is not great loss, nevertheless give it to them — do not add upon it; and this is specifically when it was truly lost, unlike here). And Rabbenu Shimshon and the Mechaber, who wrote "and it is like an animal whose lung was lost," etc. — this is what he meant: just as there it is permitted by law from the reason "once slaughtered, it was permitted," so too here.
