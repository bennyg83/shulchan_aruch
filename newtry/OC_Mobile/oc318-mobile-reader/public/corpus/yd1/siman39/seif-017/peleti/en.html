Since both came at once — see below siman 127 where Shach brought Ritva's view: when we say thus in matters of forbidden relations and similar that require two — if they believe the one who came first, he is like two; but in other prohibitions where we hold one witness is believed in prohibitions — he has no greater credibility than one witness, even if he came first; requires study. For it appears to me thus is the view of the Rambam, whom Lechem HaMishneh challenged regarding two who say he died and two who say he did not die: the Rambam's view is not that one witness when he comes first is like two — even when a woman comes first her law is like two and another woman cannot contradict her. However, regarding one witness who says she became impure in Sotah and one witness says I saw the adulterer — the Rambam rules: specifically regarding valid testimony, when he comes first he is like two, but not when a woman comes first; and Lechem HaMishneh left it requires study. And it appears according to Ritva one must say: that which the Gemara says regarding Sotah and one witness when one witness comes first he is like two — behold it is like prohibitions generally, as Shach challenged below. And one must say: since the verse is superfluous to validate one witness — the verse is unnecessary, for the general rule is one witness is believed in prohibitions. And one must say the Torah intentionally used a verse to teach that one witness in this matter is like two, since the Torah specified explicitly; otherwise it would be superfluous. But this is regarding a valid witness; women and similar — were it not that the verse expanded them, they would not be believed at all. And it is enough that the verse expanded them to be believed, but not to be believed like two. If so, one may say regarding two who say he died — the law is that nothing of forbidden relations is less than two, only the Sages equated them to two; if so, when a woman comes first she is believed like two. But regarding one witness and Sotah that require two — the Torah equated him to be like two since a verse was needed; therefore when he comes first his words stand — not so invalid testimony, where the verse taught us to be like a valid witness but not like two; he is only like any valid witness believed in a prohibition, and the law returns to Ritva's reasoning and we do not say he is like two. Examine well — and this is clear; therefore since the Rambam agrees to Ritva, certainly one should be stringent as he says, even if one witness came first and said "in order" and afterward the second contradicted him — nevertheless there is safek. Examine well.
