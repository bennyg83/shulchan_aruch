Since both of them came at once. It appears this was required: for if the buyer came first to court and they forbade on his word — afterward they would not believe the second to permit, since the first was believed. And Ulla said in Yevamot (daf 117): wherever the Torah believed one witness — it is as two, and one witness cannot contradict him; and they established it there when they permitted the woman on his word — here too when they forbade on his word. Therefore here it was required that both come at once. And all the more if the one who permits came first to court — it is permitted; but it is not relevant for the permitter to come to court first when there is no prohibition before him. And it appears that in the previous seif too it must be said that both came at once — one law applies; and both in Beit Yosef in the name of Rashbatz and in Shulchan Aruch he says "not only this but also that" — at first in seif 16 he speaks when the forbiddener and permitter are both before them, and afterward he added: even though the buyer told the seller first that it is forbidden — nevertheless since they came to court as one we do not heed what he said outside court. And Rashal Perek 1 of Chullin siman 21 brought them and disagreed, and wrote: since the permission is on account of that it was like a lost lung without examination — automatically for us, who forbid regarding lost lung, here too it is forbidden; if so, according to what Rama wrote in this siman seif 2 — in great loss or kids, etc. — one may declare kosher regarding lost lung also here — specifically in such a case. And already Moharach z"l wondered at Rama that he wrote nothing here on Shulchan Aruch's words; and accordingly what Shulchan Aruch wrote in seif 5 — if there is doubt whether on back, etc., from Rashba's words in Beit Yosef from "once slaughtered, it was permitted" — according to Rashal we should forbid there too, and the presumption of "once slaughtered, it was permitted" does not help; for what is different from here where we compare it to lost lung? But in my humble opinion Shulchan Aruch wrote well to declare kosher there, and also here in these two seifim; and Rama did well not to emend anything here or in seif 5 — for certainly we say "once slaughtered, it was permitted," and it is like a wolf that took the innards, etc. And that it does not help some great ones to say thus regarding lost lung at the beginning of this siman — is because sirchot are common in animals, and the entire lung is in the presumption that we say: if it were before us perhaps we would find in it sirchot; more common to find sirchot than not to find; and sirchot forbid in the majority of the lung except in few places that are permitted — namely in order; therefore they were concerned perhaps there is sircha in the majority of the lung. Unlike here in the two seifim and seif 5 where everyone sees this lung is entirely beautiful — only in one place there was doubt whether forbidden or not; certainly we do not say forbidden sircha is more common in this place than permitted sircha, and there is no ri'uta for prohibition — to say it was not in order more than to say it was in order. And likewise in every similar case where doubt is before us and both sides are equal — it is simple we say "once slaughtered, it was permitted"; and so in that of seif 5 the sides are equal and we see the entire lung in the place where prohibition applies there is no ri'uta — only in one place there is doubt — we say "once slaughtered, it was permitted," for sircha is found even in a permitted place; and further it is a rabbinic doubt — as Rama wrote shortly after this. And that Rashbatz attributed the reason that it was like lost lung — is for clarity of the matter, since for us it is kosher regarding lost lung; but in truth even one who forbids regarding lost lung permits here; and all sides are equal that there is full permission here; and a fortiori if combined with that even if lost it is permitted by law, as Rashi [said]: "yesterday we ate of its milk and now shall we establish it as treifah?" And so most great ones agreed; only a minority were stringent, and the acharonim were concerned for their words in this — certainly there is no prohibition for all; and one listens to the words of the authors of Shulchan Aruch, for they are correct. But nevertheless what I wrote above at end of siman 1 in the name of Rashal — if slaughtered properly is forbidden — is not relevant here; for there we say an animal in its lifetime stands in presumption of prohibition until you know it was slaughtered properly; and there the doubt is in the body of slaughter itself — so it appears clear to me.
