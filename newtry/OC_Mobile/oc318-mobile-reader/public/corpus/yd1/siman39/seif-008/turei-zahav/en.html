That it is not adhered to any place. Even though there is no sircha without a hole, as above — here it is permitted, because sircha that comes on account of a hole becomes sircha'd to another place; but hanging sircha is only peeling of moisture — so Rosh; and therefore it appears to me: even though Shulchan Aruch concluded here that examination is not required — nevertheless according to what Rama wrote in seif 1 that the custom is when there is sircha passed by rubbing they practice inflating it — the same applies here, that examination is required; for it is one reason: hanging sircha and sircha passed by rubbing — both of them we maintain as peeling of lobes, as explained.
From a tinnir — kosher. It implies that hanging sircha that is adjacent applies; but when stuck to another place there is no leniency here at all; and so in examinations of Mahariu: sircha emerging from within the tinnir — kosher — meaning hanging sircha; simple matter that otherwise it would not be relevant to say kosher, but in any case it requires rubbing, and if it passes one examines it by inflation; for plainly it is not better than other sirchot — rather regarding hanging sircha it applies; and so Rashal brought: hanging sircha also in hagahah of the R"tz — so too; except in another book he disagreed and wrote that even when adhered it is kosher since there is no moisture in a tinnir — certainly the sircha comes from elsewhere; and his words are not correct at all; on the contrary it is called "two for ri'uta"; and there rubbing and reduction do not help at all; and see siman 37 seif 2. And it appears the same applies if below half it emerges from a tinnir — it is not called "two for ri'uta" even though it adheres on two sides; and so in Hagahot Bedikot to declare kosher in this even if it emerges from a boil; and it appears that here rubbing is required as with other sirchot.
