A lung punctured in the place of cutting the onot on the back of the ona opposite the wall, and the wall seals it in sircha in the flesh between the ribs, or in flesh and bone — kosher; but if it became sircha'd to the bone alone — treifah. And the same law applies to all the whole opposite the onot above until the arugah — kosher, because the place is narrow and pressed to the wall and it seals it; and it is called "wall" until the thread that they clear from the chest, and from there onward is called "chest"; and if it became sircha'd there — treifah. And if it became sircha'd on top of a thread in the chest — its law is as if it became sircha'd in the chest and treifah. {Rama: And for us there is no practical difference in these distinctions, also in all the distinctions that will be explained until the end of the siman, for already they practiced in these lands to declare treifah everything; and one may not change, for it is the custom of the ancients in Ashkenaz and France that we are sons of their sons; therefore one may not declare kosher any sircha in the lung except two onot sircha'd in order below halfway toward the side of their root; and the same law applies to an ona in an uma in order thus. And it was already explained that they practiced to be lenient in sircha that detaches through pressing and rubbing.}
