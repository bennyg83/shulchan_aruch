And the wall sealed it, etc. — Peri Chadash s.k. 40 challenged at length on the Tur: in the Gemara we say, R' Nachman: a lung that was punctured and the wall sealed it is kosher; Ravina said: provided it is entangled in the flesh. The Gemara challenges from that which we taught: this is disqualified but returns to fitness — not to exclude such a case, etc.; and Tosafos and Rosh wrote that Rav Bar Shalom ruled like R' Nachman and not like Ravina, and from his words we learned Ravina argues with R' Nachman, and proved to his words that if R' Nachman also held like Ravina, the challenge above on R' Nachman who said a lung adjacent to the wall that raised plants — we are concerned for it, for R' Nachman holds when the wall seals it is kosher — and it answers: in a place of revita of ona; and why not answer it is not entangled, etc. — learn he holds entanglement is not required. And further, since it challenges Ravina and not R' Nachman — learn R' Nachman holds neither sircha nor entanglement is required — end. And the Tur wrote in hints, and in Tur: Rav Shalom ruled like R' Nachman that sircha seals and we do not require entanglement — and they wondered: Tosafos and Rosh derived from the challenge on R' Nachman that entanglement and sircha are not required; and Bach and Mahari Weil already strained to reconcile, and Peri Chadash rejected their words — better his silence from speech, for he wrote on the Tur that he did not understand his father's intent. And it appears to me the Tur's words are correct: that Ravina and R' Nachman disagree is from two proofs. First proof: that which the Gemara challenges on "raised plants" from "the wall sealed it," and answers it deals where it is not a place of revita, and does not answer it is not entangled — this is correct and necessary: for R' Nachman, sealing even without entanglement; but from these words to make a distant dispute that R' Nachman permits even without sircha at all — one may say R' Nachman agrees sircha is required, and the case of raised plants is not "raised plants" dealing where there is no sircha at all, for R' Nachman said a lung adjacent to the wall, and Rashi explained: stuck to the wall — and you have no greater sircha than this, until a knife is needed to separate them, as explained in the Gemara. Second proof: why does it challenge from "we taught: punctured, disqualified" on Ravina and not on R' Nachman? (And what Mahari Weil and Peri Chadash wondered — R' Nachman's words are closed, how can we challenge on them? Perhaps their intent: he holds no sircha at all — Maharsha already stood on this, and likewise Maharam of Lublin, and several intentions of Maharsha's text: if Ravina — to explain R' Nachman's words who said "the wall sealed it is kosher" — he comes to explain it means entanglement; and it is difficult how R' Nachman stated so broadly "the wall sealed it." Perhaps he erred in the simple sense that the puncture is opposite the wall; if so, since it deals in a place of revita of ona, Rashi explained the lung and wall grow as one, always adjacent — therefore stam "a lung punctured opposite the wall is kosher," for in the chest constriction it cannot be otherwise that the wall will seal, as Rashi wrote they are always adjacent; therefore one must say his intent is that it be entangled in the wall — and this is the explanation of "the wall sealed it." If so, apart from Ravina's words, one must say it at least deals with sircha, for otherwise "the wall sealed it" is superfluous, as stated; if so, it should immediately challenge R' Nachman from this braita "punctured," etc., for in any case there was no sealing initially — and from this proof Tosafos and Rosh: for R' Nachman neither sircha nor entanglement is required. But this is one explanation; however in the end they challenged: perhaps Rav Shalom ruled like R' Nachman and not Ravina, who is later; and they raised that this is not the later Ravina but Ravina who was before Rava in the days of R' Yehudah, and all the more before R' Nachman; if so, there is no place for the challenge and claim why it challenges Ravina and not R' Nachman — behold Ravina was earlier, and then R' Yosef challenged his challenge to Ravina, and R' Nachman literally was not in the world; and then Ravina's explanation, and when R' Nachman afterward came and stated his statement, Ravina's answer was already known and he could no longer challenge R' Nachman on anything; if so, there is no proof here, and he holds R' Nachman does not argue except on entanglement and does not require sircha — from these words one might make a distant dispute, and also "the wall sealed it" compels thus, as stated — and this is clear. See daf 47 in Tosafos who wrote for himself: R' Yosef challenged this challenge from the braita "punctured," etc., on what he heard initially; therefore they did not challenge Rava on what he heard initially — see there. With this Mordechai's words are also justified, whom he brought in the name of R' Chaim: healthy lung adjacent to the wall in a place of revita is kosher — meaning when there are no boils and blisters at the place of sircha; but if there are — treifah. And they wondered on R' Chaim: if the wall seals the puncture, why is it treifah for boils and blisters? Further, it speaks of raised plants that is not in a place of revita it is treifah, but in a place of revita it is kosher. See Peri Chadash in the aforementioned s.k. And what he strained — and it appears to me the matter is simple, and so Mahari Weil: if we require entangled in flesh, it does not apply to boils and blisters, for they are not entangled — how would they become entangled? And a blister impedes, and he holds like R' Chaim, like Tosafos that Ravina argues with R' Nachman, and holds Ravina is the later Ravina, and the halachah is as Tosafos wrote; therefore for R' Nachman who argues with Ravina and holds entanglement is not required — we properly say in a place of revita even raised plants is kosher; but according to what we hold like Ravina, entanglement is required, and in plants and blisters it does not apply — therefore treifah. This is the difficulty. With this one may understand what Tosafos wrote: since it answers "in a place of revita" and does not answer "it is not entangled" — learn R' Nachman argues. And Peri Chadash challenged: perhaps not in a place of revita, even entangled it is treifah — and truthfully it answers; and according to what I wrote it is not in reality that there would be raised plants even entangled; if so, one cannot say it teaches that not in the chest constriction and raised plants, even entangled, is treifah — for this is not in reality: if plants grew, there is no entanglement. Examine well.
