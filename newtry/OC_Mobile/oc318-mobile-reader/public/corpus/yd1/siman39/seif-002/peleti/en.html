And some are stringent if the lung was lost; and Shach wrote in his book that he proved it is only d'rabbanan and forbidden since he transgressed the rabbinic enactment — for otherwise what did the Sages accomplish in their enactment — and this is correct. And esophagus and gullet — they compared them, Rama above siman 33, to lung where ri'uta is common; perhaps one should be more stringent if the esophagus was lost. One may say the esophagus is safek in slaughter, as explained at length siman 33; requires study. And it appears: if the slaughterer slaughtered and went away and there is no one to examine the animal, and if he waits until the examiner comes the meat will spoil — it is permitted to eat without examination, for in a place of loss they did not decree, as explained in Laws of Yom Tov siman 498: on account of loss where it was dangerous it is permitted to eat without examination — and the same applies in such a case.
