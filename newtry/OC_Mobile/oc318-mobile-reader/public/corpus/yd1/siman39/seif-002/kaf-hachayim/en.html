(11) There: except for the lung one must examine, etc. — this examination means the examiner inserts his hand inside gently and feels all parts of the lung softly with moderation, and returns his mind to feel whether there is deficiency, exchange, sirchot, or other treifot dependent on the lung. This examiner must be expert — not only expert in all laws of lung examination, but also expert in hand and feeling, knowing every form of the lung, its place, and manner of placing it inside the live animal. (21) There in hagahah: and in some places they are lenient not to inflate, etc. — and even in places where inflation is not customary, nevertheless one should remove the lung after internal examination to examine it visually; and great ones decreed cherem on examiners in their place for this. Shulchan Aruch HaRav letter 2, part 4 letter 4; he wrote: certainly now in this generation one should not rely on internal examination. And so Toldot Zahav siman 41 letter 1: from the custom of places that inflate every lung that the Rav brought in Beit Yosef, etc., our teachers ruled in the hagahah: early ones learned some of this stringency and accepted that the examiner must remove the lung outside and examine it for several treifot that cannot be felt in internal examination by hand-feeling, only visually — namely sircha in it, or two adjacent boils, or redness in lung, or wound on wall, or sealed and disqualifying appearance, or boil on lower part, etc. — treifot requiring visual sense. This examination is called hagahah. And Raavan in siman 49 wrote: some great ones decreed cherem on examiners for this. And so Shulchan Gavoah letter 3: so is custom in Salonika. And rabbis of India, may it be built, agreed to practice in their city; and rabbis of Salonika sent a letter! bitterly to the slaughterer in their city Konya who was lenient not to perform this second examination — and beit din ruled if he transgressed after warning and did not perform hagahah examination, he must be removed from his craft — see there. And also in our time we know from the mouth of the rabbi of Konya that they did not practice hagahah on lungs of sheep and goats; and we girded ourselves as a hero — the young Rav Acha the wondrous Gaon Maharach Palagi — and we wrote there a responsum of victory and rebuke to obligate them in hagahah examination according to law and custom of all places we heard — see there, Toldot Zahav. Beit David. Zivchei Tzedek letter 12: so butchers of the city Baghdad practiced. And it seems thus is custom now in all places: after internal examination they remove the lung outside, inflate it, and examine visually for all kinds of treifot not sensed in internal examination.
