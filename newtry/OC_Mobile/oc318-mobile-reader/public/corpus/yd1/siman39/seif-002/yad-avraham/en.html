(Siman 39, seif 2 in the hagahah) And some are stringent if the lung was lost, etc. One may ask: if the enactment of the Sages was to require examination also regarding milk one already has — since if sircha is found, milk milked within three days is forbidden retroactively, as Rama wrote in siman 81 seif 2 in the hagahah — were they required to examine the lung for this? And what is the law if it was lost without examination — is the milk forbidden or not? Answer: It appears to me that this is the doubt in the Gemara, first chapter of Chullin (daf 17): limbs of neveilah that Israelites brought into the Land — what is the law? Do we say from now on it is forbidden for them to make neveilah, but what is in their hands from what was already made neveilah when they were in the desert while it was permitted — is it permitted, or not? And Rashi and the Rosh there explain that there is practical consequence in this. And it appears to me there is practical consequence regarding a rabbinic enactment such as this, that they enacted it is forbidden to eat meat without lung examination — was the enactment only on the meat itself, but if he has milk already milked while permitted he may eat it; or does the enactment apply also to what is already in his hands that was while permitted? Since this was not resolved, we hold: a safek d'oraisa lechumra and d'rabbanan lekula. And I found this explicitly in Yoreh Deah, Laws of Vows siman 218 seif 2 in the hagahah, and in Taz and Shach — see there. And we hold lung examination is d'rabbanan and safek lekula, as the Rashba wrote in a responsum and as the hagahah brings in this siman seif 17. One should not challenge: why did the Gemara not resolve this safek from that which is taught in Torat Kohanim, parashat Tazria, brought in the Yalkut there on the verse "When a woman conceives and bears" — to exclude one who gave birth before the [giving of] speech; brought in Tosafot Nazir (daf 54a) and Niddah (daf 70b) — see there. One may say: there the Merciful One made it dependent on birth, and it already passed while permitted before speech; but regarding neveilah the question is whether it depends on making neveilah while it was permitted, or perhaps it depends on the time of eating and it is now forbidden — examine well. But it is difficult for me: let it be resolved from the braita in Torat Kohanim brought in Horayot (daf 10): "A man, when there is in the skin of his flesh" — from speech onward; and Rashi explained: those lesions that occurred before speech do not impart impurity after speech. And likewise it is taught in Torat Kohanim, parashat Metzora, regarding a zav: "A man, a man when he becomes a zav" — from speech onward. And since Scripture was needed for lesions and zav to purify what was before speech — learn that generally, without a verse, even what was before was forbidden. One may answer per what Noda B'Yehuda wrote in responsa Mahadura Tinyana, siman 64: it is not asked except regarding limbs of neveilah, for when they were commanded they were not forbidden immediately, only after time; not so regarding something forbidden immediately — certainly even what was in their hands was forbidden; therefore the question does not arise for all prohibitions forbidden at the giving of the Torah whether what was in their hands was forbidden — end. According to this one may say it cannot be resolved from lesions and zav to limbs of neveilah that they are forbidden, since there the command was to impart impurity immediately, not so this command regarding limbs of neveilah. However, regarding the question, it appears there is room to forbid from the mishnah in the last chapter of Nega'im: "One who buys garments from a non-Jew must examine them first." And Rosh explains: this garment is fit for nega'im. And in responsum of Bach siman 130, acharonim bring it, s.k. 5, wrote regarding intentionally destroying the lung: it is forbidden on account of milk he already has.
