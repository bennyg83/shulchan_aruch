And there are those who are stringent, etc. In my book I demonstrated with many proofs that according to all, examination of the lung is only d'rabbanan; and so it appears in the responsum of Ben Lev, part 3, siman 20. And according to all, by law we say: once an animal was slaughtered it stands in the presumption of permissibility, and every doubt after slaughter we lean leniently; and for this reason we lean above, siman 36, seif 5 — a hole by the butcher and a wolf. Only that those who forbid hold that since the Sages required examination, they forbade even b'dieved when it was not examined, because if not so, what did the Sages accomplish with their enactment? For each one would throw away the lung without examination; and so wrote the Bach. But certainly, where the lung is before us and was examined, every doubt in it we lean leniently and establish it in the presumption of permissibility — see there (and above, siman 50, seif 1, in the hagahah, that we require specifically that we can attribute that it occurred after slaughter). And perhaps for this reason the Rama was precise in his sharp language: "and there are those who are stringent," and he did not write "and there are those who forbid" — rather he meant to say that those who forbid do not forbid by law, but hold that the Sages were stringent in the matter in order to strengthen their words:
Or in kids, etc. From the words of the R"tz, who wrote in the name of the Rav: therefore they are not accustomed to press on kids, lambs, and calves, since sircha is not common in them; and this one that became sircha'd — we say it is certainly complete sircha. It would have appeared to permit also soft calves if the lung was lost. But the Rama himself, in Darkei Moshe and in the hagahah, seif 13, wrote a different reason — that the sircha is still soft and detaches. And it is possible that this is what he meant: that sircha is not common in them; and since it became sircha'd, it is complete sircha.
