(13) And if he did not cut gently but tore with a knife in the ribs — there is no concern even without great financial loss when it is known he did not cut sircha, recognizable from the fact no sircha leak is before us. Daat Kedoshim letter 3. Mikdash Me'at letter 8. Zivchei Tzedek letter 3.
(14) Also for this a wise heart should tremble when coming to examine the lung: he should set before his eyes where he is fit to declare kosher food for many souls; his way is hidden and none sees, for Hashem is high and low sees; he should set all his eyes on the lung to examine gently and with great alacrity, and not insert his hand with arm and mighty hand forcefully. Know what is above you: behold Hashem stands over him to repay the man whose way is hidden for every hidden matter, and He will requite his hands. The essence of all: be alacritous to examine in fear of Hashem on his face, in awe and dread; and he should not speak during examination with any person, nor incline his ear to another matter — for with his soul he speaks; perhaps he will detach some sircha unknowingly and he will not know, and he bears guilt and sins of many depend on him. When he feels all parts of the lung he should feel gently and with great moderation, be expert in all laws of the lung, all its statutes and judgments; examination is only to tell a person the straight path in examination, requiring great intent; fear of Hashem is his treasure. The examiner should be kosher and God-fearing from many; who is he that will guarantee saying he is kosher and God-fearing? A wise person will hear and add learning; more, children — beware! And there will be no disaster — also blessed he will be. Toldot Zahav siman 7 letter 4. Zivchei Tzedek letter 5.
(15) And see Toldot Zahav siman 8 letter 18, who elaborated greatly on lung examination requiring first removing the rumen from the animal, then examining the lung — for several reasons: one, we are concerned for puncture of the treifot cavity, and while rumen is inside the animal the treifot cavity is not examined; also sirchot common in treifot cavity to rumen; also the lung is not examined well while rumen is inside. Therefore they practiced in Salonika, may it be built, and made an enactment not to examine lung until they remove rumen; he elaborated greatly — see there. Beit David. Zivchei Tzedek letter 6, who wrote in their city Baghdad, may it be built, the butchers include Armenians expert in detaching sirchot; if they make this enactment to remove rumen from the animal, there is concern they will detach sirchot when removing rumen, for they have great wisdom in this; on account of this they ceased. But they made another enactment: another person inserts his hand into the animal's belly and pulls the rumen out a little, and the examiner comes to examine the lung; and all while the examiner examines, this other person holds the rumen with him — thus they left the aforementioned concerns. He wrote: therefore every God-fearing person should do so when examining lung so he can examine well.
(16) If the lung is found full of moisture — examination for punctures is required, for on account of the puncture moisture came through the lung puncture, and this is the beginning of sircha.
