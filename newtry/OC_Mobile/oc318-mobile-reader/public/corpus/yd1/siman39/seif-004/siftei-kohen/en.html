Not in order, etc. Know that the poskim disagreed about the reason for the matter. Rashi and his supporters explained the reason that not in order is treifah: because there is no sircha without a hole. According to his explanation, the Tosafos conclude that the reason that in order is kosher is because when it is sircha'd in a place that is not positioned to separate, its neighbor closes it and protects it more, because the membrane goes and strengthens — end of his words. And the meaning is that the sircha closes its neighbor. And the Beit Yosef copied: the uma closed its neighbor. And the Tosafos already rejected this there; and so it is explained in the Rosh, as he explained; and so understood Maharam of Tiqqun in the hagahah on the Alfasi; and the Rashba and the Ran wrote according to Rashi that the uma closed its neighbor. In the name of the Raavad they wrote: in order, the sircha comes on account of the pressure of its reclining and not on account of a hole. The Rashba and the Ran themselves hold that there is sircha without a hole, and not in order is treifah because it is destined to separate and is considered like a hole. Maharshal, in his examinations and in Piskei Treifot siman 25, wrote on their view that it appears to them there is no sircha without a hole, like Rashi — but this is not so:
And some permit it, etc. Even without examination; and so all the acharonim; and so is the practice:
If it is from the root of the attachment, etc. And R' Meir wrote in s.t.h.: it appears to me that even if it stands in order at the place of the cut and the segments separated until the place of the sircha is visible — it is also called not in order — end of his words. But this is not clear, and it is also not implied from the words of the poskim and the acharonim; and so they practice to permit:
Even if fenestrated. That is to say, there is a window; and so is the practice; and so all the acharonim. And it implies that there is no distinction between an ona and an uma; and so in the hagahah, seif 18; and so the Bach — for such is the custom; and so other acharonim; and so they practice — unlike Maharshal in his examinations and Piskei Treifot siman 25, who forbids a window between an ona and an uma:
Like a type of membrane. In the Beit Yosef he ruled that there is no distinction between a thick membrane and a thin one, since the poskim closed their words and did not distinguish. Moreover, who can split hairs as to thickness? And the Bach wrote that this is not an argument — that the poskim mentioned in their words a membrane that they call tilla, and it is recognizable to experts by its thinness; therefore the Rama wrote in the hagahot on the Shulchan Aruch, stam: if there is a membrane, etc., not to render it treifah, and we rely on the experts who know and recognize it — end of his words. And it appears to me like the Beit Yosef, for the Semak, siman 141, and Kol Bo, and Mahariv, root 37, only mentioned a membrane, stam, and did not mention tilla. And this appears to be the Rama's view in the hagahah — that he wrote stam "membrane"; thus there is no distinction. And so explicitly in Darkei Moshe. And I wonder at the Bach, who misled the Rama's mind to something that never occurred to him; and so they practice to permit in every case:
From its root. Meaning, it is not fenestrated; but if there is a window in it, its law is like sircha; and so the poskim:
Until its middle. He mentioned customary language, since such is the way of a membrane to be found; but if not so, even more than its middle — for otherwise there is no distinction between sircha and membrane. And this is implied from the words of some poskim who did not mention "until its middle." And so Maharshal and the Bach: where there is no window, they permit even if it adhered entirely together from its head to its end:
Even if there is, etc. And in the Beit Yosef: if sircha emerges hanging from the adhesion between an ona and an uma, and it became sircha'd on that ona or uma itself — it is kosher. And we do not say: perhaps this sircha came from the uma, and its other head became sircha'd to its ona (meaning, from the cut toward the back, and the like) — for this would be a double doubt: a doubt on account of the lung and a doubt on account of the adhesion. And if you say on account of the lung — perhaps one head came in the place to which the other head became sircha'd to it — end of his words. And he brings this in Darkei Moshe and in the Bach; and so the Eitan Tzvi and the acharonim.
