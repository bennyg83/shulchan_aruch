To uma. Meaning: a thread of saliva going out from this one and sticking to that one. And the reason for treifah of sircha — Rashi explains — is that there is no sircha without a puncture, for the lung draws all kinds of liquids and [the substance] becomes thick inside it and goes out a little through the puncture, and it seals and becomes a membrane, and in the end it will come apart; and Tosafos wrote: even if there is no puncture now, in the end it will come apart and there will be a puncture:
Examination. And the reason according to Rashi's view — we say that although there is a puncture, its fellow seals it; and according to Tosafos' explanation — we say that in ksederan (aligned order) it will not in the end come apart (and see Peri Chadash what he brought in the name of R' Yitzchak Tzahalon):
Sircha. And Taz wrote: what we examine by inflation and place saliva on the place of the sircha and rely on this to declare kosher — namely, because the sircha already passed by rubbing as stated in seif 13 — consequently there was no sircha here at all; in this we require examination stringently, for if it bubbles it is treifah, and if it does not bubble it is kosher by strict law since there is no sircha. And what Rama wrote that we are not expert in examination — this refers to sircha that does not pass by rubbing, and there is sircha and treifah here, only that you come to declare kosher by force of our examination; and this does not help, since we are not expert in examination by inflation, and perhaps if one inflated properly it would bubble (and likewise in every place where examination is required by law and the main permissibility is on account of examination — we are not expert and it is treifah, and see above s.k. 7). All this appears clear to me, unlike many examiners who rely to permit even in a case of doubtful prohibition since it does not bubble — and they feed treifot; and therefore the case in seif 3 where the non-Jew took the lung before examination that we permit via inflation — if so, since the main permissibility here is via examination and we are not expert, one should declare treifah; and likewise in that case in siman 36 seif 2 where the lung membrane peeled and requires examination — it does not help according to us, as I wrote in the name of Noda B'Yehuda who disagrees with him regarding peeled lung that it helps according to us; one should also be stringent in that case in seif 3 that examination does not help unless there is great financial loss or kids and lambs; and he already challenged in Perishah why we practice examining with saliva and do not place the entire lung in lukewarm water; and Bach answered him that our examination is only as a stringency — end:
Mefulesh (open). Meaning: even if there is an opening there (even diagonally, provided the diagonal goes below half — Rashi). And Taz wrote: and it is not precise [to say] only until half; rather even if it goes from the root of the onot until any side between the onot — that is, until their heads — it is kosher when there is no opening; and what Rama wrote here "in the middle" is clear language, for there even if there is an opening there is no prohibition; unlike above half where it is treifah when there is an opening — but without an opening it is kosher even above. And so is the main ruling (Taz). And there is no distinction between ona and uma; unlike above half where it is treifah when there is an opening — but without an opening kosher even above:
In ksederan (aligned order). And Shach wrote: if it stands in order at the place of cutting and the onot separated until the place of the sircha is visible — it is implied from the words of the later poskim that it is called ksederan and kosher; and so is practiced to declare kosher — end.
Sircha. And Taz wrote: specifically from its root, for then it is not mefulesh; but if there is an opening there — treifah (and so Maharshal and Maharil). And Noda B'Yehuda rules: even if there is an opening between ona and uma — kosher, as Shach wrote s.k. 13; and he wrote further that there is no distinction between thick or thin membrane; and wrote further: if there is no opening, even if the entire lung was examined together from head to end — likewise kosher:
Kosher. And Shach wrote: however it is kosher only when the flesh of the lung beneath the sircha does not wrinkle in inflation, as the Rav wrote in the hagahah seif 9. And Taz wrote: but if that sircha also sticks to another place — treifah; and even to that ona itself — according to what will be explained in seif 9, for this is not called dependent sircha, and reduction and rubbing do not help here, since in one place there are "two for ri'uta" — so it appears to me — end (and so Maharshal and Shach declare kosher):
