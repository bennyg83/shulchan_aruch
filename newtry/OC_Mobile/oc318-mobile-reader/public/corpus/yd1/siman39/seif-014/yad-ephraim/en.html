Baer Heitev; and see in my book responsa Beit Avraham siman 12: a slaughterer who inserted his hand inside to examine and felt sircha on the wall and detached it from its place of adhesion to the wall and left it there until he would examine it by reduction as is customary; afterward the arbitrator inserted his hand to remove the lung and they searched for the sircha and did not find it at all; they examined in lukewarm water the entire area of the ona in that place and it did not bubble at all. According to the questioner, the arbitrator is not suspected of transferring sircha — only that this one is a commoner and did not know with him when inserting his hand how weak the adhesion was; if so, it did not pass from the lung through inserting his hand even with force, since it was already cut from the wall and the sircha did not impede him — that which is said his insertion caused it is only if he intentionally sought to uproot, and he is not suspected of this; therefore through touching via passing his hand it became detached. Or the first transferred it when withdrawing his hand, and we judge it as reduction; and even though in sircha not in order, with safek whether it passed by reduction or detached by force — one must declare treifah, for prohibition was established here where permissibility is more common, and it is not a balanced safek; and we attribute with certainty that this was done by the first one's withdrawal or the second's touching his hand — and it is mere saliva not on account of puncture — see there:
