And if the lung beneath it is whole and beautiful, etc. One should not interpret: if found beneath it that it is not beautiful — treifah, even though from the lung itself there was no treifah — nevertheless the mixed flesh combines to make treifah; this is not so at all. If it came to inform us there is treifah here, it should have written "treifah" and said: if beneath it is not beautiful — treifah; and it should have explained in what matter it is called "not beautiful" that we declare treifah on its account; and further there is no reason for treifah — to say "two for ri'uta"; for what ri'uta is there in a milk covering or mixed flesh? Rather it appears simple: the permission comes to inform us not to say the mixed flesh makes it treifah — that flesh is actually part of the lung; if so there is a change of appearance of treifah — as appearance of flesh or white as appearance of milk; it teaches us we peel off that flesh and do not heed its appearance, but examine the lung itself — if there is no ri'uta to declare treifah it is kosher; and this mixed flesh does not harm it at all. And again I heard that one posek declared treifah a lung on which mixed flesh was found; they peeled it and beneath it was like a scar — meaning wrinkles — for this reason he understood that whatever is not beautiful is treifah even if otherwise there is no treifah; and this is not clear at all — we may not add treifot; and the author of Rokeach never intended to teach us a prohibition for the reasons I wrote, as it appears to me. And he said: there is ri'uta here on account of that it is like sircha from the lung to that flesh — for if so examination would not help, for we declare treifah all sirchot, as above in the hagahah; rather simple: this is not called sircha at all, but thus is in its nature; and as I mentioned siman 35 seif 8 in the name of Rashal in the incident of Rinus, where skin was spread over the entire lung — they peeled it and declared kosher — here too it is so, as explained. And therefore he used here "whole and beautiful": initially the cuts of the onot were not evident on account of the covering, and also it had flesh appearance; therefore he wrote that since now below is whole from the side of cuts of onot and beautiful from the side of appearance — we do not heed the upper covering.
