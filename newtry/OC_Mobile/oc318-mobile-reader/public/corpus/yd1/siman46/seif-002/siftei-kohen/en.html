And if one returned them — if they were turned over, etc. It implies: whenever it is known they were not turned over — kosher; and so Tur; but if he returned them and did not turn them — kosher — end; and so in Sefer Lechem 171b; and he concludes: if the one who returned them did not conclude in his mind to examine this — in such a case we are concerned they were turned over; but where it is known they were not turned over — kosher, etc.
And the same law if they did not come out, etc. And Ra'avyah's view, whom Hagahot Shaarei Dura siman 88 brings, and Ran in his Orach Chaim brings: if they did not come out — kosher, whether turned over by themselves or by another; and so Maharshal Piskei Avodah Zarah siman 98 — see his name.
But if not, etc. And it is written in Sefer Lechem there: an incident came before me with a castrated rooster in which no tear or stitching was recognizable; the thin intestines were partly stuck to flesh and adjacent to skin; I said it is included in that which is written that no defect should be recognizable; and since there is a defect here I declared treifah — hands prove it was torn, and its castration also proves it had no eggs; and from that he did not explain whether we examine if they were turned over — it implies somewhat that one may not rely on examination in this — end; and in my humble opinion it appears: that which the Rav wrote "and no defect was recognizable in it" does not mean that no defect should be recognizable now — for behold, you have no greater defect than intestines found between skin and flesh; and furthermore, if so, it should have said "and no defect should be recognizable" — rather, it means that no defect was recognizable while alive — meaning it went well before slaughter, for there is no concern for crushing of limbs and falling; and so Orach Chaim explicitly — from him is the source of this law.
