Were turned over. And Shach wrote: but if it is known with certainty that they were not turned over — kosher.
Turned over. And Shach wrote that Ra'avyah permits in this, and so is Maharshal's view (and Peri Chadash wrote: we have only the ruling of Shulchan Aruch to forbid, and so Bach and Hagahot Maimoniyot).
Defect. And Shach explained: that which is written "and no defect was recognizable" does not mean that no defect is recognizable now — for behold, you have no greater defect than intestines found between skin and flesh; rather, it means that no defect was recognizable while alive — meaning it went well before slaughter, for there is no concern for crushing of limbs and falling; and so is Orach Chaim explicitly — from him is the source of this law — end; (Lechem Chamudos wrote: an incident came before him with a castrated rooster in which no tear or stitching was recognizable, and the thin intestines were partly stuck to flesh and adjacent to skin, and he declared treifah; and Peri Chadash disagrees and permits, and so Shach. Custom of idolaters in all these places is to castrate male chickens to fatten them, and they stitch the place of the tear; and world custom is to eat them and no one objects or complains, for one engaged in castration is expert that he does not touch the thin intestines; and even if at the time of tearing intestines fall between skin and flesh on their own — they came out and he did not turn over the thin intestines; and even if he inserts them by hand, presumably they know how to insert them so they are not turned over (as Mahari Weil and Hagahot Maimoniyot daf 164); a chicken whose intestines exited via the anus — treifah, for it is impossible they return to their place in order via a narrow opening (Maharshal in his Orach Chaim, brought in Hagahot Maimoniyot).
