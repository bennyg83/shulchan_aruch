One does not arrange death for them. In chapter Elu Hein (26b) R. Avahu taught — gentiles and shepherds of small livestock — one does not raise and does not lower; this "and does not lower" means there is a prohibition to lower them; and so in Talmud Yerushalmi chapter Four Deaths (Sanhedrin 56) they said there regarding Sheva ben Bichri with a gentile he is exempt; and Rashi there explains one does not save them from a pit from death and does not lower them to a pit to kill by hand; and so Tosafot ruled plainly in the first chapter of Avodah Zarah (10a) who challenged regarding Antoninus who killed his servants — but even with Israel it is forbidden because of lo moridin, etc.; and Beit Yosef wrote here that this "one does not lower" does not mean it is forbidden to lower them but to say there is no mitzvah to lower them, and such appears to him from Tosafot — end quote; and he omitted an entire passage in chapter Four Deaths that we mentioned; and what he wrote in the name of Tosafot is that he saw in Tosafot in chapter Ein Ma'amidin (Avodah Zarah 26) who wrote that even though they do not observe seven commandments nevertheless a verse permits them as written "and they shall be for you as wealth and they shall serve you" — end quote — meaning the verse permits that one is not obligated to kill them; and Beit Yosef understood from Tosafot citing to permit that also "one does not lower" is language of permission that one is not obligated to lower them — but it is not so; rather they say there is no Torah command to eradicate them and the Rabbis said there is a rabbinic prohibition; but if he did it he is certainly exempt as I brought from Four Deaths. Beit Yosef also brought proof that so appears from Rambam's words who wrote one does not arrange death for them and it is forbidden to save them as the Shulchan Aruch quoted here in his language — it appears he wrote so, for it would have implied to the Rav from his not citing at the beginning also the language "forbidden" — learn from this it is not a prohibition but that one is not commanded to surround them; and the truth is so — for initially Rambam wrote there that regarding apostates and informers there was a mitzvah to arrange death for them as Shulchan Aruch quoted at end of seif 2; and on this Rambam said regarding compelled gentiles there is no mitzvah to arrange thus for them unless he wishes — the authority is in his hand to surround. Nevertheless this is not the matter of what the Talmud said "one does not lower" — for whenever it is by hand there is prohibition to lower them; but mere surrounding such as removing the ladder mentioned in seif 2 — this is no prohibition here since one does not act by hand; and so Tur wrote explicitly; and nevertheless "one does not lower him to a pit by hand" is implied; but preventing rescue such as taking the ladder is certainly permitted. Maharshal wrote that included in this language "by hand" is also prohibition to take the ladder for it is like by hand, and he learns this from Rambam's language who wrote one does not arrange death for them and holds Rambam said language of prohibition — but he is not precise, for on the contrary we already proved Rambam said there is no mitzvah regarding gentiles as with apostates. And besides this it is impossible to say so, for even Israel with Israel who removes the ladder from him and he cannot ascend and dies there — the remover is exempt from death as Rambam wrote chapter 3 of Murder; and if this were included in killing by hand, Israel with Israel in this would be liable to death — rather it is plainly not so; and specifically actual by hand where Israel has liability to death the Rabbis forbade regarding a gentile; but arranging death such as taking the ladder and the like where Israel is exempt from court death under compulsion of a gentile — it is permitted lechatchilah as long as there is no issue of enmity as will be explained shortly — this seems clear to me. And furthermore they said in the Gemara chapter Elu Hein there — but the compelled and informers one lowers and does not raise; and we challenged — now we lower our sister; resolve the question; and why was it necessary to say "and does not raise" — Rav Sheshet said it was not needed except if she was raising in a pit that he could ascend — he drags it for he holds it above — meaning he can evade enmity and say it is so my animal does not descend into the pit — therefore I remove the hoist in the pit; and Rashi explained that which he said "and does not raise" means one does not let ascend; and apparently it is difficult what the answer fixed — still you can challenge since one lowers by hand, a fortiori one should not let him ascend; and we have no difficulty — for it teaches that one should not err to say that because of enmity one can raise him from the pit — namely where there is enmity from other gentiles toward this one he said there is no enmity here that he can evade and say for my animal's sake, etc.; and there are further resolutions in the Gemara each giving an excuse from enmity — see there; and this is the main novelty of the tanna. And as they said there before — Rav Yosef wanted to say that which gentiles one does not raise — if for payment it is permitted because of enmity; Abaye said to him he can say my son is on the roof, etc. — so too here. And Tur did not write regarding apostates and informers "one does not raise" even though the baraita said so — because the main novelty is to inform us there is no issue of enmity as I explained; and Tur already gave at the beginning a general rule that wherever there is enmity one acts to escape enmity and did not want to write what Abaye said that he can say my son is on the roof — for this is only one detail; and in truth there are many evasions anyway; and therefore he gave here a general rule that the matter depends on enmity and whatever he can evade he does and exempts himself; and therefore he also did not write at the end "one does not raise" unlike the baraita where no matter was mentioned at the beginning depending on enmity or evasion — therefore it was necessary to mention it at the end — this seems plain and clear, not like one who forced himself in explaining the Gemara's and Tur's words on this.
