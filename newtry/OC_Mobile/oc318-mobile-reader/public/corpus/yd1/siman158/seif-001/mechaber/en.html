Regarding idolaters — to save them from death; it contains 2 se’ifim. Idolaters from the 7 nations, with whom there is no war between us and them, and shepherds of small animals from among Jews in Eretz Yisrael at a time when most of the fields belonged to Jews, and those similar to them: one does not cause their death, and it is forbidden to save them if they are inclined to die — for example, if one saw one of them who fell into the sea, he does not raise him up, even if he will give him payment. Therefore, one may not heal them even for payment, unless where there is concern of enmity,
{Rama: for then, even for free it is permitted, if he cannot evade [treating him] even for free (Beit Yosef in the name of Ramban); and likewise, it was permitted to try a remedy on a Canaanite slave, if it will be effective (Tosafos and Mordechai, perek Ein Ma’amidin; and in Semag).} To what does this apply? To a Jew who is a ba’al aveiros, who persists in his wickedness and repeats it constantly — for example, shepherds of small animals, who became lawless with robbery and go in their folly. But a Jew who is a ba’al aveiros who does not persist in his wickedness constantly, but rather commits transgressions for his own benefit — for example, he eats neveilos out of appetite — it is a mitzvah to save him, and it is forbidden to stand over his blood.
