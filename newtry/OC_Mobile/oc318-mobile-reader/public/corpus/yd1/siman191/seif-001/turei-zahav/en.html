If she streams into the basin, etc. This is the language of the Tur. Many ask why regarding streaming he wrote “into the basin,” while regarding dripping he wrote “on the edge of the basin,” and they force answers. It appears to me that there is no difficulty at all. Certainly the essential distinction is between streaming and dripping. When she streams, since the urine comes out in a stream in its normal manner, she is not strained, and the urine does not return to the source; blood from the source does not normally come out with urine. But when she drips, since she holds herself back, it is possible that the urine returns to the source and brings blood out with it. However, streaming is possible only when she spreads her legs apart and places the basin between her legs; thus the urine with the blood descends into the basin, which is aligned there, and not onto the edge of the basin. But dripping while standing means that she places her legs close together, and then she cannot stream, only it drips gently from her, and she places the basin near between her legs. Therefore she drips onto the edge of the basin, for then the edge of the basin is aligned with the place where her urine exits. This appears clear to me.
But on the edge. Meaning while sitting; but while standing, even if it is inside the basin, she is tamei, for we require three favorable factors: sitting, streaming, and inside the basin.
She inserts a clean wad, etc. In Responsa Ri Weil siman 55 it is explained that a woman has two openings: one from which urine exits, which is below, close to the outside, and another from which niddah blood exits, which is above, much deeper within the body; end quote.
Particles, particles. My teacher and father-in-law, of blessed memory, wrote: it certainly appears that if she does not see actual blood after urinating, then although these particles that are found are actual blood, and when they are crushed between the fingers they dissolve and become blood, we are not concerned for them. They are no more severe than actual blood that exits with urine, which we attribute to a wound in the kidneys; all the more so these particles; end quote.
Question regarding a woman who has a veset like all women, and outside the time of her veset finds herself clean in examination from seeing blood, except that she finds red particles like the point of a needle. Some of these particles are absorbed into a linen cloth and some are not absorbed but are picked from the linen with a needle. When those particles are found they are not sharp like sand. Her practice at the time of her veset is that when the flow of blood stops from her in the normal way, she then finds something like water in which meat was soaked, and within it are the above particles, much more than otherwise. She says that many times she felt pain in the place of the kidneys, though for about nine weeks she has not felt that pain. Still, she constantly feels pain in her abdomen above that place, and it hurts her greatly, and it seems to her as though something is moving there back and forth. She finds those particles sometimes with the feeling of pain, and sometimes afterward as well, and this has been her practice for a long time. What is her law?
Answer. We find two permissions in this area. One is the nature of the particles themselves, which commonly come because of a wound in the kidneys, as copied in the Beit Yosef, siman 191, from the Ran’s responsum, even when she does not feel pain. The second is because of a wound, where she has a wound and feels pain, and this applies even with actual blood. First we will discuss the permission of the particles in themselves. The Beit Yosef copied the Ran’s responsum, which is effective to permit. Afterward he challenged it from the Mordechai in the second chapter of Shevuot, regarding a woman who finds particles in her urine, etc., where she is tamei. He answered with two answers. The first: the permission is only specifically with actual sand, but the Mordechai speaks of something like sand, not actual sand. He further answered that the Mordechai who forbids speaks where she afterward saw actual blood. Now, according to the first answer, there would be no permission here because of the particles, since there is no actual sand, and it would be proper to be stringent like it. But in truth, the Darkhei Moshe disagreed with the first answer, and so it appears to me, for it has no root or basis. It is written explicitly in the Mordechai in the second answer that the whole discussion there is whether the actual visible blood she later found forbids. According to this, it is proven that since he forbids there because of actual blood afterward, the particles she initially found do not forbid. This would have seemed to follow the Beit Yosef’s reasoning, since he raised these two matters against each other and was forced to answer. But in truth, there is great wonder at him, for there is no question here at all. The Mordechai does not speak at all about the prohibition or permission of the particles; even with actual blood there would be permission since it was found in her urine. He only comes to teach us that you should not say that since it is permitted within her urine, the same applies if blood is found afterward. This is not so; there is no permission regarding what she afterward found as actual blood. But the Ran speaks of the particles themselves, as is clear from his words, that they do not have the status of blood at all and are permitted even according to Rabbi Meir, who forbids where blood is found in her urine. Now, we might still say by reasoning that perhaps the particles of the woman before us are not similar in some respect to the Ran’s particles, and we should not learn permission from there. Nevertheless we still have permission from the feeling of pain. Since she felt internal pain in the place of the kidneys, these particles certainly come from there. Since she has a veset like all women and this is unusual, we certainly attribute it to a wound in the kidneys outside the time of her veset, as Piskei Maharai ruled in siman 47. Although for some weeks she has not felt that pain, nevertheless, since she has a wound, we attribute it to the wound even if the painful ache of that wound eased, for many wounds sometimes do not hurt although they have not fully healed. Even if it occurs to one to be stringent in this, nevertheless before us this woman constantly feels great pain above that place, and certainly we attribute it to this. Although at times the feeling of that pain pauses somewhat and returns, and she finds the particles at all times, nevertheless it is certainly all one matter, and it is an extract of what comes because of the pain, like what is written in the Ran’s responsum in the Beit Yosef in this siman regarding particles found in her urine. One should not say: the Rama brings two views in this siman regarding blood found at the time she urinates and also afterward, and writes that one should be stringent. This is not difficult at all. There he speaks without feeling pain, and the permission is only because of her urine; but where she feels pain he already wrote earlier that it is permitted even if found also after urinating. Although the Rama brought there that no permission helps her except through examination; and further, it is explicitly written in Hagahot Shaarei Dura: if she never sees except when her belly hurts below her navel, she is tahor, for a woman is believed to say, etc.; and so the Beit Yosef wrote at the end of this siman in the name of the Agur, that what she sees sometimes without pain is forbidden. None of this is difficult. All of that is where she feels pain only at the time of urinating, but without urinating she has no pain at all; therefore she has no permission when not in pain, for then she is like other women. This is unlike our case, where it is clear to her that the pain is at every time and every hour. We certainly say that it is always because of a wound. Although sometimes she does not feel pain for the moment, nevertheless she is not healed, for it always returns afterward, and consequently everything comes because of the pain. This is so even with actual blood; all the more here, where we may say that since she finds only particles, and they commonly arise from the kidneys, and this woman many times felt great pain in the place of the kidneys, we may say that these particles too are like the Ran’s particles. She has a veset like other women, and immediately when she stops seeing blood these particles come; this shows that they are a different matter.
