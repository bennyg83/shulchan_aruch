Only for a woman who has a veset, etc. Maharil’s language there: all this is for women who have vestot, and not during her veset time; but this one I wrote about who has no veset — one should then be stringent, for if not, would she never be tamei, as the Sages said. However if she has an onah like other women’s pattern — even if her veset is not fixed, such as if she advanced or delayed three or four days, or a week or less or more — only that she has a fixed onah she is accustomed to waiting at minimum from the end of a sighting such-and-such days; and in my humble opinion to rely on the repair above — end quote. And difficult: if this examination is considered an examination — if so even when she has no veset; and not comparable to what the Sages said above siman 187 seif 5 regarding a wound that if not so would she never be tamei — there it is without examination, but here she would be tamei when blood is found on the wad. And it seems Maharil was doubtful whether this examination is considered a proper examination, and says that for this woman who has no veset one should be stringent; and yet difficult — what he says, would she never be tamei — behold she will be tamei when she sees blood not during her needs. It appears he deals with one who never sees blood except during her needs; and so implied in the question there siman 203 and in Agur brought by Beit Yosef who wrote she had no fixed veset but each time she saw she saw with signs such as sneezing and sensing in her abdomen; and now this woman deteriorated such that when she sat on the vessel, etc. — it implies she no longer sees except when sitting on the vessel for her needs; and accordingly one explains what Rav wrote — not to permit except for a woman who has a veset — meaning she sees even when not doing her needs, even if her veset is not fixed — nonetheless she sometimes sees when not during her needs, and therefore between one onah and the next she is pure, as Maharil wrote. And if an adversary wishes to dispute this explanation, tell him Mahariv in responsum siman 25 relies straightforwardly on this examination and considers it a proper examination and did not mention distinguishing between having a veset or not (and Rav also wrote it appears one should permit in any case, etc., but there are...).
