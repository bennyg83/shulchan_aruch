And some say that even, etc. In my humble opinion this latter view, that of Rabbeinu Chananel and those who follow him, is supported by the Bavli and Jerusalem Talmud. In Niddah 14b, R' Yose regarding an unchecked cloth appears to render her impure because of a stain, and the Jerusalem Talmud states this explicitly. The Bavli compares an unchecked cloth to a woman urinating. Accordingly, the statement that R' Yose permits even l'chatchila with respect to pure foods applies only to the latter clause, not the first; in the first he agrees she is forbidden l'chatchila for pure foods and likewise to her husband. Tosafos, Mordechai, Agudah, Hagahos Shaarei Dura, and Rashi's wording support this. Rashba concedes that the plain sugya supports Rabbeinu Chananel but later disagrees because of his own interpretation. The Beit Yosef's distinction between pure foods and her husband is forced. Once R' Yose agrees to the l'chatchila prohibition for pure foods, the same applies to her husband. This also resolves the Ran's question how Shmuel and R' Abba can rule like R' Yose while discussing R' Meir's view: they follow R' Yose after the fact but his l'chatchila law remains like R' Meir's. The later authorities and the Rama likewise indicate that we should not reject Rabbeinu Chananel merely as a stringency.
Only while streaming. Not where she urinates while sitting and the blood is found only on the rim of the basin. In the streaming case, if the blood had emerged after the urine ceased, it should have been found on the rim; if blood is also found on the rim she is impure, because we can say it came after urination stopped. This follows the Gemara and the Ran according to R' Meir, and R' Yose agrees l'chatchila for pure foods and likewise regarding her husband, as explained above. If she sits in the middle of the basin, however, she is impure. The Rama stated the rule briefly.

Only for a woman who has a veset, etc. Maharil’s language there: all this is for women who have vestot, and not during her veset time; but this one I wrote about who has no veset — one should then be stringent, for if not, would she never be tamei, as the Sages said. However if she has an onah like other women’s pattern — even if her veset is not fixed, such as if she advanced or delayed three or four days, or a week or less or more — only that she has a fixed onah she is accustomed to waiting at minimum from the end of a sighting such-and-such days; and in my humble opinion to rely on the repair above — end quote. And difficult: if this examination is considered an examination — if so even when she has no veset; and not comparable to what the Sages said above siman 187 seif 5 regarding a wound that if not so would she never be tamei — there it is without examination, but here she would be tamei when blood is found on the wad. And it seems Maharil was doubtful whether this examination is considered a proper examination, and says that for this woman who has no veset one should be stringent; and yet difficult — what he says, would she never be tamei — behold she will be tamei when she sees blood not during her needs. It appears he deals with one who never sees blood except during her needs; and so implied in the question there siman 203 and in Agur brought by Beit Yosef who wrote she had no fixed veset but each time she saw she saw with signs such as sneezing and sensing in her abdomen; and now this woman deteriorated such that when she sat on the vessel, etc. — it implies she no longer sees except when sitting on the vessel for her needs; and accordingly one explains what Rav wrote — not to permit except for a woman who has a veset — meaning she sees even when not doing her needs, even if her veset is not fixed — nonetheless she sometimes sees when not during her needs, and therefore between one onah and the next she is pure, as Maharil wrote. And if an adversary wishes to dispute this explanation, tell him Mahariv in responsum siman 25 relies straightforwardly on this examination and considers it a proper examination and did not mention distinguishing between having a veset or not (and Rav also wrote it appears one should permit in any case, etc., but there are...).
Some say she is impure, because they permitted only, etc. It is difficult to understand why this law needed to be stated at all, since the Mechaber already limited the permission to a woman sitting and urinating in a stream. The Beit Yosef stated this according to R' Yose, who permits while standing. Nor can it mean that even while sitting and streaming she becomes impure if blood is found on the cloth, for that is obvious: even blood on both the rim and within the basin makes her impure, all the more so blood on the cloth. Likewise the Rama's phrase 'one should be stringent' is unnecessary according to what I explained above, for Rabbeinu Chananel's interpretation is the primary law.
