Standing. Whether she gushes or drips. Shach.
The rim. All the more so if she drips into the basin. Perishah and Bach. (Many ask why he wrote, regarding gushing, "into the basin," and regarding dripping, "on the rim of the basin"; see Taz.)
Manner. For we require three factors for leniency: sitting, gushing, and into the basin. Taz.
She is tahor. Because it is a double doubt: perhaps it is from the man; and if you say it is from the woman, perhaps it is not from the source. It implies that this is where it was found inside the basin upon the water, as stated in the Talmud; end quote, Shach.
Examination. The Shach wrote that one may permit this examination for a woman who has a veset, even if her veset is not fixed, but she sometimes sees when not attending to her needs; and he brought proofs for his words, see there.
To be stringent. The Shach wrote that this does not belong here as a stringency; rather, this is the root of the law. It is also difficult why Rama wrote this law at all, for he already wrote above that one may permit only when she is sitting and gushing; end quote.
Granules. The Shach wrote: but if she also found blood, she is tamei. With granules alone, she need not place them in water to see whether they dissolve. So too the Bach wrote that these granules, even when one dissolves them between the fingers and they dissolve and become blood, we are not concerned for them, for they are no more stringent than actual blood that exits with urine, which we attribute to a wound in the kidneys; all the more so these granules; end quote.
