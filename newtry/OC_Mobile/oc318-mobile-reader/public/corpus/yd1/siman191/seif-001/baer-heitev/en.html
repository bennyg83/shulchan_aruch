Pure. For the sake of the man and the Hashem of the woman, who is not of the source and is heard, that is, if he is in the cup of water, it is worthwhile in the mouth:
An examination. Shach writes that this examination may be permitted for a woman who has a veset, even if her veset is not fixed, provided that she sometimes sees blood at times other than urination. He brings proofs for this view; see there.
To be stringent. Shach writes that this is not merely a stringency; rather, it is the essential law. He also asks why Rama needed to state this ruling at all, since he already wrote above that there is no basis to permit except where she is seated and the urine flows freely.
Granules. Shach writes that if she also finds blood, she is impure. If she finds only granules, she need not place them in water to determine whether they dissolve. Bach likewise writes that these granules are disregarded even if, when rubbed between the fingers, they dissolve and become blood, because they are no more stringent than actual blood that emerges together with urine, which we attribute to a wound in the kidneys. All the more so these granules.
