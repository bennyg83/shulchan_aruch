And some say that one may permit only when she is sitting, etc. This is the view of the Tur and the Rosh, that even R. Yosei disagrees with R. Meir only when she is spurting, where there is one favorable factor; but when she is standing and dripping, which is two factors to the worse, R. Yosei concedes that she is impure. Bach challenged: if so, what does the Gemara ask, now that with one doubt we purify, with two doubts is it necessary; behold with two doubts she is pure even when standing and dripping. This is no difficulty, for the Gemara asked this question only after we conclude that with two doubts even R. Meir purifies. Necessarily, these two doubts are more than one favorable side, for with one favorable side R. Meir declares impure and requires two favorable sides; nevertheless he purifies with two doubts. We learn that since there is here a doubt that perhaps it did not come from the man at all, it is considered like two favorable factors, and R. Meir purifies. If so, the Gemara afterward properly asks: now, with one doubt, where there is only one favorable factor, R. Yosei validates; all the more with two doubts, which must be two favorable factors, for R. Meir purifies there, it is obvious that R. Yosei purifies. This is simple and no difficulty. But if it was found in a basin into which a man and woman, etc., she is pure in every case. Shach wrote in s.k. 6 that this means when it was found inside the basin. If he intended a sitting woman, his words are invalid from the outset, for when standing there is one factor to the worse and it is permitted with a double doubt; the same applies when sitting and dripping, which is one factor to the worse and is permitted with a double doubt. If he intended that when standing she must at least be pouring in a stream, which is one factor to the worse, in this his words are not refuted, but nevertheless he has no proof. His language indicates that he derives it from the Mishnah teaching "on the water," but this is not so, for it also speaks of sitting; therefore his words have no root. Only with a woman who has a veset. See Shach, who elaborated in s.k. 7 why specifically a veset is needed. It is difficult: the concern that she will never become impure does not apply, since she will become impure when she sees not at the time of her needs. Therefore he explains that Mahari"l also discusses a woman who did not see her veset except through her urine, and therefore he required a veset, end quote. But my heart does not imagine so. If this woman sees her veset blood only through her urine, and it is clear before us that this woman sees uterine blood in her urine, how did Mahari"l in the responsum permit even where she has no fixed veset, only that her usual way is always not to precede, for example from twenty days to twenty days, though sometimes she sees on the twenty-first or twenty-third and the like? This is difficult: she has no veset about which we can say there is a presumption that the flow comes in its time. If, after an interval of ten days, she saw in her urine, from where is there permission to purify her, since it is clear to us that she sees blood from the uterus in her urine? Why should we purify a sighting after ten days' interval in urine, and make impure a sighting after twenty days in urine? This requires very great study, to purify a woman when it is clear to us that blood comes from her uterus in her urine, since she sees only in this manner and has no fixed veset, merely because her way is not to precede; if once or twice she preceded, should she be pure? Further, what was all the alarm that Mahari"l raised to purify this woman, since her way is to see in urine only at a limited time, and at a limited time even with a moch examination she is impure? And if it was because she once changed by chance, over this he raised such alarm? Behold she always saw only at a limited time. And if she always saw not at a limited time, how did Mahari"l write that she sees from twenty to twenty? If the meaning is that she sees from twenty to twenty with an ordinary sighting, why specifically from twenty to twenty? In any case, since she saw not in urine, Shach's question stands. Therefore truth shows its way: it deals with seeing from twenty to twenty in the plain sense, and Shach's words, that she sees only in urine, are not so; and the words of Mahari"l in the responsum indicate not like his words. Regarding the difficulty, it appears that when Maharil wrote, "will she never be impure," his intent is not about the entire life of the woman, that she will never be impure all her days, for obviously she can become impure when she sees not in urine, as Shach asked. Rather, his intent is that whenever she sees in urine, this sighting will always be pure, and this cannot be, since this woman has no veset such that we can say there is a presumption that the flow comes in its time. Rather, she sees sometimes at this hour and sometimes at that hour; if so, how can it be avoided that once she will see after urinating? Who prevents it? She has no limited time, and on the contrary, the heat of urine is liable to bring blood, as the authors wrote. If so we would purify a woman while in truth she saw actual blood; for there is no doubt that over many days and years it will occur that once she sees her veset adjacent to urine. This is his intent, and it is correct. Therefore, if she at least has a limited time for sighting from twenty to twenty, even if it is not like a fixed veset, we have somewhat escaped this doubt; during these days we say it will not occur that she sees uterine blood in urine, for she is not prone to see in these days. But when she has no limit, who can examine whether perhaps the sighting was adjacent to urine? This is settled. One who examines Mahari"l will see that my words are correct and clear. See Beit Ephraim. And if she found blood in her urine and also on the ed, some say she is impure, etc. Shach in s.k. 8 wrote that this is unnecessary, since he already ruled above to be stringent except when she pours in a stream. Besides that one can reject this: above we say, if it were so that it came from urine, why was it found on the rim and not inside the basin? But with an examination one can say remnants of drops of blood from the urine remained stuck to the sides in the prozdor, and by the examination with the ed they were found; here there is no flaw at all. It also appears that this view holds that if it was found on the ed, we do not attribute it to urine at all; we attribute to urine only when she sees, but when it is found on the ed there is no relation at all to urine. If so, when she examined with an unchecked ed, it has the same law as above in siman 190, see there. But if we had said that this is called seeing in urine and seeing on an ed, only forbidden like when found on the rim of the basin as Shach wrote, then this impurity would be due to doubt, perhaps the urine returned to the uterus as above; and with an unchecked ed there would be a double doubt: perhaps it came from elsewhere, and if you say from the examination, perhaps from the urine. Therefore he teaches us that regarding examination we do not attribute at all to urine, and the law returns to above, siman 190, that even an unchecked ed renders impure. This is settled. Only in small granules like sand, etc. See Taz s.k. 4, who elaborated, and the end of his words is that even when earth-like grit and granules are found not at the time of urination, the law stated by Rama applies. The Gaon Chacham Tzvi in his responsa challenged him, that Ran and Rama speak only after urination, but not otherwise. The Gaon Shev Yaakov stood to the right of Taz, saying that since Ran's reason is that it is uncommon to see granules, then even not at the time of urination it is also uncommon. These words are not so, for it necessarily speaks when they dissolved, as Ba'er Heitev and Darkei Moshe wrote, and so Shach wrote in s.k. 11, see there. If so, the Gemara passage in HaMapelet is difficult for me: regarding one who miscarries something like gnats or barley grains, the Sages said she should cast them into water; if they dissolved she is impure. Is it the way of a woman to see things like gnats and the like? What difference is there between gnats and granules? Ran speaks even when she feels no pain, as Taz himself wrote. Rather, it is clear that Heaven forbid that we permit if they dissolved, for they are actual blood. Only with urine there is already a basis for permission, for she sees in urine, and R. Yosei purifies entirely even with every factor to the worse; only R. Meir renders impure, as Ran wrote, because he is concerned for a minority, perhaps the urine returned to the uterus and brought blood. Ran holds that we say it brought blood, but we do not say it brought granules, because it is more common to attribute granules to urine, which is a product of the kidneys and from there urine is extracted and comes, as is known, than to attribute them to the uterus, which does not commonly produce granules. But with a sighting not in urine, where there is no reason to attribute them to urine at all, and all examinations are presumed to be from the uterus, from where would we attribute them to urine? What place does urine have here? We attribute them to the uterus, and since they dissolved, dissolved blood is impure. Especially in the incident of Taz, where she felt pain above the uterus; if so, what place do the kidneys have here, as they are in the back near the end of the spine? It is clearer that blood comes from the uterus. The Gaon Shev Yaakov sought to say that there is a distinction between granules and the case of seeing something like sand; this is not so because it is not from a wound in the kidneys. What will he answer regarding the case of a woman who miscarried something like shells, where physicians testified that the woman had a wound in her intestines, and nevertheless if they dissolved she is impure? See what I wrote above in siman 188. I wonder at that sage: with subtle distinctions that have no root in the Gemara or earlier poskim, and with reasoning of "who can say" and "perhaps this differs from sand," he sought to permit such a severe prohibition, especially with an examination, which is a Torah prohibition. Were he saying this to be stringent we would not listen to such subtle distinctions; how much more so to be lenient. With forgiveness for his great honor, what he wrote about the Gaon Chacham Tzvi, that his words are merely extra words, was not fitting to write; for his words in this matter are clear. Heaven forbid to permit granules without urine. If one wishes to rely on physicians, that is not relevant here; see what I wrote above in siman 188. But without that, Heaven forbid to permit granules, as I wrote, for what difference is there between granules and shells and the like? Therefore one should not permit, as I have written.
