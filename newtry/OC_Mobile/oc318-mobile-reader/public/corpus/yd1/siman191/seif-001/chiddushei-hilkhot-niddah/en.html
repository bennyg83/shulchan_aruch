And some say one may not permit except when sitting, etc. This is the view of Tur and Rosh — even R. Yosi does not disagree with R. Meir except regarding a gushing stream, where there is one factor toward purity; but when dripping and standing, which is two factors toward impurity, R. Yosi agrees she is tamei. And Bach challenged: if so, why does the Gemara ask — now with one doubt we declare pure, with two doubts it should require? But we do not ask this question because we conclude that with two doubts even R. Meir declares pure; and it is clear this double doubt is more than one factor toward purity, for with one factor toward purity R. Meir declares tamei and requires two factors toward purity; nevertheless he declares pure with two doubts — learn that since there is a doubt perhaps it did not come from the man at all, it is considered two factors toward purity and R. Meir declares pure; if so the Gemara asks properly afterward — now with one doubt where there is only one factor toward purity R. Yosi permits, all the more with two doubts which are certainly two factors toward purity, for R. Meir declares pure — plainly R. Yosi declares pure; and this is clear and simple.
But if found in a vessel where a man and woman urinate, etc. — tehorah in every case. Shach wrote in s.k. 6 that this means when found inside the vessel; and if she intended regarding sitting, his words from the outset do not apply, for standing has one factor toward impurity and is permitted in a double doubt; likewise sitting and dripping has one factor toward impurity and is permitted in a double doubt; and if she intended standing, in any case it is a shower which has one factor toward impurity — in this his words are not scattered; but nevertheless he has no proof, and his language implies he inferred from the mishnah that teaches "on the water" — but this is not so, for even regarding sitting it deals; therefore his words have no root.
Only regarding a woman who has a veset — see Shach who elaborated in s.k. 7 why specifically a veset is required; and it is difficult — will she never become tamei? This does not apply, since she becomes tamei from seeing not at her time. Therefore he explains that Mahari"l also deals with a woman who did not see a veset except through urine, and therefore requires a veset — end quote. But in my heart it does not seem so, for if this woman does not see blood and her veset is only through urine, it is clear to us this woman sees blood from the makor in urine — if so how did Mahari"l permit in his responsum even without a fixed veset, only that her way is always without advancing from 20 to 30 days, even if sometimes she sees only on the 21st or 28th and the like — see there. And it is difficult — but she has no veset, shall we say in her a chazakah "its way comes in its time"? If so, if after the interval of 10 days she saw in urine, from where is permission to declare tehorah, since it is clear to us she sees blood from the makor within urine — and why is the sight after 10 days of the interval in urine tehorah, and the sight after 20 days in urine tamei? This matter is astounding — to declare tehorah a woman when it is clear to us blood from her makor comes in urine, for she sees only on the 28th and has no fixed veset, only that her way is without advancing — if once or twice she advanced she would be tehorah. And further — why did Mahari"l tremble so much regarding this woman to declare tehorah, since her way is not to see in urine except at a limited time, and at a limited time even in bedikah with cotton she is tamei; and if because once it changed in a case on this he trembled — all trembling? She always saw only at a limited time; and if she always saw not at a limited time, how did Mahari"l write she sees from 20 to 30? And if the simple meaning is she sees from 20 to 30 in a simple sight — why mention from 20 to 30? Even without this, since she saw not in urine, Shach's question stands in its place. Therefore truth guides his way — he deals where she saw from 20 to 30 literally; and Shach's words that she sees only in urine do not apply; and so they rule Mahari"l's words in his responsum not like his words. And in the question it appears — when Maharal wrote "will she never be tamei," his intent is not on her entire life that she will never be tamei all her days — plainly she can be tamei from seeing not in urine, as Shach's question — rather his intent is forever regarding seeing in urine this sight will forever be tehorah; and this is impossible, since she has no veset until we say in her chazakah "its way comes in its time", etc. — she sees sometimes at this hour and sometimes at that hour; if so, will she not escape that she sees P.A. after urine? Who withholds? She has no limited time; and rather heat of urine is likely to bring blood as authors wrote; if so we declare tehorah a woman when she truly saw complete blood, for there is no doubt in many days and years that it will happen she sees P.A. and her veset is adjacent to urine. And this is his intent and it is correct; therefore if she has at least a limited time for sight from 20 to 30, even though it is not the fixed veset, nevertheless we escape somewhat this doubt within these days — we say she will not happen to see blood from the makor in urine, for she is not likely to see in these days; but without a limit, who will test? Perhaps the sight is adjacent to urine — and the examiner in Mahari"l will see these words are correct and clear. A.C.
And if she found blood within urine and also on the ed, etc. — some say she is tamei, etc. Shach in s.k. 8 wrote this is without need, since he already ruled above to be stringent except in a shower — see there. And besides one can reject — above we say if it were from urine why was it found on the rim and not inside the vessel; not so in bedikah, for one can say remnants of a blood drop from urine remained stuck on the sides in the foreskin, and in bedikah with ed they were found — and there is no impurity factor at all. It also appears the view of this one — if found on the ed we do not attribute at all to urine, for we attribute to urine only in seeing; but when found on the ed there is no relevance to urine at all; if so, when she checked with an ed that was not examined, behold this is like above in siman 190 — see there. Not so if we say this means she saw in urine and saw on the ed — only forbidden like when found on the rim of the vessel as Shach wrote; if so this impurity is from doubt lest urine return to the makor as stated; and with an ed that was not examined, behold here is a double doubt lest from elsewhere; and if you say from bedikah lest from urine — therefore comes to teach regarding bedikah we do not attribute at all to urine; if so the law returns to above in siman 190 that even an ed that was not examined is tamei — and so it is.
Only grains like sand, etc. — see Taz s.k. 4 who elaborated; and end of his words that even when found gravel and grains not at the time of urinating — if so the law stated in Rama applies; and Chakham Tzvi in his responsum challenged that Ran and Rama deal only after urine, but otherwise not; and Gaon Shabbetai Yaakov stood on the right of Taz that Ran's reason is they are not common to see in grains, and if so even not at time of urine also not common — and these words do not apply, for it necessarily deals when they dissolved as Be'er Heitev wrote and Darkei Moshe; and so Shach s.k. 11 — see there. If so I have difficulty with this sugya of the Gemara regarding miscarriage like cumin seeds and barley that sages said she should put in water — if dissolved tamei; and is a woman's way to see like cumin seeds and the like? And what is "cumin" and what is "grains"? Ran deals even when she does not feel pain like a blow as Taz himself wrote — rather Heaven forbid for us to permit, for if they dissolved it is complete blood, only in urine; otherwise there is permission here, for she sees in urine and R. Yosi declares completely pure even with all impurity factors — only R. Meir declares tamei as Ran wrote, for he is concerned for a minority lest urine return to the makor; and Ran's proof — if urine brings blood we say; but grains we do not say, for it is more common to attribute grains to urine which is a product of kidneys and from there urine is squeezed; and when known we attribute to the makor, for it is not common to produce grains. But in seeing not in urine there is no reason to attribute to urine at all, and all bedikot are presumably from the makor — from where to attribute to urine? And what relevance does urine have here? We attribute to the makor; and since they dissolved, blood that dissolved is tamei; and especially in the case of Taz where she felt pain like a blow from above from the makor. And Shabbetai Yaakov sought to say there is a distinction between grains and her who saw like sand — this is not from a blow in the kidneys; and what will answer regarding the woman who miscarried like peels and doctors testified she has a blow in her intestines — nevertheless if dissolved tamei; and see what I wrote siman 188. And I wonder at this sage who with a thin reasoning that has no root in Talmud and earlier poskim, and who will say — and perhaps this differs from sand, for he sought to permit such a serious issur, especially in bedikah which is a Torah prohibition; and if said to be stringent they would not listen to such thin reasonings, and plainly to be lenient; and in pardoning his honor Rama wrote on Gaon Chakham Tzvi that his words are overstated language and not fitting to write so, for his words here are clear; and Heaven forbid to permit grains without urine; and if to rely on doctors — this matter has no relevance here; and see what I wrote siman 188. But otherwise Heaven forbid to permit grains as stated — what is "grains" and what is "peels" and the like; therefore one may not permit as stated.
