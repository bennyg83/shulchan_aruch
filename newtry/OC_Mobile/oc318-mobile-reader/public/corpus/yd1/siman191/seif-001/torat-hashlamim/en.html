But if they drip onto the edge of the basin. See the Taz, who wrote the reason why the Tur and our teacher the Rama wrote “edge of the basin,” and it is correct. The Shach wrote: “onto the edge of the basin, and all the more so if she drips inside the basin. Perishah and Bach”; end quote. What he wrote in the name of the Perishah is certainly not so; examine it. The Bach too, who wrote this, did so specifically on the language of the Tur, namely where she had streamed and after the stream she dripped onto the edge of the basin. Regarding this he wrote that it is an a fortiori if she dripped from the start inside the basin. But our teacher the Rama wrote that blood was found there, implying that it was found on the edge of the basin. If so, the a fortiori does not apply. On the contrary, there is more reason to declare her tamei when it is inside the basin than on the edge of the basin, as is explicit in the Talmud and poskim.
Only when streaming. Meaning sitting on the edge of the basin alone. But if it is found on the edge of the basin, or she is sitting in the middle of the basin, she is tamei, as explained in the words of the poskim, namely the view of the “some say.” So wrote the Shach.
She is tahor. The Shach wrote: it implies that this means where it was found inside the basin on the water, as stated in the Talmud; end quote. His intent is from the Mishnah’s wording, “and blood was found on the water.” But in truth this is not so necessary, for perhaps the same applies where it was found on the edge of the basin; the Mishnah spoke in the usual case. Since there is another doubt, perhaps it is from the man, and even if it is from the man it can be found on the edge of the basin, for sometimes he does not urinate so far, as is implied at the beginning of Perek Kol HaYad. Therefore even on the edge of the basin she is tahor, for several great poskim hold that even when she urinates alone she is tahor in every case, like the Mechaber’s words. If so, where one can attribute it to the man, perhaps according to all she is tahor even if it is found on the edge of the basin. One should not ask: if so, what does the Talmud ask, “Does Rabbi Yose declare tahor with one doubt in the first clause, so with a sefek sefeka should it be necessary?” What is the question? According to the Ran and his supporters, Rabbi Yose declares tahor in the first clause only where it is found inside the basin, while in the latter clause she is tahor even on the edge of the basin. One may answer that the questioner holds that if this is why the Mishnah taught an extra case, then why did the Tanna not explicitly teach the essential distinction between the first clause and the latter clause? This is clear. In this manner it appears to me to also resolve what the Bach asked according to the Rosh and Tosafot in seif 2 and left requiring study; according to what I wrote, it is resolved.
Only with a woman who has a veset. Even if she does not have a fixed veset, but her way is sometimes to see not at the time of her needs, as opposed to one who sees only in her urine. So the Shach explained the master’s words. With this, what the Taz asks in siman 187 s.k. 10, that the master’s words contradict each other, is resolved.
