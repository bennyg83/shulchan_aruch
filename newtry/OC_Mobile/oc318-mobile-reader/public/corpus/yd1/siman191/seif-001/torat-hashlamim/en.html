But if it trickles onto the rim of the basin. See Taz, who explains why Tur and Rama specifically wrote "the rim of the basin," and his explanation is correct. Shach writes: "On the rim of the basin, and all the more so if it trickles into the basin. Derishah and Bach." What he attributes to Derishah is certainly incorrect; examine Derishah itself. Bach says this only with respect to Tur's wording, where the woman first urinated in a stream and afterward the flow became a trickle upon the rim of the basin. In that case, Bach says it is all the more lenient if the trickling occurred from the outset into the basin itself. Rama, however, says that blood was found there, which implies it was found on the rim of the basin. In that case no such kal vachomer applies. On the contrary, finding blood inside the basin presents greater grounds for impurity than finding it on the rim, as is explicit in the Gemara and poskim.

She is pure. Shach writes that this implies specifically where the blood is found inside the basin upon the water, as stated in the Gemara. His inference is from the Mishnah's phrase, "and blood was found upon the water." In truth, this is not entirely compelling. The same law may apply when blood is found on the rim of the basin. Since there is an additional doubt that perhaps the blood came from the man, and even blood originating from him can sometimes be found on the rim because a man does not always urinate a great distance, she may remain pure even there. Several major poskim hold that even when the woman urinates alone she is pure in all circumstances, as Mechaber rules. Thus, where there is also a possibility that the blood came from the man, perhaps everyone would agree she is pure even when it is found on the rim. One should not object from the Gemara's question: if R. Yose is lenient in the first clause with only one doubt, surely in the latter clause, with a double doubt, there was no need to teach the law. According to Ran and those who agree with him, R. Yose is lenient in the first clause only where the blood is found inside the basin, while in the latter clause she may be pure even if it is on the rim. One can answer that the questioner held that if this distinction were the reason for the extra clause, the Mishnah should have expressly stated the principal distinction between the first and latter clauses. In this manner one can also resolve Bach's question on Rosh and Tosafot in seif 2, which he leaves requiring study.
