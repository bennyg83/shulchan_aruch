(Siman 314, in Taz seif katan 4) Meaning that Rambam requires him to say 'a particular Jew' in order to answer Beit Yosef's difficulty, and Taz strained Tur's view. His words are incorrect. On the contrary, Rambam holds that he need not name a particular Jew, and even when we do not know whether that Jew exists, the non-Jew is afraid lest they ask the Jew, or lest they ask some Jew. Tur likewise understood Rambam this way, yet wrote in Rambam's name that witnesses to the blemish are required. For according to Rambam, even though he does not name the sage, it is as though he named him, and nevertheless witnesses are required. The same therefore applies according to Tur where he does name him. Proof is that Tur also writes in Rashi's name that witnesses are required. Would one therefore say that Rashi held he need not name the sage? Rather, the explanation is as I have stated. Beit Yosef's original question is no question, as I wrote in Shach seif katan 8; see there.
