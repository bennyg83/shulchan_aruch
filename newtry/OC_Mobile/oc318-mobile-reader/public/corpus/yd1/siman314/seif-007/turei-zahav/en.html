I showed that blemish. This section follows the language of the Rambam, and it is stated in the Mishnah: a kohen is believed to say “I showed this firstborn to an expert and it has a blemish.” Rav Yehudah said: a kohen is believed to say “this firstborn was given to me by an Israelite already blemished,” etc. — until: Rav Shizva objects: one who says to someone not trusted regarding tithes “buy for me from one who is trusted” — he is not trusted. Why? Let us say: anything that is likely to be revealed, etc. There it is different, since he could deflect [suspicion] and say “Had I known you did not trust me I would have considered him trustworthy” — therefore he is not afraid from the outset. As for the latter clause, it certainly supports Rav Yehudah: “from so-and-so” — he is believed, since by mentioning the name of the reliable sender, the agent is believed, for he fears the sender may demand [an accounting] and he would be found to have lied. This supports Rav Yehudah, and we reject it — there it is different since there is a claimant and he is afraid: meaning the agent is afraid, since he sees that the sender is particular about mentioning the man’s name and thinks surely he will call him to account. But regarding the firstborn the kohen is not afraid, thinking who would bother to search out my words and ask that same Israelite whether he gave it to me blemished. The Rosh wrote that in the Mishnah and similarly in Rav Yehudah’s ruling, it does not help unless the name of the expert authority is also mentioned — otherwise he would not be afraid. The Beit Yosef wrote that in the Rosh’s view, even though the support for Rav Yehudah from the Mishnah about tithes was rejected, his ruling itself was not rejected since you cannot refute it from it — yet we only trust him in the same manner we trust in the Mishnah about tithes, requiring him to mention the man’s name. The Rambam holds that the kohen need not mention the name of the expert, following the plain sense of the language, and that the case of tithes is different since it is a Torah prohibition and we do not rely on “he would not lie” to be lenient. Furthermore, from the Tur as well it appears he does not hold like the Rambam’s view, for first he writes like the Rosh that the name of the expert must be specified, and then writes: “Some say: when he says ‘expert so-and-so,’ he is believed even without witnesses attesting to the blemish, that it fell by itself.” But Rashi explains: only when there are witnesses regarding the blemish. So too writes the Rambam. This is very difficult — it seems from his words that even according to the Rambam we require the mention of the expert’s name, and even then we require witnesses. This cannot be — for the Rambam does not require the mention of the expert’s name, as the Beit Yosef wrote, and that is why witnesses are specifically required. But when one mentions the expert’s name, since he is then believed due to his fear of being held accountable — then witnesses are not required, since presumably he would not have permitted it unless there were witnesses present. This is what the Rambam writes in chapter 2 of Bekhorot: “A firstborn that was in a kohen’s possession and a blemish appeared on it, and one witness testified that this blemish appeared by itself — and we do not slaughter based on this blemish unless the kohen who had it comes and says: ‘I showed this blemish to an expert and he permitted it for slaughter’ — he is believed, and we do not suspect he might not have shown it, since he would not suspect himself of slaughtering consecrated animals outside [the Temple], which is a sin punishable by karet. Similarly a kohen is believed to say regarding a blemished firstborn: ‘this firstborn was given to me by an Israelite already blemished and it did not become blemished in my possession’ — and we do not suspect he may have inflicted the blemish himself, since the matter is likely to become known and he fears its owners may be asked and say it was unblemished when they gave it to him.” End quote. Clearly in the latter clause he gives the reason of fear, and in the first clause he gives the reason of the severity of the transgression. Therefore in the first clause he speaks of a case where he did not mention the name of the expert — and if so, the Tur’s question stands: where does he find grounds for writing, according to the Rambam’s view, that the name of the expert must be specified and also testimony that he did not inflict it himself? Perhaps in that case alone testimony is not required, and likewise for the “some say” cited by the Tur, testimony was only required where fear does not apply — such as when the name of the expert was not mentioned. And on the Rambam himself it is difficult: why in the latter clause did he not mention that the name of the Israelite must be cited, as the Tur writes — without that, fear does not apply? It appears that the Rambam speaks in the latter clause of a case where it is known to us who the Israelite is who gave the firstborn to the kohen, and only the question is whether he gave it unblemished or already blemished. In that case the fear is well-founded — the owners may indeed be asked. This fits well with what he writes in the latter clause: “the owners may be asked and say it was unblemished” — implying that this alone is in doubt, but we do not doubt who the giver is. Whereas the Tur speaks of a case where the doubt is who gave it — hence fear does not apply from that angle, and therefore the Israelite’s name must be mentioned. According to this, the Rambam deals with two separate matters: first he informs us that there is a permission based on the severity of the sin — and there the expert’s name need not be mentioned and testimony regarding the blemish falling by itself is required, since in this case he is suspected; this is the explanation of the Mishnah. Then he writes the law of the permission based on fear, where he mentions the name of the Israelite who gave it to him, as I wrote. Thus the difficulty on the Rambam is: since the main novelty in the latter clause is to inform us of the law based on fear, and mentioning the name should have been stated — he should have written in the latter clause: “and similarly if he also mentions the name of the expert, he is believed and no testimony is needed regarding the blemish falling by itself,” etc. Rather it must be that in the first clause the mention of the expert’s name does not help to exempt him from testimony — since it is possible the expert did not fully permit it but only said “this blemish is a fixed blemish for slaughter after you bring testimony that it fell by itself.” Whereas in the latter clause the reason of fear does properly exempt from testimony, since he gave it to him — it is accordingly proven that the Rambam agrees with Rashi. All is thus settled, with heavenly assistance, unlike [others] whom I have seen who were strained on this point.
