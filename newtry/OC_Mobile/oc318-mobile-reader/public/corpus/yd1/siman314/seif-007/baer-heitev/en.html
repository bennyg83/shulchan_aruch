To an expert. He is believed even if he does not identify the scholar by name. This applies specifically to a blemish that is not obvious. We do not suspect that he permitted it on his own and falsely claimed that a scholar permitted it, because people do not eat sacred animals outside the Temple and do not rely on themselves regarding a non-obvious blemish. He certainly showed it to a scholar, and the scholar would not have permitted it unless testimony was given that the blemish arose by itself. Rosh. Regarding an obvious blemish, however, one may suspect that he did not show it to a scholar, since he knows that an animal with such a blemish is not fit as sacred property. If he identifies the scholar, he is believed because the matter can be verified. According to the Mechaber in seif 8, who rules that with respect to a Jew one need not identify a particular Jew, he is likewise believed in every case even without naming the scholar. Shach. See siman 486.
