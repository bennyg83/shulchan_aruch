And one witness testified about it, etc. The Bach wrote: however, certainly where we do not know that this bekhor was given to this kohen without a blemish, he does not need to bring witnesses to testify about the blemish, for he is believed with a migo: why would he lie, since if he wished he could say, "A Jew gave it to me with its blemish." This requires examination according to what is ruled below, that he must say specifically "so-and-so the Jew"; if so there is no migo, for he fears perhaps they will ask him and he will be exposed, unlike here, where he says that it fell by itself and there is no one to contradict him.
I showed this blemish. To a sage and he permitted it for slaughter. Even if he does not mention the sage's name, he is believed. This applies specifically to non-obvious blemishes, for we are not concerned that he will permit it on his own and say, "the sage permitted it for me," since he would not eat kodashim outside, and people do not rely on themselves regarding non-obvious blemishes. Certainly he showed it to a sage and the sage permitted it to him; and the sage would not have permitted it unless witnesses testified before him that this blemish fell in it by itself. This is the language of the Rosh, and so the Bach ruled. Rabbeinu Yerucham also wrote so at the end of Netiv 20 in the name of Rabbi Yonah, and the Beit HaBechirah brings it. It appears to me that one should emend the Rosh's words likewise: "and Rabbi Yonah wrote." Also what the Tur wrote in the name of Rabbi Yonah, that if it is an obvious blemish he is believed although he does not mention the sage's name, should read: "if it is not obvious, etc." Now it works well that the Tur concludes that witnesses must testify that the blemish fell in it, for from the Rosh's words it is not explained that the sage would not have permitted it unless they testified before him that this blemish fell in it by itself. With this, the Bach's question in seif 5 and seif 8 against the Tur is also removed; see there. But with obvious blemishes, since nevertheless he must show it to a sage, as above siman 309, one cannot say that certainly he would not eat kodashim outside, for he knows it is not kodashim since it is an obvious blemish. Therefore there is concern that perhaps he did not show it to a sage. But when he mentions the sage's name he is believed, for he would not lie about a matter that can be revealed, since they can ask him. However, according to the Mechaber below, seif 8, and his supporters, that with a Jew one need not say "so-and-so the Jew," the same applies here: in every case he is believed even if he does not mention the sage's name.
