Even his sons. This follows Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, who accepts testimony from a son or daughter regarding a firstborn's blemish. Even according to him, however, obvious circumstances of deception create suspicion. If the owner takes the entire household outside and precisely then the firstborn enters sound and emerges injured, knowledge that one family member remained behind would create serious suspicion that the person was left to inflict the blemish. We do not merely suspect that someone secretly hid there. This gives practical force to Rava's statement even though the law follows Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel.
