(There, seif 10) He is believed to say that the blemish occurred by itself. This indicates that if he inflicted the blemish, the animal would be forbidden for consumption, although the post facto eating prohibition is only rabbinic. Since it is a doubtful firstborn, one might have expected leniency as a rabbinic doubt, yet it is forbidden. Mishneh LaMelech, chapter 4 of the Laws of Firstborns, rules likewise. The difference from siman 313:4, where an animal that chased and was kicked may be slaughtered, is that there the doubt concerns only eating. Here the doubt also concerns the Torah prohibitions of shearing and labor. Because the animal was already treated stringently as a possible firstborn for Torah law, the associated rabbinic eating prohibition is also treated stringently after a blemish is inflicted.
