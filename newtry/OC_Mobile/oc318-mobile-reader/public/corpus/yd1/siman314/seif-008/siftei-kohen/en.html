A Jew gave it to me, etc. Rosh, Tur, and Rabbeinu Yerucham hold that he must identify a particular Jew. Bach writes that their reasoning is persuasive, apparently because Beit Yosef's defense of Rambam is very strained. Maadanei Melech, page 260b, also challenges it. In truth, however, Rambam's view appears correct, for Rabbi Yehudah did not mention a particular Jew. The baraita that says, 'Buy it for me from a particular expert,' is, as Rashi and Rosh explain, because otherwise he could evade the claim by saying, 'Though he is not trustworthy to you, he is trustworthy to me.' This defense is unavailable regarding a Jew. Once the owner says, 'I gave it only while it was unblemished,' the non-Jew has no excuse. Maadanei Melech's own discussion indicates that this is Rambam's view.
