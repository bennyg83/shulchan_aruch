The custom is, etc. Pirkei DeRabbi Eliezer states there: Israel practiced circumcision until they were divided into two kingdoms, and the kingdom of Ephraim prevented them from circumcising. Eliyahu, of blessed memory, arose and displayed great zeal, and swore by the heavens that no dew or rain would descend upon the earth. Izevel heard and sought to kill him, etc. Eliyahu arose and fled, etc. The Holy One, blessed is He, revealed Himself to him and said, "Why are you here, Eliyahu?" He answered, "I have been exceedingly zealous." The Holy One, blessed is He, said, "You are always zealous. You were zealous at Shittim concerning immorality, as it is stated, 'Pinchas son of Elazar,' and here you are zealous. By your life, Israel shall not perform the covenant of circumcision unless you see it with your own eyes." From here the Sages instituted that a seat of honor be prepared for the angel of the covenant, as Scripture states, "and the angel of the covenant whom you desire," etc.
And the custom is, etc. Midrash Shocher Tov expounds, "All my bones shall say," etc.: "With my knees I serve as sandak for children at the time of circumcision and uncovering the corona." The same appears in the Pesikta and is cited in Yalkut Tehillim, siman 723.
And it is proper, etc. As explained above, circumcision resembles an offering. See Zohar at the end of parashat Lech Lecha, 94b: "One who brings," etc.; and there, 95a: another opened and said, "An altar of earth," etc. See also Pirkei DeRabbi Eliezer there and what is stated below in seif 12.
(Supplement) Therefore the custom is, etc. His words are not compelling. If that were the reason, why should the restriction apply specifically to the same person, and why should one person never serve as sandak twice? We have also never seen that a sandak becomes wealthy. Rather, the custom is based upon the testament of Rabbi Yehudah HaChasid, siman 40. See there. (End of supplement.)
