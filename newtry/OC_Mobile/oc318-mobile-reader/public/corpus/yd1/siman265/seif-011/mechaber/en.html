The custom is to make a chair for Elijah, called the angel of the covenant; and when placing it, he says with his mouth that it is Elijah's chair: {Rama: And the custom is to honor after this mitzvah by being sandak to hold the infant for his circumciser (Hagahot Maimoniyot, chapter 3 of Milah); and the sandak's power is greater than the circumciser's to precede him for Torah reading, for every sandak is like one who offers incense (Maharil in the name of R' Peretz); therefore the custom is not to give two children to one sandak, as they say regarding incense — new for incense (there in the name of R' Peretz); and a woman should not be sandak for an infant where a man is possible, because it is immodest; nevertheless she helps her husband and brings the infant to the synagogue, and then the man takes from her and becomes sandak (there in the name of Maharam); but a man may do everything without a woman (so did Maharil). Circumcisers are accustomed to pray on the circumcision day, as it says, "Exaltations of God in their throat and a two-edged sword in their hand."}
