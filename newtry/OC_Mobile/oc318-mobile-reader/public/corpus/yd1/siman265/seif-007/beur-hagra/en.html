When the father, etc. As written in redemption of the son at the end of Pesachim.
(Extract) When the father, etc. For one does not bless shehecheyanu except the one performing the mitzvah incumbent on him; and we say in the first chapter of Kiddushin (29a) the father is obligated to circumcise, etc. — Mordechai chapter 19 of Shabbat — see there at length; and Tosafot Sukkah 46a s.v. ha'oseh, etc.; and so Rambam in chapter 1 of Laws of Blessings law 10: whether one performs a mitzvah for himself or for others he blesses before performing it "Blessed... to perform," but he does not bless shehecheyanu except on a mitzvah he performs for himself (end extract).
And for Rambam, etc. For his agent is like him and the mitzvah is upon him as stated.
(Extract) And for Rambam, etc. So too in chapter 3 of circumcision; and the reason: although another circumcises afterward, he brings him into the covenant; therefore he blesses shehecheyanu; and as Kesef Mishneh wrote. In chapter 1 of Laws of Blessings law 10 in the name of Ramach; and as written in Orach Chaim siman 485 s.k. 2 in the gloss; and see above; and although it is incumbent on all Israel as written in the first chapter of Kiddushin — nevertheless it is especially incumbent on him more than the mitzvah of all Israel, as Rambam wrote there; and the father of the son blesses "Blessed... to bring him in," etc. — for the mitzvah is on the father to circumcise the son more than the mitzvah commanded Israelites to circumcise every uncircumcised among them; therefore if his father is not there we do not bless this blessing; and some ruled the court or one of the people should bless it — and it is not proper to do so; and see above seif 1 in the gloss; and Rambam's words are correct — for certainly even though another performs it is as if he performed himself, from what is written in Shabbat 133a "and if there is another," etc. — Rashba, etc.; and if so certainly the mitzvah is especially upon him as the father wrote; and also Tosafot's reasoning is not correct — what he wrote there is no joy — for even "the joy is in his dwelling" is explained in the first chapter of Ketubot (8a) "to bless if not because," etc.; and here it is unnecessary as Rabbi Simchah and Hagahot Maimoniyot wrote (in the extract in s.k. 36 — when his father died, etc.); and also Rabbi Simchah's version: because three partners — she is not commanded on circumcision as written in the first chapter of Kiddushin, and the joy here is on the mitzvah — not as written; and furthermore in truth he should bless hatov vehametiv (end extract).
And in these countries, etc. Rashba and all early authorities already elaborated on this and found no reason for it — lest because it does not come from time to time redemption of the son proves (and in Ran end of Pesachim and chapter 4 of Sukkah wrote redemption of the son is different, for it is a time-dependent mitzvah of thirty days, etc. — and it requires investigation); and even regarding other sons like bought and returned and bought (Berakhot 60a — see there Tosafot and Rashba); and also lest because of safek miscarriage — if so how do they circumcise him on Shabbat and desecrate for him with twenty-four categories; and also lest because of the infant's pain — but when his father died and he inherits him he blesses two, etc. (as written s.k. 59b).
(Extract) And in these countries, etc. Mordechai there in the name of Rabbeinu Tam — see there; and in Tosafot Sukkah there s.v. ha'oseh, etc. — one must explain, etc.; and it appears mitzvot, etc.; and so in Mordechai there he wrote because of the infant's pain; but in later hagahot chapter 19 of Shabbat they challenged from what is written "his father died and he inherits him," etc.; and so Hagahot Maimoniyot challenged in chapter 3 of Laws of Circumcision — see there; and there he wrote in the name of Rabbi Simchah whose words are very strained; and in the name of Ra'aviah he wrote like the first reasoning (end extract).
