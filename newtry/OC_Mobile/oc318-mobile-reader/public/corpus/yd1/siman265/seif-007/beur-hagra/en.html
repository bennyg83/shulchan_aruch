When the father, etc. As stated regarding redemption of the firstborn son at the end of Pesachim.
(Supplement) When the father, etc. For only one who performs a mitzvah personally incumbent upon him recites Shehecheyanu. It is stated in the first chapter of Kiddushin, 29a: "The father is obligated to circumcise," etc. See Mordechai, chapter 19 of Shabbos, at length, and Tosafot, Sukkah 46a, s.v. ha'oseh. Rambam likewise writes in chapter 11 of Hilchot Berakhot, halachah 10: Whether one performs a mitzvah for himself or performs it for others, he recites before performing it, "Who sanctified us with His commandments and commanded us to perform," but he recites Shehecheyanu only over a mitzvah that he performs for himself. (End of supplement.)
And according to Rambam, etc. For a person's agent is considered like himself, and the mitzvah remains incumbent upon him, as explained above.
(Supplement) And according to Rambam, etc. He rules this way in chapter 3 of Hilchot Milah. The reason is that even when another person performs the circumcision, that person acts as the father's agent and thereby discharges the father's obligation. Therefore the father recites Shehecheyanu, as Kesef Mishneh explains. See chapter 11 of Hilchot Berakhot, halachah 10, in the name of the Ramach, and Orach Chaim, siman 585, seif 2, in the gloss, together with what I wrote there. Although the obligation rests upon all Israel, as stated in the first chapter of Kiddushin, it rests upon the father in a more particular manner than upon the rest of Israel. Rambam therefore writes there that the father recites, "Who sanctified us with His commandments and commanded us to bring him into the covenant," because the father is commanded to circumcise his son more directly than Israel is commanded to circumcise every uncircumcised male among them. Therefore, when the father is absent, this blessing is not recited. Some ruled that the beit din or one of those present should recite it, but it is not proper to do so. See above, seif 1, in the gloss. Rambam's words are correct, for when another performs the act it is certainly regarded as though the father performed it himself. This follows from Shabbos 133a, "and if another is available," etc., concerning the teaching of Reish Lakish. Were this not so, the mitzvah would certainly remain uniquely incumbent upon the father, as stated, "the father," etc. Tosafot's reasoning, that there is no joy here, is also incorrect. Even the blessing "in whose dwelling there is joy" would have been recited, as considered in the first chapter of Ketubot, 8a, were it not for the reason stated there. Here that reason does not apply, as Rabbi Simchah and Hagahot Maimoniyot wrote above in the supplement to seif katan 36, concerning one whose father died, etc. Rabbi Simchah's further answer, based on the three partners in a person, is also difficult, because the mother is not commanded regarding circumcision, as stated in the first chapter of Kiddushin, and the joy here is over the mitzvah, unlike the case discussed there. Moreover, in truth he should recite HaTov VeHaMeitiv. (End of supplement.)
And in these lands, etc. The Rashba and all the early authorities discussed this at length and found no satisfactory reason for the custom. It cannot be because the mitzvah does not recur periodically, for redemption of the firstborn son disproves this. The Ran at the end of Pesachim and in chapter 4 of Sukkah writes that redemption of the firstborn is different because it is a mitzvah dependent upon a fixed time of thirty days, but this requires study. The same applies even with additional sons, like the law of one who acquired property, then acquired again, in Berakhot 60a; see Tosafot and Rashba there. Nor can the reason be concern that the child is a nonviable newborn, for if so, how may he be circumcised on Shabbos, with Shabbos desecrated for all that is necessary? Nor can the reason be the child's pain, for when one's father dies and he inherits him, he recites two blessings, etc., as stated there, 59b.
(Supplement) And in these lands, etc. See Mordechai there in the name of Rabbeinu Tam, and Tosafot, Sukkah 46a, s.v. ha'oseh: "One must explain," etc.; "it appears that the mitzvah," etc. Mordechai likewise writes that the reason is the child's pain. The later glosses to chapter 19 of Shabbos object from the teaching, "His father died and he inherited him," etc. Hagahot Maimoniyot raises the same objection in chapter 3 of Hilchot Milah. See there. What is written there in the name of Rabbi Simchah is exceedingly strained. In the name of Raavyah, however, he writes according to the first explanation. (End of supplement.)
