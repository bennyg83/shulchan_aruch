His intention. The Shach wrote: this requires study, for in siman 19 seif 7 the Rav wrote that some say that even if he did not have the second in mind, he does not need to recite another berachah when they are of one type; and here the infants are one type. As a matter of law, I concluded in siman 19 that even with one type he must recite another berachah, and if so the same applies here. However, where there is no custom, the Bach wrote that one should conduct himself by reciting a berachah on each one, and they should be careful not to bring two infants together to the synagogue, but one after the other, so that after one is circumcised they bring the second; end quote. This is proper to do, because most often they interrupt meanwhile with idle talk that is not needed for the circumcision, and that is an interruption according to everyone. So too wrote Maharshal; end quote. The Taz wrote: one may ask why Rama wrote in Even HaEzer siman 61 seif 3 that because of ayin hara the practice with two bridegrooms is to recite a berachah for each one separately; why is this different from two infants, where he did not require instituting this? It appears to me that certainly at the outset the second bridegroom must also be present at the berachah, since he is subject to the obligation, and then there is concern for ayin hara because both are together. This is unlike an infant being circumcised, who need not be present for the berachah, since he is not subject to obligation; end quote.
And recited a berachah. The Shach wrote in the name of Rabbeinu Yerucham that if there are two mohalim, the first recites the berachah over circumcision, and the second should not recite the berachah over circumcision but only "who sanctified," etc., and both thereby discharge the obligation for the two infants. This implies that the first should not recite "who sanctified" at all; and so one should practice, unlike Maharshal; end quote.
