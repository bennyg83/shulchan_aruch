(Likkut) One who, etc. As written in Chullin 86b: “If he slaughtered, etc.” Even Rabbi Yehudah disagrees only regarding wild animals and birds, and there it says, “Rabbi Yehudah agrees, etc.” Although when the first covered, the second had not yet become obligated in covering, this is why it says, “and even if the boy, etc.” Regarding Torah reading, the opener blesses before it and the closer, etc.; this is why it says, “and the second blesses, etc.” See Mordechai in Chullin there, siman 1059, who elaborates on this. “But if, etc.; and specifically, etc.; and even, etc.” All is like slaughtering and similar laws; see there.
