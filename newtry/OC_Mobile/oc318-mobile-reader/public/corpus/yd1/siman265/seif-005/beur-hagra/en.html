(Supplement) One who, etc. As stated in Chullin 86b: "One slaughtered," etc. Even Rabbi Yehudah disagrees only regarding a wild animal and a bird, and it states there, "Rabbi Yehudah concedes," etc. Although when the first person covered the blood, the second person was not yet obligated in covering, this is the meaning of the statement, "and even if the child," etc. Likewise, in the Torah reading, the one who opens recites the blessing before it and the one who concludes, etc. This is the meaning of, "and the second shall recite the blessing," etc. See Mordechai to Chullin there, siman 659, who discusses this at length. But if, etc.; specifically, etc.; and even, etc. All follow the same rule as slaughtering and the other matters discussed there. See there. (End of supplement.)
