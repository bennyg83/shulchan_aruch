Or his intent was not on the second, etc. It requires investigation — for above siman 19 s.k. 7 the Rav wrote that even if his intent was not on the second one need not return to bless when they are one species; and here these infants are one species. Granted Ba'al HaItur himself earlier — although the Rav's words there are Ba'al HaItur's words that Tur brought there, and here Tur wrote in the name of Ba'al HaItur: since it is impossible to circumcise them together each needs a blessing — compared to slaughter where it is possible to slaughter many animals together at once; but in circumcision one cannot circumcise them together — and this is what Ba'al HaItur wrote: since it is impossible to circumcise them together, etc. — compared to slaughter — Pri Chadash; and not like Perishah who wrote it requires investigation — did not Ba'al HaItur write above in slaughter law that if they are one species one need not return to bless; and it is possible slaughter differs, for it is customary to slaughter many animals one after another, unlike circumcision — end quote; and it is not clear, for it appears even if his intent was here on many infants Ba'al HaItur requires two blessings for the reason I explained. Again I found in Rivash siman 384 — he brought Ba'al HaItur's language: and where there are two infants to circumcise in synagogue it seems to us since it is impossible to circumcise them together — they are separate bodies — each needs a blessing — compared to slaughter where there is possible to slaughter both together as we learn "the slaughterer who tilted two heads as one — his slaughter is valid"; but here it is impossible, etc. — end quote; behold explicitly as I wrote; but on the Rav it is difficult — since he rules here one need not return to bless when his intent was on the second from the start — implies he holds like the poskim who disagree with Ba'al HaItur; and although it is impossible to circumcise them together, one blessing suffices; if so when his intent was not from the start also they are one species — and it requires investigation. And for practical law I concluded in siman 19 s.k. 9 that even for one species one must return to bless — so too here; however where there is no custom Bach wrote one should practice and bless on each one, and they should be careful not to bring two infants together to synagogue except one after the other — after the first is circumcised bring the second — end quote; and so is correct to do, because mostly they interrupt in between with idle talk not needed for circumcision and it is an interruption according to all; and so Maharshal in chapter Kisui HaDam section 9 — they refrained with two infants not of one person to exempt them with one blessing, for many times it leads to their being grown and distraction.
And even if he forgot etc. Maharshal there wrote likewise the first may bless "Who sanctified the beloved" — only not to create rivalry; it appeared he would bring proof from Beit Yosef and Rabbeinu Yerucham that one blesses "Who sanctified" after each milah etc.; but in truth Rabbeinu Yerucham did not bring there except responsum of R' Meshulam to R' Shimon: if two mohalim — one blesses on milah and the second does not bless on milah but "Who sanctified" etc., and both count for both infants — end; in Mordechai chapter Kol HaBasar it implies the first does not bless "Who sanctified" at all, only the second; so one should practice.
