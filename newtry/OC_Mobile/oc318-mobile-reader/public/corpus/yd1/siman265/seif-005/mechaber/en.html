One who has two infants to circumcise — he blesses one blessing for both; and even if two circumcise — the first blesses on the circumcision and it counts also for the second, and the second blesses "Who sanctified the beloved" and it counts also for the first — even if the infant is not before him at the time of the blessing, since his intent is upon him, only he should not divert his attention in the meantime: {Rama: (An individual opinion in Beit Yosef, not like Rabbeinu Yerucham). But if he spoke in the meantime, or did not have intent on both from the start, he must bless "Who sanctified the beloved" on the first and repeat for the second's circumcision — and specifically if he spoke about matters not needed for the circumcision; but for circumcision needs it is not an interruption (Rabbeinu Yerucham, Mordechai chapter Kisuy HaDam, Avudraham); and even if he forgot and blessed "Who sanctified the beloved" after the first, he need not repeat for the circumcision, for a blessing is not an interruption (Rashba siman 362, Kol Bo), as explained siman 28 regarding covering blood; and if both children belong to one man, the father says to bring them into the covenant of Abraham our father (Beit Yosef in the name of Mordechai and Avudraham); and likewise says, "Establish the children," etc.; and prepares all circumcision needs for each separately — such as the candle lit, he makes for each one a candle separately (Maharil).}
