One blessing for both of them. The Tur writes this in the name of the Rosh. The same law appears regarding two bridegrooms, and in the Tur, Even HaEzer, siman 61, seif 3, even if the second bridegroom is not present at the time of the blessing. One may ask why the Rama writes there that because of ayin hara the custom is to recite a separate blessing for each bridegroom, whereas regarding two infants he did not require this practice. It appears that ideally the second bridegroom must also be present for the blessing, since he is personally obligated. The concern of ayin hara then applies because both are together. A circumcised infant, however, need not be present for the blessing, since the infant is not personally obligated. Nevertheless, if it is known that the second infant will not arrive soon and there will be a substantial interruption and conversation, a separate blessing should be recited for each, meaning the blessing over the circumcision, in accordance with the Baal HaIttur cited by the Tur. It is therefore proper that the second infant also be ready for circumcision immediately after the blessing for the first.
