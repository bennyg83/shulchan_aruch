And the father. Taz wrote: it seems to me if the father circumcises his son he should bless first the two blessings — on circumcision and to bring him in — and afterward cut the foreskin (for between cutting and uncovering he cannot, since he is occupied in the middle of performing the mitzvah — how can he pause in a blessing to bring him in); and so I practiced when I circumcised my son, and this is according to all; and afterward I found Maharil also ruled thus — end quote (and it seems to me specifically when he has both cutting and uncovering; but if he cuts and afterward uncovers — he should bless to bring him in after the cutting before uncovering like other people). And he wrote in Bedek HaBayit in the name of Rashbatz: when the forced converts circumcise themselves they bless "Blessed are You... to bring him into the covenant of Abraham our father."
That You brought him in. Taz wrote: it appears those who see the father should say the wording "that You brought him in — so may You bring him," etc.; and those standing far or where the father is not in synagogue should say "as he entered," etc. (and Shach wrote they always say "as he entered," etc. — see there).
From the womb. Shach wrote it should read tzivah with patach under tzadi and chirik vav — prayer language for the future; also our custom when the child has no father we skip in the blessing text the word "to his father" and say "sustain this child for his mother," etc.; and when he has neither — if we skip also his mother and say "sustain this child and let his name be called," etc. — end quote. And Taz wrote: when there are two mohalim — one circumcises and one uncovers — the one who uncovers recites the blessing; and proof from what is written in Orach Chaim siman 174 that the one who blesses on wine after the meal says it on what is finished — so too whoever uncovers finishes — end quote.
