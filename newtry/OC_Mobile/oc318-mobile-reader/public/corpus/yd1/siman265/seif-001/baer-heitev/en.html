And the father. If the father circumcises his own son, Taz rules that he should first recite both blessings, 'concerning circumcision' and 'to bring him into the covenant,' and then cut, because he cannot interrupt between cutting and periah while occupied in the mitzvah. Maharil agrees. If one person cuts and another performs periah, the second blessing may be recited between them. Rashbatz writes that forced converts circumcising themselves recite the blessing of entering Abraham's covenant.
That You brought him in. Taz writes that those who see the father should say, 'Just as You brought him in, so may You bring him,' while those at a distance or where the father is absent should say, 'Just as he entered.' Shach holds that the latter formula is always used.
From the womb. Shach discusses the correct vocalization of the prayer and writes that when the child has no father, the words referring to his father are omitted; if he has neither parent, both references are omitted. Taz rules that where one mohel cuts and another performs periah, the one who completes the mitzvah recites the concluding blessing.
