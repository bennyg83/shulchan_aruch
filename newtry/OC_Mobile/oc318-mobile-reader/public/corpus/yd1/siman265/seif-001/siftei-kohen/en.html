Between cutting the foreskin, etc. Because some poskim bless before circumcision, for all mitzvot one blesses preceding their performance; and some poskim bless after circumcision, lest the mohel reconsider and not circumcise and it be a blessing in vain; therefore they bless after circumcision before uncovering, and it is considered properly preceding its performance, he circumcised and did not uncover as if he did not circumcise; and we are not concerned lest he reconsider, since he already circumcised; however if he blessed before cutting the foreskin or after uncovering he fulfilled after the fact and need not return to bless.
To bring him, etc. The Bedek HaBayit cites the Rashbatz that when anusim circumcise themselves, they recite the blessing, “Who sanctified us with His commandments and commanded us to enter the covenant of Avraham Avinu.” It appears obvious that they also recite the blessing over circumcision.
As you brought him in etc. So is the version also in Tur, Rambam, and other poskim; but in Gemara and Asheri: as he entered the covenant so may he enter Torah and marriage canopy, and for this reason; so in Avudraham and so practiced; it appears better thus since sometimes the father is not there or he has no father, impossible to speak in second person, only hidden as "as he entered"; therefore they did not divide. Also appears even one who reads "as you brought him in" agrees "as he entered" is valid, as tannaitic teaching 133 a, those standing say "as you brought him in" etc.; and afterward 135 a those standing say "as he entered to the covenant" as above regarding blessings, end.
Or one of the people, etc. This implies that the blessing may be recited even by someone who is neither the mohel nor the father. The Bach writes that our custom is always for the mohel to recite it, but he objects because the mohel is sometimes unlearned, and not only fails to understand the blessing but skips and distorts its words. Therefore, one who fears Hashem should arrange for this blessing to be recited by a distinguished member of the congregation. Nevertheless, this custom is ancient, for the Kol Bo also writes in siman 73 that the present practice is for the mohel to recite the blessing over the wine and the blessing “Who sanctified the beloved,” and then to say, “Preserve this child,” etc.
And he says, etc. Who sanctified the beloved from the womb and ordained in his flesh the seal of holy covenant, therefore in reward for this living God our portion our Rock commanded to save the beloved of our remnant from destruction for the sake of His covenant He placed in our flesh, Blessed are You, Covenant Cutter. So is it in the Gemara, Tur, and other poskim; and see explanation of this blessing in Beit Yosef in the name of the poskim; and see in Sefer Tanya also on the blessing of circumcision and in customs; and it seems to me "therefore in reward for this," etc. is prayer and request for the future; and tzivah should be pointed with patach under tzadi and chirik vav, not as pointed in all versions of the tablets in synagogue tzivah with chirik under tzadi and kamatz vav; and so all mohalim practice saying it, for according to this it is past tense and does not fit "therefore in reward for this," etc.; also the language of commandment does not apply, only language of promise; but certainly as written and so proven in Agudah in the name of Rambam who wrote: he asks from God and says You our Rock commanded to save the beloved of our remnant from destruction, etc.; and so in Hilchot Gedolot page 24a the version: therefore in reward for this living God may our portion be to save the beloved of our remnant from destruction, etc.; and although we do not have this version and do not say thus, nevertheless we learn from it it is prayer language for the future; and one need not wonder he wrote tzivah commanded in direct address to the Holy One blessed be He and afterward says for His covenant, etc. in third person, for so is the formula of blessings and prayers in many places.
And the custom when he reaches, etc. Because after finishing the above blessing he says "Our God and God of our fathers, sustain this child for his father and mother and let his name be called," etc. until the end of the verses; and when he reaches "by your blood live" they repeat and say "by your blood you shall live" and give each time from the wine on his finger; and in Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Avudraham and Kol Bo and Ritva: when the infant has no father or has no father, other versions see there; but we practice when he has no father we skip "to his father" and say "sustain this child for his mother," etc.; and when he has neither, if we skip also his mother and say "sustain this child and let his name be called," etc.; and so in Hagahat Minhagim; and if God forbid his father or mother of the infant died, we skip from the blessing whichever it is and say "sustain this child for his father or mother", end of quote; and so in Sefer Tanya page 131a.
