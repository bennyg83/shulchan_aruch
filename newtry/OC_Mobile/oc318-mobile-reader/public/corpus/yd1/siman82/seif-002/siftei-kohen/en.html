This fowl my master the hunter permitted me — but he is not believed to say this fowl my master the sage permitted me — thus is in the Gemara end of chapter 1 daf 63; and Maharshal wrote there siman 115; nevertheless if a Torah scholar testifies on a fowl that it is accepted as pure he is believed; and especially nowadays when we have no hunter except tradition — end.
And when that hunter is presumed — meaning his master the hunter.
That seizes and eats — meaning it inserts its claws into living creatures and seizes them, like seizing of hawk and other birds above siman 57; so too Ran and the Rav the Maggid in the name of Rambam, and it appears their view there and so Rambam in Bedek HaBayit daf 61b; also Rashba in Mishmeret HaBayit did not challenge him, and so is the conclusion of Beit Yosef and so is his view here since he wrote no explanation but certainly relied on the unspecified seizing mentioned above siman 57; and so Maharshal there siman 114; and the explanation of seizes and eats is not that it literally eats but seizes in order to eat — even if it does not eat alive — unlike HaTaz who ruled like Rabbenu Tam and supporters that seizes and eats means it seizes the bird and eats it alive:
There are three signs of purity, etc. — meaning there are three signs of purity in the Gemara and poskim, and by law it is permitted with these three signs even if unknown whether it seizes; and likewise even with one sign when known it does not seize; and so per some poskim the sign must be gizzard peels as explained — all this in poskim' words and the Mechaber abbreviated and came only to say there are three signs and nevertheless we practice to forbid even if it does not seize because we are concerned perhaps it will seize later unless its beak is wide and its foot wide like a goose and it has three signs — then we rely it does not seize and it is pure; investigate:
Extra toe — Rashi: toe behind the toes; Ran: the toe before it is large and extra compared to its fellows — called extra toe.
Unless they have tradition — and it appears if they have tradition they need not check further; but certainly if found that it seizes or splits its legs one may not rely on tradition — mistaken acceptance; and so Maharshal there siman 115 and so HaTaz; but if unknown whether it seizes it appears even if known it lacks one of the three signs, i.e. no crop or extra toe or gizzard does not peel — permitted by tradition, for it could be pure only needs to recognize all twenty-four species; and since there is tradition they certainly initially knew it is not among the twenty-four; therefore though we are stringent that even three signs do not help because perhaps it will seize later, nevertheless certainly by tradition permitted as long as we do not see it seizes — true for all views; even Rashi: if unknown whether it seizes even if no bodily sign it could be pure if he recognizes twenty-four species; with tradition it is as if recognized; Maharshal and HaTaz: specifically if we see it seizes tradition does not help; investigate well; see s.k. 9:
