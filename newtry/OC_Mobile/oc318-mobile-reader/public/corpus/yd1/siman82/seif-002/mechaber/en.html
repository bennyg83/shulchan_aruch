Anyone who is expert in those species and their names — behold he may eat any fowl that is not among them and need not check (there). And pure fowl is eaten by tradition when it is a simple matter in that place that this fowl is pure; and a hunter is believed to say: this fowl my master the hunter permitted me — when that hunter is presumed expert in the unclean species mentioned in the Torah and their names. One who does not recognize them and does not know their names checks by signs: any fowl that seizes prey and eats — it is known it is among unclean species; and if he does not know whether it seizes or not — if when they stand it on a string it splits its legs, two toes here and two toes there, or it catches from the air and eats — it is known it seizes; and if it is known it does not seize — there are three signs of purity: extra toe, crop, and its gizzard peels by hand — unlike if it peels only with a knife (language of Mechaber) — if it was hard and stuck and he left it in the sun and it softened and peeled by hand, behold this is a sign of purity; and even though it has these three signs one may not eat it because we are concerned perhaps it seizes, unless they have tradition that their fathers transmitted to them that it is pure (language of Rambam there law 18):
