Some forbid and some permit, etc. — I did not find explicit dispute, only that after Beit Yosef brought Rosh's words above, he wrote: Rashba wrote in Teshuvot HaRosh: they ate in some places and not others — it appears to me they say on the places where leniency spread that unless they received thus from expert forefathers they would not practice leniency; and possible those who practiced prohibition we forbid, for I say they are permitted only that they practiced prohibition due to tame fowl similar to these found in some places, end. And Mechaber's view is Rashba argues on Rosh; and it appears to me there is no dispute: Rosh speaks where they did not receive leniency but also did not practice prohibition as he inferred from wording; Rashba speaks explicitly where they practice prohibition; and so Maharshal there siman 115 and so Derishah; nevertheless what appears from Mechaber: even per those who forbid, one leaving a permitted place to a forbidden place may certainly eat even if he does not intend to return — true, and so Maharshal and Derishah explicitly, unlike HaTaz who forbids per those who forbid lest he err; for we do not certainly know forbidders' reason, only we fear perhaps they practiced prohibition from old when tame similar fowl existed; and one may say also they did not receive leniency, therefore practiced prohibition; Rashba wrote and possible, etc.; therefore though we may not permit them to change custom, we may not forbid for this reason one from a place of leniency with accepted leniency; and one from a forbidden place who went to a lenient place may eat even intending to return, per Rashba's reasoning and also HaTaz wrote so, only he said the reason is if we forbid it would be decree on decree — incorrect, for we rule one who goes from a place that does not do melacha on erev Pesach before noon to a place that does — we apply stringencies of both places, as Orach Chaim siman 464, even though there too it is only rabbinic decree lest he be busy with melacha and forget bedikat chametz and matza preparation as Rashi explained chapter place where they practiced; but certainly once they practiced prohibition there is no decree on decree, rather the reason as explained; see responsum Maharil siman 100; see end siman 115 and what I wrote there and siman 214:"]
