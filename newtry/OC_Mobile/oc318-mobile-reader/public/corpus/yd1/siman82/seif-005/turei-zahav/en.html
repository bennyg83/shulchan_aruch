I saw fit to mention two matters that greatly puzzle me. The first concerns the words of Tosafot in chapter Shilu'ach HaKen (Chullin 139), where they wrote regarding the statement that the word "tzippor" [bird] indicates specifically a pure bird: "And if you object: it says 'even a bird has found a house' — which also applies to an impure bird — one may answer it refers to a pure bird, as proven from the end of the verse 'and a sparrow [dror] has found a nest for itself,' and tzippor dror is pure, as proven in Elu Treifot." And in the marginal notation it says page 62. I wondered — for there is no proof for tzippor dror there. What is stated there is: R' Yehudah said a bird that scratches [mesaret] is valid for the metzora's purification [so Rashi explains — meaning the tzippor mentioned in the Torah]; and this is the white swallow [snonit levanah] over which R' Eliezer and the Sages disagreed. Amimar said: where its belly is white all agree it is permitted; they disagree where its belly is yellow — R' Eliezer forbids and the Rabbis permit, and the halacha follows R' Eliezer. Mar Zutra taught: where its belly is yellow all agree it is forbidden; they disagree where its belly is white — R' Eliezer forbids and the Rabbis permit, and the halacha follows the Rabbis. "This is the snonit levanah" — on the first version this implies its whiteness is its identifying name; but on the second version — what does "the white swallow" mean? The answer: to exclude the dark-housed swallows [snonit devatei d'uchmati]. And Rashi explains: devatei means those that grow and dwell in the walls of houses and are black. From where, then, did Tosafot derive proof here regarding tzippor dror being pure? For no dror is mentioned there; and if from Rashi's mention of the black ones dwelling in walls — that was explained as "to exclude the black ones of houses," implying they are forbidden by all, and we therefore say "to exclude the black ones of houses." It seems to me there is a scribal error in Tosafot where it says "ba'Elu Treifot [ba'ET]" — it should say "ba'Ein Tzadin [ba'EZ]," and the copyist confused tet with tzadi. For at the beginning of chapter Ein Tzadin it is stated: what is meant by [the rule] that one may not trap a tzippor on Yom Tov? — this is the tzippor dror, which accepts no authority over it; the school of R' Yishmael taught: why is it called tzippor dror? Because it dwells in a house as in a field. And Rashi explains: it knows how to slip into corners from every direction — and therefore it is called dror, after dirah [dwelling], for it dwells anywhere. End of quote. And there it is certainly proven to be pure — for if it were impure, even a non-dror one would certainly be forbidden to trap on Yom Tov; and even if there is some need, trapping is certainly not permitted — it is a primary labor — except for food consumption. And this is proven from the words of Tosafot at the beginning of chapter Ein Tzadin, starting with "and one does not give, etc.": "and now that the Talmud resolves it as trapping for food, this also makes sense — for one depends on the other." So the baraita that taught the food prohibition teaches it because of the trapping prohibition; and if tzippor dror were impure, the baraita should have permitted food, as Tosafot wrote there that specifically on Yom Tov they decreed about food because of trapping — since eating living creatures is permitted — and that does not apply to an impure bird. It is therefore obvious to them that this refers to a pure bird, and tzippor dror is pure. And certainly this is what Tosafot alluded to in chapter Shilu'ach HaKen, and it was written "ba'EZ" [Ein Tzadin], but the copyist confused tet and tzadi and wrote "ba'ET" [Elu Treifot]. The passage in Elu Treifot about the black swallow is not at all tzippor dror — tzippor dror means one that dwells in a house as in a field with no fear of man, able to slip away at any moment so it cannot be trapped, as Rashi described; whereas what is in Elu Treifot refers to one that dwells in the walls of houses and hides from people — and one is impure, one is pure. In any case I am greatly astonished: why did Tosafot need proof from the Talmud for this, when there is an explicit mishnah that tzippor dror is pure — for we learned in chapter 14 of Nega'im that the metzora's purification requires specifically tzipporei dror, and "pure birds" is written regarding the metzora? And because of this the Talmud says in Shilu'ach HaKen that the mesaret bird is valid for the metzora — if Tosafot relied on that mishnah, they should not have written "as proven, etc.," since it is an explicit verse. And I am even more greatly astonished about Maharik — a second astonishment — for he wrote the opposite: that tzippor dror is an impure bird! In responsum root 198 he writes: "As for your question whether tzippor dror is pure: you did not specify — whether it refers to those that dwell in a house, as implied by 'tzippor dror' and as R' [Yehudah] said in Ein Tzadin 'why is it called tzippor dror, etc.' — and those are certainly impure, as we learn in Elu Treifot about the white swallow: 'to exclude the dark-housed swallows.' And Rashi explains: to exclude those that grow and dwell in the walls of houses and are black." — end of quote. See how he explained: those that grow and dwell in the walls of houses are forbidden by all — and this is the tzippor dror that dwells in houses, as I wrote from Ein Tzadin! And further he wrote: "and we also read in the second chapter of Avodah Zarah: what is a small pitcher? — one into which the head of a tzippor dror cannot enter; and Rashi there explains: there is no smaller forbidden piece than this — implying it is a piece of prohibition since one measures prohibition by it; however a white one is clearly implied there to be permitted" — end of quote; and he concludes one should be stringent even about the white one. And this is a great wonder about so great a luminary — that he did not know that tzippor dror is pure from the mishnah in chapter 14 of Nega'im that I cited; and he was also not aware of the Tosafot I mentioned in that responsum. And his proof from Rashi's language about the black swallow that dwells in walls — that this is tzippor dror and therefore invalid for the metzora since it is impure, as he himself wrote, while "pure birds" is explicitly written for the metzora — so obviously this is not tzippor dror at all. And also what he cited from Ein Tzadin to prove the black ones are tzippor dror — with all due respect, he did not examine that passage at all, for in Ein Tzadin it is obvious the passage refers to pure birds from which one may eat, and even so trapping them is forbidden. Furthermore what he cited from the second chapter of Avodah Zarah is completely irrelevant — for even though we measure by it for the law of prohibition, it itself is not forbidden at all. In short, I do not know how to explain [his error] except to say he was not aware of the mishnah where it is stated clearly that it is a pure bird. And if Maharik wished to forbid the bird that dwells in a house, he should not have categorized it under tzippor dror at all. So it seems to me clearly that it was an error on his part.
