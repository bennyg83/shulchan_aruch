Like a synagogue, etc. The Ramban and the Ran wrote that one should wonder at the Rambam, for his words contradict each other — for at the beginning of the chapter of partners Rabbi Avi and the Sages dispute regarding partners who vowed benefit from each other, for Rabbi Avi holds that both can enter the courtyard of partnership, for this one enters into his own and this one into his own, and the Sages forbid. And Rabbah said the dispute is regarding a matter that has a law of division, but regarding a matter that has no law of division, all agree it is permitted. Rav Yosef said to him: but behold a synagogue, when there is no law of division it is similar, and we learned "and both are forbidden" — like whom does the Mishnah come? Rather Rav Yosef said the dispute is regarding a matter that has no law of division, but regarding a matter that has a law of division, all agree it is forbidden. And the Ran wrote page 48 — and the Mishnah that teaches they are forbidden in a synagogue is the Sages and not Rabbi Avi, as we say above that a synagogue is like a courtyard that has no law of division; therefore since the halachah was decided like Rabbi Avi who permits when there is no law of division, the Mishnah that said both are forbidden in a synagogue does not exist. And one should wonder at the Rambam in this ruling that he ruled like Rabbi Avi and also ruled like the Mishnah — and likewise the Ramban challenged, who wrote how does one transfer a document to two people — end of quote. And Beit Yosef brought this challenge. And if so one should wonder also at the Rosh and the Tur and the Shulchan Aruch, who all wrote like the Rambam — that is, here they wrote they are forbidden in a synagogue, and in siman 226 they wrote both are permitted in a matter that has no law of division — and behold a synagogue is a matter that has no law of division as we mentioned. Heaven forbid to say that these great ones and also the Rambam erred in this. And Maharach Or Zarua resolved this challenge and said that it was difficult for them halachah against halachah — the halachah is like Rabbi Avi and the halachah is like an anonymous Mishnah that forbids them in a synagogue; therefore they resolved that a synagogue is different, for it is forbidden even when there is no law of division, since there is a correction that they acquire their portion to another and can afterward return to use it, and it does not appear as an artifice since many use there — unlike a courtyard of two partners, if one gives his portion to another and uses it, it appears as an artifice. And that which Rav Yosef challenged above from a synagogue — he did not hold this distinction; but we hold like the conclusion according to this distinction — end of quote. And he was not precise at all — is it that in every matter that has no law of division it is relevant to say that one of them acquires his portion? For certainly also in a courtyard of partners it does not appear as an artifice — for what is different from siman 221 seif 9 where we say one gives a gift to another, etc., and we are not concerned it appears as an artifice, and he himself is permitted to benefit afterward from it since it left his domain — for if not, the Talmud and the poskim should have written this thing that he himself should not benefit afterward from it because of artifice, which is a great novelty; but certainly not, and as the Ran wrote explicitly that in every place it helps to give his portion to another, and there is no distinction between a synagogue and other matters that have no law of division. And further — to what matter does the conclusion refer? For we have before us only an anonymous Mishnah that forbids in a synagogue — who gave us authority to dispute Rav Yosef? And did Rav Yosef not see the Mishnah as it is? And further — if we say the challenge of Rav Yosef was resolved, if so the statement of Rabbah that we mentioned remains — that regarding a matter that has a law of division there is a dispute and the halachah is like Rabbi Avi who permits — and why do we rule in siman 226 that they are forbidden regarding a matter that has a law of division? Rather perforce there is no one who disputes Rav Yosef; and if so the challenge returns to its place. And what seems correct to me is that the sugya of the Talmud is well explained according to the Rambam and the other great ones we mentioned — and this is that we were precise in the language of the Talmud: behold a synagogue when there is no law of division it is similar — and he did not say there is no law of division, but rather it appears there are three matters. One — there is in it a complete law of division like a courtyard that has enough for this one and that one, and it is reasonable they should be forbidden. The second — there is in it no law of division at all, i.e., a courtyard that does not have four cubits that one cannot say they should be forbidden. The third — is intermediate between them, i.e., a synagogue and the like, that in this it is possible somewhat through division that this one withdraws from this portion and that one from that portion and can pray in his portion, only there is no way to divide it since many enter there, and likewise the ark; and because of this he said afterward "and the books" in plural — meaning there are two books and there was enough there for division; but if there were only one book it would be a matter that has no division and is permitted. It is found that somewhat it is called as having a law of division and somewhat it is called as not having a law of division, because there is no way to divide them. And now it is well that initially when Rabbah said they dispute regarding a matter that has a law of division — this is regarding a matter that has a complete law of division, and it is in the courtyard that the Mishnah mentions — the dispute of Rabbi Avi and the Sages, for the way to divide in this specifically the Sages hold it is forbidden; but in intermediate matters, i.e., a synagogue, they agree it is permitted. And on this he challenges well — but we learned both are forbidden in a synagogue, and it is not like anyone. And Rav Yosef answers that thus he says — they dispute regarding a matter that has no law of division at all, i.e., in the courtyard that the Mishnah mentions in the dispute of Rabbi Avi; and according to this, in the intermediate matter, i.e., a synagogue, it would be in the category of a matter that has a law of division, and for everyone it is forbidden; and if so the Mishnah that both are forbidden in a synagogue comes for everyone; and therefore they ruled the halachah. So it seems correct to me to resolve clearly and properly, even though the Ramban and the Ran raised it as a challenge — nevertheless the Rambam and the Rosh and the Tur and the Shulchan Aruch are not mistaken, heaven forbid, especially in a matter explicit in the Talmud, and it is fitting to advocate for them as much as we can, especially in a matter that is abundant, with heaven's help, as is written.
