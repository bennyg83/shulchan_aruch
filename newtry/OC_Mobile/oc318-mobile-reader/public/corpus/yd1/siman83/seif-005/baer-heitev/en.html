Together. Taz wrote: it appears even when they still lie in their salting, meaning they did not fall in together a full day; but if they fell in together a full day it is like pickled, as cooked, and forbidden — meaning the brine rose on both; and it appears that even a doubt whether they fell a full day is forbidden, as Shach wrote siman 105; and we rule b'dieved it is permitted as Rama wrote afterward. And what Mechaber wrote that even when lying in one vessel it is permitted — even if touching one another — is not like Bach.
In a barrel. The reason: we say presumably they were initially salted thus together; and Taharat Chesed wrote that if there are sixty of the pure against the impure, one may buy l'chatchila and we do not establish prohibition lest the impure were more and he sold from them; and Darkei Moshe ruled siman 58 that there is no difference between brine of fish and brine of nevelah, which is always forbidden d'oraisa even if nullified in sixty.
To search. Shach wrote: and some say in siman 1 in Hagahah that although most are presumed regarding shechitah, one may not rely l'chatchila on presumption where one can clarify; here we do not permit fish by presumption but on the contrary we say from where shall it come — let there be prohibition here (and in Shelah he wrote that from Rama's words it appears herring is permitted in every case even if impure ones are found inside; but Maharshal disagrees and ruled even sixty does not help — see there section 5).
Herring. Meaning: permitted when lying together salted; nevertheless in their brine after salting there is no difference between other fish and herring, for our eyes see the brine is sharp and strong and certainly absorbs — worse than brine mixed in water that is not considered boiling, above end of seif 7.
See. Meaning: if they fell in; but if we sometimes see in a non-Jew's house that he soaks them thus, we are not stringent to beware, for we say it is merely happenstance.
To permit. Shach wrote: Maharshal ruled even in salted fish it is forbidden until sixty even b'dieved; this is not the view of all poskim and they did not practice so; and it is plain that if they fell a full day — meaning twenty-four hours — even b'dieved it is forbidden; and even if there is room to doubt whether lying thus floating twenty-four hours is forbidden — for we rule pickled is forbidden d'oraisa (and it requires study: since the brine appears sharp and strong to the eye, why is it not called pickled enough to put on fire and begin to boil).
Salted. Taz wrote: this "salted" is not precise in Arukh; however stam fish have no fat.
To be stringent. And Taz and Shach wrote that if there is doubt whether there is fat, we follow leniency; and specifically in salting; but in pickled and cooked, even without fat, it is forbidden d'oraisa.
To be stringent. And Taharat Chesed concluded there: nevertheless if one finds a place that does not practice stringency, one should not protest.
