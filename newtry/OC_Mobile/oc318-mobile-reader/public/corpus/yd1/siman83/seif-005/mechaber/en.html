Brine of impure fish is only rabbinic; therefore it is permitted to buy from a non-Jew pure salted fish even though they lie with impure ones in one vessel, lest they were not salted together. {Rama: And some forbid if they lie with impure ones in one barrel, and so is the custom not to buy them even if one sees only that they lie with salted impure fish mixed on the table where they sell them there (Terumat HaDeshen siman 174; Hagahot ShaDa; ShaDa siman 20; Or Zarua; Arukh general rule 13; Mordechai in the name of Tosafot R' Yisrael) — except for herring and merkedis, which they permit in every case, for certainly they are not salted with impure ones because there is no way to salt them together (Mordechai). And even in other fish one need not be stringent to search and check whether there are impure fish among them; and if one sees that the impure fish are floating in water with the pure ones, even herring should be forbidden (Hagahot ShaDa in the name of Maharai and R' Yerucham). But one need not be concerned lest they fell in, as long as one does not see (there). And all this is l'chatchila; but b'dieved, if one already bought them, one may permit in every case because brine of fish is rabbinic and we say perhaps initially they were not salted together, and afterward one need not be concerned for their absorption one from another (Arukh). And specifically when there is no fat in it; and ordinary salted fish have no fat in them; but if there is fat in it, it is forbidden d'oraisa, and a doubt is to be stringent (there). And where the fish are salted dry, it is permitted in every case since the impure ones are also dry (Maharai in Terumat HaDeshen). And they practice to be stringent in this too l'chatchila if they lie among one another (there).}
