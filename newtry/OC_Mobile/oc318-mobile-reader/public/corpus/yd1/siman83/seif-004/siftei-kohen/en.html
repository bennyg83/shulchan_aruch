If the pieces match, etc. It appears from Mechaber's words that if they only fit together well it is permitted and one need not see from the cuts that all is one fish; and so is Tur and Rashba in Terumat HaDeshen house 3 gate 1; and so from Hagahot Maimoniyot and Terumat HaDeshen siman 79; and Mechaber wrote below siman 101; and so is Rashi's view; and so Ran — Rashi's view, not like Bach who wrote for Rashi both are required; and he ruled the halachah thus:
Even if all salted together. Explanation: even so the kosher pieces were not forbidden from the others because brine of fish is rabbinic and we say that after they were salted they mixed together, as will be explained in seif 5; and even if all salted together the rest are forbidden and we do not say that since these with scales are permitted, presumably the rest are also of this species — rather it is a d'oraisa doubt to be stringent; and see s.k. 23 — the brine is forbidden in any case; and see siman 114 seif 9:
And if a fish is found there, etc. Explanation: if when salted a fish is found that has a broad head and spine — a sign of a pure fish, for an impure fish has a pointed head and no spine — it is permitted to eat it, for certainly it had scales before salting and they fell or were removed when prepared; but if the fish is whole before us and not yet salted, one may not rely on head and spine to permit this fish and say it had scales that fell after coming from the water; and so I found Maharshal explained Rosh and Tur, brought in Perishah, and so Acharonim; and thereby Rambam and Rashba are settled comfortably; but Mechaber omitted this law of head and spine, following Beit Yosef that head and spine do not help for Rambam and Rashba except when one recognizes by visual inspection that it is from a certain pure species — he need not mention this as it is obvious, but this is not the main point. In manuscript of Rav R' Leib Hanles in margin of Ashrei I found: it appears to explain Rosh's words — initially he says nevertheless where one recognizes, etc., meaning the non-Jew brings a barrel of fish, some recognizable with scales, some without, but head and spine recognizable — we are not concerned there are impure fish, since some in the barrel have scales; afterward he says therefore one may not rely, etc., meaning where there is no sign of scales but only head and spine — one may not rely to permit; Beit Yosef explains otherwise — end of his words; Rambam and Rashba can also be aligned; and so Rama in hagahah. And what Rama wrote head and spine — Gemara concludes both head and spine are required, not one alone; and so all poskim; and even their brine is forbidden where there is no head and spine (Gemara there daf 40 side 1); see Ran s.v.
