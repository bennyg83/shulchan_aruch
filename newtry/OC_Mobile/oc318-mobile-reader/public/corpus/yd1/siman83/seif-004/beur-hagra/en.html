And if there is found, etc. There, in accordance with Rav Huna and Rav Pappa, and what is stated there, "When was this taught," etc. Rosh writes that the same applies to a spine, since some kosher species resemble nonkosher ones, and the Gemara abbreviated the matter. This applies specifically where it is known that the fish has no scales. Ordinarily, however, we rely on the head and spine, because those exceptional species are uncommon. Rav Huna and Rav Nachman spoke in such a case. This resolves Ran's question in the name of Raavad concerning the incidents there, where they found no scales and conducted an additional examination. Why did they not inspect the head and spine?
(Addendum) And if there is found, etc. In accordance with Rav Huna and Rav Pappa. See Rashi, 39b, s.v. "Any whose head," etc.
(Addendum) And if there is found, etc. There, according to Rashi's explanation. The Mechaber omitted this because Raavad and Raah, on 63a, explain "head and spine" to mean that one recognizes them as belonging to a particular kosher fish. Rav Huna said that only when the fish is whole may one rely on visual recognition of its head and spine, but not when it is cut and one recognizes only the head or spine. Rav Nachman said that even when it is cut, visual recognition of either one suffices. See Bedek HaBayit. Ramban and Rashba challenged this explanation, as discussed in Torat HaBayit. Nevertheless, Ramban writes there: Although Rashi's explanation is primary, I am astonished that I have not seen in the works of the Geonim that the head and spine serve as signs for fish, and the matter requires great investigation. Torat HaBayit HaKatzar likewise states that one should be stringent, since none of the Geonim or earlier authors wrote this. Beit Yosef writes that Rambam, in his Commentary to the Mishnah, also explained it like Raavad. In Bedek HaBayit he explains the objection, "Rav Ukva challenged," etc.: According to Rav Huna, a sign is needed because the fish is cut, but according to Rav Nachman the difficulty remains. The Gemara answers: with ara, etc.
(Addendum) But if, etc. This is why the great authorities were uncertain regarding the fish called barbuta, as Tosafot write there, 40a, s.v. "Rav said," etc. They did not examine its head and spine, because just as some kosher species resemble nonkosher ones, so some nonkosher species resemble kosher ones. Ordinarily, however, we do not suspect this, since such cases are uncommon, as stated regarding the peres and ozniah in Chullin 62a. Therefore, signs were given regarding the head and spine. This does not apply to barbuta, which has no scales.
