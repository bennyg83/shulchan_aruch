For this is a sign, etc. This is the wording of Tur, following Rashi's explanation. Tur likewise concludes that a kalkhit is not normally found in the brine of nonkosher fish.
This applies only, etc. According to Rashi's explanation there. He omitted the statement there concerning a non-Jew who brought a tub, following Rif, according to our version of Rif, as well as Rosh and Tur, who omitted it. Beit Yosef expresses surprise at them.
And there is one who, etc. He explains the statement there, "There is no difficulty, here," etc., as permitting the other barrels. "Two" means two barrels, and the statement, "the non-Jew," etc., refers specifically to one of them. This is also Rif's view, and therefore he omitted the statements of Rav, Shmuel, and R' Yochanan. Ran and Maggid Mishneh. See Maggid Mishneh, who writes that the reading in Rif must be the reverse of that found in our texts, and should read: open, one, etc.
Meaning a kosher fish. According to Rashba's explanation and Ran in the name of Rabbenu Chananel, a kalkhit is a kosher fish. Since one kosher fish is present in the brine, we presume that all the brine came from kosher fish. This is the meaning of the Mishnah, "and brine containing a fish," meaning a kosher fish, and it is not specifically a kalkhit. This differs from Rashi's explanation. In the chapter Gid HaNasheh, 97a, however, it appears to be a nonkosher fish. Ran writes that kalkhit is the name of a small fish, whether nonkosher or kosher.
And there is one who, etc. Rashba and Ran challenge Rabbenu Chananel's explanation: Regarding tarit, we rule that the head and spine of each fish are required, and one fish does not suffice. They answer that a leniency was adopted for brine because its prohibition is rabbinic. Although the brine there is prohibited, there the Sages decreed because the actual nonkosher fish is mixed into the brine. Here, however, no other scaleless fish is present at all, only the brine.
