A non-Jew who brought a barrel full of fish brine — if one kulyatis (Arukh explains: an impure fish; and so Rashi; and others explained it is a pure fish) is floating in it, it is permitted, for this is a sign that it is from pure fish; and if not, it is forbidden. And this applies when one kulyatis suffices in a sealed barrel; but if it was open, it is not permitted with fewer than two; but with one we are concerned perhaps it came from elsewhere. And some say that if there were many barrels and all were open, and a kulyatis — meaning a pure fish — is floating in one of them, all are permitted; if they were sealed, one opens one of them and if a kulyatis is found in it, it is permitted; a second — and if a kulyatis is found in it, all are permitted. And some say that what is permitted through kulyatis applies when there is no fish or piece in it that lacks scales; but if everything is in it, it is forbidden.
