Fish entrails, etc. He follows Rambam's approach. Rambam mentions only fish eggs, as the Mechaber quotes Rambam's wording in seif 5 through "and nowadays." As Maggid Mishneh writes, Rambam ruled like R' Zeira, who said, "Do not remove," etc. According to R' Zeira, no identifying signs were stated regarding fish entrails, and Rambam therefore did not mention them. The Yerushalmi likewise concludes in accordance with R' Zeira. In the chapter Elu Tereifot, the teaching also refers to fish embryos. Ran writes that Rif and Rambam hold that even when intact, one may not rely on the identifying signs, unlike Rava, but rather in accordance with the discussion in Elu Tereifot that signs are not of Torah authority. The statement, "two ends," etc., is understood as explained there. The Yerushalmi in Avodah Zarah likewise states that one may not rely on the signs, and Tosafot cite it there, s.v. "Rava said," contrary to Rabbenu Tam.
They are not, etc. Identifying signs do not help regarding them, as explained above.
Unless, etc. It does not suffice for him to say, "They are from a particular fish, and it is kosher," even though this suffices regarding birds, because fish come from distant lands and he can evade verification more readily than with a bird. Maggid Mishneh, regarding eggs in siman 86.
(Addendum) Seif 7 and seif 5, unless, etc. Saying that they are from a particular kosher fish does not suffice even when they are whole. The reason is that the eggs of every bird are known and recognizable, unlike fish eggs. Ran. Tosafot there, s.v. "Rava said," and Rosh write that when they are whole, such a statement does suffice. The Gemara therefore answers that they were crushed. See there, and see Ran there.
The Jew. Rambam, following his own approach, writes the same regarding bird eggs, as stated in siman 86, seif 1. He did not read in Chullin there, "One may purchase," etc., "from a non-Jew." He inferred from the expression used elsewhere that it includes even Syria and outside Eretz Yisrael, where one may not purchase from one who is not an established expert, as stated there in halachah 21 and at the end of chapter 11, and as stated in the Gemara at the end of chapter 2, 39b. With a non-Jew there is no distinction. The meaning is a Jew who is not suspected, but has not been established as trustworthy, as Maggid Mishneh writes there. This also appears from Rambam's wording there: "Likewise, one may not purchase cheese," etc. See below, siman 119, seif 1, in the gloss. "Expert" means a person established as trustworthy, as explained below, "unless he was," etc., and as Rambam and the Shulchan Aruch write in seif 8 regarding eggs. He writes this in place of "from an expert."
Or a non-Jew. So write all the authorities regarding eggs of kosher birds, as stated in the gloss to siman 86, seif 1, following the reading in our texts, "from non-Jews." This is also explicit there, for the Gemara asks, "Do we purchase such things from them?" etc.; and there, 116b, "Do we suspect," etc., "one may purchase," etc. Ran likewise raises this difficulty. See Lechem Mishneh, who was pressed regarding it. Ran explains that "in every place" means even where nonkosher birds are more common than kosher ones. See Tosafot in Chullin there, s.v. "Perhaps," etc.
Unless he was, etc. In Rashi's name, "expert" means knowledgeable regarding them. Ramban, however, explains it like every "expert" in this discussion, meaning trustworthy and established as reliable. The Yerushalmi likewise groups them together: One may not purchase fish intestines and entrails except on the word of an expert, and one may not purchase tekhelet except on the word of an expert. If he says, "I salted them," even a suspected person or a non-Jew is believed, as stated in Chullin 63b: The Sages taught, etc. Raavad writes that only with one who is not an expert does "I salted them" suffice. With a non-Jew or a suspected person, all agree that he must say, "These are the fish and these are their entrails," as stated there in Chullin regarding one who says, "They are from a particular bird," etc. Here the ruling is stated without qualification, following Ramban and Rashba, who hold that the same applies to a non-Jew and a suspected Jew. Rambam has another approach, that only a Jew's statement helps, but a non-Jew's statement never suffices.
(Addendum) A person established as trustworthy. Comparable to the "expert" discussed there regarding the blood of a menstruant woman and the blood of a wound. This also appears from the Yerushalmi: One may not purchase fish intestines and entrails except on the word of an expert, and one may not purchase tekhelet except on the word of an expert. This implies one comparable to the expert regarding tekhelet. The reason is that unlearned people were suspected of purchasing them from non-Jews under the presumption that they were kosher. Ran, unlike what appears from Rashi, who explains "expert" as one knowledgeable regarding them. See Rashi there, 63b, s.v. "These are fish," etc.
