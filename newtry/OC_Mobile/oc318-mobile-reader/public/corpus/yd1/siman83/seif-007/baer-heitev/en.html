From an expert. [See Peri Chadash.] Shach wrote in the name of Rav HaMaggid: and that which he did not suffice here with saying "from a certain fish" that we recognize as pure, as with fowl below siman 86 seif 1 — because salted fish come from a distant land and can evade more than fowl eggs. And he further wrote: from Mechaber's words it appears that even when not crushed it does not help when he says from a certain fish and they are pure; but all poskim hold that when whole it helps when he says from a certain fish and it is pure.
Non-Jew. Shach wrote: from Rama's words who wrote "or non-Jew" it appears even if crushed a non-Jew is believed — against all poskim; see there; and Taz wrote: truly a non-Jew is not believed except when whole; and the same if he says of a certain fowl that we recognize as pure he is believed, as with eggs in siman 86.
In kashrut. Meaning: then he is believed whether in fish innards or fish eggs, whole or crushed, even if he says nothing; but certainly if both heads are rounded or pointed, even one established in kashrut who says he removed it from a certain fish is not believed, for he contradicts the Torah.
