Meaning: then believed for fish innards, fish eggs, whole or crushed, even silent; but certainly if both heads rounded or pointed, even one established in kashrut saying he removed from a certain fish — not believed, contradicts Torah; plain in Gemara and poskim; Maharshal; Atzei Etz Chaim: without purity sign, only two rounded or pointed — even 'I salted them' not relied unless trusted and established in kashrut — both are impurity signs — great concern for lying or mistake; heaven forbid to doubt he lies — certainly lies; certainly one established in kashrut not believed in this:
And now the custom has spread. Beit Yosef: ancients investigated — no need for expert or 'I salted them' except to permit all fish eggs red or black; doubt only in black — impure species exist; to remove doubt they forbade all black, permitted all red even immediately from non-Jew without check — no impure has red eggs; I heard impure fish eggs red when salted turn black — impossible impure remain red after salting; Or Zarua general rule 42 section 4: do not rely now on fish egg signs — not expert; appears unlike Beit Yosef — Or Zarua would have explained black vs red; presumably custom not spread in his place — Mechaber wrote black not eaten at all, even in Israel's hand where black forbidden for removing doubt; Or Zarua: do not rely on signs now — when signs not needed, e.g. found in Israel's hand — permitted as we said:
