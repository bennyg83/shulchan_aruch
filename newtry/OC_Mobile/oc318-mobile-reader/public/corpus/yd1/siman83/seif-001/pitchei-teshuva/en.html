Or with a utensil. See responsum Noda BiYehudah second edition Yoreh De'ah siman 28 regarding a fish whose scales could not be peeled even with a utensil; he soaked it in ash water called lye about three hours and afterward they peeled by hand and he permitted it, for whatever peels by any action are called scales and it is a pure fish. See there siman 29; and according to one sage one student questioned this and Rav Shmuel Neru son of the author upheld his father's position and brought many proofs; and for this reason he permitted the fish called sturgeon and in Latin roe herring; he further wrote regarding the fish called tikk; he never saw it, but he says if it has scales, even if they peel only by soaking in ash water or boiling water, it is permitted without question; and it is further explained there one should not say we cannot permit because a prohibition was practiced on it; see at length. But in responsum Bigdei Kehunah Yoreh De'ah siman 4 he expanded to forbid sturgeon and tikk because scales do not peel even with a utensil; even though Noda BiYehudah demonstrated soaking in ash water and if afterward they peel it is permitted — nevertheless one must not rely on this test for a d'oraisa prohibition; and moreover since prohibition was practiced on these fish until now they cannot be permitted; at length; and perhaps if he saw Maharshal's words he would not have written thus. And see further in responsum Shivat Tzion there that a fish that comes before us with scales must be checked if they peel; although most fish with scales peel, since it can be clarified we clarify; and what is in the Gemara that Rav Ashi checked against the sun if there are scales and permitted — he should also have tested if they peel; because they were very small and great effort to clarify, he relied on majority. Poskim omitted this incident of Rav Ashi since such a fish is not found among us with scales so small; for us we always check if scales peel; see further in Noda BiYehudah there regarding fish in some places practiced to forbid and some to permit — whether one in a forbidden place may eat based on a permitting place depends on the dispute above siman 82 seif 5; but this is specifically fish without scales where some places permit based on tradition they shed scales in water; in a forbidding place it is forbidden here too since there are no scales before us — possible there are impure species resembling these; therefore those places practiced prohibition; but a fish that has scales — one cannot say prohibition was practiced because of a resembling impure fish without scales; one need not fear a rare impure fish with non-peeling scales; we do not concern for that; we say prohibition was practiced from old when they did not know scales peel; therefore the dispute above does not apply; see there.
