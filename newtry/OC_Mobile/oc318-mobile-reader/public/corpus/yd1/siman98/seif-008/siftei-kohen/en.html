Cheilev. See above siman 90 where the laws of cheilev are explained, for that is their place.
All prohibitions, etc. Both of Torah and of rabbinic origin — so too Tur and Rashba, unlike Rambam who holds rabbinic prohibitions are nullified in fifty-nine like cheilev; and Beit Yosef wrote it appears the view of all poskim is like Rashba, and so too plain in several places.
Except chametz on Pesach, etc. Tur's words: except chametz on Pesach and yayin nesech and tevel and orlah that we have no present concern with them — certainly orlah applies nowadays even in the Diaspora, as below siman 294; rather he means we have no concern with them in this place in the laws of mixtures; and so too Perishah, unlike Etz Yosef who wrote except tevel and orlah that we have no present concern with them, for they do not apply among us — end of his words; he was not precise. And that the Rav omitted orlah even though plain in Mishnah and all poskim, and likewise below siman 294 that the measure of orlah in min b'mino is two hundred — it is possible he stated and omitted, or he came only to say except chametz on Pesach and yayin nesech whose measure is in a minute amount.
But if it gave taste in the pot, etc., and therefore, etc. Meaning: that all prohibitions in the world are not ordinarily made for taste and are nullified in sixty, since they do not give taste in more than sixty except if its taste is sensed — then it is obvious it is forbidden, as Rosh, Rashba, and Ran wrote and Beit Yosef brings in this siman, and so too other poskim, and it is plain. And therefore since if its taste is sensed it is not nullified even in more than sixty, therefore salt and spices, etc., are not nullified even in a thousand, since ordinarily they give taste even in a thousand once they are made to give taste — for a thing made for taste is not nullified also because ordinarily it gives taste even in a thousand, as proven from the words of Sefer HaTerumah that Tur and Beit Yosef brought end of siman 105. And accordingly, where it is known clearly that it does not give taste — such as if a grain of salt or spice fell into a large cauldron — it is permitted; and so too it appears explicitly in Turei Chayim klal 85 law 20; and so I found (in Tosafot Avodah Zarah 66a) end of s.v. tavlin explicitly: "and that taste is not nullified — one need not say it is not nullified at all, but it is not nullified like other prohibitions, but it is certainly well nullified, for we know certainly that the taste was nullified, such as in a large cauldron" — end of his words; and so too in poskim.
Even in a thousand it is not nullified, etc. And Ohr HaChaim wrote klal 25 that this is d'rabbanan; and he wrote further there at the end of the klal: if it is not known whether its taste is sensed or not, one permits in such a case to a non-Jew mashe'as l'fi tumo — until here.
And therefore salt and spice, etc. See Turei Chayim klal 85 diyyud-dalet that he ruled even if salt and spice fell into something equal in name, nevertheless since it differs in taste it is not nullified; but if it fell into something equal in taste it is nullified in sixty — see there.
That are made for taste — if they are forbidden because of themselves. Even if they are forbidden only d'rabbanan they are not nullified — thus Bach proved below siman 112 from the Talmud; and so too it appears from Rashba's responsum that Beit Yosef brought end of siman 134, and from the words of Ohr HaChaim and the Rav below siman 114 seif 6, who wrote thus regarding stam wine that is only d'rabbanan — see there; and so too it appears from poskim in several places.
If they are forbidden because of themselves, etc. Such as spice of idolatry and of orlah and the like — to exclude absorbed [taste] from prohibition, as below end of siman 105.
