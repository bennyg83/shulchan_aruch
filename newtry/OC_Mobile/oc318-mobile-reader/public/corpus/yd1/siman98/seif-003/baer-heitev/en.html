Forbidden. Shach wrote: Explanation — at any rate we are expert that there is a majority in it, only we are not expert whether there is sixty in it, or we do not know how much emerged from it — then it is from rabbinic prohibitions; even so we do not rely to be lenient. The reason the poskim wrote: a doubt dependent on lack of knowledge is not called a doubt, for that is the opinion of fools; unlike if the doubt came because it was spilled. Ran wrote: because in a matter impossible to determine, if we go to leniency all prohibitions would be in doubt and each would estimate what appears to his eyes — therefore the Sages came to equalize their measures; but in what comes only incidentally, such as spilled and the like, we go to leniency. Taz challenged: how do we say here lack of knowledge is not a doubt, and in siman 81 we permit treifah cheeses through sircha, and likewise udder because if we were expert it would be possible to permit through examination — it would be a double doubt; plainly lack of expertise is a doubt. He resolved: regarding cheeses there is doubt for the whole world, and regarding udder there is another remedy to estimate against all of it; unlike here where in any case there is certainly prohibition, only we do not know if there is sixty there — therefore forbidden; see there at length (and Nachalat Binyamin resolved: here it is properly lack of wisdom since impossible to determine its measure; but there by law it is possible to examine in lukewarm water and by blowing, only we are stringent to say we are not expert — therefore it is a proper doubt — see there).
