And it is before us and it is impossible to determine it, etc. Explanation: that in any case we are expert that there is a majority in it, except we are not expert whether there is sixty in it or not; or that we do not know how much prohibition emerged from it — for then it is from rabbinic prohibitions — nevertheless we do not rely leniently, as we establish regarding cheilev above siman 90. And the reason the poskim wrote: a doubt that depends on lack of knowledge is not called doubt — that is the opinion of fools; unlike in the first case where the doubt comes because it spilled. And Ran wrote: because in a matter impossible to determine, if we followed leniency all prohibitions would be in doubt and each would estimate according to what appears to his eyes; and therefore the Sages came to equalize their measures. But in what comes only incidentally, such as when it spilled and the like, we follow leniency — until here.
