Or that it is not ben yomo. Explanation: that it was of meat and is not ben yomo, and one stirred in it an olive's bulk of milk — one cannot say chein nafsho, since the absorbed matter in it is noten taam for spoilage; therefore if afterward one stirred in it a pot of meat, one needs only sixty against the olive's bulk of milk, for regarding the vessel we do not say chein nafsho according to all, even if one stirred a prohibition in it, since the absorbed matter did not become nevelah — for certainly it is not relevant to say the body of wood or metal or earthenware becomes nevelah. But if one stirred with a spoon afterward milk, it is obvious it is permitted b'dieved, for the meat was taken by hand; but an old spoon of meat and ben yomo in which one stirred an olive's bulk of milk, or the reverse, and the absorbed matter in it became nevelah — whether one stirred afterward in a pot of meat or of milk or of other things — sixty is required to nullify all that stuck from the spoon into the pot, for we do not know how much it absorbed. But if we know how much it absorbed, one need not estimate except against the meat and the milk — such as if one stirred in it an olive's bulk of meat and afterward on its day an olive's bulk of milk, one needs only sixty against the two olive's bulks.

And some do not distinguish, etc. What is written here in Shulchan Aruch — Rashba in Taharat HaBayit in the name of Raavad — is an error, for Rashba in the name of Raavad did not write thus; rather regarding vessels that can be kashered by hag'alah, even if we do not know how much was absorbed, one need not estimate against the whole but according to a good estimate of what emerges from it, as written in s.k. 12. But he does not deal at all with the law of nevelah; rather "some distinguish" is what Turei Chayim wrote at the beginning of klal 85 in the name of Mordechai, who wrote that in a thing that has hag'alah we do not say chein nafsho in it, since it is possible to separate the prohibition from there by hag'alah; and nevertheless it appears he says this specifically regarding an old vessel, but in a new vessel, even of earthenware, it certainly cannot enter the mind that the body of the earthenware becomes nevelah; and so too Maharshal in Avodat HaKodesh and in his book chapter 3 siman 36; and Bach challenged the Rav on this in what he wrote that a new earthenware vessel [gets] nevelah; and nevertheless it appears in an old one one should be stringent like those poskim, and Mechaber includes them that nevelah applies even in a thing that has heter through hag'alah, and to say that presumably now the absorbed matter in it became nevelah; but with great loss and the like one may be lenient like poskim who say regarding wood and metal we do not say nevelah, and in Maharshal's words (and Ohr HaChaim), and so too Bach; see above siman 94 seif 2 and end of siman 107.
In an earthenware vessel, etc., nevelah.
