Because we hold ta'am ke'ikar d'oraisa; and see in responsa Noda B'Yehudah Tinyana Orach Chayim siman 66 who wrote: since ta'am ke'ikar d'oraisa we derive from maseres or from purging of gentile vessels, we say specifically in issurim like them that are lavin with lashes, but not a lav without lashes such as impure milk and the like — see there; and see what he wrote below siman 118 s.k. 1; and see in Shach what he wondered on the Rav in Turei Chayim; and see in responsa Shamayim Rosh siman 144 in Hagahot Kesef HaRasna there what he wrote on this. [And what he wondered in Kuntres Pesach s.k. 2 on Rashba's words in Chullin regarding inserting a milking spoon into a meat cauldron and it spilled — see in responsa Chatam Sofer siman 86 who wrote to resolve well; and see there further that a veteran student wished to innovate for those who hold ta'am ke'ikar is not d'oraisa, but in basar bechalav it is d'oraisa since the Merciful One expressed it in language of cooking — if so one may say this is specifically when the milk received taste from the substance of the kid, but taste of the kid absorbed in the pot is not ta'am ke'ikar from Torah to forbid the milk (also Peri Megadim in introduction to laws of basar bechalav was uncertain on this — see there; and accordingly in my humble opinion resolved the world's difficulty, and it was also mentioned in Noda B'Yehudah siman 83 how we derive ta'am ke'ikar from gentile purging — see there — investigate) — and he z"l wrote that this reasoning may be said also for us who hold ta'am ke'ikar d'oraisa, nevertheless in basar bechalav it is only when it received taste from the kid itself; but he showed him the contradiction of this distinction from Gemara chapter Elu Overin — see there.]
