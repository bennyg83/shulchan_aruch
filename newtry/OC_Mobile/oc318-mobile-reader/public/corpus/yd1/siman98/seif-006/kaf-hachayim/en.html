6) However, if the issur and heter were equal in weight — such as the issur was meat without bones and the heter too was meat without bones — it is better to estimate by weight, for then the measure comes out more precisely. Perishah there. And so too in responsa Chinuch Beit Yehudah siman 33: if issur and heter are equal in weight — that is, of one species where one is not naturally heavier than its fellow, and there is no hollow or cavity greater than its fellow — plainly we may estimate in both either by measurement or weight; however if one portion has a cavity greater than its fellow, then if estimated by measurement one must compress the cavity — we say weighing is preferable, lest perhaps the cavity was not compressed well; and in two species it is impossible to estimate by weight, and even if there is a cavity one must compress the cavity — see there. Pri Tevuah there. Zivchei Tzedek sec. 4. Ben Avraham Parashat Korach sec. 2.
