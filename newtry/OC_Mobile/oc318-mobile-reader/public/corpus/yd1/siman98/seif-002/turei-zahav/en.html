If its kind became mixed. The conclusion of the poskim: its kind mixed in its kind is nullified in the majority by Torah law, as it says "follow the majority"; but the Sages required sixty in it; therefore where there is doubt such as when spilled we go to leniency and say there was sixty; but not its kind in its kind requires sixty by Torah law since they sense forbidden taste in permitted food — therefore in a case of doubt we go to stringency.
And regarding its kind in its kind. See Beit Yosef in the passage beginning "I found written," etc. — explained in detail what is called its kind or not its kind.
And if it became mixed in its kind, etc. Rashba derives it from that which we say in the Gemara according to R' Yehuda who holds its kind in its kind is not nullified — if mixed in its kind and not its kind, remove its kind as if it were not and what is not its kind is majority and nullifies it; likewise here for us, since the opposite — its kind in its kind is lighter by Torah law than mixture not in its kind — if mixed in its kind and not its kind, remove what is not its kind as if it were not and its kind is majority and nullifies it; and if it had not spilled and the mixtures were still before us, rabbinic prohibition would remain, for rabbinically majority does not suffice; but now that it spilled and we can say there was sixty in its kind against the prohibition we go to leniency; and therefore it must be known and clear to us the majority was from its permitted kind against the prohibition so there not be concern of Torah prohibition; and the language "we rely to say its kind was majority," etc., that Tur wrote — Beit Yosef already resolved see there; and Rashal Pesachim chapter 46 siman 46 wrote on this Rashba that he disagrees — and these are his words: it seems to me this is not a comparison at all; there regarding its kind in its kind in the Gemara there is no prohibition from imparting taste but the decree of Scripture for R' Yehuda that it is not nullified in its kind — therefore when there is also not its kind in its kind with it they say remove its kind so taste in not its kind be nullified and automatically all be permitted; but here what forbids not its kind through taste by Torah law? If so when the gravy spilled, even if its kind is also with it, in the end it still stands in doubt and Torah doubt is to stringency — end of his words. In my humble opinion Rashba's words appear correct and clear, for where we say in the Gemara remove its kind, etc. — that is such as slaughtered meat and nevelah meat and gravy, and there is sixty in the gravy against the nevelah meat alone, and we could have said nevertheless slaughtered meat should be forbidden since it is its kind with the prohibition and in its kind nullification in sixty does not help — even so we do not say so; rather since in slaughtered meat there is no prohibition except from the mixture, now they say remove its permitted kind as if it were not and the mixture does not harm it since the prohibition was nullified in sixty in the gravy — the prohibition is as one who does not exist and there is not even absorption in any amount; if so it is exactly the same for us that its kind is nullified in majority by Torah law and not its kind in sixty for all, and slaughtered meat mixed with nevelah and gravy and there is no sixty to nullify the nevelah — there is permitted food to nullify the prohibition in slaughtered meat since it is majority against it and in its kind majority helps by Torah law; only the gravy should be forbidden since there is no sixty against the prohibition — it gives taste in it; therefore they say we consider the gravy as if it did not mix with the nevelah since the nevelah was already nullified through abundance of its kind and is as one who does not exist, and the prohibition is considered fully permitted — so it is by Torah law, only the Sages were stringent to require sixty even in its kind; therefore here where there is doubt whether there was sixty in not its kind against the prohibition and impossible to determine since spilled — permitted, for we abandon it to Torah law that its kind nullifies in majority and sixty is not needed since possible there was sixty in not its kind — all this is correct without doubt; see what he wrote in siman 109 seif 1 from this. And a plain matter that here it deals only when mixed once in its kind and not its kind; but if it first mixed with not its kind and afterward its kind was added there, certainly already nevelah before its kind came — so it appears to me for practical halachah. And further there is practical difference between in its kind and not its kind regarding dry mixture in dry in siman 110 below. Turei Chayim wrote general rule 85 in the name of Issur VeHeter: even if milk fell on fowl meat which is rabbinic and spilled — forbidden since taste is like substance by Torah law. It appears very astounding his words — what does it help that taste is like substance, even if the substance itself is only rabbinic according to what applies now that fowl in milk is rabbinic; and in truth Issur VeHeter did not write here that it is rabbinic as Turei Chayim cited from him — found he wrote this for one who holds fowl meat is Torah law, and so explicitly general rule 40; nevertheless there is no hold on Rama — perhaps in his manuscript of Issur VeHeter it was written so, as we find in Rama's responsa at their end that he wrote in another matter in his manuscript of Issur VeHeter it was not written as in printed editions; requires study in what he wrote "since taste is like substance by Torah law" — there is nothing in this; but it appears Rama says well since we find in meat in milk the Sages strengthened their words like Torah itself, such as eating cheese after meat as Rama wrote beginning siman 89 seif 1, and likewise seif 2 "some are stringent," and Rama brings, and end of chapter 8 regarding bringing it to the table — and it is not considered decree on decree, and the source from what is in the Gemara in this in Pesachim that decree on decree is here like one decree; therefore also here proper to be stringent with fowl as decree lest spilled with animal meat, unlike its kind in its kind and spilled where there is no decree lest Torah prohibition — therefore we go there to leniency when majority is permitted.
