If a species became mixed in its species. The reason Rabbenu Tam wrote, and all poskim who stand in his method that taam ka'ikar is forbidden d'oraisa in min she'eino mino: since the prohibition gives taste in the heter, the heter becomes entirely prohibition and all of it is forbidden d'oraisa. But min b'mino, since it does not give taste in it, is nullified from the Torah in the majority, as written "follow the majority" — except that the Sages required sixty; and therefore when it is known that its majority was heter and the doubt was only whether there is sixty there or not, it is a rabbinic doubt and we rule leniently; unlike when it was not known that its majority was heter, which is a Torah doubt and forbidden. But min she'eino mino, since it is forbidden from the Torah up to sixty, once it gives taste — even if it is known that its majority was heter and it is not known that there was sixty — it is a Torah doubt and forbidden. In view of this I wonder at what the Rav wrote: "and regarding min b'mino we follow the name," etc. — his view is that if it is equal in name even though not equal in taste, it is min b'mino and forbidden d'rabbanan; and if it is not equal in name even though equal in taste, it is min she'eino mino and forbidden from the Torah; and so too from the words of Maharshal in Avodat HaKodesh and in his book Pischei HaGolah end of siman 32. And behold it is explained in all poskim that therefore min she'eino mino is forbidden from the Torah up to sixty since it gives taste; and therefore min b'mino, which does not give taste, is nullified from the Torah in the majority as we follow the majority everywhere. If so the opposite is true — we all follow taste: if it is not equal in taste even though equal in name, nevertheless since it gives taste in heter and the heter becomes entirely prohibition, it is forbidden from the Torah up to sixty; and therefore in a spill we follow stringency. And if it is equal in taste even though not equal in name, nevertheless since it does not give taste it is nullified from the Torah as we follow the majority everywhere, as written "follow the majority" — and therefore in a spill we follow leniency. And even though we establish like Rava in the last chapter of Avodah Zarah 66 regarding new wine that we follow name and not taste regarding min b'mino — that is specifically regarding yayin nesech and tevel that forbid in a minute amount in min b'mino, and likewise regarding terumah and orlah whose measure is above sixty in its species; and therefore if it is equal in name even though not equal in taste it is min b'mino and forbidden in a minute amount or in 101 or 201; and if it is not equal in name even though equal in taste it is forbidden b'noten taam; and likewise according to R' Yehuda who said in all prohibitions min b'mino in a minute amount — and therefore in such a case there is no longer [reason] because of taam ka'ikar or "follow the majority." And so it is explicitly in Tosafot Pesachim 97a s.v. Amar Rava, etc., and in Ashiri, Raavan, and Ran at the end of Avodah Zarah, and all poskim — that this [sugya] of Rava deals with yayin nesech and tevel and their kind; if so one may say specifically in those, but not for us in other prohibitions, and regarding a spill (and these words reached several great ones of the generation and they agreed with my words, also Mahar Yehoshua said they are clear matters). And this is also proven in the words of Maharai in Taharat HaBayit that I brought in s.k. 2 — for otherwise it would not need to be said that Rashba holds like Rashi — see there and investigate. And this is also proven explicitly in Rashba's novellae 109 and in the long Taharat HaBayit 102–103 — see there; and it is further proven in several proofs for one who examines and is precise in the words of Tosafot, Rosh, Rashba, Ran, and Raavan's responsum that argues with R' Efraim, printed at the end of Sefer Raavan, and all other poskim; and this is also proven in the explanation of Rosh's words in Pesachim in what he wrote "and also min b'mino," etc., even though it does not give taste — for the Sages decreed min b'mino on account of min she'eino mino, and he brings it in Beit Yosef siman 109; and this is also proven in Rosh's explanation at the end of the laws of challah — see there; and so too it appears explicitly from poskim I will bring below regarding dry [mixture] not in its species that sixty is required because if they cook them it will give taste and it is Torah prohibition — see there; and this is clear in my view. See also s.k. 8; and even though Agur and some other Acharonim also wrote in the name of Or Zarua this [sugya] of Rava that we follow name and not taste — it is possible they wrote thus for Rashi and his supporters who ruled like R' Yehuda that min b'mino is not nullified, and so too it appears at first glance in Agur — see there. Or alternatively there is relevance regarding yayin nesech as below siman 134, and likewise regarding other offerings of idolatry and their accessories that are forbidden in a minute amount in their species; or alternatively regarding a thing that has a way to permit it that is not nullified in its species — we follow name and not taste, as written below siman 102 s.k. 3 (or it is possible he holds there is relevance regarding dry in dry, and holds the reason that dry in dry in its species is not nullified, as Ohr HaChaim wrote below). And therefore in all those cases there is no longer [reason] because of taam ka'ikar or "follow the majority"; but regarding other prohibitions that are forbidden in sixty, and regarding a spill whether d'oraisa or d'rabbanan — certainly everything depends on taste, as we said; also Bach and Etz Yosef followed the Rav's words. In my humble opinion, as I wrote, again I found in Ohr HaChaim end of klal 23 that he expanded in the name of Or Zarua on the laws of what is called min b'mino in name, and we follow name and not taste; and he wrote on this at the end: "and we establish that even min b'mino in a moist thing [requires] sixty as stated above — there is no relevance except in a dry prohibition that became mixed in heter and the mixture was known before cooking, that it is in its species in the majority and not in its species in sixty" — end of his words. And there in that klal he wrote explicitly that we establish like Rabbenu Tam that min b'mino [requires] sixty d'rabbanan and not in its species sixty d'oraisa, and there is relevance if it spilled — end of his words. Behold it is proven explicitly that he holds as I wrote; except that what he wrote that there is relevance regarding dry in dry, etc., he follows his view that he wrote there: there is no reason that dry in dry in its species is nullified in the majority and not in its species in sixty, because min b'mino does not give taste when they will be cooked, but not in its species gives taste when they will be cooked — if so it would be permitted in any case: if the mixture was known before cooking it would be permitted even to cook them together alone; and if he did not know until they were cooked, then sixty is required because it is moist prohibition. Rather the reason that not in its species, even in a dry thing that does not give taste, is stricter, because it is called a thing that has a remedy, for one can somewhat recognize the prohibition and remove it from there; and nevertheless it is well nullified in sixty, for since it is also minute and one does not find it, it is well called a thing that has no remedy. But in truth his reason is forced, and the reason is clear and explained in the words of Rosh in Pesachim and Ran there, and in Rashba's novellae there 105 at the top, and in Tur s.k. 9 in the name of Sefer HaTerumah and Rokeach siman 479, and in Maharil's responsum siman 194 and Ri"d, that min she'eino mino in dry [mixture] requires sixty for we are concerned lest they cook them and it will give taste and it is Torah prohibition; and even if the mixture was known before cooking, nevertheless since it gives taste afterward it is forbidden from the Torah, as stated there (and from these poskim it is also proven that we follow taste — investigate). And so too the Rav's view below siman 109 and all other Acharonim, unlike min b'mino; and therefore since the matter depends on taste, also in dry we follow taste — and all this is clear in my humble opinion.
But if it became mixed in what is not its species, etc. It is written in Turei Chayim klal 85 diyyud-gimmel in the name of Ohr HaChaim klal 37 that even if milk fell into fowl meat, which is d'rabbanan, nevertheless if it spilled it is forbidden, since taam ka'ikar is d'oraisa; and so too in his simanim. And this is astounding, for nevertheless even the essence of meat in milk of fowl itself is only d'rabbanan; if so it is a rabbinic doubt and we rule leniently — and this is proven explicitly from what Rashba and Ran and other poskim brought as proof for the prohibition before us; and it is impossible to determine its measure, for we do not rely leniently from cheilev whose prohibition is d'rabbanan, cheilev of a slaughtered animal being d'rabbanan — see there and investigate, and see s.k. 9. And likewise below siman 111 they say: whether in rabbinic prohibition or in rabbinic measure we rely leniently; but one who examines Ohr HaChaim will see the Rav did not aim well, for he wrote there that Taz: if the water in the pot spilled after the prohibition fell, and there is doubt whether there was sixty there initially or not — he wrote in Semak that the custom is like Rabbenu Tam that taam ka'ikar is d'oraisa and we judge its doubt stringently — end of his words; and even if milk fell into a dish of fowl meat there is no distinction — end of his words; meaning there is no distinction, he follows his view that he wrote in klal 40 diyyud-vav that fowl meat in milk is d'oraisa and there is no distinction between fowl meat and animal in all matters — end of his words; and so too in several places. But according to what we establish in the beginning of siman 87 that fowl meat in milk is d'rabbanan, it is obvious we rely leniently here — and this is clear.
And if it became mixed in its species and in what is not its species, etc. In truth this law requires investigation, for Tur in the name of Rashba derived this law from that which they say: we view its species as if it were not, to be lenient, and what is not its species overwhelms it and nullifies it — for one who holds min b'mino is not nullified — end of his words (and see above siman 134 seif 4). And it is not similar at all, for there in its species it is not forbidden b'noten taam, therefore they say we view its species as if it were not, for it was nullified in what is not its species; but here how can we say we view what is not its species as if it were not? After all, now it gives taste in what is not its species, and if so it is a Torah doubt, lest there was not sixty and it gave taste. And Maharshal challenged likewise in Avodat HaKodesh siman 85 and in his book Pischei HaGolah siman 46 in the fourth law — see there; and what Bach wrote here is difficult, for nevertheless whence do we produce heter, since it fell entirely? And there is a correct reason to distinguish between them — investigate. And it appears to me that Rashba and those who follow him said only to permit its species, and to exclude that we should not say: since now it fell in what is not its species, therefore it is a Torah doubt; rather they say we view what is not its species as if it were not, and the remainder of its species overwhelms it and nullifies it — and this is that he was precise in the wording "and the remainder of its species overwhelms it," etc., for he should have said "and its species overwhelms it" — except that because what is not its species is forbidden he says that its remaining species overwhelms it.
