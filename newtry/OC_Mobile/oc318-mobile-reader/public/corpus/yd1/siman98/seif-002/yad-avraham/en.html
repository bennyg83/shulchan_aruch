(There) If it is min b'mino — such as milk mixed with fat, as the Tur wrote. Even though {Rama: the Rama wrote in the hagahah to seif 2 that for min b'mino we follow shem,} nevertheless milk and fat share one shem, for fat is also called milk, and "fat" is only rabbinic terminology. For example, regarding two pieces, one of milk and one of fat: one must call it fat relative to milk, from which it is taught. So too Rashi in Pesachim (45) regarding what is said there about milk on the walls of the lung, which is a fat canal connected to the heart, and is included in the fat of the heart that is called milk — as the Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Matza Katuv; and so too in Rashi and Tosafot in Bechorot (10) — see there; and in Sefer Maamar Mordechai he likewise wrote so from his own reasoning, and did not mention the Rashi in Pesachim that I mentioned. The Beit Yosef also wrote that the fat of the ribs, the breast, and the fatty tail are called b'sar shuman and not milk. Regarding what he wrote that the fat of the ribs is mino with the meat, I already wrote above nearby a proof for this from the Gemara in Keitzad (108). Regarding what he wrote that the milk of the fatty tail is not called milk — a proof for this appears from the Gemara in Kol HaBasar (117) and in the first chapter of Keritut (4), where they say that the fatty tail is called milk, but stam it is not called milk. See the Rambam in chapter 7 of Hilchot Ma'achalot Assurot, which the Beit Yosef brings in siman 64: the fatty tail is not called milk except for the matter of a korban — see there. The Peri Chadash disagrees with the Beit Yosef and wrote that plainly the fatty tail is like fat with milk. According to what was said above, one may say that Rashi in Pesachim holds that fat is called milk, but the fatty tail is not called milk; and see what I wrote in siman 101. The Peri Chadash wrote that even when they are divided in shem they are not two kinds if they are truly one species; and he brought proof from the mishna regarding spices: two or three names from one species combine. And likewise sourdough and dough from one species, even though they are divided in shem, are one species. He wrote that the Rambam ruled so, unlike the Mordechai. With this it appears to me that the difficulty of the Rashba is resolved — what Tosafot wrote in Bava Batra (95): let us say that according to Rava too, smell is nullified in smell, and nevertheless it is nullified in smell because smell is called min b'mino, and he follows shem. According to the Peri Chadash's view, one may say this; and there is a nafka mina.
