Such as milk, etc. In brine — grease in meat is not its kind in its kind — so too Beit Yosef below in this siman in the name of Agur, and Rashal in the name of Rosh, and Or Zarua in chapter Gid HaNasheh siman 31.
We have a non-Jew taste it. Darkei Moshe wrote from that which is forbidden to an Israelite to taste it one learns that sometimes one buys meat from the butcher and it is unknown whether it is salted or not — forbidden to taste with his tongue — until here is his language; and it is very astounding, for in siman 42 regarding marah he wrote explicitly that they taste with the tongue; therefore it appears regarding tasting with the tongue there is no concern of prohibition in a case of doubt, and therefore he mentioned explicitly regarding marah that he taste with the tongue as he wrote there Beit Yosef in the name of Mordechai — and likewise permitted if clean of blood there; and this case is different here because he needed to taste through actual eating in order to sense whether there is taste there — therefore a non-Jew must taste it; and regarding fast in Orach Chaim siman 567 it is plainly explained that there is no prohibition in tasting with the tongue alone, for there only tasting to take into the mouth is mentioned — in this there is a distinction between fast and other prohibitions. And this "we have a non-Jew taste it" implies even not an expert, for Beit Yosef deduced from Ra'avad's language that he wrote non-Jew and did not mention expert; if so the essence of the permission is through mesiach lefi tumo as explained in Beit Yosef in the name of Rashba; and in Bedek HaBayit Ra'ah challenged this that mesiach lefi tumo is believed only for testimony of a woman which is only revealing a matter in passing, etc.; and this difficulty Rashba also challenged in Terumat HaDeshen siman 79 on Rashba and resolved that Rashba holds taste is like substance is not Torah law and in rabbinic matters mesiach lefi tumo helps; and in truth Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Terumat HaDeshen HaArukh that he agrees with Rabbeinu Tam who holds it is Torah law — perhaps he does not agree with them except regarding the essence of the law, but regarding mesiach lefi tumo he properly relied on Rashi and Rambam who hold taste is like substance is rabbinic; and perhaps for this Beit Yosef intended when he wrote in this siman "here we rely on Rambam and Rashba that a non-Jew mesiach lefi tumo helps even if not expert"; but in truth we find such dispute also in Piskei Maharai siman 130 regarding firstborn that he wrote for Rosh mesiach lefi tumo helps when he does not improve his purchase, but he challenged him that it only helps in testimony of a woman as in chapter HaGozel — it appears to me Rashba and Rosh hold that where we say in HaGozel mesiach lefi tumo is not believed in Torah matters — that is where full testimony is required specifically; unlike issur and heter where it helps well, for there we do not require full testimony but if we have proof it suffices — know that a non-Jew is believed in prohibitions to permit; and if so likewise mesiach lefi tumo, for from this we have reasoning there is no prohibition and that suffices; and proof that an expert helps even when not mesiach lefi tumo because he does not ruin his presumption; but where testimony is required certainly such a thing does not help — until here in prohibitions one needs only proof — thus I resolve Rashba and Rosh's view; but for halachah Tur and Shulchan Aruch already ruled in siman 116 regarding firstborn that mesiach lefi tumo does not help, and if so it does not help anywhere there is Torah prohibition. In Tur it is written and likewise wrote "some say" Rosh that one needs, etc. — that he did not write stam; and likewise wrote "some say" Rosh — Beit Yosef explained meaning there is no decision in his words whether one needs specifically an expert. Requires study — behold Rosh wrote expert gentile baker implies we require specifically an expert, for otherwise why write this, for Rosh's way is not to write except what emerges for law; therefore it appears the opposite — certainly Rosh holds like "some say" brought before that one needs specifically an expert. But there is one novelty in Rosh's words that implies if the non-Jew knows we rely on him in another matter not regarding issur and heter it is called lefi tumo, which is not so in other lefi tumo such as testimony of a woman — this does not help; therefore he wrote in the name of Rosh that this too helps here.
