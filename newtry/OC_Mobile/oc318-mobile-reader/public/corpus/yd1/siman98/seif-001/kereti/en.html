Milk mixed with meat, etc. — from this that tasting helps, it appears milk in meat is called not its species regarding tasting; see Peleti where I expanded on pepper whether it is called its species or not.
"They shall taste it for us" — it appears even when he is not an expert, only a gentile with maslita; and also with an expert, maslita applies, for even though he must be without knowledge since it depends on his statement of issur/heter — if he knows it is not maslita — nevertheless it applies that he does not harm his trustworthiness, for they told him that they rely on his word whether this piece has milk taste or not — behold he does not harm his trustworthiness; and nevertheless he does not know the nafka mina of issur/heter; see Peleti.
To say — even though it is a Torah matter, and I raised: if after the gentile says there is no milk taste and eats it on that basis, and a Jew senses milk taste and refrains from eating — if so all reliance on the gentile is the tasting and this is rabbinic, and we rely on a gentile with maslita — see there; and the later authorities explain that it is like a matter made to reveal — forced.
And if there is not etc. — Mechaber follows Rambam that when there is an expert, even though we know there is sixty in it, it does not help if he says he senses taste — forbidden; and if he says he does not sense taste, even without sixty — permitted.
Now regarding reliance etc.; but on Jewish tasting, such as vows and the like, we rely nowadays.
