Sixty is required against the whole pot. Explanation: and if so the dish in the pot is forbidden, for what is inside the vessel is not sixty against the vessel; see siman 93 s.k. 1.
Whether they are of earthenware, etc. Meaning: and to exclude the view of Raavad who holds that in wood and metal, since it is possible to separate the prohibition by hag'alah, we do not estimate the whole but estimate well how much emerged from it — but there is no distinction.
But through boiling of something salted, etc. And it is written in Turei Chayim beginning of klal 85 in the name of Ohr HaChaim that likewise if the vessel became forbidden through pouring, one need not estimate except the measure of a peel; but if it became forbidden through pickling, one must estimate against the whole — end of his words; see siman 69 s.k. 60 and siman 91 seif 5 in hagahah 3 and what is written there.
In a vessel. Specifically in a vessel; but in a food item we establish that all of it becomes forbidden, as above siman 91 seif 5 and siman 105 seif 9.
And the custom is like the first view. As explained at the end of siman 94; see there.
And therefore if milk fell, etc., and its taste was nullified in sixty, etc. It appears that where there is no sixty, adding cold water does not help; and so too Turei Chayim klal 85 diyyud-zayin explicitly, and so too Ohr HaChaim klal 23 end of diyyud-aleph in the name of Raavan; and all poskim did not mention permission of adding cold water where there is no sixty — rather certainly, since there is no sixty, even though one adds cold water, nevertheless from the outset the taste of the prohibition was emitted and the taste was not nullified in what removes the fat afterward; and it is no better than if a piece of prohibition fell even though one recognizes it and it is whole and one threw it in — sixty is required against all of it; and as Turei Zahav and all poskim wrote, unlike Bach who relied on Or Zarua's hagahah to permit b'dieved through adding cold water even where there is no sixty; see above siman 104.
