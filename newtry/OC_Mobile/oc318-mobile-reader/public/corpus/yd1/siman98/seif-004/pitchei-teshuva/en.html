After name. Avodat HaTaharah [and see Peri Megadim in introduction ch. 3 sec. 4 regarding fowl meat and animal, ram and lamb, etc., whether they are called not its species; and see in responsa Chavot Yair siman 87 and end of seif 93 what he wrote on this]. And see in Sefer Teivat Gome authored by the author of Peri Megadim daf 78 who wrote that blood and milk are not equal in taste, even though blood coagulates and becomes like milk [requires study, for so all in Niddah 9, but we do not hold so as explained below siman 184 s.k. 7 and siman 189 s.l. 33, as Nachalat Tzvi wrote above siman 81; and see Magen Avraham siman 295 s.k. 3 and siman 494 s.k. 6] — nevertheless now it is not blood taste, and proof is clear from Chullin 111: a woman said, she forbade us blood, she permitted us liver; and Rashi explained: blood taste is in it, and she did not say she permitted us milk — learn that there is no blood taste in it. And see in responsa Beit Efrayim ch. 14 siman 30 regarding a piece of meat that fell into a dairy cauldron and was removed immediately and the meat was lost and impossible to determine its measure, and the questioner says that according to his estimate it was sixty but he does not know clearly; and he wrote there are several sides to be lenient: Peri Chadash's view that all basar bechalav that was not cooked as food of a court has no Torah issur; and also since he says it was a small piece, likely there is sixty — it is like heter that is common; therefore there is room to permit through tasting of an expert with maslita, and if there is no expert, a Jew may taste a mere tasting, and if he does not sense meat taste — permitted to eat afterward; and one who wishes to be stringent also in this regarding eating, at least for benefit — permitted plainly — see there.
