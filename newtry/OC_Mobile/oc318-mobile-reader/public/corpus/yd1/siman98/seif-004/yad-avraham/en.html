(Seif 2) If min b'mino became mixed and was spilled — if it is known that its majority was heter, it is permitted. In Tosafot, the Rosh in chapter 3, and at the beginning of the siman, it is explained that this is when the taste mixed and not the substance, or even the substance of the issur is present but not recognizable; whereas if it is recognizable, it must be discarded, as {Rama: the Rama wrote in the hagahah to seif 4.} Accordingly, it must be said that it spilled after it cooled, for if it spilled while still boiling, even if there had certainly been sixty, it cannot be permitted, since now there is no sixty and the issur is present in the pot and forbids at present — as the Turei Yeshanim wrote at the end of seif 106, and the Beit Yosef at the end of seif 100 in the name of the Rashba. And so too explicitly in siman 99 seif 4. See the Minchat Yitzchak in general rule 85: when it is impossible l'chatchila to know how much of the issur fell, this is not called "sixty through lack of knowledge"; and if it is known that the majority was heter, it is permitted in mino. If so, the same applies to a d'rabbanan issur in she'eino mino; and so too in the responsa Emunat Shmuel siman 28, and in the Peri Chadash.
