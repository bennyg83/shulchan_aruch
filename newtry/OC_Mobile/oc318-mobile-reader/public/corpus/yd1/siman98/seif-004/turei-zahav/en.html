Therefore if milk fell into the dish, etc. It implies in less than sixty this remedy does not help, and to say the prohibition is a separate thing in itself; Issur VeHeter also ruled thus; Turei Chayim general rule 83 brings it; but in Hagahat Issur VeHeter at end of the book gate 24 siman 10 wrote in the name of Mordechai Pesachim chapter 7 this remedy even when there is not sixty in the meat — permitted because milk floats above; he wrote the reason milk does not mix in gravy but floats above, and whatever can be fully separated we do not say the piece itself is nevelah, etc.; and so Mordechai implies no distinction between there is sixty and there is not sixty in the remedy — end of his words; and in Beit Chadash Maharach ruled for this reason b'dieved permitted even if there is not sixty. In my humble opinion it appears not to be lenient at all in this — what this hagahah wrote in the name of Mordechai is plainly not explicit in his Mordechai, for afterward he concludes so Mordechai implies from not distinguishing between there is sixty, etc.; and the matters explain themselves there is no taste and smell for this leniency, for even in a vessel we require sixty against it if one inserts spoon of meat into milk pot and we see spoon as at first — even so we say taste emerged; likewise milk into permitted food even though afterward floated above, even if we know it has measure as at first — nevertheless its taste was emitted and water entered its place; simply we do not say this remedy that milk floats above except stringently when there is sixty; but when there is not sixty certainly the piece itself is nevelah and it does not help afterward that it floats above — nevertheless taste remains there as Turei Chayim wrote; and here in hagahah.
