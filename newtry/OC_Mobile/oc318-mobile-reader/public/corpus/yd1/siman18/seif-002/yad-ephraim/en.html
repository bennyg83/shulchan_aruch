Avodat HaGolah; and he wrote in Shevut Yaakov part 2 siman 53: one who slaughtered with checked knife and flaw found, and certainly he did not touch joint-bone, for even simanim were not slaughtered and flesh of joint-bone intact on all sides; and he brought from Tur and Beit Yosef, Choshen Mishpat siman 306 in the name of Rashba: Rashba who exempts from paying deals with the animal-owner's knife — then if flawed in hide the butcher is not negligent if he did not check before shechitah; not so when the knife is the slaughterer's — then he was negligent for not having a good knife that will not flaw on animal's hide; and Tur deals with this, and there is no dispute — see there; and Shevut Yaakov siman 53: if we know the knife touched joint-bone — if there is still room to be lenient, such as flaw not as great as catching fingernail — there is a double doubt: perhaps law is like Rosh, etc.; perhaps on bone flawed; and flaw not so great — declare fit; also an incident: slaughterer heard knife sound on joint-bone like "krek" in Ashkenaz — recognizable thereby flaw came from there, and he declared fit; in such case all agree we say on bone flawed since legs to matter — see there; and he wrote in place of Shmuel siman 33: knife found flawed after shechitah — flaw that does not catch fingernail; they checked joint-bone and large cut found in joint-bone in depth of bone — declare fit, attribute to joint-bone, as appears from Tosafot Chullin daf 10 and Mordechai and Rosh and Beit Yosef in what he wrote to settle Tur's words in the name of Baal HaItur; and so other poskim; and so in Shechitot Mahara of Prague: large flaw found on knife and the three great men commanded to look after joint-bone and cut found — declared fit; and specifically cut in bone itself; but in white surrounding bone, which is soft — we do not attribute, for there the knife was flawed — end. And all the more in our case where it did not catch fingernail — see there.
And in my book Beit Efrayim, second part, in manuscript, I wrote regarding a slaughterer who checked the knife after slaughtering one animal and the knife was found with a large nick; and the slaughterer said that after the shechitah he felt in his hand that he had struck with the knife forcefully against the joint-bone, not in the manner of drawing back and forth. I answered that if a cut is found in the joint-bone, then there are grounds to attribute that he cut there; and since it was forcefully and not in the manner of drawing back and forth, one may attribute the nick to this and rely on Shevut Yaakov part 2 regarding one who heard a "krek" sound, etc. Also, even without this, Rivatz's view is to permit where a cut is found. But if no cut is found, then from the forceful contact alone one should not permit. See responsum Zaken Aharon siman 24 regarding one who checked the knife and slaughtered a chicken, and after he slaughtered properly, the wings of the chicken, whose ends were cut, slipped and struck forcefully against the knife during death-throes. He wrote that there is no doubt it struck its edge, for the wings whose feathers were cut off, leaving the hard bones, struck the knife from all sides; and it is known that a chicken's death-throes are very strong, such that if it touches a person's hand it hurts like a blow of iron. Since the knife's edge was downward toward the shechitah, and the chicken and its wings were below, it is like something falling with its edge onto hard ground; therefore we attribute leniently that through this it became nicked, and it is kosher.
