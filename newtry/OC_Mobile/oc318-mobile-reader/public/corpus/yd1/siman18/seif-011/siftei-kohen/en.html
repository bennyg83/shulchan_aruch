He must check, etc. It implies checking as required — avshara, atufra, and three directions; and so Darkei Moshe in the name of Maharil, not like what is written in the name of old shechitah laws that one need not check twelve times between shechitah and shechitah, because the spirit of the sages does not rest on him to interrupt — so too between blessing and second shechitah — end of his words; and it is not clear, for needs of shechitah are not an interruption, as below siman 19.
And even the first. For we are concerned lest in the hide of the first it was flawed, as above seif 1.
And one who wishes, etc. So too it appears from the Mechaber's words and Rambam and Rif, who wrote that if he did not do so — it implies specifically from this reason he must check between each and each, as is in Terumat HaDeshen; and so is the law in Kol Bo and Semag and other poskim; and so Maharshal, chapter 1 of Chullin siman 19; and so it appears to me plainly from Rif's and Rosh's words, who wrote after what they ruled the halachah like Rav Huna — "he did not break bones in it" — therefore checking is required between each and each, etc.; and so it is proven in Shaarei Torah siman 2, who wrote: if he slaughtered with a checked knife and after shechitah a flaw was found in it — all are invalid; therefore it is good to check the knife between each and each — end of his words; and so it is proven in the Talmud in what they say: like whom? Like R' Hoshaya — to invalidate the first; and so in Rashba's chiddushim, daf 14 end of 3 — for so the sugya is proven: what Rav Kahana required checking between each and each is good advice and not an obligation, etc.; and so is the view of many poskim, not like Bach who attacked Rabbenu Yerucham and wrote that Rosh and Tur are later — they saw Rif's wording and it did not seem right to them, therefore they changed the wording and wrote "and if"; but from Tur's words there is no necessity, and from Rif and Rosh it is plainly proven — aside from the wording; however, they must not take from the slaughtered animals to eat before checking the knife after shechitah.
To bring himself into, etc. And Taz wrote: if he slaughters for others and did so without permission and afterward the knife was found flawed — he must pay, even if he slaughters for free, as the law of a damager with his hands — end of his words; and see Yam Shel Shlomo, chapter HaGozel Eitzim, siman 23, and in Choshen Mishpat siman 306.
