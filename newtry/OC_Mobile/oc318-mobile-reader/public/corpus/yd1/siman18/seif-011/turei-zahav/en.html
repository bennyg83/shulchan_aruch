Many animals. The same applies to one animal — checking is required after shechitah lest it was flawed in the hide; rather it teaches here that there is prohibition even with many animals.
And one who wishes to bring himself, etc. Levush wrote: one who slaughters for others and afterward a flaw is found — he is liable to pay all the loss, for he is a damager with his hands — end of his words. And it is not clear to me, for it is in Bava Kamma (daf 99): that hide which came before Rav — it explains one who slaughtered his fellow's animal in a matter where the cause was for the rabbis and for R' Yosi it is valid, and Rav ruled treifah and exempted the slaughterer from paying because of doubt of theft — perhaps he holds like R' Yosi that it is valid; and so Tosafot, that Rav had a doubt in this; if so, why not say also there that the slaughterer was negligent by bringing himself into doubt against the rabbis, and certainly they would declare treifah for him? Rather, even so, since there is reason to rule like R' Yosi, one does not take money from his hand; all the more so here, where he thought there would be no treifot at all — certainly one does not take from him, for perhaps on the second joint-bone it was flawed; and against what is written in Choshen Mishpat siman 307, that a butcher is liable when it became neveilah for certain — but if the knife was found flawed and he checked it first, since we can say on the joint-bone it was flawed, we do not take money from doubt — end of his words; from this we learn: if he did not check, he is liable — this case is different, for it deals with the first checking that is required for everyone, and there is no doubt that he brought himself in first; therefore certainly he was negligent by not doing as required, for which he is liable; not so between shechitah and shechitah, which is only ordinary diligence, as Beit Yosef in the name of Baal HaMaor and Rashba, and so is Maharai's view here, that Rama wrote in his name that he may bring himself into doubt; certainly it is better than that hide of Rav that we mentioned; and furthermore, how is gerama relevant here? For Terumat HaDeshen siman 215 wrote that gerama is specifically where there are words of damage, or damage was done immediately; and therefore he exempted one who incited a dog against his fellow, because the damage was done after the incitement — all the more so here, where there is no treifah except from doubt — end of his words; one does not take money from this at all. And even though Terumat HaDeshen siman 186 wrote that for all stringencies that we practice, even though by law it is permitted, such as a slight shehiyah — nevertheless, since it is publicized as forbidden, the slaughterer is liable in wages to pay — it is not similar to this, for there the damage is certain, since you know it will become treifah by this; but here the damage is not certain at the time of the act, as above.
