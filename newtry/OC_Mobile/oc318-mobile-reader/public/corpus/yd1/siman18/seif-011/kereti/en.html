To bring himself into doubt — and nevertheless forbidden because of bal teshachet where the meat is treifah less than the value of kosher — see Peleti.
In this doubt: and if he slaughtered for others and did not check between shechitah and shechitah, and afterward a flaw was found — opinion of Taz: the slaughterer must pay, for he was negligent; and Taz's view: exempt; and Shach in Nehar Shalom attacked him; and see Peleti, where I clarified the law is with Taz and the rabbi, may his memory be blessed, wrote. However, he commits a prohibition and does not do well with his people — only gerama in damages we hold forbidden even though there is no liability to pay. And if the court sees he is accustomed to this, and this is a sign he is light-minded — it is fitting to obligate him to pay to fence the matter. And behold in error where they slaughter korbanot and the people hurry to bring — they hurry the slaughterer, for from him people eat at noon; because of this the slaughterer has no leisure to check the knife properly between shechitah and shechitah, and slaughters many fowl one after another without checking in between; and if afterward his knife is found flawed — behold all are treifah retroactively — and who will pay? Many enter and leave; and so in error the stumbling block is before you — many concerns; and on the fast of the chosen day they bring themselves into enormous doubts; and it would have been better not to eat at all. How many times I warned: better to do like the order of the village, but without rushing shechitah until leisure for the slaughterer to slaughter calmly and with settling; even though the Ari, may his memory be blessed, warns to hurry shechitah — he did not speak where there is concern of doubt of prohibition. And if so, all who stand to slaughter are like slaughtered; and if he prepares for shechitah only the delay from overloading the slaughterer — behold he does his work above to sweeten the gevurot as if slaughtered and standing — the Merciful will come in his request; and especially when intent is for Heaven's sake, as written. And in any case the rabbi teacher should supervise this on slaughterers on the chosen day, lest they break through and lighten their head in knife checking and checking simanim, etc., for the great toil of their work; and the wages of fowl slaughterers mentioned will go for atonement in their loss in error.
