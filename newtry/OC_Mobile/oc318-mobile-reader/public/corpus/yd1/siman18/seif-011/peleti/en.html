One who wishes, etc. — see Shach, seif kaf 18, who lengthened: not like Bach; rather the essence as Rama wrote — and these are Terumat HaDeshen's words. And in truth in Rashba's words there is contradiction: he wrote that requiring checking is only good advice so as not to lose the slaughtered; and afterward he returned and was precise on the wording "requires" — not so, it does not imply good advice but obligation; and therefore he wrote he is obligated — lest he transgress bal teshachet — see there in Terumat HaDeshen and his chiddushim. Behold from this he is obligated, but not from prohibition, as Bach wrote, only from bal teshachet; and practical difference: if treifah meat was bought with kosher meat, perhaps bal teshachet does not apply; nevertheless good advice; therefore in all places they do not distinguish forbidden; and in all Shach brought there is no contradiction to Rashba himself; and so truth is correct; therefore Tur wrote: one must check because of bal teshachet.
To bring himself in; and if he slaughtered for others and did not check and treifah was found — Taz's view: obligate the slaughterer to pay; Taz in seif kaf 8 disagrees; his proof from the hide that came before Rav, and he exempted — perhaps the law is like one who declares fit; and the same here — perhaps on joint-bone it was flawed; and see that he lengthened. And Nehar Shalom rejected him: the slaughterer knows the law like R' Yosi bar Yehudah, who is lenient on joint-bone; and behold we see in Tosafot Bava Kamma 99, s.v. mine'ach, etc., Rava himself holds the law is valid like R' Yosi bar R' Yehudah — only he was stringent that it is treifah; and Tosafot wrote: therefore he said doubt of theft and not certain theft — that slaughterer would be led to think they will be stringent on him — end; from this it appears like Taz: he should have been led to think they would be stringent; nevertheless Rav exempted; all the more here, where he did not know a flaw would be found and thought the knife would not be flawed in any slaughter — no obligation on slaughterer. But Maharai in Terumat HaDeshen siman 186 stood on this: why indeed did Rav exempt the slaughterer? And he answered: this was not clear to Rav to lead the slaughterer to think they would be stringent on him with such stringency to declare treifah — end. And if so, in our case by law of Gemara requiring checking between each and each, and all slaughterers know there is truly obligation as above to check between one and fellow without putting all animals in presumption of doubt of prohibition, and he was not careful — plainly he was negligent; and your friend will answer: Tosafot there in Bava Kamma deals with other rings and Rav had a doubt and nevertheless exempted — implies on doubt one is exempt; strained to say like Nehar Shalom: the butcher knew Rav permits, the butcher knew — and in truth Nehar Shalom goes to his method that Rav, Shach in Choshen Mishpat 306 seif kaf 10, as Terumat HaDeshen: every stringency spread and known among slaughterers, and he did not do, and thereby it became treifah — he is liable as a damager. But according to Rama there he disagrees; and so appears in Darkei Moshe: even shehiyah in last minority and it became treifah — nevertheless slaughterer exempt; all the more here exempt. And Shach's words: in their days shehiyah in last minority was not spread as certain prohibition — strained, for Rama should have emended according to his view that it does not apply in this time. And in my humble opinion, on this Tur in Choshen Mishpat wrote: and so if knife found flawed and he checked first, since we can say on joint-bone it was flawed — we do not take money from doubt — end; implies: if impossible to attribute such as he held the simanim in hand and slaughtered — clear he did not hit joint-bone — why liable even if flawed in hide? And so Rashba in responsum brought there Kessef Mishneh: exempt, for we say flawed in hide; and Semag and Perishah wished to settle: if flawed in hide slaughterer liable, for certainly his knife was bad, for good knife is not flawed from hide — and this is strained, not like Rashba above; if so, with good slaughterer attribute to joint-bone and animal not treifah; what does Gemara challenge on R' Chiya, daf 10 Chullin, who says flawed on bone — from that which dipped and ascended, etc. — what difficulty? Good knife is not flawed on hide — certainly on bone flawed; or Tur's intent who wrote plainly: and so if found flawed and he checked first — meaning he slaughtered in between and did not check in between, only checked first — if so liable as Taz's view teaches: we do not say on joint-bone afterward flawed; and if we declare treifah he is nevertheless exempt; and Beit Yosef wrote it is similar to Rav's hide; and the matter is clear in Tur's intent according to Semag: what should the butcher do if he takes a sharpened knife — is he a smith to discern iron if flawed on hide or not? And see Tosafot there, s.v. sakin: more common knife flawed on joint-bone last than first; and even though they wrote for R' Hoshaya — nevertheless reasoning did not move, and one does not take money — examine.
