And the reason is in Taz and Shach: because there is a double doubt for prohibition — one, that perhaps it did not fall on its point; and even if you say it fell on its point, perhaps it was not flawed on the ground, for ground does not certainly flaw; and it is not at all similar to above seif 13, where the knife was lost and afterward found flawed, that it is valid — there there is only one doubt, that perhaps he broke bones and was not aware; and furthermore, here, since the knife is before us and checking is required, it has not gone out ever in presumption of permissibility; but there, since it was lost, it went out in presumption of permissibility.
Soft. Taz and Shach disagree on this; and they wrote: even on the joint-bone, if he struck not by way of shechitah but by way of striking not by holacha u'va — one may attribute, for it is no worse than hard ground.
