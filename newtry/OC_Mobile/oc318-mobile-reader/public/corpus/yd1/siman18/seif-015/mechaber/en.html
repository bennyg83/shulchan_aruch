One who slaughters with a checked knife and after shechitah broke bones in it — not by way of holacha u'va (Rashba and R' Yitzchak and R' Yerucham) — and checked it and a flaw was found — his shechitah is valid, for we attribute that it was flawed by breaking the bones; and so anything similar, such as that it fell on hard ground — but specifically when we saw that it fell on its point; but from doubt we do not attribute to say that it fell on its point; and if he broke in it the joint-bone — even by way of breaking we do not attribute, because it is soft. {Rama: And there is no distinction in all this whether he checked the knife first close to shechitah or it was in the presumption of being checked first and he did not check it close to shechitah — for even so it is called a checked knife (my humble opinion, from Mordechai's glosses, chapter 1 of Chullin, and Hagahot Maimoni and Agudah; and see seif kaf 64).}
