But from doubt we do not attribute to say that it fell on its point. The reason: because there is a double doubt for prohibition — one, that perhaps it did not fall on its point; and even if you say it fell on its point, perhaps it was not flawed on the ground, for ground does not certainly flaw, as in Rosh's responsum, general principle 20, siman 14; and so Maharshal there: since there is a doubt, it is forbidden — see there. And it is difficult in general for above seif 13, where the knife was lost and afterward found flawed, that it is valid — there there is only one doubt, that perhaps he broke bones in it and was not aware, as explained there; and furthermore, here, since the knife is before us and checking is required, it has not gone out ever in a presumption of permissibility; but there, since it was lost, it went out in a presumption of permissibility, as explained there; therefore even if found afterward we establish it in its presumption of permissibility and say: perhaps he broke bones in it afterward and was not aware; and perforce one must distinguish thus, for Rosh in that responsum ruled like above seif 13, and in his rulings wrote as Taz wrote here; and so Maharshal, chapter 1 of Chullin, siman 18 and 19 — these two laws. And I did not grasp Bach's final view, who lengthened in his words and made Rosh as one who does not stand by his word — that in his rulings he retracted from what he said in the responsum, and an error went out from before the rulers — see Maharshal; and the matter is clear as written; examine; and so Taz and Zivchei Tzedek and Sefer Lamed Chet bring these two laws in the general case for halachic ruling; and so Darkei Moshe rules like Rosh's responsum.
Even by way of breaking, etc. It requires study, for it appears from the poskim that by way of breaking one attributes; and what appears from the Mechaber from Ran is not necessary, for Ran refers to where he broke by holacha u'va; and know, for Ran wrote on this and so Rashba and in Rashba's chiddushim, daf 14 and 15, and in Terumat HaDeshen end of daf 17 — it plainly implies thus — see there.
