Avodat HaGolah; and see responsum Shevut Yaakov part 2 siman 53, who wrote: if we know the knife touched the joint-bone, that there is a cut in the joint-bone and the flaw is not as great as catching fingernail — one may declare fit from a double doubt: perhaps the law is like Rosh and his colleagues that it is not treifah until it flaws the measure of catching fingernail, and perhaps on the bone it was flawed; and he wrote further an incident came to his hand: the slaughterer said he heard the knife sound when it touched the joint-bone, like they call in Ashkenaz "krek," visible and recognizable — thereby the flaw came from there, and he declared fit; for in such a case all agree we say on the bone it was flawed, since there are legs to the matter — see there; and see Sefer Beli Yadi, who challenged the first law, for he himself ruled in Choshen Aruch siman 46 that double doubt against presumption — we do not say; and on the second law he wrote it seems specifically breaking and not passing; and see Peri Megadim end of the siman, who agreed with Shevut Yaakov on the first law, and on the second wrote one who is lenient in the first instance cannot be blamed — see there.
