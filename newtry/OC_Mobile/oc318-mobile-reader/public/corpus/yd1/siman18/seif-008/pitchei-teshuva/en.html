The sage may forgive. As Shach wrote: for Rambam, who wrote the reason they excommunicate — because he will rely on his shechitah another time and it will be flawed — perhaps there is no difference of sage's forgiveness; and see responsum Brit Avraham part Yoreh De'ah siman 14, letter 3, who wrote there is another practical difference between Rambam's reasons: where the knife was lost, we hold above seif 12 that if they checked it before shechitah it helps — one may say this is specifically if he acted as required and showed to sage; but without showing to sage, even though by law not required except for sage's honor, nevertheless the meat is treifah, since he lost presumption of his fitness — he transgressed sages' enactment; and see there siman 2 letter 15 in gloss, and siman 15 there explained: even nowadays they appoint known men — practical difference where court decreed on him not to slaughter because of suspicion of bribery and he transgressed and slaughtered: since they decreed he not slaughter, they did not forgive, and it is as if he did not show his knife by law of Talmud; and for this reason they excommunicated him — as by law of Talmud, only one who took kabbalah did sages forgive their honor; and therefore one on whom they decreed not to slaughter — they did not forgive — see there.
