Like a serpent. Kesef Mishneh explains: meaning like a serpent whose spine is curved when it raises its head and tail and between them like a bow. And Mahari Chagiz wrote that it implies specifically low once; and so it appears from Rashi, who wrote: such as where it was flawed with a large flaw and the points were smoothed and the knife is low there — it implies only once — end of his words; and I know no source or hint for this stringency, since there is no flaw on it — what difference to me once or many times; and also Shulchan Aruch's wording, which is Rambam's language, indicates this, for he wrote "a knife that rises and falls" — it implies the whole knife is thus; for otherwise he should have said "a knife in which there is rise and fall"; rather the whole knife is so — end of his words; I say that no one is concerned for this stringency.
