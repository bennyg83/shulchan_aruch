The butcher must take, etc. In Choshen Mishpat siman 306 Tur wrote: butchers who take korkeshot from the kosher are called proper wages, even though they take nothing from treifot — end of his words; it implies that even in our rabbi Tur's days they practiced taking from the kosher alone; and on this basis it is determined today as well. And the reason seems that they were not suspected for such a small matter to feed treifot; and it is not similar to that of Ila in Yavneh, chapter Ad Kamah, regarding permissibility of firstborns, where there is suspicion for a great matter — so it appears to settle the custom; and I again found thus in Maharshal.
And therefore they practiced, etc. It implies; but by law one slaughters and checks for himself — and it is difficult from the mishnah (chapter 2 of Negaim): all firstlings a person may inspect except his own firstlings; and Rosh already challenged there many things that we find a Torah scholar may rule for himself; and he concludes there: specifically where prohibition was established he may not rule for himself; and if so, here too regarding checking it is fine that it was permitted, but shechitah would be prohibition by law, for while alive it stands in presumption of prohibition; and it appears that by law it is not permitted except to slaughter, for majority present, etc.; but if a ruling occurred to him he should not rule; and see what is written siman 312.
