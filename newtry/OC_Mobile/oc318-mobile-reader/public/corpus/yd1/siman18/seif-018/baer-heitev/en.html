The kosher. And Taz wrote: in Choshen Mishpat siman 306 the Mechaber wrote: butchers who take korkeshot from the kosher are called proper wages, even though they take nothing from treifot; and from this reason Taz ruled that one should permit where the custom is thus; and he wrote they were not suspected for such a small matter to feed treifot. And Rivash disagrees and wrote one should not distinguish great benefit from small benefit — always there is suspicion; and Shach wrote permissibility for another reason: we certainly do not suspect he will eat treifot, and if so he will have to sell them and fears lest they perceive — nevertheless he wrote one should abolish this custom. A slaughterer and checker who set a dinar for him between kosher and treifah, and after he declared one animal kosher he returned and said it is treifah and gave an excuse — one should believe him — Darkei Shalom siman 122. A community that accepted a slaughterer for wages that from every animal of Israel they will give him the tongue, and the kitchen is with a non-Jew — now the non-Jew slaughtered animals with Israel in partnership — if Israel must give the tongue, see Terumat HaDeshen siman 57. One who converted and returned in complete repentance may be slaughterer-checker as Nehar Shalom in the name of Rosh.
The community. And specifically one who slaughters and sells to others; but if he slaughtered for his need and not to sell to others — it is plainly permitted — Beit Yosef. And Taz wrote: from Rama's words it implies specifically from the side of custom it is forbidden, but by law certainly permitted; but in my humble opinion it seems one should forbid by law; and in any case by law it is not permitted except to slaughter — majority present, etc.; but if a ruling occurred he should not rule — end of his words; and he did not see Tosafot's words, Niddah daf 20b, s.v. kol yoma.
