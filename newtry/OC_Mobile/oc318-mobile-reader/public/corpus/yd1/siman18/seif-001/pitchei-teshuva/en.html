Flawed. See responsum Brit Avraham, part Yoreh De'ah siman 12, where they asked about a shechitah knife that two slaughterers checked after shechitah and a felt flaw was found; afterward one took the knife and rubbed on forearm flesh and thereby the flaw departed; and the answer in that slaughterer's mouth, who received it in his hand, that any flaw — even if they rub all day — will not be removed; and therefore there was no complete flaw, and the second slaughterer denied him. What is his law? And he brought that this animal is forbidden — see there his reason. [And see responsum Chasam Sofer siman 15 on what slaughterers practice when they find a doubtful flaw in the knife after shechitah — they smooth over hide or something else a while until they do not feel the flaw and declare the animal fit; and he wrote evil is this act, and in my eyes it is like feeding treifot, for a flaw was not told to Moses from Sinai. Hagram and shehiyah were told; and whatever in the knife inhibits — wherever transfer of fingernail is inhibited will be a flaw from the knife body or a protrusion of something else stuck to the knife that cannot be removed easily by hand and light rinsing, etc., only needs some time to smooth on wood and hide, etc. — all cause hagram or shehiyah; therefore whoever does so feeds treifot, and one must stand with strength against these honing destroyers — see there.]
