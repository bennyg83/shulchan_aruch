His shechitah is valid. — Thus Rosh there: it is an extra stringency to attribute to the hide more than to the joint-bone; therefore, where he knows for certain that he did not break bones, we attribute for stringency — say it was flawed in the hide, even though it is more common that it flaws on the joint-bone than on the hide — we forbid it; but where the knife was lost and we doubt whether he broke bones in it, and an additional doubt is added — we permit it, etc.; therefore we are lenient in a light matter — attribute to something else — end of his words; and in the body of the question there another reason is explained: since the knife went out in permissibility, it went out; and this that was found flawed — say he broke bones in it and he was not aware, for we establish the knife in its presumption; and say it was not flawed — end of his words; and see seif kaf 24.
