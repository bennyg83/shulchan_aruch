Since it went out in permissibility, etc. With this is resolved what is difficult from seif 15, which wrote "but from doubt we do not attribute," etc., and here we are lenient in doubt; and so from seif 16, which ruled specifically when he checked the knife after shechitah they say perhaps he broke bones, and here they say to him even if he did not check after shechitah. And indeed all is settled: certainly in the whole Torah we follow presumption unless the matter is before us to check — then we do not rely on presumption, as Mordechai, beginning of Chullin, in the name of R' Yehudah HaLevi, regarding majority present at shechitah are experts — if it is before us we do not rely on the majority; and so here with the knife: whatever is before us we are obligated to check; and so Ran explicitly with these matters, chapter 1 of Chullin; and perforce, whoever did not check — the presumption of the knife that it was good at the time of shechitah, since he checked before, nevertheless he is now obligated to check again; therefore in that of seif 15 and seif 16, where the knife was not lost — perforce he must check, and whoever did not check has no good presumption and never had a time of fitness; if he had come to ask before this doubt arose that it is now found flawed, we would not have ruled permissibility, and there is no time for permissibility here — one should be stringent from doubt as if there is no presumption; not so here, where the knife was lost and then it had a good presumption, and if he had come to ask that time we would have ruled permissibility — it turns out there was time for permissibility; therefore we say that even when found afterward and it is flawed, since it is possible it was flawed after shechitah and it already had permissibility if he had come to ask, the doubt does not forbid afterward; it turns out everything is correct. And so Maharshal ruled these laws as mentioned in Shulchan Aruch; and Mahari Chagiz was challenged: what do we care about "since it went out in permissibility" — if it was forbidden from the outset by law, what do we care that it went out in permissibility? Rather certainly the reason for permissibility here is because an additional doubt was added to be lenient after shechitah, since the knife was lost — perforce also in seif 15 one should be lenient wherever we saw it fell, even if we did not see it fell on its point; and perforce he wrote that one who permits in that of seif 13 permits even in the doubt of seif 15; and he wrote that it is an error before the rulers to permit here and forbid in the doubt of seif 15; to me their words seem correct, for the matter depends on presumption — and this is what we said: "since it went out in permissibility," meaning the doubt arose after the knife had a good presumption and ruling for permissibility; and the rulers' words are clear.
