Since it went out in permissibility — Bach challenged from seif 15, who ruled in doubt if fell to hard ground — forbidden; and Taz and Shach both answer: here, since it went out in permissibility, it is different; and Shach explains more: here the knife is before us, we establish presumption of prohibition; but in seif 13, where it was lost and permitted, you cannot forbid it afterward. And among them is the reason, even though Peri Chadash rejects him with two hands; nevertheless this is difficult to me: what we say in Chullin — lung was pierced where the butcher's hand was used — do we attribute or not? And Gemara says: reasonable to attribute, for we attribute with a wolf; and Rashi: we say in chapter 1 a wolf came and took the innards and returned them — we do not say at the place of the hole there is a hole. And difficult: what proof? There the wolf already took and they are lost and the matter already went out in permissibility; if you come afterward to forbid because of holes, we say the wolf pierced, as we say with knife he broke bones; but before us, not knowing whence the hole came, we do not attribute to butcher's hand as we do not attribute it fell on its point. Therefore more correct: in seif 13, where knife was lost, there is a good spin and many ways how flaw happened to knife and several doubts — therefore we attribute to be lenient; but in seif 15 we have no doubt the knife is in presumption of flawed, only to say perhaps it fell on its point to earth — and no more than one doubt — we do not permit; not so seif 13, where there is a good doubt the knife was not in presumption of prohibition as above.
