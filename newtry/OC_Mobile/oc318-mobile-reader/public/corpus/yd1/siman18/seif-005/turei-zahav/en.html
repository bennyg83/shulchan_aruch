All are masuchseches. Tur brought this in the name of Baal HaItur: it deals with catching; and Beit Yosef challenged: if so, even one is invalid; and many answered what they answered; and it seems to me a correct answer: after he analyzed the wording — for they said in the baraita, and Tur cited: "a knife that has many flaws" — judge it like a saw; and why did they not say "invalid"? Rather it appears they taught another law: that we find above siman 6, with a knife whose one side is like a saw, etc., that if he slaughtered on the beautiful side it is valid b'dieved; and the same is a long knife that has a flaw and a measure of shechitah remains, etc., as Rama wrote there — it teaches us: even though if there were only one catching there would be permissibility b'dieved if he slaughtered not opposite the catching, nevertheless if there are two or more there is no permissibility, but it is like a saw — meaning as if the whole is flawed and there is no beautiful part at all; so it is correct according to Baal HaItur; and there is a practical difference for this explanation even for us in this law.
