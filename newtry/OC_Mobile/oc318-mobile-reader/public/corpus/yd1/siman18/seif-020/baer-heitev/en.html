Forbidden. For he slaughtered with a flawed knife l'chatchila. A butcher who said he checked the knife after shechitah and gave it to one youth to give to another man to slaughter — that man found it flawed, and that youth is an adult and said, "It is clear to me I touched nothing hard, but immediately gave it to the second slaughterer's hand" — and it was found flawed — one may not be lenient to say he is an agent who is not expert; but if the youth is a minor, those animals the first slaughtered remain in presumption of permissibility, for we say certainly he is an agent who is not expert — Peri Chadash from Maharival. He also wrote there seif 9: they asked about one layman who deposited his knife with one man and it was found flawed; on Yom Tov 2 one came who did not know it was flawed and brought the knife to another sage, who declared it fit, and slaughtered with it and hid it; and after about ten hours they found the knife flawed — he ruled all he slaughtered is treifah, for one may not be lenient to say he repaired the knife before the second's shechitah and afterward it was spoiled in those ten hours; and see Peri Chadash there in this siman. A butcher whose knife was found flawed not at the time of shechitah — he is not disqualified, everything according to what kind of person he is — Maharam of Lublin siman 76. A butcher who checked the knife and a flaw was found and he gave to his fellow, who checked and did not feel the flaw, and fed the many — afterward the first revealed the second's disgrace, and that he did not reveal to feed his enemies — the court should fine for this and remove the first butcher — Rashdam siman 47; and see above siman 1 in the gloss.
