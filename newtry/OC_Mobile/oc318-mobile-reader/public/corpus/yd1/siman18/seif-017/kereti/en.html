He showed the knife to a sage — see Peleti, where I wrote in chapter 1; and in ordinary reading one need not show to a sage at all — see there.
They excommunicate him — because he flawed the honor of a Torah scholar.
The sage may forgive — see Shach, who wrote: according to Rambam he may not forgive, only when the slaughterer was established as expert slaughterer; but otherwise there is no authority to forgive; and see Peleti.
And to them the sages forgave — and they gave another reason in settling Rambam's words: we have no Torah scholar present in error — yesterday they were given and we do not know in error; and all this according to what they said: he must show to a sage because of the sage's honor, and there are no sages in our generation worthy to share this honor; but according to Rambam, to separate from prohibition — if he slaughtered between himself and himself, perhaps he will slaughter with a flawed knife — even in this time it applies; and what that they do not know tractate Kallah. And it seems practiced without slaughtering without receipt from a sage — if so we granted and we pardoned; for he authorized him to slaughter without any person and to set the knife; therefore these are the things I wrote in siman 1: one needs receipt specifically from the rabbi teacher, not from slaughterers even if experts, for they cannot forgive the teacher's honor.
