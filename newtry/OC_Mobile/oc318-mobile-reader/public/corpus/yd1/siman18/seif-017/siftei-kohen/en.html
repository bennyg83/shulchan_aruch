And to them the sages forgave, etc. And in Agudah, chapter 1 of Chullin, he wrote another reason: because in our time there are no Torah scholars — for they do not know even tractate Kallah; and so in Maharil's responsum siman 71 and Mahari Weil's responsum siman 163 and Maharik, root 163; and so below siman 242 regarding one who shames — that nowadays all are like ignoramuses; and Rabbenu Yerucham brings it there.
And much settling of mind is required, etc. See Maharshal, chapter 1 of Chullin siman 39, who greatly challenged the slaughterers of this time who check the knife hastily and without intent of heart, and that one should warn and be strict with them in this matter — see there.
And when it was found good the sage may forgive, etc. Alfasi's glosses; and it appears that he holds like Rashi and Ran, that they excommunicated him when he did not show his knife to a sage because of the sage's honor, as below siman 242; and it is possible to explain that they excommunicated for the rabbi's honor, therefore the sage may forgive his honor, as below siman 242, seif 32: "a rabbi who forgives his honor — his honor is forgiven"; but Rambam, chapter 1 of Laws of Shechitah, and Kol Bo wrote the reason that they excommunicated: because he will rely on his shechitah another time and it will be flawed and he will slaughter with it — end of his words. And accordingly, perhaps the sage cannot forgive; however, in the Talmud, chapter 1 of Chullin (daf 10b), it appears like Rashi and Ran, and so is the implication of the poskim.
