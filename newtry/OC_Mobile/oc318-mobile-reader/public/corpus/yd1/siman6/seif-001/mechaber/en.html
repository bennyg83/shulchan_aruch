**With what one slaughters. And it has four seifim:**
One may slaughter with every detached thing—whether with a knife, whether with flint, whether with the membrane of lake reed called ishpadnia (and tooth and nail when solitary) (Tur)—and similar cutting implements. And it must be that its mouth is sharp and has no flaw: {Rama: and it is forbidden to slaughter with other reeds or glass from which splinters separate, and one must be concerned for puncturing the simanim (Beit Yosef in name of Rashi and so wrote Ran).} A knife of which one side is like a sickle and the other side is good—one does not slaughter on the good side l'chatchila, a decree lest he slaughter on the other side; and if he slaughtered, since he slaughtered on the good side his shechitah is kosher: {Rama: and the same applies to a long knife that has a flaw and remains in it a shechitah measure without flaw—forbidden to slaughter with it even in the good place even if he wrapped a cloth on the flaw (Tur); and if he slaughtered with it and said it is clear to me I did not touch the place of the flaw—his shechitah is kosher (Mordechai end perek of Chullin and Ashri and Ta'anit and Neta'iv Tziyon and Kol Bo and Rokeach and Maharil) even if he did not wrap a cloth on the flaw; and on Yom Tov when it is impossible to sharpen the knife (Tur and Ashri and Rif Avi Ezri) and likewise on a weekday in a time of pressure it is permitted to slaughter l'chatchila if he wraps a cloth on the flaw (Tur and Beit Yosef who relied on Ittur and Ran and Sefer HaTerumah and Semag and Hagahot Maimonides) (and see above siman 18 seif 1); and l'chatchila one should be careful even when the knife has no flaw only that between the handle and the knife one should not slaughter with it (found in old shechitah texts) (Darkei Moshe siman 18 end seif 2).}
