His brother or son. His father and his sons wrote that his brother and his brothers did not allow him to do so, and that he did not speak to him
And likewise if he paid them, etc. Likewise the Rambam, and the Tur wrote on this and I do not understand his words — how can it be permitted for him to pay his debt from assets forbidden to him, behold he benefits from them, etc. And Beit Yosef wrote that it escaped our teacher the Mishnah in the first chapter of HaGozel that said explicitly as the Rambam wrote. And in my opinion it appears he challenges well — the main challenge of the Tur is on what the Rambam wrote, that one who forbade himself says he pays his debt with the forbidden thing; and they did not say in the Mishnah except that the one who forbade pays the debt of the forbidden one without the knowledge and will of the forbidden one. And that which we learned in the Mishnah and creditors come and collect from him — this is against his will, for so implies the language the Mishnah said "and they collect," unlike the language of the Rambam here that wrote "if he paid them" — it implies he himself. And the Ran brought it — Beit Yosef in siman 216 establishes also these words of the Rambam in this manner, that he himself does not pay the creditor but shows him the place where they are; and the Tur is not satisfied to explain thus. Further the Tur challenged on what the Rambam wrote here "and he must inform them," etc. — what difference does it make? And the Ran there resolved that this informing is that it is like hefker, as one who says "these are my father's assets, I do not know what to do with them — take for yourselves" — and this is also according to his way that he explained he himself does not pay; but the Tur who explains the words of the Rambam that he himself pays — therefore he challenged well. It is found regarding the law there is no dispute — whatever he does, he himself paying is forbidden, and it does not help that he informs; only regarding giving his father's inheritance to his sons the Ran also holds it is forbidden when he gives himself, because he has benefit from this; rather it deals with showing them a place and saying to them "these assets of father are forbidden to me, I do not know what to do with them — take for yourselves and do with them as you wish." And Beit Yosef wrote there — since he informs them this money is forbidden to him and therefore he gives it to them, and they are his sons and brothers who have a connection to this money — therefore he has no benefit from them and it is permitted — until here his language. And nevertheless it implies that specifically after the fact it helps if he gave or paid, but not initially even though he informs.
