If he gave, etc. The Ran explains that this does not mean he actually gives the property to them, for then he would benefit from the goodwill generated by his gift. Rather, he points out its location and says, 'My father forbade this property to me, and I do not know what to do with it. Take it for yourselves and do with it as you wish.' Likewise, regarding a debt or his wife's kesubah, the case is where beit din collects for them without his direction, but payment made at his direction is forbidden, as stated above in siman 221, seif katan 6. The Beit Yosef writes in siman 216 that regarding his sons and brothers, he may even give it to them directly, provided he informs them that the money is forbidden to him and that this is why he gives it to them. Since they are his sons or brothers and already have a family connection to this property, he derives no benefit of goodwill from them, and it is permitted. Maharshal likewise rules in Perek HaGozel Etzim, siman 60, that he may hand it directly to the father's heirs, the nearer relative taking precedence according to the laws of inheritance, and he must tell them that this is his father's money, which is forbidden to him. He may not give it to his own sons, for there is no greater benefit to him than that. If he has nothing to eat, he may borrow, and creditors may collect from his share of the inheritance. If he has other means, however, this is forbidden, and he must give the property to the father's heirs. If there are no heirs, he should give it to charity, and he need not inform the charity that it is his father's money. He may also destroy it or cast it into the sea, although it is a mitzvah to compel him to give it to charity. See Choshen Mishpat 367:5. The Perishah and Bach explain that the ruling, 'if he gave or paid,' permits the result only after the fact, without violating the vow, but doing so initially is forbidden. See there for their extended discussion.
If he paid them, etc. It appears that according to the opinion above, siman 221, seif katan 6, which forbids payment of a definite debt or a debt secured by collateral, the same applies here. This is also implied by the Ran cited above, who compares the two cases. Accordingly, this ruling concerns an oral debt or a similar obligation that is not definitively established.
