If he gave, etc. The Ran explained — not to say they are actually given to them, for if so he would benefit from their benefit; rather he shows them a place and says to them "these assets my father forbade upon me and I do not know what to do with them — take for yourselves and do with them as you wish." And likewise it deals with a debt and a wife's ketubah that the court collects for them without his knowledge; but with his knowledge it is forbidden, as above siman 221 seif kaf 6 — end of quote. And Beit Yosef in siman 216 wrote that regarding his sons and brothers, even if he actually gives to them it is permitted, since he informs them this money is forbidden to him and therefore he gives it to them; and they too are his sons and brothers who have a connection to this money — therefore he has no benefit from them and it is permitted — end of quote. And likewise wrote Maharshal chapter HaGozel Atzim siman 60, and he elaborated there and ruled that he can give directly into the hands of the father's heirs — closest relative first according to inheritance — and he must inform them this is father's money that is forbidden to him; and to his sons he should not give at all, for you have no greater benefit than this. And if he has nothing to eat, it is permitted for him to borrow, and Bach — if there are none, they pay from his portion of the inheritance; but if he has, it is forbidden — rather he should give to the father's heirs; and if there are no heirs he should give to charity, and he need not inform the charity this is father's money. However, he is permitted to destroy it and cast it to the sea; nevertheless it is a mitzvah to compel him in this to give to charity — end of quote in brief. And see in Choshen Mishpat siman 367 seif 5, and Perishah and Bach wrote that this is specifically if he gave or paid after the fact it is permitted, and it does not refer to vow prohibition; but initially it is forbidden — see there where they elaborated on this.
