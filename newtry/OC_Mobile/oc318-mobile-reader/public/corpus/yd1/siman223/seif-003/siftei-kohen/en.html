And the grandson is permitted, etc. This implies that if the grandson will not be a Torah scholar, the grandson is also forbidden. The Raavad understood the Rambam this way in his glosses, and the Bach likewise writes that, in the Rambam's view, since he said, 'If his grandson will be a Torah scholar,' it is as though he expressly stated that if the grandson will not be a Torah scholar, he too shall be barred from the property like the son. The Kesef Mishneh and Perishah, however, explain that 'permitted' means the property is acquired by the grandson, so that his father cannot give it to others, but the grandson is permitted to benefit from it in any event. This is also the view of the Raavad and the Tur, that the grandson is permitted in every circumstance. Nevertheless, the Rosh and Ran rule that the grandson does not acquire it, since nothing was conveyed to the son until the grandson would become a Torah scholar, and by that time the symbolic cloth used for the acquisition had already returned to its owner.
