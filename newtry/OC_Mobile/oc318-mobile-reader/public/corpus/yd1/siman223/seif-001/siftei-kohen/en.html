The son is permitted, etc. Presumably, he did not forbid him regarding minor matters of this kind, which do not involve so much neglect of Torah study. The Perishah writes that according to this reasoning, where a father forbade his son benefit because the son does not engage in Torah study, it is possible that the son may not perform even these services for him, unlike the Rambam. The Bach likewise rules that his status is the same as one who forbids his property to his son without mentioning that the reason is the son's failure to study Torah, and the son may not perform any service for him.
He is likewise permitted, etc. Since he is a distinguished person, or she is a woman who does not ordinarily make purchases herself, it is evident that they did not impose the prohibition with this situation in mind.
