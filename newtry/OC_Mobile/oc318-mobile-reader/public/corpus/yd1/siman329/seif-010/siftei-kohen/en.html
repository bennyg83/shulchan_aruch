One should avoid kneading with fruit juice. The same applies to egg liquid or anything not among the seven liquids. Although the Mechaber rules below that such dough is liable in challah, we would not know what to do with its challah. Even though all of us are presently impure through contact with the dead, this challah is pure because the dough was not rendered susceptible to impurity. Food becomes susceptible only through contact with a qualifying liquid, and fruit juice is not considered such a liquid. It is therefore forbidden to burn this pure challah, and eating it is likewise impossible because most of us are impure and an impure person may not eat pure terumah. It would have to be given to a minor kohen. Therefore one should initially knead only after mixing in one of the seven liquids, enabling the challah to be burned. This is explicit in Semak and Beit Yosef. See Rambam, Laws of Food Impurity, for the liquids that do not render food susceptible, from which one may learn with which liquids one should not knead.
Unless he mixes in. It appears that he must mix it in at the time of kneading, before the obligation of challah takes effect. Once he has kneaded with fruit juice and the dough has become liable, he should not mix in another liquid.
What are the seven liquids? Wine, honey, oil, milk, dew, blood, and water. Reference 110.
