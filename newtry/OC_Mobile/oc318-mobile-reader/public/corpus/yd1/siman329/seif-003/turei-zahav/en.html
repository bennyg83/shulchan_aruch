This is the view of the Rash, the Rosh, and the Rambam, unlike Rabbeinu Tam, who maintains that even if at the time of kneading he intended subsequently to cook it, it is nevertheless liable in challah because it was a thick dough when kneaded. Rather, we follow the intention present at the time of kneading only to exempt, such as where he intended to cook it, but not in the reverse direction, as stated at the end: if he kneaded it with the intention of making sponge cakes, and so forth. The Rosh wrote in the chapter Kol Shaah that because of this dispute, Maharam customarily instructed the members of his household to bake a small portion of such dough, namely dough that he had intended to cook, so that the entire dough would thereby become liable in challah, as explained in the following seif.
