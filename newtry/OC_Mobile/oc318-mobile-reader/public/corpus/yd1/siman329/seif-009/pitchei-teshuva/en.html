Regarding his suggestion that if one has another dough from which challah has not yet been removed, one may use it, Levushei Serad writes that the Shach's words require study if he means a dough that itself contains the full challah measure and that one should separate with a blessing for both, for perhaps the present dough is exempt and that would be separating from obligated for exempt. He therefore concludes that one should not separate for it from another fully obligated dough. Rather, if one has a dough below the minimum challah measure, one may join it with this doubtful dough and then separate without a blessing from whichever one he wishes. In the margin of my grandfather Rabbi Naftali Hirsch's Yoreh Deah, he writes that the Rosh's exemption applies only to fruit juices other than the seven liquids; with one of the seven liquids the dough is certainly obligated, as implied by the Yerushalmi, the Rosh, and Responsa Rashba, siman 464. Be'er Heitev was imprecise. Levushei Serad also followed that imprecision. At first glance the Tur's wording may seem to support it, but careful study of Beit Yosef and the Yerushalmi shows otherwise. The Tur's statement that the Rosh was uncertain refers only to egg liquid, not to wine or honey. See Beer HaGolah, letter 30. My grandfather likewise rules in Responsa Panim Meirot, part 1, siman 68, that dough kneaded with honey is obligated in challah according to all opinions because honey is one of the seven liquids. The statement that honey cakes are exempt, explained by Bartenura as dough kneaded with honey, refers to a mixture originally made as a soft batter. If it began and ended as thick dough, it is obligated. Dough made with sugar should have challah separated without a blessing. Responsa Beit Ephraim, Orach Chaim, simanim 11 and 12, likewise writes that the common practice of separating challah from honey cake without a blessing is incorrect.
