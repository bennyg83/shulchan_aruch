The Bach writes that even if he did not actually bake it, whenever he changed his mind and decided to bake part of the dough, whether he removed and baked that part or not, the entire dough becomes obligated. He concludes that as practical law, initially one should separate challah and only afterward remove and bake the small portion. If he first removed and baked the small portion, he must subsequently separate challah from the entire dough as well, but should place the baked portion beside the dough and separate from it and the dough together. See his lengthy discussion. The Shach records this. The Taz argues at length against the Bach and rules otherwise. He distinguishes that if from the outset one intended to bake part, the entire dough becomes obligated immediately. If initially he intended to cook all of it and only afterward decided to bake part, he must actually bake that part first. Although changing one's mind to make bread ordinarily creates an immediate obligation, that is where the entire dough will become bread. Here the whole remains cooked and only a small portion is baked, and the primary obligation is only by decree, so actual baking is required. The Nekudos HaKesef rejects the Taz's words and agrees with the Bach. See siman 100.
