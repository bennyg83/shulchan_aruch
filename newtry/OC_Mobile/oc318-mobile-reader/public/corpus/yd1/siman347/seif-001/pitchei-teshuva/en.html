One may not eulogize him. See Beit Ephraim responsa, Yoreh De'ah siman 78, who discusses this law at length and proves that the view of the great early authorities is that as long as it is still within thirty days of his death, even if it is within thirty days before a festival, it is permitted, for in such a case he is called a newly dead person. It is specifically if at the time of the eulogy thirty days have already passed from his death, and he is within thirty days before the festival, that he is an old death and forbidden. This is also the view of the Radbaz in the new responsa siman 54; see there. Nevertheless, he concludes that one should not move from the words of the Shulchan Aruch, which all Israel have accepted and upheld. However, if it is an urgent need, such as when a gadol hador died and all Torah scholars are obligated to eulogize him, then for the honor of the sage one should eulogize him even within thirty days before a festival; so he himself did in practice. He also wrote that the Gaon Rabbi Yaavetz z"l wrote that there are grounds to permit; but what he wrote, that the prohibition applies only to one's own dead for whom he is obligated to mourn, requires study. See Agudat Ezov, in the pamphlet Alon Bakhut, derush 6, who wrote in the name of Shevut Yaakov II that he ruled to permit eulogizing a Torah scholar within thirty days before a festival because of the honor of Torah. He z"l rejected his proof, but wrote that this is specifically where the deceased's relatives are present; where the deceased's relatives are not present, this prohibition does not apply according to anyone. See there for his reasoning. I then looked in Shevut Yaakov II, end of siman 25, and saw that he z"l also made this distinction: specifically one's own dead, where his heart is very bitter, raises concern according to Rav that he will spend all the money he prepared for the festival, and according to Shmuel because the dead is not forgotten from the heart and he may come to eulogize on the festival. But if it is not his own dead and he eulogizes only for the honor of his Torah, the eulogy is not aroused in his heart so strongly, and we are not concerned. The reason of the honor of Torah he wrote only as an additional support; see there. Agudat Ezov further wrote there that Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur are not like festivals for this matter; see there.
