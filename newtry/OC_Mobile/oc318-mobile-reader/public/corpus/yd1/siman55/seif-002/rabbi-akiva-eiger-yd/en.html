(Shach s.k. 4) And he does not know if after slaughter. It implies that it is known that before slaughter we do not rely on the view that in a short time projections are not consumed; but since there is a safek, they do not permit only because of the safek alone, as Shach wrote afterward; here they combine the view that in a short time there is no consumption of projections and one must inspect projections — whatever can be clarified we clarify; and if lost without inspection — one may say it is permitted:
(Siman 55 seif 2) And they are the projections in the bone. And in Rashi: small sinew whose head is connected to the spine; and in Pri Megadim ot 5 s.v. veda stood on this and wrote to be stringent like both explanations; see there:
(Shach s.k. 6) It implies even if fragments adhere together. And without rejoining at all and there is no defect in skin and flesh — one may say even in great loss it is forbidden, since from the side of breaking the leg itself it is not permitted except in great loss as below seif 5 in the hagahah; and here from consumption of projections which also requires great loss — one may say two leniencies do not combine in great loss; see below (siman 70 seif 6 in the hagahah) and in Taz (siman 91); and they are not lenient here in great loss except when rejoined — requires further study for practical law; afterward I found so in Pri Megadim:
(Shach s.k. 6) One may be lenient in great loss. Meaning only when inspecting projections; but since we are not expert in inspection here, they are lenient in great loss; but without inspection — likewise even in great loss it is forbidden, for above (siman 53 seif 3) they are stringent like the poskim that broken is like slipped even in great loss; if so it should be proven they are lenient even without rejoining, though otherwise forbidden without great loss when there is no defect in skin and flesh — and it is two leniencies in great loss, unlike what I wrote for this; one may say more that Shach deals with well rejoined: from the side of leg breaking there is no prohibition and they are lenient in great loss even without inspection at all, as Shach concludes above (siman 53 s.k. 7); requires further study:
