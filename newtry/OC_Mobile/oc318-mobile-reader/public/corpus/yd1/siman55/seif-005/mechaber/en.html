Every place where if cut there the leg would be treifah — even if it was not cut but only broken and protruded outward, if skin and flesh do not cover most of its thickness and most of its circumference — treifah; and if skin and flesh cover most of its thickness and most of its circumference — kosher. And if a bit of the bone fell off — if there is enough in skin and flesh to cover the break, even if the bone did not fall — kosher; but if there is not enough to cover it — even if the bone did not fall, if there is now not enough to cover it — treifah. And if a bone broke in a place where it would not become treifah if cut — if skin and flesh do not cover most of it, the animal is permitted but the limb is forbidden d'rabbanan; and one must also cut a bit from the permitted when cutting off the forbidden limb: {Rama: And one must cut it before salting it with the animal or bird (Mordechai and Semak); and there are those who are stringent and forbid even if skin and flesh cover most of it, if the break protruded once during life through a small hole (according to Rashi's explanation and Sefer HaTerumah and Semag and Semak), and from this the custom spread in these countries to declare treifah every break in a bone in a place where if cut it would be treifah, even if skin and flesh surround it on all sides and there is no hole at all — nevertheless the custom is to declare treifah, for there are many disputes in the law of flesh covering and not many are expert, and one should not change except in a place of great loss according to what the posek sees fit to be lenient and rule according to halacha (my humble opinion); and specifically if it did not return and rejoin; but if it returned and rejoined — the custom is to declare kosher, as will be explained.}
