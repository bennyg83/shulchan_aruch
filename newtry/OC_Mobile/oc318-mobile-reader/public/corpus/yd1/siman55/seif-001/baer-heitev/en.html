The arkum. If cut in the arkum itself — Shach's conclusion is to forbid, even if it did not protrude outward; and even if the break healed with fragments adhering together — from concern for junction of the sinews (and even below the teshi"ch, between the teshi"ch and the lower ankle — treifah, for the teshi"ch belongs above to the shin (Be'er Heitev); and Peri Chadash wrote: if cut in the arkum, i.e., in the teshi"ch itself in an animal — treifah from the side of breaking the bone if skin and flesh do not cover most of it, but not from junction of the sinews, for there is no place for junction of the sinews there and no concern for their severance; but if cut between the teshi"ch and the lower joint — one may permit; in a bird, if cut between the lower joint and the middle joint — one should declare treifah simply; we do not distinguish whether skin and flesh cover most of it or not — in every case one should declare treifah from concern for junction of the sinews, end of his words). Taz wrote that the ankle mentioned here has two explanations: one is opposite the place called kniya in a foreign tongue; the second explanation is the gelayid between the bones — if so, according to this the teshi"ch itself is kosher, for only at the place of the gelayid is there junction of the sinews, unlike in the teshi"ch, see there.
