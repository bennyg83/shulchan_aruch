One whose house was adjacent etc. It appears from the words of Turei Zahav that even if the house itself is not worshipped, it is forbidden to build a wall for the house, since he benefits the idol; and so appears plainly from the Mishnah and from Rashi in the first chapter of Avodah Zarah (16a), who explained why we taught there "they build with them bimosiot" — meaning a building that is not for the need of idolatry and they offer sacrifices, which is a utensil-of-a-utensil for idolatry — end of his words; and the Tur brings it below. It appears that the utensil of idolatry itself is forbidden; and if so, the house in which idolatry is inside is a utensil of idolatry itself and is forbidden — meaning that we forbid a dome in which they place idolatry because it is a utensil of idolatry, as explained in the Gemara; and the Bach wrote: it appears in the Mishnah chapter "all images" — one whose house was adjacent to an idol — that if the house itself was not worshipped but only that they placed idolatry in it, it would not be called benefiting idolatry in building the wall. And further, it is permitted to build a traklin in which the gentile places a dome to place idolatry in it — according to Rashi until here his words. And I did not descend to the end of his opinion; on the contrary, from there is proof, for specifically in a traklin which is a utensil-of-a-utensil it is permitted — Rashi; if not so, no. And if so, why should it be permitted to build a wall for a house in which they place idolatry? And what Rashi meant by "the house itself was worshipped" — his intent is not as the Bach understood, but he wanted to say because it teaches in the end "its stones and wood and dust defile like a sheretz" (Rashi: it does not defile by carrying) — R. Akiva says like a menstruant (defiles by carrying). And if the house itself was not worshipped but was only utensils of an idol, it would not be that R. Akiva says like a menstruant; for thus we say explicitly — R. Pinchas on this Mishnah. And for R. Akiva, for what halacha do we equate it to a sheretz? For its utensils that do not defile by carrying — see there. You will see that what he wrote is clear; also in derushah he explained Rashi's words on "R. Elazar" and Rashi's language does not indicate his explanation (and what Tosafot wrote, and similarly the pamphlet, is in what he wrote "and the house itself was worshipped" is automatically settled their difficulty).
