For favor. The Taz wrote: according to this it is forbidden to grind or bake in a mill or oven of idolatry. Rashal wrote that "not for favor" means that the whole world benefits from it without favor, but if they waive payment for an individual it is forbidden; on the contrary, he benefits from idolatry, which benefits him, as below siman 149 regarding a tax. The Derishah challenged: how is this different from wax candles in siman 139, where if they went out and he sold them, they are nullified, although this benefits the priests? This is not difficult at all. They forbade only where they made it a fixed law for their priests that it would always be so; but with a candle, on the contrary, their law makes it all belong to the idol, except that this one sells it incidentally, so it is as though he sells other things that are not connected to the idol at all, and it is permitted; end quote. The Shach wrote that with verbal favor, if others had a share in them it is permitted, for since verbal favor is only rabbinic, we follow leniency.
To its worshipers. Meaning the masses who worship that idol; so explained Rashi and the Ran.
To be lenient. The Bach ruled stringently where this profit would not have reached the priests except through this Jew who gave it. But if, had he not bought these fruits or bathed in the bathhouse, gentiles would have come and bought the fruits or bathed in the bathhouse, it is permitted. See above at the end of simanim 148 and 149.
