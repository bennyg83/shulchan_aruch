Without favor. Taz writes that according to this, one may not grind or bake in a mill or oven belonging to idolatry. Maharshal explains that 'without favor' means it is available freely to everyone. If the priests waive the charge only for one individual, it is forbidden because he receives a personal benefit from idolatry. Derishah asks why extinguished wax candles sold by priests are permitted. Taz answers that only an established priestly entitlement is forbidden. Their religious rule ordinarily dedicates the entire candle to the idol, and an occasional sale is like sale of an unrelated object. Shach writes that where others own a share, verbal favor alone is permitted because the prohibition is rabbinic and doubt is treated leniently.
Its worshippers. Meaning the general populace who worship that idol. Rashi and Ran.
To be lenient. Bach rules stringently where this profit would not otherwise have reached the priests but for the Jew's payment. If non-Jews would in any case have bought the produce or used the bathhouse, it is permitted. See the ends of simanim 148 and 149.
