And if he transgressed and built etc. It appears from the words of Turei Zahav that specifically the dome, but the idolatry itself that he hired — is forbidden; and so is the opinion of the Rif. And the Bach wondered: in the Gemara (Avodah Zarah 19b) they say that even the idolatry itself that he hired is permitted — and what the Bach answered is wondrous, to divide against the Gemara in reasoning; and all the more since Tosafot answered his difficulty. However, it appears to me to settle at length that the Rif's opinion is from what we say in the Jerusalem Talmud, halacha 7: "If he transgressed and built — R. Eliezer says permitted; R. Mana said: it is not reasonable according to this view that it is not forbidden, for he gladdens all this dome" — end (meaning: it is not reasonable according to this view that it is forbidden only if he transgressed and built, his hire is forbidden, for he gladdens idolatry all the time the dome exists). And if so, since we see from the words of R. Mana against R. Eliezer, R. Eliezer deals with the dome itself; if so, this variant we answer "it is needed only for the idolatry itself" does not exist. And even though everywhere we rule like our Gemara where it argues with the Jerusalem Talmud — meaning certainly where the Gemara argues with the Jerusalem Talmud — but here R. Yirmiyah explains R. Eliezer's words regarding the idolatry itself, and that does not exist as we see from R. Mana against him; and no difficulty — plainly R. Mana forbids, and R. Yirmiyah himself too, if he had heard that R. Mana forbids, would not have said so. But the Rosh, Ran, and Tosafot there ruled like R. Yirmiyah; and so the Rambam chapter 7 Laws of Avodah Zarah law 5; and the Rashba in Taharat HaBayit 138a; and so the Kolbo siman 97 — and this is the Rav's opinion. And great wonder on the Bach who challenged the Rav, for on the contrary I wonder why he wrote this law in the name of the Kolbo — behold it is the set Gemara, explained by the great decisors mentioned; and no difficulty from the Jerusalem Talmud, for possibly R. Eliezer also deals with the idolatry itself and R. Mana argues even regarding the dome; and it is reasonable for our Gemara that regarding the dome it is plainly permitted, and R. Mana does not exist.
