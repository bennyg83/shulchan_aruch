A dome in which it is erected. From here it follows that it is forbidden to make windows for a house of idol worship, meaning for the chamber in which the idol is situated. One may not permit this because of enmity, for Tosafos already wrote in the first chapter of Avodah Zarah, 19a, regarding ornaments for an idol, that one can excuse oneself by saying that it is forbidden for us to make ornaments for an idol.
Even the idol itself. The reason given in the Gemara is that even according to the one who says that an idol belonging to a non-Jew becomes forbidden immediately, as ruled at the beginning of siman 139, nevertheless it is not called an idol until the final blow struck with the hammer. That final blow is not worth a perutah. Since liability for wages accrues from beginning to end, he is permitted to receive payment for each small portion of work that he performs. So wrote all the poskim. However, the Rif, the Tur, and the Shulchan Aruch did not cite this novel ruling that even payment for making the idol itself is permitted after the fact. It appears that their reason is that they did not wish to reveal this leniency, in order to distance a person from the repulsiveness of this transgression, just as the Sages said in the chapter Mi Shehechshich: They said another matter, but did not wish to reveal it.
But he may build initially, etc. So ruled Rashi and the Rambam regarding the Mishnah that teaches that one may build bimosios with them, meaning something that serves an accessory of idol worship. Tosafos disagree and read in the Gemara dimosios, which means bathhouses. So too ruled the Aruch, and the Tur cited this as the conclusion. According to this, even something that serves an accessory of idol worship is forbidden. It is therefore astonishing that the Shulchan Aruch and the Rama were not concerned to rule stringently, especially regarding the prohibition of idol worship, concerning which we find many times that they were more stringent than regarding other prohibitions. Indeed, at the end of siman 139 they were stringent not to sell them even quills, ink, and other such items. All the more so, many times over, should one forbid building the hall connected to the dome of an idol. It further appears to me that even according to Rashi and the Rambam, the permission applies only when one merely assists others. The Mishnah states explicitly, “He builds with them,” and the Rambam, whose words the Mechaber copied here, wrote at the beginning of the seif, “It is forbidden to build with the non-Jews,” etc. He then states, “But he may build,” meaning specifically together with them. We find many times that mere assistance is considered insignificant, but to build it by oneself is forbidden according to all opinions. In practical halacha, however, it is certainly proper for us to rule like Rabbeinu Yonah, who saw Rashi's words and disagreed with them. To him you shall listen, in order to distance oneself from repulsiveness and anything resembling it. The Beit Yosef wrote in Bedek HaBayis, in the name of Orchos Chaim, that according to Rabbeinu Yonah, one who willingly sells his house for use as an idol temple may not benefit from the money and must cast it into the Dead Sea; but if he acted under compulsion, the money is permitted.
