And it is forbidden to benefit from them with favor. Rashi explained: when he pays them wages. According to this, it is forbidden to grind or bake in a mill or oven of an idol. Rabbeinu Tam explained “with favor” as meaning that he gives them gratitude; this is forbidden, but where he does not give them gratitude it is permitted, even though he gives them wages. So wrote the Tur. Afterward he wrote: this is not comparable to an idolatrous fair, where it is forbidden to pay tax to idolatry; there the tax is for the idol itself and its adornment, but when it is only for the priests’ statute, it is permitted. This refers to Rabbeinu Tam’s explanation, which permits giving wages here because it is not for the idol itself. At first glance one may ask: what was difficult for him from the tax? There the idol greatly benefits from the Jew, who gives it money for nothing, unlike here, where he buys from him and the gentile could also have sold it to another gentile. One may say that in any event this distinction is proven from that law in seif 75, that shops of an idol may not be rented; from there there is a difficulty against Rabbeinu Tam. Therefore one must distinguish that there the wage is for the idol itself, and he answers similarly regarding tax, for both are in the Yerushalmi. Maharshal wrote that “not with favor” means that the whole world benefits from it without favor, but if they waive payment for an individual it is forbidden, for on the contrary, he benefits from the gentile who benefits him, as below in siman 149 regarding tax; end quote. The Rambam wrote that with wages it is worse, and it appears that this is how we rule: whenever it is forbidden with favor, it is forbidden both by giving wages and by giving gratitude. The Derishah asked: how is this different from wax candles in siman 139, where if they went out and the priests sell them, they are nullified; but he is benefiting the priests. He answered that the candle was permitted initially and became an adornment for the idol, and it is permitted when he nullifies the adornment; but a garden and bathhouse were initially set aside for this. I do not know what he means, for the candle too was initially set aside for the idol. The original question is not difficult, for they forbade only where they made a statute for their priests that it should always be so. This is unlike a candle, where, on the contrary, their statute requires it all to belong to the idol, only this priest sells it incidentally; it is as though he sells other movable items that have no connection to the idol at all, and it is permitted.
