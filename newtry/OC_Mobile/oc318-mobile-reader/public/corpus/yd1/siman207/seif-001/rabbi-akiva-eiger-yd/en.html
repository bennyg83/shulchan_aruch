(Siman 207, seif 1, gloss) Likewise, if he does not recognize it. This implies that even if people in that place use this term as a substitute expression for a korban, nevertheless, if the one making the vow did not know that the word had that meaning, his vow is ineffective. In truth, Beit Yosef did not discuss such a case. Beit Yosef writes: 'If in that place and time no unlearned person recognizes the substitute expressions mentioned by the Sages, the utterance has no effect.' This shows that where the common people in the place of the vow do not recognize those expressions, they are not the local language of korban, and therefore no vow takes effect. Everywhere we follow the language of that place and time, as Rambam explains. But if he actually used a recognized substitute expression of that place, even though he personally did not know its meaning, it may be that the vow is effective. Since he intended to make a vow and uttered the local substitute expression, his mouth and heart agree. This is also explicit in Rashba.
