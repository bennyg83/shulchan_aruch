(Siman 207, seif 1, gloss) Likewise, if he does not recognize. This implies that even if, in that locale, the expression is commonly used as a substitute term for a korban, nevertheless if the person making the vow did not know that this was its meaning, the vow is ineffective. But Beit Yosef did not say this. Rather, he writes that if, in that place and time, ordinary people do not recognize those substitute expressions established by the Sages, they have no effect. This shows that when the local populace does not recognize the phrase as a term for korban, it is not considered a vow-expression there, because everything follows the language of the place and time, as Rambam explains. But if the person actually used a recognized local substitute term, even though he personally did not know its precise meaning, it may still be a valid vow, since his intent was to vow and the words he uttered are the local vow-expression. His mouth and heart are therefore consistent. Rashba says this explicitly.
