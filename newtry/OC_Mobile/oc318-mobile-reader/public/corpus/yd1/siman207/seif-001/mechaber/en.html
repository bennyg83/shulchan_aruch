The laws of designations of vows; it contains one se’if. Designations of vows are like vows. How so? Konam, konach, konas — if one said konam, or konach, or konas, “This loaf is upon me,” he is forbidden in it. And likewise in every place, we follow the language to which they are accustomed: if they have a designation for a vow, and he vows with it, it is a vow. (And likewise, if he does not recognize these languages that the Sages spoke, even if he vowed with them, it is nothing) (Beit Yosef; and so it appears from the words of Rambam, perek 1 of Nedarim and Nezirut, and perek 2 of Shevuos). But designations of designations — meaning, a designation that is very far from the language — is not like a vow. (A vow is in any language that one says upon it; it is called a vow) (and so it appears in Beit Yosef and Rambam, end of perek 1 of Nazir).
