This is permitted. The Bach appears to hold that when it is not known that the non-Jew sold it for his own needs, even with ordinary non-Jewish wine one must be concerned that he sold it in order to purchase more ordinary non-Jewish wine, as explained below at the end of siman 144 regarding idolatry. The Derishah in siman 144, however, distinguishes between idolatry and ordinary non-Jewish wine. Regarding ordinary non-Jewish wine, we do not ordinarily suspect that he took the money in order to purchase more of it. See there.
And some say, etc. The Raavad distinguishes as follows. If the creditor has the power to press the non-Jew for payment but willingly grants him additional time, it is forbidden, since the idolatrous object continues to exist through him. But if he lacks the power to press him and therefore must grant additional time, it is permitted. Tur. The Rashba, Ran, and Rabbeinu Yerucham cite the Raavad's words as their conclusion.
