A Jew who was owed a maneh by a non-Jew, and the non-Jew went and sold idols and yayin nesech and brought him their value — this is permitted. But if he said to him, before he sold [them], “Wait for me until I sell the idols or the yayin nesech that I have and I will bring you [the money],” even though it was stam yeinam and he sold and brought him [the money], this is forbidden, because the Jew wants its continued existence in order that he will be paid his debt from it. {Rama: And there are those who say that this prohibition of “wants its continued existence” is only when he cannot collect from another source, but if he can collect from another source — and all the more so if he has a guarantor — it is permitted (Kol Bo in the name of Ran; and Tur in the name of R' Yehuda; and Rosh; and R' Yerucham in the name of Tosafos; and Mordechai at the beginning of the last chapter of Avodah Zarah). However, in our times, when non-Jews do not pour libations, one should not be stringent regarding stam yeinam, and in every case where he said, “Wait until I bring you,” it is permitted (Rashba according to the Geonim, and Ran); and see below siman 144.}
