From that non-Jew. For he does not receive payment for a sale, but compensation for the damage. Even if the non-Jew is unwilling to pay until the Jew gives him the wine, it is permitted. So writes the Ran, as cited by the Beis Yosef.
Who prohibited it. This implies that even if he prohibited it unintentionally, in which case he is exempt from payment, as stated in Choshen Mishpat siman 385, it is nevertheless permitted to take its value from that non-Jew, since he did in fact cause the damage. So rule the Rashba and the Bach.
There is one who says, etc. The Mechaber presents this in the name of “one who says” because Rabbeinu Yerucham cites an opinion that permits only when the non-Jew sold the wine and afterward paid the Jew, so that when he paid the Jew he retained nothing of the wine. The Beis Yosef cites this, and his words there clearly imply that even for others the money is forbidden so long as the non-Jew has not sold the wine. This is the opinion of Rabbi Elchanan cited by the Rosh at the beginning of the final chapter of Avodah Zarah. The Derishah understood that the Mechaber wrote this as “one who says” because he held that according to the Tur, even the owner of the wine may benefit when he first takes back the wine. This is certainly not the view of either the Tur or the Mechaber. As to the practical ruling, although the Beis Yosef overlooked the words of the Rosh at the beginning of the final chapter of Avodah Zarah, from which the Tur and Rabbeinu Yerucham derived their statements, and the Rosh there rules like this opinion, nevertheless the Ramban and the Ran at the beginning of the final chapter of Avodah Zarah prohibit it. The Ran writes that this also appears to be Rashi's view, and Rabbeinu Yerucham likewise did not decide the matter. The Mordechai there, however, follows the Rosh, and this is also the conclusion of Tosafos there in the name of Rabbeinu Yitzchak, as cited by the Beis Yosef below at the end of siman 144. Therefore, where there is a substantial loss it is permitted, but otherwise it is forbidden. Although this has no practical application to ordinary non-Jewish wine, since we rule that nowadays it is permitted for benefit, it does apply to the sale of idolatry and its accessories, as the poskim state explicitly that the same law applies to idolatry. This is clear. See also the end of siman 144.
For the owner of the wine who sold it, the money is forbidden. This is a penalty, as stated by Rabbeinu Yerucham, cited by the Beis Yosef, and likewise by the Rosh there. See also siman 215, s.k. 1.
For others, it is permitted. Since the non-Jew first acquired the wine through meshichah and only afterward paid the money, the payment is not considered proceeds of ordinary non-Jewish wine. So write the Tur and the Rosh there.
