Its value. He did not receive purchase money, but compensation for the damage. Even if the non-Jew refuses to pay until the Jew hands him the wine, it is permitted. Ran. Shach writes that this applies even if the non-Jew prohibited the wine inadvertently, in which case he would ordinarily be exempt from payment, because he nevertheless caused the Jew a loss.
Forbidden. Meaning, even to others, because the wine had already become forbidden before the sale. Taz.
Belonging to the owner. This is a penalty imposed upon the owner, but it is permitted to others. Once the non-Jew acquired the wine through meshichah and only afterward paid, the money is not considered proceeds of yayin nesech. Even for others, however, it is permitted only in a major loss; otherwise it remains forbidden. Although this has little practical effect for ordinary non-Jewish wine nowadays, it is relevant to one who sells idolatry or its accessories, since the laws of idolatry and yayin nesech are parallel.
