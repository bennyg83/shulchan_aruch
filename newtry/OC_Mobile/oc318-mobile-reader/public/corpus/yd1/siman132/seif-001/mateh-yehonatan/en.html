(Siman 132, Shulchan Aruch, seif 2) A Jew who sold his wine, etc., and this is like selling ordinary non-Jewish wine. See Shach, seif katan 11. The Beit Yosef asked that it should be permitted to receive payment from that very non-Jew, as above, etc. The Bach, in the Kuntres Acharon, answered differently. Above, where the non-Jew poured the wine for idolatrous use without the Jew's consent, the Jew may therefore receive the value of the wine from that non-Jew. Here, however, where the non-Jew poured the wine for idolatrous use with the Jew's will and consent, it is forbidden even to receive payment from that very non-Jew. These words are clear; see there. According to this, however, the Bach wrote above in siman 129 that if the Jew informed the non-Jew that he would be away for a substantial period, and the non-Jew had enough time to perform the prohibited act, it is forbidden, and the same applies that it is forbidden to sell the wine to that non-Jew, since he acted with the Jew's consent. End of the Bach's words. This is not comparable at all to our case. Here, the Jew gave the wine into the non-Jew's hand in order for him to pour it for idolatrous use. There, by contrast, even if the Jew informed him that he would be away, he still did not want him to pour the wine for idolatrous use; rather, he assumed that he would not do so. Therefore it is permitted to sell it to that very non-Jew. This is clear.
