(Siman 132, Taz, seif 3) Therefore he writes here, 'Some say,' etc. The Taz explains that it is permitted to others, following Rabbeinu Yerucham cited by the Beit Yosef, and says that the Beit Yosef was imprecise because the permission is an explicit Gemara. The Taz himself is imprecise, for there is no explicit Gemara here. On the contrary, many authorities disagree, as Tosafos and the Rosh state at the end of Avodah Zarah in the name of Rabbi Elchanan. They interpret the law of shemittah as applying only when the forbidden item is no longer in the buyer's possession. Likewise, with stam wine, permission applies only where the non-Jew had already sold the wine and afterward paid the Jew. The Ran at the end of Avodah Zarah discusses this at length, concludes accordingly, and rejects all proofs for permission. Rabbeinu Yerucham also cites this dissenting view, as the Beit Yosef records. All this is explained in Shach, seif katan 3. See siman 100.
