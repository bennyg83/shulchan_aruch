It is permitted to take payment from that non-Jew. For it is not a sale; rather he says to him: you spilled my wine and caused me financial loss — and he takes the value of kosher wine; and even though the non-Jew would not pay unless the Jew gave him wine, nevertheless it is not forbidden — for he owes him as restitution for damage, and not as payment for a sale; so Beit Yosef wrote in the name of the Ran.
The farms are prohibited. This means that the wine was already banned from selling:
And if the gentile took the wine first, etc. So wrote the Tur; and the source is from what is found in chapter HaSokher (Bava Metzia 62, end) — that in the academy of R' Yannai they would borrow Shemittah produce from the poor and repay them in the eighth year; and we do not say the poor acted wrongly when receiving the repayment, for at the time of receiving it the prohibition no longer existed — and it resembles the mishnah: a harlot's payment after he came upon her is permitted for the offering, since it is merely a gift. And Tosafot there derived that the same applies to a gentile's wine bought on credit — since the gentile drew the wine immediately he acquired it, and afterward the money he pays becomes merely a gift. However, the Rosh wrote there that the money is permitted only for others — but not for the Jewish seller himself, since he benefits from the proceeds of idolatry — and he derived this from one who sells orla and betroths a woman with the proceeds, where she is betrothed but the proceeds are forbidden to him. Thus the permission for others is established Talmudic law from the Shemittah passage above, while the prohibition for himself is derived from the case of selling orla. And Beit Yosef missed the derivation of the permission for others from the Shemittah passage, and only derived it from his own reasoning based on the orla case — and therefore was troubled that perhaps orla is different because it does not take hold of its proceeds; and therefore wrote here "there is one who says," etc. that it is permitted for others — referring to the view of R' Yerucham cited by Beit Yosef; but he was not precise, for the permission is established Talmudic law as mentioned. And "there is one who says" should have been stated only regarding the prohibition for himself — namely the view of R' Yerucham cited by Beit Yosef and the Rosh as I mentioned.
