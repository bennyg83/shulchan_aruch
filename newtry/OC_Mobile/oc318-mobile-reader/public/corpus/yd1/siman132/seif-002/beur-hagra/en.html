A Jew, etc. See Rashi on the Mishnah, s.v. 'One who sells,' and the Gemara there on 62b, 'Come and hear: One who sells,' etc. See Rashi there, s.v. 'He fixed the price,' and Tosafot, s.v. 'Rav Ashi,' which cites Rabbeinu Tam's explanation. See below, siman 320, seif 6.
(Likut) A Jew, etc. Until the end of the seif this is Rambam's language, except for the phrases 'that contains residual wine' and 'the non-Jew was,' through 'he shakes it,' which are the Mechaber's language. Rambam also wrote 'before he measured,' etc., and 'if he measured before fixing the price,' etc., following the Mishnah's wording. Beit Yosef explains that Rambam understands the Mishnah as a case where the Jew measured the wine into his own vessel, handed it in that form to the non-Jew, and the non-Jew then drew it from his hand while it was still in the Jew's vessel, after which he touched it, etc. The words 'he measured' in the Mishnah and Rambam are not literal, but mean that he measured, gave it to the non-Jew, and the non-Jew drew it, etc. The reason the text uses measuring in place of drawing is that the price is not ordinarily fixed between measuring and drawing, but either before measuring or after drawing. Therefore the Shulchan Aruch writes 'drew' in place of 'measured.' (End of Likut.)
For he did not rely, etc. The Gemara there.
Since, etc. This is the Tur's language: If he measured before fixing the price, it is forbidden. Although this involves only an admixture of ordinary gentile wine, it is very likely that the non-Jew will touch it after drawing it, etc.
When does this apply, etc. The Gemara there.
(Likut) When does this apply, etc. The Gemara there says: If you think that drawing acquires, etc.; if he measures, etc. This shows that drawing is effective only in such a case.
But if, etc. There the Gemara says: No, it is necessary where he measures, etc. Although the Gemara later asks: In the end, etc., and answers that the case is where, etc., the Tur and Rama write accordingly. One can explain that this further answer was needed only to address the Gemara's question, 'In the end, when it reaches,' etc., which would prove that a poured stream is a connection. Since we rule that a poured stream is a connection, that additional answer is unnecessary, and the first answer, 'where he measures,' etc., suffices. This is why the Mechaber writes, 'or into the vessel of,' etc., even though the Gemara says that in such a case it is permitted even in the non-Jew's vessel, for it says: If the non-Jew is holding it, etc. According to the foregoing this is resolved: all that was said according to the view that a poured stream is not a connection, but according to the view that it is a connection, even if it is the Jew's vessel, once the non-Jew shakes it the wine in the vessel becomes forbidden, and the same applies to the non-Jew's vessel. The Tur and Rama, however, write according to the Gemara's conclusion. Beit Yosef writes that they understand that a poured stream involving ordinary gentile wine is not forbidden for benefit. See siman 126 in the Tur and Beit Yosef, and see the Shach. The phrase 'that contains residual wine' means even at the bottom, namely enough moisture to wet another surface. Without that amount it is not forbidden for benefit, as stated in the Mishnah in chapter 2, 29b, regarding non-Jewish wineskins, etc., whose proceeds are permitted.

(Likut) Nevertheless, if, etc. The Gemara there asks: According to whom? Not according to Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, etc. We follow Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel regarding ordinary gentile wine, even wine mixed with wine, as stated there on 74a. Nevertheless, initially it is forbidden. So writes the Tur. This requires study, for there it appears that even initially it is permitted. Otherwise, what is the Gemara's objection to Rav, since Rav was speaking initially? Ran writes that for this reason the Rif omitted the entire ruling. Perhaps the objection concerns the statement, 'When you take the money, take the value of the yayin nesech,' etc. This requires study.
Except for the value, etc. The Tur omits this, because the residual wine belongs to the non-Jew and has no monetary value. See the Shach.
(Likut) Except for the small value, etc. See Shach, seif katan 20. His objection is not difficult, because this ruling follows Ran's explanation of the Mishnah, 'He removed the funnel,' etc., that 'forbidden' means forbidden for benefit. If it meant only forbidden for drinking, even without residual wine it would be forbidden because of the non-Jew's vessels. According to this, since we follow Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, we do not follow that Mishnah. The Rif's citation of it is therefore surprising. Rather, even Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, who permits selling the entire mixture, permits only when the seller explicitly excludes the value of the prohibition. If he sells it without specification, it is forbidden even though he derives no benefit from the prohibited substance. The Tur, who does not require an explicit exclusion and writes simply that after the fact it is permitted, holds like Tosafot there, s.v. 'If there is,' etc., despite what is stated later. This accords with siman 137, seif 1, 'if it is,' etc., so the prohibition there is only for drinking. Mordechai writes likewise, and Ran cites this view in the name of others. See what I wrote at the beginning of siman 137. Orach Chaim, siman 447, seif 10, likewise says that it is not sold at a higher price, etc., proving that even an unspecified sale is permitted. (End of Likut.)
Even initially, etc. The Gemara there. We rule in Bava Batra that a buyer's vessel in the seller's domain acquires when the seller says, 'Go and acquire,' as stated there on 86a. Without such a statement the matter remains unresolved, and the authorities rule leniently that it does not acquire.
(Likut) Even initially, etc. There, at the end of the passage: When it reaches, etc.; and the case is where there is, etc., as explained above in seif katan 11. Rambam holds that a poured stream renders the entire wine forbidden even according to Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, unlike Tosafot and the Rama above, siman 126, seif 5 in the gloss. (Likut) Likewise, if he measured it, etc. Rashba explains in the name of Rabbeinu Chananel and Ramban that the non-Jew measured it for himself and had drawn it before measuring. Although he lifted it while measuring, he did not acquire it, because that lifting was not done for acquisition. The wine became forbidden immediately through his measuring, even without direct contact, because of his force, as stated there: Rav Sheshet explained it as involving a non-Jew, etc. Rashba follows his view that wine affected by the non-Jew's force is forbidden even for benefit. The Rosh and Tur, however, rule that it remains permitted for benefit. Therefore, even when the non-Jew measures it, all the further conditions are required. This is the meaning of 'but if,' etc., 'its law is,' etc. (End of Likut.)

(Likut) All this does not, etc. Although wine touched by that non-Jew is permitted, as stated there on 59b, Beit Yosef writes that according to Rambam this is understandable because Rambam limits that case to one who intended to damage the Jew. According to Rashba, who disagrees and writes that even unintended contact forbids, the matter requires study. It appears to me that in such a case even Rashba concedes, since the non-Jew touches it with the Jew's consent. (End of Likut.)
