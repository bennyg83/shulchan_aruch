An Israelite, etc. See Rashi on the Mishnah, s.v. “one who sells,” etc., and in the Gemara there 72b, “come and hear: one who sells,” etc. See Rashi there s.v. “he fixed a price,” etc., and Tosafot s.v. “Rav Ashi,” etc., where Rabbenu Tam explained, etc. See above siman 320 seif 6 and what is written there.
(Likkut) An Israelite, etc. Until the end of the seif is the Rambam's language, except that “which has a residue of wine” and “and the non-Jew was moving it” are the Mechaber's language. In the Rambam's words, too, he ruled: if he fixed the price before he measured, etc.; if he measured before he fixed the price, etc., like the Mishnah's language. The Beit Yosef wrote that the Rambam explains the Mishnah as where the Israelite measured into his own vessel and then gave it to the non-Jew, and the non-Jew pulled it from his hand in the Israelite's vessel and afterward touched it, etc. “Measured” as taught in the Mishnah and Rambam is not precise, but means he measured, gave it to the non-Jew, and the non-Jew pulled it, etc. The reason it mentions measuring instead of pulling appears to be that it is not normal to fix a price between measuring and pulling, but either before measuring or after pulling; this is why Shulchan Aruch says “pulled” in place of “measured.” For she did not rely, etc. Gemara there.
And since, etc. The language of the Tur: if he measured before fixing the price, it is forbidden. Although here there is only a mixture of ordinary non-Jewish wine, it is likely that he will touch it after pulling it, etc.
In what case, etc. There in the Gemara.
(Likkut) In what case, etc. Gemara there: if you think meshikhah, etc.; if he was measuring, etc. Thus specifically in such a case meshikhah is taught. But if, etc. There: it is necessary where he was measuring, etc. Although the Gemara asks there, “ultimately,” etc., and answers that the case is such-and-such, and so wrote the Tur and the Rav in the gloss, one can say the Gemara needed that because of its difficulty, “ultimately, when it reaches,” etc., learn from here that nitzok is connection. But since we rule that nitzok is connection, we do not need this; the first answer alone suffices: it is necessary where he was measuring, etc. This is why he writes “or into a vessel of,” etc. Although in the Gemara they said that in such a case even with a non-Jew's vessel it is permitted, as stated there: if the non-Jew holds it, etc., according to what I wrote it is understandable. All of that is according to the view that nitzok is not a connection; but according to the view that it is a connection, even in an Israelite's vessel it is forbidden, as the Mechaber explains onward: “and the non-Jew was moving,” etc., for then what is in the vessel becomes forbidden, and it is the same as a non-Jew's vessel. But the Tur and the Rav wrote like the conclusion. The Beit Yosef wrote that they hold ordinary non-Jewish wine through nitzok is not forbidden for benefit. See siman 126 in the Tur and Beit Yosef, and the Shach. “That has a residue of wine” means even at its bottom; meaning moist enough to moisten, for otherwise it is not forbidden for benefit, as stated in the Mishnah chapter 2, 29b: the skins of non-Jews, etc., but their proceeds are permitted. Moving, etc. As above in siman 124 seif 18.
For then necessarily, etc. See Shach, who challenged him.
He must take, etc. See Tosafot there s.v. “take,” etc., alternatively, etc. So ruled the Rambam in Hilkhot Zechiyah 1:14, and the Maggid Mishneh there wrote that he learned it from this sugya, unlike the sugya in Bekhorot 13a-b, “your fellow” in one way, etc. So wrote the Rambam in Hilkhot Bekhorot chapter 4. See what I wrote in Choshen Mishpat at the end of siman 194. Nevertheless, etc. Gemara there, according to Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, for we rule like him even regarding wine mixed with wine in ordinary non-Jewish wine, as stated there 74a.
(Likkut) Nevertheless if, etc. There: according to whom? Not Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, etc. We rule like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel regarding ordinary non-Jewish wine, even wine mixed with wine, as stated there 74a. Nevertheless lechatchilah it is forbidden. Tur. This requires study, for there it implies that even lechatchilah it is permitted; otherwise what does it ask against Rav, since Rav speaks lechatchilah? So wrote the Ran, and therefore the Rif omitted all this. Perhaps the question is against the statement “when you take payment for yayin nesech,” etc.; this requires study. Except the value of, etc. This is not in the Tur, for the residue belongs to the non-Jew and is also worth nothing. See Shach.
(Likkut) Except the value of a little, etc. See Shach s.k. 20. But this is not difficult at all, for he holds like what the Ran wrote on the Mishnah “he took the funnel,” etc.: this says it is forbidden for benefit, for if it meant drinking, without residue it is forbidden because of non-Jews' jugs. According to this, since we rule like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, we do not rule like that Mishnah; therefore one should wonder at the Rif for writing it. Rather, even according to Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, who permits selling all of it, etc., that is specifically when he specifies; but if he sells plainly, although he does not benefit from the prohibited part, it is forbidden. The Tur, who did not write that it is specifically where he specifies, but wrote plainly that after the fact it is permitted because we rule, etc., holds like Tosafot there s.v. “if there is,” etc., although below, etc., and as written in siman 137 seif 1: “and if it is,” etc. If so, this is forbidden for drinking. So wrote the Mordechai there, and so wrote the Ran there in the name of others. See what I wrote at the beginning of siman 137. So too in Orach Chayim 447:10, “it is not sold for more,” etc., implying that even plainly it is permitted. And even lechatchilah, etc. Gemara there. We rule in Bava Batra that a buyer's vessels in the seller's domain acquire if he said “go acquire,” as stated there 86a; without that it is an unresolved question, and the poskim ruled leniently, that it does not acquire.
(Likkut) And even lechatchilah, etc. There: ultimately, when it reaches, etc.; the case is where there is, etc., as above in s.k. 11. But according to the Rambam this is unnecessary, and even after the fact it is forbidden, as the Rambam wrote there in halakhah 24: if he measured and did not take payment, although he fixed its price, its money is forbidden, etc.; see the glosses and Lechem Mishneh there. For he holds that nitzok makes all of it forbidden even according to Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, unlike Tosafot and the Rav above in siman 126 seif 5 in the gloss; see there in s.k. 11, Likkut 2. But if, etc. There: ultimately, when it reaches, etc.

Or he held it in his hand. There: if he held it, etc.
Or he stands, etc. There: learn from here that his vessel, etc. We rule that where he said “go acquire,” it acquires, as stated in Bava Batra 86a and in Shulchan Aruch there. For immediately when it comes, etc. See Taz and Shach.
(Likkut) And likewise if he measured it, etc. So the Rashba explained in the name of Rabbenu Chananel and Ramban: the non-Jew measured it for himself and first pulled it before measuring. Although he lifted it when he measured, he did not acquire through that lifting, because that lifting was not for acquisition; and the wine becomes prohibited immediately by his measuring, even though he did not touch it, because of his force, as stated there: Rav Sheshet translated it as with a non-Jew, etc. The Rashba follows his view that force prohibits even for benefit. But the Rosh and Tur ruled that it is permitted for benefit, and therefore even with the non-Jew's measuring all these conditions are needed. This is why he writes, “but if, etc., its law is,” etc. And he lifted it first. See Shach.
And all this, etc. As above in seif 1 in the gloss.
(Likkut) And all this does not, etc. Although from that non-Jew idolater it is permitted, as stated there 59b, the Beit Yosef wrote that according to the Rambam this is understandable, for he holds it applies specifically where he intended to damage it. But according to the Rashba, who disagrees and writes that even unintentionally it is forbidden, this requires study. To me it appears that in such a case even the Rashba agrees, since he touches with the Jew's consent.
