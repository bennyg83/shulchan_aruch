And likewise if the gentile measured it, etc. So wrote the Tur — and the explanation of his words is as follows: the mishnah states: "if he set a price and measured — it is permitted; if he did not set a price and measured — it is forbidden." The first clause has two interpretations: Rashi explained it as the Jew measuring into the gentile's vessel — and the Tur elaborated at length with all the distinctions of these laws; and R' Chananel and R' Tam explained that even if the gentile measured it, it is permitted — since the case is that the gentile already drew and acquired the wine before the measuring. Therefore the Tur also recorded this interpretation — that even in this case it is permitted, as long as the gentile first acquired it. However it appears to the Tur from his own reasoning that ab initio it is forbidden, because it is close to touching the wine since he is engaged with it in measuring. The Tur was compelled to write this here — something he did not write in other places about the prohibition against a gentile's indirect force — because in other places the wine is at any rate forbidden for drinking, and therefore one would obviously not do so ab initio; but here where the wine belongs to the gentile, one might think it is entirely permitted — therefore the Tur teaches us that even so one should not do so ab initio since it is close to touching. Hence the seller takes payment for what is potentially yayin nesech since the gentile did not make a proper drawing first; and I did not find a source for this — therefore it seems the Tur wrote this from his own reasoning. Afterward the Tur wrote the interpretation of the seifa of the mishnah: "he measured without first setting a price — it is forbidden" — meaning both where the Jew measured and where the gentile measured and drew — nevertheless the wine has not yet left Jewish ownership, since the price was not set, and the gentile's mind did not settle on having acquired it — therefore it is likely he touched it. And this is what the Tur wrote: even though there is here only a mixture of stam yeinam — meaning to say: lest one think that since the first clause permits where there is no wine-residue at the bottom of the vessel, that exemption applies here too — it is forbidden, since the gentile has not yet acquired the wine — since the price was not set first; but where the first clause permits because there is no wine-residue at all, that too is permitted here. This interpretation is not correct — for why does the seifa of the mishnah state universally "their payment is forbidden," while the prohibition of wine-residue merely means one must sell minus the value of the yayin nesech? And the language "it is forbidden" implies even after the fact, while for cases involving only wine-residue, the wine is permitted after the fact as stated above. Therefore the Tur answered that the prohibition is lest he touch the wine after drawing — and therefore even without any wine-residue it is forbidden. And I see someone who forced this interpretation unnecessarily. And a difficulty arises: since in the first clause — where the gentile did not lift the vessel first to acquire it — the Tur also wrote that it is close to touching, and yet even so the wine is only forbidden ab initio; while in the seifa where he lifted it to acquire it and is more accustomed to it, why is the wine forbidden even after the fact? And it seems that in the first clause the gentile did not make a drawing in the normal manner — only measuring — and is not so accustomed to it; while in the seifa where he drew to acquire it, he is more accustomed to it and the matter is more likely to come to touching — and therefore even after the fact it is forbidden. So it seems to me is the explanation of the Tur's words — not as Beit Yosef who explained "he measured without first setting a price" as referring specifically to a gentile who measured — and Darkei Moshe already challenged this at length and wrote that the prohibition refers both to where the gentile measured and where the Jew measured, since the reason is lest the gentile touch the wine after the drawing, as the price was not set first so the gentile's mind had not settled on acquiring it; but what the Tur wrote above "however after the fact it is permitted, for we hold, etc." — there the gentile did not acquire it by drawing in the normal manner, only through his own permission or the Jew's telling him "your vessel acquires on your behalf" — and therefore he is not so accustomed to it as when he drew to acquire it. And Beit Yosef raised a difficulty: but at the beginning of the siman it writes that from a gentile who libated the wine one may take the payment — and this is no difficulty at all — for here it is different since the Jew gave him permission to touch by delivering it into his hand, which is not the case there.
