And likewise if the gentile measured it, etc. Tur likewise; and the explanation of his words is thus: in the Mishnah it states: he ceased and measured — permitted; did not cease and measured — forbidden; and this first part has two explanations — Rashi explained that the Jew measured into a gentile's vessel; and as Tur expanded these legal distinctions; and R. Chaim and Rabeinu Tam explained that even if the gentile measured it is permitted — it deals where the gentile already performed drawing and acquired before the measuring; therefore permitted also in this; and perforce Tur also wrote this explanation — that also in this it is permitted whenever the gentile acquired first; except it seems to him from his own reasoning that le-chatchilah it is forbidden — because he is likely to touch the wine, since he is engaged with him in his measuring; and Tur was compelled to write thus here what he did not write elsewhere — forbidden through the gentile's power — because elsewhere in any case it is forbidden for drinking; if so, certainly he would not do so le-chatchilah; which is not the case here, where it belongs to the gentile — he informs us that even so he should not do so le-chatchilah, for he is likely to touch; therefore libated-wine money is taken — since he did not perform drawing properly first; and I did not find a source for this; therefore it seems he wrote from his own reasoning; afterward Rabbeinu wrote the explanation of the latter part of the Mishnah above: he measured before he ceased — forbidden; and that is whether the Jew measured or the gentile measured and performed drawing — in any case it has not yet left the Jew's possession, since he did not cease the allocation; he does not rely that it will be drawn with him; and therefore it is likely he touched it; and this is what Tur wrote: even though here there is only mixture of stam yeinam — meaning: that which is permitted in the first part has wine residue in its dregs — forbidden here, since he did not acquire it yet, since he did not cease first; but in what is permitted in the first part where there is no wine residue at all — permitted also here; this is not correct — for why does the latter part of the Mishnah teach stam that his money is forbidden; and prohibition of wine residue is only "he shall sell except libated-wine money"; and why did the Mishnah say all the money is forbidden; and further that the language "forbidden" implies even be-di'eved; and if because of wine residue it is permitted be-di'eved — as he wrote above; therefore he answered: the prohibition is lest he touch after the drawing; and if so, even where there is no wine residue at all it is also forbidden; and I saw one who strained unnecessarily here; and one may challenge: since in the first part — where he did not lift first to acquire — he also wrote that it is likely to touch; and even so it is not forbidden except le-chatchilah; and it seems in the first part the gentile did not perform drawing properly — only his measuring is not so habitual with him as in the latter part, where he drew to acquire it — more habitual with him; and more likely to touch; therefore even be-di'eved forbidden; so it seems to me is the explanation of Tur's words; and not like Beit Yosef, who explained "he measured before he ceased" — deals specifically with a gentile who measured it; and they already challenged in Darkei Moshe at length; and wrote it deals whether gentile measured or Jew measured — forbidden, since the main reason is lest he touch the wine after he acquired it through drawing; and that which Tur wrote above "and nevertheless be-di'eved permitted" — for we hold, etc.; there the gentile did not acquire through drawing — only through his permission, or the Jew said to him "acquire for you your vessels"; therefore he is not so habitual as if he acquired through drawing. And Beit Yosef challenged: behold he wrote at the beginning of the siman that from that gentile who libated it, permitted to take the money — and it is not difficult; for here it is different — that the Jew gave him permission to touch, since he handed it to his hand — which is not so above.
