One who hired, etc. As stated there on 63b: Rav Ashi said, etc.
If he did not, etc. This follows Rav Pappa's resolution. Rabbi Abbahu did not dispute Rav Pappa, but objected only that the wording should have been 'and I calculate,' etc.
Although, etc. The Gemara's statement there, 'since he did not designate his lien,' is not exact, for the dinar was not acquired by the non-Jew. See Torat HaBayit there.
Specifically, etc. As stated there on 63a: What are the circumstances? If he said, 'Acquire,' etc.; rather, he said, 'Let it remain,' etc. This shows that even in such a case it is permitted.
(Likut) Specifically when he told him, etc. See what is stated there: When he advanced a dinar it is forbidden because it resembles the case where he first gave it and afterward cohabited with her, regarding which the Gemara asks that the prohibition should take effect, etc., as above. This differs from the case answered there: Rather, he said, 'Let it remain with you,' etc. This requires study, since the Gemara says that only if she first brought it as an offering is it permitted in such a case. This requires study. See Torat HaBayit. The Tur likewise disagreed with him on this point. See there. (End of Likut.)
Likewise one who, etc. He transfers the dinar to the non-Jew through his servant. It appears to me that 'his servant' is not literal, because the hand of a servant is like the hand of his master, as stated in Bava Metzia 99a. Rather, it means his hired worker.
