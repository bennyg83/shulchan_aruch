If he did not advance, etc. See Orach Chaim siman 450, se'if 6, and what I wrote there.
If he advanced him a dinar, it is forbidden. For they are then eating from the employer's property.
But if they gave him, etc. The Tur disputes this and writes: “This does not appear correct, for once he advanced him a dinar, the wine was immediately acquired for him, and the shopkeeper gave it to them as his agent.” The Beis Yosef writes that there is no doubt the Tur did not see the Rashba's words in Toras HaBayis, where the Rashba proves this from the law of one who gave a lamb as a harlot's fee and only afterward cohabited with her, in which case the payment is permitted. On this basis the Beis Yosef rules like the Rashba, followed by the Perishah and Bach. In my opinion, however, according to the Tur the cases are not comparable. In the case of the lamb, at the time of cohabitation the lamb was no longer in his possession but in hers. Here, by contrast, as soon as he advanced the dinar, the wine was acquired for him, as the Tur writes. It is therefore as though he had deposited his wine with the shopkeeper, who then gave it to the workers as his agent. Perhaps the Tur knew the Rashba's proof and nevertheless disagreed. This also appears from Tosafos in Avodah Zarah, folio 67, at the beginning of the second side, where they write that anyone who receives money from another for an object he is to give him thereby encumbers his property. See there. Rabbeinu Yerucham and the other poskim likewise state without qualification that if he advanced a dinar, it is forbidden, and they do not distinguish this case. Nevertheless, where loss is involved, we rule that nowadays ordinary non-Jewish wine is permitted for benefit.
It is permitted to give money, etc. The Bach explains that he gives his servant a dinar so that the servant should acquire wine for these non-Jewish workers. The employer transfers ownership of the dinar to the non-Jew through his servant, and the servant becomes the non-Jew's agent to purchase the wine for him. This implies that if the employer merely says, “Buy such and such wine,” the servant purchases it for the employer, and it is forbidden. It appears that this applies specifically to his own servant. It is forbidden to say this to another Jew, since that Jew thereby desires the continued existence of ordinary non-Jewish wine until he completes his agency for the non-Jew, as explained below in siman 133, se'if 5, regarding pouring wine for a non-Jew without payment. See there.
To his servant, etc. The Beis Yosef writes in Bedek HaBayis that this may be questioned, since a servant's hand is legally like his master's hand.
