(Seif 4) One who hired workers, etc., but if the gentile gave him to take out now, etc. Shach seif 31; but in my humble opinion, according to Tur it is not comparable, etc. — see there; and ostensibly it is against Gemara Avodah Zarah 63b in chapter "one who hires," which says: he came upon her and afterward gave her her hire — permitted; and they challenged it, etc.; R. Nachman bar Yitzchak said, Rav Chisda said: it is not difficult — this where he said to her "be my harlot for this lamb" — since at the time of relations he became obligated to give her this lamb specifically as hire, therefore forbidden even if he gave afterward; and that where he said "be my harlot" anonymously — since the lamb was not clarified at the time of relations, even if he gave her afterward it is permitted; if so, likewise here — at the time he gave them the dinar the wine was not yet clarified at all, and whatever wine he wishes he gives afterward to the workers; and if so, why do we say here it is as if deposited, and there we do not say thus? For behold the relations there are like giving money to a storekeeper — here; and one may answer: there is much difference between them — for here with libated wine, exchange of libated wine is also forbidden in benefit; if so, since he preceded the dinar he acquired the libated wine; and even though he can give him whatever wine he wishes, nevertheless wine he is obligated to give him; and therefore afterward when he gives the workers — perforce either he gives the wine he said initially at the time of giving the money, or it is exchange — behold he benefits from prohibited benefit, or from libated wine itself, or from exchange of libated wine which is also forbidden in benefit; unlike regarding hire, where exchange is permitted; therefore if the lamb was not clarified at the time of relations, there is no hire on her — only exchange there, and permitted; and it is not comparable at all to the case here; and it is simple.
