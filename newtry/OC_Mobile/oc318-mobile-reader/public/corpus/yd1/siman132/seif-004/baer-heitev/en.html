Shach writes that the Tur challenges this ruling, and the Tur's view appears primary: once the Jew advanced a dinar, the wine was immediately acquired for him, and it was delivered to the non-Jews as his agent. Nevertheless, in a case of loss ordinary non-Jewish wine nowadays is permitted for benefit.
To his servant. Bach explains that the Jew gives a dinar to his servant to acquire wine for these non-Jews. The servant transfers the dinar to them and becomes their agent to acquire the wine. If the master merely says, 'Buy such-and-such wine,' the servant purchases it for the master and it is forbidden. This arrangement applies only through his servant; it is forbidden to instruct another Jew in this manner, because the Jew desires the continued existence of the yayin nesech until completing the non-Jew's agency. Beit Yosef questions the entire matter because a servant's hand is like his master's.
