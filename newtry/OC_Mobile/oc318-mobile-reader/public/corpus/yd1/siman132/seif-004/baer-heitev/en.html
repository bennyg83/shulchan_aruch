Permitted. The Shach wrote that the Tur challenged this, and it appears that his words are primary, for since he gave him a dinar in advance, the wine was immediately acquired by him, and the agent gave it to them on his behalf. However, in a case of loss we hold that ordinary gentile wine nowadays is permitted for benefit.
To his slave. The Bach wrote: the explanation is that he gives his slave a dinar to buy for those gentiles, meaning he transfers the dinar to the gentiles through his slave, and he becomes an agent to buy the wine for the gentile. It implies, however, that if he said to him generally, "buy such-and-such wine," the slave acquires the wine only for the owner, and it is forbidden. It appears that this is specifically his slave; but it is forbidden to say this to another Jew, for he wants the continued existence of gentile wine until he performs his agency for the gentile, as below siman 133 seif 5. However, the Beit Yosef wrote in Bedek HaBayit that one can question this, because the hand of a slave is like the hand of his master.
