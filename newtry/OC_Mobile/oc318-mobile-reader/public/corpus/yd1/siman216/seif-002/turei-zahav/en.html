Whose seed disintegrates. This refers to their growths. Regarding their exchanges, however, there is no distinction, for even when the seed does not disintegrate he is permitted in them.
He is permitted in their exchanges. When he eats their exchanges or growths, he does not taste the fruits that he forbade upon himself, and he said only, “that I will not taste.”
Even the growths of their growths. One should not ask that the prohibited substance should be nullified in the growths that increased over it, for this is a davar sheyesh lo matirin, since a vow can be released through an opening or regret, and anything that has a permitted means of release is not nullified in a majority. Beit Yosef in the name of the Rambam. This also resolves why it is not permitted on the basis of zeh vezeh gorem, as the Beit Yosef asked in the name of the Raavad, and so the Beit Yosef wrote shortly afterward.
And likewise, if he said, etc. This is a question in the Gemara, whether he is forbidden in their exchanges. The issue is whether he can forbid upon another person something that has not yet come into existence, since he cannot forbid another person’s fruits upon that other person. This differs from when he forbids them upon himself, for he can even forbid another person’s fruits upon himself. Alternatively, perhaps with respect to exchanges and growths there is no distinction between himself and another person. We therefore rule stringently.
