Forbidden in their substitutes. In every manner that was explained, as he forbade upon himself — Shach. And see in the responsum of Rabbi Akiva Eiger siman 65 in the name of his son the Rav Maharash Eiger, who argued on this, for one of the manners explained is: when he said "these fruits are upon me" — if it is a matter whose seed is consumed, growths of growths are permitted but the grown items themselves are forbidden. And the reason that the prohibition is not nullified in the grown items is explained in Shach se'if kaf gimmel — that it is a matter that could have been permitted. And this is correct when he says konam upon me, for it is in his power to release; but when he says konam upon so-and-so, for so-and-so has no power to release, it is fitting that the growths themselves also be permitted — for now it is not a matter that could have been permitted (see above siman 102 se'if yud vav what is written on this). If so, it is not in every manner explained — see there. And see in the responsum of Brit Avraham part 4 siman 37 letter bet, and investigate.
