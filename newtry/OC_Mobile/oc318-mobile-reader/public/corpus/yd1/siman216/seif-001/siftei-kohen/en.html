And likewise forbidden in growths of their growths. In a matter whose seed is not consumed — and Bach wrote that Rambam, Rosh, and Tur did not forbid growths of growths except when he says konam what I eat or taste from them, as below se'if 2 — and not here. And such we have established — end quote. And nothing is implied from their words; for if because they wrote thus in the latter part — behold as Ran explained the Mishnah, the latter part also refers to the first part — so their words may be explained. And so it appears explicitly in Kesef Mishneh who explains thus the words of the Rambam, and so Derishah explained explicitly the words of the Tur. And so is the language of the Mishnah in Rosh: "One who says to his wife konam the work of your hands upon me, konam they are" — whether per konam or per "upon" — forbidden in their substitutes and in their growths; but in a matter whose seed is not consumed, growths of growths are forbidden. And even though in our Mishnah the language is not so — it is possible it is a scribal omission in Asheri. Nevertheless the proof is clear. And also Tosafot (page 55) wrote: therefore growths of growths are forbidden in a matter whose seed is not consumed; but in a matter whose seed is consumed, the growths are forbidden and growths of growths are permitted — end quote. And therefore when he says konam these fruits, it deals with this, since they wrote in the latter part "the growths are forbidden" — therefore one should not be lenient against the view of the Ran and the author, since there is no dispute in the matter. And also in Tosafot it appears thus:
(The tumors are allowed. But the tumors themselves are prohibited and lie in the name of the Rambam and why it will not be prohibited in the tumors that are limited to them that it is something that it does not contain in most cases and does not write in May, nor does it write anything, nor does it mean that “the chapter of the candle is made from the void.”
