In their substitutes. — meaning he exchanged them for something else, that something else is forbidden; and Rosh wrote in chapter “the one who vows from vegetables” — specifically to the one who exchanged they are forbidden, but to another they are permitted; and likewise if another exchanged them, they are permitted to the one who vowed — so Semag in the name of R' Yosef; and Ran forbids in every case, treating them like hekdesh (but where he did not say “these,” Ran wrote the substitute is permitted — see there); and Tosafot in chapter HaShutafin (Nedarim page 47) wrote like Rosh.
And the growths of their growths are likewise forbidden. This applies to something whose seed does not disappear. The Bach writes that Rambam, the Rosh, and the Tur forbid the growths of growths only where one said, 'Konam that which I eat or taste from them,' as below in seif 2, but not here, and that this is the practical ruling. End. Their words, however, do not imply this. If his proof is that they wrote the law in the latter clause, the Ran explains the Mishnah so that the latter clause applies to the first clause as well, and their words can likewise be understood that way. The Kesef Mishnah explicitly explains Rambam's words in this manner, and the Derisha explicitly explains the Tur this way. The text of the Mishnah as quoted by the Rosh also reads: 'One who says to his wife, Konam your handiwork upon me, Konam that which they produce, Konam that which they put into my mouth, is forbidden in their substitutes and their growths; but in a matter whose seed does not disappear, the growths of the growths are forbidden.' Although our Mishnah does not have this wording and it is possible that a copyist omitted something in the Rosh, what proof is there to the contrary? Tosafos on daf 55 likewise write: therefore the growths of growths are forbidden where the seed does not disappear, but where the seed disappears the first growths are forbidden and the growths of growths are permitted. End. They necessarily discuss one who said, 'Konam these fruits,' because they write in the latter clause that the first growths are forbidden. Therefore one should not be lenient against the view of the Ran and the Mechaber when no authority clearly disagrees, and Tosafos also imply their view.
Growths of growths are permitted. — but the growths themselves are forbidden; and Beit Yosef in the name of Rambam chapter 5 asks: why should the prohibition in the growths not be nullified since the majority growth surpassed them — for it is a matter that has a way to become permitted and is not nullified by majority — and does not explain the derivation; also Kesef Mishneh wrote nothing; and it is explicit Gemara chapter “the one who vows from vegetables” (Nedarim page 59a).
Fruit of Ploni. — meaning of a specific person; but if he said “fruit of such-and-such species” — meaning konam figs or grapes upon me — since he did not specify and forbade the entire species upon him, he did not treat them as hekdesh, for his intention was only regarding eating that species; therefore he is not forbidden in their substitutes or their growths — so in Ran; and not like Taz.
