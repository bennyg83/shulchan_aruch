In their substitutes. If he exchanged the prohibited items for something else, the substitute is forbidden. Rosh limits this to the person who made the exchange; others may benefit, and if someone else exchanged them, the one who vowed may benefit. Ran is stringent in every case when he said 'these,' treating the objects like hekdesh. If he did not say 'these,' Ran permits substitutes made by another.
In their growths. This concerns a plant whose seed does not decompose. Bach limits the prohibition of successive growths to a vow phrased, 'What I eat or taste from them.' Taz and Shach disagree and rule like the Mechaber.
Permitted. Taz cites a text of Rosh forbidding even substitutes of substitutes. Nekudot HaKesef writes that this is a textual error.
Of a certain person. Meaning produce belonging to a particular person. If he prohibited an entire species, such as figs or grapes, he intended only not to eat that species and did not render the individual objects like hekdesh. Therefore their substitutes and growths are permitted. Shach.
