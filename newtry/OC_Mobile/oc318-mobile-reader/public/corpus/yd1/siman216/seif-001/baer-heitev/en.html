In their substitutes. (That is, if he substituted something else for them, that other thing is forbidden.) Rosh wrote: specifically for the one who made the substitution they are forbidden, but for others they are permitted. And likewise if another person substituted for them, they are permitted for the one who vowed. Ran forbids in every case, treating it like hekdesh. However, where he did not say "these," Ran wrote that it is permitted for others to substitute — see there:
In the growth of. In something whose seed does not perish. Bah wrote that Rambam, Rosh, and Tur only forbid the growth of growths in [a case where one said] "konam, what I eat or taste of them" — and so we rule. But Taz and Sh"akh disagree with him and ruled like the Mechaber:
Permitted. Taz wrote: in Asheri, chapter "One Who Vows from Vegetables," in his rulings, he wrote: "and even the substitutes of substitutes are forbidden" — and one may wonder at Beit Yosef who did not see this passage in Rosh — end quote. (And in Nekudot HaKesef he challenged him, writing that it is a scribal error in Rosh's ruling — see there.):
Of so-and-so. That is, [produce] belonging to a specific person. But if he said "fruit of such-and-such species" — e.g., "konam, figs or grapes are upon me" — since he forbade upon himself an entire species, he did not render them hekdesh, for his intent was only [to abstain from] eating that species — therefore [produce] of their substitutes and their growth is not forbidden.
