And needless to say, etc. There, 52b, “Rabbi said: Come and hear,” etc., “regarding these there was no question,” etc. This is why he writes “and needless to say,” for they were uncertain even where he said, “that I taste.” See seif 2.
And he is likewise forbidden, etc., and if, etc. Ran on the Mishnah cited above, s.v. “With respect to a matter,” etc.; see there.
But, etc. See Ran there in the continuation, and 47b, s.v. “Come and hear: One who betroths,” etc. Where the text states, “and one who benefits may benefit, for substitutes are permitted,” it must read, “substitutes of substitutes are permitted.” Rosh likewise writes this in the second chapter of Kiddushin, on the Mishnah at 56b. In Rosh's rulings here the text reads, “and their growths and the substitutes of their substitutes,” but it is evident that this is a scribal error. He also omitted these words in his abridged rulings.
The same law applies, etc. This is from the Tur, and is the Mishnah there, “Konam, they are upon me according to,” etc. See Ran there, s.v. “Konam,” etc., and 47a, s.v. “Konam,” etc. Baer HaGolah writes that this is taken from Rosh's commentary there, and Beit Yosef likewise says so at the beginning of his discussion, but this is an error. See Rosh there.
