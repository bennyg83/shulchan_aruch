And likewise forbidden in growths of their growths. Likewise Ran — Beit Yosef brought it: just as in the latter part in se'if 2, when he says she'eini ocheil or to'em mehen, he forbids even in growths of their growths — so too here. And it appears that the view of Rosh is also thus, who wrote on page 56 that growths of growths are forbidden in the latter part because it is the body of the prohibition since its seed is not consumed. If so, also when he says these fruits are upon me — nevertheless he benefits from the body of the prohibition. And it is a fortiori, for in the latter part where he says she'eini to'em, he is permitted in substitutes and growths when its seed is consumed — nevertheless it is forbidden even in growths of growths when its seed is not consumed. All the more so in the first part where it is forbidden in substitutes and growths even when its seed is consumed — that it should be forbidden when its seed is not consumed even in growths of growths. And for this reason Rambam and Tur did not need to write explicitly in the first part that it is forbidden in growths of growths when its seed is not consumed, for they relied on the fortiori we mentioned. And therefore he properly ruled here in Shulchan Aruch in this law that growths of growths are forbidden when its seed is not consumed. And Maharshal wrote that this is the view of the Ran alone, but Rambam, Rosh, and Tur hold that in the first part growths of growths are permitted — and he derived this ruling from halachah. And it is surprising for the reason we mentioned; on the contrary, Rosh wrote explicitly that even substitutes of substitutes are forbidden, all the more so growths of growths, since it is substance in existence, for its seed is not consumed — end quote. Certainly that ruling is clear. And Beit Yosef wrote in the name of the Ran: specifically here where he specifies and says these fruits — we treat them like hekdesh in substitutes and growths; and likewise when he says fruits of such-and-such place are upon me. But when he says konam olives and grapes are upon me, as is written at the end of this siman — he did not intend except to eat that species and did not treat them like hekdesh — therefore they are not forbidden in substitutes and growths — end quote. And that growths written at the end is without precision, for in any case it is forbidden in their growths, for they are fruits of that species and he forbade the entire species upon himself — only in substitutes alone is there a distinction. And so Rosh wrote explicitly there in the Mishnah that we do not require that he say "these," but konam alone; only because of substitutes alone they forbid only when he says "these." And it is possible the Ran deals with one who says konam olives and grapes of this year are upon me — then he is permitted in growths that will grow afterward, provided he did not say "these."
