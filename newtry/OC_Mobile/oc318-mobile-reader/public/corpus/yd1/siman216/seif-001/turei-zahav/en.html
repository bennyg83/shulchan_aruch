These fruits, etc. He must necessarily see them, since he said, “these.” Nevertheless, they are not positioned before him, as in the latter clause, and for this reason the latter clause states, “before him.”
He is forbidden in their exchanges. In his rulings in the chapter “One who vows from vegetables,” the Rosh wrote concerning this that even the exchanges of their exchanges are forbidden. The Beit Yosef, however, wrote in the name of the Ran that the exchanges of their exchanges are permitted, and so the Shulchan Aruch writes here shortly. It is surprising that the Beit Yosef did not see these words of the Rosh.
And he is likewise forbidden in the growths of their growths. So wrote the Ran, as cited by the Beit Yosef. Just as in the latter clause, in seif 2, when he says, “that I will not eat” or “that I will not taste from them,” he forbids even the growths of their growths, so too here. It appears that this is also the view of the Rosh, for he wrote on 56 that in the latter clause the growths of their growths are forbidden because they are the very substance of the prohibition, since their seed does not disintegrate. If so, when he says, “These fruits are forbidden upon me,” he likewise benefits from the very substance of the prohibition. It is also a kal vachomer. In the latter clause, where he says, “that I will not taste,” he is permitted in their exchanges and growths when the seed disintegrates, yet even there he is forbidden in the growths of their growths when the seed does not disintegrate. All the more so in the first clause, where he is forbidden in their exchanges and growths even when the seed disintegrates, that when the seed does not disintegrate he should be forbidden even in the growths of their growths. For this reason, the Rambam and the Tur did not need to state explicitly in the first clause that the growths of their growths are forbidden when the seed does not disintegrate, for they relied upon the kal vachomer that we mentioned. The Shulchan Aruch therefore ruled correctly here that the growths of their growths are forbidden when the seed does not disintegrate. My revered father-in-law, of blessed memory, wrote that this is only the opinion of the Ran, whereas the Rambam, Rosh, and Tur hold that in the first clause the growths of their growths are permitted, and he removed this ruling from the halacha. This is astonishing for the reason we mentioned. On the contrary, the Rosh wrote explicitly that even the exchanges of the exchanges are forbidden, and all the more so the growths of the growths, since the actual substance remains present, for the seed did not disintegrate. This ruling is therefore certainly clear. The Beit Yosef wrote in the name of the Ran that this applies specifically here, where he specifies and says, “these fruits,” for he made them like hekdesh with respect to exchanges and growths. The same applies when one says, “The fruits of such-and-such place are forbidden upon me.” If, however, he says, “Olives and grapes are a konam upon me,” as written at the end of this siman, he intended only to forbid eating that species and did not make them like hekdesh. Therefore, their exchanges and growths are not forbidden, end quote. The word “growths” at the end is imprecise, for their growths are certainly forbidden, since they are fruits of that species and he forbade the entire species upon himself. The distinction applies only to exchanges. The Rosh likewise wrote explicitly there in his explanation of the Mishnah that it is unnecessary for him to say, “these,” and that merely saying “konam” suffices. Only with respect to exchanges do we require that he say, “these.” It is possible that the Ran is discussing one who says, “The olives and grapes of this year are a konam upon me.” In that case, so long as he did not say, “these,” he is permitted in growths that grow afterward, and in this respect exchanges and growths are equal.
