These fruits and so on. By seeing them from these words, it is not prepared for them as in the Fa because of the most part of it:
They cannot be replaced. As for the Lord’s name, he wrote in his judgments, and even in his exchange, and in the name of the Lord, he wrote, “This is possible here in the midst of the Lord’s name, and he shall not see the Lord.”
And likewise he is forbidden in the produce of their produce. So wrote Ran, cited by Beit Yosef: just as in the latter case of seif 2 — where one says "I will not eat or taste from them" — he is forbidden even in the produce of their produce; so too here. And it seems to me that Rosh also holds this, having written that the produce of the produce is forbidden in the latter case because it constitutes the very substance of the prohibition since its seed does not cease; and if so, here too where one says "these fruits shall be upon me," he is deriving benefit from the very substance of the prohibition. And it follows a fortiori: in the latter case where one says "I will not taste," exchanges are permitted and when seed ceases the produce is also permitted — yet produce of the produce is still forbidden when seed does not cease; all the more so in the first case, where exchanges are forbidden and when seed ceases the produce is also forbidden — that when seed does not cease the produce of the produce would likewise be forbidden. And for this reason Rambam and the Tur did not need to write explicitly in the first case that produce of the produce is forbidden when seed does not cease — they relied on the a fortiori argument. And therefore the Shulchan Arukh ruled correctly here. And my father-in-law of blessed memory wrote that this is only the view of Ran, while Rambam, Rosh, and Tur hold that in the first case produce of the produce is permitted — and he removed this ruling from practice; but this is puzzling for the reason we mentioned. Moreover, the Rosh wrote explicitly that even exchanges of exchanges are forbidden — all the more so produce of the produce, since it is literally the very substance when seed did not cease. Hence certainly that ruling is correct. And Beit Yosef wrote in the name of Ran that this applies specifically where he specifies and says "these fruits" — making them like consecrated items with their exchanges and produce; and likewise when one says "the fruits of such-and-such a place are upon me." But when one says "konam olives and grapes upon me," as written at the end of this siman — he only intended to prohibit eating that type, and did not make them like consecrated items — therefore he is not forbidden in their exchanges and produce. And what he wrote at the end about "produce" is imprecise — for in any case he is forbidden in their produce since they are fruits of the same type and he forbade the entire type; but regarding exchanges alone there is a distinction; and so the Rosh wrote there explicitly. And possibly Ran means: when one says "konam olives and grapes of this year upon me" — then he is permitted in produce that will grow afterward if he did not say "these"; and in that case exchanges and produce are equivalent.
