It is permitted, etc. It appears that this is the ordinary presumption. But if the reason he vowed was that wine or meat is difficult for him, or the like, we follow his intent, as below in siman 218, and he is forbidden even in their taste.
He is forbidden in a cooked food that contains the taste of wine. See above, siman 98, seif katan 5, where I wrote that one may rely upon tasting by a Jew where such tasting is permitted even nowadays. Therefore, if another person is present who may taste it, he can rely on that person's assessment. If no one else is available, we measure against 60. All this applies when the forbidden flavor is a different type from the food; with the same type, however, even one part in 1,000 is not nullified in the context of a vow, as in the following seif. See also siman 102.
