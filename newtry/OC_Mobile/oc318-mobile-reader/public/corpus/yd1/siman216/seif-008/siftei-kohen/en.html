She is permitted to him, etc. It is surprising — why did he write without specification that she is permitted to him, etc.? For according to the one who said above se'if 6 that we are in doubt whether he attached to the first word or the last word — it is forbidden; and all the more so according to the one who says he attached to the last word. And it would have seemed to say that here it is different, since the one who forbids says thus afterward — and as Bach distinguishes in se'if 6 in the name of the Rivash and Rabbenu Yerucham and the Tur. But in Beit Yosef it does not appear so, for what he wrote above se'if 6 "and there is one who permits," etc., "and he attached to the first word" — he wrote in Beit Yosef that this is the Rambam who wrote in this language that is in se'if 8. And so wrote Av Bet HaGadol: if he said "this loaf" or "this house of mine," its law is like regarding "one who says your house" in se'if 6, that we attach stringencies to the languages — end quote.
