He said to his wife, “The work of your hands is a konam,” etc. The Ran wrote that this teaches a novelty. One who specifies the fruits of a particular person, just as he specifies the work of his wife’s hands, is considered as though he said, “these,” and is forbidden in their exchanges and growths. The latter clause, where he says, “that I will eat” or “that I will taste,” teaches that even if one says, “These fruits that I will taste,” it is regarded as though he did not say, “these,” and we follow the phrase, “that I will taste.” Here too, specifying the work of his wife’s hands is like saying, “these,” as I wrote. However, this is difficult for me, for there are two expressions that contradict one another. The phrase, “from the work of your hands,” is like saying, “these,” and for this reason he is forbidden in their exchanges and growths. The Tur must also hold this, for otherwise it would be like one who says, “Olives and grapes are a konam,” where exchanges and growths are not forbidden. It must therefore be like saying, “these,” as the Ran wrote. Yet the phrase, “that I will taste,” implies permission in exchanges and growths, and we should rule stringently, as the Tur writes afterward regarding, “this house of yours.” According to the Ran there is no difficulty, for he holds that the latter expression is primary, whether it produces leniency or stringency, as the Beit Yosef wrote in his name. The Ran also arranged this law regarding one’s wife in a different order from the Tur, stating first, “The work of your hands is a konam,” and afterward, “that I will taste.” We therefore follow the latter expression. According to the Tur, however, it is difficult. It appears to me that the Tur holds that we rule stringently only where either expression could have been stated independently, such as when he says, “this house of yours,” for he could have said, “this house,” or, conversely, only, “your house.” We therefore do not know which expression is primary. When, however, he says, “that I will eat from the work of your hands,” the phrase, “that I will eat,” is primary according to the structure of his words, for the statement cannot stand without it, just as it cannot stand without saying, “from the work of your hands.” There are therefore no contradictory expressions. Rather, he means that from the work of her hands only what he eats should be forbidden. It follows that the phrase, “that I will eat,” necessarily remains primary. This appears correct.
