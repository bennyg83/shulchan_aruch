He said to his wife konam the work of your hands, etc. Ran wrote: he teaches a novelty that one specifies the fruits of so-and-so, just as one specifies the work of his wife's hands as one who says "these" — and it is forbidden in substitutes and growths. And the latter part of she'ani ocheil or to'em teaches a novelty that even when he says these fruits she'ani to'em, it is as if he did not say "these," and we follow the language she'ani to'em — for here it is as one who says "these," since he specifies, as I wrote. Only it is difficult for me: since here are two languages that contradict each other — from the language mimaseh yadeikh it is as one who says "these," for for this reason it is forbidden in substitutes and growths; and also the Tur necessarily holds thus, for otherwise it would be as one who says konam olives and grapes, where there is no prohibition in substitute and growth except that it is as one who says "these," as Ran wrote. And from the language she'ani to'em it implies permission in substitute and growth — and we must follow stringency, as the Tur wrote afterward regarding leveitcha zeh. Granted for Ran it is difficult, for he holds the latter language is primary both for leniency and stringency, as Beit Yosef wrote in his name. And Ran arranged this law regarding his wife not in the order of the Tur, but he placed first konam maseh yadeikh and afterward she'ani to'em — automatically we follow the latter language. But for the Tur it is difficult. And it appears to me the Tur holds that one may not say we follow stringency except where one language can stand without its companion — that is, when he says beitecha zeh, where it was possible to say beit zeh or conversely beitecha alone — therefore we do not know which is primary. Unlike when he says she'ani ocheil mimaseh yadeikh — the language she'ani ocheil is primary according to the order of his words, for certainly it does not suffice otherwise; and likewise it does not suffice unless he says mimaseh yadeikh. If so, there are no contradictory languages — rather he means: from the work of your hands the prohibition shall be only what I eat — it follows the language she'ani ocheil remains in any case primary, as explained — correct:
