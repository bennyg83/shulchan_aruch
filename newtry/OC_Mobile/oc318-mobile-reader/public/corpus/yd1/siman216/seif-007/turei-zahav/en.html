Even though it left his possession, etc. In the Tur it is written thus: and for example if he specified in my life and in my death konam to my house that you enter — if he died or sold it to another, it is permitted — end quote. And this is according to Rosh as Beit Yosef brought. And Beit Yosef wondered at the latter part that it is permitted — necessarily if it deals without specifying in my life and in my death, behold even if he says this house it is permitted according to Rosh; and if he specified, why would it be permitted? And Perishah brought it in the name of Maharshal: the latter part deals when he said in my life and in my death. And likewise Maharshal wrote thus, and wrote that nevertheless it is permitted since he said first leveiti — afterward in my life and in my death do not help, even though above shortly when the one who vows said leveitcha zeh we follow stringency, for there too are contradictory languages — I distinguish there, for the one who vows says thus, for it is possible he will never enter that house; but here the one who forbids says to Shimon leveiti she'ata nichnas — what does he care after it left his possession? Therefore it is permitted to enter after the death of the one who forbade, even though he says in my life, etc. And this reasoning Rivash wrote siman 357 — end quote. And I saw in Rivash there that he did not distinguish between one who vows and one who forbids except when he does not say in my life and in my death. And so it is in Ran page 46 in the matter of "he asked Avimei," etc. — and it is straightforward. But when he says in my life and in my death I did not know even the beginning of this matter, for there are no contradictory languages at all — for thus he says: that one which is my house now you shall not enter forever, even after my death. And it is very surprising — behold Tur and Shulchan Aruch wrote here shortly "one who says to his son konam she'ata neheneh li bechayai uvemosai" — he shall not inherit him, etc. And there he said first she'ata neheneh li, which is substance like leveiti — and nevertheless what he said afterward in my life, etc. helps. And likewise in the Gemara the question is raised: "my loaf upon you" and they gave it to him as a gift — "my loaf" he said to him, when it is in his possession it is forbidden; "upon you" he said to him, he made it hekdesh. And Beit Yosef brings it — behold that even though the language of ke'ari implies specifically in his possession, nevertheless "upon you" comes and forbids it. And all the more so if he afterward said in my life, etc. And it appears to me according to the words of Rosh and Tur it is in this manner: the reason he must say in my life and in my death even though he said konam this house that you enter is because a person does not put to heart to forbid his house after his death — for a person is not particular as a rule, only that he not see his enemy in his house. And therefore above when he said konam to this house that I enter — it is forbidden even if he dies, etc. — he does not need in my life and in my death, for it is obvious that one who forbids himself intends all his days, since he said this house — certainly he has some hatred for the house itself. Unlike here where he forbids his upon his fellow — even though he said zeh, his intention is that he not see his enemy benefit from his property; and after his death it is the way of the world to forgive all enemies — therefore he must say in my life and in my death. And according to this it appears to me further that even though Rosh and Tur required saying in my life, etc. — it is for the matter after death for the reason I wrote; but after sale it is certainly forbidden even without specifying after my sale, for it is one reason — that he not see his enemy benefit from this house. And this is clear in this: if for the matter of sale, what did he fix with the language in my life and in my death? Rather he should have said whether it will be in my hand or another's hand. And he told him that the language in my life implies this, for this language is needed — for if he only said in my death there would be permission in his lifetime immediately; rather certainly he needs no fix except for death but not for sale. And so it appears in siman 226 where Tur wrote: but if he said this courtyard it does not help to force him, for behold it is forbidden even after he sells. And according to this the challenges of Beit Yosef are resolved: the latter part where Tur wrote konam leveiti she'ata nichnas, etc. — deals without specifying, and he teaches that for the matter of sale it is permitted when he says leveiti, and he forbids only when he says leveit zeh; for regarding sale we do not care at all whether he says in my life and in my death. And incidentally regarding sale Tur also mentioned death — so it appears correct according to the Tur.
