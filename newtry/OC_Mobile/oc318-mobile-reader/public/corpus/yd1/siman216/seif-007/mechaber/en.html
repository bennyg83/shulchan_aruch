If Reuven said to Shimon, “Konam this house that you enter,” and the owner died or sold it to another — Shimon is forbidden, for one who forbids his own matter upon his fellow — even though it left the owner’s possession, behold it stands in its prohibition. But if he said to him, “Konam my house that you enter,” if the owner died, or sold it, or gave it to another — he is permitted. One who says to his son, “Behold, you are forbidden my benefit,” or who swore that the son would not benefit from him if he dies and the son would inherit — this is like saying, “My property is forbidden to you.” But if he forbade him his benefit and specified whether during his lifetime or at his death, “if I die he shall not inherit” — this is like saying, “These properties of mine are forbidden to you” (and see above, siman 223). {Rama: A community that vowed that everything they collect shall be for building the synagogue and building the beit midrash — they give half to this and half to that, even if they did not collect enough for one of them (Beit Yosef, end of siman 228, in the name of responsum of Rashba). One who made his son or fellow swear not to lend his money to others except with permission of Reuven and Shimon — if one of them died, he is permitted to lend with permission of the one who remains, for wherever it says “so-and-so and so-and-so,” it implies even one of them, until he specifies both together for you (Beit Yosef, siman 228, daf 72a, in the name of responsum of Rashba). One who swore to his betrothed that he would not marry a woman “upon her,” and before he married her a yevamah fell to him — he is permitted to perform yibum, for the language “upon her” is not implied except if he married her first; and he may not marry the betrothed unless they release his oath (there, end of daf 273, in the name of responsum of Rashba; and it is in siman 272).}
