The tongue is a oath. The tongue of vow is that the oxid was exalted to him and the tongue of the oath is that he would remove himself on the object:
There are those who say, etc. The Tur wrote in the name of Rambam: "One who says 'the produce of so-and-so is forbidden to me' — in any language that he forbids it to himself, it is forbidden to him, even though there is no oath whatsoever, nor mention of the Name, nor a substitute for the Name; regarding this it says 'to bind himself with a bond' — to forbid to himself things that are permitted; and likewise if he says 'this is upon me as a prohibition' — it is forbidden" — end of quote. And "in any language" does not mean specifically in the holy tongue, as Beit Yosef wrote; and the Tur explained this likewise. But from his writing "even though there is no oath" one learns that he used the language of a vow in the form of an oath, and not an actual oath — and even so it is forbidden. And one must understand what he concluded with: "and likewise if he says 'this is upon me as a prohibition'" — for this seems entirely superfluous. It seems to me that Rambam wrote this as an explanation of the verse "to bind himself with a bond" — which implies at first glance that one would forbid to himself something already prohibited, meaning he would affix to a forbidden item as in a vow; to this he says that the verse's meaning is two-pronged: "to bind" means to forbid to oneself a permitted thing through the language of a vow — namely in any language; and "a bond," as mentioned in the verse, means if he says "this loaf upon me is a prohibition."
