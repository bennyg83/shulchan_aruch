One who vows, etc. Specifically one who says modrani mimecha forbade benefit from his fellow even without attaching to the vowed object — nevertheless there is a yad. But if he said "ani noder" — this language is oath language, forbidding himself by oath and not vow language. Rosh — meaning vow language forbids the object upon him, oath language forbids himself upon the object. One who says "ani noder shelo ochel imach" is oath language, and there is no oath without swearing in oath language. See teshuvah of Rabbi Meir Alshakar siman 71, similarly in Rambam's teshuvah; and see Rabbeinu Yerucham in s.k. 2 and above siman 239 s.k. 17.
Nevertheless in nezirut, etc. This is not in Beit Yosef, but I found in teshuvah of Mahari Betz vol. 1 siman 72, p. 36, that Rabbi David ibn Zamra wrote nezirut for all — if expressed in oath language it takes effect, for its essence is oath language, for it seizes the person like an oath, for he says "hareini nazir" like "hareini nishba." Mahari Betz challenged him with words of no substance; thus it appears from beginning of Nedarim regarding what is answered there — since it taught nedarim that forbid the object, it also taught cheremim, etc. — nezirut is like an oath in this, for the person is forbidden on a matter; likewise in Rosh's commentary there. See teshuvah of Mahari Betz siman 75, p. 134. I again found in teshuvah Mahari Betz vol. 2 siman 58 that he sensed this and elaborated, but all his words are not compelling to my view — see there.
