One who vows, etc. As explained 2b Nedarim — "forbidden," excluding, etc.; and Tosafot there "nedarim," etc. Therefore the mishnah says "modrani mimecha," etc. The question — "nodrani shelo ochel," etc.; and there 16b, what is written "neder," etc.; and Tosafot Shevuot 25a, "chomer," etc.
If not, etc. Rosh there, according to his view as he wrote in his commentary 5a, "neder," etc.; and as above siman 213 seif 2. But Ran explains there "veha'lo" — not specifically a vow but an oath on a matter; and as explained chapter 2. And some say, etc.; likewise in Rosh's commentary 10a, "bemohi," in the name of Yere'im.
Nevertheless, etc. As explained chapter 2 and at its end there; and see siman 205 seif 1 in the gloss.
And some, etc. Ran in the name of Ramban 2b, from what is written in chapter 2 and chapter 3 of Shevuot, "which is issur," etc., "behold upon me I shall not eat," etc.; and in Shevuot 22 their text — one who says "eating of both upon me as an oath," and our books' text is not so but in Nedarim 24b (and Shevuot 29a) we say one who says "they shall be forbidden," etc. The first reasoning holds he mentioned a vow; and in Yerushalmi they disagree — Rabbi Yudan and Rabbi Mona hold there is no vow in oath language and no oath in vow language, and Rabbi Yosi disagrees; we follow the majority. There. And Rosh said "she'eini ochel lecha" is nothing; Rambam holds it is a vow — each follows his view.
Nevertheless, etc. As in the Gemara there; and as Rosh wrote in his commentary there, "le'afukei." And nazir too, even though, etc.
