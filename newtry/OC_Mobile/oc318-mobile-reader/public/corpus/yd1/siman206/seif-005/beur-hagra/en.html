One who vows, etc. As the Gemara says there, 2b, vows take effect by rendering an object forbidden, etc., excluding, etc. See Tosafot there, s.v. 'vows,' etc. Therefore the Mishnah says, 'I am vowed from you,' etc., rather than, 'I vow that I will not eat,' etc. Likewise, see there, 16b, regarding what is written about a vow, etc., and Tosafot to Shevuot 25a, s.v. 'the stringency,' etc.
Unless, etc. This is the Rosh's view there, consistent with what he wrote in his commentary to 5a, s.v. 'a vow,' and with what I wrote above in siman 213, seif 2. The Ran, however, explains there, s.v. 'but surely,' that the term 'vow' is not precise, and the passage actually means that he swore. See there, and see what is stated there in chapter 2, 'Alternatively,' etc. The Rosh's commentary to 10a, s.v. 'by Moked,' likewise cites this in the name of some commentators.
Nevertheless, etc. As explained there at the beginning of chapter 2 and at its conclusion. See also siman 205, seif 1, in the gloss.
And there is an authority who, etc. The Ran, citing the Ramban on 2b, derives this from what is stated in chapters 2 and 3 of Shevuot: 'What is an issar,' etc., and, 'It is upon me that I will not eat,' etc. In Shevuot 22, their version reads, 'One who says, eating from both of them is upon me as an oath.' The version in our texts is not so. Rather, in Nedarim 24b, and in Shevuot 29a, the Gemara says that he stated, 'They shall be forbidden,' etc. The first opinion maintains that the Gemara's use of vow terminology is not precise. The Yerushalmi records a dispute: Rabbi Yudan and Rabbi Mona maintain that a vow cannot be expressed in the language of an oath, and an oath cannot be expressed in the language of a vow, while Rabbi Yosi disagrees with them. We rule in accordance with the majority. This is why the Rosh writes that the expression, 'that I do not eat from you,' has no effect, while the Rambam holds that it is a vow, each following his own approach.
