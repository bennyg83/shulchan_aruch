Tzlach — the Mechaber favors two against one: one who says menudeh ani lecha she'ani ochel lecha — he ruled permitted in all; this is Rosh, Tur, and Ran's view, that menudeh or meshamtina lecha is not vow language; for them, all the more if he did not complete his words all is permitted, for it is not vow language, as explicit in their words. If the Mechaber holds menudeh ani lecha is vow language — then even completing she'ani ochel lecha is forbidden to eat with him. Rambam too, who forbids without completing to stand within four cubits, ruled when completing she'ani ochel lecha forbidden to eat from him. Even according to Beit Yosef, Rambam in meshamtina lecha — if he completed permitted, if not forbidden only to stand within four cubits, specifically meshamtina but not menudeh; and in meshamtina one may say not permitted in all but eating outside four cubits — investigate. All the more according to Raavad, Rambam equates menudeh to meshamtina — in both, if not completed forbidden to stand within four cubits, if completed forbidden to eat within four cubits. At minimum in menudeh ani lecha, even if completed she'ani ochel — though menudeh does not apply to eating — it is as if he did not complete and forbidden to stand within four cubits for those who forbid standing within four cubits without completing. Rabbeinu Yerucham, Netiv 14 ch. 3, wrote: "Menudeh ani lecha or meshamtina mimecha — even if completed and said she'ani ochel lecha permitted; nevertheless forbidden to sit within four cubits, even if he said menudeh ani lecha alone or meshamtina mimecha alone," etc. It can be reconciled with difficulty; doubtful.
And it has already been explained, etc. Beit Chadash wrote — I did not understand his words; in seif 1 he also ruled like Rambam, etc. According to s.k. 3 nothing is difficult; nevertheless one must investigate what this has to do with that — here it is worse, as Tosafot in Rabbi Yehuda's name wrote, that here with menudeh and niddina mimecha, even without completing, forbidden to benefit; and even for those who permit benefit, nevertheless forbidden to sit within four cubits, as Rabbeinu Yerucham wrote explicitly — for Rosh who permits above entirely since he did not complete, here he forbade sitting within four cubits. Thus it appears necessary from Rosh — in his Gemara commentary (7a) he wrote niddina mimecha implies "I am unsettled and separated from you," and is greater than meruchakni mimecha; according to Shmuel it is explicit that even without saying she'ani ochel lecha it is forbidden. This contradicts his rulings — rather certainly in his rulings he teaches permitted in eating, and in his commentary teaches greater than meruchakni and forbidden to sit within four cubits. It appears the Rav followed Tur's words equating menudeh and niddina entirely to meruchakni; doubtful. It appears for halachah one must not be lenient for the reason I wrote.
