(There, seif 3 in the gloss.) And it has already been explained that there are disputants — see Shach (s.k. 6). And regarding what he challenged — we find that niddina mimecha is stricter than meruchakni mimecha for some views, forbidden even in ordinary eating — one may answer that those views hold niddina mimecha implies he shall be completely removed from him even in eating, as Tosafot write; but regarding four cubits, ultimately it is yadayim she'einan mochichot. However, the gloss's words are puzzling to me in what it wrote "and it has already been explained," etc. One may distinguish — meruchakni mimecha is yadayim she'einan mochichot, perhaps he speaks of eating since one can say meruchakni regarding eating; but menudeh ani lecha she'ani ochel — the Mechaber ruled permitted in all, for menudeh is not language of eating; therefore in an unspecified statement it is clear his intention is to sit within four cubits, since for eating menudeh does not apply at all. Rambam ruled menudeh ani lecha she'ani ochel forbidden in eating, and also menudeh ani lecha forbidden to sit within four cubits — only according to his reason, for meruchakni alone is also forbidden to sit within four cubits; but according to the Mechaber, menudeh ani lecha she'ani ochel permitted in eating — one may say for all, menudeh ani lecha alone clearly means forbidden to sit. Accordingly one may say menudeh ani lecha is stricter than niddina ani lecha — for in niddina ani lecha, if he said she'ani ochel it forbids eating; one may say niddina lecha unspecified is permitted even to sit, for some hold that view; but menudeh ani lecha alone is forbidden to sit, as I explained.
