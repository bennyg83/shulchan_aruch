If he said, 'I am excommunicated,' etc. Meaning, in accordance with the first tanna, as the Rosh and the Ran write, that the halachah does not follow Rabbi Akiva, both because the halachah does not follow Rabbi Akiva against his colleagues and because the Gemara says there, 'There is no one who is concerned,' etc. All this applies when he concluded, 'that I do not eat,' which refers back to the opening clause, as the Rosh and the Ran explain. But if he did not so conclude, this opinion holds that he is always prohibited. The reason 'I am excommunicated' and 'I place myself under a ban' are permitted is that they are expressions of excommunication, as the Gemara explains, 'and the Rabbis hold,' etc., and he added, 'that I eat.' By contrast, if he did not say, 'that I eat,' the expression denotes excommunication, while 'I separate myself' denotes abstention from benefit.

(Likkut) And it has already been explained, etc. The language necessarily indicates a prohibition against benefit more strongly than a prohibition against speaking, as is evident from the Gemara's question, 'Shall we say that Shmuel holds that non-indicative incomplete expressions,' etc. Even so, it is permitted because these are non-indicative incomplete expressions.
