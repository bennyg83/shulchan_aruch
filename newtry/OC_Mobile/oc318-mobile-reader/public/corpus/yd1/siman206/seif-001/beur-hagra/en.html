Designations, etc. Mishnah and Gemara, 3a.
And this is, etc. See what I wrote in Even HaEzer, siman 27, seif 4.
That I do not. This is the Rambam's version.
And some say, etc. This is the Rosh's version; see there, 4b, s.v. 'that I eat,' etc. The Ran records both versions in his commentary to the Mishnah, s.v. 'that I drink,' and writes in the Gemara, 4b, s.v. 'if so,' that according to the initial assumption that the words 'that I eat' alone constitute an incomplete expression of a vow, the Rambam's version is apparently preferable, because according to the reading 'that I eat,' he is saying, 'that which I eat.' He answers, however, that if so, why did he say 'that I'? He should have said, 'I will eat from you.' This version is nevertheless more satisfactory, because according to the reading 'that I do not,' how do we know that it is an incomplete expression of a vow? Perhaps it is an incomplete expression of an oath. One cannot say that he said 'konam,' for then it would be a complete vow. The Ran therefore presses the interpretation that, for example, he said another expression as well, but since he did not say 'that I,' it is not a complete vow because it is phrased in the language of an oath. Nevertheless, the reading 'that I' is certainly more satisfactory. According to the Rosh's approach, this applies specifically when he said 'that I.' The wording of the Rama is puzzling where he writes 'even'; and even according to the Ran's approach, he should have written 'all the more so,' etc. See what I wrote in seif 5.
But if, etc. This follows not the initial assumption, but the conclusion according to Shmuel, as the Rosh writes. This requires study, for the baraita there explicitly states that the words 'that I eat' alone prohibit him, as explained on 4b. One cannot answer that the baraita holds that an incomplete expression need not be clearly indicative, because Shmuel's inference from the word 'king' also applies to the baraita. See Tosafot, s.v. 'the baraita posed a difficulty for him.' See what I write below.
And if he said, etc. This is the view of the Rambam and the Ran in the name of the second version, following the plain meaning of the Gemara: in any event, the expression 'I am vowed from you,' etc., implies, etc. This also resolves the baraita cited above, for one may say that according to the first tanna, 'that I eat' together with 'I am vowed from you' is effective, while 'I am vowed from you' alone also prohibits speaking with him, etc. In any event, the matter is resolved according to the Ran's explanation of that view, namely, that he said 'konam,' as above. Others say, see Taz, seif katan 1, and Shach, seif katan 3, that unless he concluded, 'that I eat,' or, 'that I do not eat,' his words have no effect. This is the Rosh's view in both his commentary and his halachic work. He derives this from the Gemara's wording, 'Shall we say that Shmuel holds,' which implies that according to the opinion that such expressions are effective, he is prohibited from eating with the other person but permitted to speak with him. This shows that the wording indicates eating more strongly than speaking.
