The laws of yados of vows; it contains 5 se’ifim.
Yados (meaning: extensions — when one began to vow and did not complete the statement, and nevertheless he is forbidden as if he vowed the entire statement, like a person who holds a vessel by its handle and through that uses the vessel as if he held it itself) of vows are like vows — provided that they indicate somewhat regarding the vow. How so? One who says to his fellow, “I am forbidden to you that I will not eat with you,” or “I am separated from you that I will not eat with you,” or “I am distant from you that I will not eat with you” — he is forbidden to eat with him. (And some say that even if he said, “I will eat with you,” in these formulations, he is forbidden to eat with him) (Ran in the name of “there are those who read,” and see se’if katan 2). But if he says, “I will not eat with you,” alone, without “I am forbidden to you,” it is not a yad. And if he said, “I am forbidden to you,” or “I am separated from you,” or “I am distant from you,” and did not complete his words, “that I will not eat with you”: with “I am forbidden to you,” he is forbidden to speak with him; with “I am separated from you,” he is forbidden to do business with him; with “I am distant from you,” he is forbidden to stand within 4 amos of him.
