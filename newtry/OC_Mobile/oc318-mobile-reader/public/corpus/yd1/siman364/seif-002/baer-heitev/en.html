To move it. We are not concerned that perhaps he was a met mitzvah, for nevertheless such a matter has publicity. Shach. This is from chapter Nigmar HaDin.
For benefit. For a person cannot prohibit something that is not his. This speaks of a built grave, for if it was attached to the ground, in any event attached property is not prohibited. So wrote Nimukei Yosef. A known grave means one buried there with the field owner's knowledge. Shach.
And it is forbidden. For the rabbis decreed perpetual tumah on it so that people not move it.
And the Rambam. The Shach wrote that this is its explanation: a found grave, meaning we did not know there was a grave there, may be moved, for presumably he was buried there without the field owner's knowledge, since it was not known. “If they moved it, its place is tamei,” meaning the field around it where there is concern for graves. But a known grave, where it is known that only he is there, may not be moved. If they moved it, its place is tahor, since it is known that there are no other graves there. Beit Yosef. End quote.
