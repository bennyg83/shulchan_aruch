Bach writes: it seems to me that this applies only when he died in his clothes; but if he fell and did not die immediately but after several days, and by then had already removed his clothes and no blood was coming out — since it had already stopped — and he died in his bed, then he must be purified and shrouds made for him as for other dead. And in this there is a distinction between one who fell from the roof and was killed — and one killed by a gentile's hand: even though when found the blood had already ceased, he is buried as found in order to arouse indignation and take vengeance — and this does not apply to one who fell from a roof. And a woman who dies in childbirth and blood comes out of her — she is buried in her clothes; but if she did not die immediately but after several days and weeks, and the blood had already ceased as with other deaths — one purifies her and makes her clean. He further found the reason: for those four types of blood, the soul had already departed with them and been absorbed in her clothes; and it is certain that she shed the blood of the departing soul as when first found — and one does not remove her blood from her — and she is buried in the same place of the blood. The reason applies to one who fell from a roof or had a house fall on him and suffered wounds and bruises on his body with blood coming from them — "their blood is not the blood of death" — but a woman in labor whose inner organs were wounded as she stoops to give birth, and blood came out on her body and the rest of the clothes, that came out with the departing soul's exhaling — these had no blood in them. And one who dies on the way in great cold — it seems he should remove his clothes, be purified, dressed in shrouds, since he has no wounds and no blood came out of him. And one who drowned in water while wearing clothes — remove his clothes, purify him, and also dress him in shrouds; and as a result, at the end of his work, he saw many communities purifying every woman who dies in childbirth and dressing her in shrouds, and then dressing her clothes over the shrouds with the sheet above them. Shach.
