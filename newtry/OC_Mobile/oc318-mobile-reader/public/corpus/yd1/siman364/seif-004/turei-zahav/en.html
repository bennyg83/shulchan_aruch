And the same is done for a woman who died in childbirth, and so forth. Maharil writes concerning a woman who fell from a roof and died that if blood was still leaving her body, she should not be washed, lest the blood be removed. It is established custom to bury in their clothes all Jews murdered by non-Jews, so as to arouse divine wrath and vengeance. Maharil was uncertain whether a woman who died in childbirth is considered to have died by Heaven like one who dies from illness. His words imply that if blood is leaving her, she too should not be washed, for why should she differ from one who fell? My father-in-law distinguishes between one who fell and one who was murdered. A murdered person is buried in his clothes even if the bleeding had already ceased when found, in order to arouse divine wrath. One who fell but died only several days later in bed, and likewise a woman who died several days after childbirth, should be washed like other deceased persons. He further writes that one who died from cold or snow, or drowned while clothed, has his clothes removed, is washed, and is dressed in shrouds. The custom, however, is to bury drowned persons in the clothes in which they are found, and likewise, Heaven preserve us, a woman who dies in childbirth. Most astonishingly, they even dress such a woman in garments and shoes when she died several days after giving birth. In my opinion this is not in accordance with the law.
