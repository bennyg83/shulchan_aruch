Or one who fell and died. Maharil writes that if blood issued from him, he should not be purified lest the blood be removed; he is buried in his clothing. Bach limits this to one who died while still in those garments. If he fell but died days later after changing clothing and the bleeding had stopped, he is purified and shrouded like any other person. A person killed by a gentile is buried as found even after bleeding stops, both to preserve the blood and to arouse vengeance. The same concern applies to a woman who dies in childbirth while bleeding, but not where she dies much later after bleeding ceased. The reason is that the life-blood may have soaked into the clothing, even into the shoes, and may include the quarter-log with which the soul departed. Thus, the blood-soaked earth near a murdered person is also gathered and buried with him. The rule applies similarly to one killed by a fall or collapse where wounds bled, and to a woman whose internal injuries during childbirth caused bleeding. Where there were no wounds and no blood, the deceased is purified and shrouded normally. Accordingly, one who died from cold or snow, or drowned while clothed, is undressed, purified, and shrouded. Many communities, however, purify a woman who died in childbirth, dress her in shrouds, then place her own garments over the shrouds and a sheet over all.
