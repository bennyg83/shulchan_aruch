For a woman who died in childbirth. See Responsa Shemen Rokeach II, siman 13, concerning a pregnant woman who died and whose fetus could not be removed by ordinary means, whether her abdomen should be opened to extract it. He writes that he does not know the basis of the widespread custom to create great urgency over extracting the fetus, and the Gemara appears to indicate the opposite, that removing it after the mother's death is a degradation. Accordingly, her burial should not be delayed in violation of the prohibition of leaving the dead unburied overnight, particularly for a woman. Yet because the present custom is to make extensive efforts to remove the fetus, burial may be delayed until the third day while they employ every possible measure and solemn adjuration. If, Heaven forbid, the fetus does not emerge within that time, she should be buried as she is and her abdomen should not be opened, which would degrade her honor; see there.
Who fell and died. See Baer Heitev and Responsa Chinuch Beit Yehudah, siman 95, concerning one who drowned in a river. He ruled that the body should be purified and dressed in shrouds even if blood flowed from it after death. He also records a case in which thunder killed a person. Rabbi Ephraim and Rabbi Yeshayah, author of Shelah, agreed that if a visible wound or injury was found from which blood had issued, he is treated as one killed violently; if no such mark is found, he is treated like other deceased persons; see there.
