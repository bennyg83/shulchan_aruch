A grave that is found. See in Responsa Chavot Yair, in the responsum of the Gaon R. David Oppenheim, regarding an incident in one city where their synagogue collapsed and they wished to rebuild it. They dug to a depth of about a man's height to lay the foundations, and found in the southwestern corner bones of dead persons outside the excavation of the foundation; the craftsmen threw away the skull and limbs. In the eastern side, five cubits away, they dug and found two graves, one six cubits from the other; and in the eastern wall one grave was found with the skull of the dead inside the synagogue and his feet outside; and in the northern wall, about six cubits from the grave found in the eastern wall, they found in the foundation excavation three graves close to each other — about two cubits in length and the width of a person — and no board or pottery was found, and no grave outline was discernible. There was an elderly gentile who said unassisted that gentiles were buried there at the time of the Swedish War. There are many questions about this. First: one can say it is a burial cluster (shekhunat kevarot) since more than three were found — and it is forbidden to relocate, and even to thin out. According to what we hold in tractate Nazir 65 and Bava Batra 101 — if there are between four and eight cubits between them, this is a burial cluster — and in our case, where they are more than eight cubits apart (measuring by a cubit of six handbreadths), and also those graves on the northern side are not four cubits apart — it may not be a burial cluster. But one can say this measurement applies to their custom of burying in caves; but nowadays in any case it would be a burial cluster — and then it is forbidden to relocate it and its place is impure and forbidden for benefit. Furthermore, even if it is not a burial cluster — since it is a gentile cemetery, does it require checking in the synagogue to protect the priests from impurity? Until what depth must one check and how thoroughly? And on whom does this expense fall — on the priests alone or on the entire community? He elaborated extensively on Ramban's approach, Rambam's approach, and the approach of Tosafot in the name of R. Yitzchak — in this matter, that in the Land of Israel there is no presumption of a burial cluster as described for outside the Land, and if one does not prove they are all buried there since they are all found in cemeteries — He concluded that it seems the graves should be relocated, and what was found in the northern grave — since there were so many dead that it is not possible for Jews to have buried in grave A many dead like that — furthermore, there is an elderly gentile who says unassisted that they are gentiles, and the prohibition of relocation is only rabbinic; and the Nosei, by relocation of a gentile from there, going around the graves — the priests are permitted to return, as follows: Shach; and even Rashi, who concedes he doubts whether perhaps there is no grave and no bone at all there. And also the question of whether the place is permitted for benefit — even if there were definite Israelite [graves] — in the synagogue of the son — in that area, the prohibition of benefit does not apply since we concluded in the Talmud that only a built grave is prohibited for benefit, but an earthen grave has no prohibition of benefit — and all the more so in our case where it is almost certain they are gentiles. And specifically since it is permitted to build a synagogue there — for commandments were not given for benefit. However, all this is regarding the strict legal ruling; but so that a holy place and house of prayer not be in a place of suspected impurity, one should dig and check throughout the entire synagogue, to its length and breadth, to about a cubit and a half — in the manner one digs other graves — and all the bones found should be relocated; and if they find no pure earth and around it — they are permitted to return without examination. Although in place A where the bones of the dead were buried, the priests must not walk there — regarding gentile graves, one should be careful here since there is some doubt lest they be Israelites — either evacuating from that same place or marking that place. And for the need of the excavation, all Israelites and Levites must contribute to the community chest — and specifically in the synagogue where Israelites are contracted for the work of foot-impurity, and also since Israelites are commanded to hear the priestly blessing, and whoever does not hear it in the gate is completely absent from the blessing — a blessing cannot come to one from the community's thorns to the bottom of the earth. He also addresses a stone that is like a gravestone and what may be said. See there at length; and also in siman 331 on this matter; and see in the book Kevod Chakhamim p. 57 who also elaborated on this and cited the regulations of the Rabbinical Court on this matter.
