It is a sign that the A.S. is divided and remains. "And thou shalt be with me."
(Shulchan Aruch, seif 3) Even with intent to move him later. Ran in his chiddushim to Sanhedrin writes that if the structure was originally built specifically with intent to move the deceased later, then even if it was built for the deceased it remains permitted. Rather, the prohibited case is where one first designated the structure for the deceased, did not place the body there immediately, and later, without any new explicit arrangement, placed the deceased there. If the earlier designation was accompanied by an act of construction, the subsequent placement of the body causes prohibition even though there was an intention eventually to move the body. If the initial designation was only verbal, it does not become prohibited because that designation occurred before actual placement. But if, at the moment the deceased is placed there, one designates the place permanently, or even mentally intends this, it becomes prohibited without any separate act. See there.

(Shulchan Aruch, seif 7) But another person is permitted. Even if a deceased person had already been buried there and was later removed, it is permitted to bury another deceased person there, because this is not considered benefit. Bedek HaBayit in the name of Rashba. Tur at the beginning of this siman in the name of Rabbenu Yeshayah does not appear to agree. See Responsa Shevut Yaakov, part 1, siman 87. Maharikash prohibits burying another deceased there. Radbaz, part 2, siman 747, assumes Rashba's lenient view as clear, and Responsa Devar Moshe, siman 67, likewise rules leniently.
