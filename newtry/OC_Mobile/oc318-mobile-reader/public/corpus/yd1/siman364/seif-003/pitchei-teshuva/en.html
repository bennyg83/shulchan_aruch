Birkei Yosef records an incident of a widow who instructed that a stone be placed on her husband's grave. After some days she went to the grave and saw that the stone was small, and she wanted to exchange it for a nicer one and add money to the stone-seller for her husband's honor. My teacher z"l was asked whether this is permitted, since she is not benefiting from that stone at all, and on the contrary adds money for her husband's honor; or perhaps, since the other stone is not being given to her merely for the added money but only through this stone, which she gives the seller to sell to another deceased, she is benefiting from this stone, which became forbidden in benefit, and the seller too afterward profits from a prohibition of benefit. My teacher discussed this at length in a manuscript responsum and concluded that she may not sell or exchange it in any way, for in every case it is called benefit. Rabbi Yom Tov Navon, in Kiryat Melekh Rav II siman 27, inclined to permit this by halachic reasoning but not in practice; see there. See Chatam Sofer responsa siman 335 regarding a cemetery wall that collapsed and another better one was built in its place: may the seven trustees of the city use the old stones to build a gatehouse for entrance to the hospital, according to the practice of being stringent with tombstones? He concludes that it is plainly permitted for several reasons, and the trustees, who are like the seven city representatives, may change it and use the stones and bricks for another building needed by the public; see there.
