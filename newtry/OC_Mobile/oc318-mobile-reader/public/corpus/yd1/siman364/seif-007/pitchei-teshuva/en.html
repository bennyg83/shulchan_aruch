A grave for his father. See Shivat Zion responsum siman 58, regarding one who prepared a stone as a marker for his father's grave, and his father's name was carved in it, and for some reason it was not possible to set that stone as a marker on his father's grave, and the matter was delayed several years, and in the meantime the place of his father's grave was forgotten, and in the course of time the son also died: may the son's sons take this stone to set it as a marker on their father's grave, erasing their great-grandfather's name and carving their father's name. One teacher's view was to forbid, like one who hews a grave for his father, for a matzeivah is like a grave, as Rama wrote in seif 1 that a matzeivah is forbidden in benefit like the grave itself; but in any case the heirs may sell the stone for a matzeivah for another dead person, as with a grave. And the author, may his lamp shine, agreed with him in forbidding setting it on the son's grave, but regarding what he wrote that the heirs may sell it for another dead person he disputes him: what Tur and Shulchan Aruch wrote, "but another may be buried in it," does not mean that even the son himself may give this grave to another; rather it deals with one who hewed a grave for his father in ownerless land or in a communal cemetery where anyone may bury his dead in that place; therefore they wrote that another may be buried in it; but one who builds a grave for his father in his own field, obviously it is forbidden to sell or give to another; and if so in our case they are forbidden to sell the matzeivah; and even though now the son too has died, nevertheless grandsons are also obligated in their grandfather's honor, as Rama wrote above siman 240 seif 24 (see what I wrote above siman 161 seif 6 on this, and examine carefully). Nevertheless he concluded that one who rules to permit the heirs to sell this matzeivah for another dead person should not be seized upon, for he has pillars of the world to rely on to be lenient: namely the Rosh's view that a matzeivah is not forbidden at all, and Tosafot's view that whatever was not forbidden has no issue of his father's honor, and also to combine Maharil's view that grandsons are not obligated in their grandfather's honor; and it appears that even this issur is only d'rabbanan, therefore one who is lenient has not lost. And he further wrote that what Rambam, Tur, and Shulchan Aruch stated, "one who hews a grave for his father," is the language of the first tanna against Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel in the baraita in Sanhedrin daf 48, and not like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel who forbids even one who hews stones for his father; this is not a settled matter, and it depends on these views, whether the rule that wherever Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel taught in our Mishnah the halacha is like him applies only to the Mishnah or also to a baraita; see there.
