Bechor before it came, etc. To understand this seif we cite Gemara and Tosafot in chapter HaZeroah (Chullin 132): we taught bechor mixed in a hundred — when a hundred people slaughter, all exempt gifts, each saying to kohen: the bechor is mine; if one slaughters all, they exempt only one. They challenge the first clause: why exempt all — let the kohen come from two sides and say: if bechor, all mine; if not, give me gifts. Rav Oshaya said: when it came to the kohen blemished and he sold to Israel and became mixed — unlike when not sold. In Bekhorot 18: a ewe not yet given birth bore two males simultaneously — one for him, one for kohen; the second he pastures until blemished and eats in its blemish and is obligated in gifts — stam mishnah, Rabbi Meir; Tosafot in HaZeroah and Rabbi Yosi exempts; Gemara: Rabbi Meir — since kohen comes from two sides; R' Yochanan — because it teaches obligated in gifts; Rashi: what he took was chullin and we return it; Rabbi Yosi — Rava: they made non-acquirer like acquirer — even not in his hand, as if in his hand, and kohen sold to Israel in its blemish — exempt; Tosafot: the challenge "kohen from two sides" is not difficult for Rabbi Meir — even when kohen takes one he has no claim on the rest from gifts; only Rabbi Yosi's reason — here each slaughters alone, kohen has no claim from bechorah, each deflects — even for Rabbi Yosi the challenge stands; answer: when sold blemished to Israel; if only mixed without sale, even all in one hand, must give one for bechor and exempt one from gifts per Rabbi Yosi; Rav Oshaya's answer is for Rabbi Meir only; Rambam wrote kodashim animal disqualified by blemish mixed — as Beit Yosef cited — did not mention sale — he holds like Rabbi Yosi, no need for sale per halacha; Ran explained Rambam: since bechor mixed in Israel's animals and kohen took one from doubt — as if kohen acquired and gave blemished to owners; Beit Yosef thought Ran explains Rav Oshaya per Rambam — he did not examine well — clear as we said; Tur follows his ruling in siman 318 as Rabbi Meir — therefore needed sale blemished per Rav Oshaya; Beit Yosef did not descend to Tur–Rambam dispute on Rabbi Meir vs Rabbi Yosi and usually does not bring Rambam here — Perishah on "give me all" — Israel cannot say he already gave one for bechor; seems he must compensate kohen for bechor; kohen may say no bechor law remains since you removed bechor status — must give gifts; or "give all" and return what was given — must give gifts from it; first proof needs mishnah Bekhorot that one must give one; his first answer is difficult — if bechor law removed, kohen should return what he took for bechor; Rashi's second answer is primary.
