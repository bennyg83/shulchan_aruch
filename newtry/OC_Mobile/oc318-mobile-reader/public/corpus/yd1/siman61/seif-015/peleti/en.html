If he lost them, etc. Perishah wrote per Rashba's novellae: if he took from an owner whose animal had gifts and lost them — exempt; he wrote per those who hold benefit of use is money the owner had benefit of use to give to whomever he wishes — he is the claimant against the damager for benefit of use and must pay and give to kohen. It is difficult: what does the Gemara challenge on the homeowner who transgressed — if wealthy he is liable, damaging priestly gifts? Why difficult — the homeowner took maaser ani from one who has maaser to distribute — if wealthy he should pay benefit of use, unlike if poor he took to exempt; one may say since he took maaser from one who has maaser, the maaser owner had benefit of use giving him maaser, and if wealthy more benefit of use to the wealthy — he cannot claim benefit of use since he gave willingly; Perishah deals when taken without consent — he did not elaborate. Indeed what Perishah concluded — pay value of benefit of use to owners and owners give to kohen — I did not understand: benefit of use belongs to owners and Torah entitled him to give to whomever he wishes — what do kohanim have with owners' payment? Therefore alongside his view he wrote thus. However I doubt the essence: certainly for this reason damaging gifts is exempt as money without claimants — Perishah wrote well: the owner claims "you destroyed my benefit of use," but the other reason Scripture exempted from payment because it is written "this" — if Scripture exempted the gifts themselves how must he pay benefit of use — is the owner's power greater than the kohen's hand? These two reasons are in doubt and burden of proof — exempt; requires further study.
Must pay to fulfill, etc. So Rosh and Tosafot for the reason it is money without claimants; Perishah agreed with Ran he is not obligated to fulfill Heaven except piety; his main proof from the Gemara for Rabbanan: poor one's reason — if wealthy pays — why like damaging priestly gifts? It is difficult: to fulfill Heaven he is obligated. One should examine Tosafot s.v. tanna: they challenged why the Gemara teaches tanna teaches "he shall pay" and you say piety — he shall pay means to fulfill Heaven as in Bava Metzia; and one may say even for no claimants he is obligated to fulfill Heaven — specifically when taking forbidden; but the transgressing homeowner took with permission — even to fulfill Heaven exempt; therefore R' Eliezer said mere piety; they wrote: if so the poor one's reason — wealthy obligated — what is the challenge — wealthy obligated to fulfill Heaven (Perishah's challenge). Perishah: the challenge is tanna teaches "he shall pay" — R' Eliezer and Sages argue and you say piety — Sages would not argue on piety — end. One may understand: nevertheless per R"T how is "if wealthy" resolved — perhaps piety and fulfilling Heaven are one or two — if one, R"Ch who answers piety means obligated in court; if two, R"Ch holds only piety needed and others obligate to fulfill Heaven — "if wealthy" does not follow from "if wealthy." In Tosafot's view the challenge is tanna teaches "he shall pay" — you say piety — law is no fulfilling Heaven as Tosafot — since he eats with permission they challenge: proven fulfilling Heaven and piety are two — why challenge "if wealthy" — wealthy obligated to fulfill Heaven; but now we sharpen R"T: the challenge "tanna teaches he shall pay" is not from "he shall pay" but how Sages argue with R' Eliezer; if so fulfilling Heaven and piety are one — the questioner did not challenge from "he shall pay" as Tosafot — only from Rabbanan; therefore the Gemara challenges "if wealthy" — no need for fulfilling Heaven — this is piety; R"Ch and Tosafot hold full payment — end; see Rama responsa 107 — he intended likewise; conclusion: piety is fulfilling Heaven — no challenge from "if wealthy"; Tosafot obligate to fulfill Heaven. However Tosafot in Piskei Tosafot: transgressing homeowner who must take — obligated to fulfill Heaven — difficult, Gemara only said piety for wealthy — requires further study; Rambam in Mishna commentary on homeowner explains he pays from piety — proven wealthy obligated to fulfill Heaven — otherwise why mention poor — end. This challenge for R"T: why challenge "if wealthy" — perhaps fulfilling Heaven — I do not know the challenge; for R' Eliezer Tosafot challenge well: perhaps he pays to fulfill Heaven — does not challenge our view that from "this" even fulfilling Heaven exempt; R' Eliezer needs the extra "one who damages gifts" — but for Sages the Gemara challenges "if wealthy" — no need for fulfilling Heaven — the other view; all languages must fit Rabbanan — forced explanation — not such a great challenge "if wealthy." One should understand how the Gemara thought R' Eliezer needs "one who damages gifts" — R' Eliezer holds one who took peah and said "this is for so-and-so the poor person" acquires for him; even from a wealthy person to a poor person — since he loses assets he is poor and it is fit for him; how can he abandon assets — he will reacquire — if he throws wealth outside he must pay what he took from pe'ah — what acquisition does he have if he must pay in full? If "this" he is not obligated by law, only piety — he has an opening and it is called migo for himself; but if "this" he must pay — what acquisition? Perhaps because he must give to the poor person he wishes — if he says "this for the poor person" he may abandon assets and reacquire; if he must pay that poor person he desires — that is the request — requires further study.
