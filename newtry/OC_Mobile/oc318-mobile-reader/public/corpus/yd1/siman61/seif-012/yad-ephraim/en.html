And see in Ketzot HaChoshen who wondered on men of action who do not seek to fulfill this mitzvah; and his son-in-law took for pidyon haben his animal and sold it to the butcher to give to the kohen who redeems the child from him, and again regretted — perhaps he is in the category of one who gives all his gifts to one kohen — and gave the gifts to his father-in-law the Gaon Rav Yitzchak Shapira who ate them for his wife; and many men of action gave to scholars going to Eretz Yisrael with money for agents to buy animals for them and separate priestly gifts — foreleg, cheeks, reishit hagaz — and a person's agent is like himself in every matter; and I was doubtful, since he cannot do it himself because the way is far from him — how can he make an agent; as Tosafot wrote in Nazir regarding separating challah — see there. And see Peri Chadash in siman 101 who challenged Beit HaLevi who wrote that witnesses testified before him that today they do not practice gifts even in Eretz Yisrael — this is not so, and the witnesses erred in their imagination that they saw animals of gentiles, and if found fit they are sold to an Israelite; but an Israelite who buys an animal and slaughters for his need — daily practice is giving the gifts; and likewise Mishna Berurah that they practice fulfilling the mitzvah with the animals themselves; but outside Eretz Yisrael they did not practice this. And see in Peleti who wrote that his pious son-in-law made a pidyon haben meal and took an animal and separated gifts and wished to give to the kohen redeeming there, and said why should we fear — if because presumptive kohanim, behold I redeem my son with him; and again retracted because of giving all one's gifts to one kohen, and gave the gifts to the husband of a kohenet — see there. And see Peri Chadash who wrote on Rambam in explanation of the Mishnah why they said the foreleg to one, etc. — because one may not give all one's gifts to one kohen; and his intent is what is written in Eruvin chapter HaDar — and this is not so, for there it deals when one always gives his gifts to one (see there, derived from "and Ira the Jairite was kohen to David" and famine came, etc.); but to one kohen incidentally one may give him all the gifts; and Rambam in his composition retracted, etc. — see there. And if so, the Rav z"l lost the redeeming kohen in vain. And behold what he wrote because of presumptive kohanim — he spoke well, for behold we presume him a kohen in this redemption; but without this reason it appears — what of it that they are presumptive kohanim, since he acts stringently; if so, better to give to a presumptive kohen than to withhold for himself — see there in Peleti who wondered why those stringent in purification did not practice this stringency; and it appears that since mostly the animal belongs only to the butcher, and also possible that even his animal — since he is outside Eretz Yisrael and also the kohanim are presumptive kohanim — they were not stringent in this. And regarding presumptive kohanim I elaborated in my responsa Beit Avraham part 1 siman 6, and there I brought the words of Yeshuot Yaakov in Bava Kamma chapter HaParah and the Taz hinted to it in Yoreh De'ah siman 323; and there it is explained that kohanim of our time after the exiles and wanderings are more deficient; and presumptive kohanim at Temple time are different, for the Gemara deals with them. And I wrote there why in Eretz Yisrael they raise their hands daily, and in our lands they do not raise hands except on Yom Tov — see there.
