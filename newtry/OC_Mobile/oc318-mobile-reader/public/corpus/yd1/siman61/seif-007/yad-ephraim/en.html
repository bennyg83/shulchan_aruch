And Rav Peri Chadash wrote in his responsa anthology at the end of his book Mayim Chayim siman 4: regarding one who assigns to a minor kohen one of the twenty-four priestly gifts , he has fulfilled giving per the majority of poskim, for a minor acquires from the Torah through another's intent giving and acquiring , see there that he brought proof from Yevamot 88: "they marry" , a deaf-mute, imbecile, and minor specifically at the threshing floor do not divide terumah, but in the house they divide; and Tosafot wrote that if he knows to spread his hands they divide , see there. And it requires study how this proof settles for those poskim who hold even through another's intent he acquires only rabbinically, and even for those who hold that since he acquired rabbinically it works also from the Torah because of hefker beit din hefker, as written in Even HaEzer siman 28 regarding kiddushin in name of Maharshal , and see what was written in responsa Beit Avraham part Choshen Mishpat at length; nevertheless it appears this fits well for "to you," but nevertheless for the mitzvah of giving one may say there is no mitzvah of giving here since this one did not acquire through his giving but as one who acquires from hefker. And I saw in Mishna Berurah that he brought the words of Peri Chadash on this and wondered: per this, it is difficult on deaf-mute and imbecile that they are counted there too as "they send to their houses," and Tosafot and other commentators had difficulty lest they defile him, and they answered they have a guardian , and they did not notice that here he does not fulfill the mitzvah of giving when giving to deaf-mute and imbecile who are not fit to acquire , and this wonder is in truth. And further he challenged per Maharam who brought Mordechai in the first chapter of Metzia that in priestly gifts, since benefit of use is not money, another's intent does not acquire and the minor did not acquire , if so, also regarding a minor it is difficult how they send to his house, for there is no fulfillment of the mitzvah of giving , see there. And one cannot distinguish between terumah and other priestly gifts, for in Chullin 134 regarding Levi sowing in a furrow etc. we conclude the reason for terumah is that he hires a cow and brings it to the city because terumah too has "giving" written in it , and it requires study per my humble opinion on Rambam chapter 12 of Hilchot Terumot halacha 17 who wrote the Sages enacted that he should attend to it and bring it to the city and take wages for bringing it from the kohen, for if he separated it and left it for beast and bird , this is desecration of God's name , see there; and it is astounding, for this is against the conclusion of the Gemara in Chullin that terumah too has "giving" written in it, and Rashi explained he must give them to the kohen, implying it is from the Torah. And behold in Bekhorot 23 regarding challah we also say to give it to a minor kohen, and challah too has "giving" written in it; and although there it deals with rabbinic challah, nevertheless if it were d'oraisa challah one would not give to a minor who is not fit to acquire , also rabbinically we should l'chatchila give to an adult who will give to the minor; and the default implies one gives immediately to the minor. And it is possible that since challah is rabbinic and a minor acquires rabbinically, this suffices and we do not trouble to give to an adult to give to the minor. But the plain meaning appears that even d'oraisa challah one could give to a minor if not that she is impure and unfit to eat; therefore it appears regarding priestly gifts, although "giving" is written, if he gave and did not delay except the delay is from the recipient who lacks capacity to acquire so it becomes his , we have no concern, for nevertheless it is now in the kohen's possession and he may eat as he wishes; and even though he lacks full acquisition that if another comes and snatches from his hand the other acquires from the Torah, nevertheless now it stands in the kohen's possession and we call it giving. And similarly we find regarding kohen acquaintances: although legally he may return them, nevertheless whatever he did not return is his from the time of slaughter and the bechor takes double; and likewise here regarding being called giving , we do not care about the recipient's acquisition being complete, only that this one gave and that one received into his hand , he fulfilled giving. And proof: in Gittin 20 regarding a get written on items forbidden in benefit , it is valid; and Tosafot wrote there: although it is written "and he shall give," nevertheless giving a get is called giving, as we hold one wheat kernel exempts the whole stack although it is not worth a perutah , see there at length; and it is plain that whatever is not worth a perutah, acquisition does not apply , yet if another took it afterward it would be his and courts do not intervene to remove it , nevertheless it is properly called giving.
And behold regarding gifts to the poor such as leket, shikhechah, and pe'ah , plainly a minor may gather although he is not fit to acquire, as explained in Bava Metzia 12: if a minor has no acquisition, his father gathers and his father is wealthy , implying if he has no father he gathers for himself although he does not acquire; and it is not reasonable to say if he has no father he should not gather at all since he has no acquisition; nor is it proven from the three inquiries at midday that they fixed time for gathering because small children sleep in the morning , it is clear small children also gather; and it appears there the reason is because "giving" is not written, only "you shall leave them" , meaning they were hefker for all; but there is no obligation placed on him to give, and therefore one need not store for the poor, unlike terumah and other priestly gifts where "and he shall give" is written , possibly one must give into the hand of one fit to acquire, otherwise it is not called giving; but I already wrote it is proven one may give even less than a perutah , there is no stringency in this law of acquisition for giving; and see Maggid Mishneh chapter 6 of Hilchot Matanot Aniyim who elaborated on the matter of giving that it suffices in any case.
But the main point per my humble opinion in the essential law regarding terumah and gifts: it is permitted to give to a minor and one fulfills giving thereby; for in terumah when the stack was smoothed and terumah obligation fell upon it, kohanim have a share in it, and kohanim are like partners in that stack, as Tosafot wrote in Sukkah 35 regarding an etrog of tevel that is like partners' etrog; and likewise in gifts , from the moment the cow is slaughtered the Merciful One acquired for him in its body , the owner's acquisition is not needed at all, for automatically all kohanim acquire a share in it, only that the homeowner has benefit of use; but the owner's acquisition is not needed at all; and since "kohen" written in the verse includes also a minor, as Peri Chadash wrote , although his proof from Sanhedrin 54 is not proof, for there are two verses in the section "lying" writes man and "when lying" writes male, unlike here , nevertheless one may say kohen means fit for service, not a minor unfit for service; but nevertheless his law is true since "man" is not written to exclude a minor, as explained in Kiddushin 53 that for a minor to divide in kodashim we exclude from "man" , it is clear otherwise even a minor is included (and one may say there the verse "to all sons of Aharon" , if not that "man" is written "as his brother" we would say even minors are included, etc. , see there; it is clear we do not exclude a minor from kohen alone, for a minor kohen is also called kohen, and whatever the Merciful One granted them they divide) , and so we say in Yoma 40 regarding Aharon's bull the Merciful One made it hefker for his brother kohanim and it is like partners in it; and likewise explained in Bava Kamma 12: priestly gifts , kohanim acquire from the high table; see there regarding bechor; and so in all priestly gifts; and likewise in Bava Kamma chapter HaGozel regarding robbery of a convert that Heaven acquired and gave to the kohen; and likewise Kiddushin 58 regarding one who steals his fellow's tevel , if benefit of use is not money he need pay only the value of the non-sacred portion and the rest belongs to kohanim whose share is mixed in; and Rabbi who holds he pays the value of his tevel , either because he holds benefit of use is money, or another reason that we say there: since so, this acquisition that the Holy One blessed be He granted kohanim is equal and equates minor and adult , the owner's acquisition is not needed at all; and per this one may say there is no proof from terumah and other priestly gifts to the five sela'im of pidyon haben that he is not redeemed unless the kohen acquires receiving the money and a minor is not fit to acquire; but nevertheless one may say that since it is a loan written in the Torah and property is encumbered from the Torah, and the minor has a share in this encumbrance , he takes what the Torah granted him in this property , all the more there is no difficulty with Maharam in Mordechai who wrote since benefit of use is not money, another's intent does not acquire, so the minor does not acquire , one may say she is the giver since the money is not the owners', because the Merciful One acquired for them , if so there is no greater "another's intent acquires" than this; and it appears they certainly acquire full acquisition and it is as if it fell to him in inheritance from his father , one who robs from him is liable. And with this one may resolve what Tosafot challenged in Sanhedrin beginning Ben Sorer uMoreh: regarding robbery of a convert we say a minor , you need not return, for you do not find a minor convert who has money , this minor has no acquisition and also no inheritance , and from this they wanted to prove another's intent acquires from the Torah; or he inherited from his father before converting; or he earned wages , see there; and per what I said you also find when ma'aser ani was given to him and he acquires from the Torah, as written "and you shall give to the convert, orphan, and widow," etc. , and one who robs from him is liable from the Torah; and robbery of a convert applies well to such a minor.
And behold that which we say in the incident of Rabban Gamliel and the elders , another's intent acquires for them , this is different; although they have only benefit of use, Maharam Mordechai wrote there that he gave them his courtyard as hire similarly, etc. , and the distinction is not understood at all, for nevertheless in what he gave them Rabban Gamliel had only benefit of use alone, and still it is difficult how they acquired through the courtyard he gave in hire since they were not standing beside it; but it appears Rabbeinu Peretz holds that to acquire the produce body they did not need Rabban Gamliel's acquisition, for the Merciful One acquired for them; but nevertheless they needed giving for this , that it come to their possession, and it did not come to their possession since they were not beside it , not considered their possession; and he wants to prove from this that even if one is not beside his field it is as if it came to his possession and automatically he acquires through his courtyard; and on this Rabbeinu Peretz answered there that "another's intent acquires" is different , meaning since Rabban Gamliel wishes to remove it from his possession and bring it to their possession, it counts as their possession even if they are not beside it, for the giver's thought counts it as their possession; and that which he stated "another's intent acquires" is not precise acquisition , in truth the owner's acquisition is not needed, for automatically he acquires; only he means another's intent acquires works even where acquisition is needed, acquiring through such a courtyard even if not beside it, for his thought makes it a guarded place for the acquirer , and all the more here where acquisition is not needed, only entry into possession suffices through the homeowner's thought removing from his possession and bringing to their possession , it counts as their possession even if they are not beside it; but for the acquisition itself that they acquire in it, there is no intent of Rabban Gamliel acquiring, for automatically he acquires from his intent, the Merciful One's intent; and I already wrote priestly gifts are not like gifts to the poor in this, for in gifts to the poor giving is not needed , "you shall leave them" is written, they are hefker for the poor and they acquire by gathering; therefore with minor and wealthy father it depends: if we say a minor acquires from the Torah, the minor acquires for himself and may gather as a poor person and his father acquires from him; unlike if he is not fit to acquire only through his father , and his father is wealthy, he cannot gather , his hand is like his father's hand. But ma'aser ani is not like other gifts to the poor, for ma'aser ani has "giving" written in it; nevertheless it is only benefit of use for owners, but the essence of the right is at the time it becomes tithed the Merciful One acquired for kohanim, Levites, and the poor , a share in it; and through the owners who are the givers it becomes clear who acquires; if so, for a minor, if we say a minor has no father , we say his hand is like his father's hand , he may give ma'aser ani to a minor, for even if you say a minor needs no acquisition for himself, nevertheless the Torah granted the poor a share in this stack and the minor is also among the poor; and by giving to him it becomes clear this is his share the Torah granted; but if the minor has a father and we say he needs no acquisition for himself only through "his hand is like his father's hand," or even an adult supported at his father's table , for acquisition his hand is like his father's , if so, initially the Torah did not grant this one to take a share in this tithe, since the right is to the poor and this one's hand is like his father's and his father is wealthy , he does not acquire for himself and his father acquires from him; and since his father is wealthy he does not acquire at all , it is like that which we say if the minor does not acquire for himself, his father acquires and his father is wealthy; likewise here there is no acquisition from the Torah, since it is his father's hand; therefore necessarily from another's intent acquiring , namely the homeowner , and his intent is to give to the minor himself who is poor and his father acquires from him , one may say the owner's intent does not help here, for it is not his, he has only benefit of use to give to a poor person not a rich one; therefore even if he stipulated on condition his father has no right in it , it does not help, since at the time the poor acquired through the Merciful One's statement this one was not included in the acquisition, only other poor people; and his giving of this one does not help at all; therefore Rabbeinu Shimshon who holds regarding eruv that what he gives only to the son helps , there the matter depends on his intent, unlike here where it depends on the Torah's intent and the Torah did not grant this one at all; therefore regarding ma'aser ani of money, since the poor acquired immediately when he separated , it is not in his power to take their portion and give to this one who is not poor at that hour , and the homeowner's will and intent do not help at all.
And I found in responsa Rashba siman 18 who wrote that on priestly gifts one does not recite a blessing, for they acquire from the high table; and regarding robbery of a convert which is from priestly gifts we say Heaven acquired and gave to the kohen, as in Bava Kamma (and I already brought this proof above per my own opinion that we hear from it that one who gives priestly gifts does not give of his own, but they are the Merciful One's, as written "and I have given you the charge of My terumot") , and so answered R' Yosef ben Plat to the Ramban z"l, and it was also brought in Sefer Temim De'im , end of his words. And if so, a minor who is included among kohanim acquires them through the Merciful One's statement, as I wrote. And behold I saw in Ketzot HaChoshen siman 243 who also brought the words of Peri Chadash on this and challenged also from responsum Maharam in Mordechai and from that which they send to their houses even to imbeciles, as Mishna Berurah wrote, and he elaborated on this and wrote somewhat along the way I wrote; but what he added that priestly gifts are like hire in exchange for service, and even disqualified kohanim have acquisition , and Tosafot already wrote a minor has acquisition for hire , and likewise they acquire priestly gifts, etc. , see there. And his words are astounding, for there what reaches him from this homeowner is wages for his work , it is not the homeowner's money and he takes his own; unlike here where the minor has nothing from the homeowner, only he takes from the homeowner and the Merciful One acquired for the kohen , what do we care if it is in exchange for service of the Holy One or another reason the Holy One wished to grant them. And on the contrary, if the reason were exchange for service, minors should not take priestly gifts. And what he wrote that also disqualified kohanim for service have acquisition , on the contrary, this is proof it is not because of exchange for their service; and so it is in truth, for the Merciful One calls it a gift. And what is mentioned in the verse "in exchange for their service" is plain this is not similar to worldly hire, for certainly the kohanim's service was not to receive reward; rather in his lifetime he mentions that because they are dear to Him He imposed their service and gave them these gifts. One could more say because they did not take a portion in the land and their inheritance remained for Israel and "the fire-offerings of Hashem they shall eat" , it would be like a definite debt to them; but in truth also this is only the Holy One's will that He granted them in these gifts, as explained regarding foreleg, cheeks, and maw , there the expounders of hidden matters said foreleg corresponds to "and he took the spear in his hand," etc. , see there. And if so, it is only the way of reward for a mitzvah alone; and the plain meaning is as I wrote, that since a minor is included among kohanim he acquires and from the Torah's commandment like another he properly fulfills the mitzvah of giving also to a minor. And it escaped Mishna Berurah that Chullin 134: renouncing benefit of use does not help and one must fulfill the mitzvah of giving.
What he wrote , know that one may consecrate a woman with benefit of use of terumah , is astounding; for certainly if benefit of use is money he properly consecrates a woman with benefit of use in terumah that she can receive a perutah from the kohen and give him the terumah; but in any case giving must be fulfilled in terumah; and even if it reached a kohen who is fit to acquire, whether through the woman or through another, he fulfilled the mitzvah of giving as required; but if he gives to a minor who does not acquire , possibly it is like placing in hefker and he did not fulfill the mitzvah of giving, as I said from the Gemara Chullin that we say he hires a cow and brings it because terumah has "giving" written , I shortened; and see there in Ketzot what he wrote per Peri Chadash further in the responsum that if he gave to the kohen against his will, although he does not wish to receive , it counts as giving , likewise giving to deaf-mute and imbecile he is exempt, as we say in Yevamot regarding a deaf-mute woman divorced , she does not need a get for the woman's intent; and also per the reason she lacks a hand , specifically there because it is written "and he shall give into her hand" , see there. Per my humble opinion one may say that also where only "and he shall give" is written, it must come to his possession in a manner he acquires through sale and gift; and plainly one who obligates himself to give his fellow an item worth a perutah does not fulfill if he gives in a manner he will not acquire; and likewise "and he shall give to the kohen" requires giving in a manner the kohen acquires. And in a get too, if it were written "and he shall give to the woman," it would need to come to her possession; and that which is written "into her hand" means like what a hand that is adjacent to her , namely standing beside her courtyard, since it is his obligation to her; or per one opinion like her hand that is guarded for her intent. And see Gittin 16 where Rashi wrote "from giving" implies any giving; and that which is written "into her hand" teaches it requires guarded for her intent like her hand, etc. (and it requires study why Rashi stated guarded for her intent which is Rabbi Oshaya's language , he should have stated Ulla's language "like her hand" adjacent, standing beside her house, for we rule like Ulla as poskim wrote , only Ashi in Bava Metzia , see there; and possibly he means even one who does not require standing beside her house needs like her hand requiring guarded for her intent as explained in the Gemara there , and it requires study). And if so, although we may say it also helps against his will , this is because he fulfills himself and "gave" , he gave in a manner the kohen would acquire; and if this one does not wish to receive, that is a delay not from him; for this reason the mitzvah of giving was not nullified; but if he gives not in a manner the kohen acquires, or this one is not fit to acquire , this is not the way of giving the Torah spoke of at all; behold it is like placing in hefker where it does not help, as I said from Chullin that we say "giving" is written , and see Ketzot on this law when he gives and does not wish to receive; and in Netivot HaMishpat there and elsewhere I elaborated on giving against will , here I shortened. And in Mishna Berurah in name of Sefer Kol Yaakov and Lekutim who disagree with Peri Chadash that in priestly gifts giving against will is not called giving , nevertheless I already wrote above we say one wheat kernel exempts the whole stack and we interpret "and he shall give" even less than a perutah; and in less than a perutah acquisition does not apply at all , if so, one may say likewise if this recipient did not acquire, nevertheless the giver fulfills the mitzvah of giving. And one may reject: less than a perutah, although not considered money, because he waives , nevertheless acquisition applies well, as we say in Bava Metzia that if they needed a perutah they complete the judgment even in less than a perutah; and likewise in Bava Kamma we are warned even regarding less than a perutah because they are not fit to waive , nevertheless we call it "and he shall give" although less than a perutah; unlike giving to one not fit to acquire at all , it is not called giving at all, for it still stands in the owner's possession for everything , one may say it does not help; therefore it appears as I wrote, since the Torah did not exclude minors from priestly gifts, they acquire gifts from the Torah properly; it is not logical that if another comes and snatches from his hand the other should acquire , and even if you say others did not acquire and he must return to the owners , he gave with intent for another, and even if the other is a kohen, nevertheless benefit of use belongs to the owners and he must return this to the owners, as Ran wrote regarding a kohen who took gifts and the owners do not want , we remove from his hand; and see Peri Chadash , the owners will return and give to the minor; nevertheless this is not proper conduct that he take a share and not acquire that it be his and another can snatch from his hand. And even regarding leket, shikhechah, pe'ah , it requires study: that which is proven in Bava Metzia that if a minor does not acquire for himself, also in leket he gathered in the field he did not acquire and whoever wishes snatches from his hand , leket became the attribute of strict judgment of minors' judgment , and why make three inquiries at midday because of children who gather in the morning in the stalks when it is possible and likely others will snatch from them and their toil and effort is for nothing , and it requires study.
