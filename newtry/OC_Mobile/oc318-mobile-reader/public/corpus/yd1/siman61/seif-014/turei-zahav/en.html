Serving in this house who assigns, etc. It is clear there are two prohibitions — in the Gemara only one matter appears: one may not serve to assign to an important guest of the homeowner, for he does not act wholeheartedly; they learned from this all the more if the assigner assigns to the homeowner himself while serving him — Tur wrote the kohen assigner may not serve an Israelite who regularly gives him gifts lest he assign against his will — astonishing: what harm if he serves one who regularly gives? The main prohibition is because he serves the one he assigns to. Also Tur's ending contradicts the beginning — at the start he did not mention guest; I saw in Darkei Moshe an old parchment reading: but if the assignee is not a Torah scholar, or the hour does not press, or the kohen serves an Israelite, or assigns to a Torah scholar guest — forbidden. This reading too is not correct and does not match Shulchan Aruch; it appears "in the house of" is superfluous — he should have said serving an Israelite; rather "in the house of" refers to the assignee — the assigner may not assign to one in that Israelite's house; then the ending matches the beginning.
