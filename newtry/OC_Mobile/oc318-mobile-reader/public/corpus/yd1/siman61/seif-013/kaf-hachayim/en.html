And one must give them with the skin and wool, etc. — it implies the foreleg need not be given with the skin; see Bach who brought a dispute and concluded that Tur and Shulchan Aruch ruled the skin need not be given with the foreleg because the law is uncertain — burden of proof. Perishah 7 wrote: where they are accustomed to scald the skin, one must give the skin with the foreleg without flaying; except where they are not accustomed to scald the foreleg — then one may give flayed skin from the foreleg — see there. However Shulchan Gavoah letter 8 challenged Perishah and raised: practically, since it is monetary, the Israelite may say "I hold like Rashi and his colleagues that he need not give the foreleg skin" — therefore where not accustomed to scald foreleg skin, give without skin; but if the kohen seized the skin we do not remove it, since he may say "I hold like the view that one must give skin with the foreleg everywhere." He wrote this is primary; so he saw practiced in Jerusalem when fulfilling priestly gifts — they give the foreleg without its skin; so he practiced; he wondered at Perishah who was rabbi in Jerusalem and ruled contrary to custom — see there; Bach in Darkei Teshuvah 17.
