(Siman 61 seif 8) They give them to a kohenet. In other priestly gifts such as pidyon haben it is not explicit; it appears to depend: per Ulla who says "kohen" includes even kohenet except in minchah there is a limitation "Aharon and his sons" are written. If so, pidyon haben and similar are like gifts and one gives to kohenet; but per R' Aviyah who says it is limitation after limitation — perhaps only in gifts where Scripture revealed, not generally; and per halacha we follow Ulla, therefore also in other priestly gifts — unless we force that halacha follows Ulla but not for his reason. In Tosafot Kiddushin they wrote in one answer that Rav Kahana took pidyon haben for his wife — perhaps therefore Rashi in Chullin on Rav Kahana who eats for his wife: kohen includes kohenet; perhaps Rashi holds like Ulla; as I wrote. I saw in chapter 30 Kiddushin he wondered on Tosafot who wrote only in gifts Scripture revealed but not generally — I am astonished: for R' Aviyah it is so, but for Ulla there is no derivation in gifts except from reasoning "kohen" includes kohenet; one may say Rav Kahana holds like Ulla; for law requires further study. I again saw Rosh end of Bekhorot and Rashba responsa 337 wrote regarding pidyon haben specifically males, as in minchah "to Aharon and his sons" is written — so too pidyon haben "you shall give the money to Aharon and his sons"; so Rambam (chapter 1 Hilchot Bikkurim halacha 10) and Radbaz (siman 196) raised.
