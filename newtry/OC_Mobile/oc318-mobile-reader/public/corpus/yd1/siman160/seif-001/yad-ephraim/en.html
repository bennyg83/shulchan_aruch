In responsa. Tiferes Tzvi, simanim 48 and 49, discusses a lender who said, “I will lend you money provided that you give one hundred zuz to so-and-so.” This constitutes fixed ribbis. If the borrower gave the money to the recipient as a gift, the borrower has no claim against the recipient, but may claim from the person who instructed him to give it under the law of a guarantor. In my humble opinion, this requires study. Since the contemplated act was fulfilled and the lender actually lent him the money, there is no claim under the law of a guarantor. Rather, the borrower may claim the value of the benefit received by the lender. One must assess how much the lender would have paid a person to obey his instruction and give one hundred zuz to that recipient. This benefit is the fixed ribbis that the lender received, and he must return it. However, if the borrower gave the money to the recipient and said, “I am giving this to you because I am required to give it to so-and-so as ribbis,” the recipient must indeed return it if it was fixed ribbis.
