All rabbinic ribbit is permitted with the money of orphans. One should not ask that the borrower is still prohibited from giving ribbit, as Tosafot ask in Eizehu Neshech, page 70, s.v. maot shel yetomim. Tosafot ask this only according to the initial assumption that even Scriptural ribbit was permitted for orphans. If so, the borrower would still violate “You shall not cause your brother to pay interest.” According to the Gemara's conclusion, however, that only rabbinic ribbit is permitted, the ruling of the Tur and Shulchan Aruch is readily understood. In rabbinic ribbit, the borrower violates only the prohibition of placing a stumbling block before the blind, as the Rama writes at the beginning of this siman. Here, since there is no prohibition upon the lender, the borrower likewise does not violate placing a stumbling block before the blind. This is straightforward. The Mishneh LaMelech, chapter 4 of Hilchot Malveh VeLoveh, requires study.
