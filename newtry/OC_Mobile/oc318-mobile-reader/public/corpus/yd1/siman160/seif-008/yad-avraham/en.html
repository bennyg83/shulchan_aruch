(Seif 18) Any form of rabbinic interest is permitted with orphans’ money. One should not ask that the borrower is still forbidden to pay interest, as Tosafot ask in Bava Metzia 70, s.v. maot shel yetomim. Tosafot’s question was based on the initial assumption that even Torah-prohibited interest was permitted, in which case the borrower would violate “you shall not cause interest.” According to the conclusion that only rabbinic interest is permitted, the ruling of the Tur and Shulchan Aruch is readily understood. With rabbinic interest, the borrower violates only “before the blind,” as the gloss at the beginning of this siman states. Here, since the lender commits no prohibition, the borrower likewise does not violate “before the blind.” This is clear, though the Mishneh LaMelech, chapter 4 of Hilchot Malveh, requires study.
