(Seif 9, in the gloss) He receives compensation for the loan. If he acted in this manner and then lent to him again, expressly stating that this second loan was made as payment of the interest that had been fixed in exchange for his agreement to lend again, one may say that this is like any other case of fixed interest. The borrower must then pay him according to an appraisal of the value of having the money available for that period. This payment must come from the profit earned with the lender's money. The Torah prohibited only a loan made willingly by the lender. Here, however, he had no intention to lend at all, except as a form of payment of the interest. The transaction therefore does not have the status of a loan, and it is comparable to one who uses money in his possession without permission, from whom interest may be accepted, as the Mishneh LaMelech writes. This requires practical study.
