The Beit Yosef writes that according to those who prohibit later ribbit even without explanation, one must determine whether, when he expressly says, “This is because your money remained idle with me,” the prohibition is avak ribbit or fixed ribbit. See there. His words imply that according to those who permit it without explanation and prohibit it only when explicit, there is no question, because the Gemara at the beginning of Eizehu Neshech, page 62, concerning Rav Safra's statement, explicitly proves that it is only avak ribbit. Since it is not forbidden without explanation, the Gemara must concern an explicit statement. In truth, however, even according to the Rama, who permits an unspecified small gift, the question remains. Perhaps the Gemara concerns an unspecified substantial gift, which is forbidden because it is evident that it is given because of the loan. Such a gift is certainly not fixed ribbit when no explanation was made, whereas an explicit gift may indeed constitute fixed ribbit. In my opinion, there is clear proof for this from Bava Metzia, chapter Eizehu Neshech, page 69: Rava said that a person may say to another, “Here are four zuz; lend money to so-and-so.” The reason is that the Torah prohibited only ribbit that comes from the borrower to the lender. The poskim and the Tur and Shulchan Aruch rule accordingly in seif 13. The Gemara's formulation, “the Torah prohibited,” plainly shows that if the borrower himself gave the lender four zuz before the loan and said that it was in order that he lend to him, it would be Scripturally forbidden. Although it is advance ribbit, since he stated it explicitly it is fixed ribbit. This is the intent of the Hagahot Ashri of Mahari'ach in Eizehu Neshech, who writes regarding advance ribbit: “This applies specifically where he did not specify that it was for ribbit.” Meaning, only then is it classified as advance ribbit and prohibited rabbinically, but where he specifies it, it is fixed ribbit. This explanation is primary, though the Rama requires study. See the Beit Yosef. Nevertheless, one may argue that where the payment is made after the loan it is not Scripturally prohibited in this manner, since no amount had yet been fixed at the time of the loan. Indeed, there are authorities who hold that it is not fixed ribbit even when the amount is fixed while the debt remains outstanding, as stated in siman 166.
