And he specified that it was so that he would lend to him. This is the view of the Tur. Even according to what he wrote in seif 4, that he did not stipulate with him, etc., here it is different because this occurs before the loan. In the name of the Rambam, the Tur writes here as well that it is forbidden even without specification, whereas above the Tur wrote in his name that he agrees with Rashi, who permits it even without specification. It appears to me that here the warning concerning the prohibition is directed toward the borrower, who knows that he is sending it because of the loan that the other will grant him, and the Merciful One examines the heart. Regarding this, the first opinion holds that even so there is no prohibition unless he specifies it, whereas the Rambam forbids it even without specification if this is his intent. Above, however, the Tur teaches that whenever the borrower gives extra without specification, we presume that it was certainly not because of the loan, since ordinarily he would not commit a prohibition. According to my explanation of the Tur's words below in this siman, seif 17, this is even more satisfactory, for the Rambam's permission above when one gives more refers to a sale. See there.
