It is permitted to say to another, “Here is one zuz; lend ten dinars to so-and-so.” It appears to me that this is not limited to giving him the zuz immediately before the loan. Even after the loan, one may give him a zuz so that he extends the repayment period, provided that the giver does not subsequently collect it from the borrower. This is not comparable to what the Taz and later authorities write in seif katan 10, that if he says, “For as long as so-and-so does not repay the money you lent him, I will give you one dinar each month,” it is forbidden. There, every additional delay in repaying the principal creates a greater ribbit obligation, and the giver is therefore treated as though he had also lent the principal. This is not so where he gives a single zuz once. After writing this, I found that it is explicit in Tosafot, the Ran, and the Mordechai at the beginning of Kiddushin, page 6b, s.v. de'arvach. See there carefully. See also Tosafot, Menachot, page 90b.
