And he specified etc. This refers to advance interest and belated interest; and Beit Yosef wrote it requires study — if he specified, whether it is fixed interest or dust of interest; and Darkei Moshe brings it; and the Taz wrote it is dust of interest and cannot be extracted in court; and so appears to be the main ruling as below siman 166 s.k. 7-8.
Or he is etc. Beit Yosef wrote it is possible even if he was previously accustomed to a large gift — it is forbidden because it is a public matter comparable to dwelling in his courtyard and using his servants as below seif 7; and Hagahot Derishah brings it.
A large gift, which presumably is as if he specified it. This is how it should read. But a small gift without specification is permitted, and it is not comparable to above in seif 4 where without specification it is forbidden, because there it is at the time of repayment. So wrote Rosh and the students of Rashba. It implies that where the borrower intends it for ribbit, even without specification it is forbidden. So wrote Baal HaTerumot, and Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe bring it; see Bach. Beit Yosef wrote that this is specifically somewhat close to the loan, but when it is very distant there is no concern at all, and even a large gift is permitted as long as it is unspecified. It appears that if he specified, even a small gift is forbidden, even very distant. Beit Yosef further wrote in the name of Hagahot Asheri and Rambam that even without specification it is forbidden where he was not accustomed previously, end quote. In Hagahat Derishah in the name of Maharshal he wrote that anything known that otherwise he would not have done for him at all is like a large gift and forbidden, end quote. It appears to me that everything depends on the matter and on what is recognizable in the sender's intent; according to this one can say that the poskim do not disagree.
