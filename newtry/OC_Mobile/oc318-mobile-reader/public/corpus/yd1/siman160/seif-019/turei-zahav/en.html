If the orphan took to his portion so much profit, etc. For another person, he did not permit this — all that was done in the prohibition should not be taken even if the borrower earned much; but here what was done in the prohibition is as if it was not done to harm the orphans, and it is as if nothing was stipulated at the time of the loan; and for the benefit of the orphans we say, even though they did not stipulate for a permitted matter it is as if he stipulated, and all that is permitted to take is valid. And it seems to me he takes half of all that the recipient earned, even much more than the fixed amount — only deducting his labor wages; since it is as if no stipulation was made at all, the orphan has the right to claim on the better side, as if it were stipulated that he take half, even if more than what the guardian fixed in prohibition — for that fixing counts for nothing, as if it was never done. And the Levush wrote that in any case he does not take more than the fixed amount; and this is very astounding — what do we care about the fixing, since it was done in prohibition it is as if it never was; and it is unlike a transaction done in prohibition which is valid, for there the transaction itself is as it should be only there is a separate prohibition — unlike here. And this is what Beit Yosef wrote: as if he stipulated close to profit — meaning he takes half the profit, as I wrote.
