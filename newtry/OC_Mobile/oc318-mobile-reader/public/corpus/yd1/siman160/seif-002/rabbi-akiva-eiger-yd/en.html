(In the gloss) But regarding rabbinic interest, he does not transgress. For this reason, if one swore to pay the interest, the oath takes effect and he must pay it. The Rashba writes this in responsum siman 938, cited by the Beit Yosef at the end of this siman. The Ran, however, writes that even regarding rabbinic interest the law is like that of anyone who swears to violate a rabbinic prohibition. Although the oath takes effect, he is nevertheless not permitted to violate a rabbinic prohibition through an affirmative act. See there. See also Responsa Torat Chaim of Maharchash, kelal 3, siman 17, that even according to the Rashba, although the borrower is obligated to pay, the lender may not accept the interest, and beit din prevents him from taking it. Where there is uncertainty whether the matter constitutes rabbinic interest, according to the Rashba, since even definite rabbinic interest must be paid by the borrower and only the lender is forbidden to receive it, in a case of doubt the lender may accept it, because the doubt concerns a rabbinic prohibition. Even according to the Ran, one may say that since the oath certainly takes effect even regarding definite rabbinic interest, and he is prevented from fulfilling it only because he may not actively violate a rabbinic prohibition, where the status is doubtful he may pay because of the rule governing doubt in a rabbinic matter. He is consequently obligated to pay by force of the oath. Even according to those who hold that where the obligation created by an oath is itself doubtful, and the matter is one of monetary doubt, he need not pay because of the doubtful oath, see Shach to Choshen Mishpat 72:23, here there is no doubt concerning the oath's validity. The oath certainly takes effect even regarding actual rabbinic interest. Since in a doubtful case he may pay, although he could otherwise claim kim li that it is rabbinic interest and thereby exempt himself, once he swore he is obligated by the oath. See Responsa Mabit, part 1, siman 151; Rashdam, Yoreh De'ah, siman 72; Rashach, part 3, siman 36; Ralbach, simanim 38 and 103; Maharit, Yoreh De'ah, siman 40; Responsa Shnei HaMeorot HaGedolim, siman 3; and Responsa Raanach, part 2, siman 22.
