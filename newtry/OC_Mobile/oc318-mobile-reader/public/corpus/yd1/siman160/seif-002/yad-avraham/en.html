Even if the borrower, of his own accord, gives him more at the time of repayment, though he did not stipulate this with him, etc., it is forbidden. It appears that the same applies to any rabbinic ribbit that arises through a loan. For example, if one lent a seah of produce for a seah in a place where it was inexpensive, and the borrower repays it in a place where it is expensive, this is rabbinic ribbit, as stated in the Gemara, chapter Eizehu Neshech, page 73, and as ruled by the Tur and Shulchan Aruch below, siman 173, seif 17, in accordance with Rashi's explicit explanation there. See there. It is forbidden even where no stipulation was made and the borrower does not say that he is giving the additional amount here because of ribbit. A conclusive proof is found in a responsum of the Rosh, cited by the Tur and Shulchan Aruch below at the end of siman 162: “If he became obligated to him for wheat, and the loan was made in one place while the claim is made in another place where wheat is more expensive, he shall pay him only according to its value in the place of the loan.” There, no stipulation was made initially, yet it is nevertheless forbidden.
