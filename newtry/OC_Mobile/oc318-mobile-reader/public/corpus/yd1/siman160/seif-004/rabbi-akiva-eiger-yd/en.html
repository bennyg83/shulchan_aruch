(Seif 6) He would send him a gift and explicitly state that it was so that he would lend to him. In my humble opinion, this applies specifically where he sends it as a gift and asks the recipient to lend him money because of it, in which case it is merely a form of inducement. If, however, he stipulated that the gift was given on condition that the recipient lend to him, then if the recipient does not lend, he must return it. At the time of the loan it is therefore considered as though the borrower then gives him the gift. This resembles one who says, "The debt that you owe me is forgiven on condition that you lend to me." That is equivalent to saying, "If you lend to me, the debt is forgiven," which constitutes fixed interest, as the Beit Yosef writes. This appears correct to me.
