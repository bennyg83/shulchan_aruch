However, if the money was not in his possession as a loan, but only by way of a sale, it is permitted in such a case. This means that he gives him something without specification and does not say that it is because of his money. It appears to me that this applies specifically where he gives him only a small item, so that it is not evident that he is giving it because his money had remained idle in the other's possession. If he gives a large amount, however, it is as though he stated this explicitly, as the Tur and Rama write in this siman, seif 6, regarding later ribbit. This applies all the more to a sale, for later ribbit is more lenient than avak ribbit, as the Rashba writes in a responsum cited by the Beit Yosef at the end of this siman and in siman 177 in Bedek HaBayit. The Beit Yosef likewise writes at the beginning of siman 161, and the Rama there in seif 2. See there. This is also the view of the Tur at the beginning of siman 173, cited by the Mechaber there at the end of seif 1 in the name of “there is one who says.” This is also evident from the Gemara there concerning the field guards, as ruled by the poskim and by the Tur and Shulchan Aruch in siman 173, seif 12: it is forbidden to add to the guards' wages in order that they wait for payment. The discussion plainly concerns a case where nothing was specified and the employer did not say that he was adding because their wages had been delayed, as is evident from Rashi and from the Rambam in chapter 8 of Hilchot Malveh VeLoveh. See there carefully. If the case involved an explicit statement, the ruling would be obvious. Moreover, why did Rava tell them to turn the arrangement into work at the threshing floor, so that they would assist in threshing? He should instead have told them merely to give the additional amount without explanation. Rashi writes: “If they add for you, this is not payment for waiting for your wages; rather, they are giving you a discounted rate.” The Nimukei Yosef writes likewise. See the Mishneh LaMelech, chapter 4 of Hilchot Malveh VeLoveh, which requires study. It is explicit that the case involves no statement, yet it is forbidden to add when paying the wages. If so, it is certainly forbidden in a sale, since rental is more lenient than a sale, as is evident from the Hagahot Maimoniyot, chapter 8 of Hilchot Malveh VeLoveh, and from the Beit Yosef at the end of this siman in the name of Rabbeinu Eliezer of Touques and Rabbeinu Yerucham. See there. Although we do not rule entirely in accordance with that view, as stated in the Hagahot Ashri at the end of Eizehu Neshech and in the Rama, siman 176, seif 6, concerning one who has already become obligated for the rental payment, nevertheless it is evident that rental is not treated more stringently. Since the addition is forbidden for rental, the same applies to a sale. See what I write at the beginning of siman 163, with Hashem's help.
