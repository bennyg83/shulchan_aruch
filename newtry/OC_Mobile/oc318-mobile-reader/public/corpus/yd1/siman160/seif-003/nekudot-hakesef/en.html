(Seif katan 11) He challenged the Rama. There is no difficulty. Where the borrower did not personally receive the money from the lender, since there is no agency for the commission of a transgression, it is considered as though he borrowed the money from the agent. The ribbit that the agent sends to the lender is therefore not ribbit, because the borrower did not borrow anything directly from the lender. We do not say that since he borrowed from him through an agent, it is as though he borrowed directly from him, because there is no agency for a transgression. But where the borrower personally received the money, he is sending the lender ribbit in consideration of that loan. Here one cannot invoke the rule that there is no agency for a transgression, because ultimately this ribbit belongs to the lender. This is clear. What he further wrote, 'this is astonishing, for there is an explicit Mishnah at the end of the chapter Eizehu Neshech,' etc., also presents no difficulty, because there the borrower personally receives the money from the lender, as I wrote in Shach, seif katan 22. What he further asked, how this differs from one prohibited by a vow from deriving benefit, etc., also presents no difficulty, because ultimately he derives benefit from him. The proof he brought from orphans' funds also presents no difficulty, because there was another possible remedy there as well; the Gemara merely mentioned one of them, as I wrote in Darkei Moshe. His proof from the responsum of Rashba, because he did not give it to the guardian, etc., is also not valid, because a guardian is treated like the litigant himself. We therefore have no basis to reject the explicit words of Rashi, Mordechai, and Maharik concerning a sheaf of wheat, especially since I cited many later authorities in Shach there who agree with this view.
