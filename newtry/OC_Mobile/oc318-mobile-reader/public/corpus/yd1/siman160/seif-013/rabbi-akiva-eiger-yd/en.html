(Shulchan Aruch, seif 18) With the money of orphans. This applies even to those who are themselves orphans of orphans, as the Mishneh LaMelech writes in the name of Maharit, responsa part 1, siman 144. If their father lent money with rabbinic interest and then died, the orphans may not collect even the interest that accrued after their father's death. This is more stringent than the case of one who borrowed from orphans without any stipulation concerning interest. If orphans themselves lent money with rabbinic interest and did not collect until they reached adulthood, they may collect the interest that accrued while they were minors, but not what accrued after they became adults. This remains true even if, while they were minors, they capitalized the entire amount as principal at the time of the loan. The Mishneh LaMelech concludes this. Maharikash, however, writes in siman 161 that where a promissory note containing rabbinic interest came before orphans, they may collect what accrued after their father's death. See there. He further writes that if the borrower undertook to pay the lender's successors, the orphans may recover rabbinic interest through beit din as though it had been given to their father as rabbinic interest after his death. The Gidulei Terumah writes that the same law applies where the owner became mentally incompetent, for an administrator is appointed to supervise his property. The Mishneh LaMelech writes that the same applies to a deaf-mute.
