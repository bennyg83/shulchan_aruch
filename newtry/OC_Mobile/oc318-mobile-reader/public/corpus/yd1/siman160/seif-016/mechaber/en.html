It is permitted to say to his fellow, “Here is a zuz, and tell so-and-so that he should lend me,” and even to the son of the lender it is permitted to say so, provided that he is an adult and does not rely on his father’s table. {Rama: Some say that it is forbidden for the lender to take from this recipient of the zuz, so that they will not come to subterfuge (Maggid Mishneh, perek 5 of Hilchos Malveh V’Loveh). Some say that it is permitted for a Jew to say to his fellow Jew, “Go and lend me money from so-and-so, a Jew, with interest,” and it is permitted to afterward give the interest to the agent to bring it to him, for the Torah forbade only interest that comes directly from borrower to lender, and the agent does not do any prohibition, for this interest is not his. And if due to “a person’s agent is like himself,” there is no agency for a matter of transgression; and one should not publicize the matter before an am ha’aretz (Mordechai in the name of Rashi). And this is the primary ruling, even though there are those who cast doubt on this permission and are stringent to forbid it (Beit Yosef and Nimukim), one may rely on it at a time of need. Nevertheless, if the borrower received the money himself from the lender and only sends him the interest, it is forbidden (there, in Mordechai). But if the lender’s agent lent to the borrower with interest and made a document in the name of the lender, it is as if the lender himself lent to him, and it is forbidden, even if the agent did so without the knowledge of his sender, for the document is made the essence of the loan and it is written in the name of the lender (Maharik, shoresh 17).}
