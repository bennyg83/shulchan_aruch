Sending. It means that the Lord is not in any great way, nor in any way, nor in any way, nor is it possible for his father to support him, and that he is not allowed to do so with any great sin, but that he is not even close to his father, that his father has no need for his father, but that his father is not allowed to live here
Permissibly. The Levush and the Taz challenged the Rama regarding this law and brought several proofs that it is a complete prohibition. In Nekudot HaKesef he refutes all the Taz's claims and concludes that the Rama's words are correct and clear; see there. So too the Bach rules, writing that this is how we rule. However, this is specifically when he sends through a Jew. Through a non-Jew it is forbidden, for one cannot say "there is no agency for a transgression," since the non-Jew does not transgress through his agency, as Bnei Noach are not prohibited in ribit.
