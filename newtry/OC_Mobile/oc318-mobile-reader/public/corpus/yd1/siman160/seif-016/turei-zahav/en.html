There is no agent for a transgression. Mordechai wrote this in the name of Rashi; and Beit Yosef wrote about it that a mistaken student wrote this and attributed it to a great authority, and that it is an outright prohibition — and the proof is from what the Tur wrote: "and he should also not go back and take it from the borrower," and also from the law of a Jew who lends at interest through a gentile, etc.; and Darkei Moshe explains the words of Rashi: there is no proof from what the Tur wrote that he should not go back and take it, since there the loan is from the lender to the borrower and only the payment of interest is through another — in this [Mordechai] distinguishes; and based on this Rema wrote afterward: "nevertheless, if the borrower himself received the money," etc. And the Levush disagrees with Rema based on powerful reasoning, even though he has no explicit proof in his words. And I will bring a proof for his position, with Heaven's help. First I say: what [Rema] distinguishes between whether the loan itself was also made through an agent or not — there is no place for these words, for either way it is difficult. If he is not his agent against the lender, then there are two transgressions — of the principal with the agent, since they are like borrower and lender, each obligated to the other, and the agent [re-]loans to the lender and gives him interest, like a Jew who has a gentile's money and lends it to another Jew on behalf of the gentile — which is forbidden because there is no agency for the gentile, as stated in the Talmud (72a) — so too here, since there is no agent for transgression, the agent himself becomes a party and he too is like a borrower vis-à-vis the lender, and likewise with the lender — and furthermore, if the giving of interest to the agent's hand is not a transgression — what transgression is there if he takes the loan itself from the borrower in his own hand? For the loan itself involves no prohibition, even if the borrower said "lend me at interest" — since if afterward no interest is given, what is his sin and what is his transgression? And if you say there is a sin in that very statement of "lend me at interest" — then if he did not mention interest but simply "lend me," there is no prohibition; and if the giving of the interest into the agent's hand is nothing — and a difficulty: an explicit mishnah at the end of chapter Eizehu Neshekh regarding early and late interest states: he was sending to him [a gift] because you leave my money tied up with you, or regarding late interest: because of my money that sat idle with you — and there the interest is brought through an agent; and furthermore, what is the difference from a debtor who had a vow not to benefit from his friend — for we say in siman 221 seif 8 that it is forbidden to give him through his agent, and we do not say "there is no agent for transgression" — but rather it is certainly clear that "there is no agent for transgression" only helps to exempt him from the liability of excision [karet] as in shechutei chutz, or of lashes as in chapter 1 of Bava Metzia regarding one who has the corners of his head rounded by another — but there is still a prohibition, and his thought was carried out through the agent. It is obvious that this is forbidden. And furthermore, proof from what is stated in chapter Eizehu Neshekh (Bava Metzia 70a): what should we do with orphans' money? We see a trustworthy man and give it to him in court, close to profit and far from loss — and Rashi explains: the court has power to expropriate the assets of the recipient in favor of the orphans and to stipulate that he give close to profit, etc.; and there the interest does not come from the recipient's hand to the orphans' hand, and even so is it forbidden with outright interest, but only with close-to-profit and far-from-loss based on the court's expropriation — and one cannot reject this by saying the court becomes guardian in place of the orphans and they themselves are in the lender's place, for if so, the same challenge would arise: why in truth do they do this? Why should they not do so through an agent of the orphans and earn even outright interest? Rather, it is certain that this is an outright prohibition; and so it is clarified in Beit Yosef in this siman below under "a guardian," in the name of Rashba: "for the guardian is not appointed on their behalf to keep it for himself but to give it to the widow or the money's owner, and the guardian is thereby a facilitator of sin," etc. — and I quoted it in seif 20. And the Levush correctly wrote that this matter should be erased from the Shulchan Arukh and no person should rely on it at all under any circumstances, and certainly a mistaken student wrote this in the name of Rashi, and such a stumbling block did not emerge from the righteous one.
