And he is not supported at his father's table. The Tur concludes: But if he is supported at his father's table, it is forbidden. Since he did not conclude, “but if he is a minor and is supported,” this implies that whenever he is supported it is forbidden in every case, even if he is an adult. A minor, however, is forbidden even if he is not supported at his father's table, for his father is nevertheless obligated to support him when he has nothing, as the Beit Yosef writes. Although the Beit Yosef leaves this requiring further study, this appears to be the primary ruling. Although the first chapter of Bava Metzia, 12b, states that a minor who is not supported at his father's table is considered an adult, that applies regarding a found object, because his hand is not like his father's hand. Here, however, the reason is that it is considered as though the father received it, since when the minor son has nothing the father is obligated to support him. So it appears to me.
There is no agency for a transgression. The Mordechai writes this in the name of Rashi. The Beit Yosef writes that an erring student wrote it and attached it to a great authority, for it is absolutely forbidden. He proves this from the Tur's statement that the donor may not go back and take it from the borrower, and also from the law of a Jew who borrows with ribbis through a non-Jew, etc. The Darkei Moshe defends Rashi's words, arguing that there is no proof from the Tur's statement that the donor may not go back and take it, because there the loan itself passes from the lender to the borrower, and only the payment of ribbis is through another person. The Mordechai distinguishes this case. Based upon this, the Rama writes below: Nevertheless, if the borrower himself received the money, etc. The Levush disputes the Rama with strong reasoning, although his statements contain no proof. With God's help, I will bring proof for his words. First, the distinction between whether the loan itself is also carried out through an agent or not has no basis. Either way there is a difficulty. If the intermediary is not considered the borrower's agent vis-à-vis the lender, then there are two transgressions. The principal and the intermediary are like a lender and borrower, for the intermediary is the principal's counterparty and gives him ribbis. Afterward, the intermediary is like a borrower vis-à-vis the lender, who lends him the money and receives ribbis from him. We find similarly that where a Jew holds a non-Jew's money and lends it to another Jew as the non-Jew's agent, it is forbidden, since there is no agency for a non-Jew, as stated in the Gemara, 72b. Here too, since there is no agency for a transgression, the intermediary becomes the borrower's counterparty and likewise the lender's counterparty. Furthermore, if there is no transgression in the ribbis payment made to the intermediary, what transgression occurs if the intermediary takes the loan into his own hand from the lender? There is no prohibition in the loan itself, even if he says, “Lend to me with ribbis,” provided that he afterward pays no ribbis. What then is his sin? If you say that the statement itself is sinful because he mentions ribbis, then if he merely says, “Lend to me,” without mentioning ribbis, there should be no prohibition, since the loan is permitted and the later payment of ribbis to the intermediary would supposedly be meaningless. This is astonishing, for there is an explicit Mishnah at the end of Eizehu Neshech regarding advance and delayed ribbis: If he sends him a gift so that he will lend him his money, or afterward because his money had remained idle with him, it is forbidden. There, at the time of the loan they knew nothing of the ribbis, and nevertheless it is forbidden when an agent brings the ribbis. There is a further difficulty from one who is forbidden by a vow to derive benefit from another. In siman 221, seif 8, we rule that it is forbidden to give him benefit through his agent, and we do not say that there is no agency for a transgression. Rather, it is clear that the principle that there is no agency for a transgression is effective only to exempt the principal from the punishment of kares, as in the case of slaughtering offerings outside the Temple in the chapter HaIsh Mekadesh, or from lashes, as in the first chapter of Bava Metzia regarding one who rounds another person's hair through an agent. A prohibition nevertheless remains, for he commits a forbidden act and his intent is fulfilled through the agent. It is obvious that this is forbidden. There is another proof from the Gemara in Eizehu Neshech, Bava Metzia 70a: What should be done with orphans' money? We find a trustworthy person and give it to him through beit din under terms close to profit and remote from loss. Rashi explains that beit din has authority to declare the recipient's property ownerless for the benefit of the orphans and to stipulate that he give them terms close to profit, etc. There too, the ribbis does not pass directly from the recipient to the lenders, who are the orphans, yet ordinary ribbis is forbidden; only terms remote from loss and close to profit are permitted through the power of beit din. We do not say that even absolute ribbis is permitted merely because the loan itself does not pass directly from lender to borrower. One cannot reject this by saying that beit din acts as the father of the orphans and therefore stands in their place, for this is true only to benefit them, not to their detriment. Moreover, if so, the question remains: Why do they act in this manner? They should appoint an agent and thereby allow the orphans to take even absolute ribbis. It is therefore clear that this is absolutely forbidden. This is likewise explicit in the Beit Yosef later in this siman, s.v. Apotropus, in the name of the Rashba: “Because he did not give it to the guardian for the guardian to retain for himself, but so that he would give it to the widow or the owner of the money; the guardian is merely an intermediary in the sin,” etc. I copied this in seif 20. The Levush therefore wrote correctly that this ruling should be erased from the Shulchan Aruch, and no person should rely upon it at all, even in pressing circumstances and great need. Certainly an erring student wrote it in Rashi's name, and such a stumbling block did not emerge from the mouth of that righteous man.
