(Taz, end of seif katan 8) But if he is not an important person, there is no prohibition. See the Ran in the first chapter of Kiddushin. He explains that according to Levi, acquisition by exchange is performed with the vessel of the transferor because of the benefit the transferor receives from the recipient, even when the recipient is not an important person. In that case, however, the benefit is worth less than a perutah. Since acquisition by exchange is effective with a vessel even when the vessel is worth less than a perutah, it is effective even where the recipient is not important. The Ran further explains that according to Rav, acquisition by exchange is not performed with the transferor's vessel, even when the recipient is an important person, because the transferor gives it on condition that it be returned, and receipt of something given on condition of return is not considered a benefit. See there. Accordingly, one may say that Rav does not disagree with Levi concerning the basic principle. Even receipt from a person who is not important is still considered some benefit. Acquisition by exchange is different, because the object is given on condition that it be returned and there is therefore no benefit at all. Here, regarding interest, many authorities hold that even a benefit worth less than a perutah is forbidden, at least rabbinically. It may therefore be forbidden even where the recipient is not an important person. Examine carefully, and see Shulchan Aruch, Orach Chaim, siman 223, and Magen Avraham, seif katan 8.
