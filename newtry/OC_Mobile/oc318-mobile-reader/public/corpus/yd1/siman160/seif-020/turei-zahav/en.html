They are exempt from paying. This is because they did not stipulate the ribbis and did not take it directly from the borrower. Rather, the borrower gives it to the guardian who stipulated with him, and the guardian gives it to the widow or orphan. Even regarding actual theft, in such a case they are not obligated to return it, as we learned: One who steals and feeds the stolen property to his children, the children are exempt from paying. All the more so regarding ribbis. The guardian is likewise exempt, because the borrower did not give it to the guardian for the guardian to retain for himself, but so that he would give it to the orphan. The guardian is merely an intermediary in the sin and is not subject to the obligation of return. So wrote the Beit Yosef in the name of the Rashba.
Except with proof. It is obvious that as long as he retains possession of the orphans' money, he is believed against the orphans, with an oath, that the ribbis came to them, and they consequently suffer the loss of the ribbis that he returned.
