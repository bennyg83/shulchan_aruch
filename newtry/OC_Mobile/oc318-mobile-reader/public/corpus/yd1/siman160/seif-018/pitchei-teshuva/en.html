Of orphans. Maharit permits orphans to borrow at rabbinic interest from other orphans and to collect a debt their father incurred to orphans under such terms. Maharshach distinguishes where the deceased father had lent at rabbinic interest, arguing that the heirs cannot collect it. Mishneh LaMelech rejects his proof but accepts the practical conclusion.
Or Torah study funds. Whether funds belonging to a Torah institution or another body without identified private owners may be lent at fixed interest is discussed by Rashba, Besamim Rosh, Maharit, Pnei Moshe, and Shemesh Tzedakah.
But rabbinic interest is permitted. See Shach and Urim VeTumim.
Permitted. Magen Avraham cites Shibolei HaLeket that one may borrow with interest for a Shabbat or mitzvah meal. Be'er Yaakov explains that this means only rabbinic interest.
