He is believed without an oath. Although the words of this gloss follow what the Beit Yosef writes in siman 177 in the name of a responsum of the Rashba, concerning a borrower who undertook in the loan document to pay a tax, etc., nevertheless I do not understand their reasoning. Granted that the orphan holds a document against the borrower, why should he be believed without an oath? Perhaps it refers to a case where the orphan has a guardian who testifies on his behalf, for a guardian is believed to testify, as written in Choshen Mishpat, siman 37. The context, however, does not imply that this is the case. The Levush explains this ruling by saying that we do not presume people to be wicked, and whatever the orphan received he certainly received permissibly. His words are exceedingly astonishing, for in siman 169, seif 25, we rule that if the lender holds a document, he takes payment only after swearing when the borrower claims, “You took ribbis from me.” I can resolve this ruling only by saying that since the Sages were lenient regarding ribbis for orphans, permitting them the dust of ribbis, they were also lenient here: When the parties dispute whether Torah-level ribbis was paid, the orphan need not swear. Yet I do not know the Talmudic source for this ruling. For wherever the matter is not merely rabbinic ribbis, the chapter HaRibbis states that orphans who consumed property that was not theirs should follow the deceased to the graveyard. This law is also written in siman 177, seif 9; see what I wrote there. One who wishes to rule in accordance with this gloss must bring proof, especially where money is to be extracted without an oath. So it appears to me.
