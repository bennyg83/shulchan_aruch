In orphans. The Bible concludes that it does not have an orphanage in it because it is engaged in ripening as women:
As long as they don't grow up. And who is not his father wants to support him and to deal with his needs, he has no judgment on this in the name of the donations he has given:
Trusted without an oath. Although the words of this gloss follow from what Beit Yosef wrote in siman 177 in the name of a responsum of Rashba regarding a borrower who at the time of the loan accepted by contract to pay a tax, etc. — nevertheless I have not grasped the reasoning of this ruling: granted that the orphan has a contract against the borrower, even so why should he be trusted without an oath? And possibly it refers to where the orphan has a guardian and he testifies for him — for a guardian is trusted to testify as written in Choshen Mishpat siman 37; but the matter at hand does not appear to deal with this. And in Levush he gave a reason for this law: because we do not impute to people that they falsely made a memorandum, and certainly all that he received [he received] in a permitted way — and his words are very puzzling, for in siman 169 we rule in seif 25 that if the lender holds a document, he takes an oath and collects upon this very claim that the borrower asserts he took interest from him; and I cannot settle this law except by saying: since [the Sages] were lenient in the laws of interest for orphans — that even rabbinically forbidden interest [avak ribbit] is permitted with them — they also were lenient in this: that when there is a contradiction between them, as to whether he took biblical interest, he need not swear about it. But I do not know the source of this law in the Talmud — for of all interest that is not rabbinic it is said in chapter HaRibbit: "orphans who ate [from the money of others] — let them go after their father" [meaning they inherit the obligation to return it]; and also in siman 177 seif 9 this law is written — see what I wrote there. And one who comes to rule like this gloss must bring a proof, in particular since it involves extracting money without an oath, in my view.
