The orphan is believed. At first glance the Rama's words imply that an orphan has some special advantage over other people, for below in siman 169, seif 25, we say that if one claims, 'I did not take interest,' he must swear, whereas an orphan would not. This requires study. This law comes from a responsum of the Rashba cited by the Beit Yosef in siman 177. There the Rashba discusses one who, at the time of the loan, undertook in writing, etc. When the orphan later came to collect the debt, the debtor claimed that the orphan must deduct what he had already given for sustenance, while the orphan claimed that he had received nothing. The Rashba ruled that the orphan is believed, and even if the debtor says, 'Swear to me,' we pay no attention. His meaning is that this is a loan evidenced by a document, and the debtor says, 'Swear to me before you collect,' in accordance with the rule governing a creditor holding a document, where if the debtor says, 'Swear to me,' the creditor must swear before collecting, as stated in Choshen Mishpat, siman 82, seif 2. If the debtor does not demand an oath, even though he contradicts the creditor, the creditor collects without an oath. See there. Nevertheless, here we do not heed the debtor's demand, as the Tur and Shulchan Aruch write there in Choshen Mishpat, seif 10, that if the debtor says, 'Swear to me that this debt contains no interest,' while the creditor stands upon his document, we do not require him to swear. There is no indication that the law differs between an orphan and anyone else. The Rashba's responsum simply happened to involve an orphan, and there is no reason to distinguish. Olat Tamid likewise writes: one who owes money to orphans, etc., and the orphan claims that he received no prohibited interest, the orphan is believed without an oath, because we do not presume people to be wicked, and whatever he received was certainly received permissibly. End. Since he did not explain the law in its simple sense as a claim that nothing at all was received, it follows that he understood it to mean that the orphan claims he received nothing in a prohibited manner, but only permissibly. In that case he is believed without an oath, just as ordinary people are below in siman 169, seif 25. It is possible to explain the Rama's words in the same way as Olat Tamid. This is clear.
