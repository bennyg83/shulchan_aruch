The orphan is believed. From the Rav's words it seems at first glance that an orphan has some additional advantage over other people, for we say below in siman 169 seif 25 that if one claims, "I did not take ribbit," he must swear, unlike with an orphan. This requires study. This law is from Rashba's responsum that Beit Yosef brought in siman 177, and there he wrote only regarding one who accepted upon himself at the time of the loan in a document, etc.; and when the orphan comes to collect his debt, the other claims that he should deduct what he gave him for maintenance, while the orphan claims that he received nothing from him. The orphan is believed, and even if he says to him, "swear to me," we do not heed him, end quote. The meaning is that it is a loan by document, and he says to him, "swear to me before you collect," according to the law of a loan by document, that if the borrower claims, "swear to me," he must swear before collecting, as stated in Choshen Mishpat siman 82 seif 2. But if he does not say "swear to me," even though he contradicts him, he collects without an oath; see there. Nevertheless here we do not heed him, as Tur and Shulchan Aruch wrote there in Choshen Mishpat seif 10, that if he says, "swear to me that this debt is not ribbit," and the lender stands on his document, we do not listen to him to make him swear. But that there should be a distinction between an orphan and other people is not implied at all, for the case in Rashba's responsum happened with an orphan, and the same applies to other people, since there is no reason to distinguish. So too Ateret Zekenim wrote: one who owes orphans, etc., and the orphan claims that he received no forbidden ribbit, the orphan is believed without an oath, for we do not presume people wicked, and certainly whatever he received he received in a permitted way, end quote. Since he did not explain it simply, that it speaks where he claims he received nothing at all, it is clear that he holds that specifically when he claims he did not receive in a prohibited way, but in a permitted way, he is believed without an oath, as below in siman 169 seif 25 regarding ordinary people. It is possible to explain the Rav's words this way as Ateret Zekenim explained; this is easy to understand.
