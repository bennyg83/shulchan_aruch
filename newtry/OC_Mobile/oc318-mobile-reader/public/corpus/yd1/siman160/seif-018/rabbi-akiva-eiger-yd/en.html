(Shulchan Aruch, seif 20) And he fed the orphans. This implies specifically that the interest had already been consumed for their maintenance. If it still exists, it is recovered from them. In the Rivash's responsum cited by the Beit Yosef, however, it is clear that regarding minor orphans, even if the money still exists, it is not recovered from them, because the obligation to return interest is only a mitzvah and not a lien upon property, as stated below in siman 161, seif 5. Minor orphans are not obligated in the performance of mitzvot. See there. Nevertheless, it appears from that responsum that this applies specifically after the money was given to the minor orphans. If the interest remains in the administrator's possession, it is recovered from him. The Mishneh LaMelech likewise writes this. At first glance, there is room for analysis. We maintain that one may acquire property on behalf of a minor. It should therefore be said that as soon as the administrator received the money from the borrower, he acquired it on behalf of the orphans, and it is considered as though it came into their possession. Perhaps, since acquisition on another's behalf is derived from the law of agency, this falls within the rule that there is no agency for the commission of a transgression, and he therefore cannot acquire it for them. Independently, one may also say that since it is forbidden for the orphans to receive the interest, acquisition of it is not considered beneficial to them, because it causes them to violate the prohibition of interest. The wording of the Shulchan Aruch, that they are exempt from payment even after reaching adulthood, is difficult to me. Why did he not state the same law regarding an administrator acting for adult orphans, who are likewise exempt, as appears in the source of the ruling in the Rashba? See Responsa Kerem Shlomo, siman 4.
