Any interest that is by rabbinic law is permitted with the money of orphans, or of hekdesh for the poor, or of Talmud Torah, or for the needs of a beis haknesses. {Rama: And so they are accustomed to be lenient (Beit Yosef in the name of Rambam and Rosh), even though there are those who are stringent that it is permitted only in beis din (Mordechai; and so it is implied in Maharai, and in Terumas HaDeshen, siman 65, and in Chochmas Shlomo, siman 250; and Rashi, perek Eizehu Neshech, daf 70, end of amud 1). There are places whose practice is that an apotropos lends the money of orphans with fixed interest, and it is a mistaken practice and one should not follow it (Maharil, in a responsum); and any apotropos who does so — see in Choshen Mishpat siman 34 whether he is disqualified. But with interest that is by rabbinic law it is permitted, as long as the orphan has not grown up to engage in his money in the way of other people; even though he is already 13 years old, since he has not reached the category of understanding he is called an orphan regarding this matter (Beit Yosef in the name of Terumas HaDeshen, and the words of the Beit Yosef himself). One who owes money to orphans, and when he comes to repay he claims that he gave them interest and wants to deduct it from his debt, and the orphan claims that he did not receive it — the orphan is believed without an oath (Beit Yosef in siman 176 in the name of a responsum of Rashba).}
