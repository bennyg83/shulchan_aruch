Provided that he does not go back and collect it from the borrower. The Taz and later authorities write: “It appears obvious that, all the more so, the borrower may not first give the intermediary a gift as payment for procuring a loan from the lender, whereupon the intermediary gives the recipient of that gift the same amount, less, or more, for this is full ribbit.” With due respect, it is astonishing that they did not examine the matter, for the Rama cites this very law in the gloss below, seif 16. It is explicit in Tosafot at the beginning of Kiddushin, page 6, s.v. de'arvach, and in the Maggid Mishneh, chapter 5 of Hilchot Malveh VeLoveh. It is explained there that this is not full ribbit, but an evasion of ribbit, because the intermediary appears to be the borrower's agent, as the Rama writes, “so that they not come to employ an evasion.” The Taz overlooked all of this and wrote, “It appears obvious,” as though this were a novel ruling reached by his own judgment. See Shaar HaMelech, chapter 5, halachah 1. It appears to me that the intermediary may not personally receive the loan money, because he then appears to be the borrower. This requires study in the Mordechai and Beit Yosef. This is analogous to what the Darkei Moshe and the Rama write in siman 170, seif 2, concerning a non-Jew who borrowed from a Jew, with another Jew guaranteeing only the ribbit. This is permitted, provided that the guarantor does not personally take the money from the lender. If, however, the borrower's collateral is given and the lender relies upon it, it is permitted. This follows all the more from that case. Although agency does not apply to a non-Jew, as stated at the beginning of siman 168, so that the Jew could be viewed as the borrower, it is nevertheless permitted. Certainly here, where agency applies between Jews and the ribbit does not pass directly from borrower to lender, it is permitted. Consider this carefully.
