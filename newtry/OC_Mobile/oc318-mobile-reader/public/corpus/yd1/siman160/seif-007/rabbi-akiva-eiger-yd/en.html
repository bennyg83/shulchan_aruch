(Shach, seif katan 10) And the Baal HaTerumot likewise writes this, as cited by the Beit Yosef. In truth, the Baal HaTerumot appears to hold that it is forbidden even without intent. He writes that if one was accustomed to sending the lender gifts, it is permitted, provided that he does not intend the gift because of the loan. This implies that if he was not accustomed to doing so, it is forbidden even without such intent. The Mishneh LaMelech likewise writes this. This is true only because the Baal HaTerumot follows the Rambam, who holds that even an unspecified gift is forbidden, as the Beit Yosef writes. They disagree only on the following point. According to the Rambam, it is forbidden only where he intends it because of the loan, in accordance with the Taz's explanation in seif katan 3, which is also adopted by the Perishah, Lechem Mishneh, and Mishneh LaMelech. According to the Baal HaTerumot, however, it is evident that even without intent it is forbidden. According to the Taz's explanation in seif katan 12 of the Mechaber's view, it may be that they do not disagree. According to the Rosh and the Tur, however, whose view the Rama adopts as normative, an unspecified gift is forbidden only when the borrower expressly states its connection to the loan. All of this is therefore irrelevant. Even if he intends the gift because of the loan, it is permitted, unless it is an unusually large gift, in which case the circumstances themselves demonstrate its purpose and it is treated as though he expressly stated it. The Shach's words here, which imply that he rules as a matter of halachah that intent alone renders it forbidden, require study.
