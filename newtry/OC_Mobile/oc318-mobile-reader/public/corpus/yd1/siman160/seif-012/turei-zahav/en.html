He shall not say to him: Inform me. This means that by commanding him and having him submit to his instruction, it constitutes verbal ribbis. The Rambam writes, “Know whether a certain person has arrived,” etc., as the Beit Yosef cites. The Beit Yosef is forced to explain that according to this there are two separate matters, and that the reason for “he shall not say” is that it constitutes monetary ribbis, even though the lender does not benefit from the borrower's pocket and did not stipulate, etc. This is difficult to me, for both the Mishnah there and the Rambam bring this law only in connection with verbal ribbis. It further appears to me that one should note why the Rambam specifically says that he should honor and feed him, rather than say that he should give him a certain sum of money, and why he mentions a person from a particular place. All of this appears unnecessary. Therefore, it appears to me that the essential point is that it is certainly normal practice to honor a guest with food, either so that if the host later comes to the guest's place the guest will reciprocate, or because of kindness. Here, however, the lender comes and instructs the borrower to do so. Although the borrower would have done so on his own and there is no monetary ribbis, what basis does the lender have to command him? Clearly, he relies upon the fact that the other is his borrower. It is therefore verbal ribbis, because he commands him concerning matters on account of the loan.
