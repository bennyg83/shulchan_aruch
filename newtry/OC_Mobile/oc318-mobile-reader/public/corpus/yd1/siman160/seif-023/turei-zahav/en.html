The Beit Yosef writes this and derives it from the words of the Rosh, who writes: If a kohen lent money to a Jew on condition that the Jew give him his terumah and maaser, according to the opinion that the benefit of discretion has no monetary value, the payment is not extracted from him. This implies that according to all opinions there is nevertheless a prohibition, but it is fixed ribbis only according to the opinion that the benefit of discretion has monetary value. End quote. Were it not for the holiness of the mouth that said this, I would say that it is entirely permitted. There is clear proof from the Gemara cited in the Shulchan Aruch, siman 172, seif 4: If the lender says to the borrower, “If you sell your land, sell it only to me at its fair value, and on this condition I lend to you,” it is permitted. This is precisely the present case. It appears to me that there is no proof from the Rosh's words cited by the Beit Yosef. There, it is different because the kohen receives the terumah without payment, which is a substantial benefit. It is therefore correct to say that the donor gives him that benefit because of the loan. Here, however, with labor or the sale of land, the benefit does not come to him without payment. The giver therefore grants no benefit relevant to ribbis. One should not object that it must be forbidden because Choshen Mishpat, at the beginning of siman 307, states that a craftsman is considered a paid custodian by virtue of the benefit that the owner gives him the object to repair and thereby allows him to earn the fee. This is not so. Although the lender receives this benefit, it is not considered any loss to the borrower upon which the designation of ribbis could fall. Otherwise, the case in siman 172 would also be forbidden, since an ordinary sale is beneficial to both buyer and seller, as stated below in siman 227, seif 1, regarding an average sale, etc. Rather, it is obvious that ribbis does not apply merely because of such benefit. The same is true in the present ruling. This requires further study.
