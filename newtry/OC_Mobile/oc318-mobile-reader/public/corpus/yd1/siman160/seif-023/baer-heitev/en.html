Work. Shach explains that the lender is a craftsman and the borrower is not. The lender may not condition the loan upon receiving all work that comes to the borrower. If the borrower himself is a craftsman and the loan is conditioned upon performing work cheaply, this is plainly fixed interest. Taz argues that, were it not for the Rosh's authority, he would permit the first case, comparing it to a lender's condition that if the borrower sells land, he sell it to the lender at market value. Nekudot HaKesef distinguishes the cases.
