If a guardian lent the orphans' money at fixed ribbit, and the borrower earned for his own share as much profit as he had undertaken to give them, he must give it to them. The Taz maintains that even if half the profit exceeds the amount that had been fixed, he must give the orphans the entire half that he earned. Since the prohibited stipulation is void, the arrangement is treated as though they had stipulated that the orphans are close to profit and remote from loss, except that the recipient deducts payment for his labor. See there. I do not understand why the Taz requires payment for his labor. With orphans this is unnecessary, because such payment is needed only to avoid avak ribbit, as is explicit in the Rosh in Eizehu Neshech regarding the rule that one pays him as an idle worker. Tosafot, page 70, and the Rambam, chapter 6 of Hilchot Sheluchin VeShutafin, halachah 2, likewise write explicitly that without such payment they come to avak ribbit. See there. The Taz himself writes explicitly in siman 177, and the Be'er Heitev there in seif katan 9, that it is avak ribbit. Since this is so, it is clear that the orphans are not required to pay him for his labor, and none of the poskim who permit rabbinic ribbit with orphans' money ever stated such a requirement. Consider the matter: with an ordinary person, the recipient is liable for half the loss, so half the capital is a deposit. If he receives no payment for his labor, he is working with the depositor's half for the sake of the loaned half, as explained by the poskim and by the Tur and Shulchan Aruch at the beginning of that siman. With orphans, however, all loss rests upon the recipient, so the entire sum is a loan. He is nevertheless permitted to give them half the profit because their money produced that profit, and this is avak ribbit, which is permitted with orphans' funds. Payment for labor therefore changes nothing. See responsa Mayim Amukim of the Re'em, siman 23, who permits lending orphans' money on condition that if the borrower does not repay by a specified date, he will give them an additional sum as a penalty. This is not payment for waiting, but only an evasion of ribbit, and the Sages did not decree against it with orphans' funds, just as an arrangement close to profit and remote from loss is permitted with orphans' money.
