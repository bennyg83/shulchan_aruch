It is forbidden to derive benefit from its coal. It appears to me that this is forbidden even after the fact. The proof is from the end of the chapter Mashilin: Regarding a coal of consecrated property, one who benefits from it is liable for me'ilah; regarding its flame, one may not benefit, but one is not liable for me'ilah. A coal of an idol is forbidden, as Rashi explains from the verse, “Let nothing of the banned property cling to your hand,” while its flame is permitted. The Gemara asks: Why is the flame of an idol completely permitted, as the Baraisa states “permitted,” meaning that the Sages did not decree even rabbinically, whereas regarding the flame of consecrated property the Baraisa states that one may not benefit? It answers that idol worship is repulsive and people distance themselves from it, so the Sages did not decree concerning its flame; consecrated property is not repulsive and people do not distance themselves from it, so the Sages did decree. If the coal itself were forbidden only l'chatchila, it would necessarily be only a rabbinic prohibition. We would then find that the Sages did decree concerning an idol, while the Gemara implies that they did not decree concerning it. Rather, it is clear that there is a Torah prohibition regarding the coal because of “Let nothing cling,” as Rashi explained. Therefore, it is forbidden even after the fact. The Beit Yosef, because of a question in this siman, wrote that a coal is forbidden only l'chatchila, and my father-in-law, of blessed memory, wrote that this is obvious. In my humble opinion, they were not precise at all. The Beit Yosef's question will be explained in seif 6, with Hashem's help, and there is no difficulty.
It is permitted to derive benefit from its flame. This means when only the flame itself is present. If the flame is attached to a coal, it is obvious that it is forbidden to warm oneself from the flame, no less than from the coal itself. This is also proven in the chapter Kol Shaah, Pesachim 27a, from the discussion of a torch opposite it, etc. The flame is permitted because it has no substance. It appears obvious that even dim coals that are no longer glowing are forbidden, since they have substance. There is no distinction between substantial and slight benefit, for the verse states, “Let nothing cling to your hand.”
