(Siman 142 in Taz s.k. 4) He extended in matters without substance — the matter is simple that the beam in the Mishnah means one cause; and what Ramah wrote regarding an idol in bread baked with forbidden wood and a garment woven on an asherah beam — forbidden — meaning this Mishnah of beam and bread is not the halacha, since we establish in chapter "every time" that matter like the one who says zeh v'zeh gorem forbidden; rather this Mishnah regarding idol etc. is the halacha; and garment was mentioned incidentally since it is also in the Mishnah; and so appears from the Tur and mechaber who wrote at first plainly "he took from it a beam" etc. forbidden for benefit; and what he brought from the Rosh first chapter of Chullin did not descend to its depth — there the Rosh does not deal at all with zeh v'zeh gorem law, but comes to say if he slaughtered a dangerously ill animal with a gentile's knife the animal is not forbidden although there is only one cause — the gentile's knife; for if he did not slaughter it, it would die; and nevertheless permitted; not comparable to a new oven that the oven is forbidden — or he baked bread in a forbidden oven — or beam wove the garment — forbidden for everyone and we do not permit because of benefit thus permitted after the fact — there certainly important benefit since oven, bread, and garment all were made and completed in prohibition — not so regarding dangerously ill. Such is the Rosh's intent; but the Rosh there did not come to say we rule halacha that a new oven must be demolished. Also what he distinguished between a garden fertilized with idol manure where planting without them is possible but not so good — is great forcing as appears from his own words. Also the whole sugya of chapter "all images" (Avodah Zarah 49a) does not appear so — see there. Also what he wrote there is clear proof from chapter "every time" (Pesachim 27) "shall we say you heard R. Eliezer that bread is forbidden regarding idolatry because its prohibition is stringent" etc. — wonder, for there the Gemara answers "unless from terumah" etc.; and further it is taught explicitly and so R. Eliezer forbade in all Torah prohibitions; and if so how can decisors rule like the challenger and not like the answerer? And also the two baraitot of orlah peels that the Gemara there in chapter "every time" challenges each other — rather certainly his words have no dawn at all; but in truth the matters are clear — see there in Shach at length where they were explained as he wrapped — see there.
