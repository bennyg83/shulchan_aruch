Burned it. Meaning a Jew; but if a gentile burned it, he nullified it. Taz.
From its flame. The Taz wrote: this means where there is only the flame by itself. But if the flame is attached to a coal, it is obvious that it is more forbidden to warm oneself from the flame than from the coal alone. The permissibility of a flame is because it has no substance. It appears obvious that with a coal, even if it is dim and not glowing, it is forbidden, since there is substance there; and there is no difference between great benefit and small benefit, for it is written, "let none of it cling to your hand." If so, even after the fact it is forbidden. The Beit Yosef wrote that it is forbidden only at the outset, and the Bach wrote about him that this is simple; but in my humble opinion they were not precise in this at all; end quote. So too the Shach wrote in s.k. 10, that even after the fact it is forbidden if one benefited from its coal.
