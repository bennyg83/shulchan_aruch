It is forbidden to sit in its shade. See Baer Heitev and Shev Yaakov, responsa 34 and 35. Certain trees are called by a foreign-language name resembling the asherah mentioned in the Torah. The trees themselves are permitted, and there is no concern that they were worshipped. Certainly, if non-Jews cut down such a tree and sold it to a woodworker for his craft, there is no basis to prohibit it, for there is no greater nullification than this. It may even be used for sacred purposes. Although Orach Chaim prohibits candles formerly used for idolatry from synagogue use, that concerns an item certainly associated with idolatry, not a doubtful case. Nevertheless, one who is stringent because the tree bears a name associated with asherah and is considered repugnant for synagogue use is credited with a mitzvah.
