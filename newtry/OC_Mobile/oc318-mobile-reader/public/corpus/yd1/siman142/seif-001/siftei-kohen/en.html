And its ashes. This is Perishah's language: meaning, for example, that it was an idol of a Jew, which cannot be nullified, or it was of a non-Jew and came into a Jew's hand before the non-Jew nullified it; or one may say it speaks where it burned by itself according to R. Yochanan, who holds that if it broke by itself it is forbidden, as the Tur wrote in siman 146, end quote. What he wrote, for example that it is an idol of a Jew, etc., is not correct, for at the end of Temurah it is explicit that it speaks of that of a non-Jew, and so wrote Tosafot in Perek Kol Shaah, Pesachim 27b, s.v. chutz, etc. What he wrote, or that it is of a non-Jew, etc., means that even according to the one who says an idol that broke by itself is permitted, nevertheless when a Jew burned it, it is worse, for we are concerned lest the Jew lift it and acquire it by lifting and afterward nullify it, so it becomes an idol in a Jew's hand, which is not nullified, as is explicit in the Gemara Perek Kol HaTzelamim, Avodah Zarah 42a. With this is settled what Tosafot in Perek Kol Shaah left in wonder; see there and examine carefully.
