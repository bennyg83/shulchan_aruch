A pot in which he cooked using wood, etc. The Kesef Mishnah explains these words of the Rambam as follows: If he cooked food in a pot and first placed forbidden wood beneath it, and after the food was partially cooked by the forbidden wood he placed permitted wood beneath it and completed the cooking, the food is forbidden. He asks why this should be so, since even if the permitted wood were absent it should be permitted as zeh v'zeh gorem, namely, the permitted pot together with the forbidden wood, similar to a new oven that was cooled. End quote. He then offers another very strained explanation, as he himself acknowledges, and leaves the matter requiring further study. I do not understand the basis of his question. Even in an old oven, the food is forbidden whenever a torch of forbidden wood is opposite it. Here, the forbidden wood is beneath the pot, and the improvement of the wood is present in the pot. Therefore, even according to the authority who permits zeh v'zeh gorem, this case is forbidden, just as bread is forbidden above because the improvement of the wood is present in it, even when baked in an old oven. The Levush understood this seif as discussing a case in which forbidden wood and permitted wood were placed there simultaneously, and nevertheless the food is forbidden because perhaps it was first cooked by the heat of the forbidden wood and became prohibited. He was not precise, for we certainly do not entertain such a concern. The proof is from Pesachim 49a concerning ordinary leaven and terumah leaven that fell into dough, which the Gemara considers zeh v'zeh gorem. Were this concern valid, we should likewise suspect that the terumah leaven acted first. Rather, it is clear that we do not establish such a prohibition. Here the case is one in which the food was certainly cooked first with forbidden wood, as the Kesef Mishnah wrote. The Kesef Mishnah further asks that in the Gemara's first version, which mentions a pot regarding its being fired with forbidden wood, the case parallels the oven explicitly mentioned there. Yet in the second version the Gemara says, “This is not relevant to our master's explanation.” End quote. It appears to me that there is no difficulty. The Rambam also explains the second version like Rashi, that the pot is forbidden because one derives benefit from using it even before cooking, unlike an oven, into which bread is placed only after a second heating. From this the Rambam derived his ruling. Since the second version is stringent because the pot is used cold before cooking begins, while the first version does not regard that as an independent benefit but as part of the later cooking, we learn that regarding an old pot, if the beginning of the cooking was performed with forbidden wood and it was completed with permitted wood, even the first version agrees that the food is forbidden, because part of the actual cooking by fire occurred through the prohibition.
Although this is zeh v'zeh gorem, etc. The Rambam wrote this seif in Hilchos Maachalos Asuros concerning matters such as orlah and mixed vineyard species, where the question “although this is zeh v'zeh gorem” is appropriate. Regarding idol worship, however, he did not mention this law of the pot at all, because, as I wrote shortly, in the case of idol worship zeh v'zeh gorem is truly forbidden according to the Ramah and the Rosh. The Mechaber here follows his own view that there is no distinction between idol worship and other prohibitions. We have already proven that there is a distinction. It appears that this is why the Tur did not cite the laws mentioned by the Mechaber in seifim 6 and 7, although they are explicit in the Gemara. They have no practical relevance here, because regarding idol worship zeh v'zeh gorem is actually forbidden even in the case of an oven, while the Gemara states these laws concerning orlah. This resolves the Beit Yosef's question against the Tur.
