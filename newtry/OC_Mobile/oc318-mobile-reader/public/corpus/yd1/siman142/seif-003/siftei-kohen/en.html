The garment is forbidden for benefit; if it became mixed, etc. It appears whatever was not mixed, casting the value of the benefit ininto the Dead Sea does not help; and so regarding bread in seif 4; and so explained in Beit Yosef and Kesef Mishneh; and not like the Levush in Orach Chaim siman 445 seif 2 who wrote regarding bread baked on chametz coals that one leads benefit of prohibition ininto the Dead Sea and permitted for benefit; end; is incorrect. However it appears proven from the Jerusalem Talmud and several decisors one can sell garment and bread to a non-Jew except prohibition value when the non-Jew cuts garment and bread before the Jew in manner impossible to sell again to another Jew like yayin nesech above siman 134; sell to non-Jew except yayin nesech value; and that casting the value of the benefit ininto the Dead Sea is forbidden is because as long as not mixed and there is prohibition of object of object of idolatryatryatry in garment or bread; no redemption for object of object of idolatryatry; but when sold to non-Jew except prohibition value; no benefit from object of object of idolatryatry and prohibition not in his hand; permitted. And we hold above siman 134 seif katan 11 that certainly yayin nesech mixed in wine does not help selling to non-Jew except yayin nesech value even though certainly Israel will not again buy wine from non-Jew; there different for actual prohibition; but bread and garment actual prohibition not recognizable; and although we say "there is wood benefit in bread"; meaning to say it is more stringent than this-and-that-causes, for prohibition benefit is recognizable; but not that it should be actual mixture; rather forbidden to sell garment and bread wrim in manner he can sell afterwards to Israel; as explained in Orach Chaim siman 467 regarding grain sunk in river forbidden to sell to non-Jew before Pesach much together from benefit; and see end of siman 23, siman 57 seif katan 21, siman 84 seif 5, end of siman 86, siman 112 seif 11, and siman 134 seif 11.
