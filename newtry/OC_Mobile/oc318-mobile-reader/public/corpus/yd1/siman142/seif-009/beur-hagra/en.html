The asherah, whether, etc. This follows the first Tanna there, 48a, “What is an asherah,” etc. The ruling “one may not sit,” etc., refers to it.
Forbidden to sit etc. And even in shade of shade ; Tur; and so in our Gemara text before us; but the Rambam permits shade of shade; and per later version ; from which it is implied in shade of shade permitted and no more; except he explains shade of shade means shade of branches and leaves; but the Tur explains like Rashi ; meaning the shade extending beyond its place; and so the Ran and Shulchan Aruch; and so in Jerusalem Talmud there: they say in the name of R. Chiya: shade forbidden and shade of shade permitted ; which is shade which is shade of shade they say: all that if it falls it touches it ; this is shade; all that if it falls it does not touch it ; this is shade of shade; and the Rif and Rosh omitted all this; and so Shulchan Aruch.
As short as it. Tosafot there, s.v. “If,” etc.
And if not etc. Rambam ; and omitted from what was said in the Mishnah specifically "it robs the public" ; meaning the path is beneath it and included in "there is no other path"; and omitted what we say there specifically regarding an important person ; he did not have the text "if there is no" etc. until the Mishnah; and so the Rif did not have it; but the Raavad explains "it robs the public" ; the path preceded and afterwards the house of idolatry was built ; and as written in this seif.
And specifically to pass, etc. This is evident because the first clause says, “One may not sit,” while the latter clause says, “One may not pass.” See the Ran there.
