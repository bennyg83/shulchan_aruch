It is forbidden to sit in its shade. This is the Rambam's wording. The Gemara states that even the secondary shade is forbidden. Rashi explains that as long as the length of the shadow has not exceeded the height of the tree, the shadow is thick and dark and is called its primary shade. Beyond that point, the shadow is thin and faint and is called its secondary shade. So too the Tur rules. The Ran and the Kesef Mishnah question why the Rambam did not forbid the secondary shade. As a matter of practical halacha, it is certainly forbidden, for this is explicit in the Gemara.
But to pass through its shade, etc. Even sitting there is not intrinsically forbidden by strict law when one does not intend to derive benefit. The concern is only that he may linger while sitting and come to intend to benefit. Mere passage is therefore permitted. Passing directly beneath it, however, is forbidden because of the ritual impurity of idols.
