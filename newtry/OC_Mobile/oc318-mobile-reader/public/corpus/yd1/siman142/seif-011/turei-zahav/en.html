Therefore, a field that was fertilized, etc. This implies that it is permitted specifically after the fact, but not l'chatchila. The same applies to an animal that was fattened, etc. The rule that zeh v'zeh gorem is permitted applies specifically after the fact. Although the beginning of the seif states that it is permitted to plant, which implies even l'chatchila, that case is different because at the time of planting there is no present benefit; the benefit comes later on its own. So wrote the Beit Yosef in the name of the Ran. Above in seif 3, I explained the distinction between the zeh v'zeh gorem in this case, which is permitted according to the Ramah as cited by the Tur, and the zeh v'zeh gorem discussed above, which the Ramah forbids.
And an animal that was fattened, etc. See what the Rama writes at the end of the siman, distinguishing a case in which it was fattened with forbidden food throughout its entire life, in which case it is forbidden.
