See Sha'ar HaMelech, chapter 6 of the Laws of Foreign Worship, who raises a further question on the Mechaber. The Mechaber holds that a coal of foreign worship is prohibited only by rabbinic law and only ab initio. If so, what is the Talmud's question there: "Why is a flame of foreign worship permitted, whereas a flame of consecrated property is forbidden?" What difficulty is there? One can answer that since a coal of foreign worship is prohibited only by rabbinic law, the Sages did not decree against its flame because of its coal, as that would constitute a decree enacted to safeguard another decree, whereas with consecrated property, etc. He therefore concludes that even the Mechaber spoke specifically of a coal produced from wood of foreign worship or from the wood of an asherah, as his precise language here in Shulchan Aruch indicates. His statement in Beit Yosef likewise concerns such a case. If, however, the coal itself became prohibited, such as when one made it an offering to foreign worship, the Mechaber certainly agrees that it is prohibited according to the Torah. The Talmud's question is therefore well founded. See there.
