And if the animal was dangerously ill — it is forbidden etc. From this language it appears all the animal is forbidden and leading benefit of knife payment to slaughter it to the Dead Sea does not help; and so explained in Kesef Mishneh chapter 7 Laws of Avodah Zarah on the Rambam's language (which is the mechaber's language): and this is his language — and it appears our Rabbi's opinion is to forbid all the animal; and he followed his reasoning in "he took a beam from asherah and wove the garment in it" — the whole garment forbidden for benefit — end; and therefore meaning the Rambam's opinion is even leading benefit of knife payment to the Dead Sea does not help, like taking a beam; and as will be explained in seif 3. And although he wrote there that according to the Rambam the Rashba and Ran wrote if he cut meat with it the meat is forbidden, for he benefited from idolatry; however leading benefit to the Dead Sea suffices — end their language — meaning certainly regarding meat, because the meat was cut with an idol knife the whole meat was not made in prohibition; it appears leading knife benefit to the Dead Sea suffices; but regarding dangerously ill, since perhaps it would have died if not slaughtered and there is great benefit here — therefore the whole animal is forbidden; and this distinction is mentioned in the Rosh; and this is why the Rambam and mechaber changed their language in the end and wrote "and likewise forbidden to cut meat with it" and did not also write "and if he cut meat with it the meat is forbidden" as in the beginning — for otherwise it would imply leading benefit to the Dead Sea does not help as in the beginning; but nevertheless leading benefit to the Dead Sea is also required regarding meat, as explained in Beit Yosef and Kesef Mishneh who wrote from the conclusion "and if he cut" in a destructive manner permitted — it appears all that is not destructive manner forbidden — end; and see siman 10 s.k. 5 what he further wrote on this.
