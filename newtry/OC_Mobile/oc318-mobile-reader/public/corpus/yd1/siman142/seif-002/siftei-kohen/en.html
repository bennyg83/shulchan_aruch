He slaughtered with it etc. Deals with an old knife not kashered from its prohibition; therefore although from appurtenances of idolatry one may slaughter ab initio since he is damaging it, nevertheless from [being] absorbed-from-idolaters it is forbidden to slaughter ab initio, as above seif 10; and as the Rav concludes.
Because he damages. This is not benefit — in its lifetime it is designated for three things: breeding and birth, plowing, and eating — end Rashi.
And if the knife is new. Such as where he cut a piece of wood for an idol — for otherwise appurtenances of idolatry are not forbidden until they are used, as explained at beginning of siman 139; and so it is in Chullin 8b.
If the animal was dangerously ill, it is forbidden, etc. From this language it appears all the animal is forbidden and casting the value of the benefit of knife payment to slaughter it ininto the Dead Sea does not help; and so explained in Kesef Mishneh chapter 7 Laws of Avodah Zarah on the Rambam's language (which is the mechaber's language): and this is his language; and it appears our Rabbi's opinion is to forbid all the animal; and he followed his reasoning in "he took a shuttle from asherah and wove the garment in it"; the wrim garment forbidden for benefit; end; and therefore meaning the Rambam's opinion is even casting the value of the benefit of knife payment ininto the Dead Sea does not help, like taking a shuttle; and as will be explained in seif 3. And although he wrote there that according to the Rambam the Rashba and Ran wrote if he cut meat with it the meat is forbidden, for he benefited from object of object of idolatryatryatry; however casting the value of the benefit ininto the Dead Sea suffices; end their language; meaning certainly regarding meat, because the meat was cut with an knife of object of object of idolatryatryatry the wrim meat was not made in prohibition; it appears leading knife benefit ininto the Dead Sea suffices; but regarding dangerously ill, since perhaps it would have died if not slaughtered and there is great benefit here; therefore the wrim animal is forbidden; and this distinction is mentioned in the Rosh; and this is why the Rambam and mechaber changed their language in the end and wrote "and likewise forbidden to cut meat with it" and did not also write "and if he cut meat with it the meat is forbidden" as in the beginning; for otherwise it would imply casting the value of the benefit ininto the Dead Sea does not help as in the beginning; but nevertheless casting the value of the benefit ininto the Dead Sea is also required regarding meat, as explained in Beit Yosef and Kesef Mishneh who wrote from the conclusion "and if he cut" in a destructive manner permitted; it appears all that is not destructive manner forbidden; end; and see siman 10 seif katan 5 what he further wrote on this.
A way of loss and destruction. Such as when the pieces are destined as gifts and similar.
