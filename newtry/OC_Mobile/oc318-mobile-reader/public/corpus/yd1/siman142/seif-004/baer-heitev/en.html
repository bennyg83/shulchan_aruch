Shach wrote: it appears it refers even to a new oven; and so from what he wrote in s.k. 6 regarding cooking on coals etc. — appears new oven too; but it does not appear to me: although the Rambam did not distinguish between new and old — that is regarding orlah whose coals and ash are permitted; but regarding idol wood whose coals and ash are forbidden — how can it be permitted cooking on coals in a new oven? Behold the whole thing causes prohibition and forbidden wood has not yet gone away. And in truth the Beit Yosef goes according to his reasoning who holds coals of an idol are forbidden only ab initio; but in my eyes it is wondrous how one can say so from several proofs in Gemara and Tosafot and decisors — see there where he extended; and his conclusion not like the mechaber, but coals of idolatry even after the fact forbidden; and so Turei Zahav — even in an old oven, scrape all fire from it and cook or bake in oven heat forbidden even after the fact. And Shach further wrote wonder on the mechaber who in Orach Chaim siman 445 ruled regarding chametz that if he cooked or baked — that bread and dish forbidden for benefit, and likewise its charcoal forbidden for benefit; and it appears there in Beit Yosef that even if he cooked on dim coals and ash of chametz the bread and dish forbidden; and here he wrote cooking on coals that even if they are burning permitted — investigate — end.
