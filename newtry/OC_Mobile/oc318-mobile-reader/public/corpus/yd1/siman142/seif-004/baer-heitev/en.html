He shall carry it [yolik]. The language of the Shach implies that anything that was not mixed in [with permitted food] does not benefit from carrying the benefit to the Dead Sea. However, I have already written that even without mixture there is a permit to sell to a non-Jew when he cuts it, etc. (as written above, s.k. 6, see there). And what it says — that all the remaining loaves are permitted — implies even for eating; and it is not comparable to yayin nesech which is forbidden for drinking, which is prohibited on account of a non-Jew's touch; but bread which was baked with a forbidden item is only forbidden for benefit — and since he carried [the benefit] to the Dead Sea, he no longer benefits from the non-Jew.
Permitted. The Shach writes that this implies it applies even to a new oven, and similarly what is written in seif 6, cooking upon coals, etc., implies that it also applies to a new oven. But this does not appear correct to me. Although the Rambam did not distinguish between new and old, that is regarding orlah, whose coals and ashes are permitted. But with wood of an idol, whose coals and ashes are forbidden, how could it be permitted if one cooked on coals in a new oven? The entire cause is forbidden, and the prohibited wood has not yet disappeared. In truth, the Beit Yosef follows his own view, that a coal of an idol is forbidden only lechatchilah. But in my eyes this is astonishing, for it is impossible to say so because of several proofs from the Shas, Tosafot, and the poskim; see there at length. His conclusion is unlike the Mechaber: a coal of avodah zarah is forbidden even bedieved. So too the Taz rules, that even in an old oven, if one swept out all the fire and cooked or baked with the heat of the oven, it is forbidden even bedieved. The Shach further writes that here too one must wonder at the Mechaber, for in Orach Chayyim siman 445 he ruled regarding chametz that if one cooked or baked with it, that bread and dish are forbidden in benefit, and its charcoal is forbidden in benefit. It is implied there in the Beit Yosef that even if one cooked with dim coals and ash of chametz, the bread and dish are forbidden; yet here he writes that if one cooked upon coals, even burning ones, they are permitted. This requires study.
