If he took from it wood, it is forbidden in benefit. If he fired an oven with it — whether new or old — he should let it cool, and afterwards heat it with permitted wood. And if he did not let it cool and baked bread in it (while the flame is opposite it) (Beit Yosef), behold it is forbidden in benefit, for there is “improvement of the forbidden wood” in the bread (Tur). If it became mixed with others, he should take the value of that bread to the Dead Sea, and all the other loaves are permitted. If he raked out all the fire and afterwards cooked or baked with the heat of the oven, it is permitted, for the forbidden wood is gone. {Rama: That we say there is “improvement of the wood” in the bread is regarding something forbidden in benefit, but if he baked or cooked near something forbidden for eating, even if the flame is opposite it, it is permitted (Mordechai, perek HaSocher, and Tosafos, and so too it appears).}
