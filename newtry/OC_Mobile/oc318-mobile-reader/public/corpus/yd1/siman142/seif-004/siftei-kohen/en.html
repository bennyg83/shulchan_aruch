He heated the oven with them etc. Until end of seif 6: in truth the words of the Tur, mechaber, and other poskim need a craftsman to reconcile them — therefore I am compelled to copy the language of the Gemara and the poskim.
The law of an oven heated with prohibited fuel. We learn in chapter "every time" (Pesachim 26b): oven they heated with orlah peels or vineyard kilayim stalks; a new one must be demolished, old allowed to cool; he baked bread in it (in that heating); Rabbi says bread forbidden, Sages say bread permitted; cooked on coals; on "bread permitted" (and halacha established like Rabbi); and it is taught whether new or old allowed to cool; no difficulty: that which teaches "must be demolished" is R. Eliezer who says zeh v'zeh gorem forbidden; and that which teaches "allowed to cool" is Rabbis who say zeh v'zeh gorem permitted; meaning therefore although new oven was forbidden, when allowed to cool and heated with permitted wood it will be zeh v'zeh gorem. Tosafot, Rosh, and Ran wrote: although this-and-that-causes forbidden ab initio, as proven chapter "all images"; and from benefit [old] allowed to cool although this-and-that-causes, because ab initio nevertheless forbidden here since it has no remedy except demolition it is considered after the fact; but in any case whether new or old allowed to cool ab initio, since there is only minor loss of wood value; and in cooking on coals Shmuel and Rabbi Yochanan dispute; one says only dim but glowing forbidden; and one says even glowing permitted (and proven there even for the one who says zeh v'zeh gorem permitted they dispute; and so proven in Rif and Rosh; see there); and that Rabbi said bread forbidden is when flame opposite it; as long as bread was in oven the flame burned opposite it and he benefited from prohibition while prohibition still existed in kind; and the Ran wrote the Rif's opinion is even in glowing permitted; and the Rosh forbids in glowing; end Gemara and decisors with slight explanation of mine; and behold the Tur wrote: nevertheless if he baked in it (meaning in old not cooled) bread permitted (zeh v'zeh gorem permitted); and although zeh v'zeh gorem permitted, if flame opposite it bread forbidden; and Ramah wrote even for the one who says zeh v'zeh gorem permitted, here regarding object of object of idolatryatry in bread baked with forbidden wood forbidden; and so appears from R. Avraham Rosh's words whom the Mishnah brought for halacha; end; and Beit Yosef wrote: I do not know Ramah's reason here, for garden fertilized with object of object of idolatryatry manure we permit in chapter "all images" as permitted (and as below end of seif 11); also wonder on our Rabbi; if because he brought the Mishnah for halacha he was so precise; also on the Rif he could write so; and in truth it appears between Rif and Rosh no necessity to say they brought it only because it concludes "it became mixed" etc. to teach where he baked in new not cooled (and it appears we can also say where he baked in old with flame opposite, as above) forbidden for benefit; and they relied on what he wrote before regarding garden fertilized with object of object of idolatryatry manure; halacha like the one who says zeh v'zeh gorem permitted; end.
I will now explain the Tur's ruling. And now I will clarify: aside from this, one should wonder at the Tur who wrote "and if flame opposite it the bread is forbidden"; implying he rules like the one who says even glowing coals permitted, contrary to the Rosh’s position, as proven explicitly in the Rosh that even for the one who says zeh v’zeh gorem permitted, he ruled so; see there; because there is wood benefit in bread when the coals are glowing; and so he wrote in the glosses there: the halacha is zeh v’zeh gorem permitted etc., and the halacha is there is wood benefit in bread when the coals are glowing, to forbid bread baked with forbidden wood; end; and so he wrote in Tur Orach Chaim siman 445 explicitly. And further because here regarding an object of object of idolatryatry, even with dim coals it is forbidden even for the one who says zeh v’zeh gorem permitted, because the Rosh wrote there that what we permit with dim coals according to all is specifically regarding orlah peels and kilayim stalks since they are subject to burning and we rule all that is burned its ash is permitted; but regarding chametz we rule like the Rabbis who say crumble and scatter to the wind, which is in the category of all that is buried, whose digging is forbidden; and all the more with glowing coals; Rabbenu Yonah; end; and if so regarding an object of object of idolatryatry too, where we rule grind and scatter to the wind as the Mechaber wrote in siman 147, and its ash too is forbidden as the Tur wrote in seif 1 of this siman; if so even dim coals would be forbidden, for whenever the coals are forbidden there is wood benefit in bread and forbidden even for one who says zeh v’zeh gorem permitted. And so in the glosses there: but with dim coals permitted; this applies only to orlah wood and kilayim stalks; but chametz on Pesach even its ash is forbidden; end; and so in Orach Chaim siman 445: if he cooked or baked with it; that bread and dish forbidden; and if it is permissible; for the Rabbis who say ash is permitted; a dish cooked and bread baked are not forbidden unless the bread was baked and dish cooked while the chametz body still existed or the coals were glowing; but if they were extinguished, permitted. And according to the Rabbis that its ash is forbidden; in every case forbidden; end.
The proaccording to resolution of the Tur's ruling. And it appears to me to settle all this: the Tur wrote initially "and if flame opposite it forbidden" as stated in Gemara; to say since flame opposite there is wood benefit in bread and forbidden, even for the one who says zeh v'zeh gorem permitted; and he comes only to say wherever there is wood benefit in bread forbidden even for the one who says zeh v'zeh gorem permitted. And afterwards he wrote Ramah wrote even for the one who says zeh v'zeh gorem permitted here regarding object of object of idolatryatry in bread baked with forbidden wood forbidden; because regarding object of object of idolatryatry even ash forbidden; if so whatever baked with forbidden wood has ash benefit in bread and forbidden even for the one who says zeh v'zeh gorem permitted; and even baked in heat of oven baked with forbidden wood allowed to cool from oven and no coals and ash remained forbidden because oven heat forbids more than dim coals, as proven explicitly in chapter "how one roasts" (Pesachim 75a) where we learn there oven heated with orlah peels and allowed to cool and baked bread in it; according to the forbidders (Rashi: according to forbidders in chapter "every time" Rabbi says bread forbidden); what is it? And plainly there for the permitters it appears explicitly even for the one who says cooking on coals permitted "from dim" all the more because reasonable oven heat from flame is better than dim coals; and so Tosafot there explicitly: R. Yitzchak answered coals are different; their heat not from flame but from themselves and merely dust; but oven heat allowed to cool from flame heat of flame that is complete prohibition; therefore it is a question; end; and if so although there plainly for permitters; because not worse than coals; but regarding object of object of idolatryatry where even ash forbidden plainly forbidden even in oven heat. And this Ramah wrote thus regarding object of object of idolatryatry bread baked with forbidden wood; meaning in every manner baked with forbidden wood forbidden; but certainly Ramah admits where he heated with wood of object of object of idolatryatryatry a new oven; permitted to bake again when allowed to cool, for there is neither ash nor heat of forbidden wood, only the oven is forbidden and we rule zeh v'zeh gorem permitted; and therefore Ramah was precise and wrote "bread baked with forbidden wood" forbidden; and Beit Yosef understood Ramah holds new oven must be demolished and forbidden to bake in it; and this certainly is incorrect; rather Ramah's and Rosh's opinion as I explained; and now what the Tur wrote and so appears from R. Avraham Rosh's words who brought the Mishnah for halacha; meaning he brought the Mishnah "he baked bread in it forbidden" in chapter "all images"; and should have explained with flame opposite; or he relied on what he said in chapter "every time" that wherever ash forbidden even dim coals forbidden, all the more oven heat; and as I wrote; and conversely in every manner he baked bread in that heating forbidden; and on the contrary wonder on the Tur why he was not precise so plainly from the Rosh's words in chapter "every time"; rather one can say he was not pleased to be so precise because he wrote at the end R. Yona's words "and it is possible to tell him" he does not believe; but since in chapter "all images" he brought the Mishnah plainly for halacha and in chapter "every time" brought R. Yona; the matters appear he holds thus according to Ramah; and as I wrote; and now the mechaber's words are fitting; and Beit Yosef's wonder is settled with necessity that Ramah's and Rosh's opinion as I explained from the Rosh first chapter of Chullin whom he hinted; and so R. Yerucham's opinion. And the Rambam's opinion also appears like Ramah and Rosh according to how I explained their opinion; he wrote chapter 7 Laws of Avodah Zarah: he took forbidden wood from it, heated oven with them, allowed to cool, then heated with other permitted wood and baked in it; he baked bread in it and did not cool; bread forbidden for benefit; end; and since he wrote "allowed to cool" plainly appears even in new he speaks and meaning like the one who says zeh v'zeh gorem permitted; and so he ruled in several places zeh v'zeh gorem permitted; and if so why was he silent and wrote "he baked bread in it" and did not write "bread forbidden for benefit"; appears whether new or old; and in old why; behold zeh v'zeh gorem; and so Beit Yosef challenged. And further in chapter 16 Laws of Forbidden Foods regarding oven heated with orlah peels he wrote if he allowed to cool all fire and then cooked in its heat, and likewise if he cooked on coals permitted; and did not distinguish new and old; and appears there even in new permitted forbidden wood went away; and so explained in Beit Yosef and Kesef Mishneh there; and if so wonder why he forbade in chapter 7 Laws of Avodah Zarah plainly even in new; he should have permitted in allowed to cool oven or cooking on coals; or his opinion like Ramah regarding object of object of idolatryatry since ash forbidden all the more oven heat; if so in every manner bread baked with wood of object of object of idolatryatryatry forbidden; but new oven heated with wood of object of object of idolatryatryatry cooled and baked with permitted wood; bread permitted since not baked with forbidden wood and no even object of object of idolatryatry ash, only baked in oven that is forbidden and we rule zeh v'zeh gorem permitted; and Beit Yosef wrote the Rambam deals in chapter 7 Laws of Avodah Zarah when flame opposite; and difficulty; if so he should not have been silent; and from Beit Yosef's own words it appears this explanation is forced in his eyes; see there; rather the clear as I wrote; and this is the Rambam's language in chapter 3 Laws of Chametz and Matzah: if they cooked chametz in it or baked bread in it; that bread and dish forbidden for benefit, and likewise its charcoal forbidden; end; because since ash forbidden, in every manner bread baked in it forbidden; and as I wrote; and this is why he did not distinguish flame opposite or not (I again later found in Tosefta first chapter of Orlah: oven heated with orlah peels; if a new one must be demolished, if old allowed to cool; baked and cooked on coals permitted in all forbidden pleasures mentioned there except asherah wood; end; from this clear proof for what I wrote).
The Mechaber's view. And now I will explain the mechaber's opinion: what he wrote "he heated with them the oven whether new or old allowed to cool" etc.; meaning because zeh v'zeh gorem permitted; and although ab initio zeh v'zeh gorem forbidden as will be explained; and from benefit old allowed to cool because ab initio nevertheless zeh v'zeh gorem forbidden; nevertheless an oven is considered after the fact since it needed demolition; and nevertheless whether new or old allowed to cool ab initio, since there is only minor loss of wood value; and in putting bread there first ab initio and then heating forbidden.
And what he wrote "and if he did not cool it and he baked bread in it, it is forbidden" — since he was silent it implies even in old he deals with; and therefore necessarily when flame was opposite it, as the Rav explained — for if cooked on coals or in oven heat he wrote nearby that permitted; and he follows his reasoning who explained the Rambam's words so; but per my humble opinion even in oven heat or cooked on coals the Rambam forbade here regarding idol, and specifically regarding orlah where ash and coals are permitted he distinguished so — which is not the case with an idol, as I wrote above.
And what he wrote “it became mixed with others, he shall lead payment for that bread to the Dead Sea” — implies wherever it was not mixed leading benefit to the Dead Sea does not help; however I already wrote in s.k. 8 that even without mixing one may sell the bread to a gentile when he cuts it before the Israelite — see there.
And the rest of all the loaves are permitted — even for eating; and not like yayin nesech which is forbidden to drink since it was forbidden because of a gentile's touch; but bread, because it was baked with something forbidden for eating, is forbidden only for benefit; and since he led benefit to the Dead Sea, he no longer benefits from the gentile's action — thus wrote R. Yonah in the name of the French rabbis; and it is the Rosh's and Tur's opinion — see there.
He removed all the fire, etc. And what he wrote "he allowed to cool all the fire" etc.; appears it refers even to new oven; and so from what he wrote in seif 6 "cooked on coals of wood of object of object of idolatryatryatry" etc.; appears new too; and so Beit Yosef and Kesef Mishneh; since the Rambam did not distinguish new and old, appears even in new; and I did not descend to the end of his opinion: although the Rambam in chapter 17 Laws of Forbidden Foods did not distinguish new and old; that is regarding orlah whose coals and ash permitted; but regarding wood of object of object of idolatryatryatry whose coals and ash forbidden; how permitted cooking on coals in new oven? Behold wrim thing causes prohibition and forbidden wood not yet gone; and so explained in Beit Yosef; but regarding wood of object of object of idolatryatryatry whose coals and ash forbidden; how permitted in new oven? Behold wrim causes prohibition. And in truth Beit Yosef goes according to his reasoning who wrote coals of object of object of idolatryatry forbidden only ab initio; but in my eyes wondrous how one can say so from several proofs; see there where he extended; conclusion not like mechaber but coals of object of object of idolatryatry even after the fact forbidden; and so Turei Zahav; even in old oven scrape all fire and cook or bake in oven heat forbidden even after the fact. And Shach further wrote wonder on mechaber who in Orach Chaim siman 445 copied Rambam's language if he cooked or baked; that bread and dish forbidden and charcoal forbidden; and appears there in Tur and Beit Yosef in explanation of this Rambam that even if he cooked on dim coals and chametz ash the bread and dish forbidden; and here mechaber wrote cooking on coals although burning permitted; and so wonder on Magen Avraham who wrote here like mechaber's words and wrote coals of object of object of idolatryatry permitted after the fact; end.
