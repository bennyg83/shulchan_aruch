I already wrote per my humble opinion forbidden even without this; and so what Shulchan Aruch wrote nearby "scraped" etc. — this is not regarding idolatry but orlah and other prohibitions; and it turns out the Rambam holds like the Rosh except in one matter he disputes: he wrote regarding idolatry "heated oven scraped" — appears even in new completed in prohibition scraping with permitted heating helps; not as Rosh and Ramah wrote nearby in Rosh's name; and Ramah's reason appears: regarding oven completed in prohibition with no further need for any repair — unlike bread baked with forbidden wood and beam; not so new oven completed with forbidden wood — nevertheless man does not benefit from it until second act bakes bread with permitted wood.
He scraped all fire etc. It appears it refers even to new oven and permitted for zeh v'zeh gorems; and Beit Yosef challenged: behold new oven completed in prohibition and if he bakes in that heat no permitted cause — not zeh v'zeh gorems unless he heats second time with permitted; and so Rashi explicitly chapter 25 49a. And he answered: the Rambam is master of this law and holds even in new oven first time is zeh v'zeh gorems since oven itself not forbidden but only completed with forbidden wood and they went away; and one can wonder: what of bowls and cups seif 5 forbidden for benefit; and difficulty on pot seif 7 — therefore must be the Rambam did not permit in scraping except old oven; and do not wonder he was not careful to explain this since he relied on what he said afterwards bowls cups pots etc. as Shulchan Aruch wrote seif 5; and pot means oven; and all this Rambam wrote regarding orlah where zeh v'zeh gorems permitted in oven; but regarding idolatry as we said Rambam inclined to be stringent in zeh v'zeh gorems — oven and even scraping even in old oven forbidden from zeh v'zeh gorems; and so Tur wrote "and if he baked bread in it" refers to old; and therefore Tur wrote permitted for the one who says zeh v'zeh gorems permitted; for in first view no distinction between idolatry and orlah.
