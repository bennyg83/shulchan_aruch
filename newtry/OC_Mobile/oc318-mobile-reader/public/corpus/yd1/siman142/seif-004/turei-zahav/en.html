While the torch is opposite it. I have already written that, in my humble opinion, it is forbidden even without this. Likewise, what the Shulchan Aruch writes shortly, “he removed all the fire,” etc., does not apply to idol worship, but rather to orlah and other prohibitions. It follows that the Rambam agrees with the Rosh. Nevertheless, it appears that they disagree in one matter. The Rambam writes concerning idol worship that if one heated an oven with it, the oven should be cooled. This implies that even if it was a new oven completed through the forbidden wood, heating it afterwards with permitted wood is effective, unlike what I wrote shortly in the name of the Rosh and the Ramah. The Rambam's reasoning appears to be that the concept of an object being completed through the prohibition applies only when no further act of rectification is needed, as with bread baked using forbidden wood and a garment woven with a forbidden shuttle. A new oven, however, though completed with forbidden wood, does not provide benefit to a person until a second act occurs, namely baking bread in it with permitted wood.
He removed all the fire, etc. This wording implies that it applies even to a new oven and that it is permitted because it is zeh v'zeh gorem. The Beit Yosef asks that a new oven was completed through the prohibition, and if one bakes using that original heat there is no permitted cause at all. It is zeh v'zeh gorem only if one subsequently heats it a second time with permitted wood, as Rashi explicitly explains in the chapter Kol HaTzelamim, 49a. He answers that the Rambam, who is the source of this law, holds that even the first use of a new oven is zeh v'zeh gorem, since the oven itself is not forbidden; it was merely completed through forbidden wood, which is now gone. This is surprising, for how is it different from bowls and cups in seif 5, which are forbidden for benefit? The same difficulty applies from the pot in seif 7. Necessarily, the Rambam permitted the case of removing the fire only in an old oven. One should not wonder why he did not state this explicitly, for he relied on what he writes afterwards concerning bowls, cups, and pots, as the Shulchan Aruch records in seif 5. A pot there means an oven. All this was written by the Rambam regarding orlah, where zeh v'zeh gorem is permitted in the case of an oven. Regarding idol worship, however, we have already explained that the Rambam rules stringently concerning zeh v'zeh gorem. Thus, even if one removes the fire from an old oven, it is forbidden because of zeh v'zeh gorem. Similarly, the Tur's statement, “if he baked bread in it,” refers to an old oven. He therefore writes that it is permitted according to the authority who permits zeh v'zeh gorem, since according to the first opinion there is no distinction between idol worship and orlah.
