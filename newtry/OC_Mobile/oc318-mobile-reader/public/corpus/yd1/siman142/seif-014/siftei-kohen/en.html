That has idols. In the Tur and Rashi: that an idol stands in its courtyard.
Because it was made for beauty and not to worship it, as it says, their gods, etc. The wording is unclear, for what connection does the verse have to what he wrote, that it was made for beauty? Even if it had been made to worship it, it would be permitted because of the verse. It appears to me one should emend: "and furthermore, as it says," etc. So it is in the Gemara: we do not say that the bathhouse is made as beauty for the idol; rather, it is made as beauty for the bathhouse. Another explanation: this one stands over the sewer and everyone urinates before it; the verse says "their gods" only when they treat it with the practice of deity, etc. Perhaps this is what he means: who proves that it was made for beauty and not to worship it? Since it says, etc. So it appears Ateret Zekenim understood, for he wrote that it was made for beauty and not to worship it, since they disgrace it and the Torah said "their gods," etc. According to this, one must explain the Gemara as follows: it is made as beauty for the bathhouse, because it is not the way to make a bathhouse as beauty for it, since a bathhouse is not an object of beauty, as Rashi explained there; therefore, necessarily it is made as beauty for the bathhouse. Another explanation: it is made as beauty for the bathhouse, for this one stands over the sewer, etc. This is forced.
