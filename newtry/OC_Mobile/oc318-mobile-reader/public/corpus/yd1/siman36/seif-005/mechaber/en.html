If it is found punctured in a place where there is [room] to attribute [the puncture] that it was made after slaughter — such as if the slaughterer passed his hand forcefully, or tore it forcefully, or a wolf took it and returned it punctured — we attribute to be lenient and it is kosher; and even if there are many punctures not at the place of its teeth we attribute them all to the wolf. {Rama: And we do not attribute to the slaughterer's hand unless the puncture is elongated, for it is customary to be from force of the slaughterer; but if it is round (Beit Yosef in the name of Maharil and Mahari Weil) we do not attribute to the slaughterer, for its way is not to be round; and any puncture that is open (Hagahot Mordechai ch. 1 of Chullin) or that blackened, or red around the puncture (Beit Yosef in the name of Maggid Mishneh) — and even if it is only red around the ribs opposite the puncture — treifah, and we do not attribute for any matter, for certainly it was from [while] alive (there).} And likewise if worms are found on it that punctured it and it is not known whether they punctured before slaughter or after slaughter — kosher; and if there is no place to attribute and the matter is doubtful whether it was punctured while alive — they make another puncture and compare them; if they are similar it is kosher, for just as this one was made after slaughter so was the first; and if they are not similar — treifah. And one does not compare a lung of a thin animal to a lung of a thick animal, but thin to thin and thick to thick (Ri"f, Rambam, Rashba, Tosafot, and Hagahot Maimoniyot in the name of R' Chananel). {Rama: And some say one does not compare from this animal to another animal (Rashi, BeHag, Rosh, Tur, Agudah, Semag, Ran, and R' Yerucham brought Rashi's view at the end); and even in one animal one only compares thick to thick — meaning from lobe to lobe — or thin to thin — meaning from segment to segment; however nowadays we are not expert and one should not rely on comparison at all to declare kosher (Kol Bo in the name of the Geonim, and Maharil and other acharonim).}
