That the slaughterer passed his hand forcefully. Tur's language: such as the slaughterer passed his hand or tore forcefully; and the Mechaber goes to their reasons, for Beit Yosef wrote the word "forcefully" applies also to passing the hand; and so is implied in poskim; and Rabbenu Yerucham wrote likewise a non-Jew — brought in Beit Yosef; and Bach disagreed and wrote we do not attribute to a non-Jew; and so in examinations of acharonim; and so in responsum of Maharit part 1 siman 37 — see there.
Or a wolf took it. Or a dog and the like; and so in poskim; and Rabbenu Yerucham netsiv 6 ch. 3 wrote likewise a non-Jew; brought in Beit Yosef; and Bach disagreed; and so in examinations; and responsum Maharit — see there.
We attribute them all to the wolf. Bach wrote the reason: we say it bit and returned and bit — and it appears proven from the Talmud Piskei Tosafot daf 49 and novellae and poskim that wherever there is room to attribute we attribute even without resemblance; and so in examinations of acharonim.
And any puncture that is open. And hakafah does not help (even where they rely on hakafah) — so in Hagahot Mordechai there; and so Maharshal; and so is implied from what Rama wrote at end: open puncture, etc.; and so in examinations.
Or red, etc. And written in examinations and brought by the R"tz kabbalah on punctures: if you find a red puncture soft around it — certainly a new puncture and we attribute to slaughterer's hand; and so in examinations of acharonim.
And likewise if worms are found, etc. From Taz's language it appears at first glance there is at least a doubt, only we attribute to be lenient that after slaughter it separated; but Rambam ch. 6 and all in him and Hagahot Maimoniyot and Semag and Ran and Rashba and Tur and Beit Yosef and Bach and Maharshal and Rama and other acharonim wrote worms on lung — kosher; and so is implied in Talmud and Rashi and Tosafot and poskim — see there.
Worms, etc. Maharshal Piskei Tosafot siman 91 ruled: specifically when the worm is found inside the puncture or on the puncture proving the puncture came from the worm that punctured; and one must find the worm on the lung too even if recognizable as from liver worms — see responsum Chavot Shalom siman 44; and so in examinations.
That punctured it, etc. Bach wrote: specifically regarding lung we say thus; but in stomach and fowl and the like, if we see they are punctured, since a flaw arose we are concerned perhaps before slaughter — unlike lung where we attribute after slaughter.
Kosher. So wrote Darkei Moshe; and in hagahah Ohel Moed (general rule 51 ot 2): however Rabbenu Gershom Meor HaGolah required placing the lung in sun until it warms if many exit — see there; and what Rama wrote nowadays we are not expert — in Sefer Api Ravrevi wrote where there is still some side to permit one may be lenient.
