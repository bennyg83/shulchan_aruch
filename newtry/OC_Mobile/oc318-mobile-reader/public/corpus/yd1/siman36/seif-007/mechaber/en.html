A lung that was poured out like a pitcher and its upper membrane remains intact without puncture — if the bronchi stand in their place and did not melt — kosher; and if even one bronchus melted — treifah. How is this done? They puncture it and pour it into a vessel that is deep in the limb; if white threads are seen in it, it is known the bronchi melted and it is treifah; and if not, only the flesh of the lung melted and it is kosher — provided the appearance of the water is clear; but if they are murky or putrid — treifah. {Rama: And some permit even if murky or putrid (Rosh, Rashba, Razah, Ran, Tur, Sefer HaTerumah, Semag, Hagahot Maimoniyot, and Kol Bo siman 101, and above siman 37); and one who is expert to examine that the bronchi did not melt may rely on them in case of great loss.}
