If a thorn was found in the esophagus along its length or its width and it is not stuck in it — kosher (if there is no drop of blood on it from outside) (Tur and Hagahot Maimoniyot); but if it is stuck in it — forbidden, even if there is no drop of blood, for we are concerned perhaps the esophagus was punctured and healed and it is not discernible. {Rama: Some permit if there is no drop of blood from outside, for we are not concerned perhaps it healed (Ra'avan, Rosh, and Hagahot Ashiri in name of Sefer HaTerumah); and thus they practiced to be lenient in our city with those geese that are fattened to make fat from them, for there is a remedy in the city to examine after puncture of the esophagus because it is more common than lung adhesions; and they practiced to be lenient when a thorn sits in the esophagus if it is not punctured on both sides or there is no drop of blood from outside; and one must be very careful with this for it is a d'oraisa prohibition; and it would be better not to examine at all and rely on the majority than to examine and be lenient where there is a financial loss.}
