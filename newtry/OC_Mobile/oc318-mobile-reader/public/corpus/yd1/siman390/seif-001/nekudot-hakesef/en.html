(There, seif katan 3) But it appears to me. This is incorrect. Having a Jewish woman cut the nails is not considered a shinui. If it were, why do the Gemara and poskim say that he should cut them with his hands or by means of a shinui? They should simply have said to have another person do it. Rather, another person cutting one's nails is ordinary conduct and is not itself a shinui. The correct explanation is as I wrote in Siftei Kohen. Taz's question on the Gemara is also no difficulty. Since the act can be done by a shinui, they permitted it only by that method, because wherever a matter can be corrected in a less problematic fashion, that is preferable. His further question on Rama is likewise no difficulty. There the ruling permits him to prepare his nails for a brit milah. His purpose is not beautification, but to shape the two thumbnails needed for the milah and periah, sharpening their sides for that purpose.
