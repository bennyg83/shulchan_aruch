All hair. This is from Masechet Semachot, cited by Rosh, siman 100, and Tosafot Yevamot 43a.
And the moustache... and from the sides. See Rosh, siman 67, following Raavad, who rules like R. Ami that the moustache and side hairs may be trimmed whenever they interfere. Rosh disagrees. Torat HaAdam writes that Raavad's leniency applies specifically during Chol HaMoed, whereas a mourner remains forbidden, and Rambam likewise holds this way, based on the baraita including all facial hair. Ritz Giat writes that during the seven days it is forbidden but afterward permitted. Ramban brings proof from the Yerushalmi that it is permitted even within seven days. Beit Yosef cites the Yerushalmi and compromises in Shulchan Aruch like Ritz Giat. Ramban's position, however, is primary, as his Yerushalmi proof shows, and Tur likewise follows it. (Likkut) And hair upon. This is unlike Raavad, who limits the leniency to Chol HaMoed. The Yerushalmi records two teachings concerning moustache trimming and nail cutting, one permitting on the festival and prohibiting in mourning, and another the reverse. Rav rules like the lenient position in each context. Rav further says the moustache has the same law as nail cutting, provided it protrudes over the mouth and interferes with eating.
