Both the hair, etc. This is in Moed Katan, brought by the Rosh in siman 100, and Tosafot in Yevamot 43a, s.v. be-makom, etc.
And the hair, etc.; and from the sides, etc. See the Rosh, siman 67, and this follows the Raavad, who ruled like Rabbi Ami that the moustache and the sides may be trimmed whenever they interfere, unlike the Rosh. In Torat HaAdam he wrote that the Raavad said this specifically regarding chol ha-moed, but regarding mourning it is forbidden, and this is also the Rambam's view, from the above baraita: both the hair of his beard, etc. The Ritz Giat wrote that within seven days it is forbidden and after seven it is permitted. The Ramban brought proof from the Yerushalmi that even within seven days it is permitted. See the Beit Yosef, who brings the Yerushalmi and rules in the Shulchan Aruch like the Ritz Giat, making a compromise. But the Ramban's words are primary, as he proved from the Yerushalmi, and this is also the view of the Tur.
(Likkut) And the hair upon, etc. This is unlike the Raavad, who wrote that it applies specifically on chol ha-moed, but in mourning it is forbidden, as stated in Evel Rabbati: how is haircutting, etc. This is not so, for in the Yerushalmi chapter 3 we say: regarding the moustache and nail-cutting, one teaching says that on the festival it is permitted and for a mourner it is forbidden, and another teaching says that on the festival it is forbidden and for a mourner it is permitted, etc. Chiyya bar Ashi said in the name of Rav: the halachah follows the one who is lenient here and here. Rabbi Yose bar Acha said, Rabbi Shimon bar Rav said in the name of Rav, as in Torat HaAdam: the halachah follows the one who is lenient in the laws of mourning. Rav said: the moustache is like nail-cutting for all matters. Rabbi Yirmiyah said: provided they extend, meaning that they extend over the mouth and interfere with eating.
