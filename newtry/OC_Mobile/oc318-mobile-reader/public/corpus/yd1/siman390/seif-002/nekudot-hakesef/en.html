(Ibid., seif katan 3) But it appears to me. This is incorrect. Having a Jewish woman perform it does not constitute an unusual manner. If it did, why did the Gemara and the authorities say that he should remove them with his hand or in an unusual manner? They should have said that another person should do it. Rather, this is certainly not unusual, because it is common for one person to trim another's nails. The correct ruling is as I wrote in Siftei Kohen. His further question against the Gemara is also not difficult, for here, since it is possible through an unusual manner, the Sages permitted it only in that manner, because whenever the procedure can be modified, that is preferable. His additional question even according to the Rama is likewise no difficulty. There it states that he may prepare and trim his nails for the circumcision. His intent there is not cosmetic. Rather, he prepares the two thumbnails needed for circumcision, when they are slanted and pointed, by trimming their sides so they remain sharp for uncovering the membrane. This is clear.
