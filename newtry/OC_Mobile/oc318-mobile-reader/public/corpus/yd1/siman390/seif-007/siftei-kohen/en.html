And not specifically a non-Jewish woman, etc. In my humble opinion, he specifically said a non-Jewish woman. Otherwise, even for a man one should permit shaving or cutting his nails through another Jew. Rather, certainly, since the other Jew is his agent, it is forbidden. If so, why should it be more permitted for a woman through her agent than by herself? It is also forced to say that he merely used the language of chol ha-moed, for the Mechaber holds in Orach Chayim siman 532 that it is permitted to cut one's nails on chol ha-moed. Rather, Hagahot Maimoniyot holds that cutting nails on chol ha-moed is forbidden, and says that through a non-Jew it is permitted, since instructing a non-Jew is a shevut and they did not decree in a mitzvah context. If so, the same applies to a mourner, for we rule that instructing a non-Jew is a shevut even in a matter unrelated to Shabbat, as above in the laws of kilayim of animals, siman 297 seif 4. Therefore here it is permitted specifically through a non-Jew, for instructing a non-Jew is forbidden only as shevut, and in a mitzvah context they did not decree; but through a Jew it is forbidden. Examine carefully.
