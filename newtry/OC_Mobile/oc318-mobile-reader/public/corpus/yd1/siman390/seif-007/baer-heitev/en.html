A Jewish woman. The Shach writes that the text specifically permits use of a non-Jewish woman. Otherwise, a man should likewise be permitted to shave or cut his nails through another Jew. Since the other Jew acts as his agent, it is forbidden. Asking a non-Jew is only a rabbinic restriction, and the Sages did not impose it for a mitzvah. The Taz objects that seif 5 permits a woman to remove hair after seven days, so why may she not cut her own nails normally, since nails are no more stringent than hair? Moreover, even according to the Rama, siman 393, seif 3, permits cutting nails for the purpose of circumcision. Why should it not be permitted for immersion required by a mitzvah? Nekudot HaKesef answers that here it can be done with an alteration, and whatever can be corrected in that manner is preferable. The mohel's nails are different because they are not cut for beauty, but to shape the two thumbnails needed for peri'ah. See siman 393.
