"To comb," etc. One seif is missing here, because seif 4 and seif 6 are marked but seif 5 is not. This is unlike the Taz, who divided seif 5 into two seifim. Rather, there must be a separate seif, as it appears in some editions of Shulchan Aruch, reading: "A woman may remove hair after seven days. {Rama: Some prohibit this even for a woman, and this is the primary ruling.}" So too in the introduction to Semak; see there. See Beit Yosef, who cites the two opinions. Bach rules that in a place where a woman ordinarily removes these hairs, such as excessive hair at the temples and beside the temples, she may remove them after seven days; see there.
"The woman," etc. Beit Yosef implies that even a man may do so, because there is neither pleasure nor joy involved. It appears that "woman" is therefore not precise. Bach writes that he found this to be a textual error and that the reading should be "to comb his head," etc.; Taz likewise has this. Hagahos Semak in the name of Or Zarua, however, permits specifically a woman and not a man, and Bach cites it.
