And the festival does not help. The Mechaber states the same in Orach Chaim 548:9 and below, siman 399:4. It appears that even the combination of a festival and friends' rebuke does not help if the festival occurs before thirty days, as is also explicit above, siman 389:5, regarding pressing or laundering garments. However, Responsa Noda BiYehudah, Orach Chaim, siman 14, discussing a case where the time of rebuke arrived on Chol HaMoed, concludes that on Chol HaMoed itself there is certainly no permission. On the eve of the festival, if friends rebuke him, there is stronger basis to permit even within thirty days, though he says this only as theoretical halachah and not practical ruling until he can further settle the matter. It is astonishing that he does not mention Shulchan Aruch siman 389, where the matter appears clear and no distinction between pressing garments and haircutting is warranted. I later found Responsa Teshuvah MeAhavah I, siman 124, who makes this same objection and brings many authorities prohibiting it. He concludes that those who permit haircutting within thirty days for a father or mother because of the festival and rely on Noda BiYehudah may keep their ruling, but he does not wish to join them. See also Responsa Panim Meirot II, siman 53, by my ancestor.
At three months. See Responsa Noda BiYehudah, Orach Chaim, siman 14, at the end, who writes that the statement that the period until friends rebuke him is three months applies to those who shave only the hair of the head. For those who regularly shave the beard, whether with scissors or depilatory, the period of noticeable neglect is shorter. Responsa Chatam Sofer, siman 347, likewise writes that for Ashkenazim who regularly shave their beards, the period of rebuke is thirty days. If thirty days of mourning for a father or mother overlap with the public mourning beginning on the Seventeenth of Tammuz, he should take a minimal haircut on the Eighteenth of Tammuz sufficient that he is no longer in a condition for friends to rebuke him. See there. Noda BiYehudah also discusses whether one whose time of rebuke falls on Chol HaMoed may shave. See Responsa Adnei Paz, siman 8, who explains Rama's intent to be that after three months, the ordinary measure of rebuke, one may thereafter cut his hair whenever he chooses according to his former practice.
