(There seif 2 in the gloss) And likewise one should not roast, etc.; however b'dieved it is not forbidden, etc. This law is from the Gemara Pesachim daf 76 side b: this fish that was roasted with meat — Rava forbade eating it in kutach; Mar bar Rav Ashi said: even in salt it is also forbidden. Behold Rambam in chapter 9 of the laws of forbidden foods ruled like Mar bar Rav Ashi that fish roasted with meat is forbidden to eat in kutach; and they challenged him from that which Mar the son of Rav Ashi forbids eating even in salt; and they said to him that Rambam holds that Mar bar Rav Ashi deals lechatchilah, but b'dieved if they roasted it is permitted to eat alone — that is, if so what does Mar bar Rav Ashi seek here, to teach us that alone it is forbidden lechatchilah, for Rava forbade it even b'dieved? Or that even Mar bar Rav Ashi deals b'dieved, and he came to disagree with Rava who permits b'dieved? If so, poskim are puzzled in forbidden foods, and one must say that Mar bar Rav Ashi never said except lechatchilah; and they said to him: if so what does Mar bar Rav Ashi seek in the matter? It appears to me to resolve Rava's matter: for one may say according to what we hold that an oven whose surface was smeared with dough and bread was baked in it — the bread is forbidden entirely because we are concerned lest one come to eat from it in kutach; if so why does Rava not forbid regarding fish to eat them even in salt, lest one come to eat in kutach? And one may answer that this decree applies only to bread, for if they permit him to eat alone he will do so lechatchilah and come to be lenient to bake bread with meat, and it will be very common; and the Sages decreed. And all this applies to bread, not so to fish, for lechatchilah it is forbidden to roast with meat because of danger; therefore if it happened by chance and they roasted with meat, it is a matter that is neither here nor there for the Sages. And now it is well to say that Rambam explains that which Mar bar Rav Ashi said as we said, namely that it is forbidden only lechatchilah, and one cannot resolve except Rava's words — that which we do not forbid eating alone; and according to the poskim' challenge to this — that even in salt it is forbidden lechatchilah — if so it is a matter neither here nor there for the Sages. [Said the editor: his words are puzzling, for if Mar bar Rav Ashi holds there is danger and therefore it is forbidden lechatchilah, then plainly b'dieved is also forbidden, for there is danger; and in truth the main question is on Rava — resolve for me: there where he baked with milk literally or with meat literally in it, we certainly decree; not so here where it is only odor; and in truth we hold that odor is nothing, and even if one forgets and eats with kutach there is no concern, only lechatchilah it is forbidden because it is something unseemly, as Ri wrote — end of gloss of the great rabbi Rabbi Shlomo Cohen, may God protect him, Vilna first edition.] And regarding what Turei Zahav wrote about a large open oven regarding danger — it appears to me this depends: if we say that regarding danger we forbid in any amount, as with that snake venom — if so Magen Avraham already wrote that where forbidden in any amount, even in a large open oven it is forbidden; and therefore in this case a large open oven does not help at all. Not so according to Peri Chadash who distinguishes between snake venom and here — regarding venom which is danger to oneself, it is well to say it is not nullified; not so meat and fish which are forbidden only through mingling of meat and fish taste — if there are sixty it is as if nullified and as if there was no meat there, and it is well nullified like other prohibitions; according to this it is well to distinguish between a large open oven and a small sealed one.
