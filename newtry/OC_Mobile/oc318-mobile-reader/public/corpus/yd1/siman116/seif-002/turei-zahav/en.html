However b'dieved it is not forbidden. That is regarding odor alone; but if they were cooked together they are forbidden even b'dieved. And Issur VeHeter wrote klal 23, and these are his words: Mordechai wrote, and Ibn Ezra in chapter 1 of forbidden foods — a matter involving danger has the law of a prohibition to nullify it in sixty, and is more stringent, for even dry in its kind requires sixty — end of his words; and all the more so a piece of meat that fell into a pot that has fish or vice versa — there is danger and requires sixty against it; but regarding taste of vessel absorption there is no concern, etc. — end of his words. And Darkei Moshe challenged: behold it is stated in Tur that we are concerned for exposure even in one hundred se'ah of water, and it is impossible that there not be sixty even if a snake drank from it — end of his words; and so it is written in Maharil — a flask of fish fell into a pot of meat and was cooked there, and Maharil forbade everything because of danger — end of his words; it implies sixty does not help, for danger is more stringent than prohibition; and Rashal chapter 35 siman 15 wrote that even lechatchilah one need not be stringent in a large oven regarding this, even though regarding the prohibition of meat and milk we are stringent lechatchilah; and therefore it appears plain to me that those breads they bake in one oven with meat — there is no danger at all to eat them with fish even if the oven was sealed.
