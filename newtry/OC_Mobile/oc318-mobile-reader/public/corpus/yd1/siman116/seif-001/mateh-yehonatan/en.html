(Siman 116 in Shulchan Aruch seif 1) And now that snakes are not found among us, it is permitted. Turei Zahav s.k. 8 and Peri Chadash s.k. 1, who elaborated for our benefit, for we find many matters forbidden by count even though the reason is nullified; nevertheless another count is needed to permit them. And the true difference is: certainly everything the Sages forbade without any condition — such as not to do melachah on erev Pesach after midday because of the offering of the Pesach, or not to eat close to minchah according to those who hold that close to minchah gedolah we learned, and because of Pesach — and even though now there is no Pesach offering, for the reason is nullified, nevertheless since it was already forbidden by count without any condition, now too it is forbidden. Not so where at the time it was forbidden there was also a condition, such as milk of a gentile that requires a Jew to sit and see it, and that is because he mixes impure milk into it; therefore since if a Jew sees from the beginning it is not forbidden, now too when there is nothing impure among us — it is as if a Jew sees, for even at the time of the decree it was not forbidden in such a case. And likewise exposure — initially exposure was forbidden only because of a snake; and if it is known that there were no snakes there, it is permitted; therefore now when snakes are not common, it is as if he guarded it and saw that the snake did not drink from it; and the matter of a count requiring another count to permit does not apply here.
