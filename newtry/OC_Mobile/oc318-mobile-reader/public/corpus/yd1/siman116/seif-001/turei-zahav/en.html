And now that there are no snakes, etc. And there is no issue here of a matter forbidden by count, etc., for it was forbidden only because of snakes; and see above in siman 115 seif 2 in the name of Semak: Tur — a beverage that drips drop by drop has no issue of exposure; and Beit Yosef wrote that this is in the lower vessel, but in the upper vessel there is issue of exposure. And one may challenge: behold in Gozel (daf 115) it is implied in Tosafot's words beginning "When," etc., that the reason it is permitted drop by drop is that the snake thinks there is a person there and does not come — if so one should permit the upper one too; and one may say that Beit Yosef wrote thus according to what Rambam wrote regarding the reason of drop by drop — the snake fears the bubbling of the beverage, etc.; according to this one must forbid the upper one, and it requires investigation why Beit Yosef wrote this as plain, when in truth it is a dispute. And there is further room for investigation in Beit Yosef in what he wrote on Ran — perhaps he explains that the cat we say in the Gemara is not precise, etc.; it is puzzling, for behold other impure animals die from venom as Tosafot wrote in the last chapter of Gozel in Bava Kamma (daf 115); if so there is a violation of bal tashchit as Tosafot wrote there, and this is proven from that we challenge on the cat itself; and if so how does Ran compare other impure animals to the cat? And one must say that Ran explains thus: in that which the tanna said one should not give to drink to another's animal — which is superfluous — he intended to teach us that there is a distinction in the matter, namely regarding one's own there is prohibition only lest he eat it, and that is when fit for eating; but regarding an impure animal one need not be careful about this — are we dealing with fools that he would kill his animals? as Tosafot wrote there in Bava Kamma; and if in a case where he needs to kill it, plainly it is permitted and there is no bal tashchit here; and as we will write further in this siman. But regarding another's impure animal there is prohibition even when not fit for eating, from the aspect of damaging his property; and we challenge: but it is taught — rather he gives to his animal; and we say it deals with an impure one, and that is necessarily where he needs to kill it as written; if so the challenge is obvious, and we answer: regarding the cat — venom does not kill it; and on this we challenge: whichever way you want, what difference between his and another's, for necessarily there is damage since it is forbidden for another's; if so there is no permission in his because of bal tashchit but because he needs this — the challenge is obvious, and we answer that it weakens and then becomes healthy: in this there is prohibition regarding others from the aspect of damage, for when he wishes to sell it at the time of its weakness; but regarding his own it is permitted even when he does not need this but only to test the water, for there is no bal tashchit since it becomes healthy again. It turns out that Ran wrote well that regarding his own there is no prohibition at all with an impure animal: if venom kills it he would not do so himself; and if he needs it, certainly permitted; and if it weakens and becomes healthy, likewise no prohibition; and if it does not become healthy again, it is like killing it literally, for a person does not do this unless he needs it; but regarding another's it is forbidden in every case — not only where it kills, but even when weak and becomes healthy it is forbidden; and all is well. In Derishah he brings here what there is to challenge from what is written in Orach Chaim siman 472 that one does not recite kiddush on wine that was exposed even if strained in a strainer — it implies but it is permitted at least for drinking, and that is like Rabbi Nehemiah who permits when strained in a strainer; and here Tur ruled that a strainer does not help, like the Sages; and it appears he teaches us not to say that since one wishes to perform a mitzvah with it he should not be concerned for danger, since in any case a strainer helps for Rabbi Nehemiah even for drinking, as stated in the chapter of the eve of Pesach — a cup of blessing combines for good and not for bad regarding pairs; he teaches us that nevertheless one does not recite kiddush on it.
