(Siman 116, seif 6) It is forbidden to eat foods and beverages that a person finds repulsive, etc., for all these are included in "You shall not make your souls detestable." The Taz wrote that one receives only lashes of rebelliousness, because this is a general prohibition that includes many matters. The Prisha wrote that this is not a general prohibition, because all forms of repulsiveness are one category, and the Torah prohibited only one matter. The reason one does not receive full lashes is that this is merely a rabbinic asmachta. The Tevuos Shor, in siman 13, challenged the Prisha, because the words of the Semag escaped his notice, and he agreed with the Taz that it is a full derashah, but nevertheless a general prohibition. In my opinion, the law accords with the Prisha, because the view of the poskim is not like the Semag but like the Prisha, as is explicit in Makos, daf 18, and Menachos, daf 58, that anything bearing a single name is not considered a general prohibition. This is also the view of the Rambam and the Ramban in Sefer HaMitzvos, and the Kesef Mishnah wrote similarly regarding the Rambam at the beginning of chapter 11 of Hilchos Avodah Zarah. As for the Tevuos Shor's challenge to the Prisha from Sanhedrin, daf 63, that the verse "you shall not serve them" is a general prohibition encompassing every form of worship and nevertheless is considered a general prohibition, he overlooked what the Ra'avad wrote at the end of chapter 3, s.v. Avodah Zarah.
