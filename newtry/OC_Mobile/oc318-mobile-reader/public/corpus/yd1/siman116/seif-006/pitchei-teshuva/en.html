Leading to danger. See Baer Heitev of Maharit, of blessed memory, what the Sages forbade not to cut down a fruit tree, etc. [and in Baer Heitev this below s.k. 8]; and see in responsum Bashamayim Rosh siman 334 that he wrote: where needed for its place it appears permitted, for Rambam said the main prohibition is specifically through destruction; and it is possible that if needed for its place it is not called through destruction, and one should not be stringent through a gentile at all — end of his words there; and see in She'ilat Yaavetz part 1 siman 76 what he wrote on this. [And at the end of his words he wrote that plainly if one uproots a tree with its roots and with the earth of its nourishment so it can live from it and plants it elsewhere, there is no prohibition here at all, for plainly this is not cutting and it was not forbidden in any manner, for behold it is as planted in its place, and as we hold regarding orlah in a tree uprooted and the rock with it — if it can live it is exempt from orlah, all the more so here — see there; and see in responsum Chut HaShani siman 102 that he wrote on this: I will not act according to him to be lenient that not for need at all I would not permit even to uproot and replant elsewhere; but to be stringent, such as for need of its place, nevertheless where it suffices; and it is possible with uprooting with the root to plant elsewhere it is forbidden to cut, for so it is implied from the precise language of the Gemara in Bava Kamma — see there.] And see in responsum Tzemach Tzedek siman 41 that he wrote: if the tree harms the vines, he may uproot it — see there [and in responsum Chut HaShani there he elaborated on cutting down a fruit tree and concluded: whatever is not clear that the gain of cutting exceeds the fruit — the tree complains and it is forbidden to cut it, and there is danger even in safek; but if there is gain, even only where needed to build houses in our time and in our country — certainly preferable to a palm tree it is permitted; but if one can uproot with its roots and a clod of earth with them so it can live and one can plant them elsewhere, it is forbidden to cut them — see there]. And what Baer Heitev wrote further: one should not wash, etc. — see in responsum Orach Chaim siman 4 s.k. 7 that he wrote that so it appears in Sdei Chemed; but in our country they are not concerned for exposure at all, even for rinsing the mouth, and all the more for washing hands — see there. And see in responsum Adnei Paz siman 25 and siman 34 that he wrote one may call two sons by one name whether one died or both are alive; and there in the omissions he wrote that nevertheless one should be concerned for ayin hara; and if one died one should not call the second by that name because of bad fortune — see there. And it appears plain that also with a gentile who is not his brother bad fortune applies; and perhaps from this reason "the names of the wicked shall rot" — we do not mention their names because of bad fortune. However in Yoma daf 38 it is not quite implied thus — see there. And see in the will of Rabbi Yehudah HaChasid how many matters one must be careful about because of danger. And what he wrote there: one should not marry a woman whose name is like his mother's or whose name is like his father-in-law's — see in responsum Chachmat Adam klal 123 din 13 that he wrote this is specifically when they are tripled, such as a man named Reuven and his son-in-law named Reuven, and if he takes another son-in-law named Reuven — then he will not succeed; and likewise a woman named Rivkah, etc.; and so explicitly in Shulchan HaTahor siman 472; and the world errs in this — end of his words; and see in responsum Noda BiYehudah second edition part Even HaEzer siman 79 that he wrote all his will was only for his seed after him and not for other people; and even for his seed there is concern only for the essential name given at birth or at circumcision, but not for a name changed at illness — and see there further that he protested against most of the world who give their daughters to a gentile without concern (see below siman 217 s.k. 76 in the name of Chavot Yair), and this is against the words of the Sages; and to the will of Rabbi Yehudah HaChasid they are concerned [and see on this in Pithei Teshuva to Even HaEzer siman 2 s.k. 7 and siman 50 s.k. 14].
