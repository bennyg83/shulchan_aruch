Do not abhor. Tur wrote in the name of Rashba that for this they would administer lashes of rebellion (and one is not flogged for it, for it is a negative commandment stated in general terms). And Turei Zahav wrote, and these are his words: I found in an old collection — these are days of great danger to let blood for kabbalah according to Rabbi Yehudah HaChasid: alef-alef-tav, that is Rosh Chodesh Nisan, Elul, Tevet; when the first day of one of these falls on Monday or Wednesday; and likewise one who slaughters a goose on that day or eats from it is in danger, God forbid. And in another collection I found likewise, only that in place of the first day of Tevet it is written the fifteenth of Tevet when it falls on Sunday, that is Rosh Chodesh Tevet that falls on Shabbat, and that is the first day of Rosh Chodesh. We further find that the Sages forbade because of danger not to cut down a fruit-bearing tree, and Rosh wrote: if he needed its place it is permitted. And from this I permitted someone who had land with trees to cut the trees even though they have fruit in order to build a dwelling house on it. And Rashal wrote on what is stated in the Gemara regarding uncovered beverages that were not strained, that it is forbidden to give them to his animal to drink, and Rashi explained the reason — because of danger lest he slaughter it afterward and eat it; and from this they practiced to forbid sheep in which grows a type of scab of nega that are accustomed to die in them; and in days of drought I saw that the community made a ban and forbade all sheep until the plague ceased — end of his words. And one may say: behold we said above in siman 60 that even if the animal ate its own deadly poison it is kosher, and it implies there is no prohibition at all because of danger, except specifically in eating the deadly poison of a person, for perhaps there is in any case prohibition from the aspect that it was dangerous. And Rashal further wrote: one who has a bad dog that harms and spoils food, even if it does not bite, may give it deadly poison and he does not violate tza'ar ba'alei chayim; nevertheless I heard to protest not to put a needle in bread for them, for there is concern lest it go mad, and there is great danger to life — end of his words (see in Lechem HaPanim this siman).
