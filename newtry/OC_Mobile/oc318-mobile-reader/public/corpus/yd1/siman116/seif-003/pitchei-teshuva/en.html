B'dieved. See Baer Heitev regarding whether a matter involving danger is nullified in sixty; and see in responsum Chavot Yair siman 64 that he ruled to be lenient that it is nullified; and so wrote in responsum Shevut Yaakov part 2 siman 104 that one should not be stringent at all — see there; and so in responsum Chut HaShani siman 67 — specifically snake venom is not nullified as Tur wrote that we are concerned for exposure even in forty se'ah of water; but a matter that is not venom, even though it involves danger, is nullified in sixty — see there. And that which Turei Zahav brought proof from Maharil — a flask of fish fell into a pot of meat and he forbade everything, Maharil, because of danger — see there; they already challenged him in Shach in Nekudat HaKesef and in responsum Chut HaShani there — one may say there were not sixty, therefore he forbade everything — see there. And in my humble opinion it appears that nevertheless Turei Zahav proved well: for if you say that where there are sixty it is permitted, and therefore Maharil forbade because there were not sixty — it would be asked why he did not command to add until sixty so the fish taste would be nullified; for even though in prohibitions it is forbidden to add because of nullifying a prohibition lechatchilah as stated in siman 99 seif 6, nevertheless here certainly nullifying a prohibition does not apply, for this is not a prohibition — only as long as they perceive fish taste in the pot of meat there is danger; and when they add and fish taste is no longer perceived in the pot of meat, there is no longer danger (and see chapter 8 of Terumot mishnah 4 and 5, and understand well; and there is to resolve) [and see in Magen Avraham siman 495 s.k. 15 and in responsum Binyan Olam part 1 siman 27]. Also squeezing applies only if absorbed in a piece, not in something moist. Or that even where there are sixty it is forbidden; nevertheless the law follows those responsa that it is nullified [also in responsum Chut HaShani siman 101 he wrote that this is the main ruling, and there he was asked about fish broth that was poured into a vessel of fat, and after it cooled they removed the congealed fat — what is the law of the broth and fish; and he answered: behold in prohibitions in such a case we require estimating sixty against the whole, and it does not help that they removed the prohibition that congealed — Shach siman 98 s.k. 16; and if so, all the more so here regarding danger, for regarding safek danger is more stringent than prohibition for all — investigate. In truth, since we see from Rambam that Rav Geviriya in remedies and natural matters omitted this matter of fish roasted with meat, presumably according to the depth of his wisdom he knew and understood that natures changed in this matter; and although we should not rely on this to act and eat them one after the other, for perhaps it is like a matter forbidden by count, nevertheless we have gained that we emerge from the stringency of danger, for now at least there is no danger. And it is possible that even in their time the danger was only in a certain type of fish and not in types found among us at all, and there is only a light rabbinic prohibition; for although a matter forbidden by count from the Torah is forbidden as a fence and guard of a prohibition, in such a case it is very light. And even in a Torah prohibition Raavad holds it suffices to estimate well, etc.; and further it is possible that through pouring, which is only swallowing and emitting, they did not decree on this even in their time; and behold we practice to be lenient to eat meat in a fish vessel, Beit Yosef, even at the same meal through mere rinsing — evidently we hold there is no danger in mere mingling of tastes. Nevertheless regarding the congealed fat one should investigate somewhat: perhaps if one breaks a piece of congealed fat he will find some of the broth water between the layers, and then it is certainly forbidden; but if one cannot determine, it appears one need not be so stringent — end of his words there. And see in Sefer Tiferet Lemoshe that he wrote in the name of his grandfather the gaon Rabbi Yaakov, of blessed memory: when a fish fell into a pot with meat or vice versa, he would forbid the vessel without hagalah, for although they cook fish in a meat vessel or vice versa, that is because the forbidden taste emerging from the pot does not create venom; but when meat and fish were cooked together in the pot, venom was cooked in it and absorbed without sixty in the pot and it is forbidden — see there. [And see in Sefer Chacham Tzvi that he wrote: if a deadly creature called shafin fell into food or beverage — Beit Lechem Yehudah wrote that sixty does not help, for the reason is venom; and see in Magen Avraham siman 216 s.k. 23 that he wrote that one in a thousand that is dangerous in food — perhaps one may rely and permit at least in sixty; and I also found in Sefer HaBrit that they have no venom at all — see there.]
