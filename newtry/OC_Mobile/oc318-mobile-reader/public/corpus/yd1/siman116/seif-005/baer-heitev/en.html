Beverages. Beit Yosef wrote even if they are covered (in an iron vessel).
The tekufah. Shach wrote that people practice placing iron on all beverages and foods. And on foods and beverages that are cooked or pickled or salted they do not place anything, and it is good to be stringent in this to place iron on them. And Turei Zahav wrote, and these are his words: Darkei Moshe in the name of Abudraham — I found written that one must be careful not to drink water at the time of the tekufah because of danger lest one be harmed and swell, and the reason is that a drop of blood falls between tekufah and tekufah; but the sage Ibn Ezra answered on this that it is mere superstition, etc., and there is no danger at all, and some of the Geonim said there is no divination in Jacob, rather the ancients said these things to frighten people that they should fear God and repent — end of his words. And Rambam wrote that it is forbidden to walk on a shaky bridge or into a ruin and the like, and see above siman 153 the law that it is forbidden to be alone with a gentile because of danger.
In the city. It is stated in the Gemara: when plague is in the city, a person should not walk in the middle of the roads; when peace is in the city, he should not walk at the sides of the roads. And Rashal wrote: it appears that the latter clause deals specifically with night, for by day harmful forces are not common; but the former clause regarding plague — there is no distinction between day and night.
