At the time of the tekufah. It is written in Darkei Moshe in the name of Abudraham: I found written that one must be careful not to drink water at the time of the tekufah because of danger lest one be harmed and swell; and the reason is that a drop of blood falls between tekufah and tekufah; but the sage Ibn Ezra answered on this that it is mere superstition, etc., and there is no danger at all; and some of the Geonim said there is no divination in Jacob — rather the ancients said these things to frighten people that they should fear God, blessed be He, and repent so that God, blessed be He, would save them from the four seasons of the year — end of his words.
And not at its end. Since the matter has already become established, God forbid; and further it is stated in the Gemara chapter HaKones: when plague is in the city, a person should not walk in the middle of the roads because the angel of death has permission and walks openly; when peace is in the city, he should not walk at the sides of the roads because he has no permission and hides himself. And Rashal wrote: it appears the latter clause deals specifically with night, for by day harmful forces are not common; but the former clause regarding plague — there is no distinction between day and night.
