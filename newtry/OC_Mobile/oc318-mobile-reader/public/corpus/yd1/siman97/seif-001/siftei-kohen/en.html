Even to eat it alone. A decree lest one eat it with meat:
And one may not bake, etc. Meaning: l'chatchila one may not bake any bread with paladin or pashkida in an oven even if wide and not sloped, for we are concerned lest it drip onto the bread; but b'dieved if the oven is not sloped or the bread is above the pashkida we do not establish prohibition, for perhaps it dripped — since even if it dripped it did not drip beneath the bread itself; but if we see that it dripped from the pashkida outward, even if it did not drip beneath the bread itself but the bread is far from the pashkida — the bread is forbidden even b'dieved, as stated in Hagahot Ashiri, Hagahat Sh"D, and Hagahat Or Zarua chapter 39 in the name of Or Zarua — all this is proven in Turei Chadash chapter 36 section 9 and in my simanim 108 — investigate; and the reason for the matter appears to me because we are uncertain whether cooking spreads through the entire vessel, as written in siman 92 s.k. 16; therefore where we do not see dripping there is a double doubt — perhaps it dripped or not, and if it dripped perhaps it did not spread through all of it; but when the oven is sloped, presumably it dripped beneath the bread itself; and likewise when we see dripping there is only one doubt, as stated — investigate; and even though in Hagahat Or Zarua there it writes in the name of Or Zarua that without seeing dripping there is in any case a double doubt — perhaps it dripped or not, and if it dripped perhaps they will not eat it with milk — end of his words — perhaps the Rav does not consider this "perhaps they will not eat with milk" as a doubt at all, since the custom is to eat bread with all kinds of foods, it is close that one will also eat it with milk; therefore what the Rav wrote in the hagahah here "and if it dripped beneath it" means it dripped beneath the pashkida even if far from the bread, and not like Bach — see there — investigate; and Beit Chadash wrote: and not as Bach wrote there that the bread is forbidden only when it dripped beneath it literally, and he understood thus from Hagahot Ashiri that Beit Yosef brought — he did not analyze, end of his words — he did not look in Turei Chadash, for one who examines there will see the Rav brings in the name of Hagahot Ashiri explicitly the opposite, that even if it did not drip beneath the bread it is forbidden, and likewise in my simanim 108 there, and it is proven there the Rav's view as I wrote:
