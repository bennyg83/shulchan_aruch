Lest it come to be eaten with meat, etc. From this it appears to me that a mortar in which they grind spices and use the spices both with meat and with milk, and once they ground garlic there with goose-fat gravy and afterward return that gravy onto the goose — it appears to me that that mortar is forbidden to grind spices in again even to eat with meat, lest one come to eat with milk, similar to here; and it requires kashering specifically, and even b'dieved it is forbidden without kashering like the bread here; and it is not similar to what was written in siman 96 seif 3 regarding spices ground in a meat mortar forbidden with milk but permitted with meat — there it deals with a mortar designated for meat specifically; do not err there — so it appears plain to me.
And if it dripped beneath it. We do not require that the fat be visible beneath the bread literally; rather, whenever it spreads outside the pashkida and is absorbed in the oven we are concerned lest the absorption reached the bread — so it appears in Hagahot Ashiri.
And even if it is in a pan. I was asked regarding a woman who baked paladin of cheese in an oven on its floor without a vessel, and afterward placed a pot of boiling meat on that spot where the paladin had been. I answered that there is no prohibition here, for earthenware of an oven that absorbed from milk is no worse than a pot in which they cook milk and it touched a meat pot — there is no prohibition, as the poskim write regarding two pots that touched one another, and Rama brings it in siman 92 seif 8; and here where he was stringent regarding a pan, that is l'chatchila only.
