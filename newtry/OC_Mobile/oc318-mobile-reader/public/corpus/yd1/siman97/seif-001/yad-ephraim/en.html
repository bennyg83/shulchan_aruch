One does not bake bread in an oven, etc. She'elat Ya'avetz part 1 siman 103 wrote he prevented in his home warming dairy foods in a pottery oven after using it hot for meat foods — not because of absorption concern, for we are not concerned from vessel to vessel as Taz wrote siman 97 regarding a woman who baked paladin, etc., and likewise siman 92, and Taz Orach Chayim, and Magen Avraham siman 451 that even on Pesach they were lenient — end of his words; but the prevention was lest one come to a stumbling block by placing foods without a vessel, and such an oven absorbs and emits, for it does not leave its mouth; however in pressing need one need not prevent using vessels and iron ovens in Ashkenaz, for they immediately whiten through heating and that is their kashering, even if a wet forbidden thing spilled on it — all the more so when it is only doubt; and one who wants to be stringent for himself lest perhaps they place food without a vessel immediately when the forbidden thing spilled and it has not yet whitened — nevertheless in a place where they are not accustomed to warm foods without interruption, one need not be stringent at all; and know that daily practice is cooking meat in ovens and stoves where they also cook milk and vice versa, even though it is impossible that food did not spill there; and they also place a permitted pot beside forbidden and are not concerned for sparks — see siman 118 — end briefly; and see there he wrote: even if a meat vessel stood in dairy gravy or vice versa, the food is not forbidden, only the vessel; and greater than this there is a case in Beit Din general rule 55, etc., referring to Maharam in Hagahah there explained in hagahah siman 92; and this matter is not relevant here, for there it is the law of second vessel, whereas here the oven is boiling and what spills on it is a first vessel and the bottom dominates and absorbs and forbids the food if there is not sixty — and see siman 177 part 1 where Bach wrote to be concerned when placing a bowl, etc., and Magen Avraham wrote there is no transfer from vessel to vessel — see there: She'elat Ya'avetz wrote the world practices leniency with our rolls that are very recognizable as kneaded with milk; he challenged Maharam Hagiz in Hilchot Ketanot siman 56 who wrote baking biskogis (in their language, a kind of cracker) with milk — his father Mahari Hagiz answered it is not "in the shape of an ox" and is like other bread since they make them frequently; his son Maharam understood he returns to the permission (and likewise Maharam in Leket HaKemach on this) and did not notice a textual error — it must be they are not called "in the shape of an ox"; he meant those biskogis are not like "in the shape of an ox" that we say is permitted, and it is like other bread, for since they are somewhat thin it does not help because they make them frequently — one substitutes other bread to eat with meat, therefore they are forbidden entirely — see there.
