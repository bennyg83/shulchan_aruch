Dough. Taz wrote: from this it appears that a mortar in which they grind spices used with meat and milk, and once they ground garlic with goose-fat gravy — that mortar is forbidden to grind spices again even for meat lest one eat with milk; it requires kashering specifically and even b'dieved is forbidden without kashering like bread here; and some say regarding what was written in siman 96 seif 3 that spices ground in a meat mortar are forbidden with milk but permitted with meat — there the mortar is designated for meat specifically; do not err there — end of his words (and Peri Chadash disagrees and permits — see there).
That we taste it. Turei Chadash wrote we are not expert now in this tasting; and Shach wrote this is l'chatchila, but b'dieved if an Israelite tasted and there was no meat taste and it was cooked in milk it is permitted, for only regarding a non-Jew's tasting we do not rely even b'dieved, unlike an Israelite's tasting (and Peri Chadash wrote even l'chatchila).
Paladin. Shach wrote: l'chatchila one may not bake any bread with paladin or pashkida in an oven even if wide and not sloped; but b'dieved if the oven is not sloped or the bread is above the pashkida we do not establish prohibition; but if we see dripping outward, even if not beneath the bread but the bread is far from pashkida — forbidden even b'dieved; we do not say double doubt whether it dripped beneath or not; and if you say dripping is because perhaps they will not eat with milk — the Rav does not consider this "perhaps they will not eat with milk" as a doubt since the custom is to eat bread with all foods, it is close one will eat with milk; therefore what the Rav wrote in the hagahah "and if it dripped beneath it" means beneath the pashkida even if far from bread, not like Bach — see there; Taz wrote: I was asked regarding a woman who baked paladin of cheese on the oven floor without a vessel and afterward placed boiling meat there — I answered no prohibition, for oven earthenware that absorbed milk is no worse than a pot cooked with milk touching a meat pot — no prohibition as poskim write regarding two touching pots, Rama siman 92 seif 8; here stringency in a pan is l'chatchila only — end of his words (and in Nekudat HaKesef he wrote requires study, for unlike two pots where prohibition is absorption, here prohibition is from outside on the oven — see there).
