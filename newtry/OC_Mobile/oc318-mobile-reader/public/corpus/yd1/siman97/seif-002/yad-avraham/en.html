(there in the hagahah) One may not bake any bread with paladin in an oven lest fat drip onto the bread. And if it dripped beneath it, its law is as if kneaded with it — meaning since its law is as if kneaded with it, if it dripped beneath it, forbidden even to eat with salt, as this siman states; but if it did not drip beneath it literally, it is not as if kneaded with it and permitted to eat with salt l'chatchila. And also if it became mixed with milk b'dieved it is permitted, for it is taste of permitted in permitted from fat to oven and from oven the absorption reached the bread, and it is a second taste in bread; and even though it is cooking, Rama already wrote in siman 95 seif 2 that b'dieved permitted — meaning if they already put it in milk it is b'dieved if we forbid; but nevertheless l'chatchila forbidden to eat with milk, for what was already cooked is not considered b'dieved, for one can still eat without milk; and if you permit putting it in milk it is l'chatchila. See Tur, Beit Yosef, Darkei Moshe, Shulchan Aruch, hagahah siman 95, Turei Chadash, and you will understand the root: to cook l'chatchila in a meat vessel in order to put it in milk — even for Beit Yosef forbidden, for in the Gemara chapter Kol HaBasar (daf 111) it states regarding fish that rose, etc., and likewise Taz in that siman regarding fish that were cooked, etc.; only if already cooked — Mechaber and Beit Yosef permit l'chatchila to put in milk; and for Rama forbidden unless they already put it — we do not forbid. All this is clear and plain; I only wrote it to refute what Rav Moshe Ozar wrote in Even Ozar that for Rama if bread was already baked with pashkida and did not drip beneath it, taste of permitted in permitted permits eating with milk — he did not understand well; perhaps he means if one has no other bread to eat with milk that is b'dieved, as Rama wrote siman 108 regarding reicha; I do not know whence he compared to reicha of Shavuot where we are lenient (however taste of permitted in permitted is lighter than reicha as Magen Avraham wrote beginning general rule 57) — and Rama was precise there siman 108 in his wording that for this matter of reicha this is called b'dieved — implying specifically for reicha when one has no other bread to eat with milk, not for another matter. See Be'er HaGolah siman 95 seif 5. And likewise Peri Megadim: l'chatchila forbidden with milk, b'dieved permitted as taste of permitted in permitted; but if one has no other bread to eat with milk, there is room to be lenient as Magen Avraham wrote that it is lighter than reicha.
