(Likkut) What is, etc.; if they came, etc. The Yerushalmi in Nazir chapter 6 says: what is a met mitzvah? Anyone for whom one calls out and the townspeople do not come. If the townspeople came, he withdraws. Until what point? Until there are enough bearers of the bier, their replacements, and the replacements of their replacements, when he is not needed; but if they need him, this is not so. This applies when they do not recognize him, but if they recognize him, this is not so; and when his honor does not require it, but if his honor requires it, this is not so. A nasi's honor requires it. And what is the law of a kohen becoming tamei for the honor of a nasi? When Rabbi Yudan the Nasi died, Rabbi Yannai announced: there is no kehunah today. When Rabbi Yudan the Nasi, grandson of Rabbi Yudan the Nasi, died, Rabbi Chiyya bar Abba pushed Rabbi Zeira in the synagogue of Gufna of Tzippori and made him tamei. The phrase "what is the law," etc., means where he is not a met mitzvah; this is what they said in Ketubot chapter 12, 103b: on the day Rabbi died, kehunah was suspended, as cited in Torat HaAdam and in the Rosh, Hilkhot Tumah. So too in the Yerushalmi in Berakhot chapter 3 and Nazir chapter 7, unlike Tosafot in Ketubot there, s.v. oto, etc. The Yerushalmi there explains that they treated him like a met mitzvah. The Yerushalmi asks whether a student may become tamei for his teacher, and the matter is not resolved; therefore he should not become tamei. This is from Torat HaAdam. See seif 11 regarding a nasi, etc. So the Raavad wrote, while the Ramban left it requiring study.
(Likkut) What is a met mitzvah, etc.; if they came, etc. This is Evel Rabbati chapter 4, halachah 29. "If he has," etc., is there in halachah 30: always, etc.
If he has all his needs, etc. This is the Yerushalmi there, and the Rosh brings all of it there.
A corpse that is found, etc. In the Yerushalmi: if one was killed between Tiberias and Tzippori, the presumption is that he is a Jew.
