The custom is that all relatives of the dead. See Adnei Paz responsa siman 11, who wrote regarding one whose relative, close enough to invalidate testimony, died elsewhere, while there are mourners here who do not yet know of it, that he need not change his clothing according to the custom on the first Shabbat. Since the essence of this custom is for the honor of the mourners, and so it is implied by the Rama, who wrote that it is for the honor of those mourning with him, here, where they are not mourning, he is certainly exempt; indeed, there may even be a prohibition because of disrespect for the honor of Shabbat. See there. But it seems to me that when the Rama wrote, "nevertheless the custom is," he speaks even where there are no relatives mourning here; and so it is explicit in the Darkhei Moshe, see there. Therefore he must change his clothing.
