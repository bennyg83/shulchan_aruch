One does not mourn for him, etc. It implies that this speaks where we do not know that his months were completed. This is what the Beit Yosef objected to in Hagahot Ashri, writing: I do not know why he made it conditional on the hair and nails not being completed, etc. This is unlike the Bach, who wrote about the Beit Yosef: what Hagahot Ashri wrote, "and his hair and nails were not completed," etc., is because if his hair and nails were completed, one certainly mourns, since although it is doubtful whether his months were completed, we assume they were completed because he did not die until after thirty days and his hair and nails were completed. The Beit Yosef wrote about this Hagahot Ashri, "I do not know," etc., and an error came from his pen, for the matter is simple as I explained. These are his words. I have not understood his final intent, for the Beit Yosef is speaking here where he died within thirty days, and so too Hagahot Ashri speaks there, saying at the end of Moed Katan: where we do not know whether his months were completed or not, and his hair and nails were not completed, and he died on day thirty, one does not mourn for him. It is forced to say that dying on day thirty is like after thirty, for we rule regarding mourning that day thirty is like within thirty, as Rav Ashi concludes in chapter Yesh Bekhor, beginning of Bekhorot 49. So too the Ramban and Tur explicitly hold that if he died on day thirty, even if his hair and nails were completed, one does not mourn for him where it is not known that his months were completed; and this is the Mechaber's view.
