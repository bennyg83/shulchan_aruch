And the thirtieth day is included. See Chatam Sofer responsa siman 343 regarding an infant who died on the thirty-first day from birth before the hour at which he was born had arrived. One rabbi ruled that one must mourn for him, because we do not calculate hours. Others objected to him: since in any event there is a dispute in Tosafot Arakhin 31, then although the general practice is that for all matters we do not require the period to be from hour to hour, nevertheless regarding mourning, where we rule that the halachah follows the lenient opinion, he should have been lenient. Chatam Sofer z"l discusses this at length and concludes that the rabbi ruled well, and one must mourn in such a case. First, the dispute applies only to years, but regarding days, whether to calculate hours into a day, there is no dispute at all, and according to all opinions a complete twenty-four-hour period is not required. Therefore the sugya in chapter Rabbi Eliezer de-Milah, Shabbat 136a, regarding eight days for an animal, is plainly not a full twenty-four-hour calculation, unlike Peri Megadim above siman 15; see what I wrote in Pitchei Teshuva there s.k. 2. Moreover, Tosafot ask in several places that most women give birth to viable children, and they answer because of the severity of yibbum, etc. Since there itself we do not calculate hours, how could we be lenient and calculate hours regarding mourning? Furthermore, we say that the halachah follows the lenient view in mourning only in a dispute that touches solely on mourning. But where the dispute concerns other matters and laws of mourning branch out from it, and for the other matters it has already been decided that we do not calculate hours, then even though a stringency for mourning branches from it, we do not follow leniency. For if you do not say so, regarding pidyon haben too we should say that if the child died on the thirty-first day before the hour of birth, the halachah follows the lenient party in monetary matters for the defendant, and the father should be exempt from redemption by saying kim li. This is clearly absurd. Rather, since their dispute was not asked regarding money, and so my teacher z"l wrote in Hafla'ah at the end of the first chapter of Ketubot, here too it is the same. Therefore the rabbi's ruling stands without any hesitation; see there at length.
