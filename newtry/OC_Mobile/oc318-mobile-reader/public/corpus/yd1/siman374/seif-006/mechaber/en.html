Whoever mourns for one — one mourns with him if one's relative died and specifically while before him; but if not before him, one need not practice mourning except for his wife — for even though he mourns for her, he does not mourn with her except for her father or mother because of honor for his father-in-law and mother-in-law; but for her brother, sister, or son or daughter from another man he does not mourn with her; nevertheless he cannot force her to adorn herself, but she may mix him a cup, spread his bed, and wash his face, hands, and feet; and likewise she does not mourn with him except when his father or mother died; but for his other relatives who died she does not mourn with him: {Rama: And some say that nowadays they are lenient in this mourning-with-the-mourner, for it is only because of honor for mourners, and now all practice to forgive; and so nowadays they do not mourn at all with mourners (Tur in the name of Rosh, and he in ch. Aveilut Met, and Ramban in Torat HaAdam and Hagahot Maimoniyot ch. 1); and whoever is stringent in this is only among the astonished (Hagahot Maimoniyot ch. 2); nevertheless they practice that all relatives disqualified for testimony for the dead show some mourning themselves all the first week — meaning until after the first Shabbat, when they do not bathe and somewhat change garments like other Shabbat (great ones of Austria practiced thus); and some places practice further stringency in other matters — the main point as I wrote; and all this is for close report or when he was at the death; but for distant report there is no place for this mourning at all (all explained in Torat HaAdam siman 291); one who wishes to be stringent for himself to mourn for one he need not, or wear black for his relative — they do not protest (Rosh klal 20).}
