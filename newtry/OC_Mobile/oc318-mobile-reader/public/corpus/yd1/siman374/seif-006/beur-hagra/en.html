(Likkut) And so she, etc. From [the Talmud's] statement "and so she whose [husband's kin] died" — but the eighteen [rulings] mentioned there were not said for the honor of his wife (only for his father-in-law and mother-in-law); and similarly it is not said "for the honor of her husband" (only for her father-in-law and her mother-in-law). And so it is taught explicitly in the Jerusalem Talmud: "If his father-in-law or mother-in-law died (or one of his wife's relatives — such is our version of the Jerusalem Talmud, and it is the baraita of Avot deRabbi Natan; accordingly one may say that Shmuel corrupts also the final clause, taking the opening clause and meaning it applies equally to the final clause; and the Ramban's words follow his textual version) — he may not compel her neither to apply eye-paint nor to rouge, but conducts himself with her in the manner that she conducts herself; and so too she: if her father-in-law or mother-in-law died, or one of her husband's relatives, she may not apply eye-paint nor rouge, but conducts herself with him in the manner that he conducts himself." Shmuel said: this was said only regarding his father-in-law and mother-in-law, but regarding all other relatives — no. And so too in Avot deRabbi Natan (ch. 11); except that there it also teaches his wife's relatives, and [that text] is corrupted — or one may say it teaches this because he cannot compel her:
And nevertheless it is the custom, etc. But by strict law he is obligated in all seven and thirty days of mourning, as above — "in the manner that she conducts herself," etc. And so too in the Gemara, which says ("he intended to observe seven and thirty for her," etc.): "the Tzidanite fish compelled him," etc. Tohorat HaBayit — end quote.
