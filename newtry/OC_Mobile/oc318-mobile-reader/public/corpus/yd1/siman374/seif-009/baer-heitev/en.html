For him. The Taz and the Shach wrote that there is a scribal error here in the gloss, and this is how it should read: "and the second is still alive after thirty days." This means that one does not mourn for the first, and we do not say that since this one is alive and viable, presumably the one who died was also viable. This is unlike the Levush, who wrote that it refers to the second, that one does not mourn for him even if he died after thirty days, because presumably he too was a nefel; this is certainly incorrect. The Bach and the gaon Rabbi Moshe Charif, of blessed memory, agreed that one certainly mourns for the second who died after thirty days, unlike Maharam of Lublin in his responsum, who also erred in this; end quote, Taz.
