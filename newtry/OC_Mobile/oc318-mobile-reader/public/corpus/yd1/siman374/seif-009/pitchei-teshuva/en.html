Complete months. See Me'il Tzedakah responsa siman 5, who wrote of a woman who immersed for her husband on the twenty-fifth of Nisan and was with him on the twenty-seventh. In the morning her husband left on a journey and remained away until the end of Elul. She became pregnant from the relations in Nisan, her pregnancy became recognizable, and she sometimes felt the fetus while her husband was away from home. Then, on the night of Rosh Chodesh Shevat, she delivered a fully formed child, with hair and nails, and it lived eight days and then passed away. That year Cheshvan, Kislev, and Tevet were all deficient months. They were uncertain whether they were obligated to mourn for this infant: do we require all nine months to be full thirty-day months, such that two days were still lacking, or not? He answered that they are obligated to mourn for him, since it was already known clearly that the lunar months of pregnancy had been completed, with additional days besides; this is called having completed its months.
