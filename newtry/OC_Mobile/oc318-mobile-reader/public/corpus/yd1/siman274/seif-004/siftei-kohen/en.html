There must be, etc., until: if they appear as one, it is invalid. The Bach writes that since the ruling does not state that the scroll is invalid whenever there is less than the width of a small letter between one word and the next, it follows that it is invalid only when the words have been brought so close that they appear as one. If they do not appear as one, and a child who is neither especially clever nor especially foolish reads them as two words, the scroll is valid even though the space between them is less than the width of a small letter. The measure of a small letter is therefore required only for the preferred manner of writing and not as an indispensable condition. Likewise, the measure of a hairbreadth between one letter and the next, and no more, is only the preferred practice. If the letters are separated by more than a hairbreadth, this does not invalidate the scroll unless a single word appears to such a child as two words. Rambam, chapters 7 and 8, plainly supports this explanation. End quote.
