Or when he wishes to roll, etc. In Rosh's responsum general rule 3: one correcting a Sefer Torah — while the ink was still wet he needed to roll the Sefer Torah and took with the quill the written ink so the word would dry quickly and not smear — is there here concern for degradation of mitzvah? There is no concern here, for it is not comparable to lighting a weekday lamp from a mitzvah lamp, for there the second draws from the first and degrades it; but here since the word is written it is not degraded by taking part of the ink so it dries quickly. And even regarding a lamp — if he degraded it without enhancing a weekday lamp from it we find no prohibition in this, but it is forbidden to enhance a weekday lamp by degrading a mitzvah lamp — end quote. These words are very astonishing, for regarding a lamp certainly degradation is not applicable, for when one lights from it its light lacks nothing; rather it merely appears as one who draws light from it, as Rashi wrote at the beginning of "With what may we light" regarding degradation of mitzvah — all the more here where he actually takes ink and certainly degrades it, for the more a letter is filled with ink the more it endures, and if so when he takes from it he certainly degrades it. And I was further astonished at Rosh on this, for there in chapter "With what may we light" Rav said one does not light from lamp to lamp, and he sought to state the reason as degradation of mitzvah, and he remained refuted and concludes the reason is degradation of mitzvah — it is degradation of a mitzvah if one lights a weekday lamp from it; and here plainly it is degradation of mitzvah if ink that was already sanctified will go to waste. And furthermore we may say that here where there is actual degradation, all agree there is concern for degradation of mitzvah; and furthermore we establish that items of holiness are not discarded, as written in Orach Chaim siman 21 — therefore it appears clearly that Rosh did not permit this except by dipping the quill there and drawing excess ink, and writing afterward in the Torah with it. And if this was his intent he should not have distinguished between this and that of the lamp, for regarding a Chanukah lamp we hold like Shmuel that one lights from lamp to lamp even if there were degradation there; and if he would say he holds that here the degradation is greater than from a lamp — as we mentioned, on the contrary he wrote that regarding a lamp there is greater degradation; therefore that responsum of Rosh is certainly astonishing, and at minimum we have no permission here except in the manner I mentioned — to dip the quill there — and not in another manner. As stated, clear and correct.
