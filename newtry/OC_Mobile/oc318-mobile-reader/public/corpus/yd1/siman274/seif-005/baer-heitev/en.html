Ink. It was written by the Bible, but it was not for the sake of anything else, and it was written that even from the name of the Lord was given to him, and that he would not be allowed to write any of the things that he had written, and that he would not be able to do so
To roll. Rosh explained in a responsum: while the ink was still wet he needed to roll the Sefer Torah and took with the quill the written ink so the word would dry quickly and not smear. Taz wrote it appears clearly that Rosh permitted only dipping the quill and drawing excess ink, and writing afterward in the Torah with it; but if ink that was already sanctified will go to waste, it is degradation of a mitzvah. And furthermore we establish that items of holiness are not discarded (and Nachalat Yaakov wrote there is no degradation of a mitzvah at all here, for here one acts for the honor of the Torah that it dry quickly, and this does not apply to "items of holiness are not discarded" — see his words).
