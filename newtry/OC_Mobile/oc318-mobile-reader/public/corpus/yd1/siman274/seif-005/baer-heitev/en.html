To roll. The Rosh explained in a responsum that while the ink was still wet, he needed to roll the Torah scroll, and he took the written ink with a quill so the word would dry faster and not be erased. The Taz writes that it is clear that the Rosh permitted only dipping the quill and drawing up the excess ink, and then writing with it afterward in the Torah. But if some of the ink that had already become sanctified will go to waste, that is a disgrace of the mitzvah; moreover, we hold that tashmishei kedushah are not discarded. In Nekudot HaKesef he writes that there is no disgrace of the mitzvah at all, for here he acts for the honor of the Torah, so that it will dry more quickly, and the rule that tashmishei kedushah are not discarded does not apply here; see there.
