Remove it—the Taz wrote: and he said to him, why should he remove from a written open mem that is attached—for it helps if he drags the tail alone and it remains like a nun, and then writes the tail anew; for here it detracts more, since the scribe's intent appears that he is not concerned for the masoret at all, and certainly for this purpose they wrote it, and it is as forged from within and entirely invalid—unlike where he wrote some error unintentionally, etc.
Punctuation. It appears that if there was punctuation, there is a remedy by scraping it away. This is unlike vowelization, where he creates a defect in the body of the word, changing its meaning between the reading and the tradition; but with punctuation of verses, the meaning of the words does not change. Thus the Taz. He further writes: I have seen in many Torah scrolls that if the Divine Name occurs at the end of a line, they make near the Name two ink lines like this. This is a transgression, for it makes a change from what was in the Torah of Moshe. Therefore those lines must be completely scraped away. See siman 276 seif 1. It further appears to me that a Torah scroll lacking the dots that are supposed to appear in certain places, such as over "elav" in "where is Sarah," or in other places, is not invalid for reading from it, even though it has not yet been corrected, because those dots are only an enactment of Ezra, as Tosafot write in Zevachim, Perek Rabbi Yishmael. This should read: Piskei Tosafot, Perek Kol HaKorbanot, siman 232.
