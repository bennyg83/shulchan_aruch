They appear as one. The text does not invalidate merely because two letters or words are closer than the ideal space of one small letter. That measure is required initially. The scroll is invalid only when, in the judgment of a child who is neither exceptionally clever nor foolish, the two actually appear as one. It follows that if a missing letter at the end of a word is suspended between the lines, it is not automatically invalid as though the suspended letter were part of the line. The practical issue depends upon how the word is read and whether the letters appear joined.
