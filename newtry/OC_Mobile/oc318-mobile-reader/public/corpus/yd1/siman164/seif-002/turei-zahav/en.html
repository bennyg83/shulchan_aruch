If another person came, etc. This is astonishing. He ruled in seif 1 that there are those who permit a mashkanta d'Sura, meaning even leasing it back to the borrower himself. The Levush writes here that the case concerns where the lender did not take possession of the field. This does not appear correct in my humble opinion, for whenever he did not take possession of the field at all, he did not acquire it, and it is merely an ordinary loan, as stated in the Gemara: When did he acquire it, such that he may return it and then lease it back to him? Certainly, the intervention of another person between the lender and borrower is of no effect here. Furthermore, if this were so, the Shulchan Aruch should have explained explicitly that the case concerns where the lender did not take possession, since earlier he did not mention that there is any distinction in this regard. It was only the Rama, who follows him, who mentioned it. Therefore, it appears in my humble opinion that this certainly refers to the first opinion with which the seif began, concerning one who had a field mortgaged to him, etc., which implies even a mashkanta d'Sura. The Beit Yosef explicitly rules in this manner, for regarding this very case he states here that nevertheless, if another person intervened between them, it is permitted according to all opinions. This means after the lender had taken possession of it.
