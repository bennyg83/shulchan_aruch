Mortgaged in his possession. Even for many years. One might have thought that since he has already acquired it with respect to its produce, it would be permitted; nevertheless, it is forbidden for him to lease it back to the borrower, since the borrower owes him money and the lender thereby receives from him more than he lent him. All the more so, if the lender never took possession of it at all, he did not acquire it in any respect, and there was nothing here except an ordinary loan. This is what is stated in the Gemara. It is obvious that this is forbidden according to all opinions even in a mashkanta d'Sura, as the Rama writes below. The Beit Yosef cites a dispute: according to Rashi it constitutes fixed ribbis, whereas according to the Rambam it constitutes avak ribbis. In siman 172, seif 2, it will be explained as a matter of halacha when it constitutes fixed ribbis.
To the owner of the field. The Beit Yosef writes in the name of the students of the Rashba that if another person comes to rent it from the lender, the owner of the field may then rent it from that person, since another person intervened between them, and it is permitted. The Darkei Moshe writes, however, that the responsum of the Rashba cited afterward by the Beit Yosef implies that it is forbidden even where another person intervenes. So too is it stated below in siman 172 in the name of the Rosh.
With a mashkanta d'Sura. It is explained below in siman 172 that this means that he mortgaged it to him in a manner in which he will not need to redeem it from him. Rather, the lender will consume its produce for a specified number of years and will then return it without payment. In this arrangement, it does not bear the status of a loan, but rather of a lease.
