The borrower takes hesset. This is not a migo that extracts money. The Shach explains that correctly, unlike the Sema who says the reason is that he first needed to lie. That reasoning was considered but not accepted. See Beurim: if the lender admits that the note is antedated while the borrower says he never borrowed, then if the lender says the note came from the witnesses into his possession before the loan, the migo does not help because he admits the note is invalid. If he says the borrower handed it to him when the loan was made, then even though he admits the note is antedated, he is believed that the loan occurred even without a migo. If he says the borrower handed the note to him before the loan and he later lent him the money, he is believed only where he has a migo.
