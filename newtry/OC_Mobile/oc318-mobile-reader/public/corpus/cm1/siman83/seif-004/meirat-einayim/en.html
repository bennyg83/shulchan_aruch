The borrower takes an oath of hesset and is exempt. Even those who hold that migo may be used to extract money agree here that the lender is not believed to say, 'I had another document and lost it,' by a migo that he could have remained silent and sued on this authenticated document. Here the lender admits that his initial appearance in beit din was based on an invalid document, and a migo is used to demonstrate the truth of the claim initially advanced, not to replace it with a different claim. Tosafot write this explicitly. It is therefore surprising that the Ir Shushan explains the borrower's exemption simply on the ground that migo is not used to extract money, for this case is the law of the Gemara and applies even according to those who disagree with that general principle.
