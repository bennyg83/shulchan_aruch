The judgment with the word. In the words of the column, he was told to a man:
He was silent when he was taken. The column is not like this judgment, and it is heard by the highest and most embodied in the diaspora:
I will keep them for myself. See siman 125:3, where Rama discusses an agent who retains for himself money the sender gave him to deliver to another, claiming that the intended recipient owes him. The Ir Shushan distinguishes between that case, where the debtor himself sent the money and the agent can say that from the outset he intended to acquire it for himself, and a case where the debtor sent money through an agent to pay several creditors and the agent's initial intention to act for all of them was known. If the agent then gives everything to one creditor, that creditor cannot keep it all, because the agent had already acquired proportional rights in the money on behalf of all the creditors. Even if one creditor seizes it from the agent, it is removed from him and divided among them. The reference to seizure is significant because Ritva rules that if Reuven lends an article to Shimon and Levi seizes it from Shimon without permission, claiming Reuven owes him, Levi's seizure is effective and Shimon must compensate Reuven for negligent guarding. This shows that seizure from an agent or custodian may also be effective in an ordinary deposit. But that rule applies where only Reuven, the depositor, has a right in the property. Here, by contrast, once Reuven delivered the money to Shimon as agent for all the creditors, Reuven's ownership interest ended to that extent and Shimon acquired on behalf of them all, as though each had already received his share. Therefore a single creditor cannot seize the shares of the others.
