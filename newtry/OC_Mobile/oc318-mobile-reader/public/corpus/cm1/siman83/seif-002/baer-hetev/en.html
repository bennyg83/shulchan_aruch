Sent. The wording implies that even if witnesses were present when the agent received the money and the debtor said, "Take" or "Give this maneh to so-and-so," the agent may sometimes retain it for himself. Although siman 125, seif 3 indicates that once the agent admits he originally received it for delivery to the creditor he cannot later keep it for himself because receipt on behalf of the creditor was already effective, this case is different. Here the agent claims that from the outset he never intended to acquire it for that creditor, but intended to acquire it for himself. This works only where it is known that the debtor owes the agent money. Otherwise he may retain it only if he received it without witnesses or under circumstances where he could claim return through a migo, or where the debtor merely said, "Go pay my creditor," language that does not itself effect acquisition for the creditor. Shach.
For myself. The Ir Shushan writes that if a debtor sends one agent to pay several creditors, and the agent gives all the money to one creditor, or one creditor seizes it from the agent, beit din removes it and divides it among them. The Sema cites this, and the Bach agrees. The reason is that once the debtor said, "Give this amount to this creditor and that amount to that creditor," the rule is that "give" is equivalent to acquisition on behalf of each creditor, so the agent acquired each designated share for its intended recipient.
