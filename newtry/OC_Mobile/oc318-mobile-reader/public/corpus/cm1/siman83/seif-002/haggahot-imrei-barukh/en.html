(Shach, seif katan 7) And so it is explicitly in Ramban's novellae, chapter Chazakat HaBatim. Note: However, Ramban's words cited in Shitah Mekubetzet at the beginning of Bava Metzia, s.v. 'and one who says half is mine,' read: 'The law they stated, that we do not say migo to extract money, is possible, but I have a difficulty with it, and in chapter Chazakat HaBatim it will be explained with God's help,' end of his words. It is also evident there from Ramban that even if we generally say migo to extract, there are circumstances where we do not. For example, one claims that another owes him money and the defendant denies it. The defendant possesses utensils ordinarily lent or rented, which he purchased from the claimant. The claimant could have asserted falsely that he merely lent those utensils and, in a case where such a claim would have enabled him to recover the utensils, nevertheless he is not believed on the separate debt claim through a migo that he could have falsely claimed he never sold those utensils. A migo of this kind, and similar cases, certainly is not used to extract money. This is the substance of Ramban's intent; see there.
