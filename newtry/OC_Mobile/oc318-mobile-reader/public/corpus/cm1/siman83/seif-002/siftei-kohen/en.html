Likewise, if he sent, etc. This implies that the ruling applies even where witnesses were present when the agent received the money and the sender said to him, “Bring” or “Give this maneh to so-and-so.” Although below, siman 125, seif 3, it appears that in such a case all authorities agree that the agent cannot retain the money for himself, since he has no migo, as Baal HaTerumot and Tur write there, and as I explain there in seif katan 20, there is nevertheless no contradiction. One cannot answer that our case is different because it is known that the sender owed money to the agent. After all, the reason given there is that the agent is not believed through a migo that he could have said, “I returned it,” because an instruction to “bring” is treated like an acquisition on behalf of the recipient. Rather, the distinction appears to be as follows. There the agent later says, “It is true that I initially received the money in order to bring it to so-and-so, but I will not do so and will instead retain it for myself.” Since he initially acquired it for the recipient, he can no longer retain it for himself. Here, however, the agent says, “I never intended to acquire the money for so-and-so. From the outset I intended to retain it for myself, and I remained silent only so that you would hand me the money.” This is also implied by the wording of Rashba’s responsum cited by Beit Yosef above in siman 58, section 5: when Yehudah demanded payment, the agent said, “I acquired it not through you, but by virtue of my own debt,” and the proof Rashba brings there from the first chapter of Bava Metzia, concerning one who states his intention at the outset, likewise indicates this. See there. This applies, however, only when it is known that the sender owes the agent. In that case the agent may retain the money even though he received it before witnesses. This is also implied by Rashba’s responsa cited by Beit Yosef above in siman 58 and below in siman 125, and Darkei Moshe here likewise speaks of a known debt. Otherwise, the agent may not retain it unless he received it without witnesses, or the circumstances give him a migo that he could have said, “I returned it.” Alternatively, the ruling applies when the sender merely said, “Go and pay my creditor so-and-so,” for such wording is not treated as an acquisition for the creditor, as I write below in seif katan 21 and in seif katan 42 in the name of Raavan.
