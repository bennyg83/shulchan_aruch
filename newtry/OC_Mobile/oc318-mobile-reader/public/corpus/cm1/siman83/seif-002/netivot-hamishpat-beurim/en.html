And likewise if he sent. See Shach, seif katan 2, through his discussion that the expression "bring" can have the force of "acquire." His intent is to ask that if "bring" has the force of "acquire" by Torah law, the agent should be believed to say that he acquired the money for himself even where he lacks a migo. The wording is nevertheless strained. See Tumim, who asks from siman 268 concerning a found object, where if one said, "Acquire it for me," the finder is not believed to say, "I intended to acquire it for myself," in a case where he lacks a migo. There is no difficulty, because there he can say, "I remained silent so that you would give me the money." This applies, however, only to an agent appointed by the borrower. If the lender appointed the agent before witnesses, or told the borrower, "Give it to this agent whom I appointed to receive the money from you," and after receiving it the agent says, "I received it for myself," he must return it to the lender, because he received it on the lender's behalf. By receiving it silently, he discharged the borrower from the first lender, since the borrower delivered it to the lender's agent pursuant to the lender's instruction. Thus the agent, by remaining silent upon receipt, discharged the borrower from his debt, for the lender can no longer collect from him. This is therefore like one who discharges another person's debts, who is liable to pay as explained in siman 76. In addition, it somewhat resembles the rule in siman 58, where one can say, "What have I to do with you, since by your seizure you bring me to loss?" According to the first reason I gave, even if the agent proves by witnesses that the lender himself owes him money, he still cannot retain these funds.
