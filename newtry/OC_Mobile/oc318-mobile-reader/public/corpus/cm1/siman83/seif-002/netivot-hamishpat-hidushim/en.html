But if both are still within their due dates, then if the borrower expressly says that the payment is toward the debt that has a guarantor, the lender cannot say that he received it toward the other debt, since he cannot compel payment of that other debt yet. If the borrower gives payment without specification, however, the lender may say that he accepted it toward the other debt. If the guaranteed debt is still within its term while the other debt is already due, then even if the borrower pays without specification, the lender cannot say that he accepted it toward the other debt. If the borrower transferred land in payment and the unguaranteed debt is earlier, then when the due date arrives the lender may collect that land on the earlier debt and sue the guarantor on the second debt. This is because he could have sold the earlier debt instrument to another person, and if that purchaser later collected from the land, the guarantor would certainly become liable. This is also the ruling of Ketzot HaChoshen and is correct. For the law of an agent appointed by the lender and whether the agent may seize, see Beurim.
