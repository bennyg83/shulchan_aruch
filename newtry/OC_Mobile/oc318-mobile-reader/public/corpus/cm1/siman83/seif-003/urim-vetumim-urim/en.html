If the lender appointed an agent to receive payments on his various debts, and the borrower paid the agent without specifying which debt the payment should apply to, while the agent is no longer present, and the borrower says that the agent expressly accepted the money toward a debt that had a guarantor or the like, the lender cannot object merely from uncertainty. Perhaps the borrower is telling the truth. But if the lender says, 'I expressly instructed the agent to accept payment only toward the debt without a guarantor,' then whatever the agent said cannot worsen the lender's position, because the lender can say, 'I sent him to benefit me, not to harm me.' The lender therefore prevails and is believed because he still holds the debt document. Shach.

Radbaz cites Ritva that if a borrower owes the lender personally and also owes him as a guarantor for another person's debt, and makes a payment, the lender cannot say, 'I applied it to the debt for which you were guarantor.'
