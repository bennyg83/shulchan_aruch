You received them. This proves that if the lender admits he accepted the money for this particular purpose, he can no longer claim that it was applied to another debt. Responsa Ran cited in siman 58 indicates the same, and Responsa Maharshdam 36 and 112 so rule. Maharshdam further writes that where the borrower paid through the lender's agent and the agent is not before us, the borrower is believed without an oath that payment was for this debt because the lender's contrary claim is uncertain. The Shach understands this where the lender admits he fully empowered the agent to receive payment on whichever debt the agent chose. If the lender says he merely sent the agent generally to collect money, he can argue, "I would have agreed only to application toward a debt without a guarantor. By applying it to a debt that did have a guarantor, the agent deviated from my intent. I sent him to benefit me, not harm me," as at the beginning of siman 182. Because the lender still holds the document, the Shach would believe him. See Responsa Mahara Sasson 227.
Orally. The guarantor too became obligated orally at the time the money was advanced, together with a kinyan. Sema.
The guarantor. The borrower has a migo. Even if the original loan was made before witnesses, he can still say, "I paid this debt as well," because one who borrows before witnesses need not repay before witnesses. The guarantor is therefore also exempt, even if his guaranty was made with a kinyan, because whenever the borrower is exempt the guarantor is likewise exempt, as stated at the end of siman 49 and in siman 129, seif 8. Sema.
Kablan guarantor. The Shach finds this difficult because siman 129, seif 15 rules that a kablan is liable. He suggests that perhaps this case differs because the lender admits receiving money, but finds the answer strained. Baal HaTerumot, the source of this rule, appears to hold the same generally, as discussed in siman 129. The matter requires study.
Before witnesses. In the first case, where he did not repay before witnesses, there is no need for two separate debt documents; one document suffices. Two documents are mentioned only because of the latter case involving payment before witnesses. See siman 58, seif 2. Sema and Shach.
