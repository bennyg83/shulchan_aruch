I gave them to you, and you accepted them on that condition, etc. It follows from this that if the creditor admits that he accepted them on that condition, he can no longer claim that the payment was for another debt. This is also evident from the responsum of the Ran cited by the Beit Yosef above, siman 58, see there, and Maharashdam rules this way plainly in responsa, simanim 36 and 112, see there. He further writes there that if the borrower paid through an agent of the creditor, the borrower is believed, without an oath, to say that he paid the agent toward this debt when the agent is not before us, because the creditor is making only an uncertain claim. See there. It appears that this refers to a case where the creditor admits that he appointed the agent with full authority to receive payment toward whichever debt the agent chose. But if the creditor says that he merely sent him to collect his money generally, the creditor can say: I would have consented only to payment of the debt that had no guarantor, while the agent accepted it toward the debt that did have a guarantor and thereby deviated from my instructions. I sent him to improve my position, not to impair it, as explained below at the beginning of siman 182. In my view the creditor is believed in this, since he holds a promissory note. Examine this carefully. See also the responsum of Mahara ibn Sasson, siman 227.
Even if he was a kablan guarantor. This is difficult for me, for below, siman 129 seif 15, the ruling is that a kablan remains liable. One might answer that this case is different because the creditor admits that he received money, but this is strained. Moreover, the Baal HaTerumot, who is the source of this ruling, holds the same rule generally, as I wrote there in siman 129. See there. This requires further study.
If he did not repay him before witnesses, etc. In the case where payment was not made before witnesses, it is unnecessary for him to have two promissory notes. One note is sufficient. The text states that he has two notes only because of the latter clause, which discusses payment made before witnesses.
