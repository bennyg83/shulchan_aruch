If the lender sent an agent to receive repayment on his debts and expressly authorized him to apply the payment to whichever debt he wished, and the borrower paid him, but the agent is no longer present, while the borrower says that the agent expressly accepted it toward the debt that had a guarantor or a similar protected debt, the lender cannot object. His own claim is uncertain, and perhaps the borrower is telling the truth. But if the lender says he sent the agent only generally to receive money, the lender may say, "I sent you to improve my position, not worsen it," and the lender is believed that this was the scope of the agency because he still holds the debt instrument. Here too, the lender effectively says concerning the debt for which he became kablan, "You paid me." But according to the Shulchan Aruch in siman 129:15, where the borrower claims payment on an oral loan the kablan is not thereby exempt, the ruling here is correct. The lender says, "I was not paid on this debt. The payment I received applied to another debt." It is strained to distinguish between saying, "I was not paid," and saying, "I received money but applied it elsewhere." In either case, this debt itself remains unpaid.

Knesset HaGedolah cites Radbaz that if one debt is within its due date and another is overdue, the lender cannot say, "I accepted the money toward the debt within its term," because a person does not ordinarily repay within the term. This is correct. He remains uncertain regarding an ordinary loan without a fixed term, where the default term is thirty days. Since the borrower may ordinarily be believed to say he repaid, perhaps this presumption is not strong enough there. Radbaz also cites Ritva that where one person owes the lender directly and also owes him as guarantor for someone else, if he makes a payment the lender cannot say, "I accepted it toward the guaranteed debt," because a person does not ordinarily leave his own direct debt unpaid while paying a debt arising only through guaranty. This is difficult, because the lender has the right to apply a payment to whichever debt he chooses.
