And they do not know which document. This is difficult from the end of Bava Batra. If a person says, "One document I hold against you is paid," without identifying which, the larger document is treated as paid. Once there is a definite status that one document was discharged and we do not know which, why here may the creditor choose the document to which the payment applies? Perhaps the distinction is that where the debtor paid without specifying, the lender originally possessed the right to apply the money to whichever debt he wished. The debtor who later claims that the lender specifically applied it to one particular document bears the burden of proving that designation. In the Bava Batra case, by contrast, the creditor himself admits that one specific existing document is paid but cannot identify it. There the uncertainty arose after a definite admission of discharge attached to one of the documents. Still, the matter requires further clarification.
