Seif 1: If the lender admits that he received from him the amount of the document, etc. Note: I was uncertain about the following case. Reuven and Shimon were partners and jointly had debts owed to them by one person. Reuven also had a separate personal debt against that same debtor. The debtor came to pay Reuven the debt belonging to the partnership. Reuven received the money and later claimed that he accepted it toward his personal debt, not toward the partnership debt. As a result, the partnership claim was lost because the debtor became impoverished and had no means to pay further. Do we say that Reuven is entitled to designate the payment as he chooses, just as an ordinary creditor who holds several claims may apply a payment to whichever debt he wishes, even though the partnership debt is thereby lost? Or do we say that a partner is different, because a partner is obligated to guard partnership property and may not damage or divert partnership funds? Here the debtor intended to pay the partnership debt, and only because the partner applied the money to his own debt was the partnership harmed. Perhaps Reuven is then regarded as negligent or as one who damaged the partnership. Although I did not find an explicit ruling, there appears to be a proof from siman 176. There, if one partner collects his own share out of a partnership debt, he may in a situation of loss say, “I saved my own share.” Thus, even though the debt itself belongs to the partnership and ordinarily a payment would be partnership property, a partner who collects for himself in a situation of loss can validly treat it as his own share and is not regarded as one who damaged or negligently harmed the partnership. All the more so here, where he is collecting a debt that genuinely belongs to him personally rather than to the partnership. He may say that he collected his own debt and is not considered to have damaged the partnership, because he intended to save what was his. In siman 176 this is limited to a situation of loss because the underlying debt belongs to the partnership and one partner ordinarily may not divide partnership property on his own without the other’s authorization. Here, however, the debt he collects is his own. He may therefore designate it to himself even where no special danger of loss existed. It also appears that in this case he need not have announced before witnesses, “I am saving this for myself.” It is enough that he says he intended so in his heart. Where he collects partnership property and claims he collected only his personal share, his act is not treated as a division unless he states it before witnesses. But where he says that he collected payment of his own entirely separate debt, no declaration before witnesses is needed, and an internal intention suffices. This appears clear.
