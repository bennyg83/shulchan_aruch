If land was appraised for a creditor and beit din subsequently appraised that land for this creditor's own creditor, it is returned to the original owner, because the second creditor's power cannot be greater than that of the first. The second creditor has an advantage in one respect: if it was appraised to the first creditor at one hundred and to the second at two hundred, or to the first at two hundred and to the second at one hundred, the original owner can recover it only for two hundred. If land appraised for a creditor was sold or given away by that creditor, appraised for the creditor with the borrower's consent, or inherited after the creditor died, it is not returned. {Rama: Some say that if the creditor died and it was inherited, it is returned when there is only one heir. But if there were several heirs, they divided the estate, and this land fell to one heir's share, it is no longer returned.} If land was appraised to a woman and she married, or was appraised away from her and she married, the husband has the law of a purchaser regarding his wife's property: he does not return it, and it is not returned to him. {Rama: Some say this applies only after the woman dies.} See note 707.
