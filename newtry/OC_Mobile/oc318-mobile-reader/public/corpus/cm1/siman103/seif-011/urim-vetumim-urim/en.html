And then it was found that the deposit was in possession of the authority in the hand of the clerk to return to Dashum in error, but there was no permission in the hand that the clerk would return if he wanted to carry out his daughter, Dea, who had known and wanted to flee:
Either we commit suicide, and so on, they are both able to cancel the deposit of the depositor and do not know from him that he has committed suicide, and that he has not been condemned by the words of the column, and that he has not been cursed in the same hour as the author of the book of the book, and that he has not been written here
It is not considered an appraisal. It appears there is a distinction between the two cases. In the first case the custodian knew and retained the property. Therefore, even though the appraisal is returned, he need not return produce that he consumed. This was not a mistaken waiver but merely resembles a mortgage without deduction, and such produce is not extracted by beit din. In the second case the custodian did not know. The waiver was therefore made in error and is not a valid waiver. The depositor must return all produce that he consumed. This is straightforward.
But if he later became wealthy, etc. Ramban in the third chapter of Bava Batra holds that if Reuven sold a field to Shimon without responsibility, Reuven's creditor found no other property and foreclosed the field from Shimon, and Reuven later became wealthy, Shimon can compel Reuven to redeem the field from his creditor because appraised property is returned. The field would then return to Shimon. This does not appear from the unqualified language of the decisors. Tosafot there likewise seems not to accept this rule, because they ask for a practical difference in a case where the debtor later becomes wealthy. See Tumim at the end of this siman for the formula of an appraisal document.
