An erroneous appraisal. See Sema, seif katan 28, through his statement that the bailee cannot retract, only the depositor and not the bailee. At first glance this is difficult, for in siman 227, seif 4, in the gloss, it is ruled that even the party who caused the overcharge may retract, although he knew of it. What difference is there between misleading someone as to the value of the sale and misleading him through some other error? In every place where the sale is void as to one party, it is void as to both. It therefore appears that if the bailee gave the depositor money for the object, certainly the bailee too could retract, since there was no acquisition of the object itself. This is comparable to a violent taker who paid money. Certainly even he may return the object and take back his money, because he did not acquire the object. Here, however, there is a different reason. The case is no worse than that of a robber who refused to return the stolen object, where beit din descended to his property and gave land or movable property to the victim after appraisal. Certainly the robber could not afterward return the stolen object and recover the land, because the victim acquired the land through a valid collection by beit din. The beit din collection was properly effected in the land itself, and he acquired it through that collection. But if the robber repents and wishes to fulfill the ruling of beit din, while the victim wishes to return the land so that the robber will restore the stolen object, certainly beit din rules that the victim may return the land and take back his own object, because he accepted the land only because the defendant was acting violently. The same is true here. Since the bailee denied the claim in beit din and beit din awarded the depositor land, it is exactly as if they had awarded land against a robber. The robber cannot undo it, while the victim may do so. But if money was given for the object, even the victim may return the object and take the money.
