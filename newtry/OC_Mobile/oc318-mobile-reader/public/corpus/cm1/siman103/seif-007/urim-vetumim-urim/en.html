If the custom is, etc. Where there is no custom, we follow the condition written in the document, because any monetary condition is valid. Sema. This assumes the condition does not contain an asmachta, as explained below in siman 207. Otherwise the debtor can say, "I assumed I would repay on time and therefore undertook the condition." It appears that a condition can allow sale without appraisal and announcement. But a condition allowing the creditor personally to descend into the debtor's property and seize it cannot be valid as a mere monetary condition. What difference is there whether he personally descends or an officer of beit din acts? The Torah itself prohibited a creditor from taking the debtor's property on his own. This is therefore not merely a monetary rule, and a condition cannot override the Torah prohibition.
