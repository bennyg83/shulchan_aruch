One may not descend upon the property and sell it without them. See above, Tur siman 61:12, where he cites a responsum of the Rosh: Although documents commonly state that the lender has authority to descend upon the borrower's property, one may not transgress Torah law, since the Mishnah teaches that one who lends to another may not take collateral except through beit din. It can be explained that the clause granting the lender authority to descend upon the property applies where he could not find a judge willing to take collateral for him, in which case he has permission to act on his own behalf. See there. I further demonstrated there that because the Rosh and Mechaber make the matter depend upon local custom, they mean that this limitation applies where the custom is not to act according to the literal language of the document. Where the practice is to follow the document literally, we follow it even though it departs from the ordinary law, because every condition concerning monetary matters is valid, and the borrower knowingly accepted it.
