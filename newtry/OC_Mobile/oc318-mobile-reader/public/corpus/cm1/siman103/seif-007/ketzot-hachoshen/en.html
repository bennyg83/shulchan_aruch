A place where the custom is to write in documents that the creditor may descend to the debtor's property and sell without appraisal or announcement. This implies that where such a term is effective, the creditor may act under the document without separate permission of beit din. Tur in siman 61 cites Rosh that even if documents say the creditor may descend to the debtor's property, one may not violate the Torah rule that a creditor does not take collateral except through beit din. In my view the two rules can be reconciled. The Torah restriction concerns seizure for collateral. Actual collection is different. Nimukei Yosef, citing Rabbeinu Tam, permits self help when the creditor is taking property in final collection rather than merely as security. Here the document expressly authorizes the creditor to descend to the property and sell it without appraisal or public announcement. This is plainly a collection provision, not merely a collateral provision. Once the debtor has given the creditor contractual authority to sell, the objection, "Who appraised it for you?" no longer applies. The creditor was expressly empowered to determine and carry out the sale. A monetary condition is valid, and the Torah prohibition against private collateral seizure is not violated. Rosh's responsum can be speaking where the document grants only a general right to descend to the debtor's property, meaning to seize collateral, or Rosh may not accept Rabbeinu Tam's distinction. Our practical law follows Rabbeinu Tam that final collection is permitted in circumstances where mere collateral seizure is not, even regarding food preparation utensils. Therefore where the document clearly authorizes actual sale and collection, the creditor may proceed according to that term. If it grants only a general power to seize property without specifying sale or collection, the prohibition remains and the condition cannot authorize private collateral seizure.
