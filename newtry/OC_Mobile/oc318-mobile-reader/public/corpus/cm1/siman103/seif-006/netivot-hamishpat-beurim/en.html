The principal answer is that of Tumim. These authorities understand the dispute between Abaye and Rava in Pesachim 31a as follows. Abaye holds that a creditor collects retroactively, so if the creditor consecrated or sold the property, once he ultimately collects it the consecration or sale takes effect retroactively. Rava disputes this. According to these authorities, however, if the creditor sold it after the time for payment had arrived and afterward ultimately collected it through appraisal and public announcement, even Rava agrees that the sale takes effect retroactively. Rava disputes only a sale made before the time for payment. Certainly these authorities also agree that without appraisal and public announcement the sale is nothing. This is also evident from Baal Olat Tamid in Baal HaTerumot. I had already reached this conclusion from my own reasoning, and this is the principal ruling.
