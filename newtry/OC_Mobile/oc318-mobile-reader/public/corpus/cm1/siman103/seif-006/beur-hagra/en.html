Land which they made... Kesubos 98a establishes the case where an announcement had been made, yet the widow was challenged, "Who appraised it for you?" See Tosafos there. The Gemara's case where the property rose in value indicates that the same appraisal principles apply to a widow.
If he went down... even... and some say that if... Baal HaTerumos cites Rabbeinu Chananel from Pesachim, where Abaye and Rava dispute a creditor's acquisition of collateral. The implication is that even Rava agrees that ownership arises from that point forward. Rabbeinu Chananel therefore establishes their dispute where the collateral is already in the creditor's possession. Otherwise, if every creditor automatically acquired the property, appraisal and announcement would be unnecessary. This explains the view that where the property was given as a pledge specifically for payment, the creditor may acquire it accordingly. The first view follows Ramban: Rava's reason is that the debtor may still redeem the property, and he retains that right until beit din finally assigns it to the creditor. Only then does the rule of "what is right and good" govern reversal of the appraisal. See Baal HaTerumos. This remains so even with an explicit apotiki, because the debtor himself may redeem it; the stronger restrictions concerning an explicit apotiki apply against purchasers.
