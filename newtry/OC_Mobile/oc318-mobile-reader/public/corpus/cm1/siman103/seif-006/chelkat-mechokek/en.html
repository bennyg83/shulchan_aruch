The heirs cannot take it from the purchaser. It appears the case is where the heirs knew of the waiver but tried to act deceptively and recover from the purchaser unlawfully, thinking that the widow herself bore responsibility. The Rosh says in the responsum, cited later at the end of Tur siman 104, "They acted deceitfully, and their hands shall not succeed." But if the heirs did not know of the waiver, for example, she had waived the ketubah to their father and they believed her, did not swear her, and sold land through beit din in order to pay the ketubah, and only afterward discovered that she had waived it, why should the judicial sale not be void as a mistaken sale? Every sale made in error is reversed, and the purchaser should recover his money. Bach at the end of siman 104 explains the Rosh's responsum differently: the heirs deceived the woman and pressured her until she waived the ketubah. Since beit din had already announced the property for sale, they should have publicized the waiver immediately. Their failure to do so shows that there was mockery or deception, and the waiver is invalid. According to Bach, if they have not yet paid the woman they must pay her. In my view, as stated, the waiver is valid against the heirs but ineffective against the purchaser because their conduct toward him was deceptive. If the widow already took money after the waiver, she returns it; if she has not yet taken it, she receives nothing.
