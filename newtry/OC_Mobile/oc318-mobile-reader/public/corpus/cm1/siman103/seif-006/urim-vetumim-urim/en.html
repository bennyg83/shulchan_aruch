Or it is a mortgage. Sema explains that with an ordinary loan the lender must prove that the due date has passed, whereas with a mortgage he need not do so because he is believed through a migo that he could have claimed purchase. This must mean that the mortgage is not established by witnesses and no public report exists that he holds it as a mortgage, for otherwise he has no such migo, as explained below in siman 149. See Tumim. I wrote that the principal explanation of "or it is a mortgage" is different: without a mortgage he ordinarily cannot sell the land at all, but if the land was given to him as a mortgage he may sell it. This is the view of Rav Chananel Gaon. See Tumim.
He may sell it, etc. One should not object from siman 73, where a pledge appears to require appraisal. Sema says that here too at least an ordinary beit din of laymen is required. Taz distinguishes land from movables. See Tumim for my objections. The correct explanation appears to be that everyone requires appraisal and public announcement. The dispute is only whether, if the creditor sold before appraisal and announcement and afterward appraisal and announcement were performed while the property was already in the purchaser's hands, the sale takes effect. According to the first view it does not, because at the time of sale it was like something not yet legally transferable. According to the second view it is effective because the later appraisal reveals retroactively that the property was properly his to sell. In any event appraisal is required. I further wrote in Tumim that all this concerns selling to others. If the creditor wishes to retain the property for himself, everyone agrees that appraisal and announcement are required, because without them he has no legal hold at all. Rabbi Yehudah Albarzeloni in Baal HaTerumot.
