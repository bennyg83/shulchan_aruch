Siman 375 states that one who enters with permission has the superior hand: if the improvement exceeds the expenditure, he receives the improvement, and no oath is mentioned there. Nor can it be said that he first recovers expenses and then receives a share of the remaining improvement. One who enters with permission is treated like a sharecropper, as Tosafot and Rashbam write in Bava Batra 42, and it is clear from many sources that a sharecropper does not first recover all expenses separately. Bava Metzia 77 distinguishes according to who supplies the seed, showing that where the sharecropper supplies it, the seed is borne from his share. Likewise, Bava Metzia 103 allocates particular expenses between the sharecropper and the owner. Thus, if the improvement exceeds the expenditure, he does not recover the expenditure independently; it is included in the improvement. The oath would apply only where the expenditure exceeds the improvement and he seeks to recover expenses. Here too it is difficult to identify the subject of the oath, because he receives the improvement even if it exceeds the expenses. It therefore appears that in the present case, where the improvement exceeds the expenditure, he receives only the amount of his expenditure. Any excess has the law of improvement arising automatically and would appear as interest. The statement means that he receives the improvement attributable to his expenditure, up to the amount of that expenditure, and concerning this he swears and collects. The phrase “treated like one who entered with permission” is not absolute. If the expenditure exceeded the improvement, he would not recover all expenses, for one who spent money without producing improvement receives nothing, whereas one who entered with permission generally recovers his expenses. The comparison means only that insofar as he recovers expenses, he must swear, and we do not say that because he acted with permission no oath is required.
