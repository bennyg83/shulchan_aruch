If the lender descended into the borrower's property, etc. The Mechaber already stated the basic rule in seif 6. The novelty here is that the fruits the lender consumed are deducted from the debt. Since his entry was unauthorized and conferred no valid right, the fruits count toward repayment. This requires explanation because the Rosh's responsum, cited by the Tur, dealt specifically with one who entered property of orphans before suing them, where collection requires an oath and until he swears he has no right to the property. Perhaps the Mechaber understands that the Rosh spoke even in places where custom or a contractual clause permitted direct entry into the borrower's property without formal appraisal, or according to the opinion that after the term a creditor may descend after appraisal without prior leave of beit din. Even there, with orphans he may not do so before taking the required oath. From this the Mechaber derived the general rule that where no valid custom authorizes such entry, the same applies between an ordinary lender and borrower.
