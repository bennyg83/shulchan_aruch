(Seif 10.) "And the creditor sold it." Although the purchaser is generally no better than the person from whom he derives his rights, that principle does not apply when the creditor sold or gifted the property. Tosafos explains the reason. Ordinarily we say, "Whatever right the first person had, he sold to the second." Here, however, even the first creditor is not strictly required by law to return the property; he must do so only because of the principle of what is upright and good. Therefore the second possessor need not return it. I find this difficult in light of siman 312, where a landlord must notify the tenant thirty days before the term that he wishes him to leave, and if the landlord sells the property the tenant can tell the purchaser, "You are no better than the person from whom you derive your rights. Just as he could not remove me without notice, neither can you." How is that different from here? There too the landlord's duty to notify is not a Torah obligation. One may answer that there the landlord was negligent by failing to notify the tenant. Here, by contrast, the creditor committed no negligence, and the Sages did not extend the equitable requirement to the purchaser.
