The Sema asks that the responsum of the Rosh from which this rule comes concerned property of orphans, and the Rosh's reason was that collection from orphans requires an oath. Before the creditor swears, who says he will ultimately be entitled to collect? The property therefore remains in the heirs' possession. This seems to imply that where the debtor is alive the creditor would keep the fruits, so how can the Shulchan Aruch state the rule generally? Bedek HaBayit says that the incident happened to involve orphans but the same law applies to a living debtor. That alone does not answer the Rosh's express reliance on the oath. The Sema suggests that perhaps the relevant document contained a customary clause permitting entry without judicial appraisal, or that the Rosh followed the opinion above that after maturity the creditor may enter without appraisal. Neither suggestion appears in the Rosh, and according to what I explained above even those authorities do not allow a creditor who keeps the land for himself to dispense with appraisal and announcement. The matter is clear. There is no need for the Rosh to prove the general rule, because the Gemara explicitly says that a creditor does not eat the fruits until the days of announcement are completed. In the Rosh's incident, however, appraisal and announcement had already been completed during the debtor's lifetime. Before the final order placing the creditor into the property, the debtor died. The creditor then entered the property on his own, without authorization of beit din. Had the debtor remained alive, completion of the announcement period would have entitled the creditor to the fruits. But after the debtor's death there remained a possibility that the debtor had paid before the final transfer. The creditor could not collect from the heirs without an oath, and until he swore the property remained presumptively theirs. Therefore fruits taken during that interval were deducted from the debt. Before completion of the announcement period, by contrast, everyone agrees that the creditor has no right to fruits, whether the debtor is alive or dead. Whatever he consumes is deducted from the debt. The Mechaber's general wording is therefore correct.
