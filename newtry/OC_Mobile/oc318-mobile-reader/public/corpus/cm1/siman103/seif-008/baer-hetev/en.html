They deduct. The Sema writes that this requires study. The Rosh stated this in a responsum concerning one who entered the property of orphans before bringing his claim to judgment, because one may collect from orphans only after taking an oath. So long as he had not sworn, he had no right to the property, for who can say that he would indeed swear? The property therefore remained in the possession of the orphans, and the produce he consumed was deducted from his debt. Why then does the Mechaber state the rule here without qualification, implying that it applies to every creditor and debtor? There is no difficulty in light of what the Mechaber writes in Bedek HaBayit: It appears that the Rosh's rule is not limited to orphans. With any person, entry into property without authorization of beit din is not a valid seizure. The Rosh mentioned orphans only because that was the case that occurred. See Responsa Maharshadam, Choshen Mishpat, siman 203.
