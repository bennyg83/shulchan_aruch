We do not listen to him. See Netivot HaMishpat, who writes that nevertheless, if four other appraisers come on their own and say that the property is worth more, we follow the majority of opinions. Otherwise, how could there ever be a case where a sale by beit din is void because they erred in even a small amount? See there. Shaar Mishpat writes that Responsa Rashba in Toledot Adam, siman 361, explicitly implies that even if others come and appraise it at less or more, the original appraisal is not reversed, even if those others outnumber the beit din. Perhaps one can say that the Rashba speaks only where those others who say that beit din erred did not formally constitute themselves together as a beit din. In that case we say that the beit din presumably examined the matter more carefully. See there.
