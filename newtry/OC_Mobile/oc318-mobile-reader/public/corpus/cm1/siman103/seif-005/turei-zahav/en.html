(Seif 9) He does not recover his expenses. Regarding Sema's dispute with the Mechaber in seif katan 15 over who is considered the possessor, the Mechaber's ruling appears correct. There is clear proof from Bava Batra 24. If a city preceded a tree, the tree is cut down and its owner receives no payment. If the tree preceded the city, it is cut down and he receives payment. If it is uncertain which came first, it is cut down and he receives no payment. The Gemara asks from the case of a tree near a cistern, where uncertainty prevents cutting it down at all, and answers: there the duty to cut is uncertain, whereas here the duty to cut is certain. Since the tree certainly must be removed, even in doubt we say that it is cut down, and regarding payment we tell the owner, “Bring proof that the tree came first and collect.” So too here, the appraisal certainly must be reversed, and only the payment is uncertain; we therefore say, “Bring proof and collect.” This is not contradicted by seif 10, where land was appraised to a creditor and Rav Aha and Ravina dispute whether the appraisal is reversed. There the uncertainty concerns the very return of the field, so the purchaser retains it under the rule that we follow the more lenient view. Here the return is certain and only payment is doubtful, precisely like the case in Bava Batra. The Mechaber's ruling is therefore correct. The glossator writes in defense of Sema that the cases are not comparable. There the tree must in any event first be cut before payment, and once it has been cut the owner must bring proof to collect. Similarly, in Bava Metzia 110, when orphans claim that they produced the improvement, they bear the burden of proof because the case concerns an apotiki and the creditor first collects the entire land and improvement, after which he pays them the value of the improvement. He is thus the possessor, as Rashi, Tosafot, the Rosh, the Tur in siman 115, and other authorities explain. Beit Yosef himself writes so in siman 115. The glossator further challenges the Shach's proof and concludes that Sema is correct, because here the creditor is not removed from the land until his money is first paid, and he is therefore certainly the possessor.
