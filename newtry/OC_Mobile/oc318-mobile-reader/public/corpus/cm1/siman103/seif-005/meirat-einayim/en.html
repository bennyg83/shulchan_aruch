If the lender does not wish, etc. This means not only where the lender gives the borrower a choice, saying, "Give me cash equal to my debt, or give me land and I will pay you the surplus," in which case we listen to him. Even if he does not offer the borrower that choice and insists specifically upon money, we nevertheless heed the lender. The Mechaber then concludes that if the situation is reversed, so that less than the minimum useful measure of land would remain for the borrower and the borrower seeks to compel the lender to accept that remainder in cash, we do not heed the borrower. If, however, the borrower offers the lender a choice and says, "Either take this remainder in money, or give me enough land so that I will retain the minimum measure of a field, while the remaining land stays with you against your debt; I will sell the land you give me to someone else and pay you for it," the Tur writes, based on Baal HaTerumot, Shaar 3, that we heed him. The Mechaber concludes that if the lender seized property from a purchaser, we heed the purchaser even in a case where the purchaser gives the lender no choice. This is the meaning of "he can compel him to take it in money," meaning without offering an alternative. This wording alone is not decisive, since the Tur too writes "that he take it from him in money." There is therefore no need to posit a dispute between the Tur and the Mechaber, as though the Mechaber followed Rabbeinu Yerucham, who holds that we do not take account of the borrower's loss, while the Tur holds that we do. Careful reading of Rabbeinu Yerucham indicates that he agrees in the type of case I described. Moreover, the Rosh, in responsum klal 80, ruling 8, cited by Rama at the beginning of this siman in the gloss beginning "If the land does not amount...," also writes that we do not take account of the borrower's loss. It is difficult to say that the Tur would state his ruling anonymously against his father the Rosh without mentioning him. Examine this carefully.
