Responsibility for the sale rests on the heirs. Meaning that if a creditor whose lien predates the ketubah comes and takes the property from the purchaser, the purchaser recovers from assets inherited by the heirs from their father. But a creditor whose lien is later than the ketubah cannot take from the purchaser. Since the widow sold it for the ketubah, the purchaser receives her priority. The Tur advises an widow selling for her ketubah to state expressly that she is selling for the ketubah, lest people say she is selling for support and call her ravenous. If she does not care about that concern, it may be better not to specify, because then she can later collect from purchasers for the ketubah and say that whatever she sold earlier was for support. Conversely, if a creditor seeks to take what she sold, she can say she sold it for the ketubah. See Bach, who cites Tosafot and asks whether this is not deception and falsehood. See above, siman 93:29.
