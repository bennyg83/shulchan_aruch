If the creditor does not wish to buy. The Shach writes that when the creditor says, "Sell me the remaining share or pay me," this resembles the rule of buy or sell. Even if the creditor does not want to own the whole property himself but wants the property sold so that he can be paid, the Shach treats that too as within the same rule, because he could first buy and then resell. This appears contrary to Rosh, cited in siman 171, who says the rule of buy or sell applies only where the party himself wishes to acquire the whole property, not where he merely wants to force a sale to someone else. Rama and the Shach there accept Rosh. Perhaps a distinction can be made. In siman 171 the parties voluntarily became partners with one another. The special rule therefore allows one partner to say only, "Either buy my share or sell me yours," because the partnership was with this person, not with outsiders. An ordinary creditor never entered a partnership with the debtor at all. His right can itself be sold to others. It is therefore more plausible that he may demand sale of the whole indivisible asset when necessary to realize his claim. Still, the distinction requires further consideration.
