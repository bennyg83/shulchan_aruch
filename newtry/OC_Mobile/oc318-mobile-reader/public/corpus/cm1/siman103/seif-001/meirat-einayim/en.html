And afterward they proclaim, etc. The form of the proclamation appears in the Gemara, and the Beit Yosef cites at length from the Baal HaTerumot the language used in the written proclamation. The essential point is that the purchaser receives a document recording that the proclamation was conducted according to law, that the property was sold to him, that the creditor was paid, that responsibility for title to the property remains upon the debtor, and that from that day onward the debtor has no right in the land. See Darkei Moshe. The Tur writes that the proclamation should state whether the sale is being made to pay a creditor or to pay a woman’s ketubah. Some purchasers prefer property being sold for a creditor’s debt because a male creditor may be less exacting about the form of coinage he receives than a woman. Others prefer a sale for a woman’s ketubah because a woman may not want the entire sum in cash at once, since she is not ordinarily engaged in commerce. See the Perishah.
