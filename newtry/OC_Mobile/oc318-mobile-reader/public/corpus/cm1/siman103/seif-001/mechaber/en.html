The appraisal of a borrower's land, the law when an error was made in the appraisal, and the perpetual right to reverse an appraisal. It contains 11 se'ifim.
When three appraisers assess the borrower's land, they appraise it only according to the current time and the price at which land is then sold in that location. {Rama: If the creditor is not entitled to the entire parcel, such as where the debt is one hundred and the land as a whole is appraised at five hundred gold coins, we do not simply give him one-fifth of the land. Rather, we appraise how much of this land or house could be sold for one hundred gold coins and give that portion to the creditor. In general, he is given only something he can sell immediately to collect his debt, without concern for the borrower's loss, as stated in Responsa of the Rosh, rule 80, responsum 9. See below, seif 5 and notes 130 and 506.} The property is then announced for whatever period they consider appropriate, until the bidders cease increasing their offers. If the creditor is seizing the land from purchasers, it must be announced for thirty days, as with the property of orphans; see the beginning of siman 109. The purchasers come and name prices as they wish. If the property brings the amount of beit din's appraisal or more, the money is taken and given to the creditor. If the creditor wishes to take it at that price, he has priority. If it does not bring the appraised amount, it is transferred absolutely to the creditor at the appraised value. {Rama: Others disagree and hold that the same applies when he collects from unencumbered property. Since the property is sold against the owner's will through beit din, it requires appraisal and announcement just like the property of orphans.}
