(Seif 4) For one who forecloses upon a purchaser's property. Sema's distinction, in seif katan 5, between a purchaser and heirs comes from Maggid Mishneh as cited by Beit Yosef. The Tur's words, however, do not imply this. He cites Rambam here regarding one who comes to collect from the borrower's property, which indicates that he understands Rambam's reference to foreclosing upon a purchaser's property as stating a greater novelty: even where the property is forcibly taken from a purchaser, beit din act as his agents, and nevertheless a mistaken appraisal is void. It follows that the distinction is only between heirs and a creditor, whether the creditor collects from free property or from encumbered property. The reason appears to be that with heirs, beit din serves as the father of the orphans and stands in their place, so its act is not characterized as agency. In collection for a creditor, by contrast, beit din acts as agents. This is also clear from Rif as cited by Maggid Mishneh in chapter 22 of the Laws of Creditor and Debtor. Since Maggid Mishneh himself writes that his distinction is not fully satisfactory, it is proper to rule in accordance with the Tur.
