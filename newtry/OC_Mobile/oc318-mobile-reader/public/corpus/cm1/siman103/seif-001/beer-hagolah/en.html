This was explained above in siman 98, seif 9. At the beginning of siman 109 it is explained that announcement is made when property is sold to pay a woman's ketubah or a creditor, as stated there.
This too is explained there. Sema cites Baal HaTerumot that an announcement deed is given to the purchaser, recording that the announcement was performed according to law, that the land was sold, that the creditor was paid, that responsibility for that land remains upon the debtor, and that the debtor has no further authority over the land from that day onward forever.
Tur, seif 7; Baal HaTerumot writes likewise in Shaar 3, and Tosafot write so at the end of Arakhin 21b. However, Tosafot write there that no announcement is required, but the property is placed directly in the creditor's hands at the appraisal value, and the Yerushalmi likewise states this. Beit Yosef writes that we do not rule so; announcement is required, and this is also Rabbeinu's view, because the Bavli concerning the appraisal of orphans' property that was undervalued by one sixth, Ketubot 100b, disagrees with that Yerushalmi. The Bavli establishes the case as involving property for which no announcement is made, etc., rather than as one in which the creditor accepted it at the appraisal price. Baal HaTerumot explicitly writes so in that Shaar in the name of Raavad.
Tur; the Rosh likewise writes in his rulings there in the name of Maharam.
