The sale is void. The reason is that they are regarded as agents, and an agent who errs even by the slightest amount may have the transaction reversed, as stated below at the end of siman 109 and in the laws of agency, siman 182, because the principal may say, "I sent you to improve matters, not to impair them." The same applies if they erred and sold for even slightly more than its value, in which case the purchaser may retract, as explained there. This applies specifically when they collect from property that had been sold, because a purchaser is not obligated to trouble himself to sell property in order to pay the seller's debt. But when they award property to the lender from unencumbered assets of the borrower, their status is not that of agents but of beit din. Their ruling is therefore that, where no announcement was required and they undervalued or overvalued by one sixth, the transaction is void, as stated in siman 109. The reason is that the borrower himself was obligated to make the effort to sell his property and pay the lender. The same applies when they collect from orphans to pay their father's debt, because they too were obligated to sell and pay their father's debt from the assets they inherited from him.
