The sale is void. Beit din is acting merely as an agent. There is no personal payment obligation upon the purchaser, and he can remove the creditor's lien by paying if he wishes. Beit din's power is therefore no stronger than that of an ordinary agent, whose sale is void even for a small error. See Tumim.
The debtor's property, etc. The reason given is that a debtor and heirs have a personal obligation to pay the creditor, for even heirs are obligated to discharge their father's debt. Beit din therefore has enhanced authority on their behalf, and if it errs by up to one sixth its act remains valid. A purchaser, however, has no personal obligation to pay the creditor. Only the land is encumbered, so beit din acts as an ordinary agent, and the purchaser may say, even concerning a small error, 'I sent you to improve my position, not to harm it.' This is the Mechaber's view. See Tumim, where I wrote that this does not appear correct. A debtor should be treated like a purchaser, because he too can give the land directly without beit din, and beit din does not have the same paternal authority over him that it has over orphans. Therefore even a small error invalidates the act with respect to the debtor, while only in the case of orphans does beit din's enhanced authority preserve the sale. This is the primary ruling.
