Land that was appraised. When an appraisal is reversed and the land returns to the debtor, no new kinyan is required. If the creditor is a minor, the debtor need not wait until he becomes an adult. Shach:
He does not receive his expenses. This is a dispute among the authorities, and the Mechaber rules this way because the burden of proof rests upon the party seeking to extract. The Sema writes to the contrary: the creditor is presently established in the field by law, while the debtor seeks its return only by virtue of the principle of what is right and good. The creditor is therefore the one from whom property is being extracted, and the debtor must reimburse his expenses:
He swears and collects. He has the law of other planters employed by the townspeople. Shach, unlike the Sema:
He does not give. Even appreciation that occurred by itself is not paid. But produce the creditor already consumed cannot be recovered from him, since the debtor's right of return is only by virtue of what is right and good:
And it appreciated. According to the Sema, the property does not return even if the debtor wishes to pay its current value. The Shach writes that if he is willing to pay its present value, the creditor must return it:
At a time of high prices. Otherwise the debtor would always wait for the land to rise in value and then pay only the original amount, which is not consistent with what is right and good:
