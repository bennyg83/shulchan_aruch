And if the creditor demolished and rebuilt, but did not increase its value. See Sema, seif katan 15. See Tumim, who asks from below, siman 175, seif 5, where the ruling follows the Rosh that the adjacent landowner must bring proof. The Rosh in Perek HaMekabel explains that the land stands in the purchaser's possession, and the Sages instituted the adjacent landowner's right only because of the principle of what is right and good. Therefore, in a case of doubt we do not remove money. Here too the return of an appraised property is only because of what is right and good, so the Mechaber appears to contradict himself. In my opinion, the Mechaber follows the Taz. Since an appraisal is subject to return, and the doubt concerns only the payments, we say: Go bring proof, similar to the case in Bava Batra 24b. Nevertheless, this land should be no worse than a pledge in the creditor's possession when the parties dispute the amount of the loan. The Shach rules in siman 72, seif katan 94, that the creditor is considered the possessor. Here too, where there is a dispute among the authorities and each party asserts kim li, it is comparable to a dispute over the amount of the loan in which each makes a definite claim, and the holder of the pledge is considered the possessor. The Mechaber holds that the holder of a pledge is considered the possessor only when the land is actually worth the amount he claims. If the pledge is worth less and he can only say, It is worth that much to me, he is not considered the possessor. See siman 72, seif 19, Shach, seif katan 102. Here the reason given by the Shach there does not apply. Therefore, in our siman 103, where the land did not increase in value and is not worth more, the creditor receives only his expenditures against the land. In siman 175, however, the land is worth the amount claimed by the purchaser, and therefore the purchaser is considered the possessor.
