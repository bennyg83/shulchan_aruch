But an appraisal... See siman 175 seif 23.
Whether from encumbered property. Kesubos 91b.
Or from their heirs. Since Bava Metzia says that a husband is treated like a purchaser, it follows that an heir is treated differently and an appraisal may be returned to him, as Rashi and Tosafos explain there.
And some say that a purchaser... They establish the Kesubos case where there was no announcement at the first seizure. According to Rami bar Chama, even when a creditor comes to collect, a purchaser cannot remove him by payment. See Beer HaGolah and below.
And if the creditor... This is like one who spends on his wife's property: he receives only his expenditures, and like one who knowingly bought property that was not the seller's. See Bava Metzia.
And if he improved it. As there, and like the law of a neighboring purchaser in siman 175 seif 6.
And if it improved on its own... and if it decreased. Bava Metzia 108a discusses a rise or fall in value. The same appears in the case of pearls according to the initial assumption that the reason is return of appraisal.
And some say... They distinguish a neighboring purchaser, who is treated like the owner's agent under the principle "I sent you to improve matters," including later resale. Nimukei Yosef explains this and the Rama records it in siman 175.
And if they acquired... As with a neighboring purchaser. See Nimukei Yosef there.
He assigned it. There, "he assigned it," as Rashi explains.
