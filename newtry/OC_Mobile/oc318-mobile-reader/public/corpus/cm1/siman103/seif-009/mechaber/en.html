If beit din appraised land for a creditor, whether it was unencumbered property of the borrower or encumbered property held by a purchaser, and at a later time the borrower, the person from whom it was seized, or their heirs obtain sufficient means and bring the creditor his money, the creditor is removed from that land even if he held it for many years. {Rama: Some say a purchaser cannot remove the creditor after the creditor collected the land, because an appraisal is reversed only for the original owner when the creditor collected unencumbered property, not for a purchaser.} If the lender demolished and rebuilt but did not thereby improve the land, he is not reimbursed for his expenses. If his expenditures improved it, he swears and collects as one who entered with permission. If it improved by itself, such as through an increase in value, he receives only the amount of his debt. {Rama: Some say that if he spent money and improved it, the appraisal is not reversed at all. If it increased in value, it is reversed only at its current higher value.} If the land declined in value, the lender does not bear the loss. To remove him, they must give him the entire amount of his debt. If they made a kinyan with him that he would not be removed, they can no longer remove him. {Rama: If the borrower voluntarily transferred the land directly to the lender without beit din, it is not returned to him.} See notes 4, 8, and 41.
