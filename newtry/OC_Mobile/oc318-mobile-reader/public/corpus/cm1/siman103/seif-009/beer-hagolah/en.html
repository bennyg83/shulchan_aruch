The language of Rambam, chapter 25 of Hilkhot Malveh. Maggid Mishneh writes that this is the halachic ruling in HaMafkid, Bava Metzia 35a: an appraisal is always reversible. Tur writes likewise.
Maggid Mishneh also writes there that, according to Rambam, for this reason he carefully wrote that we remove the creditor from that land. Ramban writes likewise, and anyone who does not judge this way in such a case is not considered a judge at all.
Maggid Mishneh writes that this also appears from the incident of the two mansions in Mi SheHayah Nasui, Ketubot 91b. This is the principal ruling, and it is also the view of Baal HaTerumot in Shaar 3. In my humble opinion Ran there inclines to this view, for he resolves, according to Rambam, the passage in HaMafkid, 35a, which says that a husband has the status of a purchaser, etc., as speaking where the appraisal was made before she married.
Shitah Mekubetzet likewise writes in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah, explaining that because the purchaser acquired the creditor's lien, the rule of "You shall do what is upright and good" does not apply to him.
