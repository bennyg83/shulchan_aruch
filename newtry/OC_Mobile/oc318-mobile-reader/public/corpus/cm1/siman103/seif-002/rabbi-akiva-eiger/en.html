[Rama] And some say that a purchaser cannot remove the creditor by paying him money. Note: Gedulei Terumah also infers from the words of the Maggid Mishneh that even regarding the debtor’s heirs, some maintain that when property was appraised away from them it is not returned. See there. The Rosh’s position, that property appraised away from a purchaser is not returned to him, requires analysis in light of the Gemara’s case where property was appraised away from a woman and she married and died, in which case it is not returned. Why did the Gemara not teach the greater novelty that even if property was appraised away from her after the marriage, for a debt predating the marriage, it is not returned, since it is not returned to a purchaser? See Shach, seif katan 19. The difficulty can be resolved. Examine this carefully. My son-in-law, Rabbi Hirsh, showed me that this proof is already mentioned by Ramban in his novellae on the sugya of the cow and the cloak, and I rejoiced that, with God’s help, I had independently arrived at it.
