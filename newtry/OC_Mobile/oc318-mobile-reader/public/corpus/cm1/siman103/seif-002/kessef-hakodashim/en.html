Seif 3. (Three appraisers assessed it.) Since an appraisal is an estimate rather than ordinary testimony, the rule that a matter is established by two witnesses may not apply to it in the same way. The appraisers may therefore be able to say that they erred in their estimate and that the property is worth more or less than they first stated. Their later appraisal may then control. Alternatively, additional experts can be added and the majority of expert opinions can be followed against the first assessment. If no other experts are available, the later statement of the original appraisers may displace the first. This is certainly so where the appraisers did not initially testify directly before a fixed beit din of three, but rather a court officer reported their appraisal to beit din, as is commonly done. In that situation they can plainly return and state the correct appraisal, even if they had previously confirmed to beit din that the officer accurately reported what they had said. There is then no need to add further appraisers merely because correction is easily possible.
