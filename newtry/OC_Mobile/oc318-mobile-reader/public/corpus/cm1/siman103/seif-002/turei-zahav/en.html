(Seif 6) If the period passed. Sema's question from a pledge of movable property presents no difficulty. Movables are intended for use and consumption, and the sale could not be treated as final at that time because the purchaser might dispose of them and later be unable to establish their value if it is claimed that they were worth more. Land is different, for it remains before us, and the purchaser can prove that the sale was lawful and that the land was not worth more. Sema's suggestion that the present case involves three appraisers is puzzling, for the authority would certainly not have omitted such a condition.
