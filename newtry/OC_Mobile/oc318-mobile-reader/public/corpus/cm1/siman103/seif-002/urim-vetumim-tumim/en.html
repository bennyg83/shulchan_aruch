It is assessed at one hundred ten, which is the intermediate amount. The Sema writes that most authorities hold that it is assessed at one hundred, following the majority of appraisers who say it is worth no more than one hundred. I found proof for this in Ketubot 100. The Gemara says that an agent's error of any amount invalidates the transaction, unlike an error by beit din, because beit din consists of several people while an agent is one person. According to the Rambam's view here, however, the presence of three would not explain anything. Two appraisers expressly say that the property is worth only one hundred. Only because of the third one's estimate does the final value become one hundred ten. The error is therefore attributable to that one person, while the other two did not err at all. What meaningful distinction would then remain between beit din and a single agent merely because the mistaken estimate occurred within a group of three whose other members disagreed? It follows that the majority valuation governs, as Rashbam holds. This is clear practical law, as the Sema also rules.
