We do not tell him: give him one fifth of the land, etc. Responsa Tzemach Tzedek, siman 27, writes that this applies when the creditor collects from the borrower himself. We strengthen the creditor's position by requiring that a separate parcel equal in value to his debt be appraised for him, without concern for the borrower's loss, because of the policy against closing the door to borrowers. But where there are later creditors, the earlier creditor may, according to Tzemach Tzedek, collect only one fifth of the land, because we are concerned for the later creditors' loss, as seif 5 shows that we are more concerned for a purchaser's loss than for the borrower's. Shaar Mishpat disagrees. Since one fifth of the land is not worth the amount of the debt, taking it is less than collection of the debt and is not a valid collection at all. He concludes that whenever the debt itself must be collected by appraisal, there is no distinction between collection from the borrower, from a purchaser, or in the presence of later creditors. The Chacham Tzvi, in the Lviv edition, additions at the end, siman 28, likewise rejects Tzemach Tzedek and calls the distinction mistaken. In seif 5 the purchaser suffers a loss whereas the foreclosing creditor does not; here, why prefer the loss of the later creditors over that of the earlier creditor? Until the earlier creditor receives his debt, the later creditor has no right at all. The earlier creditor must therefore receive a portion of the house that he can sell immediately and from which he can recover the full monetary amount owed, rather than having to wait until a buyer appears for the entire house.
