(Seif 7.) "If the custom is not to descend to the debtor's property." The words of our master imply that even in a place where there is no custom nullifying the law, one does not follow a clause in the document allowing the creditor to descend to the property without beit din. Responsa Rosh, rule 64, law 13, appears otherwise. He first writes that we follow the wording of the document, but in that particular case he says that the creditor cannot act as he wishes, etc. The Mechaber's wording here does not appear to follow this. Nevertheless, it seems that even according to the Mechaber, the wording of the document is effective wherever there is no contrary custom. This is unlike a clause saying that the parties will litigate under non-Jewish law, which is ineffective everywhere, as explained in siman 26, seif 3. Here the restriction exists only because the Torah did not authorize a creditor to descend to the debtor's property on his own. Since the debtor expressly accepted that the creditor may do so, the stipulation can be effective. By contrast, parties cannot stipulate to employ non-Jewish law in a matter that constitutes a transgression against Hashem.
