The lone opinion is nullified by the minority. Shaar Mishpat writes that the wording is not limited to a single dissenter. If there are five appraisers, two appraise at two hundred and three at one hundred, the majority likewise controls. This is explicit in Shevuot 42a and Temurah 27b, where valuation follows the majority of opinions. The latter passage also indicates that if three already made an appraisal and four later appraised at a higher amount, the appraisal is revised. He adds, however, that if a beit din of three appraised movable property of orphans for collection by a creditor, and four later appraise it at a lower amount, the original appraisal is not reversed. He derives this from Temurah and calls it a new and correct rule; see there.
