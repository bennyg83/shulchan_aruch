There is no man forbidding anything. P. There is no way to prohibit this “even though they have heard from his mouth that he intended for the miracle of Hashem’s promise to Abraham, and that he does not believe in his vision, and that he will be blessed with him.”
If he had a partnership share in it, etc. See Yoreh Deah siman 4, where it is explained that if one slaughters another's animal for idolatry, we also say that perhaps he intended only to distress the owner. If he had a partnership interest in the animal, the Tur writes there that according to the Rambam the animal is prohibited, like the ruling here regarding wine, while according to the Rosh it is not prohibited. Here the Tur states without qualification that the wine is prohibited. It appears there is a distinction. Wine is divisible, and since the partner could have first taken his own share and afterward distressed his fellow by declaring that he poured the rest for idolatry, we do not presume that his declaration was merely to cause distress. By contrast, a live animal cannot be divided before slaughter, so the same reasoning does not apply. In the Derishah I gave another reconciliation.
Or if he was an apostate, etc. In such cases we presume he actually intended to pour the wine for idolatrous use, whether because he is an apostate or because he had been warned beforehand.
