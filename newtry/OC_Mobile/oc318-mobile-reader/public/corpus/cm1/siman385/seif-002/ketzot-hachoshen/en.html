For he is like a gentile. Beit Yosef, in Yoreh Deah siman 4, explains why the Gemara establishes a case with an apostate Jew rather than a gentile: a gentile does not obey Jewish legal judgment. Another explanation is possible. Eruvin teaches that a Noahide can be subject to death even for theft of less than a perutah and that such theft is not subject to ordinary restitution, Rashi explaining this through the principle that his warning itself is his capital liability and therefore the more severe liability blocks monetary payment. If so, in the case of one who renders another's wine idolatrous by lifting it, the reason for monetary liability is that he first acquired the wine through lifting. A Noahide's lifting in the course of that transgression could likewise be subject to the more severe liability, eliminating the monetary payment according to Rashi. Tosafot, however, holds that the principle of the more severe punishment blocking the lesser was stated only for Jews and does not apply to a Noahide. According to Tosafot, this alternative explanation would fall away. The Gemara's own sequence also favors establishing the case as a Jew. It explains why impurifying, mixing, and rendering wine idolatrous each had to be taught, including a possible objection from the more-severe-punishment principle. If the case were a gentile, that discussion would not fit according to Tosafot. Thus the Gemara's choice of an apostate Jew is understandable.
