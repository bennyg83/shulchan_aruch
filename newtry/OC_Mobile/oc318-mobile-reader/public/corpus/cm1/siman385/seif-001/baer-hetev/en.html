For example. See the Shach, who explains that the Rambam rules like Rav, that one may derive other cases from the rabbinic penalty, as shown by his general formulation, "one who damages another's property," and by his conclusion, "and similarly in every comparable case." By contrast, Ramban in Dinah DeGarmi rules that the halachah follows Shmuel in monetary law and that one does not derive other cases from a penalty. According to Ramban, liability is limited to rendering terumah impure, mixing prohibited produce with permitted produce, and libation, and does not extend to other cases. This is also the view of Rav Hai Gaon in Mishpetei Shevuot, and of the Rif and Rosh at the end of HaChovel. A reason and proof for the Rambam appears from Chullin 40a, where the Gemara challenges from the case of libation, indicating that the halachah follows Rav. See above siman 382 concerning one who preempted another in covering the blood, and below siman 386 regarding authorities who hold that dina de-garmi is a penalty and nevertheless derive comparable cases from it. Shach.
From the best quality. Although the liability is rabbinic, the Sages fashioned it after Torah law and required payment from the best land. So writes the Rosh at the beginning of Bava Kamma. The Sema writes that siman 419 explains that this applies only when payment is made from land. With movable property, anything is considered "best quality," even bran, because if it cannot be sold here it may be taken elsewhere and sold there, unlike land.
After him. The Sema explains that the son is exempt even if the father had already stood in judgment, and compares this to siman 108, seif 2, unlike the case of an informer who died in siman 388, seif 2. In my view this is incorrect. In siman 388 the reason is that the first opinion holds dina de-garmi to be strict law rather than a penalty. The alternative opinion there, which imposes liability only when the father had already stood in judgment, holds that dina de-garmi is a rabbinic penalty and that the Sages did not penalize the son after the father unless judgment had already been rendered. The same should apply here: if he had stood in judgment, the son is liable. This is explicit in the Mordechai in HaChovel in the name of Maharam, who is the source of that alternative opinion, and in Responsa Maharam. They write that the Sages did not penalize the son, as stated in Moed Katan 13 regarding one who rendered produce impure, mixed it, or made a libation. The comparison to siman 108 is inapposite, because there the debtor had been ordered to pay more than the actual loss. Indeed, at the beginning of that seif the Mechaber writes that when one became monetarily liable in beit din because of informing and then died, collection is made from his heirs. Thus the law is as I have written. Shach.
