Seif 1. 'One who damages his fellow through damage that is not visible.' Note: In chapter 5 of Gittin, in the sugya of one who renders terumah impure or mixes prohibited produce, the Gemara says regarding one who pours libation wine that the rule of the more severe punishment exempts him from payment. Rashi explains that he thereby performs idolatrous worship 'and is stoned.' The word 'is stoned' is difficult. Rashi should have written 'and is liable to stoning.' The expression 'is stoned' implies that only if he is actually executed does the exemption from payment apply, but that is incorrect. First, Ketubot makes clear that one liable to capital punishment is exempt from payment even without warning or when acting unwittingly. Second, even if Rashi were following the view that unwitting capital offenses do not exempt from payment, why would he not explain the case as an actual deliberate libation without prior warning, in which case payment would remain while execution would not occur? Necessarily, this opinion must hold that even unwitting capital offenses exempt from payment. Rashi therefore should have written 'and is liable to stoning.' This requires study.
