Ten barren areas per se'ah. This is the Rambam's version in chapter 8 of Hilchot Sechirut, and the Maggid Mishneh writes that the Rambam's teacher, Ibn Migash, had the same reading in the Gemara in HaMokher Peirot. In his explanation, Ibn Migash says this means ten barren rows per se'ah, or alternatively ten barren plantings. The Mechaber follows the Gemara and Rambam according to that version. The Tur, however, writes: the owner accepts ten barren trees per hundred. That is also Rashbam's version. Rashbam explains that a worker who undertakes to plant another's entire field in the ordinary density, for example ten plantings per beit se'ah, is allowed ten barren trees per hundred. If ten trees fail to produce fruit, he need not replace them, because it is normal for some plantings to fail and the owner waives that amount. If there are more, such as eleven per hundred, the worker must replace all eleven, because beyond the tolerated amount there is no waiver. According to the Rambam's version, ten barren units per beit se'ah, the case must involve a planting pattern with far more than ten trees in a beit se'ah. Otherwise, if all ten were barren, nothing useful would remain, which is unreasonable. This difficulty may be why the Maggid Mishneh explained one possibility as ten barren rows in a se'ah, meaning that a typical field is arranged in rows and even if ten rows in each se'ah were left entirely unplanted or unsown, the owner must tolerate them. His second explanation is ten barren saplings. He deliberately says saplings, because mature trees are not planted in such large numbers within one beit se'ah. With young plantings, when a planter sees no sign of success in some of them he may leave them and plant others. The ruling would teach that even if he damaged the field by leaving ten failed young saplings, the owner must accept that. This second explanation is strained. Rama writes on the Mechaber, "and some say, per hundred," implying that there is no disagreement about what "barren" means or what the owner must accept, but only about the numerical measure. Darkei Moshe similarly paraphrases the Maggid Mishneh's explanations as ten rows of barren trees per se'ah or ten trees, not distinguishing young saplings from mature trees. This shows that Rama understood the Rambam and his teacher as discussing a field planted at a much denser rate than Rashbam's case. That appears the sounder interpretation.
