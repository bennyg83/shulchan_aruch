The landowner takes one half, etc. The Tur explains the calculation. This is in a place where landowner and planter normally divide the improvement equally, while a sharecropper receives one third of the improvement. If the total improvement is twelve dinars, the landowner takes his usual half, six. From the planter's half, the sharecropper takes four, which is one third of the total improvement. Two dinars remain for the planter, one sixth of the total twelve. The reason an original planter ordinarily receives more than a sharecropper is that the planter establishes the trees in the garden or field from the beginning, while a sharecropper merely enters an already planted field and tends it.
Nevertheless, he cannot withdraw, etc. Nimukei Yosef writes that he cannot place a sharecropper in his stead and cannot say, "I will take one quarter of the garden or field and work it myself, while you hire a sharecropper to work your three quarters, give the sharecropper one quarter, and one half will remain yours." The reason is that all portions of the arrangement are legally bound to one another. Darkei Moshe cites this.
