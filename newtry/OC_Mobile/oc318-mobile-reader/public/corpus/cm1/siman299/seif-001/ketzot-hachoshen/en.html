If a deposit was entrusted to two people, it is like a loan to two. Rama elsewhere writes that two custodians who accepted a deposit together are not necessarily guarantors for one another unless they expressly stipulated. Here and in siman 77, however, the Shulchan Aruch and Rama imply that two people who jointly accepted a deposit have the same mutual guaranty as two joint borrowers. The case of one who entrusts an ox to five people does not contradict this. There the Gemara discusses the ox escaping and damaging others after one guard withdrew. The joint guaranty exists toward the owner of the deposited ox, not toward third parties injured by it. Regarding outside damage, each negligent guard is liable only for the portion attributable to his own failure. Therefore the rulings are consistent. If the deposited animal itself is lost, the owner can invoke the custodians' mutual guaranty. If it escapes and injures another person, the injured third party cannot treat all custodians as guarantors for one another. That is why siman 396 speaks only of the negligent custodian's liability for the damage caused outside.
