Produce. The general principle is as follows. A husband with rights in his wife's property, or one who gives another the corpus of property effective from today while reserving its produce to himself until death, and similar cases, may wish to sell or lease future produce to another. Since the produce has not yet come into existence, it cannot be transferred directly. Rather, he must say, "Acquire this land for its produce," as the Tur and Mechaber write in siman 209. Here the law teaches that such a person has no authority to transfer the land itself to another so that the other can acquire the produce by way of sale; he may transfer only by way of lease, for there is a legal distinction between sale and lease, as stated in siman 212, seif 6. Although these people hold the field as though they acquired it for its produce, they may not introduce another person into that right, because the owner of the land trusted them and not another. Although the next siman states that a tenant may sublet land to another, that applies specifically to rental of a house, as explained in siman 212; see there and seif katan 13.
