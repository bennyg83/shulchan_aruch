Unless he owns only the produce rights, etc. In the Perishah I explained Maharik's words. The general rule is this: a husband regarding his wife's property, or one who gave another the corpus from today while retaining the produce until death, and similar persons, may own only the right to future produce. If one of them wishes to sell or lease those future produce rights to another person, he cannot transfer the future produce by itself because it does not yet exist. He must say, "Acquire this land for its produce," as explained in siman 209. The ruling teaches that such a person has no authority to transfer the land itself to another in the stronger form of a sale for produce, but only in the more limited form of a rental. The Rambam, Tur, and Mechaber above in siman 212 explain the difference. One who acquires a field for its produce may plant, sow, or leave it fallow as he wishes. A renter does not have such broad powers. Here the person holding only produce rights is treated as though he acquired the land for its produce with respect to his own use, but he cannot bring another person into that full right. The owner trusted him, not an outsider. Although the Tur and Mechaber shortly write that a renter of a house may sublet it, I explained there and in the Perishah that this is specifically with a house, where the renter receives possession under a deed or witnesses and must return the same structure intact. A field is different. A subuser might sow it beyond its proper measure, work it continuously without leaving it fallow, plant legumes where wheat should have been planted, or otherwise weaken the land substantially without the owner knowing. Although siman 212 says that one who purchases land for its produce can transfer that right again, perhaps one who explicitly sells such produce rights has accepted that the purchaser may use them however he wishes, giving that purchaser more power than these limited holders. Alternatively, even there "transfer it again" may mean to transfer it in the form of a rental, not an outright sale of the full produce right. See the Perishah.
