Unless he has fruits. Sema, seif katan 8, attributes to Rambam a novel rule that one who purchased a field for its fruits cannot sell his right. Although one who purchases a field for a fixed term certainly can transfer his interest, Sema distinguishes that such a time-purchaser may alter the body more extensively, while one who purchased only fruit-rights may not. In my view this is incorrect. Rambam explicitly distinguishes a renter from one who purchased a field for its fruits: the renter may be restricted from subletting, while the fruit-right purchaser may transfer everything he acquired. Tur cites this language, and Taz says the purchaser can even transfer the use to many occupants. Rambam's real distinction concerns a person who owns only the future fruits themselves, not a proprietary interest in the field for its fruits. For example, one sells an object but reserves the fruits of a particular field for several years, or reserves a right to dwell in a house. A person can create such a reservation in a davar shelo ba la'olam. But the reserved future fruits or bare dwelling-right cannot themselves be sold, because one cannot sell a davar shelo ba la'olam or an intangible right. Nevertheless, the holder may be able to rent out that fruit or dwelling use. Ordinarily, whatever cannot be sold cannot be rented. But future-use rental is different because rental functions as a personal obligation upon the landlord to provide the use. This is proved by a generic-donkey rental, where the landlord can become obligated to provide a donkey even if he owns none at the moment; a purchaser of a generic donkey in the same situation acquires nothing. Thus rental can operate through personal obligation even where sale cannot. This raised a further doubt for me. If one rents "this donkey" while it is not presently his, must he provide it once it later enters his possession? In sale, both "this field" and a generic future field fail when the property is not yet his, while a personal obligation can work. Since rental combines a present personal duty with future use, perhaps even "this donkey" can become effective when it later comes into his possession. The matter requires study.
