Rental of land. While land is presently leased, the owner cannot lease it to another for a period beginning only after the current lease unless he uses a formulation effective from now for later and an appropriate kinyan, such as money. If, however, he leases it for the future to the present tenant, although from the landlord's perspective the property is currently not in his possession for use, the tenant can transfer his present leasehold right back to the landlord, who could then create the future lease immediately. Since the matter lies within the power of both parties, perhaps the new lease is effective now and is not treated as acquisition of something not yet in existence. One cannot prove otherwise from the rule that betrothal with the woman's own stolen property is ineffective, because there she did not expressly transfer the property back to the man. Here, where present control can be restored and immediately leased, the issue is different. This requires further study. If after several days in a rented house there was a recognized commercial custom permitting withdrawal from the lease, and the tenant then remained in the house without withdrawing and merely said that he was not presently retracting, that may not constitute a new kinyan establishing an absolute lease. Even though continued possession in the landlord's presence can operate as chazakah without a separate instruction, he did not expressly state that he was now acquiring the lease irrevocably. His words may merely indicate that he was not exercising his right to retract at that moment. This requires careful study.
