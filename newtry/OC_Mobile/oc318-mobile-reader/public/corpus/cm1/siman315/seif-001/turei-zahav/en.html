(Seif 2: unless he has produce.) The Sema's explanation is unclear to me. Through rental rights, this person can place another in his stead. Why, then, should we be concerned with the idea that the owner trusted only him? In the cases listed, such as a husband, the holder necessarily owns nothing in the body of the land. How then can he later sell and say, "Acquire the land for its produce"? It therefore appears to me that this is Rambam's essential point. Whenever a person acquired no right in the land itself, only in its produce, he cannot sell the future produce because it is property not yet in existence. But through a rental arrangement he can effectively transfer his own position, meaning that he withdraws and another enters in his stead. A person can waive and stipulate regarding a future right, as explained in siman 209. Therefore rental works properly: this person withdraws, the replacement enters in his place by virtue of the rental payment, and occupies the same position. The Sema's distinction between a house and a field is also extremely strained. I discussed this in siman 212.
