Rental of land. The Mordechai, as cited by Sema, teaches that a chazakah cannot effect a future acquisition unless it is made in a manner capable of taking effect immediately, unless one says 'from now and after thirty days.' The same principles known from sale apply to rental. Mishneh LaMelech records a dispute whether, while a house is still rented to a first tenant, a second tenant or buyer can acquire rights for after the first term by chazakah, and whether the first tenant's permission helps. The question concerns a present acquisition effective after the term; without 'from now' a chazakah ordinarily cannot take effect later. By money, however, many authorities hold that a landlord may sell or rent for the period following the existing tenancy, and cannot retract, because the passage of time comes automatically. Netivot HaMishpat adds that the property must be identified, and that an ordinary present rental is construed as beginning now unless the parties expressly suspend the acquisition. Beit Yaakov disputes this and holds that transactions made while the first tenant still possesses the house may be retracted before the new term begins, relying on the Mordechai that chazakah requires a present ability to acquire. Other authorities, including Netivot HaMishpat, Ateret Tzvi, and Divrei Mishpat, reject or limit that view, especially where the transaction is expressly from now or established commercial custom gives effect to it. See the cited responsa for the extensive discussion.
