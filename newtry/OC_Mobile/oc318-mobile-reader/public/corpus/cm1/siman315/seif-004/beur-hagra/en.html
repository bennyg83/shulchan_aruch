Reuven, etc.; because, etc. Bava Metzia 76b teaches that when craftsmen were hired and one party deceived the other, etc.; but if the donkey drivers already traveled, etc. That case clearly involves no formal kinyan, because the opening clause says they have only resentment against one another, and the entire passage proceeds this way. Likewise, Yevamot 106a relates the case of Rav Pappa's relative, where he said, 'Give it to her.' This is not enforced as a condition, since conditions do not operate in chalitza; rather, it is enforced as compensation for services. The chalitza remains valid, and absent the defense that she was merely putting him off, payment would be due just as to one who rescued another. See Rivash 476.
