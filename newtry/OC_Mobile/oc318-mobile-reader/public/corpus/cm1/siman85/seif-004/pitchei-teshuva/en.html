You should have collected your debt. See Baer Heitev, which notes that the Shach argued at great length against this ruling. Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat siman 60, writes in his case that he would not rely upon the Shach's view in siman 85 seif 3 that each party always collects from the other. First, the Shach's case concerns one who actually borrowed from the other at that time, whereas Chatam Sofer's case was considerably weaker because no new loan was then made; rather, a promissory note was given without a loan, years after the original loan had already become due. The reasoning of Baal HaMaor therefore does not apply. Moreover, Chatam Sofer writes that even independently he would not rely upon the Shach there, because the Shach did not have before him the words of the Ramah cited in Shitah Mekubetzet to Ketubot 110a, a work not yet published in his time. Those words were also unavailable to Tumim. Therefore, Chatam Sofer concludes, one should not depart from the ruling of the Shulchan Aruch and Rama.
