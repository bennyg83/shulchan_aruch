Another time has not yet come. The time of the time of his book:
Even if it contains a credibility clause. I explained in Perishah that this resembles one who produces a document containing a credibility clause and the other party brings witnesses that he paid in their presence. The document is then weakened and collection cannot be based on the credibility clause. So too here, the circumstances themselves testify that payment already occurred. Even a clause granting credibility equivalent to two witnesses would not seem effective against such a presumption, because such enhanced credibility does not prevail in every circumstance, as explained at the beginning of siman 71.

I sold you the field only, etc. This is also the Tur's wording. I explained in Perishah why the argument is not simply, 'I needed money.' The other party could answer: You should have sold to someone else rather than to me, thereby avoiding any later opening for such a claim. See there further.
Only so that I would have, etc. Shimon cannot answer Reuven, 'You should have made a prior declaration that this was the reason for the sale,' because Reuven can say: I was afraid the matter would become known to you, since a person's friend has another friend, and I relied on the promissory note in my possession. In ordinary cases, where the claimant has no comparable support for his position, failure to make such a declaration indicates that he fully intended the transfer. See Perishah.
