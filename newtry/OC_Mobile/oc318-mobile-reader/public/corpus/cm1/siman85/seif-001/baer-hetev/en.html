Time. Meaning the due date of his debt document. This applies specifically where he produces a document or proves the matter with witnesses. Otherwise Reuven is believed through a migo that he could have said, "I did not sell to you and never received money from you," as stated in the Rama to seif 3. Shach.
Ne'emanut. The Sema explains that we treat payment as virtually established by the circumstances. Even if the creditor was granted credibility equivalent to two witnesses, that clause does not overcome this presumption, because such ne'emanut is not effective for every purpose, as stated at the beginning of siman 71. Even according to the earlier rule that a party may prevail where he has a migo, no migo exists here. If he said, "I never sold to you," he would contradict the sale witnesses. The clause granting credibility like two witnesses authorizes him to contradict witnesses concerning payment, not witnesses concerning some entirely different fact.
Believed. The Shach writes that no authority can mean that an authenticated document may simply be invalidated as forged because circumstantial evidence suggests the debt is unlikely. Rashi and Ran's phrase that the other party produces a deed of sale and says, "Your debt document is forged or paid, for if I owed you..." means that he challenges the document's legal force because he knows with certainty that he owes nothing and therefore says the document must be forged or paid. If he alleges only forgery against an authenticated document, he is plainly not believed. Nor is there a migo of payment, because that would be a migo against witnesses.
Claim. In seif 3 it will be explained that even if it was the very day the debt became due, the creditor could not necessarily have compelled payment earlier that day. The Sema writes this. One might distinguish the Gemara's rule that a person sometimes borrows for a single day from the question whether a person sells a field for only a day. Nevertheless, the Shach accepts the Sema's conclusion.
To collect. Why not simply say that he needed the money? The Sema answers that if so he could have sold to someone else rather than to this creditor, avoiding the creditor's later argument based on the sale. Elsewhere, failure to issue a formal protest may prove that a transfer was genuine because a claimant cannot safely rely only on his own future assertion. Here, however, he could say that he relied upon the debt document already in his possession. The Taz discusses this and Chacham Tzvi responds in his glosses.
