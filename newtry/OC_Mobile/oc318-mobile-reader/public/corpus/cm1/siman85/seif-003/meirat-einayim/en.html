Whether or not it is a place where they write the document and then give the money.
In the Perishah I gave two reasons.
First, if the creditor had refused to give him the document of purchase and instead demanded repayment, the debtor might have denied that he had money.
The creditor therefore could not safely seize the purchase money for the debt without endangering the sale.
Second, a person does not want to become subject to another as a debtor. This applies where he now creates a written obligation to Shimon, and the willingness to enter that obligation does not necessarily show that no earlier reciprocal debt existed.
The surrounding circumstances can therefore explain why a creditor enters a new transaction with his debtor rather than set off the earlier debt immediately.The practical rule is that one party's later payment or transaction with the other does not always create a conclusive presumption that no earlier debt existed.
The explanation depends on commercial reality, whether the money had already passed, whether the seller might refuse the transaction, and whether the party had another reason to proceed without asserting setoff.
These distinctions explain the different formulations in the Tur and the rulings later in the siman.
