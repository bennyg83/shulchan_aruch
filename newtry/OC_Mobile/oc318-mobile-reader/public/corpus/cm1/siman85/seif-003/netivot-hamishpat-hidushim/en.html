This applies only in a place where they collect. The Shach concludes that one can never claim, "Perhaps I owed you," because the other party can respond, "That is why I borrowed from you, so that I could collect intermediate quality land from you while you would collect inferior quality land from me." Even if the borrower also owns intermediate quality land, he can say that perhaps at the time of collection the circumstances would be such that each factor would contribute to the result. Tumim challenges this, and the principal ruling follows the Mechaber, because a person does not ordinarily borrow before first paying a debt whose due date has already arrived. If one of the loans bore permitted profit, such as a mortgage with deduction or an iska arrangement, he cannot make this argument. See the Beurim.
