And specifically where the document. As stated in chapter Chezkas HaBatim, a person may purchase a claim against himself in order to avoid litigation. See Rashbam there.
And there is an opinion that says... This is inferred from the Gemara's question: what are the circumstances? If the due date had arrived, what is the reason of the Sages? The Gemara does not answer that the Mishnah concerns a place where documents are written before money is paid, as in the preceding Mishnah. The reason is that here the debtor cannot say, "I feared that I saw you preparing to take the money." If so, he simply would not have lent him the money. That differs from the sale of land, where he saw that the other wanted to buy movable property that carries no property lien and therefore sold him land so he could later seize it. See Bach.
And some say... that even... and if he. They hold that this case is equivalent to the preceding Mishnah. The Gemara does not give that answer because it already taught the dispute of Admon and the Sages in such circumstances in the previous Mishnah. A second Mishnah would be unnecessary. Therefore the Gemara explains that the novelty here concerns the principle that a person may purchase a claim against himself. For this reason the Gemara challenges the Mishnah according to Rav Sheshes rather than Rav Nachman. According to Rav Nachman, the Mishnah's rule that each collects from the other teaches that each creditor appraises property according to his own debt. According to Rav Sheshes, however, where one's debt is ten and the other's five, that feature adds nothing. This supports the present ruling. Baal HaMilchamos writes that the Rif omitted the Gemara's question and answer because they follow Rav Sheshes, while we rule like Rav Nachman. Thus the Mishnah concerns a place where the document is written first and the money is then paid. Baal HaMaor writes that the Gemara challenges Rav Sheshes because according to Rav Nachman the borrower can say, "I borrowed from you so that I could collect from you..." and thereby collect inferior land from you. Ramban challenges him: if so, why does the Gemara say that in a place where the money is paid first, etc., all agree, etc.? He could say, "I wanted to sell you my inferior field and then collect your intermediate land." Rather, it is not normal for people to borrow in order to sell their property. Furthermore, even according to Rav Sheshes one can construct a case where the second creditor has superior land and the first has inferior land. The first creditor would collect superior land now, while the second debt has not yet matured, and afterward the second creditor would collect inferior land from him. Rather, the correct explanation is that according to Rav Nachman the case can be established in a place where, etc. This is why the two rulings are made dependent on one another: this applies only in a place where, etc., and even, etc.
Reuven has inferior land and Shimon superior land. This follows Ramban's words cited above and concerns a case where the second debt has not yet matured. See the Sema. The same point could have been illustrated where one has superior and intermediate land while the other has inferior land, as stated above and in accordance with Baal HaMaor. Ramban formulated it this way in order to refute Baal HaMaor, alluding to his question that even according to Rav Sheshes such a case can be found. Necessarily, therefore, that line of argument is not accepted. The Shach's words here in seif katan 12 are imprecise.
