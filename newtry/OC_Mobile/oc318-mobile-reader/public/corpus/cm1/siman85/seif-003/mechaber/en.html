If Reuven produces a promissory note against Shimon and Shimon produces a later note against Reuven, and Reuven's debt had fallen due before he borrowed from Shimon, Shimon may say to Reuven, 'Had I owed you, you should not have borrowed from me; you should have collected your debt from what you owed me.' {Rama: This applies only when the first document is indisputably valid. If there is a claim against it, such as a concern of asmachta or the like, Reuven may say, 'I feared that I would be unable to collect with my document because of the asmachta, so I had to borrow because I had no time to litigate and press you,' as stated in Responsa of the Rosh, rule 42, responsum 2.} Some say there is no distinction between a place where money is given before the document is written and a place where the document is written before the money is given. Others say the foregoing law applies only where the money is given first. Even if Reuven has inferior land and Shimon has superior land, Reuven cannot say, 'I borrowed from you so I could collect superior land from you while you would collect my inferior land.' In a place where the document is written before the money is given, Shimon cannot say, 'You should have collected your debt instead.' Rather, each collects his debt. Nevertheless, if both have the same grade of land, whether superior, intermediate, or inferior, or one has superior or intermediate land while the other has inferior land, each retains what he holds. The same applies if one has intermediate and inferior land and the other has inferior land. But if one has superior and intermediate land and the other has inferior land, each collects from the other. If the date of Shimon's note against Reuven preceded the due date of Reuven's note, each retains what he holds in every case, because Shimon cannot say, 'You should have collected your debt,' since it was not yet due. This applies even when only one day remained. For example, Reuven lent Shimon money by a note for 5 years, and on the final day of the 5 years Reuven borrowed from Shimon for 10 years. Shimon cannot say, 'If I owed you, why did you obligate yourself for the sake of one day?' Reuven collects his debt after 5 years and Shimon collects his after 10 years. {Rama: If Reuven demands payment from Shimon and Shimon replies, 'You paid me this week; why did you not retain that money for your debt?' and Reuven says, 'I trusted you,' Reuven is believed through a migo that he could have said, 'I did not pay you,' or, 'The time for you to pay me had not yet arrived.' If a woman consecrated her property and directed that her daughters live in the houses and pay rent, and one daughter later produced a gift document predating the gift to the consecrated estate, while the other heirs say, 'If your document were genuine, why did you pay rent?' then if the daughter gives a valid explanation for the rent, the law follows her; otherwise, it follows the heirs, as stated in Responsa of the Rosh, rule 70, responsum 2.}
