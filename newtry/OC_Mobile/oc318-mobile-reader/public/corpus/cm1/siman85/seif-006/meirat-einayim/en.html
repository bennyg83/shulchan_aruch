The law is that each remains with what he has. This includes cases where the two parties have property of equal quality, or one has superior or medium land and the other inferior land, or one has property while the other has none, as explained with the reasons in seif 3.The purchaser collects from the borrower.
The borrower cannot argue that since the seller himself could not have collected because the mutual debts would have been set off and each would have remained with his own property, the note should be considered already paid and the purchaser no stronger than the seller.
Once the note was validly transferred, the purchaser acquires the claim and may enforce it according to its terms.
The prior possibility of setoff between the original parties does not automatically extinguish the debt instrument as against an assignee. The exact result depends on the nature of the mutual obligations and on whether the opposing debt was already legally collectible at the time of the transfer.
