And the first view is primary. Meaning that even though the items were written into the ketubah, since they were not appraised at fixed monetary values the husband never accepted responsibility for them. If she asks that they now be appraised so that he will assume responsibility and he refuses, Rivash, siman 150, writes that no one can compel him to accept responsibility. He may leave the property under her control as nichsei melog. So too was ruled above in siman 65:11. I saw in a letter of Rambam, page 33, that he expressed astonishment at a judge who ruled that whenever a husband wishes to return his wife's dowry and no longer bear responsibility for it, the law is with him. Rambam writes that he was immediately astonished at that ruling. In truth I do not understand the astonishment. Rivash's rule is sound: who can compel the husband to accept responsibility? Perhaps Rambam was discussing a case where the husband had already accepted responsibility and the items had entered his possession, in which case he cannot withdraw. Even this requires study, for why should this differ from any other debt that the borrower may repay before its due date, as explained in Choshen Mishpat 74?
