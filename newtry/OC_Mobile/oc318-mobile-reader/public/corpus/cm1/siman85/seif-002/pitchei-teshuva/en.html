That contains a clause of credibility. Beit Yosef cites Baal HaTerumot comparing this case to one in which the creditor says that the payment was applied to another debt, as explained in the preceding seif katan. Drishah writes that, according to this comparison, it requires study whether seizure is effective here just as it is in the case of 'another debt' in siman 58, or whether the two cases are not completely analogous. Panim Meirot, part 2, end of siman 86, rules simply that seizure is effective. He proves this from siman 88 seif 12 concerning one who claimed wheat and admitted barley, where seizure is effective. Since the party is in possession, he may say, 'I know that I did not admit or waive the rest; I merely did not wish to claim everything at once,' as the Sema explains there. So too here, one who is already in possession may say, 'I trusted you, and for that reason I did not collect the money from you earlier.' Panim Meirot nevertheless concludes that everything depends upon the judge's assessment. If there are strong indications against the creditor, such as his failure to demand payment for a long period when he could easily have collected earlier, such a powerful presumption can justify removing the money from his possession. It is somewhat surprising that he did not mention Drishah's express uncertainty. Shaar Mishpat cites Panim Meirot and argues that his proof is not compelling; from the Taz below in seif 3 it appears that seizure is entirely ineffective. See there.
