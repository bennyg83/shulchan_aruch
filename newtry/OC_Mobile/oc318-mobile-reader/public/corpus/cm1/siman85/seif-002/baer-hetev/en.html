How can the borrower say, "You should have foreclosed on the property," when the lender can respond, "Had I pressed you, you could have paid me cash or sold the field to someone else. I therefore had to persuade you to sell it to me, and I was compelled to give you the money first because this is a place where the practice is to pay the purchase money before writing the deed." I asked several scholars, and they agreed that reason dictates that the lender may make this argument and that a valid note should not be invalidated merely by the inference that he should have foreclosed. However, in a place where the deed is written first and the money is then paid, the ruling of the Tur and Mechaber is correct. The borrower may say, "If I truly owed you, you should have retained the purchase money as payment of your debt, since you already held the deed of sale." Shach.
