Seif 1: “And I will give you a small wage.” Note: The Sema cites the Tur's wording, “I will give you payment corresponding to the benefit.” The baraita indeed says that he will pay according to the benefit received. I have explained elsewhere that Rambam intentionally changed the wording. If the recipient pays the full value of the benefit he receives, he is liable for unavoidable accidents only where the merchandise is especially easy to sell. With ordinary merchandise he is exempt from accidents. But if he pays only a small wage, less than the value of the benefit, then even with ordinary merchandise he is treated as a borrower and is liable for accidents. I offered another resolution. Even with ordinary merchandise, the benefit of being permitted to inspect or conditionally take the item can make him a borrower for ordinary accidents. The distinction between especially marketable and ordinary merchandise may instead concern loss caused through normal use. With especially marketable goods he is treated also as a purchaser and is liable even where the item dies through its permitted use; with ordinary goods he retains the ordinary borrower's exemption for loss caused by the work itself. This also resolves Tosafot's difficulty in Bava Metzia 81. Regarding the Sema's citation of Hagahot Mordechai that hiring a worker requires no formal kinyan, see the authorities I cited elsewhere. I have also discussed whether, where the principle of kim lei bid'rabba minei prevents judicial collection, the wage obligation nevertheless takes effect through speech for purposes of a moral obligation or seizure. The same question arises in the laws of prohibited payment.
