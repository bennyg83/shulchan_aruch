Because he cannot swear, he must pay. The reason is that he admitted part of the claim. The claimant demanded two borrowed cows that, according to him, died during the borrowing period so that the borrower is liable for accidental loss. The defendant admits liability regarding one of them, and with respect to the second he is obligated in a Torah oath as one who admits part of a claim. Since he cannot take that oath, he must pay. This differs from the preceding siman, where one animal was borrowed and one was rented, one died, and the other remains alive. There the parties dispute only the dead animal, while regarding the living one the defendant says, "Here it is, take it." There is therefore no partial admission. This is straightforward.
