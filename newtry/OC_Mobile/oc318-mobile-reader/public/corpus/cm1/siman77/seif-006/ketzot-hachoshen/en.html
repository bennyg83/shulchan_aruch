Two who borrowed. According to the Mechaber, two co borrowers are only ordinary guarantors for one another, so the creditor collects one borrower's half directly and reaches the other half from him only if the fellow borrower cannot pay. I was uncertain whether the guaranty portion is itself documented where the two signed a debt instrument together. If it were, perhaps the creditor could collect that guaranty share from encumbered property and the borrower could not claim payment against it, like a guarantor expressly written in the document. It appears that the guaranty remains oral. The signatures in the document attest to the underlying borrowing. Each borrower is a principal debtor only for his half. His guaranty for the other half arises automatically by law from their having borrowed together. He never signed a separate written guaranty undertaking. Therefore the guaranty component is external to the document and has the law of an oral guaranty. Knesset HaGedolah cites a responsum explaining why Rambam specifically discusses two who borrowed by document: with an oral loan, if one raises a claim that would exempt him from guaranty, he may have a migo of payment; with a document he lacks that migo as to the principal loan. Nevertheless, even in a written loan the guaranty itself does not thereby become a written obligation. The document strengthens the principal debt but does not automatically give written status to the secondary guaranty.
