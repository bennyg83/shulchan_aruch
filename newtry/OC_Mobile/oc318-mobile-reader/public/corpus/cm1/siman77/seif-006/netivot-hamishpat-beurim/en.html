And one of them admitted. Ketzot HaChoshen writes that if the lender himself makes only an uncertain claim, the admitting partner is not considered financially interested in the testimony. See there. These are also the words of Tumim in siman 37. I already challenged him there. Here, since the admitting partner has himself become obligated to pay, he is effectively also a claimant seeking the money from the other partner. He therefore has the status of a litigant and is disqualified from testimony whether his testimony would benefit or harm the other party, even where he has a migo that would remove ordinary financial interest. A proof is the Ran in the chapter HaKotev concerning the cattle herder who is not treated as a witness, because after he admitted that he had seized the animal after the owner's death and therefore became liable to pay, he became a litigant. The same applies here.
