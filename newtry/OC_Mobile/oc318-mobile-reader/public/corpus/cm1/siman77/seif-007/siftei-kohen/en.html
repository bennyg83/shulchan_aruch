Even if both are written in one instrument. Darkei Moshe attributes this to a responsum of Rashba, siman 1086, and it is so marked in editions of the Shulchan Aruch and in the Sema. In truth, Rashba does not say this there. He was asked whether one joint creditor who forgave his share in the instrument caused the entire debt to be forgiven. He answered that only his half is forgiven, for he expressly forgave only his own share; even if he forgave everything, his fellow’s share is not forgiven, because his fellow appointed him to sue and litigate, not to forgive. The Rama may have understood this as referring to an instrument written in both names, because the preceding responsum discusses such an instrument. That inference is not compelling. The wording, “He appointed him as agent to sue and litigate, but not to forgive,” instead suggests that the person who forgave was not the named creditor but one acting under authorization for his fellow. The Beit Yosef also quotes Rashba as saying that even an express forgiveness of everything does not affect the other’s share, notwithstanding an authorization, which again indicates that the forgiver was acting as an authorized agent. One cannot simply equate an authorized agent with a partner. An agent who errs may be told, “I sent you to improve, not to damage,” and even a slight deviation voids the transaction. This argument does not ordinarily apply between partners, for each is empowered to act in partnership matters. It may therefore be that a partner’s forgiveness is effective against the debtor, as though the partners stipulated that each may act for both. Responsa of Maharam appear at first to show otherwise, for partners who released a gentile without the third partner had to reimburse the third, and Maharam likewise rules that a partner who voluntarily released a debt must pay his fellow’s loss. Yet those cases may concern former partners after dissolution. Alternatively, the forgiveness may be effective against third parties, while the forgiving partner remains liable internally to reimburse his fellow’s share. Otherwise no outsider could safely transact with one partner without the other’s approval. This requires study.
