Nevertheless, one of them has no power to waive his fellow's share, as the Sema wrote. The Shach, however, writes that this ruling is not compelling. The Rama learned it from a responsum of the Rashba, and there it implies that this applies specifically where the note was not written in the name of the one who waived, because he is only in the position of an agent. But if the note is issued in both of their names, so that they are partners, this does not necessarily follow. We find a distinction between an agent and a partner: regarding an agent who erred, we rule in siman 182, seif 3, that even for the slightest deviation the transaction is void, because the principal may say, 'I sent you to improve matters for me, not to impair them.' This argument does not apply to a partner, as written in siman 176. Accordingly, one can properly distinguish between them. From the words of Maharam in the responsa printed with Maimonides it appears, at first glance, that a partner cannot waive the debt. The same appears from Maharam's responsum in Mordechai concerning a partner whom the ruler coerced to release a debt, where he writes that this is only because he was coerced, but if he released it voluntarily he must pay. The Rama cites this briefly in siman 388, seif 4. It may be answered, however, that there the partners had already separated from one another, although originally they had been partners. Alternatively, even if a partner has power to waive, it is as though they stipulated that each may act as he wishes, but if he causes a loss he must reimburse his fellow for his share. Otherwise, no one could conduct business with one partner until the other partner consented. Thus, with respect to third parties, whatever one partner does is effective, but as against his fellow partner, if he releases or waives the debt he must pay him his share. This requires study. Thus concludes the Shach.
