And some say that even nichsei melog she takes only to the extent that she seized them. Rashba expressly writes, as cited by the Beit Yosef, that she does not lose her nichsei melog. Maggid Mishneh likewise states that she loses none of them and takes whatever still exists, and concludes that Rashba established these laws this way. Later, regarding an actual rebellious wife, he again writes that although seizure does not help for nichsei tzon barzel, she loses nothing of nichsei melog, and even property in the husband's possession is removed from him and returned to her. Rambam's position is the same. I therefore do not know who are the "some say" cited by the Rama that even nichsei melog require seizure. The Bach also writes in the name of a responsum of the Ran that according to Rashba, Ramban, and later authorities even nichsei melog require seizure, which directly contradicts Maggid Mishneh's report of Rashba. I did not see the Ran's responsum mention nichsei melog. At the end of the siman, in the Bach's summary of the laws of "he is repulsive to me," he writes in the first category that even of her nichsei melog she receives only what she or her father seized, attributing this to Rashba and Ramban. This too is astonishing. Responsa Mahari Weil says that all she possesses of her dowry, everything she brought into the chuppah, and all her jewelry remains in her possession, but those items were nichsei tzon barzel and therefore he emphasizes possession. Nichsei melog require no seizure, as Maggid Mishneh states, and even what is held by the husband is removed from him.
