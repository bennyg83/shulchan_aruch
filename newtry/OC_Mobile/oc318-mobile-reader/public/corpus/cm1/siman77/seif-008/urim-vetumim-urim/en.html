Some say he can collect, etc. Mechaber writes this as 'some say' because below in siman 132 there are authorities who hold that even without any waiver, where there are two guarantors the creditor collects half from each. Why is this unlike two borrowers, who are likewise guarantors for each other, where waiver of one can release both if the creditor could have collected everything from one? The difference is that with two borrowers, waiver of one can directly extinguish part of the principal debt itself. Here, even after one guarantor is waived, the borrower's principal debt remains. Since the debt itself survives, the second guarantor who guaranteed it does not automatically become released and may remain liable for the entire amount. One might ask: if so, why did the creditor need to waive the first guarantor at all, since he could have sued the second one anyway? Rosh raises a similar argument regarding two borrowers who are guarantors. The Shach, and I in Tumim seif katan 8, explain that this question alone cannot release the remaining guarantor where no independent reason shows that the waiver was intended for all. With two borrowers there already is such a reason, and the additional consideration supplies support. Here it does not.
