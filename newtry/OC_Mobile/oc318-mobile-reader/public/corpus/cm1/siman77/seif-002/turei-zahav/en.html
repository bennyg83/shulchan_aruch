(Seif 2) “Some say that if the matter was not established.” In my opinion the Ramban and Rosh dispute whether one partner who bought on behalf of the partnership makes the other a direct debtor. The Ramban treats the second partner as though he personally purchased from the seller. The Rosh allows him to say: “My partner bought from you, and I already settled with him by leaving partnership property in his hands sufficient to pay you.” This differs from a case where both expressly borrowed together or one admittedly acted as the other’s messenger while the borrowed money remained partnership property. Even the Ramban would obligate the second partner only in a general partnership where each is the other’s agent. If two people merely placed a fixed sum into a limited venture and one bought on credit without the other’s knowledge, agency should not be presumed merely because the purchase benefited the venture.
