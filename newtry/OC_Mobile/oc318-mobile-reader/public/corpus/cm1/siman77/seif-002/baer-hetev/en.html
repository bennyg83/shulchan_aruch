Admits. The Sema explains that this follows the Rosh as interpreted by Mahari bei Rav. Even if the Rosh appears to disagree with Ramban, that disagreement is only where one partner borrowed without the other's knowledge. In such a case the other partner did not become obligated merely through the borrower's act, and even if he later admits that the money was also for his benefit, since at the time of the loan he could have denied the matter, neither he nor his property became bound to the lender. But where it was already known at the time that the loan was for the partnership, even the Rosh would agree that the other partner is bound. Darkei Moshe therefore rules like the Rosh, and the Rama writes here that this is the proper practical ruling. Although Mahari bei Rav spoke of a fact known from the outset, the Rama extends it to a matter later established by witnesses because the rationale is the same: where denial would not have been credible, the partner is bound. Moreover, the witnesses' testimony shows retroactively that the partnership purpose was a known fact from the outset. The Sema rejects the Ir Shushan's reading that the Rama's "some say" is a third view distinct from the Rosh. The Shach, however, sides with the Ir Shushan and says its reading is legally correct. The Taz further limits Ramban's rule to partners generally empowered to act for the partnership, where each is the other's agent. If two people merely placed a sum together for one limited venture and one bought on credit without the other's knowledge, he is not the other's agent merely because the purchase benefits the joint venture.
To pay. This does not contradict siman 93, seif 13, where Shimon can require Reuven to swear that he does not know a certain debt to be partnership money. The Sema explains that there Shimon's claim is that he and Reuven jointly borrowed one maneh from Yaakov earlier, that the joint debt remains unpaid, and that Yaakov is now suing Shimon for it; Reuven knows whether it is the same joint debt. The Shach's teacher says the cases are even more plainly distinct. Here the second partner may be exempt from direct liability toward the outside lender, yet his partner can still claim internally that the borrowed money entered the partnership and that each must bear an equal share. That is precisely the type of internal accounting addressed in siman 93.
From the share. The Sema asks why this needs stating, since if Reuven's money is physically present among the partnership assets, a creditor can obviously collect it. He answers that because the lender cannot collect personally from Shimon, his only remedy is when Reuven's own funds remain identifiable within the partnership. The novelty is that the lender may then recover the full amount he advanced from the borrowing partner's share, as in seif 5 where two borrowed and one denied.
