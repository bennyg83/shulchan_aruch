(Shach, seif katan 12) For one who denies is like one who has nothing, etc. Note: It appears that nevertheless he need not pay until the lender first sues the second debtor and imposes a shevuat heset upon him. This is especially true where the lender can impose a Torah oath on the second debtor, such as an oath to contradict a witness. Ordinary people do not swear falsely, and he may therefore admit and pay. If he neither admits nor swears, beit din can descend to his property. According to this, the Bach's question cited by the Shach in seif katan 13 is answered. The practical difference in treating the first debtor as an interested witness is that if he were not considered interested and could obligate the second debtor by his testimony in a Torah oath, the lender could not collect the entire debt from the first until the second was brought to judgment and obligated to swear.
