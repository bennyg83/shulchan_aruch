Specifically where he admits, etc. The Tur cites Ramban: when the partner admits the matter, even though he did not initially enter into a formal kinyan, the partners are agents for one another. But if the partner denies and says that this matter never entered the partnership, the other partner's admission does not harm him at all until it is clarified that the money was borrowed for the partnership.Some say that if, etc., he need not pay, etc. This is the Rosh's view as explained by Rabbi Yosef ibn Lev. The Tur first cites Ramban and then writes that the Rosh did not say so in a responsum, but ruled that a lender cannot collect from the partner who was not present at the loan merely because they were partners in their money.
Unless that partner subjected his person and property, one partner cannot create a personal lien upon the other merely by his statement. Rabbi Yosef ibn Lev explains that the Rosh disputes Ramban specifically where the loan was made without clarifying that it was for the partnership. In such a case the absent partner is not bound merely because he later admits that the loan was used for partnership purposes, since at the time of the loan he could have denied that fact. But where the circumstances themselves made it known at the outset that the loan was for the partnership, even the Rosh agrees that both partners are bound. Rama follows this understanding and rules like the Rosh, especially since he views the Rosh as aligned with Rambam and as the later authority.
Rama further understands that where witnesses now establish that the loan was in fact for the partnership, this is equivalent to a matter that was known from the outset. Since their testimony reveals retroactively that the loan was for partnership business, the absent partner could not successfully deny it. This is unlike the Levush, who treats this as an independent third view. The distinction is clear: a later personal admission could have been denied at the outset, whereas facts established by witnesses reveal that the matter was objectively partnership business from the beginning.If the partner admits, he need not pay. This does not contradict siman 93, seif 13.
