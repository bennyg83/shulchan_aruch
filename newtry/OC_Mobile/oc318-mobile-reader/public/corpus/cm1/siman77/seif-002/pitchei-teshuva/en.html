From another, for the needs of the partnership. See Responsa Shevut Yaakov, part II, siman 181. Reuven and Shimon were partners and held a promissory note against Levi for one hundred belonging to the partnership. Levi, in turn, produced a claim against Shimon alone, unrelated to the partnership, for one hundred, and wished to set off that debt against what he owed the partnership. Reuven demanded at least his own half, fifty, arguing that Shimon’s private debt was none of his concern. Shevut Yaakov answers that at first sight the law plainly favors Reuven. Levi cannot seize money belonging jointly to two partners on account of a private debt owed by only one of them when the loan had no connection to the partnership. Indeed, even if Reuven voluntarily allowed Levi to retain the partnership funds, Shimon would not necessarily be obligated to reimburse Reuven, unless Reuven was holding property of Shimon, because this resembles one who pays another person’s debt without authorization. In this private debt the partners are not jointly responsible or guarantors for one another. There is, however, another possible basis for Levi’s position. If Shimon has no ready cash with which to pay him, Levi may perhaps take assignment of the partnership note as property of the debtor, in accordance with Shulchan Aruch siman 101 seif 5, which provides that where a debtor holds a note against others, the creditor may receive that note at its appraised value, even where the debtor has other movable property.
