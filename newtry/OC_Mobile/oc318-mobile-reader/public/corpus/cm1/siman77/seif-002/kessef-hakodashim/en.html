(See Shach, seif katan 3.) The Urim VeTumim writes, following Maharik and Mabit, that when partners borrowed and all the money benefited the partnership, the rule that one does not first collect from a guarantor does not apply. See there. It appears that this reasoning does not apply where each partner contributed money to one partner for investment, the business sustained a loss, and that managing partner returned to one partner the full sum that he had contributed without deducting his share of the loss, and then sought to collect from the other partner the loss attributable to the first partner whose full capital had been returned. There is indeed an argument that since, had there been profit, each partner would have benefited from the entire fund, the loss may likewise be collected from whichever partner the manager chooses. Nevertheless, the Shach's language indicates that the ruling of Maharik is not universally accepted, and we should not broaden the dispute by ruling that way even where there was no actual common benefit. Where the manager returned the money to the other partner knowing there had been a loss and could have deducted the appropriate share, it is even more reasonable that he cannot then withhold that amount from the second partner. Although this is not precisely the case of seif 8 in siman 130, according to the Sema's explanation there, we may nevertheless learn from the underlying principle that he brought the loss upon himself. Perhaps the same applies even to an arev kablan. This requires careful study.
