If it was not established, etc. See Tumim, where I labored to reconcile Rama according to Rabbi Yaakov Beirav's understanding of Rosh, distinguishing between proof and later admission. Even though a litigant's admission is ordinarily like a hundred witnesses, 'it was established' here means that at the time of the loan the lender already knew through witnesses that the funds were for the partnership. If this fact is admitted only afterward, or even proved by later witnesses, it does not create the same guaranty relation retroactively. The reason is that a partner is always directly liable for his own half. The question is whether he also became guarantor for the other half. Under Rambam and Mechaber, who compare the case to two joint borrowers, if the lender knew the joint purpose at the time he extended the money, one can say he relied on both and each became guarantor. If the lender did not know, he never relied on the second partner and that additional guaranty never arose. This is how I understand Rama. As practical law, however, I agreed with most authorities that Rabbi Yaakov Beirav's approach is not accepted. According to Rosh, the second partner never becomes directly liable to the lender merely because the money was known to be for the partnership, unless witnesses establish or he admits that the borrowing was actually done on his agency. Even then, if the lender did not know this at the time of the loan, I wrote that the guaranty element may still fail because the lender did not rely upon him at that time. See there.
