From whichever one he wishes. The Shach in siman 132 cites authorities who disagree regarding two guarantors. In my view a distinction should be made. If the two guarantors undertook their guaranties after the loan, through a kinyan, their obligations arise independently as security to the creditor. Each undertook guaranty of the entire debt, and a security obligation is not naturally divided. The creditor may therefore collect from whichever one he wishes, as Rashba and Maharik explain. If, however, both became guarantors at the time the money was advanced, their liability rests upon the fact that the lender advanced the money in reliance upon them. Since there were two, the lender extended the loan on the strength of both together. Each should therefore bear half, and the creditor should not collect all from one unless the other has no means. Thus the scope of the guaranty depends upon how the guaranty arose.
