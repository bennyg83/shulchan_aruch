Two who became guarantors. This follows from Bava Basra 106b and Bava Kamma 9a regarding brothers who divided property and then a creditor came, etc. If each guarantor were liable for the whole, how could the creditor take the share of only one of them? A person's property is regarded as guarantor for his debt, as stated in Bava Basra 174a.
And in siman 132... As stated in the Tosefta cited above: one who lends another through two guarantors, etc. According to the first opinion, one must explain the Tosefta as referring to unconditional guarantors, who are like two borrowers because they themselves became directly obligated. See Maggid Mishneh there. The plain sense of the Tosefta, however, indicates ordinary guarantors. Tosafos in Bava Basra and Bava Kamma already resolve the proof from the brothers by explaining that the creditor had designated the property as an apotiki. See there. So too below, siman 107, seif 7, and the Rosh in Bava Kamma there.
