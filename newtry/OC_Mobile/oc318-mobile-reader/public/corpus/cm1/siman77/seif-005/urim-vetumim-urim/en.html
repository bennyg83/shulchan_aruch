And they said, "We paid." Here the case concerns a documented debt. For an oral loan, if the two borrowers have no legal connection to one another in the loan, it is obvious that each may testify concerning the other's liability, whether the defendant denies the loan entirely or claims payment. With a single document one might have thought that because both obligations appear together, each borrower is interested in the other's case and cannot testify. The ruling therefore frames the defense as payment and teaches that they may nevertheless testify for one another. Above, where the case is an oral loan, the Mechaber simply says that the defendant denies, because the denial may take any form, either "I never borrowed" or "I repaid," while the other borrower testifies that payment was not made. Sema, and this is clear.
They may testify for one another, etc. If three people lent money to one borrower and saw that he intended to deny the debt, and they contrived that one would sue while the other two testified, thereby extracting the money, and afterward admitted that all three were actually lenders, the money is not removed from their possession. They are believed through a migo, although they acted improperly and violated the command to distance oneself from falsehood. See Tumim, seif katan 7.
