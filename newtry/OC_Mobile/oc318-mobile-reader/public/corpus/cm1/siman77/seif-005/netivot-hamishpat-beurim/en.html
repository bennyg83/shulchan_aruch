Unless there was money. See Sema, seif katan 10. In my view the Rama plainly speaks of a case where the partner argues, "You owe me a corresponding amount," as explained above. This applies specifically after the partnership funds have been exhausted and one partner seeks payment from the other's personal property. The latter can then say, "The loan was yours; I owe you for it, but you owe me a corresponding amount." If partnership money itself is still present, however, the creditor collects his entire debt from the money presently held by the partnership. Certainly, if Reuven owes money to Shimon and it is known that Reuven received money from Levi, but it is unknown whether Reuven received it as a loan or as a deposit, Shimon cannot seize Levi's money on the mere possibility that it was a loan and therefore became Reuven's property. So long as it has not been established that the money left Levi's ownership, another person cannot seize it on the basis of uncertainty. The same applies here. When money was borrowed for the needs of the partnership, the borrowing partner did not thereby acquire the lender's money for himself personally, and the other partner cannot claim the lender's money on the uncertain basis of a corresponding debt between the partners.
