And one of them admitted, etc. The Tur writes that the one who admits becomes liable for the entire debt. Even according to the Rambam and Mechaber, who ordinarily do not allow collection of the entire joint debt from one borrower unless the other cannot pay, here the other cannot be collected from because he denies the debt, so he is treated like one who has no assets. Therefore the creditor collects everything from the one who admitted. This is also explicit in Baal HaIttur and Hagahot Mordechai.
His testimony is not, etc. The Bach asks that if the admitting borrower has assets, why does his testimony matter, since the creditor can collect everything from him? He answers that the borrower presently has no assets but remains interested because he may later become wealthy, and therefore questions the rule that a possible future enrichment does not create disqualification. In my view that rule is correct. Here the case can instead be where the admitting party has assets elsewhere while the denying party has local assets, or where one has medium quality land and the other only inferior land, or a similar practical distinction.
But if he produced, etc. The Bach says that where there is a signed document the testimony of the two borrowers is unnecessary, while in the first case the denial of one borrower matters. He further writes that if one merely claims he repaid, even witnesses saying that he borrowed and did not repay would not create liability because perhaps the creditor already owed him property of corresponding value. That last statement is plainly mistaken. Two witnesses that the loan remained unpaid obligate payment, and one witness creates the rule that one obligated to swear who cannot swear must pay, as all authorities rule in siman 75.
They may testify for one another, etc. Maharshal rules that if three persons jointly lent one hundred to a borrower, one claimant asserts the debt and the other two testify so that the hundred is collected, and afterward the witnesses admit the truth was otherwise, they must return the money and are not believed through a migo. In my view his ruling is not correct; examine the matter carefully.
