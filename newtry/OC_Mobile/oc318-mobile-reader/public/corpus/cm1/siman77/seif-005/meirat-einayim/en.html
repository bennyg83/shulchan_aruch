And one admitted, etc., concerning his fellow's oath, and if, etc. This is the correct reading. The Tur writes: if the second admits that both borrowed from him as partners, the one who admitted becomes liable for the entire amount, but his testimony is not accepted to obligate his fellow in a Torah oath because he is interested in the testimony. By his testimony he would pay only half and the other would pay half.All of them claimed and said, 'We repaid,' etc.
They may testify for one another because none is financially interested in the other's testimony, since a separate amount is written against each. The Tur and Mechaber speak here of claims of repayment, whereas earlier they spoke of claims of liability, because here the case concerns one document under which they jointly borrowed. If they merely testified that the third one borrowed, their testimony would be unnecessary, since the holder can collect on the document without them. If the case were three independent oral loans, with two testifying that the third borrowed, there would be no novelty because they have no connection to one another. The novelty here is that even though they are linked through one document, they may nevertheless testify concerning repayment.
