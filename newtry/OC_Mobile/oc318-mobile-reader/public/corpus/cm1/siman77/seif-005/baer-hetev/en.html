We repaid. The Bach writes that the change in wording requires explanation. In the first clause it says that one denied the claim, while here it says, 'We repaid.' It appears that here there is a written instrument supported by witnesses, so the testimony of the two joint borrowers is unnecessary. In the first clause, however, it must say that one borrower denied the loan. Had he claimed repayment, there would be no Torah oath based on the single witness and no monetary liability based on two witnesses, even if they were not personally interested, because one who borrows before witnesses need not repay before witnesses. The Bach adds that even if one or two witnesses testify that he borrowed and did not repay, such as where they say they never left him unattended, there would still be no Torah oath or monetary liability, because perhaps the creditor held clothing or articles belonging to the debtor in an offsetting amount, thereby satisfying the debt. The Shach writes that this is an error. Plainly, where two witnesses say they never left him unattended, they create monetary liability, and with one witness the defendant becomes one who cannot swear and therefore must pay, as all the authorities and the Taz in siman 75, seif 13, state. If three people lent one hundred to a single borrower, and one of them brings the claim while the other two testify, and they extract the one hundred from him, but afterward they admit the truth of their arrangement, they must return the one hundred to the borrower and are not believed through a migo. Maharshal rules this in Bava Kamma, chapter Shor Shenagach Arbaah VeChamishah, siman 18. The Shach writes that his ruling does not appear correct. Examine the matter carefully.
