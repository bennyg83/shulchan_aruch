The same applies if both of them, etc. See Tumim. I wrote that this ruling requires study in light of our acceptance of Ramban's view that when two people jointly deposited property, one cannot sue the custodian without the other. Why should a documented obligation differ from an oral one? See there.
Each one, etc. This is where both admit that no payment was made. If they claim payment, see above at the end of siman 71.
They are obligated to pay, etc. The wife's obligation becomes collectible upon her husband's death or divorce. The same applies during marriage if she has separate property from which collection may be made. Nevertheless the creditor need not wait during the husband's lifetime until the wife ultimately collects her ketubah. The husband must presently pay the entire obligation. See below, simanim 86 and 227, regarding whether the wife is required to sell the expected value of her ketubah or her melog property for this purpose.
They may later recover, etc. The Ir Shushan writes that this applies only in a place where women commonly conduct independent financial dealings. Elsewhere, although the wife may be obligated toward the lender, the husband and his heirs cannot demand reimbursement from her, because she is believed to say that all the borrowed money came into her husband's possession. The Sema and Shach write likewise. This rule must be applied with judgment, according to what the judge sees in the circumstances.
Except through a migo that I repaid you. This refers to a case where the lender did not write the husband a receipt acknowledging payment. See below, siman 130:3, and what I wrote there.
A house. I do not know why the wording here differs from the responsum, which speaks of a debt of two hundred rather than a particular gift. The language of the gloss itself likewise indicates a debt, and so it appears in Even HaEzer 102:6.
Except that neither may collect half by himself. Although generally one of two joint creditors cannot collect without the other, where the other is present in the city there are circumstances in which one can collect. Their express condition was therefore that even then neither could collect independently. See Tumim. I wrote that the rationale of this ruling requires study.
