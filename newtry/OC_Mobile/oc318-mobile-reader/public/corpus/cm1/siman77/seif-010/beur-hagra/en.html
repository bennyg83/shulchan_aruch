The same applies if, etc. Rashba proves this from Ketubot, from a husband's rights in his wife's property, and from Shevuot. His reasoning follows Raavad's explanation that denial to one partner is a complete monetary denial because the other partner may claim the entire debt. We do not follow that view generally, as stated above. Nevertheless, where there is one joint deed, the ruling is correct, as in Bava Metzia and siman 76:1. Sefer HaTerumot explains that even according to Ramban, because partners trust one another, a deed held by one partner need not be scrutinized.
(Likkut) The same applies if, etc. The Rashba there brings proof from a husband's rights in his wife's property at the end of chapter HaSholeach, from Bava Kamma 71a, from the case where it is written for half, etc., and from Ketubos 94a concerning two brothers, etc. According to this, where two people deposited jointly, either one may claim the entire amount, in accordance with the Ra'avad. The Ran in chapter Shevuas HaPikadon likewise writes that the Rashba follows the Ra'avad. Yet in seif 9 the ruling follows the Ramban. This also makes difficult what is written in siman 76, seif 1, in the Rama's gloss, that because they became partners, etc. That ruling is from Sefer HaTerumos, who explains it according to the Ra'avad. He adds, however, that even according to the Ramban, since partners trust one another, a document held by one of them need not have been scrutinized by the other. End.
