And she is not given a get until twelve months. Maggid Mishneh writes that she is held for twelve months and the husband is asked not to divorce her during that period, because it is degrading for Jewish women to be divorced by their husbands because of a quarrel, and perhaps within that time they will reconcile and remain married. This implies that if the husband insists, he may divorce her without a ketubah, though we request that he not do so. See the Ran. Rashba holds that the Gemara's twelve-month delay was stated only where she claims, "he is repulsive to me." For an actual rebellious wife, immediately after four weeks of announcement and the second consultation she loses everything, and if the husband wants he may divorce her at once without being asked to wait. The Rosh and Tur, however, hold that throughout the twelve months, if the husband chooses to divorce, she has not yet lost her ketubah. Even in the "repulsive to me" case, where she expressly waives the ketubah, that waiver is not final during the twelve months. All the more so in an ordinary rebellion, where immediate divorce is not appropriate, she is given a twelve-month period after the four weeks of announcement.
