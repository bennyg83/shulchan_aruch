Two who borrowed... Yerushalmi, beginning of chapter Shevuas HaPikadon: "and he dealt falsely with his fellow" teaches that when his admission is discovered through an oath, it excludes one who denies a claim made by one of the partners, and excludes one who denies despite witnesses or a document. Ramban explains that one partner demanded payment and he denied him, but this denial has no practical effect because in any event he may not give the property to that partner alone without the second partner. Rabbi Yose said: this teaches that two who borrowed from one person, even if they did not write that they are each responsible and guarantors for the other, are nevertheless responsible and guarantors for one another. Meaning, just as one who received a deposit from two people can say, "I became obligated only to both of you together," so too where two borrowed, or where someone deposited with two, he can say to each, "I lent or deposited to both of you together." "But they do not act this way," meaning that the practice is to write explicitly that they are responsible, etc. If two deposited together and one seeks to take his share, they do not listen to him. The Yerushalmi asks whether the holder should be treated as denying that one's share and thereby become liable. The phrase "they do not listen" is read accordingly, as though it said "if they do not listen." If one person deposited with two and this one denied him, he is liable; if the other denied him, he too is liable. If he deposited with them the value of one perutah, does it result that each brings an offering for half a perutah? The Yerushalmi answers that there, etc., an oath takes effect on the value of one perutah, etc. Meaning, since each is responsible for the whole, the oath applies to the value of one perutah. Although our Gemara rejects this derivation, the Rosh writes there that the two baraisos differ: our Gemara teaches an exclusion for one who admits, whereas there the teaching excludes one who denies. This is the source for "two who borrowed."
Or who purchased. A purchase is also included with a loan under the verse "and he dealt falsely with his fellow," as stated in Bava Kamma 105b: "and he denied it, excluding..." "You sold it to me, what is the law?" etc. Necessarily the exclusion applies to all the cases, for otherwise how could the Yerushalmi derive the law of a loan from a deposit?
Or who received. The verse there expressly mentions a deposit. Likewise, the Yerushalmi cited above begins and ends with a deposit: "One who deposited with two, if this one denied him..."
Whether by document... As stated in the Yerushalmi there, "excluding one who denies despite witnesses or a document." This shows that the earlier case concerns an oral obligation. Although in Shevuos 37a Rabbi Chiya said to Rabbah, "A baraisa supports you: and he denied it, excluding..." and Rabbah answered, "If because of that proof..." while the conclusion there quotes Rabbi Yochanan that one who denies money for which there is, etc., the Yerushalmi follows the plain sense of the Gemara's rejected explanation. If we were to say that he admitted, etc., rather must it not be, etc. Since the Yerushalmi explains denial to one partner in its plain sense as complete denial, the same applies to a document, meaning he completely denies the documented obligation. Later the Yerushalmi records Rabbi Yochanan and Reish Lakish as both saying that the reference to witnesses should not be included, because it is as though his admission emerges through the oath. Why? Because witnesses may die, whereas a document is not ordinarily lost. The Yerushalmi then says that the real reason must be that witnesses may forget their testimony. Thus the baraisa's references to a document and witnesses are literal. Even according to the Bavli's rejection, it appears that the verse covers every case, since the baraisa says, "excluding a borrower..." If the case concerned only a documented debt, there would have been no need to say "excluding a borrower..."
(Collected note) Whether orally. The Yerushalmi necessarily concerns an oral obligation, because with a document each partner can make the claim, as stated in siman 76, seif 1, in the Rama, and below in seif 10 in the Rama. See what I wrote there. This is where it is known that they borrowed jointly. Otherwise, siman 67, seif 26, would present a difficulty. (End of note.)
Even though. As above from the Yerushalmi.
And if there is no. The law is like that of a guarantor, as stated in Bava Basra 173. See there, and as stated below, "for he has the law of..." This is contrary to the Rosh and some commentators, who hold that each borrower is liable for the entire amount like an unconditional guarantor. There is proof from the Tosefta: if two borrowed with one document and one of them paid his share, the document remains in force and the creditor writes him a receipt for his share. The Rosh there gives a forced explanation of this. Furthermore, Bava Metzia at the beginning of chapter 3, 34b, states regarding partners who borrowed, etc. See Rashi there, s.v. "and one paid..." Likewise, in chapter HaSho'el there, 96a, regarding partners who borrowed, etc.
(Collected note) But... and he collects... This follows from Bava Metzia 96a, "partners who borrowed..." The Gemara asks whether each is a borrower of the whole. If the Rosh's view were correct, each would indeed be a borrower of the whole. See Nimukei Yosef there and likewise at the beginning of chapter HaMafkid, and the Rosh there. (End of note.)
For he has the law of... As the Yerushalmi says, "responsible and guarantors..." Some sought to infer from the word "responsible" that he has the status of an unconditional guarantor. See Sefer HaTerumos, section 44, part 1. This is incorrect, because responsibility of property is also expressed in terms of guaranty. Rambam likewise writes at the beginning of chapter 18 of Hilchos Malveh VeLoveh: one who lends another without specification has all of the borrower's property responsible and pledged as guarantors for this debt, etc. See there. Bava Basra likewise says there of a dayan who allowed a creditor to collect before, etc., that he held a person's property to be guarantors for him, etc.
And he collects from each... As stated in the Tosefta cited above.
Unless they expressly stipulated. As stated in the Tosefta at the end of Bava Basra: one who lends another with two guarantors may not collect the entire debt from one of them. If he said, "on condition that I may collect from whichever of them I wish," he may collect from whichever he wishes. This is like the law of a guarantor in Bava Basra there, where an unconditional guarantor is liable even though, etc., contrary to Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, as Rabbi Yochanan states there.
And if one paid... Meaning, even though one who pays another person's debt generally need not be reimbursed, as stated in Kesubos 108a, and Tosafos there, s.v. "ha mani," cite the Yerushalmi's reason, "I could have appeased him and he would have forgiven me," and the same rationale can apply to a guarantor. See siman 130, seif 2, where there is a dispute concerning this. Nevertheless, regarding a guarantor, if at the time he became guarantor the debtor told him, "Become my guarantor and pay," the debtor is liable to reimburse him according to all opinions, as stated above in siman 130, seif 2. Proof comes from Bava Basra there, the incident of the guarantor for orphans, etc., and the baraisa concerning a guarantor. Here too, it is as though the other partner authorized him. Their partnership was undertaken on this basis. See the Shach, who disagrees.
Therefore, two who borrowed... He acts as the other's agent, as stated in Bava Basra 42b: a partner is considered one who enters with permission, etc., meaning that he shares in the increase, etc. Rabbeinu Chananel explains that these are two separate principles. First, we learn that he shares with his partner even where the increase came through his own physical labor and effort, although the other partner did nothing. The statement concerning a field not ordinarily planted, etc., is a separate rule: if he planted such a field, he receives reimbursement for his expenses, etc., because he is considered as one who entered with permission for every purpose. Bava Kamma 113b likewise says regarding one found at the threshing floor, etc., that there is no concern of theft if the collector is Jewish, because his fellow must reimburse him. See Mordechai there.
(Collected note) Therefore, two... Meaning, the other borrower cannot say that the half of the debt that was paid on his behalf was actually paid toward a different debt. Taz and Shach. (End of note.)
