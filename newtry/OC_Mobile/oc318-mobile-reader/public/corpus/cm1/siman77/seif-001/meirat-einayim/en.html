He may then recover, etc. This teaches that he is not governed by the rule concerning one who pays another person's debt, where the borrower need not reimburse him, as the Tur and Mechaber state in siman 128. Here it is different. Since he too was obligated and became a guarantor for his fellow, it is as though the other borrower authorized him to pay on his behalf. The Rosh writes this in a responsum cited by the Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe. Rama therefore concludes, "Accordingly, two who borrowed, etc." Meaning, not only where at the time of payment one explicitly states that he is paying on behalf of his fellow may he recover his fellow's share, but even if he pays without explanation, we presume that his intent was to recover from the other borrower. The Beit Yosef also cites in siman 65, in the name of the Rashba, that if both borrowers died and the heirs of one found the promissory note in their possession and claim against the heirs of the second that their father paid the note, they collect nothing on that assertion alone.
