Who received a deposit. See Baer Heitev. See Responsa of Rabbi Moshe Rothenburg, Choshen Mishpat, siman 13, who writes that if two partners entered into a partnership with a third person, we do not say that the two partners became guarantors for one another's share of the loss. The author's view in responsa is that even with a deposit they do not become guarantors unless they expressly stipulated so. At the least, therefore, one should not extend the rule and say that partners automatically become guarantors for one another. There is a substantial reason to distinguish the cases. When two borrow, receive a deposit, or purchase jointly, an immediate and definite obligation arises at the time of the transaction to repay the creditor, return the deposit, or pay the seller. Here, by contrast, when the partnership is formed there is no definite obligation of one toward the other, and they did not necessarily contemplate a future loss, similar to the reasoning stated in Orach Chaim, siman 93. Moreover, no posek is found stating that when two persons form a partnership with a third, the two thereby become guarantors for one another. Another rabbi disputes him there. See there.
