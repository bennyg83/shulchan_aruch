I will support and maintain her, etc. This is Rambam's language, Hilchot Ishut 14:15. Maggid Mishneh explains that just as regarding a rebellious wife we rule with Rav Huna that rebellion means refusal of marital relations, while a woman who says, "I will not work," is not classified as a rebel but may be compelled through disciplinary measures, deprivation of support, or sale of her ketubah rights so that he can hire domestic help, so too a husband's legal status as a rebel refers to refusal of marital relations. The end of the mishnah concerning a husband who rebels parallels its beginning concerning a wife who rebels. But if he merely withholds support, according to Rav we compel him to divorce, or according to Shmuel we compel him to support her; there is no separate law of rebellion that increases the ketubah. The Tur, however, writes that a husband who withholds support is also called a rebel, like one who withholds marital relations, and if she wishes to wait rather than seek divorce we add to her ketubah. The Rosh likewise writes that even according to Rav Huna, who holds that a wife's refusal of labor does not make her a rebel, a husband who rebels by refusing support is called a rebel. See the Bach, who writes that the Rosh is alone against the other authorities in this rule.
