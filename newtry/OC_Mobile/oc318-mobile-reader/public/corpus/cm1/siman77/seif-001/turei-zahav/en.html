In the Shulchan Aruch, seif 1: he certainly repaid on behalf of his fellow. Meaning that the lender cannot say that half of the payment should be applied to some other debt. The Tur writes here concerning Rambam: "This shows that even though each one has enough to pay the entire debt," etc. One may ask why the Tur did not infer this from Rambam's opening statement, where he explicitly says that the lender may collect from whichever one he wishes. One may answer that one might otherwise have understood Rambam as speaking where only one debtor presently has enough to pay the entire debt. The novelty would then be that the lender may either collect everything from the one who presently has assets, or wait and later collect half from the other when he acquires assets. We find similar wording in the Gemara, Perek Kol HaBasar 104, "and he gives it to whichever kohen he wishes." Tosafos explain there that no scholar is presently available, and the meaning is that he may either give it immediately to an ordinary kohen or wait until a scholar arrives. Therefore, the Tur specifically infers from Rambam's later words that if one debtor has enough to pay the entire debt, the lender need not sue the second at all, even if the second also has assets.
