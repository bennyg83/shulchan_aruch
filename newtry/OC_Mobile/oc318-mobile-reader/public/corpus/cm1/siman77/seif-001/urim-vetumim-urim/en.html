He certainly paid on behalf of his fellow. Even if he said nothing, we do not presume that he intended the excess as a gift. He may recover from his fellow the portion he paid for him. Sema. Shach adds that if he paid the entire debt, he cannot say, 'Half was my own payment and the other half represented an unrelated debt owed by my fellow.' That too is correct. But if he paid only half, he cannot later say, 'A quarter was my own share and a quarter was for my fellow, so I still owe another quarter on my own portion.' We presume that a person paying part of a joint debt first satisfies the full portion that personally falls upon him. If the partner for whom he paid later dies, his heirs may say, 'Perhaps our father already reimbursed you.' Sema. This is obvious.
