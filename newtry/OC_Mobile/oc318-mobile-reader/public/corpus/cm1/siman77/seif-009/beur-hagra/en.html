(Anthology) Two, etc. Rashba writes that one partner may always claim the entire amount. The case in Ketubot is different because the litigant seeks to create liability through his assertion that he has a greater interest. Bava Kamma and the end of HaSholeach support Rashba, since partners may act without formal authorization in such circumstances. His words are clear.
And if he claimed, etc. Since he has no authorization, even though he acts as his fellow's agent, this helps only with the defendant's consent. Against his will the defendant may say, 'You are not my litigant,' as stated in Bava Kamma and at the beginning of siman 122. Here the claimant has no share in his fellow's portion, so even the broader opinion in siman 122:5 does not help.
All this, etc., but, etc. See Bava Batra 106b and the explanations of Rashbam and Rosh. The proof is not conclusive, since a deposit may differ. Bava Metzia 39b apportions half or a third to the known heirs, and Rava disagrees only because of a migo.
