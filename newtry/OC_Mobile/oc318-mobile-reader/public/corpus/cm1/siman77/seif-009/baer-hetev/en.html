To take. The Sema writes that the absent partner cannot be compelled immediately to surrender his share, but the claimant must still litigate with the present partner regarding that share even while the other partner is out of town, as stated at the end of siman 122. The Shach disagrees. Why litigate now if even a judgment would not require immediate payment? Nor does he see a basis to compel deposit of the amount with beit din. Siman 122 concerns property known to belong jointly to both partners, where the present claimant may even receive his own share; the question there is only whether the litigation can also determine the remainder.
And heard. The wording implies that if we know the absent partner did not hear of the litigation, then even if he is in town payment should not be made to the one who appeared until the other is notified. The Sema writes this, and the Shach says the stronger formulation should be that unless it is established that he heard, even ordinarily payment should be withheld.
Everything. We presume that the absent partner certainly prefers that payment be given to the present one and that the latter acts as his agent. Sema.
In his hand. The Shach disagrees with this ruling; see there.
