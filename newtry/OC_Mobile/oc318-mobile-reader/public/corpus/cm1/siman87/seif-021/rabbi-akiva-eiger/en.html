[Shach, in the continuation] And greater than this, it appears to me that if the claimant seized. Note: In my humble opinion, according to what the Shach wrote in seif 25 in the name of the Rekanti, that where one is obligated in a rabbinic oath and refuses to swear, and the claimant seized property from him, the matter is doubtful, then according to the side that seizure is ineffective, the present claimant cannot say, “I hold that a heset oath requires holding a sacred object.” The defendant can respond: “I am willing to swear, and even if the law requires an oath while holding a sacred object, had I refused to swear altogether your seizure would still be ineffective. As for excommunication, I say that I hold that a sacred object is unnecessary.” One must therefore explain that the claimant can combine two positions and say: “I hold that seizure is effective where one refuses a rabbinic oath, and I also hold that a heset oath requires holding a sacred object.” This is a double claim of “I hold.”
