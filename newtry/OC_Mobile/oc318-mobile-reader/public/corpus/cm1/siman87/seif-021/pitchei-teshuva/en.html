Knesset HaGedolah, Hagahot Beit Yosef letter 29, writes in the name of Responsa Geonim, siman 274, that where the custom is not to employ these additional stringencies, a party who demands that they be used is not heeded. Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat siman 73, discusses a claimant who wished to add special threats to an oath. He analyzes the Midrash in Vayikra cited by the Sema and the words of the Ran in chapter HaDayanim, and cites Knesset HaGedolah. He concludes by testifying that for nearly thirty years he had sat in a position of halachic authority and had grown up among great sages, yet had never heard or seen threats of this kind. In a matter of established judicial practice, the absence of such precedent is itself evidence. Therefore, according to Knesset HaGedolah's testimony in the name of the Geonim, the claimant should not be permitted to add threats. All the more so in Chatam Sofer's time, when the governments had already prescribed an oath formula containing severe covenantal curses, there was no basis to add anything further. Such oaths are no stronger than the severe oath of the sotah, for which the explicit curses suffice. The claimant's proposal to make the defendant lie upon a bed and spread a shroud over him has no source at all. It is also impossible in law to administer an oath while the person lies down, since an oath is taken standing. Moreover, it is forbidden even to sit in a synagogue when the holy ark is open, all the more so to lie down, and especially if a Torah scroll is placed in the person's hands. Chatam Sofer therefore writes that the proposal should be utterly rejected and not spoken of.
