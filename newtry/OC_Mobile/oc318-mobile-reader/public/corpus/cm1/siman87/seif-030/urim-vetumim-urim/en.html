The law favors the one in possession. We do not rule like Rav, who derives from "and its owner shall accept it, and he shall not pay" that once one swore he remains exempt even if witnesses later came and contradicted him. If so, the same would apply to one who seizes property on his own where he has a migo. We do not, however, rule this way. Rather, he does not pay only so long as he has not been contradicted, but if witnesses contradict him he must pay. So too, one who is in possession and is believed by virtue of a migo is treated like witnesses, Sema. Many have misunderstood his intent, but this is clear.
With the one in possession. Even if one witness required him to swear and he swore to contradict the witness, and afterward he seized property, he nevertheless must take an oath. One witness does not assist him so as to exempt him from an oath. See Tumim, seif katan 27, contrary to Taz.
