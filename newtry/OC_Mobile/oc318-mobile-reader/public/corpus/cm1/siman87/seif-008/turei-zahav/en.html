(Seif 22) “To impose an unspecified ban.” The Sema writes that there is no distinction between one who swears and collects and one who swears and is exempt; in every case the opposing party must first accept the ban upon himself. In my humble opinion, no such acceptance is required where one swears and collects. Likewise, where a person liable for a heset oath reverses it upon his opponent, the person reversing the oath need not first accept a ban. The entire reason for the ban is to prevent people from imposing gratuitous oaths, hoping to pay some amount to escape an unnecessary oath. That concern does not apply here, because the defendant does not willingly reverse the oath; he does so only to save himself. The language also indicates this, for it says, “Anyone who is liable for an oath,” meaning one against whom the claimant holds a right to an oath. When an oath is reversed, however, the defendant has no right to compel an oath from the claimant. He does not seek an oath, but protects himself by saying that the claimant may swear and collect. Similarly, one who ordinarily swears and collects has a right to collect by means of his oath, and the defendant saves himself through that oath and would prefer that the claimant not swear. It is therefore inappropriate to say that the defendant holds a debt against the claimant requiring him to swear. Proof comes from siman 82, seif 9: “The borrower cannot say, ‘Either swear and collect, or release me from the ban.’” There the discussion concerns a reversed oath. If even in reversal the person reversing the oath must accept a ban, how could one have thought that he might ask the creditor to release him from the ban? Even if the creditor swears, the borrower would first have to accept the ban, and thus could never avoid it, for the creditor could say, “I will swear after you accept the ban,” and after the ban he could decline to swear. The Sema in siman 82 appears to have sensed this, and therefore explains that the borrower says, “Swear and collect while I do not first accept the ban, or impose an unspecified ban.” I do not know how this can be read into the text. If acceptance of the ban is legally required, why must the Mechaber teach that the borrower cannot demand that the creditor swear while he refuses to accept it? That is plainly contrary to law. The Sema himself raises this type of objection in siman 85, seif 3, against the Ir Shushan, saying, “God forbid that one should say this.” Nor can one answer that this very point teaches that acceptance is required even when the other party swears, for then the text should have said, “He cannot say, ‘Swear and collect while I refuse to accept an unspecified ban.’” See what I wrote in siman 82, seif 11. Nevertheless, it appears to me that even oaths imposed on an uncertain claim, listed at the beginning of siman 92, require the person imposing the oath to accept a ban, whenever the oath is one by which the defendant swears and is exempt.
