He is believed unless, etc. Meaning, even through another oath the claimant cannot impose this matter by extension, as the Tur and Mechaber write at the beginning of siman 94, because an oath may be extended only to a matter for which an admission would create monetary liability. The same is written in siman 93:9. See the Tur, who distinguishes that specifically regarding a rabbinic oath, if the defendant says, "I already swore," he is exempt, because the Sages did not establish one rabbinic enactment upon another. But regarding a Torah oath, if he says, "I already swore," he must take an oath of hesset to that effect. The Mordechai and Rabbeinu Yerucham, however, rule that he is believed and need only accept a general ban upon himself, as Rama concludes. Darkei Moshe cites them. See there.
