One who became obligated, etc. See Baer HaGolah. The Yerushalmi states that one who became obligated by beit din cannot simply assert that he fulfilled the ruling. If the obligation arose from his own statement, he is believed to say he paid; if imposed through others, he is not believed. The same is said regarding oaths: if he undertook it himself, he is believed to say he swore, but if imposed through others, he is not. This supports the present rule, just as in monetary matters the limitation applies where he refused the ruling and was placed under ban, as explained in siman 79. See also siman 93:9.
(Anthology) One who, etc. R. Barukh rules that he must take a hesset oath concerning whether he fulfilled a Torah oath. The Gemara discussed the period before hesset was instituted. See Mordechai.
