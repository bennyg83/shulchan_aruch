And he said, "I swore." See Shach, seif katan 50, who writes that the case is where the defendant now wishes to swear simply that he owes nothing. At first glance this is difficult. Even according to the claimant's assertion that the defendant has not yet sworn, the only oath presently due is precisely that he owes nothing. One cannot answer that beit din had previously ruled that he must swear, "I paid," and now he wants to use a broader formulation, because a defendant can ordinarily change from one exempting formulation to another, as explained in siman 79. Perhaps the case is one where the claimant had the parties' claims written down in a manner that prevents such a change. The claimant says that the required oath was specifically in the language "I paid," while the defendant says that he already took whatever oath beit din imposed, though he no longer remembers its exact wording. As to the current demand, the defendant is willing to swear generally that he owes nothing.
Before the attendant. See Shach, seif katan 7, who writes, "However, if he said, I swore before witnesses." There appears to be a textual error. It should read, "However, after release from the oath, if he said, I swore before witnesses." Without such release, Shach already wrote earlier in this seif katan that if the defendant says he swore before the synagogue attendant and witnesses, he is believed. The present statement must therefore concern a case after formal release.
