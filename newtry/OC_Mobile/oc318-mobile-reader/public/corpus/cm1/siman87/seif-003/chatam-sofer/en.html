Shach, seif katan 25, 'I am uncertain, and this requires study.' Note: See Urim, seif katan 24, and Ketzot HaChoshen, seif katan 12. It seems obvious to me that an oath is certainly required, for even according to the ordinary law, if the defendant does not wish to swear, he may reverse the oath onto his opponent, who then collects upon swearing. Taking the law into his own hands cannot improve his position beyond what he would have been entitled to in court. This corresponds to the case of one who seized property, regarding which the Shach writes that seizure is ineffective, as stated above. From the Gaon Ksav Sofer.
