(Netivot HaMishpat, seif katan 1) Or that they are standing in the marketplace. Note: At first glance there is proof for this from the Raavad cited in Shitah Mekubetzet to Bava Metzia, passage beginning "and the Raavad wrote": there is no claimant and defendant here such that one should be considered more in possession than the other, and therefore there is no Torah oath. See there. See also Tosafot, Bava Metzia 2b, passage beginning "let us see from whom the money came," and the other party must take a Torah oath.
