(Seif 6) “The same applies to one who swears and collects.” The responsa printed with Maimonides, Laws of Monetary Claims, siman 11, derive this from the difficulty posed by the case of the ingot of Rabbi Abba, namely the rule cited by the Mechaber in siman 75 concerning one who claims, “You snatched an object from me.” There we rule that the person from whom it was snatched need not swear. In the case of “fifty I know and fifty I do not know,” the responsum proves that the claimant collects with an oath where the defendant required a migo, as explained there. The author overlooked the words of the Mordechai cited by the Rama here. See the novel point I wrote in this siman, seif 30, and you will find that there is room for error here; the Rama was brief where he should have explained at length. A case came before me in which one litigant had a supporting witness and was therefore exempted from an oath. Later a second witness came and contradicted the first supporting witness. I ruled that the later witness cannot undo the earlier exemption. The proof is from Ulla’s rule that wherever the Torah believed one witness, he is treated as two, as ruled in Even HaEzer, siman 17, regarding testimony permitting a woman to remarry after she had already been permitted on the basis of one witness.
