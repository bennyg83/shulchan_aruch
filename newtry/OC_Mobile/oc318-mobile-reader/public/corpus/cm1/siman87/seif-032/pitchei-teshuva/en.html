If the claimant seized, etc. See Responsa Tzemach Tzedek, siman 7, who writes that this applies only in such a case. If, however, the defendant transferred the oath to the claimant, allowing him to swear and collect, it is as though he said, "I trust you if you swear." The claimant can no longer seize afterward and then swear in support of his claim, since he had already been able to prevail by taking the oath, declined to do so, and instead accepted that his fellow would be believed if he swore. His later seizure is therefore ineffective. This is except where witnesses later came and contradicted the defendant, in which case the property is certainly taken from him, because his acceptance of credibility related only to the claimant himself and not against witnesses. He discusses this at length and concludes that all the members of the beit din agreed with him. See there.
