Above, in siman 69:6, the Mechaber writes that if Reuven produces a ruling of beit din and the heirs answer, "Perhaps he swore to you or paid you," they are exempt without an oath, and only a general ban is imposed in the presence of the heirs. That language might imply that the ban is imposed for the sake of the heirs, so that they will state whatever they know, unlike the Mechaber's wording here, which appears to say that the ban concerns only the claimant, so that he will admit if the debt has been paid and return the document. The Tur in siman 69 writes that a general ban is imposed in the presence of the heirs, as explained earlier. He refers to the responsum of the Rosh cited there concerning one who produced the deceased father's handwriting stating that he owed one hundred, while the heirs said, "Perhaps he paid you." The ruling is that a general ban is imposed in the heirs' presence upon anyone who knows that the money was paid to admit it, and if it was not paid, to testify; the heirs likewise must state if they know that it was not paid. I explained there that the ban applies both to the claimant, so that if it was paid he will surrender the handwriting, since it is degrading to the heirs that a needless document remain outstanding against their father, and to the heirs, so that if they know it was not paid they must pay. The same applies when the claimant produces a beit din ruling that their father owed an oath. The ban is directed to both sides. The claimant must return the ruling if he knows that payment was made, and if the heirs know it was not paid they must pay, for we assume that their father would not have sworn falsely but would have paid. It appears that this is also the Mechaber's intent. In siman 69 he abbreviated by writing that the ban is imposed in the presence of the heirs, meaning also upon them, and here he merely begins the formula of the ban, that whoever knows it was paid should admit it, as if he had written "and so forth," including the heirs if they know that it was not paid.
