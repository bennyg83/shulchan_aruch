And one of them takes the oath. It is further written there that if one has a claim against many people, one of them may swear, whichever the claimant chooses, while the others stand by the oath and answer Amen. Answering Amen is considered acceptance of the oath, as derived from the verse, "And the woman shall say, Amen, Amen," and one should not multiply oaths. It appears that this applies specifically to a curse formula or vow where one says that a certain thing shall be forbidden or a curse shall rest upon whoever did the act, and the others answer Amen. It need not literally be Amen; the same applies if they say yes or any expression showing acceptance, as explained in Yoreh Deah siman 236. But if one partner swears in Hashem's name that he personally owes nothing, the others' answering Amen certainly does not suffice. This appears correct.
