So we say in Ketubot 94a, 'An oath to one is an oath to one hundred.' The Sema writes in the name of Hagahot Maimoniyot that the same applies if one has a claim against many people: whichever one the claimant chooses swears, while the others stand at the oath and answer amen, because answering amen is considered an oath. He adds that this applies specifically where the oath is formulated as a curse, or 'this curse shall fall upon whoever did such-and-such,' and the others answer amen, or another formulation clearly accepting that punishment. But if one partner swears by Hashem's name that he personally owes nothing, the others' answer of amen certainly does not suffice.
