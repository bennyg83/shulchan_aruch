That arise from uncertainty. See Baer Heitev in the name of the Shach. Rabbi Akiva Eiger comments on the Shach's example of an employer and worker who dispute the agreed wage. He is surprised that this case should require the law of reversal of an oath. Plainly the employer can impose an oath upon the worker directly, because it is no worse than a case where two witnesses establish that the worker was hired, in which the worker still collects only with an oath. As for the Shach's earlier example of a custodian who says regarding a deposit document, 'I returned it,' one cannot similarly object that the depositor may simply say, 'Swear to me that you did not return it,' as with a loan document. The practical difference is within the term of the deposit. According to the Shach below, siman 108 seif katan 7, the custodian may then be believed by virtue of a migo that he could have claimed unavoidable accident. In such a case the law of reversing the oath is indeed needed.
