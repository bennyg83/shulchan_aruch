Ibid., gloss to seif 25, "because of the prohibition." Note: one who studies Mordechai, Bava Metzia, siman 229, will understand the ruling of Piskei Mahari, siman 36. It concerns specifically a woman who says, "You betrothed me through intercourse," thereby accusing him of having committed a prohibition, for Rav would administer lashes in such a case. It is also possible that she was a niddah, as is common among women in our times. Therefore Mahari wrote that in this case Rabbi Meir agrees with Rabbenu Simchah. Although Rabbi Meir disputes him regarding an accusation of theft, holding that one may administer an oath even though the claimant accuses the defendant of a prohibition, he agrees in a case of "you betrothed me through intercourse," because the principal claim is not monetary but concerns the prohibitions of her relatives and of a married woman. But where the claimant does not suspect the defendant of having committed any prohibition, even if the essence of the claim concerns prohibition and permission, while a monetary claim follows from it, as in all the cases brought by the Shach in siman 87, Mahari agrees that an oath may be imposed. Moreover, it appears to me that in the case of "you betrothed me" it is not clear that any monetary consequence follows at all, for he may divorce her and an arusah has no ketubah. See Mahari there, where the case concerns divorce. This differs from the Shach's cases. Accordingly, if she says, "You betrothed me with money," where no prohibition was committed, and especially where the enactment of Rabbenu Gershom against divorcing a woman against her will is observed even for an arusah, an oath may be imposed according to all opinions. This appears correct. In the Shach's cases, where a husband administers an oath to his wife, although he suspects her of wrongdoing, the Tumim has already written that an oath that she has not been paid may be administered even in such a case.
