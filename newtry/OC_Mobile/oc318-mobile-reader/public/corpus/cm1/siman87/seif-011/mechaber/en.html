There is a further distinction between them. If a Torah oath is reversed to the opposing party by saying, 'Swear and collect,' he is not heard if the other party does not wish to do so. Likewise, those who swear and collect cannot, without the defendant's consent, reverse their oath to the opposing party even though their oath is rabbinic. Nor may the oath of partners and similar oaths imposed upon an uncertain claim be reversed, because the claim itself is uncertain. Some say, however, that if one claims, 'Let him swear to me, because I suspect him of this,' the law follows him. {Rama: The claimant may nevertheless say, 'I want from you only a heses oath, not the partners' oath,' so that only the heses oath can be reversed to him, as stated in Piskei Maharai 32 and in the manner explained in seif 12. But one who swears and is exempt, namely one taking a heses oath, may reverse it to the opposing party. Thus, only a heses oath may be reversed. Even a heses oath may be reversed only when no additional oath is appended to it against the defendant. If another oath is appended, it may not be reversed.}
