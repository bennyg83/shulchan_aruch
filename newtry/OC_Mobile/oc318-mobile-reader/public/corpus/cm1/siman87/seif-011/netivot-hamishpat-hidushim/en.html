Likewise, those who swear and collect. The Shach holds that one who has seized may say, "I rely upon Rashi and Tosafot," who allow reversal of the oath. See Beurim: this applies specifically to those who by Torah law would collect without an oath. But for those who by Torah law cannot collect even with an oath, seizure does not help. Those who swear and are exempt under a Mishnah oath may reverse it. See Beurim: the same applies to a deposited note where he is believed to claim return through a migo of unavoidable accident. He may reverse the oath like any other hesset oath, because this is no worse than an ordinary loan where the debtor may say, "Swear to me." However, regarding whether we descend to his property if he refuses to swear, his law is exactly like one who claims unavoidable accident: if he refuses to swear, we descend to his property. This differs from ordinary persons who swear and are exempt under a hesset oath, where refusal to swear does not permit descending to their assets.
