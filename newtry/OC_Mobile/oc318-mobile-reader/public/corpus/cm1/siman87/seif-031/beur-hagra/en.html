He claimed that there is... by a kinyan. A kinyan is like a written document in that he cannot claim payment, as explained above in siman 70. See Sema and Shach.
And he... and swears. He is believed because the document is not in the claimant's possession, as explained at the end of siman 41.
And afterward... he must pay. The oath adds nothing, as explained above, because it does not cause him to acquire the money. Thus the text continues, "And if there is..." This is from Baal HaTerumos, and similarly above in siman 82 seif 1, "If afterward he found..."
And he is not considered suspected... A document is not equivalent to witnesses in this regard; it merely removes the borrower's credibility. For where the borrower has a migo he can be believed even against a document, as explained in Bava Basra, despite the general rule that a migo does not prevail against witnesses.
