The one imposing the oath says, 'Amen.' See Baer Heitev. Tumim writes that, as he explained earlier in siman 82 seif 9 and here in seif 12, the custom is now for the party to accept a ban directly by saying, 'I accept the ban upon myself,' and no additional response of 'Amen' is required. Responsa Shav Yaakov, end of siman 6, writes that in several matters the custom is to require an actual formal acceptance of a ban in place of a general ban. For example, a litigant is required to accept a ban that he will not cause the opposing party to take a needless oath, even though Shulchan Aruch siman 16 mentions only imposing a general ban before the oath. Responsa Noda BiYehudah, second edition, end of siman 36, likewise implies this.
