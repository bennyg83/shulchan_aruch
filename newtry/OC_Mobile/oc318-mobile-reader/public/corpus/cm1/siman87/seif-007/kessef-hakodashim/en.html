To impose a general ban. When a general acceptance of a ban precedes an oath containing many distinct particulars, the declaration should state that he is not causing the other party to swear falsely with respect to any particular, since each point is effectively a separate oath for that matter. A general ban does not apply in the same manner to heirs, even if the claimant asserts with certainty that they know the facts or that their father instructed them, because independently of the heirs themselves beit din advances claims on their behalf and thereby brings about the oath.
