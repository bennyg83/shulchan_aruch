The same applies to one who swears and collects. Responsa Chut HaShani, siman 6, writes that if he had authority to distinguish, he would divide the cases as follows. Whenever by Torah law the claimant should collect without an oath, and the Sages imposed an oath as an added safeguard, a supporting witness exempts him from that oath. Examples include one who partially impairs his document, a case where one witness says the document was paid, a creditor collecting in the debtor's absence, collection from encumbered property, or a borrower who says, 'Swear to me that I did not repay you.' In all these cases, the claimant's substantive Torah right to collect exists independently of the oath. By contrast, in cases where Torah law would not allow collection even with an oath, and the Sages created a special enactment allowing collection upon an oath, such as a worker or a victim of robbery, a supporting witness does not exempt the claimant. One has only the specific dispensation enacted by the Sages, and they conditioned that dispensation upon an oath. He concludes that this distinction is sound for practical halachah. The Shach makes a similar distinction above in siman 82, seif katan 13, and in the Shulchan Aruch below, siman 92 seif 9. Tumim, seif katan 6, agrees.
