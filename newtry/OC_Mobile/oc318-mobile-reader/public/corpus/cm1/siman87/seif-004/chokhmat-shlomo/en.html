Seif 13: The defendant was disqualified from taking an oath, etc. Note: I was asked by the late rabbi of Leshnov whether, where the defendant says, “I am disqualified from an oath,” and there is one witness supporting that assertion, the defendant is thereby exempt from the oath under the law of a supporting witness. I answered that in my view there is no doubt. The primary rule that a supporting witness exempts from an oath is derived by the Rosh in the first chapter of Bava Metzia from the law that one witness can impose an oath, through an a fortiori argument. The principle of dayo then limits the derived case to the scope of its source. If the claimant brings one witness who says the defendant is disqualified from taking an oath, that witness is not believed to disqualify him so that the opposing party may swear and collect. A person becomes disqualified from oaths only through two witnesses. Conversely, a single witness who supports the defendant’s assertion that he is disqualified cannot exempt him from an oath of hesset or the like. There is an additional reason. Where one witness supports a litigant regarding the underlying money, even the litigant’s own admission can affect monetary liability, whether to exempt himself in an appropriate context or to obligate himself. It is therefore understandable that testimony can operate to impose or remove an oath. But to brand a person wicked or legally disqualified, his own admission is ineffective, because a person does not render himself wicked by his own statement. The presumption of valid status is therefore stronger, and one witness is insufficient. Since there are in any event authorities who dispute the whole doctrine of a supporting witness, even they can agree in this case. It is preferable to minimize dispute. This is what I answered to that rabbi.
