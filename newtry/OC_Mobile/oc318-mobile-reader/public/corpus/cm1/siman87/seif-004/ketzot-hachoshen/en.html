It was exchanged. The Tur says, regarding Rambam's law, that perhaps Rambam holds a pledge is not "here it is." The word "perhaps" is puzzling because Rambam explicitly states elsewhere that a pledge is not considered "here it is." Bach explains that Rambam's explicit rule may concern a pledge not taken for collection, whereas the present item is being delivered specifically for collection and might therefore be stronger. If the item's value were fixed and it were accepted as complete payment, this would indeed resemble payment rather than collateral. I would explain the Tur differently. Rambam's explicit case concerns collateral given at the time of the loan, where Rabbi Yitzchak's special acquisition of a pledge may not apply. The present case can involve collateral taken after the loan, which the creditor acquires under Rabbi Yitzchak even if the debtor gives it voluntarily. Such collateral has a stronger character and is more naturally treated as "here it is." The Tur therefore says only "perhaps" Rambam still rejects it. The Sema elsewhere suggests collateral given at the time of the loan is more readily called "here it is." In my view the opposite is more plausible. Collateral taken after the loan is actually acquired under Rabbi Yitzchak and therefore has a stronger basis for being treated as already in the creditor's possession. The Shach himself concludes that collateral taken after the loan through beit din can qualify as "here it is." Rambam's present ruling, however, appears to reject that even in a later pledge, and I have already explained that voluntary and judicial later pledges are comparable for Rabbi Yitzchak's acquisition.
