He said to him, "A maneh and a vessel..." Bava Metzia 100a, and the Gemara further says "and moreover, it is 'here it is.'" See Rashi there, which shows that this is so even though the claimant does not concede.
(Collected notes) He said to him, "A maneh..." this is not... With a maneh we do not find such a formulation, unlike Rashi's statement that "here it is" means "I did not spend them..." For if so, "here it is" would exist only with a deposit, not a loan, since a loan is given to be spent. Our text of the Gemara says, "It is as though the lender is already holding them," and Nimukei Yosef notes that Rashi did not have the word "lender" in his text. Nevertheless, the plain sense of the sugya indicates that the law applies even to a loan. (End of collected notes.)
