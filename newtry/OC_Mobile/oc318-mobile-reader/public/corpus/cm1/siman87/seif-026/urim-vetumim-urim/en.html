And he brought witnesses, according to his claim that he would then bring before the “dead witness” and that he would not belong to the orphan, admits that he is exempt:
He must take a hesset oath. If one says to another, 'I already litigated with you and you were found liable to pay me,' and the other says, 'We never litigated,' then if the claimant says it is now impossible to relitigate because the claims have been forgotten, the witnesses have departed, the case turned on *shuda deDayanei*, or something similar, the defendant must swear that he was never found liable in beit din. But if the matter can simply be litigated again and nothing has changed, what purpose is served by an oath? The parties should present their claims anew and the law should be decided accordingly. Shach correctly reaches this conclusion. See above, siman 24, where I explained that the same law governs.
To take a hesset oath. The same applies where a person is obligated to give something not by strict law but because of a communal or rabbinic enactment. He must still take a hesset oath on denial, because the Sages did not distinguish when they instituted hesset. Maharam, cited by Shach.
