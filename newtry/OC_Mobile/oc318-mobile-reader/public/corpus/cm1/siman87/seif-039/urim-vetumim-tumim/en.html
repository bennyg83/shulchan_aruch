There is one who says that one who utters the Name of Heaven in vain, etc. This ruling requires study, for the Mechaber wrote it without citing any dissent. The Hagahot Mordechai derived this ruling from the version of the Behag, which reads: regarding that woman about whom the daughter of Rav Chisda said, "I know of her that she utters the Name of Heaven in vain," Rava transformed it into a matter of an oath. He explained that this was because she violated "You shall not take [the Name in vain]." Yet Tosafot in Shevuot 46b, s.v. aval shevuah, cite this very statement of the Behag and explain that she would take oaths and fail to fulfill them, such as swearing that she would not eat and then eating. Thus Tosafot disagree with the Hagahot Mordechai. How, then, did the Mechaber cite this without mentioning the view of Tosafot, especially since in siman 92 the Mechaber cites an opinion that even one who violates an oath concerning his own conduct in such a manner is not disqualified as a witness, all the more so one who merely utters the Name of Heaven in vain? There is an even greater difficulty according to the Mechaber, who ruled above that one is not disqualified for an offense that carries no lashes, as I discussed at length above in siman 34. Yet it is explicit in the Gemara, Temurah chapter 1, that uttering the Name of Heaven in vain is prohibited by a positive command and carries no lashes at all. How, then, could such a person be disqualified according to the Mechaber? One who examines Rambam, chapter 12 of Hilchot Shevuot, will see that he explains the statement at the beginning of Nedarim that one who hears the Name uttered in vain must impose excommunication as referring to a vain oath, not to merely uttering the Name in vain. See also Taz in Yoreh Deah siman 334. In any event, the Mechaber's ruling requires study. Even according to the Rama one must say that the disqualification is d'rabbanan, and this too requires study, for surely there is greater basis to rely upon Tosafot than upon the Hagahot Mordechai.
