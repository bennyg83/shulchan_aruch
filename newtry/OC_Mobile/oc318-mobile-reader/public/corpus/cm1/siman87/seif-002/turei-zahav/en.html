(Ibid.) “For even a note.” The Sema writes that even if he now gives the claimant a note, it is considered “here it is.” According to the first view, however, this is not “here it is,” because the claimant did not originally rely upon that note. This presents a difficulty. At the beginning of Bava Metzia, page 5, the Gemara asks: according to the view that “here it is” exempts from an oath, why is a verse needed to exclude land from an oath, since all land is “here it is”? What is the difficulty? The verse is needed for a case in which the defendant now wishes to give land, which would not be “here it is,” and the verse teaches that he is nevertheless exempt because land is excluded by Scripture. We must therefore say that the Torah’s exemption of a partial admission involving a lien on land applies only where the land was already encumbered, not where the defendant now seeks to create the encumbrance. The same should then be true of “here it is.” Further study is also required regarding the Tur. Here he appears to agree with the Baal HaItur that a note is “here it is,” yet in siman 88, seif 32, he writes that a note is like land, and an admission regarding a note does not generate an oath just as denial regarding it does not generate an oath. Why is that reason needed? He should already be exempt because the note is “here it is.” According to the first view, the matter is clear, since a note is not “here it is.” Although at the beginning of Bava Metzia, page 4, the Gemara says, “The note that he admits is ‘here it is,’” that is only according to the questioner, who knew no other ground for exemption. According to the conclusion, the exemption rests on another reason, and we do not say that a note is “here it is.” It therefore appears that the Tur agreed with the Baal HaItur here only regarding collateral, not regarding a note.
