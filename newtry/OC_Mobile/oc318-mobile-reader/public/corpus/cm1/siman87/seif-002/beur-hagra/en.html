He claimed from him... Bava Metzia 5a. See Rashi there and Beer HaGolah.
And he admits... Specifically so, as explained in seif 4.
He claimed two hundred... As the Semag explains from Bava Metzia 4b, where a document is considered "here it is" because the debtor cannot deny the two selaim written in it. The Yerushalmi at the beginning of the second chapter of Kesubos discusses a similar issue. Its implication is that exemption with a document is due not simply to inability to deny, but to the rule of denial involving a lien on land. Rabbi Chiya there holds that even "here it is" can require an oath, and Rabbi Avin reports Rav's qualification concerning a written debt. The later discussion concerning "silver, selaim" shows that according to our ruling we follow Rabbi Avin. Accordingly, the Tur and Shulchan Aruch in siman 88 seif 29 are correct, unlike the Shach there. Thus here the qualification "such that that amount..." refers specifically to an obligation imposed by beit din, like the case there in Kesubos, and as stated in Bava Metzia 17.
(Collected notes) He claimed two hundred... See Rambam, Hilchos To'en VeNit'an 4:4. The commentators dispute his ruling from Bava Metzia concerning "selaim, dinarim," where the Gemara gives the reason of a lien and not the reason that he cannot deny. Nimukei Yosef likewise writes so there. (End of collected notes.)
