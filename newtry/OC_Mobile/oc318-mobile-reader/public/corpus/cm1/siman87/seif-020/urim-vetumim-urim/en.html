And his family. Ordinarily a person's family protects and conceals wrongdoing for him, and therefore they too are included in the punishment.
For all Israel are guarantors for one another. Certainly mutual responsibility exists regarding all transgressions. In other areas, however, if others had no practical ability to protest they are not punished. Regarding a false oath there is nevertheless broader communal punishment. Sema.
They dismiss him. Meaning either that they remove him from the beit din or that they divert him from the immediate subject to another matter. According to Rashi, once this procedural step is taken he can no longer retract the relevant undertaking. See what I wrote above in siman 22.
The judge should tell him to state it explicitly. Where there is concern for evasion, we do not rely solely upon administering the oath "according to the understanding of beit din." The judge should make the litigant articulate the relevant facts. So writes the Rosh at the beginning of Bava Metzia concerning two people holding a garment.
Or frighten him further with other matters. Such as a bier and burial shrouds, in order to remind him that every person ultimately dies and that he should not endanger his soul with a false oath.
