He is made to swear in any language he understands, and before administering the oath they frighten him, meaning that they impress fear upon him. They say to him: "Know that the entire world trembled when the Holy One, blessed is He, said, 'You shall not take the Name in vain.' Regarding all other transgressions the Torah speaks of cleansing, but here it says, 'He will not cleanse.' For all other transgressions punishment is exacted from the sinner, but here it is exacted from him and from his family. Moreover, he causes punishment to come upon the enemies of Israel, because all Israel are guarantors for one another. Regarding other transgressions punishment may be suspended for two or three generations when a person has merit, but for a false oath punishment is exacted immediately. Things that fire and water cannot destroy are destroyed by a false oath." If he says, "I will not swear," he is dismissed from the oath and pays what his fellow claimed. If he says, "I will swear," while his fellow continues to demand the oath, those present say to one another, "Please depart from the tents of these wicked men." They tell the person swearing, "We are not administering the oath according to your private intention, but according to our intention and the intention of beit din." Rama: If there is a possibility of deception, the judge should require him to articulate in his oath every avenue of deception that could reasonably be in his mind. A judge may also require an oath while holding a sacred object even where it is not ordinarily required, or may frighten him with additional statements, if the judge sees a need.
