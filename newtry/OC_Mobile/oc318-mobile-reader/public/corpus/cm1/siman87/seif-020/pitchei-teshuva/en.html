And the custom is to administer the oath through a curse. The Sema writes that because the Mechaber omitted the word 'ten,' it appears that he understood Rashi and the Tur differently. In Drishah the Sema writes that, in his view, one must mention the Divine Name or a substitute Name. Urim similarly writes that the party at least says, 'So-and-so is cursed to Hashem if he lies.' This form is not as severe as saying, 'I swear by Hashem,' and although the Tur mentions saying it before ten people, the Mechaber omitted that requirement. Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat simanim 71 and 73, disagrees. An oath expressed as a curse with the Divine Name also carries severe consequences for the entire world, as explained at the beginning of chapter Shevuat HaDayanim. Correspondingly, the Sages required ten people because the Divine Name is not pronounced explicitly. Since the Divine Presence rests wherever ten are assembled, an oath before ten is in some measure like one in which the Name itself is mentioned. This resembles the rule in Yoreh De'ah siman 228 seif 21 that many people who swore together in one assembly are treated as though they swore upon the understanding of the public even if they did not state so explicitly. Here too, because the oath is taken in a place where the Divine Presence rests among ten, it is as though the Divine Name accompanied the oath. See there.
