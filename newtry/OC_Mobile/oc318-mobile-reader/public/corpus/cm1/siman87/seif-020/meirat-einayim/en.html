In any language that he understands, etc. The Tur cites Rambam: 'Our teachers ruled that oaths are administered only in the Holy Tongue. It is not proper to rely upon this ruling. Although the custom in all Jewish courts is to administer even a hesset oath in the Holy Tongue, one must nevertheless inform the person swearing so that he understands the language of the oath.' The meaning is that even according to those teachers who require Hebrew, the person must understand exactly what he is swearing. Thus an oath recited in words the litigant does not comprehend is not acceptable merely because the formula itself is in Hebrew. The essential point is that the oath's meaning and scope be made clear to him so that his acceptance is knowledgeable and the warning has its intended force.
It says concerning them, 'and He will cleanse,' etc. Meaning, with other transgressions the verse says 'He will cleanse' and 'He will not cleanse': He cleanses those who repent and does not cleanse those who do not. Regarding a false oath, however, the verse says only that Hashem will not cleanse.
From him and from his family. The Tur writes, 'and from his family who cover for him,' following the Gemara. The wording may imply that the family is punished because relatives ordinarily conceal his wrongdoing, not that only those specific relatives who actively cover for him are punished. This may explain why the Mechaber omits that qualifier.
And from his family. Meaning that the very punishment visited upon the sinner is ordinarily not imposed upon his family, although a lesser punishment may come upon them for other sins. Here, however, his family is punished with his own punishment, as derived from 'Do not allow your mouth to cause your flesh to sin,' with 'your flesh' referring to relatives, as in 'do not hide yourself from your own flesh.'
For all Israel are guarantors for one another. The Gemara says mutual responsibility applies in other matters as well, but ordinarily only where one had the ability to protest wrongdoing. With a false oath the collective consequence applies even where others could not prevent it, as derived from the verses 'swearing and lying,' followed by 'therefore the land mourns.'
Things that are not recorded, etc. Meaning the wood and stones of his house. All of this is derived from Scripture, as I explained in the Prisha.
They release him and he gives, etc. Meaning: once beit din releases and dismisses him from before them, if he then leaves he can no longer retract and say, 'I will swear'; he must pay. This is Rashi's, Tosafos's, and the Rosh's view. The Ran explains Rashi that even if he did not physically leave beit din, once the judges turned away from that matter it is as though he left and he can no longer retract. See what I wrote above in siman 22 and below in siman 94. It appears Rambam also agrees, unlike the Beit Yosef's understanding that Rambam follows Rabbeinu Tam and assigns no significance to leaving beit din.
And his fellow demands it. The wording is precise and explains why the warning says, 'Depart, please, from the tents of these wicked men,' implying both the person swearing and the person who causes him to swear. Once the claimant sees that the defendant is prepared to swear, he should seek a compromise rather than cause the oath to be uttered. By persisting in his demand for the oath, he shares responsibility.
But according to our understanding. Rambam and the Tur write this as well. The Baraisa, however, has 'according to the understanding of the Omnipresent' instead of 'according to our understanding.'
The judge must tell him to explain, etc. Meaning, it is not enough merely for him to swear according to the understanding of beit din; the substance and meaning must be made explicit to him.
To administer an oath while holding a sacred object where it is unnecessary. This rule was already stated above in siman 15, seif 4.
Or to frighten him with other measures. In the Derisha I cited Vayikra Rabbah on the verse 'If a person sins,' and the Beit Yosef likewise cites a practice of bringing before those about to swear a bier used for the dead and inflated skins and extinguishing lamps. See the Derisha for the significance of these frightening measures.
If the judge sees a need for it. The Ran writes in chapter Shevuas HaDayanim that a Torah scholar does not require as much intimidation.
