He understands. The Tur quotes the Rambam: "Our teachers ruled that oaths are administered only in the sacred tongue, but it is not proper to rely on this ruling. Although all Jewish courts have adopted the practice of administering oaths in the sacred tongue, even a hesset oath, nevertheless the person swearing must be informed of the meaning so that he understands the language of the oath." The phrase "nevertheless" means that even according to those teachers who hold that the oath is taken in the sacred tongue, its substance must still be explained to the person afterwards. So writes the Sema. See Responsa Radbaz, siman 155.
The claimant. It appears that the Sema carefully writes this in order to explain why the court says, "Please turn aside, these wicked people," which implies both the person swearing and the person who compels him to swear. The reason is that once the claimant sees that the defendant is prepared to take the oath, he should reach a compromise with him so that he not cause the oath to issue from the defendant's mouth because of him. Since he did not do so, but instead demanded the oath, he too is included. Sema.
Our understanding. This is the wording of the Rambam and also of the Tur. In the baraita, however, instead of "according to our understanding" it states, "according to the understanding of the Omnipresent." Ibid.
Other things. The Beit Yosef writes that some bring before those taking an oath a bier used for the dead and inflated skins, and they extinguish the lamps. In the Derishah I explained why these things are brought, namely in order to frighten the person taking the oath. The Ran writes that a Torah scholar does not require such extensive intimidation. Ibid. See Responsa Maharit, at the end of siman 70.
