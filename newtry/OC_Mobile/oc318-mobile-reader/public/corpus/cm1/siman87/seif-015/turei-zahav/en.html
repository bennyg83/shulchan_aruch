(Ibid., “such as where he gave his hand”) The glosses to Maimonides cited above state explicitly that both parties agree that he gave his hand as a pledge to pay what he owed, and he afterward says that he paid. He is not thereby exempt from an oath. It appears that this is also the Rama’s intent. His words, “and he denies,” may mean that there is a monetary denial between them, not that he denies the original hand pledge. The Sema emends the text to read, “such as where he said that he gave his hand,” implying that he understands “he denies” as denial of the pledge itself. This is not exact. The same rule applies even where he admits the pledge; the point is that he must swear a second time. This appears correct to me.
