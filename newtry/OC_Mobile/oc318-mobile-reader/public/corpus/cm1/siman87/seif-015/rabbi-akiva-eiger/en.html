[Shach, continuation] But to me it appears clear that it does not. Note: It appears to me that the Shach means as follows. Although perhaps the objection of "you have left no room for anyone" does not apply because seizure is required, and in any event the claimant must swear, nevertheless, if seizure were ineffective merely because the defendant is disqualified from swearing, the defendant would be completely exempt by Torah law without an oath. The claimant’s seizure would then operate only to allow him to swear and collect. This differs from an ordinary defendant who is fit to swear, where an oath obligation rests upon him and beit din compels him and may place him under ban, even though they do not descend to his property. There one can say that if the claimant seized, his seizure is effective to insist that the defendant perform his obligation to swear, except that the defendant may tell him to swear instead by reversal of the oath. But where the defendant is disqualified, no such obligation exists, and the seizure would serve only to allow the possessor himself to swear and collect by Torah law.
