The Shach writes that this requires study. In many places we find that tefillin possess greater sanctity than other sacred books. Mahari Weil's proof from the Mordechai is not a proof, because the Mordechai is not discussing a judges' oath, but an oath by which a person prohibits something to himself. In that context it is obvious that sacred writings have the law of a Torah scroll, as stated in Yoreh Deah siman 237. A judges' oath is different, because several formulations qualify as an oath concerning a personal prohibition but do not qualify as a judges' oath. In particular, since the reason for requiring the person to hold a sacred object is to frighten him, it is obvious that no proof can be brought from an ordinary oath. It therefore appears that specifically a Torah scroll is required, and the words of Mahari Weil and the Rama require study. The Taz likewise disagrees and concurs with the Shach. He concludes, however: "One can defend the later practice, because in later generations they were lenient regarding oaths, as noted below in seif 19, and perhaps they likewise adopted a leniency here."
