And some say, etc. This is the opinion of Mahari Weil in responsum 171. His words require examination. Since even tefillin should not be used initially, all the more so other sacred books should not be used, for we find in many places that the sanctity of tefillin is more stringent than that of other sacred books. In my humble opinion, the proof Mahari Weil brings there from the Rosh and Mordechai is no proof. The Rosh stated only the law after the fact. The Mordechai likewise was not discussing an oath administered by beit din, but one who swore upon sacred writings to prohibit something to himself, and whether he must fulfill that oath. Plainly, for that purpose other sacred writings have the same status as a Torah scroll, as explained in Yoreh Deah, siman 237. This differs from an oath administered by beit din, for we find several matters that constitute an oath regarding a personal oath but not regarding an oath administered by beit din. In particular, since the reason for holding a sacred object is to instill fear, no proof can be brought from a personal oath to an oath administered by beit din. It therefore appears that specifically a Torah scroll is required. The words of Mahari Weil and the Rama require examination.
