Money or an object. Meaning, not only money, where with a partial admission we say that he must take an oath because he may merely be temporarily evading payment until he has funds, as stated in Bava Kamma, Bava Metzia, Kesubos, Gittin, and Shevuos; but even with an object one can similarly say that he is temporarily evading, as stated in Bava Metzia 5b. See Tosafos and Hagahos Ashri there. Likewise, even one who denies everything and therefore takes a heses oath requires the rationale that he may merely be evading payment, as stated in Shevuos 40b.
He may. See Sema and Beer HaGolah.
"There never was such a thing," or "I returned it to you." See the Mishnah at the beginning of Perek HaDayanim: one who is claimed for a maneh and says, "I owe you nothing," is exempt; if on the next day he says, "I gave it to you," he is exempt. See also Shevuos 40b, where a heses oath is administered. We rule according to the first version, as explained in siman 75. The same applies to a partial admission where he says there never was such a thing, and to a partial admission where he says he returned it, as stated there 42a. Thus the text says "or I returned it to you," even though he cannot say there never was such a thing, for one who borrows in the presence of witnesses need not repay in the presence of witnesses.
Or that he purchased it. As stated in Bava Basra 46a, that another person has a chazakah. The discussion there concerns movable property. Even where witnesses know that the article came into his possession, but do not know whether by sale or as a deposit, and it was seen in his possession so that he cannot say there never was such a thing or that he returned it, he may nevertheless say, "I purchased it," and takes a heses oath, as Tosafos and the Rosh explain there.
If he admits part. As stated, "Why did the Torah say that one who admits part of the claim shall swear..." See Bava Metzia 5a and the other cited places.
Likewise... Shevuos 40a, Bava Metzia 3b, and elsewhere.
Even if he does not... As explained above, siman 75 seif 23. See Tosafos in Kesubos 12b and 18a, and the Yerushalmi: wherever two witnesses would obligate payment, one witness obligates an oath. The Yerushalmi challenges this with land and fines, but not with a doubtful claim. See Ran at the beginning of Perek HaDayanim.
Whether with a loan or... See Tosafos, Bava Kamma 107a, and Kesubos 18a.
(Collected notes) Whether with a deposit. We apply the principle of intermingled passages, not uprooted passages. Thus the laws of three judges and experts are derived in the beginning of Sanhedrin without removing the verse from deposits, and "money or vessels" is expounded for the general and specific formulation of the oath of bailees. Further proofs are from Bava Metzia 97, Shevuos, and the shepherd case in the first chapter of Bava Metzia. (End of collected notes.)
But he is liable... According to the first version there, as the Rif proves from the shepherd case at the beginning of Bava Metzia.
And even if he admits... Following Rav Sheshes, since the discussion in Perek HaShoel, Bava Metzia 98 and 100, follows his view. Moreover, this is a case of "here it is," and this is a matter determined by reasoned judgment, as the Rif writes regarding the dispute of Rabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov and the Rabbis concerning one who returns a lost item.
And it is not called "here it is"... but... As stated in Bava Kamma 105b: where he says "here it is" he is exempt, but if it is standing in the meadow it is not called "here it is." This distinction is specifically with a loan. Regarding a deposit, some hold that even if it is in the meadow it is considered available, as indicated by Bava Metzia 5a. See Beer HaGolah and siman 88 seif 24.
(Collected notes) And it is not called "here it is"... This is unlike Rashi there. Tosafos are forced into difficulty, while the Baal HaMaor explains that the Mishnah's proof is not from the admission itself. Rather, Rabbi Shimon, who exempts in a case of "here it is," holds that because the money is ready in his possession it is considered already held by the claimant. So too, in the Mishnah, since the property is before them and ready to be divided, it is viewed as though already divided, leaving no oath and the rule is that the burden of proof rests on the claimant. (End of collected notes.)
And even if... From the first chapter of Kiddushin: "There is no maneh here..."
And some say... for even... Bava Metzia 4b. They write that the passage in Kiddushin is no proof, because there he never became obligated to him, whereas here the creditor acquired a lien. The Ran at the beginning of Perek HaDayanim explains that since a creditor does not acquire a pledge except where it was seized after the loan by authority of beit din, or according to the Scriptural decree of Rabbi Yitzchak, an ordinary pledge is not considered "here it is." The Ritva at the beginning of Bava Metzia distinguishes a debt document, which is "here it is" because the debtor cannot deny it at the time of the claim, from a pledge, where he could have denied it when sued; giving the pledge now does not make it the equivalent of cash already tendered. See Shach.
