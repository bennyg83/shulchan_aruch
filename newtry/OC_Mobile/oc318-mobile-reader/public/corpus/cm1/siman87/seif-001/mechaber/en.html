The laws of a heses oath and a Torah oath. It contains 39 se'ifim.
If one claims money or an object from another, and the defendant could have retained it by claiming, 'This never occurred,' 'I returned it to you,' or, 'I purchased it,' then if he admits part of the claim, he is obligated to take a Torah oath. Similarly, if he denies the entire claim but one witness contradicts him, he must take a Torah oath, even when the debt is known only through that witness. If he denies the entire claim and no single witness contradicts him, he is exempt from a Torah oath, as stated by the Tur in seif 10. This applies both to a loan and to a deposit. He must nevertheless take a heses oath. Even if the claimant says, 'You have one hundred of mine,' and the defendant says, 'This never occurred,' he takes a heses oath, as stated by the Tur in seif 9, provided the claim is definite, as explained above in siman 75. Even one who admits part of the claim is obligated only to take a heses oath if he says, 'Here is the part I admit.' It is not deemed an immediate tender unless the admitted portion is ready in his possession before beit din for him to give at once. If he says, 'It is in my house, and I will give it to you,' this is not an immediate tender. Even giving collateral for the admitted portion is not considered an immediate tender. Some say collateral is considered an immediate tender, since even a document is treated that way.
