This excludes a case where he borrowed money and the lender said, 'Do not repay me except before witnesses,' or where an object was deposited with him before witnesses and the witnesses or beit din now saw the object in his hands before the lawsuit.
In such cases he cannot make those claims concerning the identified object.
The same applies where the claimant demands items ordinarily lent or rented and witnesses currently saw them in the defendant's possession.
In all such cases he lacks the ordinary migo that supports possession.
The rules of hesset and of a partial admission therefore depend on whether, at the time of the claim, the defendant had a legally available full-denial defense regarding the very property in dispute.
Where the property was seen in his possession in circumstances excluding 'I returned it' or 'it never happened,' that supporting migo falls away.
