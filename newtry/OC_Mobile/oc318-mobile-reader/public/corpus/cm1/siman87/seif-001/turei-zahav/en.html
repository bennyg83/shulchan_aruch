(Seif 1) “If he admits part of the claim.” The Sema cites the Ran concerning one who claimed one hundred gold coins from another, twenty as a loan and fifty as damages for an injury. The claimant swore that the defendant injured him, and the defendant denied everything. The defendant is treated as one who admits part of the claim, because we are witnesses that he owes twenty gold coins once the claimant has sworn. It appears to me that the Ran disagrees with the Rashba cited by the Mechaber in seif 5, who rules that there is no partial admission produced from a denial. According to the Ran, the Rashba’s case would all the more certainly count as a partial admission. In the Ran’s case the partial liability arises only from the rabbinic enactment that an injured person swears and collects, yet it is still called a partial admission. How much more so in the Rashba’s case, where the partial liability is imposed by Torah law, because one who says, “I never borrowed,” is regarded as having said, “I did not repay,” and is therefore liable by Torah law. They must therefore disagree. Since the Mechaber ruled in seif 5 like the Rashba, we do not follow this ruling of the Ran. This is why the Rama did not cite it in the Shulchan Aruch.
