If he admits part. If the claimant demands fifty as a loan and fifty for bodily injury, the defendant denies everything, and witnesses establish that the claimant was injured and is believed upon oath as explained in siman 90, then if the claimant has not yet sworn the defendant is deemed one who admits part. If the claimant has already sworn, the defendant is liable to an oath by force of witness testimony. See the Beurim to siman 91, where I explain that even if the claimant swore only after the claim was made in beit din, the defendant remains liable to an oath on the remainder. Ketzot HaChoshen likewise writes this, and it is correct, unlike the Shach. This applies specifically where the claimant cannot collect from encumbered property because of a legal obstacle, for example because the defendant can claim payment or because he owns no land. Otherwise, the claim is one secured by a lien on land, for which no oath is imposed:
Ready in his hand. This refers to a loan. With a deposit, even if the item is in the house, and certainly if it is standing in a meadow, it is considered an immediate tender so long as its value has not diminished. See the Beurim. With stolen property, however, it is not considered an immediate tender until it is actually ready in his hand before beit din:
A pledge. Since appraisal by beit din is required, it is not an immediate tender. But if the creditor was authorized to sell it without permission of beit din and it is clear that he will certainly be able to obtain the amount due when he sells it, it is considered an immediate tender.
