If a person makes a claim against another that would not obligate him to pay money even if he admitted it, then although he denies it, he is not required to take a heses oath. For example, if the claimant says, 'You said you would give me one hundred,' and the defendant says, 'This never occurred,' he takes neither a heses oath nor is an unspecified ban imposed, because even an admission would not obligate him. The same applies when he lacks the means to pay; no oath is administered concerning his denial, as explained in siman 97. If the claimant says, 'You cursed me,' or, 'You defamed me,' and the defendant says, 'This never occurred,' no ban is imposed, and similarly in all comparable cases. {Rama: Some say an unspecified ban is imposed, because if the claim were proved true he would be fined. If one claims, 'You robbed me,' or, 'You stole from me,' and the defendant denies it, then although the claimant's own allegation would disqualify the defendant from taking an oath, the defendant nevertheless swears to him. This applies only when he claims the money, so that the primary claim is monetary. If the primary claim concerns a prohibition, no oath is administered, as stated in Piskei Maharai 34. If a woman claims that a man vowed to give her something as her hire for relations and he denies it, he must swear, as stated in Rivash 370. See note 50.}
