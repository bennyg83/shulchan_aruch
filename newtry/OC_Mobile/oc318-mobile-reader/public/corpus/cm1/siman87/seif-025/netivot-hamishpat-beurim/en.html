You said to me, one hundred. See Shach, seif katan 38. There is a textual error. The correct reading is that likewise where the defendant did not become monetarily liable through two witnesses but only through the claimant's oath, one witness does not generate a Torah oath in the same manner. See Shach, seif katan 51, who writes that one who made a verbal commitment to a poor person can be made to swear regarding it. Tumim asks why this differs from a handshake pledge. In my view, where beit din cannot seize property and can enforce only through personal coercion, such as striking until the person himself performs the obligation, no oath is imposed. Charity is different because property may actually be seized for charity, and therefore an oath may be imposed. There is also a textual error in Shach. The correct citation is to the responsum of Maharam of Prague, siman 726, and the clause beginning "but one who says to sell" is erroneous.
