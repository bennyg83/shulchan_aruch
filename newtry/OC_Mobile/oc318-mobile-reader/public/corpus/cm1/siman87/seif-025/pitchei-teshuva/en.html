Only one oath. Responsa Radbaz, part 2, siman 619, was asked about Reuven, who appeared under a power of attorney against Shimon while also having a separate personal claim against Shimon. Shimon became liable to swear concerning both Reuven's personal claim and the claim prosecuted by Reuven as agent. Reuven demanded one oath regarding his own claim and a second oath regarding the claim of his principal. Is he entitled to two oaths because in the agency claim he acts on behalf of another, as though the principal himself were suing, or does the authorization to 'go litigate for yourself' make both claims his own, so that Shimon should take only one oath with the second matter included by extension? Radbaz answers that the Rambam states the general rule in the first chapter of Laws of Claims and Admissions, and it is attributed also to Rav Hai Gaon in Shaarei Shevuot, though it is not explicit in the Babylonian Talmud. He therefore concludes that Reuven is entitled to two separate oaths. One should not extend the rule beyond this. Although the principal wrote, 'Go litigate for yourself,' that language prevents the defendant from saying, 'You are not my litigant,' but does not transform the agent into the owner of the underlying claim. The principal can still recover the proceeds from him. Thus it is as though two different people asserted two claims against Shimon, and Shimon can be required to take two oaths. Nevertheless, beit din should teach Reuven that he gains nothing by insisting upon two oaths, because the gravity of an oath imposed by extension is the same as that of an independent oath. If Reuven nevertheless insists upon his strict legal right, Radbaz rules that he is entitled to two oaths, since the Gemara concludes that the holder of the authorization remains an agent. Birkei Yosef, siman 24 letter 5, explains that Radbaz needed the rationale that the claims are like those of two different people because Reuven wished Shimon to swear both oaths immediately. If Reuven asked Shimon to swear now only on the agency claim while choosing to postpone the oath on his personal claim, he would be entitled to do so even without that rationale. Nimukei Yosef, cited by the Rama in siman 24, rules that a claimant may say, 'I do not wish you to swear to me now.' A handwritten responsum of the Rif, siman 1, cited by Tashbetz part 2 siman 2, similarly rules that when one party says, 'Take your oath or exempt me from it,' and the other replies, 'I do not require your oath now and I do not release you from it,' the latter is entitled to postpone because the defendant may later admit. Radbaz himself says the same in part 4, siman 203. Birkei Yosef also questions Radbaz's assumption that with claims belonging to two people the defendant must take two separate oaths. Responsa Panim Meirot, part 1 siman 91, cited above in siman 24, appears to hold that even where two people both wish to impose their oaths immediately, the defendant may discharge himself with one oath. Birkei Yosef concludes that Radbaz's ruling remains doubtful to him.
