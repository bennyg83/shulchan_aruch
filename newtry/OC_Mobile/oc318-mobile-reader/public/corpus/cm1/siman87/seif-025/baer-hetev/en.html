On the contrary, with respect to prohibitions the Gemara at the beginning of Kiddushin states regarding a sotah: We have found this principle in a matter of prohibition, but from where do we know it regarding money? The law of extending an oath to additional monetary claims is then derived from the law of sotah, which is a matter of prohibition. Moreover, the Rambam writes in chapter 15 of Hilchot Ishut that a husband may impose a hesset oath upon his wife that she did not miscarry, or that she miscarried a fully formed fetus, where that claim would obligate him to pay her ketubah. The Tur and Shulchan Aruch likewise rule so in Even HaEzer siman 154, seif 15. The Rambam and Semag further write that he may require her to swear while holding a sacred object that she did not commit adultery during the marriage, and afterwards she collects her ketubah. The Tur and Shulchan Aruch likewise rule in Even HaEzer siman 115, seifim 7 and 9. See there. It is difficult to say that those cases speak only where she is presently claiming the ketubah, for the wording does not imply this, and the Maggid Mishneh in Hilchot Ishut there clearly implies that she is not claiming the ketubah. Moreover, if she were making a claim, the husband would be believed, as stated by the Tur and Shulchan Aruch there, siman 154, seif 7. This requires study. Shach.

Rashi in chapter HaPoalim appears to hold that where the intercourse carries a liability of karet, the man is not obligated to pay in human law because the rule that one receives only the more severe punishment exempts him from monetary liability. Since women who engage in prostitution generally do not concern themselves with immersion and are presumed to be niddot, as the Rosh writes in a responsum cited by the Tur in Even HaEzer siman 26, intercourse with them carries karet. He should therefore be exempt from payment in human law even if he admits the act, and if he denies it no oath should be imposed upon him. The Shach responds that the Bach was not precise. Rashi there is speaking of one who has intercourse with his mother, which is an offense punishable by death imposed by beit din. With offenses punishable by karet, the accepted rule throughout the Gemara is not like Rabbi Nechunya ben HaKanah, who would equate karet with death imposed by beit din for this purpose. All the authorities rule this way in many places. This is also explained in siman 350. See also siman 380, seif 3. Shach.
