And he says, 'I did not borrow,' etc. The Beit Yosef cites this wording from a responsum of Rashba. From the fuller version cited in siman 75 one learns that if the claimant demands one hundred, the defendant says, 'I never borrowed,' and even two witnesses testify that he borrowed fifty, after which he changes his claim and says, 'I borrowed fifty and repaid it,' he must pay the fifty because two witnesses established him as a denier and one who says 'I did not borrow' is treated as saying 'I did not repay.' Nevertheless, the fifty is not considered an admission in part for purposes of a Torah oath on the balance, because the witnesses themselves did not establish that the fifty remained unpaid. His liability arose only from the legal consequence of his own first denial. Rabbi Chiyya's principle that witness testimony should be no less effective than partial admission applies only where the witnesses testify that fifty remains owed, parallel to an express admission. Although Rashba's responsum discusses one witness in detail, careful reading shows that the same principle applies to two witnesses in this respect. Since one does not become an established denier merely by one's own unsupported words and may ordinarily move from one exempting claim to another until witnesses contradict the first claim, the text is best read as explicitly including that witnesses came and contradicted him. This rule is accepted according to both Rambam and Rashba.
Likewise, one who becomes liable for part, etc. The reason is that the verse says, 'of which he says, this is it,' indicating that the partial admission that triggers the Torah oath must come from the defendant's own mouth.
Through the substance of his denial. For example, he says, 'I owe you nothing because the facts were such and such,' and he mistakenly thinks those facts exempt him. Beit din does not accept his legal conclusion and rules that those very facts create partial liability. That is not a true partial admission. A similar case appears in Rashba regarding one who says, 'I paid interest on your behalf.' But if he says, 'I owe you nothing because today I paid you this amount and yesterday that amount,' and from his own account it emerges that some sum still remains due, this is considered a partial admission and he is liable for the corresponding oath.
