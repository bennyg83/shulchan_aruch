(Shach, seif katan 15) Although the Ran's words were not clear to me in what he wrote, etc. Note: At first glance one can say that a single witness who obligates an oath is not called 'believed,' because the defendant can contradict him by taking an oath. Only if the defendant refuses to swear must he pay. Therefore the principle that, since the Torah believed him, etc., applies regarding a single witness only where an act has already occurred and the litigant was required to pay because of that witness's testimony. In such a case the witness has effectively risen to monetary effect like two witnesses. See the Ran's wording in the sugya of the witness who testified regarding wine in chapter Kol HaNishba'in. Nevertheless, the Ran did not take this approach.
