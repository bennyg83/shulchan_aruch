And witnesses came that he borrowed, etc. The Sema emends the text this way, because without witnesses the defendant can change from one exempting plea to another. With witnesses proving the loan, however, he remains exempt from the partial admission oath because Rabbi Chiya's first rule does not apply when the witnesses do not expressly testify that the debt remained unpaid; we merely disbelieve his plea of repayment. The Shach objects that this should still fall under Rabbi Chiya's first rule and therefore emends the case to one witness. Although with one witness the defendant may be obligated through the rule that one who cannot swear must pay, the case is not included in Rabbi Chiya's first rule. See Tumim above at the beginning of siman 75, where I concluded that both approaches remain within a doubt in law.
