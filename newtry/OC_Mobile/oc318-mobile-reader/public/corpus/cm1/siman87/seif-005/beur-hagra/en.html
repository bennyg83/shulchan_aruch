There is no oath, etc.; for example. Meaning, the claimant brought one witness that the defendant borrowed a maneh, and the defendant then changed his plea. Had he not initially denied the loan, he would have been believed, for even when two witnesses establish the loan, a borrower is believed to say that he repaid, as explained above in siman 75:13 in the gloss. But once he first denied, two witnesses would render him established as one who falsely denied, and the same consequence follows from one witness through the rule that one who cannot swear must pay. The same applies where he first advanced another plea of this kind.
And likewise there is no, etc. For example, regarding fifty the creditor stipulated, 'Do not repay me except before witnesses,' or a similar case. Rashba proves that the oath of partial admission, derived from 'of which he shall say, this is it,' requires an actual admission. In Rabbi Chiyya's first rule, liability arises from the defendant's own plea, since had he said, 'I borrowed and repaid,' he would not be liable, yet even there the Torah rule is learned specifically from two witnesses. All the more so, without two witnesses it does not apply. There is also a clear proof: although one who does not contradict a single witness is treated as contradicted by two witnesses for payment, he does not thereby swear on the remainder. The Gemara derives the law of witnesses by an a fortiori argument from admission, whereas that argument does not apply to one witness, who cannot impose a fine even in such a case. Thus Rashba's statement that the rule applies specifically with witnesses means that with one witness the a fortiori argument is unavailable. With two witnesses, however, liability does follow, as Shach infers from Rambam and Shulchan Aruch having limited the exemption to one witness. There is still both an admission from the defendant's mouth that he did not repay and testimony that he borrowed, so it is as though the witnesses establish both elements. Even in a fine, this would not be called self-incrimination based merely on his earlier denial. The witnesses need not testify that they never left him. This also explains the discussion in Ketubot 18a and its proposed cases.
