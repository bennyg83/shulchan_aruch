We do not make him swear, etc. Sema explains that perhaps witnesses will later come and testify contrary to his oath, causing desecration of Hashem's name. Yet concern for a needless oath alone would not prevent administering the oath, because it is not certain that such witnesses will come. Shach objects. If the defendant knows his oath is true, why should witnesses testify to the opposite? Are they suspected of falsehood? And if he knowingly swears falsely, the curse rests upon him in any event. Shach therefore explains the case differently. For example, the defendant is asked to swear that he repaid, but he says, 'Tomorrow I will prove payment with a document. If I swear now and later the document proves my case, the oath will have been needless.' Sema writes that if the claimant refuses to wait, he may formally waive reliance on any later proof or testimony concerning this matter; then the defendant must swear now.
