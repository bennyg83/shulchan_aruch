And he is believed by virtue of a migo. See Baer Heitev. Teshuvot Avodat HaGershuni, siman 100, discusses one whom beit din exempted from an oath because a supporting witness testified for him, after which the claimant seized property without witnesses. It rules that the seizure is effective, and the possessor must take a Torah oath contradicting the witness. The principle that wherever the Torah believed one witness he is treated as two does not apply, because the Torah believed a single witness here only to exempt the defendant from an oath, not to determine title to money and remove property from one who has seized it. Teshuvot Shevut Yaakov, part I, siman 151, rules likewise. As to two litigants who sue one another, one being obligated in a Torah oath and the other in a heset oath, see what I wrote above, siman 15, seif 2. The Tumim writes that strictly speaking the Torah oath should be administered first, but the matter is entrusted to beit din. If administering the other oath first may lead to compromise, they may do so. Netivot HaMishpat agrees. See also what I wrote below, siman 93, seif 5, seif katan 8.
