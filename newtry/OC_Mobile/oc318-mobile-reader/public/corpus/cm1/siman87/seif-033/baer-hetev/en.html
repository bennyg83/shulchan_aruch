The Sema writes that this wording implies that the reason he should not swear now is the concern that the matter may later be clarified contrary to his oath, with the result that his oath will be shown to have been false. The Ir Shushan writes similarly. He adds that if the claimant does not wish to wait, he should nullify all witnesses he has concerning this matter and undertake not to bring them later to disqualify the defendant. But because of concern for an oath that may ultimately prove unnecessary, we should not prevent the claimant from imposing the oath, because perhaps the matter will never become known and he will lose his claim. The Shach writes that Tosafot in chapter HaMafkid, the source of this ruling, states the opposite: It appears like an oath taken in vain, and it is disgraceful to swear where the matter will later become independently verifiable. Baal HaTerumot likewise writes so, contrary to Rashi's explanation. It further appears to me that even Rashi agrees that merely because of concern that the claimant may later produce witnesses and thereby disqualify the defendant, we do not create an enactment that benefits a deceiver. Rashi there speaks only of concern that the pledged article may subsequently have become damaged. If the defendant says, "Perhaps you will bring witnesses," it is obvious that we do not heed him, because on his own premise he is certainly lying. This law can therefore arise only where, even if witnesses later come, the defendant will not be disqualified but will merely become obligated to pay. Examples include where the claimant later produces the document, as stated at the end of siman 75; where the defendant says, "I do not know whether you lent me"; or where the parties stipulated that repayment could be made only before witnesses and the defendant claims, "I repaid you." In all such cases he is not disqualified, because even though he must pay, it remains possible that his oath was true and he did in fact repay. The same applies to similar cases. It follows that the reason given by the Ir Shushan and Sema regarding witnesses, namely concern for a false oath, is incorrect. One should not object from the end of siman 75, where we say that if the defendant says, "Nullify all witnesses," we do not heed him. I already explained there that this is where he does not identify the witnesses specifically, whereas here he does identify them. Shach. See Responsa Radbaz, simanim 150 and 164.
