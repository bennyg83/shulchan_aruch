There is no, etc.; therefore, etc. We rule that the benefit of discretion is not money. See below, siman 203:1, following Ulla in Kiddushin 58a. Rav Asi of Huzal agrees, and one sage's view does not prevail over two. Although Rava in Nedarim 85a appears to treat discretionary benefit as money, he speaks there only to establish one tannaitic view and does not adopt it, as is clear from Bava Metzia 11b. Rambam rules in several places that such benefit is not money, following the conclusion of Kiddushin and Pesachim. This also explains his ruling in the laws of vows, where the relevant prohibition operates through the possibility that the property could become permitted to him, not because the benefit itself is money. The same reasoning applies to gifts for the poor. Even according to the view that discretionary benefit is money, Mordechai writes that no hesset oath is imposed here, because one version of the Gemara limits hesset to a case involving an actual monetary basis, and the first version disagrees only where an ordinary monetary claim exists.
