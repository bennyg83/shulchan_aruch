And he answers that he already returned it, he is exempt without an oath. This is difficult, for the Rambam and Tur write at the end of siman 95 that concerning consecrated property one takes an oath resembling a Torah oath, and they conclude that funds of the poor and contemporary charitable endowments have the law of ordinary private property and an oath is imposed concerning them. The Mechaber writes likewise at the beginning of siman 95. The Mordechai cites a responsum of Maharam concerning Shimon, who gave Reuven two zekukim to invest and to distribute a fixed amount annually to charity. Shimon later demanded their return, while Reuven answered that the money had been given to him to distribute both principal and profit as he saw fit and refused to return it. Maharam ruled that if Shimon admits that he vowed the money to charity but merely does not want it to remain under Reuven's control because he wishes to distribute it himself, Reuven need not return it to him and need not swear that his account is true, because Reuven has only the benefit of performing the mitzvah. According to that responsum there is no difficulty. Rama's wording can perhaps likewise be reconciled if we assume a minor textual error: instead of "he answers that he already returned it," the text should read, "he answers that he has already taken possession of it," meaning possession of the mitzvah of distribution and that he does not wish to relinquish it. This would match the responsum exactly. Darkei Moshe's abbreviated formulation, however, is difficult, because it says that Shimon claims from Reuven charitable funds in his possession so that Shimon may distribute them, and Reuven denies the claim, and it rules that Reuven need not swear. The word "denies" sounds like a total denial. One can force an explanation that Reuven denies Shimon's right to reclaim them and says they were given to remain in his hands. But the Ir Shushan explicitly understands Rama literally, that Reuven says he already returned them, and adds that the same applies if he says he already distributed them to the poor. That reading cannot stand in light of the difficulty stated above.
