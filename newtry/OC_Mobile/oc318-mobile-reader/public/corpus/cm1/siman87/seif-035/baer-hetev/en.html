Exempt. The Sema asks from what is stated in siman 95, that one swears in the manner of a Torah oath concerning consecrated property, and that charity funds and consecrated funds in our time have the status of private property and one administers an oath concerning them. See there for his resolution. He further writes that there is a slight scribal error in the Rama: where it says, "and this one responds that he already returned it to him," it should read, "that he retained it." The Taz writes that there is no difficulty at all. In siman 95 the Tur explicitly writes of a case where the community has a claim, because the community acts as the hand of the poor and it is as though the poor themselves are claiming. Here, by contrast, the claimant himself admits that the money belongs to the poor and says that he gave it to the depositary, while the depositary denies this. It is therefore money without a specific claimant. Only beit din could claim it on behalf of the poor, and here they know nothing about the matter. The Chacham Tzvi writes in his glosses: "I do not know what difficulty they saw. At the end of chapter HaChovel the Gemara explicitly distinguishes between a fixed amount and one that is not fixed. Here the amount is necessarily not fixed, and it is therefore money without a claimant, which is why he is exempt from an oath."
