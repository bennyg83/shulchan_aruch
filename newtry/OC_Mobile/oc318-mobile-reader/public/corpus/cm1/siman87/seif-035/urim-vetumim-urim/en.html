The benefit of discretion. Even according to the authority who treats the right of discretionary benefit as monetary property, the Sages did not institute a hesset oath concerning that interest alone.
That he already returned it. Sema reads the text instead as, 'that he already took possession of it in order to distribute it to the poor.' According to that reading there is no oath because the dispute concerns only discretionary benefit. But if the defendant says he already returned the charity property and the claimant denies that, there is an ordinary monetary dispute, and we rule that one can swear concerning property dedicated to the poor. Taz writes that such an oath applies only where the claimant is the community or beit din, who act as the hand of the poor. Against a private person, the defendant can say, 'You are not my proper litigant.' See Tumim. According to that reasoning even beit din could not extract, because beit din itself does not possess definite personal knowledge. I further wrote that even if the claimant is the charity collector, there is no oath according to the view that a migo can exempt from an oath. See there.
