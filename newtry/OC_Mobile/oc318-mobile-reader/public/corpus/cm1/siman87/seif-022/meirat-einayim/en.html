And the one imposing the oath shall answer Amen. In the Perishah I wrote that although the Tur and the Mechaber state in simanim 71 and 82 that he need not answer Amen, there is no contradiction. There they discuss a case in which the person taking the oath wishes to leave the synagogue, and they teach that he need not remain there merely in order that the other party answer Amen. Here, however, the one imposing the oath is presumably present and wants the oath to be taken before him according to the understanding of the Omnipresent and of beit din. Since he is present, he should answer Amen initially, so that the oath will inspire greater fear. This is clear. See the Perishah for what I wrote further there.
