[Shach, letter 10] And he brought proof from Tosafot in the chapter Shevuot HaDayanim. Note: In my humble opinion, that source proves only a case like the one discussed in Responsa Mahari Beit Levi, involving doubt whether there is an obligation to take the oath of a guardian. It does not prove a case of doubt whether there is a Torah oath. One may say that there, because there are two versions regarding whether a heset oath is required on a claim of “it never happened,” and because by Torah law he is certainly exempt, the doubt concerns only rabbinic law, so Tosafot write that in doubt he is exempt. The Ran there likewise writes that since by strict law he is exempt in every case and the Gemara is uncertain how Rav Nachman instituted the oath, we follow the lenient course. It appears to me, however, that there is stronger proof from Tosafot, Shevuot 40a, s.v. “To teach you the force of Rabban Gamliel,” who write that the strength of a rabbinic ruling is demonstrated by exemption. Their meaning is analogous to the rule that the strength of a lenient ruling is greater, because the one who permits must bring proof, while the force of one who prohibits is not as great since in doubt the item is prohibited. So too regarding an oath, the force of one who obligates is greater, because he must bring proof, whereas in doubt the defendant is exempt. Thus they state explicitly that in a case of doubt whether a Torah oath is required, we do not administer the oath. Examine this carefully.
