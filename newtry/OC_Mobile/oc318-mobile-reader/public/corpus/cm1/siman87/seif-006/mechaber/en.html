If the defendant admits part of the claim and one witness supports him, some say the witness exempts him from an oath, while others say the witness does not exempt him. {Rama: The first opinion is primary, and it is proper to rule accordingly, as stated by the Tur in siman 84. The same applies when the claimant's handwriting supports the defendant; he is exempt from an oath. Some say likewise that a claimant who ordinarily swears and collects may collect without an oath if one witness supports him. If a person became obligated to take an oath, reversed it to the opposing party, and has one witness supporting him, he is exempt from the oath, as stated in Responsa Maimoniyot, Sefer Mishpatim, responsum 81. The rule that one supporting witness exempts from an oath applies only when the defendant makes a claim that can be believed without a migo. If he is believed only through a migo based on a different claim, he must swear as though he had made that other claim. See notes 1, 80, and 86.}
