One who became obligated... Maharam brought proof from Shevuos 32b that in the case of Rabbi Abba's silver bar the opposing party need not take an oath, as explained above in siman 75. By contrast, where one says, "I know about fifty and do not know about fifty," he held that the claimant swears and collects. He also cited Rabbeinu Tam in Bava Kamma 62a regarding the takanah for property entrusted in a tied sack. According to this approach, the supporting witness explains why no oath is required in Rabbi Abba's case. There are additional proofs and disputes among the Geonim, Raavad, Ramban, and Rosh as to cases where the defendant says he does not know the amount. For our law, however, the essential point is that where one witness supports the party who otherwise would swear and collect, that witness exempts him from the oath. This applies likewise where an oath has been reversed onto the opposing party. This is the principal ruling, unlike the Taz. The Taz asks from Shevuos, where in the case of a suspected oath-taker the opposing party swears and collects, although one witness supports him. The answer is that the ordinary rule of a supporting witness does not operate in the same way against a suspected oath-taker, for otherwise money could constantly be extracted through a single witness. See Tosafos in Shevuos 47a and Bava Metzia 5a, and the responsum of Maharam; the Shach likewise rules this way.
(Collected notes) One who became obligated... See Hagahos Maimoniyos, Mishpatim, siman 61. It first rules that one who swears and collects, when supported by one witness, collects without an oath, deriving this from the case of Rabbi Abba's silver bar according to its approach. It then extends this to one upon whom the oath was reversed, and gives further proofs that a supporting witness exempts from an oath. Even though its first proof is not compelling according to our ruling that in an ordinary case of "I know fifty and do not know fifty" the opposing party need not swear, the substantive rule remains true, as stated above. (End of collected notes.)
And that which we say... Meaning, the case of Bava Basra 70a, where the Gemara asks..., and says.... Although he has one witness that he returned it, he must still swear, because we do not believe him merely because one witness supports his claim while the document remains in the other party's hand. His credibility comes only from the fact that he could have claimed that the item was lost by accident, and on such a claim he would have been obligated to swear. This also answers Rabbeinu Yonah's objection cited by the Rosh at the beginning of Bava Metzia, unlike the Rosh's conclusion there that a supporting witness would exempt even in that circumstance.
