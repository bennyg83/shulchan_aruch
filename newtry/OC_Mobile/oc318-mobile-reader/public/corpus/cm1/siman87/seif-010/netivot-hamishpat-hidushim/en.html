We do not extract. Nevertheless we place him under ban. Shach, unlike the Sema. This rule of not extracting applies to ordinary oaths. But in an oath imposed because of one witness, if the witness is present and creates a Torah oath, then where the claimant seized property in satisfaction of his note after payment, he may force a Torah oath upon him. For refusal to take a Torah oath we may descend to his property. As explained above in siman 58, however, he may impose that Torah oath after payment only where the witness had not already testified before beit din. If the witness had already testified and beit din had already imposed a rabbinic oath on that basis, he cannot later impose a Torah oath after payment. The rule that we do not compel collection from property applies specifically to those who by Torah law could take without an oath and the Sages merely required an oath. But where by Torah law they cannot collect even with an oath, such as a worker, robbed person, injured person, and similar cases, if they seized and refuse to swear we remove the property from them.
