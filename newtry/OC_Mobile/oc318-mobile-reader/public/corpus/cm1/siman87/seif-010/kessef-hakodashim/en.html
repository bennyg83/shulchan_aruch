(See Shach, seif katan 51.) The Shach cites the Shiltei Gibborim that if a person vowed to sell a particular object he can likewise be compelled by oath, and the Shach challenges this. In my humble opinion the Shiltei Gibborim may mean that when he says the person "can retract," he means through formal release of the vow. And when he says that regarding a vow to sell he is obligated, the obligation also derives from the vow itself. The case may involve a poor person, where the promise can also have the status of a charitable vow, as explained in Bava Batra, chapter Yesh Nochalin, concerning the son of Yose ben Yoezer, where the Gemara applies the principle that a statement to consecrated property is effective like delivery. See there and Tosafot. In any event, as a practical matter it is clear that where the intended recipient is poor, one who vowed to sell is bound by oath as well.
