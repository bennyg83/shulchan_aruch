This means that by strict law he would collect without an oath, such as a creditor holding a promissory note where the debtor says, "Swear to me," in which case the oath is only rabbinic, as stated in siman 82, seif 8. The same applies to one who comes to collect from orphans, a woman who has impaired her ketubah by admitting partial payment, one witness who testifies that the debt was paid, and similar cases. But a hired worker, a victim of robbery, an injured person, and similar claimants, if they seize property we remove it from them when they refuse to swear, because by strict law they would not collect even with an oath, and it was the Sages who enacted that they may collect upon swearing. This is clearly implied by the Ran, and so writes Gidulei Terumah. This is straightforward. See the end of siman 108, seif 14. The Taz explains that "we do not remove it from him" means that we do not take the money away from him until he swears the mishnaic oath, as with other persons who swear and collect. Nevertheless, the other party may require him to take a hesset oath that he holds none of the other's property, and he is placed under a ban until he swears, as stated in seif 9 and above, siman 82, seif 7. See there. Gidulei Terumah further writes that the same applies if a hesset oath was reversed and the claimant seized property but refuses to swear, in which case we remove the property from him. This too is straightforward. The rulings of Maharam of Rothenburg cited by Recanati state: In a case where one is obligated in a rabbinic oath and refuses to swear, while the claimant has seized property from him, I am uncertain and the matter requires study. If the defendant is disqualified from taking an oath and the opposing party seized property, it appeared to me that the seizure is ineffective, for otherwise no disqualified person could survive, since anyone could seize his money and demand a hesset oath from him. I remain uncertain. The Shach concludes: It appears clear to me that the seizure is ineffective where the defendant is disqualified from swearing.
And he seized. Even if he seized in the presence of witnesses. This is also the view of the Rosh, Ramban, Ran, and Baal HaTerumot. The Mechaber likewise rules this way without qualification in Even HaEzer siman 56, seif 11, and this is the primary ruling. Responsa Maharit, siman 112, also rules so. Ibid.
