One who gave another his hand as a pledge is not thereby exempt from taking an oath. {Rama: This applies specifically when he would otherwise be obligated to swear, such as when the claimant says that the defendant gave his hand as a pledge to pay what he owes and the defendant denies it. But if the claimant says that the defendant gave his hand as a pledge to give him a gift, and the defendant denies it, he is exempt from an oath. Beit din merely warns him concerning his pledge. The same applies if one says that the other swore to give him a particular item and the latter says the oath was forgiven, as stated in Responsa of the Rosh, rule 10, responsum 2; or if he says the oath or hand pledge was subject to such-and-such condition. He is believed through a migo that he could have denied it. See above, seif 16.}
