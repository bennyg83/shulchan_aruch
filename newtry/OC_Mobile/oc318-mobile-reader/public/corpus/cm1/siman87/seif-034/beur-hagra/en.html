One who gives a hand pledge... Although a hand pledge is an expression of one's faithfulness, since he pledges his faith in God, and violating it is comparable to denial of a fundamental principle, as reflected in the verses concerning one who strikes hands with a stranger and one who gives his hand to covenant, nevertheless it is not an oath taken while holding a sacred object. See Hagahos Maimoniyos, Hilchos Shevuos, siman 3, and Yoreh Deah siman 237 seif 10 in the gloss. Shevuos 38b states that a judge who administers an oath improperly is considered to have erred. Here the claimant did not say, "Give me your pledge and be exempt," but the hand pledge itself occurred independently, like the illustration in Bava Kamma. All the more so if he gave his hand when no oath was then required. See Mordechai at the beginning of Perek HaDayanim. His implication is that where holding a sacred object is required, the hand pledge does not suffice, but where it is unnecessary he is discharged even by a pledge concerning future conduct. See above, the gloss to seif 16.
And specifically... Even if there are witnesses to the hand pledge, since his only obligation is to keep that pledge it is as though he is obligated only in that form of oath, which he already made. Even one already obligated in an oath can sometimes discharge it by voluntarily swearing, as above. Thus there is no second oath upon an oath, and even a heses oath is not added to a Torah oath according to the Rif. The Tur similarly explains that an oath is imposed only on denial of money, not on denial of an oath, even when monetary consequences result. This also explains the continuation, "The same applies if..."
(Collected notes) And specifically... As above in the gloss to seif 16. Here it is as though he never became obligated in any oath other than the hand pledge, for any duty to pay exists only in order to enforce that pledge. Even according to the first opinion there, which does not discharge him where he had previously become obligated in an oath about the past, here his obligation is only the hand pledge concerning future conduct. Furthermore, there is no inherent monetary obligation, only the religious duty to keep his oath, as explained in siman 209 seif 4 in the gloss. Therefore no further oath is required, as explained above in seif 25. (End of collected notes.)
