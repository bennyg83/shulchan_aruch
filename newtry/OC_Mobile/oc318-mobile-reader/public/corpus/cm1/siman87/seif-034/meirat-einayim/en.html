He is not exempt from the oath. As Rama explains, the case is where Reuven claims that Shimon owes him one hundred and Shimon answers that he already paid. Shimon is therefore obligated to take an oath of hesset. He cannot escape this oath by saying, "I already gave you my hand as a solemn pledge that I would pay by a certain time, and that time has passed." The earlier undertaking, made before the due date, is not equivalent to the present oath, which is taken after the time has passed and states that he actually paid. If he did not pay, this later oath would itself be false. This applies specifically where the claim is for an existing debt, so that even without the solemn pledge the defendant would have to swear when sued for nonpayment. But if the claim is merely that he pledged to give a gift and has not done so, and he denies it or says that he already gave it, no oath was instituted merely because of the alleged solemn pledge.
