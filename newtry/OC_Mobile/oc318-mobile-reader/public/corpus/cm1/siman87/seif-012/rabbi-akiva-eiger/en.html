[Sema, letter 18] Alternatively, it concerns a case where the claimant agreed to take the oath. Note: In my humble opinion, the Sema means a case where, before the witness arrived, the defendant was obligated in a Torah oath and the claimant agreed to take the oath in his place, and afterward the witness arrived. In such a case we do not say that the claimant can protest, “Had I known this, I would not have reversed the oath,” for it may be that he indeed could make that claim, but since he does not wish to swear he cannot compel the defendant to have the claimant swear, because one supporting witness exempts the defendant, as the Shach writes in answering the Sema’s difficulty. Yet the Shach’s words themselves are difficult to me. According to the Sema’s first answer, that the case concerns one obligated only in a heset oath, even without the supporting witness the claimant can say: “Originally you could reverse the oath to me, but now that I have one witness you are obligated in a Torah oath, so either swear or pay.” It must therefore be that the effect of reversing the oath is that even if the defendant later wishes to swear, that is ineffective. The Sema therefore properly asks that the claimant can say, “Had I known this, I would not have reversed the oath.” To this he writes the alternative case, and then the Shach’s answer can indeed be applied. Examine this carefully.
