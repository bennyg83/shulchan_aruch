If the claimant says. Mahari Weil discusses an opinion that a claimant may say, "I waive my document and wish to proceed as an ordinary oral claimant," thereby imposing only hesset and gaining the ability to reverse it. He notes that Rosh in Perek HaKotev does not accept such a consequence from the distinction between Torah and rabbinic oath. The Shach offers several answers. His strongest is that under our rule a supporting witness can exempt. If the claimant discards the document and proceeds as an oral claimant, the existing witness that had supported collection may now become a supporting witness for the defendant and remove the hesset entirely. Thus the strategy does not produce a meaningful advantage. More generally, where one witness originally created a Torah oath, if the defendant refuses to swear and pays, the claimant does not afterward have to take a separate hesset merely because the dispute is now framed as an oral claim. So long as the witness stands uncontradicted by oath, he has the legal force necessary to compel the defendant to swear or pay. Payment does not erase that evidentiary structure. Rashba's discussion in Perek HaKotev likewise indicates that the practical differences cannot be reduced simply to the ability to reverse a hesset oath. The entire relationship between the document, witness, and supporting witness must be considered. This supports Mahari Weil's objection to using the case as a broad proof that every claimant can choose freely between the document route and an oral hesset route.
