Therefore, even if the first oath was also taken while holding a sacred object, he still must swear now. Otherwise, in any event, the law of seif 14 would require a new oath. If the opposing claimant says, 'According to your own statement you are suspected of false swearing. I will therefore swear and collect,' Shach says we listen to him, as explained below in siman 92. See Tumim. Baal HaTerumot in the name of Rabbi Sherira Gaon, the opinion copied by Mechaber, appears not to accept the rule allowing the claimant to swear and collect. Nevertheless, as practical law, in Tumim I already defended Rif's view that in this situation a person can become suspected on the basis of his own admission. Moreover, Mechaber and Tur later rule in Rabbi Meir HaLevi Abulafia's name, without dissent, that the claimant may swear and collect. Therefore the correct practical law is as Shach states. Why is the defendant not treated as one returning a lost claim, since he could have denied any partial liability? The answer is that ordinarily a person is reluctant to brazenly deny his creditor, but here he has already demonstrated brazenness by denying and swearing falsely. One must therefore say that now he regrets that conduct and no longer wishes to be brazen, and his admission can again generate a partial-admission oath. See Tumim at length.
