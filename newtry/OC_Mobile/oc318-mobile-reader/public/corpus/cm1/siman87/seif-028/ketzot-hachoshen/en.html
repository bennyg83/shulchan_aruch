He is not disqualified from taking an oath. The Tur says that if he admitted the falsehood without doing so as part of repentance, he may still swear concerning the balance because he is not disqualified. The Sema understands this to mean that he must take a new oath on the remainder because he has now admitted that his first oath was false. In my view no new oath is necessary merely because the first oath was false. Bava Kamma teaches that one who first swore falsely concerning a deposit and later changed his account is not automatically required to swear again where he would otherwise have a migo. Tosafot explicitly says that even though he admitted the first oath was false, he can be believed without a second oath. Accordingly, if one first swore hesset that he never borrowed and later admits borrowing but says he repaid, the prior oath can still suffice where no new Torah oath has arisen. The present case is different only because he first took merely hesset and, after admitting part, now becomes obligated in a Torah partial admission oath with a sacred object. The second oath is required because the legal character of the obligation changed, not because the first oath was false.
