[Shach, subparagraph 72] And if the claimant wishes, he collects against his will. Note: This means that the reason the defendant is not disqualified from swearing is that the claimant can insist that he must swear, and one is not disqualified on the basis of his own admission, as below in siman 93. This is puzzling to me. There it is possible for the claimant to say, "I do not believe you that you are disqualified." Here, however, the defendant says that his earlier hesset oath was false, so by his own account he certainly is disqualified and cannot be made to swear. The ruling above in seif 14 means that the defendant may say, "I will swear, for my hesset oath was truthful." In truth, however, the defendant may reverse the oath, because the claimant says that he swore falsely and is therefore disqualified from swearing, so the opposing party should swear and collect. The practical ruling requires study.
