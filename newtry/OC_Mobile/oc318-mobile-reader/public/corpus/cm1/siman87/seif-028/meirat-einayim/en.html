A person does not become disqualified from taking an oath through his own admission. The Tur writes that if he admitted, not in the course of repentance, that he had sworn falsely, he swears concerning the remainder, because he does not disqualify himself. In the Perishah I explained that this means he must now swear again concerning the remainder, since he admitted that his first oath was false. Specifically when he admits by way of repentance do we say that if he owed more than he admitted he would have confessed that as well, and therefore he need not swear again. The Mechaber omitted the Tur's statement that he swears concerning the remainder. This follows his approach in the Beit Yosef, where he writes that the Tur's language is not precise, for even if the admission was not by way of repentance, he already swore concerning the remainder and was exempt through that oath, since he is not disqualified by his own admission. The Beit Yosef's intention must be that either way he is exempt. If the admission was by way of repentance, even if a person could disqualify himself, his remorse and confession indicate that had he owed more he would have admitted it. If the admission was not by way of repentance, he is still exempt by the oath he already took, because a person does not disqualify himself. In the Perishah and Derishah I wrote that this explanation is not primary, and the Ir Shushan also rejected it.
