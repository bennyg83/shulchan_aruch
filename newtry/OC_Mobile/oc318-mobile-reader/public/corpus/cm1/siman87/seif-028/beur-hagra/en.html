And if he admitted, etc.; he is not disqualified, etc. As stated in chapters 3 and 8 of Sanhedrin concerning testimony, a person cannot render himself wicked. The same applies to oath disqualification, for the two are governed alike, as explained above in siman 92:3. Tur writes that he then swears concerning the remainder, meaning according to the law of partial admission. Although he already swore falsely according to his own admission, that prior oath was only hesset, and he is not disqualified through his own confession. A person may obligate himself monetarily, as stated in Bava Kamma and Shevuot. With respect to his own monetary loss he may even accept disqualification upon himself, but he cannot use his confession to harm another. This is also the principle in Yevamot and at the beginning of Makkot: he is believed to obligate himself but not to contradict his prior testimony as against others.
