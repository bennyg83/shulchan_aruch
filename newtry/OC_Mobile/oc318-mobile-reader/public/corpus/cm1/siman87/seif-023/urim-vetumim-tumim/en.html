And then he need not, etc. The Shach asks that in Even HaEzer siman 93 the Rama rules, based on a responsum of the Rashba, that a widow who swears concerning her ketubah must take the oath in the presence of the heirs. This appears inconsistent with the rule here that the oath need not be taken in the opposing party's presence. The distinction appears to be between one who swears and is thereby exempt and one who swears and collects. Where the oath merely exempts the person in possession, and the opposing party has died so there is no concern that the claimant will be embarrassed to lie in his presence, the Sages were lenient and did not require the oath to be administered before the heirs. But where the person swears in order to collect, the oath itself creates the basis to compel payment from the other side. We do not impose a monetary obligation on a person in his absence when his presence can reasonably be required. The principle is comparable to the rule that an owner should come and stand by his ox when judgment is rendered concerning it. Therefore the widow must swear in the heirs' presence before collecting from them.
