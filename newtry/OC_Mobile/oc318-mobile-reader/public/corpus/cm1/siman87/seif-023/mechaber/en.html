Every oath required by law is taken in the presence of the opposing party or in the presence of the witness, if the claimant knows the truth only through that witness. In the latter case, he need not swear in the claimant's presence. If they administered the oath outside his presence, and he swore properly and subject to the intent of beit din, he is exempt. {Rama: This is so even though he did not swear in his own locality, where many people are present and one might say he would be ashamed before them. Initially, however, the claimant may require him to swear in the place where the transaction occurred, where the local people know about it, or in another public place. If the defendant is not traveling there now, the claimant must wait until he comes there, and the defendant must then swear there; he cannot say, 'I will swear only in my own city.'}
