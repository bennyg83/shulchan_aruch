(Seif 34.) "He is not exempt from the oath." Meaning that afterward he must still swear concerning his denial, namely that he repaid. This resembles the case mentioned by the Rama there. Even according to the "some say" in seif 17, cited by the Rama, that if he initially swore properly he is exempt from a second oath, here he agrees that the handshake does not exempt him because it was not the proper oath. Here there was only a handshake, without the serious warnings given when heset is administered. This is also implied by Hagahot Maimoniyot, chapter 11 of Hilchot Shevuot, which explains the reason through the greater severity of an oath compared with a handshake. The Sema gives a different reason for why he is not exempt here: at the time of the first oath he had not yet uttered a falsehood, whereas now, in the second oath, he swears that he already repaid. This reasoning appears in Responsa Rashba, siman 964, which is the "some say" in seif 17. But here, which follows Hagahot Maimoniyot, that explanation cannot be correct because the source specifically bases the rule on the lesser severity of a handshake compared with an oath.
