He denied everything... This is demonstrated in Perek HaKosev, where the Gemara says, "If he is clever..." Rashi and Tosafos there were pressed to explain the practical difference. The Rif and Rosh likewise discuss it. The Raavad explains that even after she swore concerning her ketubah he can require a Torah oath; see the responsa attributed to Ramban, siman 109.
Likewise if... This is demonstrated at the beginning of Bava Metzia 5a in the shepherd case. The Gemara asks that even now he should be obligated under Rabbi Nasan's rule. The sequence is best explained as follows: when he initially denied everything, he already took a heses oath, before it became known that he was disqualified. Rabbi Zeira then says that if Rabbi Chiya's rule applies he must take the Torah oath even though he already swore. Later the Gemara asks how the oath can now be imposed, since it turns out the prior oath was worthless because he was suspected. This explains both rulings in the Shulchan Aruch and Rama and also why the Gemara asks that he is a shepherd, despite the stronger concern that he was a robber: it indicates that an oath had initially been administered.
