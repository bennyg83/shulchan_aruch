The procedure for an oath administered by the judges is as follows. The person swearing holds a Sefer Torah in his arm, stands, and swears using the Divine Name or an appellation, either as an oath or as a curse, spoken by himself or by the judges. How does he swear in his own words? He says, 'I hereby swear by the Name, by Hashem, God of Israel,' or, 'I hereby swear by the One Whose Name is Merciful,' or, 'by the One Whose Name is Gracious, that I owe this person nothing.' Similarly, he may say, 'Let me be cursed by Hashem, God of Israel,' or, 'by the One Whose Name is Merciful,' or, 'by the One Whose Name is Gracious, if I possess anything belonging to this person.' How is it administered through the judges? They say, 'We administer an oath to you by the Name, by Hashem, God of Israel, or by the One Whose Name is Gracious, that you possess nothing belonging to this person,' and he answers, 'Amen.' Alternatively, they say, 'Let so-and-so son of so-and-so be cursed by Hashem, God of Israel, or by the One Whose Name is Gracious, if he possesses money belonging to so-and-so and does not admit it,' and he answers, 'Amen.' {Rama: Some say an oath administered by the judges must be taken before a minyan, as stated in Mahariv 184, or on a public court day. I have not seen this practiced.}
