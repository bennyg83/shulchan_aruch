(Seif 35.) "He is exempt without an oath." The Sema asks from siman 95, where it says that nowadays oaths are imposed concerning sacred property. It appears there is no difficulty. In siman 95 the Tur explicitly writes that if the community has a claim by virtue of property dedicated to the poor, the community acts as the hand of the poor, as though the poor themselves were making the claim. Here, by contrast, the claimant gave the custodian money, and once the custodian denies the claim while the claimant himself admits that the money belongs to the poor and that he merely possesses the benefit of disposal, this changes nothing. The custodian is not a litigant vis-à-vis the poor, and this is money with no present claimant. Specifically where authorized representatives sue on behalf of the poor does the rule differ, but here the poor know nothing of the matter. If representatives explicitly sued the custodian in the name of the poor, perhaps he would be required to swear, but that is not the present case. This appears to me correct. The gloss adds: I do not know what difficulty they saw. At the end of Perek HaChovel we explicitly distinguish between a fixed amount and an unfixed amount. Here the amount is necessarily unfixed, so it is money with no claimant and he is therefore exempt from an oath. This is straightforward.
