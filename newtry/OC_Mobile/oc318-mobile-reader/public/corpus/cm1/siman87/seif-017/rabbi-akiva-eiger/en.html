[Shach, letter 31] In the case of an employer who disputes with the worker. Note: I am astonished, for here there is no need to invoke the law of reversing an oath. It is obvious that the employer can impose an oath upon the worker, since this is no worse than a case where two witnesses testify that he hired him, in which the worker collects only after taking an oath. This requires study. As to what the Shach wrote initially regarding one who claims concerning a deposit documented in writing, “I returned it,” one cannot object similarly that even without reversal the claimant can impose an oath by saying, “Swear to me that you did not return it to me,” just as with a loan document. For one may answer that there is a practical difference within the fixed time, according to the Shach’s ruling in siman 108, seif katan 7, that the custodian is believed by a migo that he could have claimed unavoidable accident. In that case one must indeed resort to the law of reversing the oath.
