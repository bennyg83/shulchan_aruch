If he is among those who swear and are exempt, etc. This excludes those who swear and collect. If such a claimant refuses to swear, there is ordinarily no need to compel him, since he can simply forgo the oath and not collect. If he already seized property and refuses to swear, however, this view would appear to require returning it, because he seized without authorization and is unlike one who swears to be exempt, who already holds the property lawfully. The next seif cites an opinion that even one who swears and collects, if he seized first, is not dispossessed. At first glance one might think he should then be placed under a ban or even compelled until he swears that the money is truly owed and that the seizure was proper, all the more so than one who swears to be exempt. But one can distinguish: with those who swear and are exempt, without compulsion the rabbinic enactment would be ineffective because every defendant could simply refuse to swear. With those who swear and collect, the enactment originally meant only that beit din would not award collection without an oath. If the claimant already seized for himself, perhaps the Sages never imposed an oath in that situation. I explained at the beginning of siman 82 that the Rosh and Tur appear to hold that one who has an authenticated document and seizes before swearing is not dispossessed and is not even placed under a general ban, because the authenticated document itself supports the truth of his claim. If he has not seized and refuses to swear, however, the document is removed from him so that he cannot later seize. Ran distinguishes further between seizure of money, which is retained, and seizure of movable property, which is returned.
