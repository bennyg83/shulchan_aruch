And beit din does not descend to his property. Maharam Recanati was uncertain where a person liable for a rabbinic oath refuses to swear and the claimant has seized his property. In my view the seizure is ineffective. Since beit din itself may not descend to the defendant's assets merely because he refuses a rabbinic oath, the claimant has no greater right to perform self help against those assets. His remedy is the court imposed ban or other sanction, not seizure. The rule concerning a creditor holding a document is different. Where the creditor must swear before collecting and refuses, the debtor may withhold payment because the original rabbinic enactment itself conditioned collection on the creditor's oath. The money never becomes collectible until that oath is supplied. Likewise, where the defendant is a suspected person who cannot take hesset, the Shach rules that the claimant's seizure does not help. This accords with the principle that private seizure cannot exceed the coercive power beit din itself possesses.
