Seif 25 in the gloss. (And the same applies if he has nothing.) At first glance this refers to a case where it is certainly known that he has no assets, like the case in the Responsa of the Rosh concerning a woman who certainly had none, and like the case in siman 99. Where it is not known, one can make him swear either that he has no property or that the underlying claim never occurred. This also appears from the Shach at the end of siman 96. There is, however, room for analysis. The issue resembles denial of an oath concerning "I have nothing." The general rule regarding denial of an oath is that a claim such as "we were never partners" can constitute denial of an oath because, even if he admitted the partnership, the claimant would still have no definite monetary claim. This differs from a claim of "the matter never occurred, and in fact you owe me," where he flatly contradicts the claimant on both assertions. According to this, the Shach at the end of siman 96 follows his own view in siman 99, adopting the Rambam's position that the oath "I have nothing" is itself imposed by law, like the oath that entrusted property is not in one's possession. Perhaps the substantial circumstances there also give the claim the quality of certainty, even though the oaths of partners likewise rest on substantial grounds. According to the authorities who hold that the oath of having no assets is not imposed by strict law, the matter may differ. See also siman 82, seif 3. There too one could seemingly ask that the case is denial of money that cannot be collected except with an oath, as the Shach writes here, in siman 75, and at the end of siman 22. One may distinguish that there, by law, the money must first be deposited. This requires study. Regarding a general ban, however, it appears that one may impose it even where the only denial is denial of an oath obligation. This seif supplies no proof to the contrary.
