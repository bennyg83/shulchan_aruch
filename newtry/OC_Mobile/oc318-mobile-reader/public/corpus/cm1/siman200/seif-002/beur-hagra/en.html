If they were in the domain of, etc. This is the Rambam’s wording, following the Rif’s reading: if they are in the domain of the custodian, the buyer does not acquire until he lifts them or removes them from that domain.
Nevertheless, if, etc. The Mishnah there says, “If he was clever, etc.,” and the Gemara explains: if it is in the owner’s domain, etc. Likewise in Maaser Sheni chapter 5 and Kiddushin chapter 1. The baraisa there, according to our reading, states “in the domain of the person with whom they are deposited, etc.”
Or he gave him, etc., and by kinyan. Rental, however, is not acquired through a kinyan sudar and requires money, as Tosafos writes in Bava Metzia 11a, s.v. “and its place,” and Kiddushin 27a, s.v. “and its place.” See above siman 198 seif 9.
Or even, etc., and that, etc. There: “until he accepts upon himself, etc.” But in the seller’s own domain he cannot acquire for the buyer through himself, as appears in Eruvin, Pesachim, Gittin, Nedarim, and Bava Metzia. For this reason the law separates the seller’s domain from the custodian’s domain. See Tosafos there, s.v. “four.”
That he should rent, etc., and acquire, etc. Meaning that the rental itself is acquired by the act described above, as stated, “and these were received, etc.”
And if they were, etc. Rashbam, s.v. “until he accepts, etc.,” because this operates through the law of conferring a benefit, whereby the seller causes the buyer to acquire.
