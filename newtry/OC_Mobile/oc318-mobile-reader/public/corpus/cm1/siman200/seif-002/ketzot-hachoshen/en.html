Reuven executes a gift deed giving his house to Shimon, places the deed inside that very house, and says, "I give you the house together with the gift deed that lies inside it." Could we say that Shimon's courtyard and his gift deed arrive simultaneously, just as the Gemara says a woman's courtyard and her get can arrive simultaneously, and a slave's hand and his emancipation document can arrive simultaneously? After consideration, the answer is clearly no. So long as both house and deed remain entirely in Reuven's domain, there is no independent acquisition by which Shimon can acquire either one. Divorce and emancipation are different because they do not require acquisition in the ordinary sense. A woman can be divorced and a slave emancipated against their will. The Torah requires delivery of the get, not a voluntary property acquisition. This is why a get written on prohibited property can still be valid even though prohibited property is not subject to ordinary acquisition. A gift deed, however, requires the recipient actually to acquire the deed. Scripture speaks of acquiring the book of purchase. If the deed is written on property legally incapable of acquisition, the gift deed should not work. Kiddushin can nevertheless follow the divorce rules because kiddushin and divorce are textually linked. Thus in divorce, once the courtyard and get arrive together, the requirement of "he shall place it in her hand" is satisfied because the courtyard becomes her hand through the very delivery. In a gift, the recipient must first have a mode of acquisition for the deed. The house cannot acquire the deed until the house is his, and the deed cannot make the house his until he has acquired the deed. There is a true circularity. This also explains why an emancipation deed not written for the slave's sake is invalid. One might ask why the slave cannot at least acquire his body through the document as an ordinary gift deed, since a master can write, "My slave is acquired to you," and transfer a slave by document without the special requirements of emancipation. The answer is that to acquire himself through an ordinary gift document, the slave would first need the document to enter a hand legally independent of the master. His own hand is still the master's hand. The special emancipation law solves this through simultaneous arrival of hand and get, but once the document is not a valid emancipation document that special mechanism is unavailable. A delivery to a third person can perhaps acquire the slave's monetary freedom, though a proper emancipation document remains necessary for permission to marry a free Jewish woman. The matter still requires study.
