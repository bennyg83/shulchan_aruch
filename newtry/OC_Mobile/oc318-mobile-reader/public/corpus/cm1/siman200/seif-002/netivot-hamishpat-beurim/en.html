Or even by speech alone. See Shach, seif katan 6, who writes that one must specifically say, "Acquire for him." This is very difficult. If the buyer was present when the seller instructed the depositary, then saying "give" should certainly suffice, because the buyer acquires through ma'amad sheloshtan, and for ma'amad sheloshtan "give" is enough. If the buyer was not present, then even saying "acquire" does not necessarily prevent the buyer from later retracting, as explained in siman 235, seif 23, regarding acquisition for a buyer through another person, where the buyer can retain the advantageous position. The Gemara says that where the goods are in the depositary's domain, once the depositary accepts the instruction the acquisition is complete, implying that neither side can retract. It therefore appears that the case is one where the buyer had previously appointed the depositary as his agent to acquire. Once the depositary is the buyer's agent, the agent's courtyard is like the buyer's courtyard. It is enough that the seller consents and tells the depositary to make the acquisition for the buyer, and the depositary accepts and says that his courtyard shall acquire. Indeed, even the word "give" should suffice. The dispute whether "give" is equivalent to "acquire" applies principally to gifts. In a sale, once the seller has already received the consideration, he is obligated to deliver the object to the buyer, and the delivery resembles payment of a debt, where "give" is treated like "acquire." Ran in Gittin explains similarly concerning emancipation because a slave often pays for his own freedom; Beit Yosef in Even HaEzer analogizes this to debt. All the more so in an ordinary sale, where the seller received full value. Nevertheless, siman 125 and the authorities there indicate that even with debt, if nothing is presently delivered into the agent's hand, it is possible that "give" is not always treated as "acquire."
