[Shulchan Aruch] Since the seller accepted upon himself. Note: See Responsa Nachalat Shivah, siman 30, which writes in the name of Responsa Maharshak that a gentile has no acquisition through a courtyard, because courtyard acquisition is derived from agency and there is no agency for a gentile, unless he is standing beside his field, in which case it is effective by virtue of the mechanism of a hand. See Shulchan Aruch, Yoreh Deah, siman 320, seif 6, where "he should transfer the place to him" means by kinyan agav. See Responsa Avodat HaGershuni at the end of siman 25, where it appears obvious to him that a gentile does have courtyard acquisition, except that he finds the rationale difficult, since there is no agency for a gentile. According to Maharshak, it appears to me that even standing beside the courtyard should not help. For regarding a man, courtyard was not derived at all from the law of a hand; that derivation applies only to a woman, from the law of a get, as explained in the first chapter of Bava Metzia. See below, siman 243, Shach, seif katan 10. Even regarding a gentile woman there is room for discussion, because she is not subject to the laws of Jewish divorce and therefore perhaps the hand based derivation does not apply to her. It is true that Nimukei Yosef in the first chapter of Bava Metzia writes that a found object acquired through a guarded courtyard is acquired by virtue of the law of a hand, and the commentators expressed surprise because for a man this was not derived from the law of a hand. Moreover, the discussion in Avodah Zarah 71b proves that a gentile does have courtyard acquisition. Much later, after Sefer Mekor Chaim on the laws of Pesach was printed, I saw that in siman 448 he addressed this point. See Responsa VeShav HaKohen, siman 4.
