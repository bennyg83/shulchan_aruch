Some say that if the measuring vessel belongs to the seller. See Sema, seif katan 28, who says that the seller did not intend for the buyer to acquire until the measure was full. This is difficult. According to this opinion it is not enough that the measure becomes full; the seller must actually pour it out. Sema's entire discussion there is therefore difficult and appears garbled. It seems there is a textual error in Sema's cross reference. His words should be attached to Mechaber's phrase "the measuring vessel belonged to one of them." Mechaber there means whether the vessel belonged to buyer or seller. Sema asks why, if the buyer's vessel is located in the buyer's own domain, acquisition should not occur immediately. The answer is that the case concerns the seller measuring into a buyer-owned measure solely for measuring, and then pouring from that measure into the seller's separate vessel located in the buyer's domain. Because the seller puts the goods into the buyer's measure only temporarily for measurement, we presume he did not intend the buyer to acquire through the measure itself. By contrast, where the seller measures into the buyer's vessel and the goods will remain in that vessel for the buyer to take home, acquisition occurs. At the end, where Sema speaks of the seller's measure, it appears there is another textual error and it should read "the broker's measure," as Sema elsewhere indicates. If only two log were sold, filling that measure is equivalent to completing the specified quantity. All this follows Mechaber's view, like Rif and Rambam, that the seller's vessel in the buyer's domain can acquire for the buyer through measurement. Rama then cites an opinion requiring actual pouring out, corresponding to the dissenting view in seif 5 that even "go and acquire" may not suffice. This also answers Taz's question on Sema.
