Some say that four cubits, etc. Rosh, Bava Metzia chapter 1, siman 28, writes that according to all views a person’s four cubits acquire for him. See there and Sema. The Yerushalmi, Gittin chapter 8, halachah 3, states similarly regarding kiddushin. Rabbi Yasa in the name of Rabbi Yochanan says this enactment was said concerning gittin but not gifts. Rabbi Yochanan’s teachers and Reish Lakish’s teachers argue: if a lost object, which one does not acquire through the owner’s intent, is acquired within four cubits, then a gift, which one does acquire through the giver’s intent, certainly should be. The Bavli’s statement on 78b that this is “for gittin” refers, according to Rabbi Yochanan, to the broader rule that “near her” can mean even one hundred cubits, but Rav’s four-cubit enactment itself is not so limited. Close examination shows that the statement “four cubits acquire only for gittin” attributed to Rabbi Elazar in the name of Rabbi Yochanan in the Bavli is the same statement as Rabbi Yasa in the name of Rabbi Yochanan in the Yerushalmi, for Rabbi Yasa is Rav Asi. Since the Bavli cites only his statement, this indicates the halachah follows it. The Yerushalmi then records Rabbi Zeira’s challenges to Rabbi Yasa from kiddushin and debt, and Rabbi Yasa’s answers. These are the same questions and answers found in the Bavli there. See Tosafos there, s.v. “If so.” This explains siman 120 seif 1, where if he says “throw it to me,” etc., even from a distance, etc.
