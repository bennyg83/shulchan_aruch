The acquisition of movable property through one's courtyard, and how one's vessels acquire. It contains twelve seifim.
If movable property is situated in the purchaser's domain and that domain is guarded for him, then once the seller agrees to sell, the purchaser acquires even before measurement, provided the price was fixed. Rama: Some disagree and hold that in a sale or gift it is enough that the courtyard be guarded according to the seller's or donor's awareness. This appears to be the proper ruling. If, however, the seller rents his own courtyard to the purchaser while the items being sold remain mixed with the seller's other property, it is considered an unguarded courtyard and the purchaser does not acquire. The purchaser also acquires if he is standing beside the domain. Rama: Some say he must actually be inside it, and standing outside does not suffice. Some also say that a person's four cubits acquire for him as they do with a found object.
