Guarded with his knowledge. Meaning, this excludes a courtyard that is not guarded for the purchaser. Even if it is guarded for the seller, the purchaser does not acquire, because the purchaser's courtyard must resemble his hand or agent, which are guarded for the purchaser; an agent is like the principal. The Tur and Mechaber write this below in siman 268, seif 3. Those who disagree hold that only regarding a found object do we require the courtyard to be guarded for the person acquiring it, because there is no other person intentionally transferring ownership. In a sale or gift, however, another person is transferring ownership to the purchaser or recipient, and the transferor's guarding is considered equivalent to guarding by the acquirer, so the latter acquires through a courtyard guarded for the transferor.
They are still mixed together, etc. "Mixed" is not precise. The meaning is that they remain alongside the other produce and have not been completely separated from it. Hagahot Maimoniyot writes there that if there is no separation between those produce items and the householder's produce, he does not acquire, and the courtyard is considered unguarded. See there.
As with a found object. Ran in the first chapter of Bava Metzia writes this in the name of the Yerushalmi in Gittin. His reason is that because another person is intentionally transferring the property to him, the case is stronger than a found object, for which four cubits acquire. Ran distinguishes, however, and writes that this is only if the person was there first and the vessel was then placed within his four cubits. If the vessel was there before the person arrived, he does not acquire. Rabbeinu Yerucham, netiv 21, part 2, does not distinguish, and Tosafos there likewise do not. Since Rama states the law without qualification, it appears that he does not make this distinction.
