To rule. The Shach writes at length to prove from the Gemara and poskim that the Mechaber's view is primary. Nevertheless, he concludes: I am not worthy to decide the matter, and it remains a monetary doubt. But to rule initially as the Rama writes is certainly not correct, for I have already demonstrated that the majority of poskim agree with the Rif and Rambam and that this is the primary view.
However. The Shach and Taz disagree with the Rama here and write that his words are very astonishing. He derived them from the Hagahot Mordechai, but there it is clear that this was said only according to the first answer in the Gemara, which requires that even in a sale or gift the courtyard be guarded on behalf of the purchaser or recipient. The Rama, however, rules like Rav Pappa, according to whom acquisition occurs even when the goods are mixed. See there.
Mixed. This is not literal. It means that they are lying alongside the owner's produce and are not completely separated from it. The Hagahot Mordechai there states: If there is no separation between these produce and the owner's produce, he does not acquire, and it is considered an unguarded courtyard. Sema.
And some say. At the end of this siman, the Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe express surprise that the Tur did not record this mode of acquisition after the Rosh ruled that it acquires. They overlooked that the Tur writes it at the end of siman 243. It is also difficult that the Rama cites this law here as 'some say,' while in siman 243, seif 22, the Mechaber states it without qualification. Shach.
With a found object. The Ran writes that this applies specifically when the person arrived before the object and the object was then placed within his four amot. If the object was there first and the person later came, he does not acquire it. Rabbeinu Yerucham, Netiv 20, part 2, makes no distinction, and Tosafot in the first chapter of Bava Metzia likewise do not distinguish. Since the Rama states the law without qualification here, it appears he makes no distinction. Thus far the Sema. See what the Shach writes on this matter.
