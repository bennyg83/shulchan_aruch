Every place where he has permission to place it. Meaning, for example, a side alley or a courtyard belonging to both of them, excluding the public domain and certainly the seller's courtyard, as he concludes.
Unless he measured, etc. Meaning, the seller measured the goods and placed them into the purchaser's vessels standing in a side alley. If the purchaser himself measured them, he acquires through the measuring even without his vessels, because while measuring he performed meshichah, as the Tur writes in this siman, seif 13, and Rama in seif 7 in the gloss. See there.
Therefore, a person's vessels do not, etc., nor in the seller's domain, etc. The Mechaber follows Rif and Rambam, who hold that the Gemara's unresolved question was resolved: the purchaser's vessels in the seller's domain do not acquire. Their reason is that two elements are required, specifically the purchaser's vessels and the purchaser's domain. For the same reason he writes below in seif 5, "Just as the purchaser's vessels do not acquire for him in the seller's domain, so the seller's vessels do not acquire for him in the purchaser's domain." Both elements are required. This is unlike the view of the Rosh and Tur, who hold that both questions remain unresolved in the Gemara, whether the vessel is subordinated to the domain or not. There are several practical differences between the views, as the Tur writes in seif 8. See the Beit Yosef and Derishah for further discussion.
If it is in the seller's domain. Meaning, excluding the public domain, where even saying "go and acquire" is ineffective.
