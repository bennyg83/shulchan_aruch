Seif 7: The produce was in an alley. The Raavad objects that the Rambam did not specify into whose vessel the seller poured the produce. If it was the seller's vessel, even measuring the entire kor would not transfer ownership in an alley. It appears that the Rambam's opening case concerns the buyer's vessel. Since the seller measures into the buyer's vessel in an alley or jointly controlled area, the buyer acquires only after the full measure is completed when the seller insists on selling precisely a kor for thirty. The Rashba explains the Rif this way as well. If the produce were both in the buyer's premises and vessel, no measuring would be needed once the seller agreed. In the public domain, the buyer's vessel does not acquire without permission to place it there. In the seller's premises, even the buyer's vessel does not acquire until the seller says, “Go and acquire,” or until an effective act removes it from the seller's control. The Gemara therefore concerns the buyer's vessel in an alley or jointly owned premises, where measuring is required because the seller is particular about completion of the entire kor. This is the Rambam and Rif's approach. The Mechaber's emendation of the Rambam's phrase from “neither in the seller's nor in the buyer's domain” to “not in the public domain” is unnecessary. The original wording explains why measuring is both necessary and effective: the place is not fully the buyer's, where no measuring is needed, and not fully the seller's, where measuring alone would not suffice. The same logic applies where the goods are in the buyer's premises but in the seller's vessels. The seller's act of measuring is then treated as authorization to acquire. If both the premises and vessel belong to the buyer, measuring is unnecessary. Contrary to the Beit Yosef, the Rif and Rambam therefore require measuring in an alley even into the buyer's vessel. Rabbi Zeira's language also implies that at least measuring is required. The distinction is that in an alley the buyer's vessel has some but not complete acquisition power, so the seller's insistence on an exact measure must be satisfied. In the buyer's own premises and vessel, acquisition does not depend on measuring at all. This also explains Rabbeinu Yonah's view that if each seah is drawn separately, each is acquired even where the seller said “a kor for thirty,” because each independent act of drawing requires no further measuring.
