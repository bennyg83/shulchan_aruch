Even if he fixed the price, unless he unloaded them and brought them inside. Then his house acquires for him. Even if the price was fixed only after the earlier meshichah, he acquires because at the time the price was fixed the goods were still lying in the purchaser's domain. Sema:
He intended only to measure. Although the Rashba writes that where another person intentionally transfers property, the recipient can acquire without personal intent to acquire, that applies specifically where we know that at least the transferor intended the act itself to effect acquisition. Here, just as the purchaser intended only measurement, the seller likewise intended only measurement. Furthermore, perhaps in a sale, even if the seller intends acquisition, it does not occur without the purchaser's intent, because the purchaser may not yet wish to become obligated for the price. A gift is different, because acquisition is an unqualified benefit to the recipient and the giver's intent may suffice:
The purchaser measured, he did not acquire. See the Beurim. Regarding a non Jew, even according to this opinion he does not acquire without intent to acquire. Neither his courtyard nor his hand acquires without his knowledge, because such acquisition works only through the law of agency and there is no halachic agency for a non Jew. Here the case is a Jew. Specifically, if he physically lifts the item in his hand, a Jew acquires even without conscious acquisition, because that is no worse than his courtyard, which can acquire without his knowledge.
