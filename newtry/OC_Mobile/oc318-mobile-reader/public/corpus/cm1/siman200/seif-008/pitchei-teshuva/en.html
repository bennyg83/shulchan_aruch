Responsa Mabit I, siman 145, writes that even if the owners of a ship themselves pulled the ship and assisted in transferring it to the owners of the merchandise, the latter would acquire through that meshichah, because the shipowners thereby became their agents. Thus, if one purchased a house from an artisan seller and hired that very seller to repair a lock or perform another act of chazakah for him, the purchaser acquires through the seller's act, since the seller acted knowingly as his paid agent, and the same is true even without payment. Similarly, if the purchaser told the seller to level the boundary between the purchased field and his own field as an act of chazakah, or told him to pull the cow being sold from the public domain into the purchaser's domain or into a side alley so that the purchaser should acquire it, the purchaser acquires. The seller has become his agent to remove the object from the seller's domain and bring it into the purchaser's domain, and there is no difference between this seller-agent and any other agent. Thus, if the shipowners pulled the vessel at the direction of the cargo owners in order that they acquire it, the cargo owners acquire even if the shipowners themselves did not intend the legal acquisition. Shaar HaMishpat cites this ruling and questions why Mabit states it so simply, since it depends upon a dispute among the authorities. He nevertheless concludes that where one bought an object but did not perform meshichah and said to the seller, “Send it to me through your son, your servant, or your agent,” once the seller sends it and those persons perform meshichah or hagbahah, the purchaser acquires. They become the purchaser's agents even though he did not instruct them directly, but merely instructed the seller to tell them to send it. This follows from the sugya in HaShoel, and the same principle applies to acquisition. See above, siman 192, seif 1, seif katan 2.
