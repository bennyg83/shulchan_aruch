See there in the gloss: Until he pours it out. The wording is imprecise, for it implies that after he pours it out, even onto the ground, the buyer acquires it. This is not so, since we are dealing here with an alleyway, and Rabbeinu explicitly writes that in an alleyway the buyer’s vessel is required. The Sema writes here: “If the measuring vessel belongs to the seller, the merchandise remains in his domain at all times, even after the vessel has been filled, until he pours it out.” This means that for as long as the merchandise remains inside that vessel, no acquisition can take effect by virtue of the buyer’s vessel or the buyer’s domain. Once he pours it out, however, it leaves the seller’s domain, and the appropriate mode of acquisition can then operate for each case according to its law: in the buyer’s domain, he acquires it immediately; and in an alleyway, he acquires it when it is in the buyer’s vessel. The Rama’s wording, “he does not acquire it until he pours it out,” implies that the buyer acquires it immediately after the pouring. That is incorrect, as I have written.
