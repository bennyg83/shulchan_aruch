Provided that he lifts it in order to acquire it, etc. Meaning, he lifts it intending to acquire it at the stated price if it pleases him. Therefore the seller cannot retract, while the purchaser can retract and say, "I lifted it in order to acquire it only if it pleased me, and it does not please me." Nevertheless, he is liable even for unavoidable accidents so long as he did not reveal before the accident that he did not want it. This is Rambam's view. Tosafos, the Rosh, and the Tur hold that even if he did not lift the utensil specifically in order to acquire it, it nevertheless enters his responsibility for unavoidable accidents so long as he did not reveal that he does not want it, provided it is an item readily sold and the seller has not fixed a price for him. We presume that he intended to acquire it; otherwise he would not have taken it. It is surprising that Rama omitted their view and did not note that some disagree with Rambam.
Until he fixes the price and lifts it. "Lifts it" is not literal; the same applies to any other mode of acquisition. The Tur writes: "Until he fixes its price and the purchaser acquires it through one of the methods of acquisition."
Some say that he is a paid bailee. This view compares the case to one who takes utensils from an artisan to send them to his father-in-law's house, intending to pay the artisan if they are accepted, whose law is explained above in siman 186. Even when returning the utensils because they were not accepted, he becomes a paid bailee due to the benefit he had from displaying them proudly in his father-in-law's house. So too here, he had the benefit of choosing whether to keep the items or return them. Although there he enjoyed an additional benefit from showing them off at his father-in-law's house, he had agreed with the artisan to pay for that very benefit when returning the items, as explained there. Others say that nevertheless here he is only an unpaid bailee, because there the benefit was greater. See the Derishah. It requires study why the Mechaber omitted, and Rama did not add, the opinion cited by the Tur in the names of Rambam and the Rosh that he is liable even for unavoidable accidents.
