Yod) This is the language of the Rambam there, chapter 4, halachah 14, from a statement of Shmuel, Bava Batra 88a.
Kaf) There. The Maggid Mishneh explains that Rashi understands this to mean small utensils that are always sold for an issar, where their price is known. The Rashba writes that "their price is fixed" can mean that the seller himself fixed the price, or alternatively that he fixed one price for anyone who comes to purchase such an item from him. Otherwise, the buyer does not acquire merely because comparable items are generally sold in the city at a known fixed price, because his mind is not yet settled; perhaps this seller will not agree to that price. The Rambam's wording inclines toward Rashi's view.
Lamed) This is explained in Nedarim 31b. The Maggid Mishneh writes that the Sages treated the item as being in his possession only regarding liability for accidents, but he may certainly retract because the price has not yet been fixed. Rav Yosef ibn Migash writes likewise.
Mem) The Tur, seif 16, cites this in the name of some authorities, based on the baraita in Nedarim that on returning the item he is exempt because he has the status of a paid custodian.
Nun) There in the name of the Ramah, following Rashi's explanation in HaOmanin, Bava Metzia 81a, that he becomes a paid custodian specifically where he took the items to send them to his father-in-law's house, since he benefits by displaying them proudly, as explained above in siman 186.
