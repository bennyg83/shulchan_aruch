Until the measure is filled, it belongs to the seller. Presumably the broker or other person who lends the measuring vessel intends it to be lent to the seller until it is filled, so the produce is considered to be in the seller's utensil. Once the measure is full, the lender's intention is that it become the buyer's utensil until the buyer empties it into his own containers. Therefore the seller may retract until the measure is full. The Tur adds that this is so even if the measure has markings showing one log, two logs, and so forth, and the seller fixed a price for each mark.
