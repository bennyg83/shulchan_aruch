The seller, etc. As above, because in the buyer’s domain, if the goods are in vessels that do not belong to the buyer, he does not acquire them.
Likewise, etc. The Gemara was forced there to explain the first clause as the seller’s vessel and the latter clause differently. In the subsequent baraisa concerning one who pulled, etc., it establishes the case as bundles of garlic. Accordingly the earlier baraisa need not be forced either. This is why the ruling says that the goods were piled there. Therefore, in the buyer’s domain, once he accepted them, etc. This is why the first clause also speaks of the buyer’s domain, since the goods are in another person’s vessel.
