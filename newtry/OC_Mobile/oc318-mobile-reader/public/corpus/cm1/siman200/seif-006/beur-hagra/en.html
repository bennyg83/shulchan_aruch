Pulling, etc. Bava Metzia 9b states, 'Pull this animal and acquire,' etc. See Tosafot there. Only when the seller is absent must he say 'pull'; when present, express instruction is unnecessary, as stated in Bava Batra and Bava Kamma. This is the meaning of 'and he pulled it, etc., even, etc.' See Baer HaGolah. Shabbos discusses removing a purse through its opening even though the purse remains the owner's vessel, and Avodah Zarah shows that placing measured wine into a Jew's vessel prevents the prohibition from taking effect. The Yerushalmi states: one who says, 'Pull the animal to acquire it,' acquires it; 'to acquire its offspring,' does not; 'to acquire it and its offspring,' acquires both. It asks why one who says, 'Pull the animal so that its load will be acquired to you,' would not acquire the load, and answers that the distinction applies when the animal was not pregnant. If it was pregnant, its fetus is treated like its load.
