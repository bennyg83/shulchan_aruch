If they were, etc. This follows the Shulchan Aruch’s wording here. This seif, seif 9, and seif 10 all follow the Rambam and the Rif’s approach. They hold that whenever the seller himself measures the goods, even from one of his vessels into another of his vessels, the buyer acquires, because the seller is effectively transferring the use of his vessel to the buyer. They therefore explain the Gemara’s phrase “measures into his basket” as referring to the seller’s basket. The one measuring must be the seller, because if the buyer measures, he would acquire through meshichah regardless, as Tosafos writes there, s.v. “measured,” and as stated above in the gloss. The Mishnah’s phrase “measured but did not pull” refers to measuring and placing the goods on the ground, as the Gemara itself indicates when it asks from that case. See Rashbam there. The Maggid Mishneh did not examine that passage closely. This also resolves Tosafos’ question there. The Gemara’s distinction concerning a measuring vessel is thus understood literally even when the vessel belongs to the seller. The rule that a seller’s vessels do not acquire for the buyer even in the buyer’s domain applies where the seller himself is not measuring. The Gemara could not establish the case as one where the seller measures, for then the earlier difficulty would return: even before the measure is filled, the buyer should acquire, just as with the buyer’s measuring vessel. The Rambam accordingly explains “a kor for thirty” as involving the buyer’s domain and the seller’s vessel. Were it the buyer’s vessel, he would acquire upon accepting the goods even without measurement. Rather, the seller’s own measuring effects acquisition as explained. This is also why the Gemara challenges from the case of the measuring vessel, even before it is filled. The case similarly concerns a side alley or jointly owned courtyard, where mere placement does not acquire without the seller’s measurement. According to the Rif, the case cannot be in the public domain, because measurement cannot acquire there even with the buyer’s vessel. Where the seller says “a kor for thirty,” the entire quantity is treated as one measure, so acquisition does not occur until the measure is complete. The Rambam does not read the alternative text requiring marked divisions in the vessel as dependent on an express hin by hin stipulation. Even ordinarily the buyer does not acquire until the relevant marked measure is reached. In a neutral measuring vessel, markings do not help. The Rambam holds that even the buyer’s own measuring vessel requires such markings in this context. This forced him to explain the earlier case as one involving the seller’s vessel. The same reasoning also resolves the Rashbam and Tosafos’ difficulty concerning the first portion. He likewise holds that a broker’s vessel does not acquire even in the buyer’s domain until the measure is filled, as the Rif writes regarding the Mishnah on 87a. This also explains why the Gemara did not answer by distinguishing a third party’s vessel between the seller’s and buyer’s domains: a third party’s vessel has the same law as the seller’s vessel.
