The produce was in a side alley. See the Beurim. The general framework according to the Mechaber, as the Sema explains him, is as follows. If the seller says, "a kor for thirty," and the produce lies on the ground of a side alley, and he measures it into the purchaser's utensil standing in that side alley, then until measurement of the entire kor is complete the acquisition of the entire kor is incomplete and the purchaser acquires nothing. But if the entire kor is already in the purchaser's utensil in the side alley, the purchaser acquires even without measurement according to the Mechaber in seif 3, who does not require measurement in that situation, because the acquisition of the entire kor is complete. According to the Rama there, who requires measurement, he does not acquire. If the purchaser's utensil is in the purchaser's own domain, two favorable factors are present and it is equivalent to meshichah before him, so each se'ah that is measured into the purchaser's utensil in his domain is acquired immediately. If the seller measures into the seller's utensil while it stands in the purchaser's domain, the purchaser does not acquire until the whole kor is measured according to the Mechaber. According to the Rama's gloss above in seif 5, even after the whole kor is measured into the seller's utensil in the purchaser's domain, and even if the seller says, "Go acquire it," he does not acquire. According to the Rama's gloss here, even if the purchaser's utensil is in the purchaser's domain, and even if the purchaser performed meshichah or hagbahah, he does not acquire until the whole kor is measured. This is true even if the entire kor lies in one utensil but has not been measured. The reason is that by saying "a kor for thirty," rather than "a se'ah for a sela," the seller reveals that he needs the proceeds of the whole kor and does not intend to transfer anything until the entire kor is measured. Accordingly, if the purchaser performed meshichah or hagbahah on the entire kor, or if the whole kor is in the purchaser's utensil in his domain before measurement is complete, there is a doubt of law because of the dissenting view cited by the Rama here. If the seller's utensil is in the purchaser's domain, even after the whole kor is measured there remains a doubt of law because of the Rama's view in seif 5 that even saying "Go acquire it" is ineffective. If the entire kor is in the purchaser's utensil in a side alley but has not been measured, there is likewise a doubt of law because of the Rama's view in seif 3 that measurement is specifically required. In all these doubtful cases, the money remains wherever it presently is:
