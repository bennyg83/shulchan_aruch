The produce was in a side alley or courtyard belonging to both. Even if it was already in the buyer's utensils, because the seller specifically said, "I sell you a kor for thirty," rather than, "a seah for one sela," he revealed that he intends a single transaction and that no part should be acquired without the whole. Therefore either party may retract until all thirty seah have been measured. But if the seller measures directly into the buyer's utensils while they stand in the buyer's own domain, there are two factors in the buyer's favor, both the domain and the utensils. The Rif, Rambam, and Ramah hold that neither party may retract from what has already been measured even before the full kor is completed. In that situation they are presumed to have intended each measured portion to be acquired successively. For this reason the Mechaber also writes that even in the buyer's domain, if the produce remains in the seller's utensils and he said "a kor for thirty," there is no acquisition until the whole kor is measured. The limitation to the seller's utensils is deliberate. If he measures into the buyer's utensils in the buyer's own domain, acquisition occurs successively. See the Perishah.
