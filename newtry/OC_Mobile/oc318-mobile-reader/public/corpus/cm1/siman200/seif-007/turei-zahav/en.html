Seif 10. The phrase “the measure belonged to one of them” cannot mean either the seller or buyer indiscriminately. Otherwise the seller's vessel would be superior to a third party's borrowed vessel, even though a borrowed vessel acquires only when filled, while Rabbeinu states that markings can suffice in the seller's own vessel. The Rambam retained the Gemara's wording, which all commentators explain as referring to the buyer's measure. The Raavad's gloss, “meaning, the buyer's,” is therefore explanatory, not an objection. The Rambam's phrase that “he relies on the markings” also points to the buyer. If the measure belonged to the seller, why would the buyer's reliance on markings create acquisition? Accordingly, the Rama's citation of a dissenting view that identifies the measure as the buyer's does not reflect a true dispute. The Rambam agrees. Many additional proofs support this, but there is no need to elaborate.
