Neither a domain, a vessel, meshichah, nor lifting effects acquisition unless the measure and price were first fixed. Until then neither party relies on the transaction, because they may fail to agree on the total. If the item has a fixed market price, however, it is acquired even without an express price. Likewise, if the seller says, 'I sell it to you at the value fixed by three appraisers,' the purchaser acquires even though the price is not fixed in advance. If produce is in an alley or joint courtyard, or even in the purchaser's domain, but remains in the seller's vessels, and the seller begins measuring into his own vessels, then if he said, 'I sell you a kor for thirty sela,' either party may withdraw even during the final seah, because the produce remains in the seller's vessels and the entire measure is not complete. The seller's vessels do not acquire for the purchaser even in the purchaser's domain. Rama: Some say that whenever the seller made clear that he wished to sell only the entire kor as one unit, the purchaser does not acquire until the whole quantity is measured, even if it is in the purchaser's domain and vessels, or even if the purchaser performed meshichah or lifting. If the seller said, 'A kor of thirty seah, at one sela per seah,' each seah is acquired as it is lifted and poured out, because a separate price was fixed for every seah and the produce is neither in the seller's domain nor in the public domain. If it had not been in the seller's vessels, then once it was in the purchaser's domain it would be acquired upon agreement to the price even without measurement, as explained in seif 3. Rama: Some say that each unit is acquired only because the purchaser is presently in possession, and therefore if he has not paid he may still withdraw. If the seller opened a barrel of wine and, after half was measured, the purchaser wishes to stop, while the seller says the remainder will sour, the purchaser must take the entire barrel or accept liability for any spoilage of the remainder and pay according to the barrel's value at the time of purchase. Some say this applies when the seller measured, but if the purchaser measured, he acquires in every case because his measurement is equivalent to meshichah. Others disagree. Once the transaction has been acquired, neither party may withdraw merely because a dispute later arose and the seller refused delivery; the dispute is adjudicated separately.
