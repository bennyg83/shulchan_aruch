Nun) There, seif 10, from a baraita at the beginning of Bava Batra 86a.
Samekh) As Ravina said to Huna son of Mar Zutra there.
Ayin) So writes the Rosh there in his rulings. Rambam likewise rules this in chapter 4 of Hilkhot Mekhirah, halachah 12. The Maggid Mishneh writes that it is straightforward and derives from the incident involving gourds whose price had been fixed, Bava Batra 88a.
Peh) The Tur there derives this from the passage at the end of Avodah Zarah 77b, which the Rif cites at the end of the chapter Beit Kor.
Tzadi) This is the language of the Rambam there, chapter 4, halachah 7.
Kuf) From the statement of Rav and Shmuel, Bava Batra 86b.
Meaning, he measured the entire kor.
Reish) So writes the Maggid Mishneh there in the name of the Rashba and Rabbenu Yonah, and he states that this is also the view of the Raavad.
Shin) There, in the statement of Rav and Shmuel.
Tav) The Kesef Mishneh writes that it appears this ruling refers only to the buyer's domain. The Maggid Mishneh writes that the Rif appears to support the Rambam, for on this statement he writes that the case can be found in a side alley, or alternatively in the buyer's domain when the produce is in the seller's utensils. His words incline toward the Rambam's view, although it is possible that he refers to the period before measurement, whereas at the time of measurement the seller measures into the buyer's utensils. The other approach is primary. The Kesef Mishneh writes that although we rule that the seller's utensils do not acquire for the buyer in the buyer's domain, one must say that because the seller himself is measuring, it is as though he told the buyer, "Go and acquire." Rambam holds that such an instruction is effective even where the produce remains in the seller's utensils in the buyer's domain.
The reason is that we are uncertain whether to give effect to the first expression or the final expression.
