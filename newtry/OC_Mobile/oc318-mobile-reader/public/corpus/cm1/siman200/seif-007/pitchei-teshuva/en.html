And there are those who disagree. This is the Tur's opinion. The Taz asks from siman 275, concerning one who tills the property of a convert, where an act of chazakah is ineffective when he did not intend to acquire, and likewise from Tur, siman 194, seif 5, concerning one who possessed property for years without intent to acquire. The Taz answers that here, when he measured the object, he had already intended to acquire it. Rabbi Akiva Eiger, Yoreh Deah, siman 37, explains that unlike one who tills a convert's property believing it already his, here he does desire to acquire, but mistakenly thinks the acquisition must be by meshichah. Therefore the lifting itself can acquire the utensil. Rabbi Akiva Eiger suggests another distinction: here there is another person's conscious intent to transfer ownership, and that may allow acquisition even without the recipient's specific intent. Yet the Taz's distinction seems necessary from Tur, siman 268, who rules that one who falls upon a found object nonetheless acquires it by his four cubits and we do not say that by revealing his preference for falling upon it he rejected acquisition through four cubits. Since there is no transferor there, why should he acquire through four cubits without specific intent? Evidently, once he is actively seeking to acquire the object, a mistaken choice of mechanism does not prevent acquisition. Still, there is also support for distinguishing cases where another person intends to transfer. For the Rama, siman 275, seif 21, rules concerning one who builds on a convert's property that he does not acquire even the stones when he intended to acquire only through the building. See Rabbi Akiva Eiger for the continuation, and see below, siman 268, seif 1, seif katan 1.
