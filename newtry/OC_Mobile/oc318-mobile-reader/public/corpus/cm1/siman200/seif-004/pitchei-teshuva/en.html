He acquired. See Ketonet Passim by the Gaon Rabbi Yaakov Meir of Brisk, end of chapter 5 of Bava Batra. He records a case in which one person sold twenty oxen, each at a fixed price, and the purchaser performed meshichah on only some of them. The seller then wished to retract. He ruled that the seller could retract from the remainder. Although a price was fixed for each ox, seemingly resembling the rule that where one says, “a kor at thirty, each seah for a sela,” each unit is acquired successively, oxen are not identical. One ox is superior and another inferior, and the uniform per-ox price was evidently derived from an assessment of the aggregate value of all the oxen together. The entire group is therefore one sale. Even though a separate sum was stated for each ox, the price was calculated as part of the total package, so the seller may retract from those not yet acquired. He concludes that this appears correct but still requires study.
