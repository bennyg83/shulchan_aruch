Just as the purchaser's vessel does not acquire. Even HaEzer discusses two baskets, the woman's inner basket placed within the husband's outer basket, with the get thrown into the inner one. The law distinguishes whether the inner basket has a bottom, because the woman's vessel in the husband's domain may fail to acquire unless he does not object to its location. Beit Shmuel inferred that the case must be on the woman's own ground, and from there concluded that a purchaser's vessel inside the seller's vessel does not acquire even when both are standing on the purchaser's ground. I do not accept the first premise. A purchaser's vessel certainly acquires in an alley where he has permission to place it. The Gemara in Perek HaSefinah says so, and the Shulchan Aruch rules it. Rosh's qualification that measurement or an express instruction may be needed concerns the seller's lack of settled intent before measurement, not an inherent inability of the vessel to acquire in an alley. A get has no such incomplete measurement issue. Likewise, when Ran calls the ground "hers," he can mean a side street or alley where she has a right to place vessels. Ramban's own language explicitly uses an alley. The larger question, whether a purchaser's vessel inside the seller's vessel acquires when both stand on the purchaser's ground, depends on the dispute about whether the surrounding domain defeats the vessel. According to Rambam, the rules of purchaser's vessel in seller's domain and seller's vessel in purchaser's domain are fixed, not merely doubtful. One may therefore require both the purchaser's vessel and purchaser's domain. According to Rosh, Tur, Rabbi Meir HaLevi Abulafia, and Rabbi Yosef ibn Migash, however, the matter is doubtful: does the domain nullify the vessel or not? On that approach, where the purchaser's vessel is inside the seller's vessel but both are in the purchaser's domain, the buyer acquires whichever way the doubt is resolved. If the domain overrides the vessels, the purchaser's domain acquires. If the vessels are controlling, the purchaser's own inner vessel acquires. This is stronger than the Tur's law where two separate transactions produce a double doubt. Thus under our accepted approach following Rosh and Tur, the buyer should acquire in such a case. Beit Shmuel's inference does not stand.
