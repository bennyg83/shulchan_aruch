Just as, etc. This is Rambam's view, following Rif, who holds that the question was resolved that the buyer does not acquire. The proof is that the Gemara says the seller told him, 'Go acquire,' and does not answer that perhaps there, etc. Rif has another reading as well. The same applies to the seller's vessels in the buyer's domain, as shown by the explanations involving emptied produce and garlic bundles. Ri Migash and Rosh hold that both questions remained unresolved, and therefore the possessor retains the property.
Some say, etc. See Sema and Rashbam. A person's vessels acquire wherever he has permission to place them, and he need not acquire the place itself. See Tosafot, who distinguish a vessel from land, which cannot be acquired by mere speech. This opinion is cited to exclude Rif and Rambam, as explained below in seif 7 and seif 10.
