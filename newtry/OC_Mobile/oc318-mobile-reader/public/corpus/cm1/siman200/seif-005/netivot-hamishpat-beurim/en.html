Just as the buyer's utensil does not acquire. Nevertheless there is a practical distinction between a buyer's utensil in the seller's domain and a seller's utensil in the buyer's domain. In the latter case, according to Mechaber, measuring can suffice, as appears later in seif 7: when the seller finishes measuring the entire kor into his own vessel located in the buyer's domain, the buyer acquires. Beit Yosef explains that measurement is like saying "go and acquire." By contrast, a buyer's utensil in the seller's domain does not acquire merely through the seller's measurement; an express "go and acquire" is needed according to Rosh and Tur. Lechem Mishneh explains the distinction. When measurement occurs within the seller's vessel, the act of measuring naturally shows that the seller is lending or relinquishing his vessel for the relevant transaction. But the buyer still needs rights in the seller's underlying land, and measurement does not naturally signify a grant of the surrounding courtyard. Therefore an express instruction is required for the buyer's vessel in the seller's domain. According to what I wrote above, this can be stated more sharply. Where the seller's vessel is in the buyer's domain, as soon as the goods reach the buyer's courtyard air they would ordinarily be acquired. The seller's vessel is the only possible interruption. By measuring the full kor, the seller shows that he is no longer relying on the vessel as his independent possessory barrier, effectively allowing the buyer's courtyard air to operate. But where the buyer's vessel sits in the seller's domain, the buyer needs the seller positively to lend the relevant place and air for acquisition. Mere measurement does not imply that broader grant.
