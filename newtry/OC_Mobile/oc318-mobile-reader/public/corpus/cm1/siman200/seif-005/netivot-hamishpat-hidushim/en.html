Just as the Rambam and the author of the island are not in the hands of the Rambam, but in the hands of a seller, it is not a measurement that, if he said to him, Zil and Kendek, and as a buffer in the column, and in the hands of a seller, he takes the balance of measurement and the taste:
Even if the seller said, "Go and acquire." This helps only with the buyer's utensil in the seller's domain, because the utensil itself already belongs to the buyer and the seller's statement merely shows that he does not object to use of his property. Where the seller's utensil stands in the buyer's domain, the utensil itself does not belong to the buyer and cannot be transferred to him through speech alone. Sema. See Beurim: according to this dissenting view, measuring likewise cannot help with the seller's utensil in the buyer's domain. This directly disputes the Mechaber in seif 7, who rules that where one sells "a kor for thirty," once the entire kor is measured, the buyer acquires even though the grain remains in the seller's utensil in the buyer's domain.
