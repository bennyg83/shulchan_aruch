Piled upon that land. Since the movable property lies upon the land, it is acquired together with the land even if he did not expressly tell the purchaser to acquire it agav the land. We regard the movable property upon the land as though it were part of the land itself and attached to it.
And some say that even if it is piled there, etc. This applies specifically where the land is not guarded for the purchaser. Otherwise he would acquire through the law of courtyard. See the Derishah.
Specifically the language "agav," but "with" is ineffective. Meaning, if he says, "Acquire the land and acquire the movable property with it," this language is ineffective. The reason appears to be that "with it" implies that the same acquisition applies equally to both, which does not work, whereas the language "agav" indicates that the movable property is acquired incidentally through the land.
And some disagree. The Levush rules in favor of those who disagree, based on the verse cited above, which says "with fortified cities." This proves, he says, that the word "with" works just like "agav"; one is Scriptural language and the other rabbinic language. This is not precise. According to his reasoning, the Gemara's question whether one must say "agav" would be difficult from the outset, because Scripture already says "with," which he equates with "agav." Rather, one must say that the Gemara's question assumes that the verse does not mean that Yehoshafat used the word "with" as the formula of acquisition. The verse merely reports that he gave his sons the movable property together with the land through some acquisition. It does not reveal whether at the time of acquisition one must say "agav," "with," or nothing at all. Therefore, once the Gemara resolves that one must say, "Acquire it agav this land," it is possible that the specific word "agav" is required and "with" is insufficient. The verse is not discussing the actual words spoken at the acquisition. This is clear.
