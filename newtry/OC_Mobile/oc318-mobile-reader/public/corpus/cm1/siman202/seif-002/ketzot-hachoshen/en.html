Sema explains Rambam that when the movables physically lie on the land, they are acquired together with the land even without the transferor expressly saying, "Acquire them incidentally with the land," because the movables are treated as attached to and part of the land for this purpose. Ran, Rosh, and other authorities questioned Rambam's ruling. In siman 275 I brought a clear proof supporting Rambam that where the movables are actually piled on the land, no additional formula of incidental acquisition is needed.
Some say even when they are piled there. Sema says this refers to a courtyard not guarded for the recipient, because otherwise ordinary courtyard acquisition would already suffice. According to what I wrote in siman 200, however, where another person is intentionally transferring the property, no physical guarding is needed at all. The transferor's control makes the gift effectively guarded. Shach gives another explanation: courtyard acquisition does not help because the courtyard and gift arrive simultaneously. I already discussed this in siman 198. Shitah Mekubetzet indeed says that a courtyard must belong to the recipient before the movable enters. Yet this appears contrary to several Talmudic passages. The strongest difficulty is Bava Metzia concerning one who says, "Pull this cow in order to acquire the utensils upon it." The Gemara asks that even if he said, "Acquire the animal and acquire the utensils," the utensils should not be acquired because the animal is a moving courtyard. It concludes that the animal is tied. If simultaneous courtyard and gift acquisition were impossible, tying the animal would not solve anything, since the animal and utensils are still being acquired at the same time. Thus the Gemara appears to recognize simultaneous courtyard and gift acquisition. This remains a substantial challenge to the Shach's distinction.
