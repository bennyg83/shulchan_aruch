It is necessary. The Sema writes that this applies only when the land is not guarded with the recipient's awareness, for otherwise he would acquire through the law of courtyard. This is incorrect. The Ran and Maggid Mishneh imply that even in a guarded courtyard the requirement that the movables be piled upon the land does not help in this manner, and this is explicit in Raavad's gloss. The Tur and Rama likewise state the rule without distinction. The reason appears to be that a person's courtyard acquires for him only when it is already his courtyard. Here, however, the courtyard and the gift come to him simultaneously. This appears to me the principal ruling. Shach. See also Responsa Shav Yaakov, Choshen Mishpat siman 15.
They disagree. The Levush rules like the dissenting authorities because Scripture says "with fortified cities," proving that the word "with" is effective like "by virtue of" for acquisition of movables together with land. The Sema writes that this is imprecise. If so, why did the Gemara ask whether one must say "by virtue of"? It is an explicit verse. Rather, the verse does not mean that those words were necessarily spoken at the time of acquisition. It merely describes that the father gave his sons movables together with the land in one transaction. Once the Gemara's question was resolved that the formula "acquire by virtue of it" is required, specifically that formulation is necessary and "with" is insufficient. The Taz writes that according to the Levush one can say that this itself was the Gemara's question: whether the verse implies that this precise wording had to be used at the time of acquisition. If so, the conclusion would be that "with" is effective like "by virtue of." Nevertheless, since Rashi holds that "by virtue of" is specifically required and the Raah disputes him, we certainly do not follow the Raah against Rashi, especially to extract money.
