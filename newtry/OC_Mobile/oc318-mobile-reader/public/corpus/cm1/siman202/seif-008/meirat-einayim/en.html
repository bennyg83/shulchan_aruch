If he took possession of the slaves, he did not acquire, etc. In the Perishah I explained that we rule like Rav Nachman, who says that a slave is treated as movable property except in Torah-law matters, in which Rav Nachman concedes that a slave is treated like land. Chazakah as a mode of acquisition is itself Torah law. Therefore, if he takes possession of the land, he also acquires the slaves through the law of chazakah, just as one who takes possession of one field in order to acquire additional fields and has paid for all of them acquires those other fields, as explained in siman 192. Specifically with acquisition agav, land cannot be acquired agav other land, but through chazakah together with payment it can. There, one acquires even fields in ten different countries because all land is regarded as one underlying unit. Slaves, however, move themselves, and therefore he does not acquire them unless they are standing upon the land. When they stand there, he acquires them even if he did not say, "Acquire the slaves agav the land," because slaves are considered land for this purpose. If they were treated merely as movable property, according to the opinion cited above he would have to say, "Acquire agav the land." We also require that he already paid for the slaves, analogous to acquisition of land in ten different countries. But if he explicitly says, "Acquire the slaves agav the land," he acquires them under the law of movable property and need not have paid their price, because a slave is considered movable property for this purpose. Nevertheless, since slaves are moving movable property, they must be standing within the land. See the Perishah.
Even if he says to him, "Acquire them agav the land." Meaning, unlike where they are standing within the land, in which case according to Rambam he acquires them even without saying "Acquire agav the land." Even those who disagree with Rambam concede that if he took possession of the land and also paid the price of the slave, he acquires by the law of land acquisition, as explained above. See the Perishah.
