The land by rental and the movables by gift. Rabbeinu Yerucham records a dispute whether movables can be acquired incidentally with land that is merely borrowed rather than rented. Some say no; Tosafot appears to say yes. Shach asks why a loaned land right should differ from rental and cites the episode of Rabban Gamliel and the elders as proof that it works. This is not decisive. There are recognized differences between loan and rental. For example, Rosh says a loan right may not be acquired through barter even where rental is stronger. Rental for a fixed period can have the law of a sale in some contexts. The Rabban Gamliel episode itself says that the places were rented, not borrowed. Rosh's question why they did not merely lend the place by sudar appears in Bava Metzia, where the sugya is explaining the mechanism through courtyard acquisition. A borrowed courtyard can certainly function as a courtyard. That does not prove that borrowed land can serve as the land component of incidental acquisition. Tosafot, however, ask the same question in Kiddushin, where the sugya explains the transfer through incidental acquisition with land. Their question therefore does indicate that borrowed land can support incidental acquisition. Thus the dispute remains, and the proof comes specifically from Tosafot's formulation, not from Rosh.
