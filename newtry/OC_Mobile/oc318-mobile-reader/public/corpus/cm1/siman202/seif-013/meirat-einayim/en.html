Even though he pulls the animal, etc. The Gemara leaves unresolved whether pulling the animal also pulls and acquires the utensils upon it, because the modes of movement are not identical. The animal is acquired through walking, while utensils are acquired through dragging. The buyer also cannot acquire the utensils through the animal as his courtyard once he pulls the animal, because an animal is a moving courtyard, as the Mechaber concludes.

Regarding a found object, if he said, etc. Since this is an unresolved question in the Gemara, the authorities hold that in a sale we leave the property, because of doubt, with its prior owner. Even if the buyer seized it through meshichah, it is removed from him. With a found object there is no prior owner. Therefore, out of doubt, the first person who pulled is treated as having acquired it, and if another later seizes it from him, it is removed from the latter and returned to the first, who is now treated as the prior possessor.
