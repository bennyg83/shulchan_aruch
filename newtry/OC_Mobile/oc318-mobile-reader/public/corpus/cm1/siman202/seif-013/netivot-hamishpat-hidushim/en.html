Even though he performed meshichah of the animal. This is an unresolved question in the Gemara whether meshichah of an animal, whose normal movement is walking, also effects meshichah of utensils carried by it, whose normal movement is dragging. Sema:
He did not acquire the utensils. Seizure does not resolve the doubt in his favor. The Ran writes that seizure does help here because he performed the meshichah with the owner's permission, making it authorized possession, unlike the case above in seif 3. Shach:
The animal is tied. It need not be sleeping. Since an animal lacks legal intent, tying is sufficient, unlike with a slave. Sema:
Regarding a found object. The reason is that the issue is an unresolved question. Since a found object has no prior owner, the one who pulled it acquires at least by virtue of the doubt and becomes the presumptive owner. Therefore, if another later seizes it, we remove it from him. In a purchase, by contrast, we leave the property with the seller, who is the original presumptive owner. Sema:
