But when it is known that he has no land. The Rama is abbreviated here. In the responsum of the Rashba cited by Beit Yosef it is explained that this applies specifically where the document merely states in general terms that the movable property is acquired agav land. In such a case, if the parties are frequently in one another's company, it is as though they know that he owns no land, for if he did we would have become aware of it. Even so, if the document states that it is not a mere standard form, so that he has personally acknowledged that he owns land, a person is believed concerning himself. All the more so if he expressly acknowledges that he owns land, or if he writes that he owns four cubits of land in his courtyard. This is treated as an acknowledgment that he owns land, and his acknowledgment concerning himself is effective.
