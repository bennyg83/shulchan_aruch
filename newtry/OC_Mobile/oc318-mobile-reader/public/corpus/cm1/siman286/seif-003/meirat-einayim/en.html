He takes the dowry only from his own share, because he never acquired it. Mahariv, siman 104, rules this and explains that even if the father entered a kinyan regarding the promised dowry, that kinyan was not made in favor of the son. It was made in favor of the other parent who had agreed to marry his daughter to the son, obligating the father to pay a penalty if he failed to give the promised amount. Mahariv concludes in siman 105, citing Responsa Maharam, that if the father dies before the wedding date and the other family and the brothers do not wish to perform what the father accepted, they are exempt from the penalty. See Even HaEzer 50:6 in Rama's gloss.
Because he did not acquire it during his father's lifetime. Mahariv writes in siman 104 that the son does not acquire the amount his father fixed for him. Rather, the heirs divide equally. On what basis should the son acquire? This is neither a shechiv mera gift nor an instruction because of death. The doctrine that it is a mitzvah to fulfill the words of the deceased applies, according to Tosafos, only where the property was first deposited with a third party for that purpose. Nor does an assessment of the father's intent prove otherwise. Even if his father vowed that amount and we infer that he wanted to fulfill the promise, who says his intention was that this son take the whole amount before division? On the contrary, perhaps the amount was promised only while the father lived. After his death, when the property falls to all his sons and this son already receives an inheritance share, perhaps the father would not want to divert inheritance from the others, especially where this son's inheritance share already equals the promised dowry. See there. Mahariv similarly writes in siman 142 concerning Reuven who arranged his daughter's marriage and fixed a dowry, then died before the wedding. He rules that the amount is not taken from the common estate; the engaged daughter must supply it from her own inheritance share. At most, the common estate bears only the amount expressly stated in the penalty document. This is not comparable to Maharam's ruling exempting from a mere penalty upon unavoidable death, because there the obligation was only a penalty. Where there is an actual debt document, it is like a loan and a claim of unavoidable circumstances does not cancel it.
