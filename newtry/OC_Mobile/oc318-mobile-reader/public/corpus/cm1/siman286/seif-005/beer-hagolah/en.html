Kaf) There, halachah 14, from the Mishnah and baraita concerning a father who sent shushvinut on behalf of his son. The Maggid Mishneh explains that the father sent it in the son's name, while Rashbam explains that he sent it through the son.
Lamed) The Maggid Mishneh writes that his teacher likewise ruled this way and discusses the reason at length.
Mem) The Maggid Mishneh writes in chapter 9 of Hilkhot Nachalot, halachah 14, that his teacher did not rule like Rambam, and the Raavad also objected. The Maggid Mishneh defends Rambam: perhaps the other brothers will never marry, or if they marry they may not marry in circumstances comparable to the brother for whom the shushvinut was sent. The recipient can say, "I will deal with you only as you dealt with me," as explained in chapter 6 of Hilkhot Zekhiyah. Thus it is possible that the shushvinut will never be returned to the brothers except for this particular share, causing them to lose their portions. We do not tell them to abandon a certainty in reliance on a doubt. Moreover, the plain wording of the baraita supports Rambam, and therefore the returned shushvinut goes back into the common estate.
