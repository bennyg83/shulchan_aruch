(Seif 1: and some disagree.) This is Rabbeinu Yeshayah's view. The Derishah explains that he holds that even without an express stipulation we do not assume the siblings waived claims against one another. Each must therefore bear the amount spent specifically on himself. One cannot ask why we do not apply the rule at the beginning of siman 288 that clothing already worn out is not appraised. There all the siblings were adults at the time of the expenditure, and since they did not protest against one another we presume mutual waiver. Here, however, the minors were incapable of protesting. Another commentator answers that there all were adults and equally suited to the same clothing. Since one prepared for himself and another did not, they presumably waived equalization. Here the siblings are not suited to identical clothing, and the same applies to food. We therefore do not presume that they accepted unequal expenditures from the outset.
