And if they protested against him, etc. Shaar Mishpat writes that although, where they did not protest, even one who is not the senior brother is generally supported from the common estate and the brothers are presumed to waive claims against one another, there is still a distinction between the head of the household and another brother. With another brother, even after he has already spent money and clothed himself, the others may object with respect to what remains before them. With the head of the household, however, they must protest at the outset, when he first dresses or provides for himself. If they did not protest then, the matter is deemed waived even prospectively, and when the estate is later divided they do not assess even the clothing he is wearing. This is stated by Nimukei Yosef in the first chapter of Bava Kamma and by the Derishah. See there.
