If they protested against him, etc. Shaar HaMishpat writes that although, where no protest is made, siblings living together are generally presumed to waive ordinary household expenditures to one another, there is a distinction between the head of the household and another sibling. With an ordinary sibling, even after he has already dressed himself from common funds, the others may object regarding future expenditures. With the household head, however, the protest must be made initially when he dresses or provides for himself. If no protest was made at the outset, the expenditure is treated as waived even prospectively, and one does not later assess even what is presently upon him. This is the rule stated by Nimukei Yosef and Drisha.
