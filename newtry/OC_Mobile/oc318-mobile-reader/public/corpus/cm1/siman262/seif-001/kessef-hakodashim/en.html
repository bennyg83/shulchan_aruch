We rule concerning despair without knowledge that where the item's weight or importance ordinarily causes the owner to notice its loss, the finder may keep it in a place where most people are gentiles, or where there is no identifying mark, because the owner is presumed to have learned of the loss and despaired. It requires study whether this rule requires certainty that the owner learned of the loss, or whether a reasonable doubt that he learned suffices. The language of the authorities and the Shulchan Aruch can be read both ways. If the owner knows with certainty that he became aware only after the finder had already found it, while the finder is uncertain when that awareness occurred, one might initially treat the finder as saying "I do not know whether I became obligated." Yet it appears that the original owner's chazakah remains and the situation is closer to "I do not know whether I discharged my obligation." There is also room to consider whether the finder should have known the relevant circumstances. The language of the authorities suggests an objective presumption of awareness, which may supply the necessary evidentiary basis, but the matter requires further study.
