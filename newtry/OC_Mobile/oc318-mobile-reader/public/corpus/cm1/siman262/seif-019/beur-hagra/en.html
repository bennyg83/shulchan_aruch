(Supplement) He found, etc. This follows the latter version. Rambam. Rosh also rules this way. Although Rosh writes that we follow all the versions stringently, it appears that the text is mistaken and should read, 'the halacha accords with all, etc.' As he continues, the versions do not dispute: the middle version explains 'it was not permitted' in the first version, and the last version explains 'he turned his face' in the middle version. The latter version is actually lenient. Rambam too, though he usually follows the latter version, must accept the first version because it is explicit in the baraita: if some of the produce, etc. The only difference is that according to Rambam, the last version rejects Rav Zevid's rule. Rosh, however, says that it does not dispute Rav Zevid, for the Gemara says, 'both concern a basket and produce,' implying specifically a basket and produce. Tur writes: all this concerns a basket and produce, but regarding a jug and flax, according to Rashi they always belong to the finder, while according to Ri they always belong to the person who gives the identifying mark for the vessel; my father Rosh writes likewise. See Tosafot and Rosh. Tur's phrase 'always' is not exact. (End of supplement.) See 62.
