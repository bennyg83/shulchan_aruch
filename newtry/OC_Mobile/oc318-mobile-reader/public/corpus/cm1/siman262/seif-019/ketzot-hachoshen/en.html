Where fruits are found beside a container and circumstances indicate that they belong to one owner, the Gemara and the decisors do not define how close they must be. A comparison can be drawn from Avodah Zarah concerning stones found near a Mercurius. Rashi explains that stones within four amot are considered within the immediate area of the idolatry. On that approach, fruits found within four amot of the vessel can be treated as coming from it. Tosafot, however, understands true proximity there as only about one amah or half an amah, while the four amot rule has a separate reason. According to Tosafot, the corresponding distance here would be much smaller. A difficulty arises from the rule that where majority and proximity conflict, we follow the majority. If so, why do we attribute fruits beside a vessel to that vessel rather than to the larger number of fruits coming from elsewhere? Likewise, why are nearby Mercurius stones forbidden rather than attributed to the majority of ordinary stones? Ramban answers in Perek Lo Yachpor that an object found in its actual place is stronger than ordinary proximity. "Here it was found, here it was" creates a combination of proximity and ordinary presence that even majority does not defeat. Thus stones within the Mercurius's immediate area, or fruits immediately beside their vessel, can be treated as located in their proper place rather than merely nearby. Rosh, however, appears to apply the majority rule even to strong local proximity, which would make these cases difficult. Torat Chaim distinguishes ordinary proximity from manifest proximity and says sufficiently clear proximity can defeat majority, estimating as much as fifty amot in some contexts. Yet the Mercurius passage indicates that beyond four amot the stones are permitted, so a general fifty amah rule cannot apply. Beit Shmuel writes in Even HaEzer that a purse merely found near a dead body is not enough for identification, because the purse may have been there earlier, and says this cannot be compared to fruits found beside a vessel. I do not see why the cases differ in principle. The purse's proximity to the body should be measured by the same rules governing fruits beside their vessel and stones beside Mercurius. The relevant distance may therefore depend on the dispute between Rashi and Tosafot, approximately one amah up to four amot according to the context.
