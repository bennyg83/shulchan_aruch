(Supplement) One who finds, etc. This is Rambam's wording, and it requires study. With two objects, number is not an identifying mark, as stated on 20b and 25a: what does he announce? the number, etc. Rosh answers in seif 5 that here the finder announces, 'I found needles,' and the claimant says they were in cases and there were two. Meaning, many needles were found even in one case, but the case itself is not an identifying mark because needles are ordinarily stored that way. Therefore, according to Rosh, one case belongs to the finder. Rambam's formulation without cases remains difficult. Bach answers that many cases were found but they were arranged two by two. Nevertheless, why did Rambam alter the Gemara's language? Perhaps he had a different text. (End of supplement.) See 370 and 660.
