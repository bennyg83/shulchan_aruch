(Collected note) He found, etc. Specifically three, and he announces "coins," while the claimant gives the identifying mark of their number. Gemara; see Rosh there. It requires study why Rambam and Shulchan Aruch omitted this.
(Collected note) And they are, etc., that is, etc. This follows Rabbeinu Chananel, whose view is accepted against Rabbi Yochanan, and Rabbi Yitzchak Migdal'ah agrees with him; the halachah does not follow one against two. He announces "coins," as stated there in the Gemara, "what does he announce, etc.?" This applies even according to Rabbeinu Chananel and Rabbi Yitzchak Migdal'ah.
Or if they were, etc. The Gemara there.
So that if, etc. This should be placed after "like a ladder," and so is the wording in the Gemara and Rambam.
(Collected note) Or if one was here and one there and one on top of them, or one partly on another, so that if he inserts a stick between them they are all lifted at once, he must announce them. If they were arranged like a circle, like a line, like stones of a quarry, or like a ladder, it is doubtful and he should not take them. The Gemara does not read, "at least resolve one, etc." Rather, Rav Nachman says independently, "all, etc." The case of a ladder therefore remains unresolved. The words of Shulchan Aruch are difficult. See below in the gloss, which follows Rashi, the Rosh, and our reading.
(Collected note) And he should not take them. The Rosh writes instead that he must announce them because a doubt involving a prohibition is treated stringently. Rambam does not require this, because money is not extracted from doubt, as with doubtful firstborn animals and similar cases. See below siman 260:10 in Shulchan Aruch and the gloss.
