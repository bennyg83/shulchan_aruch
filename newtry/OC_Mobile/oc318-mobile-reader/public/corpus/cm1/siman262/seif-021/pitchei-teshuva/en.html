And every Torah scholar, etc. See Baer Heitev, who writes that it requires study whether this applies nowadays. See Shevut Yaakov, volume 1, siman 167, who writes that Sema's doubt cannot displace Rama's certainty that every Torah scholar is presumed to recognize his property. He explains that since the object certainly does not belong to the finder, why should we cause a Torah scholar a loss without reason merely because of a doubt? See Birkei Yosef above, siman 15, paragraph 3, who questions this somewhat, though in practice he appears to agree. See what I wrote in siman 15, seif 1, seif katan 1. Where the owner of the lost object has already seized it and has a migo, we certainly do not remove it from him even if he is an am haaretz, provided the finder found it before the owners knew it was lost. All the more so with a Torah scholar, who may even seize it initially in such a case. See there, and what I wrote above in siman 4, seif katan 5.
