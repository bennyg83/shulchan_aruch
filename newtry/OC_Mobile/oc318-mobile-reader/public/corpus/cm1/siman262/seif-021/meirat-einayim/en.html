He must announce it so that if a Torah scholar comes, etc. From this wording the Beit Yosef and Kesef Mishneh infer that announcement is required even in a place where Torah scholars are not found. The Maggid Mishneh, however, explains that Rambam too holds announcement is required only when the object was found in a place frequented by Torah scholars. Rama apparently accepts this, for he concludes, "specifically if it was found in a place where Torah scholars are common," and does not present it as a dispute. The Tur likewise states the point without dispute and gives the reason: when it is found where Torah scholars are common, such as a beit midrash, the Torah scholar who lost it does not despair. He thinks the finder will reason that perhaps it fell from a Torah scholar and will return it, and the finder therefore takes it and announces it.
Or regarding the tractate, etc. Rambam writes: how so? If he was studying tractate Niddah, he may say, "I am studying Mikvaot," so that they not question him about Niddah; if he slept in one bed, he may say, "I slept in another," lest someone find evidence of a seminal emission there; if he lodged with Shimon, he may say, "I am lodging with Reuven," so as not to burden the host with additional guests; or if he made peace between people, he may alter details to make them love one another. Rashi explains tractate, bed, and lodging differently, as I recorded in the Perishah. In the Derishah I explained practical differences between Rashi and Rambam. According to Rambam, there is no specific mitzvah in the alteration itself; it is simply permitted as proper conduct. According to Rashi, there is an element of mitzvah, modesty, or humility. Further, according to Rambam, changing the name of the host is permitted specifically while one is lodging with Reuven and fears that the questioner too will lodge there and together they will burden that host. He therefore says he is lodging with Shimon. It is permitted to say this because even if the questioner lodges with Shimon, Shimon will bear only one guest, and people ordinarily host a guest. According to Rashi, by contrast, the point is not to publicize or praise the host, and this applies even after one is no longer lodging there. The Ir Shushan writes, "if he is lodging with Shimon and says he is lodging with another." Rambam, whom the Mechaber copied, actually says "with Reuven." Perhaps the Ir Shushan thought one should merely say "with another" without naming him, lest he burden that other person. His explanation does not seem correct. If the questioner asks where he is lodging, normal speech requires an actual answer naming someone, and there is no undue burden in the circumstances for the reason explained. In the Perishah I gave a mnemonic for the three permissible alterations by a Torah scholar: lodging, learning, and bed, meaning that one trains his mouth to say "I do not know" rather than disclose the literal truth in these matters.
And when he is particular to turn his garment inside out, etc. This is a statement of Rabbi Yochanan in chapter Elu Kesharim. Rif and the Rosh do not record it there, and Rambam and the Tur likewise omit it. In the Derishah I explained that they apparently omitted it because the anonymous Gemara in chapter Elu Metziot seems to disagree. There the return of a lost object depends only on whether he is truthful in those three matters, implying that this applies to every lost object, even a garment. Rabbi Yochanan makes return of a garment depend on his care in turning it, and although he spoke only of his garment, even there the Gemara should also have required truthfulness in speech.
Every Torah scholar is presumed, etc. It requires study whether this rule of Torah scholars applies today, since in several areas we do not apply the old legal status of a Torah scholar today, such as the litra of gold in Yoreh Deah simanim 243 and 334, release of vows before a single expert in Yoreh Deah 228, and judging alone unless the parties accepted him, in which case even one who is not an expert may judge alone, as explained in Choshen Mishpat simanim 3 and 22. See below in siman 287 regarding one who dies leaving adult and minor sons. If one adult improved the estate, the increase is normally shared, but if he is a Torah scholar whose Torah is his occupation we do not say that he should labor and neglect his learning for the others; the increase is his. Nimukei Yosef writes there that this status of Torah scholar does not apply today. The Beit Yosef rejects that, and the Shulchan Aruch does not cite Nimukei Yosef's view. Darkei Moshe, however, says Nimukei Yosef appears correct, while the Shulchan Aruch remains silent. See there.
