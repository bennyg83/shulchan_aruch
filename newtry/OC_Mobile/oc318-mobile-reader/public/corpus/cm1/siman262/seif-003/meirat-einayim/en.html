An identifying mark in its location. The finder announces, "I found such-and-such a lost object," and the claimant comes and says, "You found it in such-and-such a place, where I left it."
Or because of its weight. Meaning, he had been carrying it on his back or shoulder, and when it fell the load became lighter. Although he certainly did not notice the instant it fell, for then he would have lifted it immediately, he presumably noticed the loss shortly afterward, before this finder found and lifted it.
Since it came into his hand before despair. This is what the Sages call despair without knowledge, which is not valid despair. Meaning, even if the owner would have despaired immediately had he known of the loss, in fact he did not yet know when the item came into the finder's hand. The finder therefore took it unlawfully before despair occurred.
