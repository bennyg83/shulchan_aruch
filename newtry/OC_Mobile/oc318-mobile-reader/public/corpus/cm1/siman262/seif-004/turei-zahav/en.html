(Seif 6, scattered coins.) The Gemara says that this is not despair without knowledge, because Rabbi Yitzchak states that a person commonly checks his purse at all times. The Tur likewise writes that the owner notices the loss. Rambam, in chapter 14 of Hilchot Gezelah, writes: “Despair without knowledge is not despair. How so? If a dinar fell from him and he did not know it had fallen, even though when he later learns of the loss he despairs, it is not presently despair until the owner knows it fell. But if the owner still says, ‘Perhaps I gave it to so-and-so,’ or the like, this is not despair.” This is difficult, because regarding money we presume that the owner has already despaired. One may answer that Rambam excludes a case where the owner knows that a dinar is missing but does not know that it fell, because he thinks he may have given it to someone. The latter clause explains the former. It further appears to me that Rambam discusses one who had many dinars in his purse and one was lost. In such a case a person does not immediately notice until he counts the money. The Gemara makes a similar distinction at the end of HaGozel, page 118, between one who has other coins in his purse and one who does not. I wrote likewise in siman 355 concerning theft. This explanation is also correct regarding a found article here.
