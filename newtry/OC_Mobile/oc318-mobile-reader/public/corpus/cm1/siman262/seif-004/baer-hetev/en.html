From two. Whether they are partners or not, the owner does not despair. With three, however, this applies only when they are partners. Initially, the finder must announce it, since perhaps they are partners in it. But once it is known that they are not partners, or they acknowledge this and each says, 'It is mine,' the item belongs to the finder. So writes Rabbeinu Yerucham. The same applies if its value is only one and a half perutot, since it is not worth two complete perutot. Shach.

Although in siman 260, seif 9, I wrote that according to the Mechaber, in a case of despair without knowledge the finder acquires and need not return the item so long as its owner has not been identified, and we do not say that it should remain in safekeeping, this case is different. Since it fell from among two or three people, it is possible that the owner never despairs, continually assuming that it is with one of them and that they will return it to him. Shach.
Worth. And that he saw them searching for it. But if it is not worth two perutot, or he saw that they did not search for it, and he took it after enough time had passed for a person to check his purse, it belongs to him. Thus writes Rabbeinu Yerucham, Netiv 20, part II. The Tur and the other later authorities do not make this distinction, and it appears that they disagree. This also appears to me primary. It seems that Rabbeinu Yerucham derived this from Rashi and Tosafot on 24b. It appears, however, that Rashi wrote this only according to Rav Nachman, who does not accept the possibility that they may be partners. Likewise, Tosafot required that he see them searching only according to Rava, who holds that despair without knowledge is effective. According to our ruling that it is not effective, even if he did not see them searching and took the item after sufficient time to check one's purse, the owner does not despair, since perhaps they are partners and he waived his share, and for that reason did not search immediately. Examine this carefully. Ibid.
