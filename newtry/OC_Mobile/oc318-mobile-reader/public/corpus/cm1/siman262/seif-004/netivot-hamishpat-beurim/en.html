Worth two perutot. See Shach, seif katan 4. His words are difficult. What difference does it make that despair without knowledge is not valid? If the parties did not see one another searching, no one suspects the other and there appears no basis to preserve ownership. His intent can be understood from Tosafot's explanation that the case of partners concerns a situation where they searched together. Because they searched together, each assumes the other may have found it. Since he does not suspect his partner of theft but only of withholding it temporarily to trouble him, he does not despair. If they did not search together, he does not attribute the loss to his partner and therefore despairs from an item without an identifying sign. According to Abaye, however, who holds that despair without knowledge is not despair, even if one partner became aware of the loss, the other partners may still not know of it at all. Their shares therefore remain subject to despair without knowledge. Nor can the finder take the loser's one-third share alone, because perhaps the loser waived his own share to the other partners who were unaware. Accordingly, Sema's rule that if it is known the object belongs to one of them it need not be returned concerns a case where they searched together. Otherwise the loser may think someone else found it and will announce it because the object has an identifying sign.
