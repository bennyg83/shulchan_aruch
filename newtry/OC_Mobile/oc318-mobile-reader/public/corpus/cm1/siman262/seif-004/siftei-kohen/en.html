That fell from two people, etc. The Sema writes that with three people the rule applies specifically when they are partners, and initially one must announce the find lest they be partners. If it becomes known that they are not partners, or they acknowledge that they are not partners and each claims the object as his own, it belongs to the finder. Rabbeinu Yerucham adds that the same is true where the object is worth only one and a half perutot, since it does not contain two complete perutot.
Until Eliyahu comes. Although above, siman 260, seif 9, I explained that according to the Mechaber, in a case of despair without knowledge the finder may retain the object so long as its owner has not become identified, here is different. Since it fell from two or three people, it is possible that the loser never despairs, thinking that it remains with his companions and that they will return it.
Specifically when it is worth two perutot, etc. Rabbeinu Yerucham adds that he must have seen them searching for it. If it was worth less than two perutot, or he saw that they did not search for it and took it only after enough time had passed for the owner to feel his pocket, it belongs to him. The Tur and later authorities do not make this distinction, and it appears that they disagree. In my view their position is primary. Rabbeinu Yerucham appears to have derived his rule from Rashi and Tosafos on page 26b, but Rashi stated it only according to Rav Nachman, who does not apply the possibility that they are partners, and Tosafos required that they be seen searching only according to Rava, who holds that despair without knowledge is effective. According to our ruling that despair without knowledge is ineffective, even if the finder did not see them search and took it only after enough time to inspect one's pocket, the loser does not necessarily despair, for perhaps they are partners and he waived his share and therefore did not immediately search.
