And if afterward, etc. The Gemara says 21b only that when it came, etc. But if he did not know that the owner had despaired until afterward, there is no issue, as in the incident of the pitch where that man did not know that the owner had despaired because he was blocking the opening. Would one think that if he did not know of the despair it would nevertheless be permitted?
And if it was, etc. This follows from the Gemara's mention of two perutos, and otherwise where would Rav Nachman's statement apply? Rambam, however, holds that even in such a case he must return it, according to the Rif's reading: "some say, even though there is not a perutah's worth for each one, etc." Rif writes that Rava disagrees with Rav Nachman. This is why Rambam and Shulchan Aruch in siman 260:3 speak of three non-Jews, as explained there. See what I wrote at the end of siman 90.
