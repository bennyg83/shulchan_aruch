Partners. If we would say that the companions were not partners in the object, then even if it were worth a great deal the loser would despair, saying: I told those traveling with me several times that I lost this object and they did not return it; clearly they intend to steal it, and I cannot successfully claim against them because each will say, 'I did not find it.' This differs where they are partners, because the loser does not suspect his partners of intending to steal it; he assumes only that they are withholding it temporarily to distress him, and therefore he does not despair. Likewise, when there are only two travelers, he does not despair, because even if the other one denies having it and is prepared to swear that he did not find it, the loser thinks: today or tomorrow I will seize some of his property and retain it in place of my lost item.
