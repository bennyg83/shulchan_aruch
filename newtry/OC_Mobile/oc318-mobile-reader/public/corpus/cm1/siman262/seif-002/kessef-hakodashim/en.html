That fell from two people. If two people live in one house, and one says that he lost money there and demands it from his fellow and from a third person who was present, the third cannot answer: even if I found money, I need not believe that it was yours, for perhaps your roommate lost it and he is not claiming against me. Just as an identifying mark can establish the claimant's ownership, so too the fact that one person is actively claiming and crying out that he lost the money while the other makes no claim can itself establish that the loss was his. This resembles the evidentiary reasoning in Shlomo HaMelech's judgment between the two women.
