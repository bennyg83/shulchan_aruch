Therefore, one who finds, etc. The word "therefore" refers to seif 3: if it is an item whose owner would likely have noticed the loss immediately, it belongs to the finder. For this reason, the money and other items listed here belong to the finder. The Tur writes: a person frequently checks his purse for money. Purple wool strips are valuable. Fig cakes, loaves, and strings of produce are food items and important to him, so he checks them. Fleeces of wool and bundles of flax are heavy, so he notices when they fall. Since they have no identifying mark, he despairs. Their location is also not an identifying mark because none of these items is ordinarily placed on the ground intentionally; they came there through falling and their exact original place is unknown.
And loaves of a baker. This excludes loaves baked by a private householder, because one person's baking differs from another's and each householder's loaf has an identifying characteristic in the manner it was baked.
