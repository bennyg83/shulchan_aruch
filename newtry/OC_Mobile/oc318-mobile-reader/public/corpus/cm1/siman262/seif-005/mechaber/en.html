If one finds an object after its owners have despaired of recovering it, such as when they said, 'Woe for my monetary loss,' it belongs to the finder even if it bears a distinguishing mark. Likewise, an object showing that it was lost long ago and that its owners despaired belongs to the finder, even if its body or location bears a mark. Rama: Saying 'Woe for my monetary loss' establishes despair only regarding lost property and similar cases. A debt owed by a non-Jew is ordinarily not abandoned merely because the creditor said this, since every debt is uncertain of collection and he may have spoken from doubt without definitively despairing.
