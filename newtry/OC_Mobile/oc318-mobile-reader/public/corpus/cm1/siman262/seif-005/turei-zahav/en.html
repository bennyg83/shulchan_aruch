(Seif 12: arranged like a tower.) According to our master, location is an identifying mark even for coins, for he writes that a coin is returned based on the identifying mark of its place, etc. This creates a difficulty. Why do the coins need to be arranged like a tower? If they are not arranged like a tower, why should they belong to the finder? True, there is no proof that they were intentionally placed, but the case should still remain one of doubtful placement. Although intentional placement would not help in a completely unguarded place, because even if the owner intentionally put them there he would later forget them, it should still matter because the owner could identify the place. Since location is an identifying mark, the finder should take and announce them. With scattered produce or scattered coins, one can say that the very scattered arrangement proves they were not intentionally placed, so there is no location identifying mark. But ordinary coins that are not stacked and are lying in a normal manner might still have been intentionally placed. Why not treat this as doubtful placement, like items found behind a fence in siman 260, where specifically if they are moving do we infer otherwise, whereas if they remain stationary the place can be an identifying mark, as the Maggid Mishneh writes in chapter 15 of Hilchot Gezelah concerning doubtful placement, meaning doubtful falling? One must answer that coins are different because people do not ordinarily place loose money directly on the ground without a pouch, so we do not treat ordinary loose coins as possibly intentionally placed. The Tur also writes that two coins do not constitute an identifying count. This is difficult: if two is not an identifying mark because the minimum plural is two, why should three be an identifying mark? Since fewer than three are not announced, three should be equivalent to two, just as with two one cannot infer anything from the fact that he did not state a higher number. One may answer that when the contrast is between singular and plural, a person normally states the actual plural amount at the outset. But when the distinction is within plural amounts, a larger specified number becomes meaningful.
