(Seif 16, one by one.) Beit Yosef asks Rambam what identifying mark exists in groups of two. The Sema, seif katan 32, resolves this. Beit Yosef also asks from the Gemara’s question, “What are badei?” It appears to me that Rambam explains the baraita’s words “badei needles” as two separate expressions. “Badei” means nails. The Gemara therefore asks, “What are badei?” and answers that something used for hanging articles is called “badei,” meaning a nail ordinarily used to hang objects needed by a person. Rambam therefore mentions only needles and separately mentions nails, which are not otherwise stated explicitly in the Gemara, because this is the meaning of “badei.” The Tur’s reference to ordinary needles follows Rambam’s legal ruling, although regarding the interpretation he follows Rashi, who reads “badei” as attached to “needles.” Beit Yosef asks according to Rashi why, if they fell, the finder must announce them. I do not understand the difficulty. In every doubt whether an article fell or was intentionally placed, we rule stringently, except regarding coins, as stated in seif 12. (The number is an identifying mark.) The Sema, seif katan 33, quotes Darkei Moshe. Darkei Moshe asked why coins that are not arranged like a tower do not bear an identifying mark, whereas needles found in pairs do, as stated here. He answers that everyone commonly carries coins and commonly loses them by falling, whereas people carry needles and pegs only as needed. The owner therefore knows their number and pays attention to it. I have not understood the original difficulty. Just as with coins their tower-like arrangement proves that they were placed there, so too with needles, finding them in pairs proves intentional placement.
