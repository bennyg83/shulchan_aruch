Small bundles of grain found in a public domain belong to the finder because they bear no mark. If found in private property, they belong to the finder when they appear to have fallen, but if deliberately placed, they must be announced, since the location serves as a mark. Full sheaves found either in private or public property must be taken and announced. Rama: Their location is a mark. A place where everyone puts such goods, however, such as barrels on a riverbank, is not a mark because all unload there. One who finds barrels of wine, oil, or similar goods before the storehouses have opened must announce them, because the producer's marking is distinctive. After the storehouses open, that marking is no longer distinctive and no announcement is required. This applies when all barrels are alike and full; if partially empty, the quantity remaining is a distinguishing mark.
