There is an identifying mark in their markings. Each householder who sells many barrels commonly places the same distinctive mark near the stopper on all of his barrels, but the mark of one householder differs from another's. Therefore, if a barrel is found before the storehouses have been opened for general sales, it must be announced. If the owner comes and gives the mark, it is returned to him. We do not suspect that he already sold it and that it fell from a purchaser, because the season for sales has not yet begun. Likewise, if someone comes and says, "I bought this barrel from so and so, and this is the mark upon it," we believe him. We do not say it must have fallen from the seller, because how would the claimant know the mark? Nor do we suspect that he himself bought one barrel and still has it, learned the mark from his own barrel, and is now falsely claiming another barrel that another purchaser bought from the same seller. Before the opening of the storehouses, we do not suspect multiple sales. After the storehouses have opened and such sales are common, both concerns exist. Therefore the barrel is returned neither to the original seller nor to a claimant relying only on the householder's mark. See the Perishah and Derishah for further discussion.
