If they remained in his possession, etc. The same applies if the merchant himself finds them after enough time has passed for him to have sold the produce onward. The owner despairs, assuming that the merchant has already sold it and that the purchaser will not know to whom it should be returned. Tosafot, the Maggid Mishna citing Ramban and Rashba, and the Nimukei Yosef all indicate this.
In his possession, etc. The Sema writes that if the coins were found before the produce had remained with the merchant long enough, the case is despair without knowledge; therefore, if the owner later comes and identifies them, they must be returned even after a long time, and the finder must proclaim them or return them if he knows the owner. This terminology is imprecise. Despair without knowledge applies only to an item without an identifying mark; where there is a mark, the owner does not despair at all. The case here necessarily concerns scattered produce or the like, without an identifying mark. The Sema should therefore have said simply that it is despair without knowledge and must be returned if the owner is known.
