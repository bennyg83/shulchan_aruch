They remained. Meaning, the coins remained in the money changer's possession before the finder found them for long enough that, by the time they were found, the owner had already despaired. But if they were found before they had remained with him that long, it is despair without knowledge, and the finder must return them when the owner of the money appears and gives an identifying mark, even after a long time. He must therefore announce them, or return them himself if he knows to whom they belong. So writes the Sema. The Shach adds that the same applies if the money changer himself finds them after enough time has passed for him to have sold them to others, because the owner then despairs, assuming that the money changer has already sold them and that the purchaser does not know to whom they should be returned. So write Tosafot at the beginning of page 27, and this also appears from the Maggid Mishneh in chapter 15 of Hilchot Gezeilah, law 7, in the names of the Ramban and Rashba, and from the Nimukei Yosef.
