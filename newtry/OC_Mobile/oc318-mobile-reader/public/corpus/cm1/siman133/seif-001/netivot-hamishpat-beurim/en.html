Ketzot HaChoshen writes that property may be removed even from the possession of a minor, because the presumption that whatever is in a person's possession belongs to him does not apply to a minor. He proves this from the Maggid Mishneh in chapter 13 of Hilchot To'en, who states that the possession of a deaf mute, mentally incompetent person, or minor does not constitute proof, because their claims are unlike those of ordinary persons and their purchases are not valid by Torah law. From this Ketzot concludes that even with movable property they have no chazakah. This conclusion should be rejected. Land is not comparable to movable property. A minor has no fully binding acquisition of land through a purchase and may always retract, as explained in siman 235, Sema, seif katan 29. Therefore he is not believed merely on such a claim, and a gift is uncommon enough that we do not ordinarily plead it for him. In addition, chazakah of land ordinarily derives its force from a document, and minors are not competent participants in documentary transactions, as explained in siman 235, seif 8. They likewise are not capable of making an effective protest. Movable property is different. Minors are capable of acquisition by rabbinic law, and where another person intentionally transfers property to them their acquisition has Torah effect, since property declared ownerless by beit din is ownerless by Torah law, as explained in Even HaEzer. They therefore certainly have the ordinary presumption of possession. Even if one were to say that they are incapable of making a legal claim themselves, they are no worse than an adult who is absent, for whom beit din advances appropriate claims in order to preserve property found in his possession. This is clear. See what I wrote concerning this in siman 149, seif 18.
