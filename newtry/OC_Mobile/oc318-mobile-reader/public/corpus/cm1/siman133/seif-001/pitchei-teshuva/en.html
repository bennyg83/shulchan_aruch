See Baer Heitev, who concludes that according to what was written there, one may later say, "I purchased it," and therefore the present case must be understood differently. See what I wrote there in siman 72, seif 18, in the passage beginning "that they are sold," in the name of Responsa Rabbi Akiva Eiger, siman 135. He distinguishes between a case where the witnesses do not themselves know that the object originally belonged to the claimant and know this only from the possessor's own statement, in which case the possessor is also believed when he says outside beit din that he purchased it, and a case where the witnesses themselves recognize the object as belonging to the claimant, in which case he is not believed to say outside beit din that he purchased it. According to this, one can say that the case here is where the witnesses themselves recognize the object.
