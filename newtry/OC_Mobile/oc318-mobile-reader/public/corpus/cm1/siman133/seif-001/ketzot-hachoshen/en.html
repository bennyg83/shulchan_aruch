Property found in a minor's hands can be removed when witnesses establish that it belonged to another person, even if it is an item not normally lent or rented. Ramban writes in Hilchot To'en that the possession of a deaf mute, mentally incompetent person, or minor does not create the ordinary presumption, because they lack the mature plea needed to support possession. Maggid Mishneh correctly explains that this does not mean minors can never acquire property. A minor can certainly acquire in recognized ways, and even a mentally incompetent person can receive a gift where another transfers it for him. The point is that their bare possession is not evidentiary proof equivalent to an adult's possession. Land law says the same. The possession of these people over land is not proof because they cannot make the ordinary factual plea required to support a prescriptive possession. Movables likewise require a supporting plea. Mere holding by a minor is therefore like possession without a claim. If, however, the minor comes through his father's possession, the matter changes. Although the authorities in siman 149 dispute whether a minor can rely on the father's land possession, that issue is affected by the fact that minors are not capable of receiving a legal protest. With movables, where that concern does not arise, beit din can advance for the minor whatever claim his father could have made concerning property that was in the father's hands. See siman 110, seif 5.
