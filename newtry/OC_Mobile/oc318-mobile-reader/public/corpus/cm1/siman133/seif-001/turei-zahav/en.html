(Seif 2). “He is believed through a migo that the object was lost through accident.” It appears that Rambam does not accept this migo. In siman 70 he holds that when a lender says, even after the loan, “Do not repay me except before witnesses,” the borrower is not believed to say that he repaid without witnesses. Ran explains that we are witnesses that he is lying, as Beit Yosef cites. If so, no migo helps here, because it would be a migo against witnesses. Although in a loan there is no migo of accidental loss, since a borrower of money is liable for accidents, after the loan the borrower was not legally required to obey the instruction, yet Rambam still disbelieves him because we are witnesses against his claim. The Mechaber rules there like Rambam and here like the opposing view. One must force the distinction that the evidentiary presumption there is not so strong as to defeat an actual migo, although that answer is strained.
