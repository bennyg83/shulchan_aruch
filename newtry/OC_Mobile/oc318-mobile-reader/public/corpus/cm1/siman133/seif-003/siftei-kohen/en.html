This is properly considered a case of witnesses and seeing. The Tur likewise writes this. The Sema says that the underlying case involved witnesses who testified that the item had been delivered to him as a deposit, and that the expression 'witnesses and seeing' therefore led the Rosh to rule that it must be removed from his possession. This is surprising. Once witnesses now saw the object and he stated that it was held as collateral, thereby losing his migo, why should it matter whether the original deposit was made before witnesses? The Tur and Mechaber imply the opposite. It is enough that witnesses saw the object in the possessor's hand before litigation and that he then said it was held as collateral. This itself is deemed 'witnesses and seeing,' even though there are no witnesses to the original deposit, for the reason given by the Rosh and Tur: his entire claim depends upon an oath, since beit din would award him the object's value through a migo that he could swear he returned it. Once the object is seen in his hand, he cannot swear that he returned it, and must restore it. Ordinarily we require witnesses to the deposit and do not suffice with present sight alone only where, when it was seen, he did not say that it was held as collateral. He may then say that at the time it was seen it was already his by purchase, since no witnesses saw the deposit, as explained above in siman 72, seif 18. Nor did the responsum of the Rosh involve two witnesses to the deposit; there was only one witness. See there.
