[Shach, letter 5] And this is astonishing to me. Note: In my humble opinion, the Sema's reasoning is as follows. Only where, if the witnesses had seen the object on their own, the challenger would have been believed, such as where there are witnesses to the deposit, then even if the possessor himself showed the object to witnesses and said, "I purchased it," or, "It is pledged to me," the migo that it is in his possession does not help, because statements made outside beit din are not legally operative statements, and the object is deemed seen. But where there are no witnesses to the deposit, mere observation by witnesses does not harm him, because his possession itself supports the claim that it was purchased. We therefore come to adjudicate only on the basis of his own admission. Since we rely on his words for purposes of adjudication, he is believed in all of his statement, and we do not divide his statement in half. This also explains the reasoning of Mabit cited by the Shach in the next seif katan. What the Shach writes, that in the responsum of the Rosh the incident involved not two witnesses but one witness, is also understandable. One witness suffices in this respect, because if witnesses saw the object and there is one witness to its delivery, he can no longer say, "It is pledged to me," through a migo that he could say, "I purchased it," since that would be a migo to contradict the witness, resulting in an obligation to swear which he cannot discharge. Even if he displayed it only before one witness, he is likewise not believed for this reason. Examine this carefully.
