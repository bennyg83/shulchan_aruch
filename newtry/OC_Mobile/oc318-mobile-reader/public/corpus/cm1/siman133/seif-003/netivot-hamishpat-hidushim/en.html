Even if the possessor himself showed the item before witnesses and said that it was held as a pledge, we accept the part of his statement that is against his interest, namely that the item belongs to the claimant, but not his assertion that it is a pledge. Sema. The reason is that a claimant who relies on possession of a pledge must swear. Therefore, his earlier statement before witnesses does not establish the pledge claim; everything depends on what beit din ultimately rules. Shach and Taz. The Sema writes that his inability to say that the item is pledged, after he told witnesses that it was pledged, applies only if there are also witnesses that it had originally been deposited with him. The Shach is surprised by this. Once there are witnesses who saw the item and he has no migo, even without witnesses to the original deposit he is not believed. Nor is the rule limited to his physically showing it to witnesses. Even if he merely admitted before witnesses that it was pledged to him, he cannot later claim before beit din that it is pledged, and must return the item without payment. See the Beurim. The Sema's intent is that witnesses to the original deposit are needed where the lender does not seek to collect the loan at all, but the owner sues only for return of the item and the possessor answers, "It is pledged to me." He is then making a claim directly to the body of the pledge, which is like a claim of purchase and ordinarily requires no migo. For that situation witnesses to the original deposit are necessary:
So that he cannot claim, "I returned it to you." He is not believed through a migo that he could claim unavoidable loss. According to the authorities above in siman 70 who hold that even bringing other witnesses does not help after such a warning, certainly a migo does not help. Even according to those who allow other witnesses, a migo still does not help. The Gemara explains that the purpose of the warning, "before so and so and so and so," is to prevent him from evading payment. If a migo remained available, he could always evade payment, since there would always be some migo. His warning therefore necessarily intended to eliminate the migo. See the Beurim. If, however, he claims, "I purchased the article but have not yet paid you," so that his claim concerns only ownership of the article itself, he is believed.
