We properly call this "witnesses and seeing." The Tur likewise writes this, based on a responsum of the Rosh, klal 106. The incident there involved witnesses who saw that the object had originally been delivered to him as a deposit. The expression "witnesses and seeing" itself ordinarily means witnesses to the delivery and witnesses who later see the object. Therefore the Rosh ruled that the object is removed from his possession because he has no migo. Even though he voluntarily showed the object and said, "It is held by me as a pledge," we do not apply here the principle that the mouth that created the prohibition is the mouth that removes it. Once he had already admitted outside beit din, in the presence of the witnesses who saw it, that the object was in his hands, we accept the portion of his statement that operates against him, but not his additional assertion that he holds it as a pledge.
And he should warn him before those witnesses not to return it, etc. The wording implies that the claimant is required to do this, because otherwise the possessor would be believed to say, "I hold it as a pledge," through the migo that he could claim, "I returned it." In the responsum, however, the Rosh says only that it is advisable to do so as an added precaution. The Tur, when citing the responsum, does not mention it at all. In the Perishah I explained that the responsum teaches that even if the claimant intends to sue immediately after the witnesses see the object, it is still prudent to give this warning in case judges are not immediately available. The Tur speaks where the claimant in fact sued at once. The Mechaber speaks where he does not intend to sue immediately, in which case he should indeed give the warning. See also seif 1.
