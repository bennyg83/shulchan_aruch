And it was seen. The Tur also writes this, based on Rosh, rule 106. In the incident there, witnesses had seen that the article was delivered to him for safekeeping, as is also implied by the words “witnesses and it was seen.” The Sema therefore explains that the Rosh ordered the article removed from his possession because he himself had admitted outside beit din, before the witnesses who saw it, that it was held as collateral. We accept the portion of his statement that operates against him, but not his assertion that it was collateral. The Shach writes that this is difficult. Once witnesses now see the object and he says it is held as collateral, his migo is gone; why should it matter whether the original deposit was witnessed? The Tur and Mechaber likewise imply that present observation by witnesses is enough. In the Rosh's responsum itself there was only one witness to the original deposit. The Taz also writes that he does not understand the Sema's reason.
