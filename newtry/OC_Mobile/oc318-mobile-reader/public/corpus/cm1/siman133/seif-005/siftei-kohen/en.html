Even if the possessor died, etc. See the Sema, seif katan 14, who writes that the Raavad did not state his ruling where the possessor died, but only while he remained alive. This is not compelled, and the plain meaning of the Raavad indicates that he refers also to heirs. The Darkei Moshe likewise writes that the Rambam disagrees with the ruling of the Geonim, the Maggid Mishneh considers the Rambam's position primary, and the Raavad follows the Geonim that property is not removed from heirs unless the claimant swears while holding a sacred object. The Tur likewise cites only the Geonim. Accordingly, the Rama here plainly means that the claimant must swear while holding a sacred object, as is also implied by the expression 'swear and take.' Nevertheless, as a matter of law it appears that only a heset oath is required, for this is explicit in the Rambam and Semag according to the Geonim, and is also proved by the Tur. See below siman 296. The text here is therefore corrupt, and some editions of the Tur indeed read differently. See Responsa Maharan Sasson, end of siman 155.

Without an oath, while others say that he must, etc. The Rosh at the end of section 18 of the chapter Chazakat HaBatim also appears to hold that heirs are supplied with every plea their father could have raised and may be subjected to an oath even on an uncertain claim. His first answer there, which points the other way and which he did not accept, follows Tosafot. The Mechaber's ruling therefore requires study, since he himself cites below, siman 150, seif 6, both opinions, including the Rosh. The Rama too should have written in siman 150 that the latter opinion is primary, as he writes here. The Sema in siman 149, seif katan 37, resolves the Rama's words with difficulty, while the Mechaber's ruling remains still more difficult. It appears that the Mechaber may be distinguished as follows. There, the claimant comes to collect, because he does not claim ownership of the produce itself, and therefore must swear while holding a sacred object, as the Geonim rule concerning a lender holding collateral, who must so swear even on an uncertain claim, like all who swear and collect, as explained above in siman 72, seif 17. Here, by contrast, once he must return the object, the rule of those who swear and collect does not apply. The owner could only require a heset oath after the object was returned, and a heset oath is never imposed on an uncertain claim according to any view.
