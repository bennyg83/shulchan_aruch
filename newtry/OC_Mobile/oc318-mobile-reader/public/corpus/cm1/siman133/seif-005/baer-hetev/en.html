To swear. The heirs must take a hesset oath because we advance on their behalf every claim their father could have made. So writes the Sema. See Responsa Mahara Sasson, end of siman 155, and Responsa Rashach, book III, siman 70. The Taz asks that in siman 297 the ruling is that property is taken from heirs without an oath, which also creates a difficulty for the Tur and requires study. The Shach writes that the matter requires study because the Mechaber himself brings in siman 150 seif 6 an opinion that the claimant must swear. Rama too is difficult, for he should have written there, as here, that the latter opinion is primary. The Sema in siman 149 seif katan 21 resolves Rama only with difficulty. The Shach suggests a resolution for the Mechaber: there, the claimant comes to take property and does not claim that the produce itself belongs to him, so he must swear with a Torah-level form of oath, as the Geonim ruled regarding a creditor holding collateral, discussed in siman 72 seif 17. Here, however, since the possessor must return the object itself, the category of “swearing and taking” does not apply. At most he could impose a hesset oath after returning it, and hesset is not administered on an uncertain claim according to any view.
