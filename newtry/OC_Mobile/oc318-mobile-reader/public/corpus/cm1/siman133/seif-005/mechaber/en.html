When does the rule that the possessor is believed apply? To objects not commonly lent or rented. With objects commonly lent or rented, if the claimant has witnesses that the object belonged to him and they now saw it in the possessor's hands, and the claimant says he lent or rented it to him, then although he has no witnesses showing how it came into the possessor's hands, the possessor is not believed to say, 'I purchased it,' or, 'I hold it as collateral.' He must return it even if it was in his possession for three years. Even if the possessor died, it is removed from his heir without an oath. Some say the claimant must swear and then take it. This appears to be the primary view. If the heir makes a definite claim and says, 'It was given or sold to my ancestor in my presence,' the claimant takes a heses oath.
