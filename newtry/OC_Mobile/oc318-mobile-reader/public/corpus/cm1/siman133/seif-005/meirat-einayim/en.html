When is the possessor believed, etc. This refers back to seif 2, where if the item was not delivered to him before witnesses, even though it was later seen in his possession, he is believed to say, 'I purchased it.' Here the Mechaber explains that this applies specifically to an item not ordinarily lent or rented.
He must return it. Meaning, after the claimant swears a hesset oath that it came into the possessor's hand as a loan or rental. The Tur writes this explicitly. The Mechaber did not need to state it because it is obvious that whenever one prevails against a definite claim he must at least swear hesset concerning the opposing claim. It is also implicit in the Mechaber's conclusion, where if the heir asserts a definite claim the claimant must swear hesset. Certainly when the possessor himself is alive and asserts a definite claim, the requirement is no less.
Even if it remained there for three years. A three-year chazakah does not establish ownership of movable property. Perhaps the owner forgot to whom he lent or rented it, and sometimes people lend or rent for more than three years. The Tur gives another reason as well, but this is the principal reason according to the Tur, as I wrote in siman 135, where there is a practical difference between the explanations.
Even if the possessor died, etc. Rambam holds that the claimant takes the item from the heirs without any oath at all, because there is no definite claim against him. See below siman 149:22 for a similar ruling of the Mechaber, and what I wrote there, and in siman 150:6 and siman 297. The Geonim, however, hold that he must swear hesset, because we advance for heirs any claim their father could have made. This is what Rama means in the gloss by 'some say that he must swear,' namely a hesset oath. He does not mean an oath while holding a sacred object, as Raavad holds by comparing the case to those who swear and collect listed in the Mishnah. Maggid Mishneh already rejects that view, and Raavad himself did not write it regarding a deceased possessor but regarding one who is still alive. Examine the sources and you will find this explanation correct.
