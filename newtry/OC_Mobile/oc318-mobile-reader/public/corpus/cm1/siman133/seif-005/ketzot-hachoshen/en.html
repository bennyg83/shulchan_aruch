The Shulchan Aruch requires the depositor to warn the custodian before the same witnesses not to return the object except before them. One must ask why the custodian should not still be believed that the object was held as collateral through a migo that it was lost through unavoidable accident. Both claims require an oath, and according to the Shach even a claim of unavoidable accident regarding a written deposit requires a rabbinic severe oath. Perhaps once the warning was made outside beit din and the custodian later remains silent in the face of the witnesses' testimony that no return occurred before them, the case is treated as one of denial of a deposit. He can then become liable even for unavoidable accident. See what I wrote in siman 294, seif katan 1.
