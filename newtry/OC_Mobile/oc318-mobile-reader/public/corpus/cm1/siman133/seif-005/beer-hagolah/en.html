Maggid Mishneh cites Shevuot 46b, where Rav Huna bar Avin sent a ruling, etc. Rif and many Geonim hold that in every such case the possessor is not believed, and there is no need for witnesses to have seen him conceal the utensils. This is the principal ruling, and there is a compelling proof from HaMekabel, from the incident where Rava removed property, Bava Metzia 116a.
Tur in the name of Baal HaIttur: since movable property is not ordinarily documented by deed, chazakah does not apply to it in the same manner. Rabbeinu Yonah likewise writes that perhaps the owner forgot to whom he lent or rented it.
There and in Rambam, from the cited incident involving Rava.
Rambam writes so there in chapter 9, law 5. Maggid Mishneh explains that according to Rambam, the owner takes a heset oath only where the person holding the utensils makes a definite claim. Against an heir, who does not make a definite claim, the owner does not take a heset oath, because a heset oath is not imposed on a doubtful claim. Maggid Mishneh writes that Rambam's view is the principal one.
There, law 3, he writes in their name that the owner takes a heset oath.
Raavad writes in his gloss there in chapter 9 that anyone who takes property by claim must swear while holding a sacred object. Rama does not specify whose view he follows. Sema writes that the oath is a heset oath, because Maggid Mishneh rejected Raavad's view from the fact that the Mishnah did not list this case among those who swear and collect. Bach writes that this is also the view of Tur and Semag, who do not cite Rambam's ruling or what he writes in the name of the Geonim. The Rosh likewise writes in Chezkat HaBatim that in a case involving orphans he is made to swear even on a doubtful claim, as explained below in siman 150, seif 6.
