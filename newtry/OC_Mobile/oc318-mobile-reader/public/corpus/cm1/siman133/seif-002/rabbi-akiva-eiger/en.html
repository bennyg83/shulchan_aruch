[Sema, letter 9] For otherwise he would be believed. Note: This is difficult for me. He should still be believed to say, "It is held by me as a pledge," through a migo that he could say that it was subsequently lost through unavoidable circumstances. If the answer is that he would then have to swear while holding a sacred object, even without the warning he must swear in that manner because he seeks to collect. In truth, the responsum of the Rosh states only that he should warn him so that he cannot claim, "I returned it," meaning that due to the warning he will at least have to swear. According to what Ran writes, as cited by the Shach, siman 89, seif katan 10, the Sema's words can be reconciled: without a warning, if he wished merely to remain silent and retain the object until he is paid its value, he would not need to swear.
