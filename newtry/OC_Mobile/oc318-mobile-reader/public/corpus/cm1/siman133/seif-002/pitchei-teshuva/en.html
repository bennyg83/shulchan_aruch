He is likewise believed to say that it was purchased. See Baer Heitev. According to what is written there, that he may subsequently say, 'I purchased it,' the present case must be explained accordingly. See what I wrote above in siman 72, seif 18, citing Responsa Rabbi Akiva Eiger, siman 135, which distinguishes between a case where the witnesses know that the article belonged to the claimant only because of the possessor's own statement, in which case he is also believed outside beit din when he says that he purchased it, and a case where the witnesses themselves recognize from their own observation that it belonged to the claimant, in which case he is not believed outside beit din to say that it was purchased. According to this, the present case may be speaking where the witnesses themselves recognize it.
