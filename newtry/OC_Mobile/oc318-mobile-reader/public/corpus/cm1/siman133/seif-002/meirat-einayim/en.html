That it is presently in his possession. Wherever the expression 'presently in his possession' is used, it means immediately before the claim in beit din, in circumstances where he cannot say that he returned it afterward. If, however, he himself displayed the item in beit din, that does not weaken his position, because he can say, 'Here is my document together with its receipt': he voluntarily showed it while asserting that it was purchased, given to him, or held as collateral, and he is believed through a migo that he could have withheld it and claimed that the matter never occurred or that he returned it. By contrast, if witnesses saw it in his possession before litigation, then even if he told those witnesses that he bought it or held it as collateral, he is not believed when the claimant has witnesses that it belongs to him, because statements made outside beit din are not treated as formal claims. Thus they saw it in his possession immediately before litigation. With this explanation the Mechaber's wording in seif 3 is understood.
He is likewise believed to say, 'I purchased it,' through a migo that he could have said, 'I returned it to you.' See siman 64, where I explained why we accept this migo and do not say that he would be reluctant to claim return because he would then have to conceal the item so that it would no longer be seen in his possession. In siman 72, seif 18, Rama writes that if he earlier claimed before witnesses, even long before litigation, that he purchased it, he can no longer come before beit din and claim purchase through a migo of return, because once he claimed purchase the item is no longer poised for return.
Through a migo that they were lost through unavoidable circumstances. In siman 70, seif 3, it is stated that in such a case the claim does not help with respect to a loan, because there is no such migo there, as a borrower is liable even for unavoidable accidents. What the Mechaber writes here, that he must take a Torah oath, follows because had he claimed unavoidable loss he would not have been exempt without a Torah oath.
I returned it to you before so-and-so and so-and-so. But if he merely says, 'I returned it before witnesses,' without naming them, and says that they have gone overseas, he is not believed, as the Tur writes in siman 70, seif 6. See also the Perishah here.
