The claimant said, etc. Many have labored to explain Rabbenu Yonah's ruling cited by the Tur without finding a satisfactory interpretation. In my view the case was as follows. Reuven deposited an object with Shimon and told him not to return it except before witnesses. Levi then came to Shimon and said, Reuven sold it to me and instructed you to give the deposited object to me. Shimon knew of this claim and delivered the object. Reuven now demands the object from Levi. Levi says, I bought it from you and in Shimon's presence you instructed him to give it to me because I had purchased it from you. Reuven denies this and says that Shimon delivered the object without his command or consent. Rabbenu Yonah rules that although Levi has a migo that he could have said, I bought it directly from you and received it from you, nevertheless, once Levi admits that the object came to him from Shimon, Reuven can sue Shimon for return of the deposit. Shimon plainly would be liable, because he cannot claim that he returned it: he was required to return only before witnesses, and he has no migo of unavoidable loss because the object is now seen in Levi's possession. Therefore the original bailee must pay the depositor. Once Shimon must pay Reuven, Shimon in turn may recover the object from Levi, saying: what right do you have to retain property for which I am legally obligated to pay? Since I must compensate Reuven, the object is effectively mine as against you, not Reuven's, and you cannot keep my property. Rabbenu Yonah apparently holds that even if Shimon knows that Levi's claim is factually correct, since Shimon must nevertheless pay Reuven because he cannot prove the authorized delivery, he may recover the object from Levi. Above in siman 58 the law does not appear so, but that is not a decisive objection against Rabbenu Yonah. Accordingly, even if Shimon is absent, Reuven may proceed directly against Levi under Rabbi Natan's rule, since Reuven has a claim against Shimon and Shimon has a claim against Levi. The case must involve Shimon having no assets, for otherwise Reuven should simply collect from Shimon, and indeed under every explanation of Rabbenu Yonah the case must be one where the original bailee offers no practical source of recovery because he wrongfully disposed of the deposit. Rabbenu Yonah's wording, Since it may be said that it came to him from the bailee, means not that the parties dispute whether Levi received it from Shimon. They agree that he did. Rather, Levi says that Shimon delivered it in Reuven's presence and by Reuven's command, which legally makes it as though Levi received it from Reuven himself because Shimon acted merely as agent. Likewise, when Rabbenu Yonah says, Since we suspect the bailee of not having returned it, the meaning is that if Shimon delivered it to Levi on Reuven's instructions there could be no better return of the deposit. Our suspicion is that Reuven tells the truth and Shimon improperly delivered it to Levi without authorization, so there was no valid return, despite the requirement of witnesses. This reading appears to me more satisfactory than all the explanations of the commentators I have seen.
