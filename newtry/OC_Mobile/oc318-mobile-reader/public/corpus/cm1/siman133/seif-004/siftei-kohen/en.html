He is believed through a migo that he could have said, 'You sold it to me,' as stated below in siman 134. Responsa Mabit, part 2, siman 205, writes that the Rosh's ruling in responsum klal 106 applies only where witnesses saw the object, but not where the possessor admitted holding it. His words do not appear correct to me, for what difference is there between witnesses who saw and witnesses to his admission? His statement there that in admissions of debt the possessor is believed is, however, obvious. See there. See also Responsa Maharan Sasson, end of siman 107.
And I told him, 'Do not return it,' etc. The Sema, seif katan 10, implies that even if the claimant brings witnesses that he warned the custodian not to return the item except before those particular witnesses, and those witnesses testify that it was not returned before them, the possessor is still believed. Perhaps the warning was later revoked before other witnesses, or perhaps the custodian returned it before other witnesses who, had he been present, could have testified and exempted him, as above in siman 70, seif 4. Perhaps he also gave the owner a written acknowledgment that it had been returned. This is likewise implied by Bedek HaBayit, which states that even if the claimant brings witnesses that before them he said, 'Return it to me only before witnesses,' the possessor may answer: perhaps the custodian returned it to you before witnesses, and I do not believe your statement that he did not. Moreover, I know with certainty that you are lying, because you sold or gave the item to me, and therefore the custodian must have returned it to you. This is contrary to the Bach, who understood the Shulchan Aruch to rule only where the claimant brought no witnesses to his warning, but that if he did bring witnesses the item is removed from the possessor. It appears to me that Bedek HaBayit means that even where the claimant proves his warning, the item is not removed, as explained. A further proof appears from Bava Batra 45b. There it is evident that even according to the view that one who deposits before witnesses must return before witnesses, if the possessor claims, 'You sold it to me,' or, 'You instructed the custodian to sell it to me,' he is believed even where witnesses prove the original deposit. The reason must be that he says, 'I know with certainty that you are lying, because you sold it to me.' The same applies even if witnesses testify that the custodian did not return it before them.
