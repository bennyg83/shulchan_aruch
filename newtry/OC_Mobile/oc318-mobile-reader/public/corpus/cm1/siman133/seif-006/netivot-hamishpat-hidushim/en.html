They were stolen from me. The Mechaber's ruling stands. The Shach rules that the owner is believed specifically where he says, "You stole them," or, "So and so, who is not established as a thief, stole them." Even where the current possessor has a migo, we do not ordinarily establish people as thieves. But if the owner says merely, "They were stolen from me," he is believed through the migo that he could have said, "I lent them," because here there is no issue of branding a particular person a thief, since there are many thieves in the world. Nor is this a migo used to extract money, because when the item was sold the owner must reimburse the purchaser under the market protection enactment. Even with a gift, it is not considered a migo to extract where the item is one ordinarily lent or rented, because such an item remains under the original owner's presumptive ownership. Wherever the owner is believed to recover an item by claiming theft, he must reimburse the purchaser under market protection if the purchaser paid for it, but not a donee or heirs who paid nothing. Regarding items ordinarily lent or rented, where the householder does not ordinarily sell his utensils, and whether witnesses who saw the item in the possessor's hands are required, see siman 357 and the Shach there, and what I wrote there in the Beurim.
