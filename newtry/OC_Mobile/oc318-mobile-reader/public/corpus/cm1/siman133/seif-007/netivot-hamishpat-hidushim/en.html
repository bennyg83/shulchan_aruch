And the other claims that they were stolen. The Shach explains this as a case where the owner claims that he lent the item to a certain person, who then sold it to the current possessor. This is treated like theft, and therefore the owner is not believed, because we do not presume a person to be a thief who sells what is not his. Rama then cites dissenters who hold that this is not treated as theft, since the item originally came lawfully into the seller's possession. One cannot explain the case as a claim of actual theft, because regarding actual theft Rama would not disagree, as is clear from seif 6. Practically, the Shach and Taz rule like Rama. Even where the owner claims actual theft, so long as he does not identify that specific person as the thief, he is believed, as explained above. See Beurim for the reconciliation of Rama's language.
