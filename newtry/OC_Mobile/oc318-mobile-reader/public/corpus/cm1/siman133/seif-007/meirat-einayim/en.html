And the other claims that they were stolen, etc. This follows the principle stated above that one who claims goods were stolen, when no report of theft arose in the city, is not believed. The Maggid Mishneh and Beit Yosef explain the Rambam this way. It is therefore surprising that Rama concludes that some disagree and then says, "since that person is not believed," because all agree that a claim of theft is believed in the relevant circumstances. The Tur disagrees with the Rambam only according to the Beit Yosef's interpretation of the Rambam, where the possessor does not claim, "They were stolen from me." The Mechaber's own conclusion says that the claimant asserts he had lent the item to that third person. See the Perishah and Derishah. This requires study.
