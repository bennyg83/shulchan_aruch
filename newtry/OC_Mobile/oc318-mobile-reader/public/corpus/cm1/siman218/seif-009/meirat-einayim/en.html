Less than nine kav. Nine kav is the minimum measure of a field, as the Tur writes in this seif. It appears that although this seif too is taken from Rambam, the present clause concerns a rule with which Rashbam and the Tur also agree. The phrase "less than nine kav" here includes the quarter-kav allowances. For example, if he sold forty-four se'ah, the permitted excess would be thirty-five quarter-kav units. Proportionately this is more than one quarter kav per beit se'ah, yet it still does not reach the minimum independent field size until the excess reaches thirty-six quarter-kav units, which equals nine kav. The Tur writes this; see there. The phrase "to strengthen the seller's position" refers to the rule that the purchaser must pay money for the excess, not to a change in market price. In the following seif Rambam's distinctive view begins: even if the excess together with the permitted quarter-kav allowances reaches nine kav, the purchaser must still repay in money if the market price changed, as explained there.
To strengthen the seller's position. Meaning, the rule requiring money is for the seller's benefit. If the seller instead wishes to take back the excess land itself, the purchaser must return it. The Tur writes in the name of Rashbam that the distinction between less than nine kav and nine kav applies only to an excess beyond the agreed measure. If the seller delivered less than the agreed amount by more than one quarter kav per beit se'ah, he always deducts from the purchase price. Since this field contains no additional land, we cannot obligate the seller to give the purchaser land from elsewhere. It appears that the same applies if the seller wishes to supply the deficiency from another field: the purchaser can say, "What use do I have for a separate tiny field like this?" The seller must therefore deduct from the price. These are the Tur's words.
