Seif 24. In the gloss: But if he said, "My property in so-and-so's house," etc. Note: Concerning one who says, "All my property in so-and-so's house," see the Sema and Shach. It appears to me that the law parallels the case of contradictory formulations such as "a kor for thirty, each seah for a sela," where the first and last phrases point in different directions. There is a doubt whether to follow the first expression or the last, and the law therefore follows the party in possession. This resolves the apparent contradictory inferences in the wording of Rabbeinu. If he says only "my property in so-and-so's house," movable property is not included even if the recipient presently possesses it. If he says "all my property" without adding "in so-and-so's house," movable property is included even if the donor remains in possession. But if he says "all my property in so-and-so's house," there is a legal doubt whether the first or last phrase governs, and we follow the possessor. This appears correct.
