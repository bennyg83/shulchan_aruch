If one says, "I am selling you Reuven's field," and when the purchaser seeks to use it the seller says, "This is not the field that formerly belonged to Reuven, but merely a field called by his name. The field that actually belonged to Reuven is this other one, which I bought from him and intended to sell you," the seller bears the burden of proof. If he provides no proof, the purchaser acquires the field universally known as Reuven's field.
