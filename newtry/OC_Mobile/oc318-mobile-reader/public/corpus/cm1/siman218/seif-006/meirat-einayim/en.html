Some say specifically when they do not contain four kav. Rama writes this as an alternative view even though the Mechaber did not explicitly say that when one says “approximately a beit kor,” even four kav or more are measured with it. The Mechaber follows the Rambam in chapter 28. The Tur understands from the Rambam's placement of this rule after all the earlier laws that the preceding limitations apply only when the seller says “I sell you a beit kor,” whereas if he says “approximately a beit kor,” all irregularities are measured with it. Rama therefore teaches that some disagree and hold that the distinction between “a beit kor” and “approximately a beit kor” concerns only height and depth, not an excess beyond four kav. This alternative view is what the Tur cites in Rashbam's name. The Rosh agrees with that first position. Yet the Tur there says that even when one says “approximately a beit kor,” it must not contain more than four kav, while exactly four kav can be measured even where he says simply “a beit kor of earth.” It is therefore difficult that Rama writes “specifically when they do not contain four kav.” It appears that the text should read, “specifically when they do not contain more than four kav,” thereby excluding the Rambam's broader view.
