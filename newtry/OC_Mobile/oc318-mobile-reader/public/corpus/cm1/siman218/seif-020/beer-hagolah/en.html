Gimel) There in the Mishnah.
Dalet) The Maggid Mishneh explains the Gemara according to Rambam: Rabbi Yochanan says that the latter clause is decisive. If the seller said "half of it on the southern side," the buyer takes that southern half whether superior or inferior, but the valuation is adjusted. If the southern half is inferior, the buyer receives a larger measure so that its value equals half the field; if superior, the measure is reduced. Likewise, in the first clause the adjustment is by value, while the buyer always takes the inferior portion. The Raavad and Rashba agree to this explanation, and it is primary. Other approaches exist. The reason is that the seller has the upper hand and prefers retaining a small amount of superior land rather than a large amount of inferior land.
Heh) The Mishnah and baraita there.
But where an entire field is sold, since most sellers have no additional land alongside the field from which to give the buyer anything, the Sages did not distinguish even in the case of one who actually owns adjacent land. So writes Rabbenu Shmuel, as cited by the Sema.
Vav) The Gemara explains on 108a that the mongoose is a small animal that eats and damages grain. The reason one must make a fence and two trenches, one wider than the other, is explained there in the Gemara.
