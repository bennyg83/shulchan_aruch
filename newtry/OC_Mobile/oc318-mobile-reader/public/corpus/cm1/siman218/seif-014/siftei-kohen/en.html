The Maggid Mishneh, Beit Yosef, and Ir Shushan correctly explain that the Rambam and Mechaber also agree with Rashbam, and the Sema's objections are unjustified. The Bach likewise explains that the Rambam means that the purchaser receives according to the less favorable wording, whether the land is deficient or excessive, and he rejects the Tur and Sema. I also found an express contradiction to the Sema in the Rambam's commentary to the Mishna in the chapter Beit Kor: the purchaser receives the smaller of the stated measurements, and this is the meaning of following the less favorable expression. Thus the less favorable wording always disadvantages the purchaser and means the lesser measurement, not, as the Sema explains, the wording that diminishes the seller's first statement. The Sema's explanation is also difficult because the source Mishna never mentions the phrase 'a beit kor.' Clearly the Rambam and Mechaber use that phrase here only as an illustration of the expression 'measured by a rope,' and their intent accords with the Raavad, whose words follow. It appears that Ri Migash, Rashi, and Rashbam likewise agree with the Raavad, whose ruling is correct: the less favorable wording is applied as a leniency for the defendant. If the buyer has already paid, he is the claimant. If he has not paid, possesses the land, and the seller claims additional money, the seller is the claimant. Depending upon the case, a deficiency or an excess may be either favorable or unfavorable to the defendant. The Maggid Mishneh writes that even where the seller comes to collect money, the purchaser is compelled to pay for an excess because land remains in its owner's presumptive possession, just as is said regarding contradictory language in a rental, as explained in chapter 7 of Hilchot Sechirut. He plainly does not mean that the purchaser is forced to pay if he does not want the excess land, for presumptive ownership has no relevance there. Likewise, in the rental case one pays for the thirteenth month only after having occupied it, as explained below in siman 312:15. Rather, he means that if the purchaser wishes to retain the entire parcel but refuses to pay for the excess, he is compelled to pay, and until payment the entire land remains the seller's because land stands in its owner's presumptive possession. The Maggid Mishneh says this because the Raavad's plain language, 'the seller claims the additional money,' might suggest otherwise. In my opinion, the Raavad agrees and speaks only where the purchaser wishes the land to remain his and the seller merely claims payment for the excess; in that situation the law may favor the purchaser according to the less favorable wording. It is also possible that the Maggid Mishneh understood the Raavad this way and came only to clarify his intent. Examine carefully.
