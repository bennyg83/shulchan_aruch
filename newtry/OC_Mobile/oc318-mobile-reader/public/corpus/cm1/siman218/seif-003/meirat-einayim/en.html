Or a minority of them within a majority. Maimoniyot is also printed with "or," but the Maggid Mishneh writes that the correct text is "and a minority of them within a majority." This is also Rashbam's and the Tur's version of the Gemara. The meaning is as follows. The four kav of rocky ground are distributed in a long narrow strip throughout sixteen se'ah of the field, with one kav of ordinary soil separating them, as explained above. If the strip were perfectly even, each beit se'ah would contain one quarter kav of rocks. The question concerns a strip that is not fully even: in one place it is somewhat wider than the proportional measure, and in another somewhat narrower, so that a majority of the rocks, namely nine quarter-kav units out of the sixteen, extend through a minority of the majority portion of the field, meaning seven beit se'ah, while the minority of the rocks, seven quarter-kav units, extend through the majority of the sixteen beit se'ah, namely nine beit se'ah. This is an unresolved question in the Gemara and therefore the burden of proof rests on the claimant. The Gemara does not ask what the law is if half the rocks are in one half of the field and the other half in the other half, because when the visible disparity does not reach a true majority, it is obvious that there is no significant objection. See the Perishah.
Like a straight line. In that configuration the plow cannot enter between the rocks to plow from one side to the other. Rashbam. From this we learn that what was stated above, that the rocks are "swallowed within the majority of the field," meaning spread in a strip across sixteen beit se'ah with one kav of level ground between them, does not mean that the kav of level ground is divided into, for example, one hundred tiny pieces so that the plow cannot pass through each open portion. If so, this would be the very question the Gemara raises here. Rather, the kav of level ground is scattered among the rocks in the strip in such a way that the plow can enter between each section. This is clear. See the Tur, whose printed versions read: "Likewise the question was raised if they stand in a circle or in many rows alongside one another." It appears that "in many rows" is a scribal error and should read "in a row," meaning a single line with many rocks beside one another so that the plow cannot pass between them. See the Perishah.
Or like a circle or a triangle. In all these configurations the plow cannot easily enter among them. Rashbam. Know that the Gemara arranges the questions in this order: like a ring, meaning a circle, what is the law; like a row, what is the law, etc. Rashbam explains that these questions are presented as sequential "if you say" inquiries: if a circle can be cultivated, what is the law with a row, and so forth, with each later form less cultivable than the previous one. The wording of Rambam and the Mechaber, however, places a row before a circle, and therefore cannot be explained in that sequence.
Or they were at the sides. Maggid Mishneh explains: imagine the rocks clustered in the center, with projections extending from the rocky mass in various directions and the field entering between those projections. The question is whether they are considered dispersed because the field penetrates between the projections, or clustered because the projections remain attached to the main rocky body without any full separation.
Or they followed a winding path. Maggid Mishneh explains that the rocks are attached to one another along a winding line, with parts of the field entering into the bends. Rashbam had different versions and a different explanation. See there.
And the burden of proof rests on the claimant. The Tur writes: therefore, if the purchaser already paid the money, he must accept them; if he has not yet paid, he need not accept them.
