What he writes, "When does this apply," etc. Meaning, when does the preceding rule apply that the purchaser has the stronger position and gives the seller according to the value at the time he bought it?
When the land had been inexpensive, etc. The same law could have been stated where the market price remained unchanged. Rambam merely uses the wording of the baraita and Gemara, which discuss a change in price. For the same reason the preceding seif concludes that the purchaser gives according to the value at the time he bought it, because the Gemara there likewise discusses a changed market price. What Rambam writes, "if there was an excess, etc., he returns only money," means that the seller has the stronger position, as explained in seif 9 concerning a field where the excess is less than nine kav: he returns money in order to strengthen the seller's position. What he writes, "if the excess was half a kav, etc., in money," means that if the land became cheaper, he must give money according to its value at the time of acquisition, or return cheap land according to the value at the time of return, as explained above concerning a field. Rama's gloss, "everything written from the beginning of seif 9, etc.," is based on his understanding that seif 9's phrase "if the excess was less than nine kav" includes even a case where there are nine kav with the quarter-kav allowances but not nine kav apart from those allowances. Therefore Rama says that this is only Rambam's view. In my humble opinion, however, the correct explanation is as I wrote above: seif 8 states a rule accepted even by Rashbam and the Tur, first giving the straightforward case where the excess is not a field-sized amount, so money must be paid to strengthen the seller; even so, if the seller owns an adjoining field he can take land. Rambam then adds a greater novelty: even where the excess reaches nine kav together with the quarter-kav allowances, if the seller has no adjoining field the purchaser must pay money. This is clear. Rama further writes that from seif 9 until "here" is Rambam's wording. This requires study. Why does he write "until here," meaning through seif 12, when seif 12 too follows specifically Rambam's approach and not that of Rashbam and the Tur, as I explained above? One must say that a typographical error occurred and the gloss should be placed after seif 12. There the phrase "until here" is appropriate, meaning until seif 13. Examine carefully.
