He said to him, "with its identifying marks and boundaries," etc. The Tur's wording is: "I am selling you a beit kor of land as it stands within its marks and boundaries," etc. In the Perishah I wrote that the Tur carefully adds "as it stands" because the case requires that the seller stand within the field, or say "this beit kor," and also say, "Take it within its boundaries and signs." Only then is there extra language. If he stands within it or says "this," the law would otherwise be that an ordinary deficiency or excess is accepted, as explained in seif 8. By adding, "take it within its marks and boundaries," he adds a special qualification, and therefore we say that a disparity up to one sixth is accepted. The Tur alludes to this by writing "as it stands," meaning he says "this" or physically stands within it. See the Perishah. This is unlike the Levush, who copies Rashbam's wording in a way that leaves room for error.
If the deficiency exceeds one sixth, he deducts. Meaning, he deducts the entire deficiency, including the first one sixth, just as where a deficiency exceeds one quarter kav per beit se'ah under the language "more or less," the entire amount is deducted. The Tur likewise writes below that where the excess is greater than one sixth, the purchaser pays money even for the first one sixth. The Tur mentions that there because he proceeds to discuss a case where the excess is less than nine kav and the purchaser must give money or land. With a deficiency, however, the seller does not supply land from elsewhere; he deducts money for the entire deficiency, as explained above.
