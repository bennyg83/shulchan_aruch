If the excess was greater, etc. He explains the Gemara's statement that if there is an additional significant amount of nine kav the land is returned, following Rashbam. Others explain that if the excess itself reaches the stated measure, the purchaser must return land. The subsequent discussion, 'there, where it had been expensive, etc.,' means that if the excess is nine kav and the purchaser must return land, the court addresses the purchaser. Everything is arranged to strengthen the seller's position. Since the land was expensive at the time of sale and became cheaper while in the purchaser's possession, if the purchaser returns money he pays according to the original high price; if he returns land, he returns according to its present lower value, because the seller is not compelled to accept the land on the former terms. If the purchaser wishes to pay money, the seller is compelled to sell the excess, because the purchaser cannot be forced to return land at its present low value. The baraita's wording is then explained consistently, all for the seller's benefit. This also resolves the phrases 'if you give him land,' 'we compel the seller to sell,' and the Mishna's general formulation.
