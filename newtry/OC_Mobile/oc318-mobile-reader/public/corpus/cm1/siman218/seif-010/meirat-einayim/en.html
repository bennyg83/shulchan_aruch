If the excess was more than nine kav, etc. From here through seif 13 the Mechaber rules according to the Rambam, unlike the Tur's explanation following Rashi. I discussed this at length in the Derishah and showed that the flow of the Gemara supports the Rambam, while Rashbam and the Tur must force several passages. I also answered the Maggid Mishneh and Rama's objections. Here I will state only the Rambam's general method. He agrees with the Tur that where the seller says “more or less,” a quarter kav per seah is waived, but no more. If the excess exceeds that amount, even the quarter kav amounts themselves must be returned. He also agrees that in this general area the seller has the stronger hand. The dispute concerns a case where the excess beyond a quarter kav per seah, together with the waived quarters themselves, reaches nine kav. Rashbam and the Tur hold that once the total reaches nine kav, the seller no longer has the stronger hand. The purchaser may return the actual land even against the seller's will. The Rambam holds that the seller still has the stronger hand unless, after allocating a quarter kav to each seah as the ordinary waived amount, there remain a full additional nine kav beyond all those quarters. Only then must the seller take back the land itself even against his will. Even where the excess is that large, however, the purchaser still does not always have the stronger hand as to valuation. If land was expensive at the time of sale and is cheap when the excess is returned, the purchaser cannot simply return the excess land at its present low value. He must pay its value as of the original sale, or return at the present cheap rate enough land to equal that original value. The seller can say that the fall in land values occurred in the purchaser's domain and fortune. Conversely, if land was cheap at the sale and has become expensive, the seller benefits from the increase, because the excess was never sold to the purchaser. Thus, according to the Rambam, the seller's hand is generally stronger. Only when values remain as at the original transaction, and only where the additional excess beyond all the quarter kav portions is itself nine kav, is the seller compelled to take back the actual land. I have not found a fully satisfying rationale for the Rambam, and the Maggid Mishneh and Rama also question why, if nine kav remain and constitute a field in their own right, the seller should be able to force the purchaser to pay money instead of returning the land. One can answer with difficulty as follows. Since a quarter kav per seah is ordinarily waived, even when some excess is added and the combined total reaches nine kav, the seller can say: Had there been no added excess, you would have kept those quarter kav portions without payment as part of your purchase. Now that there is excess, you cannot reverse matters and force me to take back those same waived portions as land. I may instead treat them as part of the transaction and demand their value like the rest of the field. Only when there are nine kav beyond all the ordinarily waived quarters is there a distinct field-like excess that must be returned in kind. This is the general approach of the Rambam, and on this basis the details of the Mechaber's language, which closely follows him, can be understood.
