And specifically where he said “fields,” etc. This gloss belongs here. A printing error placed it in seif 10. It comments on the Mechaber's rule that if one says, “All my fields,” even three or four are included, but gardens and orchards are excluded. Rama then adds that this is specifically when he said “fields.” This follows Rabbeinu Yonah, cited by the Tur, who writes that if he uses the expression “all my landholdings,” everything is included, even orchards and houses. From there we learn that when Rama says, “but if he said land,” he means “all my land,” in the wording of Rabbeinu Yonah. Still, it is possible that even if he says merely “lands,” without “all,” and he owns only one field and one orchard, the orchard is included, because he used the plural “lands” and these are his only two parcels, while an orchard is legally land. Perhaps for this reason Rama omitted the word “all.”
