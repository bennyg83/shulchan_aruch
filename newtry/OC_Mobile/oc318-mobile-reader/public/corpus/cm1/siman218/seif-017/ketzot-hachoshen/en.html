A particular beit kor. In the next seif the Shulchan Aruch speaks of "a particular place" or "this orchard." The implication is that if one sold merely "an orchard" or "a beit kor" without identifying a particular one, the seller cannot later say, "I sold you only the name," and must provide a complete orchard or a full beit kor. Beit Yosef explains that Rambam and Tur intentionally wrote "a particular beit kor" or "an orchard I have in such and such a place," because without such identification there is no basis to say that only the name was sold.
Bach was troubled by this, arguing that even without saying "this" or identifying the location, perhaps the seller should still be able to say, "I sold you only the name." In my view Rambam holds that the doctrine of selling merely the name applies specifically where the seller said "this," or identified a particular place in a manner equivalent to "this." Ritva in Perek HaMekabel writes that the case is one where the seller said, "this beit kor of earth." Since he identified this parcel, if the parcel is commonly called by that name, the purchaser must accept it even if it does not contain the full measure. Ritva notes that this is the reading of Raavad. Rambam follows the same text. If the purchaser says to the seller, "Sell me a beit kor," whether the seller can still answer, "I sold you merely something bearing that name," depends on the dispute in siman 321. Rif, Rambam, and Rosh do not distinguish whether the lessor said the formula to the lessee or the lessee to the lessor, and under their view the same doctrine can apply here. Rabbi Meir HaLevi Abulafia, Ramban, and Rashba distinguish where the lessee initiated the formulation, and according to them a purchaser who requested a full beit kor would not be subject to the mere name defense.
