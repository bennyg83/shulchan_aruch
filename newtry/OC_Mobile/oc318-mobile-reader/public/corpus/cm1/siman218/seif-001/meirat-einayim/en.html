And none of them contains a quarter kav. It appears that Rama added this limitation because the Mechaber concludes that the purchaser takes those rocky or sunken areas without additional payment, following the Rambam. The Tur cites the Ramah as agreeing, provided no single one contains a quarter kav. If one contains a quarter kav, it is not sold as part of the rest of the beit kor without payment. According to the Rosh and Rashbam, however, who hold that such areas remain the seller's and the purchaser must accept the field with them interspersed within it, even a quarter kav can be present. Proof appears from the Tur's later citation of Ramban, based on the Yerushalmi, that when pits are less than ten handbreadths deep, or rocks less than ten high, they are measured with the field provided no one of them is wider than ten handbreadths or four cubits, according to the respective opinions. He does not place a similar width limitation in the first clause, where they are ten handbreadths deep or high and therefore not measured, yet the purchaser must accept a field interrupted by them. This implies that in the first case even a depression or rock wider than four cubits and even larger than a quarter kav does not void the sale. The Mechaber and Rama omit the Yerushalmi limitation concerning width in the following law, and I do not know why. Perhaps they hold, as Darkei Moshe suggests, that Rashbam disagrees with Ramban. In the Derishah I wrote in the Beit Yosef's name similarly, although in my glosses to Darkei Moshe I questioned where the disagreement lies. Rashbam's statement that a rock ten high is not measured when it is four cubits wide does not necessarily conflict with Ramban's statement that a lower rock is measured up to that width. At first glance Rama may have added the limitation of less than a quarter kav because the Rambam later says that a single rock of a quarter kav has a separate identity and is not sold with the field. Even that is not conclusive, for perhaps there the Rambam means only that it is not counted toward the measured area, while here, where a full beit kor of level ground is separately measured, even a quarter kav obstacle does not void the sale.
