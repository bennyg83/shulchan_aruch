The same law applies, etc. Tosafos there, s.v. “a beis kor, etc.” Ramban notes that the latter clause says “if he said to him, ‘approximately a beis kor, etc.,’” without the word “soil.” Our text does contain “soil,” but the Yerushalmi’s version does not.
And its width is four. Then it is an independent domain, like the law of Shabbos. This explains why the Mishnah distinguishes a depth of ten from less than ten. The Yerushalmi asks about a depression ten deep but less than four wide and resolves that it is measured with the field.
And they do not contain, etc. Meaning that if such an area contains a quarter-kav, it is not sold and remains with the seller even according to Rambam and Shulchan Aruch. Ramah explains that this is a significant area, as stated above in seif 5.
And the buyer acquires these, etc. Like the rule of consecration there, where they say these areas are not measured with the field. The Rambam’s Hilchos Arachin appears to hold that they need not be redeemed at all. Rabbeinu Meir likewise holds this. This is why the Gemara asks, “Why should they not become independently consecrated, etc.?”
Some say, etc. Rashbam explains that in consecration they are not measured with the field but must be redeemed separately at their own value because they are unsuitable for sowing. The questioner initially thought them suitable for sowing and therefore asked that they be redeemed according to the seed measure as well. The wording “let them be consecrated independently” is nevertheless more natural according to the Rambam.
Nevertheless, etc. The Rosh infers from the phrase “they are not measured with it” that the rest of the field is still measured and included.
Provided he can, etc. Otherwise the concern “a person does not wish to give his money, etc.” applies here too. There.
