But wrapping alone. The Rama appears to hold that number alone is an identifying mark even when both claimants are not before us and the documents concern one debtor and one creditor. He states this expressly in Darkei Moshe. This is not correct, because each of those two necessarily knows the number just as the other does, and the three documents are regarded as one unit. The Ramah's words prove otherwise. The Rosh, who holds that number alone can be effective, also agrees here, for he does not establish the Gemara as involving one debtor and one creditor. The Tur's citation of the Ramah means that, unlike the preceding view, the Ramah requires the mark of wrapping in addition to number.
Wrapping alone, and this applies. The Rama follows his interpretation in Darkei Moshe, that the Ramah requires number together with wrapping or tying only in the sense that either wrapping or tying may accompany number. This interpretation is difficult. The Tur's phrase, 'the Ramah wrote,' indicates disagreement with the preceding rule that number alone suffices. The Ramah also says, 'although we rule that tying or number alone is an identifying mark,' where according to the Rama he should not have mentioned number. His explanation that the other party did not think to alter the wrapping but would think to separate the documents is likewise implausible. The Ramah's language means that the person who wrapped them recognizes the wrapping, while the other party did not pay attention to it. There is no reason to suppose he would separate documents entrusted to him, and indeed he would prefer to leave them together so that he too could later identify them. The correct explanation is as stated below.

The second does not know. If one gives the mark of wrapping and the other gives the number, Darkei Moshe rules that the documents go to the one who states the number, while Beit Yosef is uncertain and the Sema treats the marks as equal. According to the explanation below, the rationale that the other party failed to notice applies specifically to wrapping, not to number. Since the second party does not know the number, it is evident that the documents are not his. They are therefore given to the one who states the number. Thus Darkei Moshe is correct in result, though not in reasoning, and the Sema is incorrect in result.
They are given to the one who knows. The Ir Shushan says that wrapping alone is never an identifying mark, even with three documents, while people are careful about number; therefore only the one who gives the number receives them. This is not correct, as the Ramah's words plainly show. Many later authorities offered differing interpretations and departed from the Ramah's plain meaning. In my view his words are clear. The Mishnah teaches that one who finds a packet or bundle of documents returns it, and the Gemara asks whether tying is an identifying mark, answers that the documents are wrapped, and then asks what the finder announces. The central novelty of the Mishnah is that the wrapping or tying is itself an identifying mark, as Rashi and other commentators explain. Yet the Gemara also requires three documents, for with only two the wrapping is not reliable in this case. The reason is that the two documents concern one debtor and one creditor. They may have been repaid together and returned wrapped, or loaned together and delivered wrapped. The party who wrapped them recognizes the wrapping, while the other party may not have attended to it. Therefore, with two documents, even if one knows the wrapping and the other does not, they are not returned. With three, however, the combination acquires evidentiary force. When both claimants are present and one knows either the number, wrapping, or tying while the other knows none of them, they are given to the one who knows. If one knows the number and the other only the wrapping or tying, they are given to the one who knows the number. If one knows both marks and the other knows only the number, they are equal, because failure to notice the wrapping is not dispositive. When both are absent and only one claimant later appears, he must state both number and wrapping or tying; neither mark alone suffices, since with one debtor and one creditor number alone is obvious, while wrapping alone is subject to the concern just explained. This resolves the Beit Yosef's questions and preserves the Ramah's precise language. Accordingly: with two documents of one debtor and creditor, they remain deposited in every case until Eliyahu comes, even if one claimant states number and wrapping and the other states nothing. With three documents and both claimants present, one adequate mark against complete ignorance suffices; number prevails over wrapping; two marks against number alone are equal; and where both are not present, both number and wrapping or tying are required. Even then, such marks do not permit collection from encumbered property where collusion remains possible.
