(In the Tur, seif 8: The Ramah wrote, etc.) The Beit Yosef left these words unresolved. The dispute concerns the Mishnah’s rule that one who finds a bundle of documents must announce it. According to the Rosh and Tur, the documents must be bound so that the owner can identify their number; if loose, they may not have fallen together and the owner despairs. The finder therefore announces only that he found documents, and the claimant states their number. The Ramah, however, treats the binding itself as an identifying mark. Where the documents involve one borrower and one lender, both parties may know both their number and how they were bound. Therefore the claimant ordinarily must state both marks, especially when the competing party is absent, because absence may reflect ignorance of one detail rather than abandonment. If both parties are present and only one provides a valid sign, that sign may suffice because the other had an immediate opportunity to respond and failed. The form of the public announcement must not disclose either identifying detail, lest a fraudulent party be alerted and use information already known to him from prior possession. The core distinction is therefore between what the finder announces and what the claimant must supply, and between a case where the rival claimant is absent and one where he is present. This resolves the Beit Yosef’s objections and is close to the Bach’s explanation, though the Bach’s rule concerning two borrowers and two lenders is not compelled. When the opposing party stands and protests, a sign is not decisive if we know that one document could not belong to the person giving it.
