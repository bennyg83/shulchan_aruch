The smaller amount is waived. In the glosses of Rabbi Akiva Eiger to Shulchan Aruch it is written: This applies because the uncertainty concerns how much was waived. If the amount waived is known, but there is a legal doubt whether the waiver is effective, then at first glance the obligor should be exempt because of the doubt. However, from Beit Shmuel, siman 66, seif katan 9, it appears that he does not agree. In my humble opinion, this requires study. See Nimukei Yosef, first chapter of Bava Batra, concerning one who established a right to place beams, where it appears that even if the doubt concerns the waiver itself, namely whether a waiver occurred, and the issue is a legal doubt, the party in possession prevails. Likewise, Shulchan Aruch Even HaEzer, siman 106, rules that even where the woman is healthy, in an unresolved Talmudic question whether she waived her kesubah, the heirs prevail. The Rif there, however, actually holds that because of doubt she does not lose her kesubah. End of quote.
