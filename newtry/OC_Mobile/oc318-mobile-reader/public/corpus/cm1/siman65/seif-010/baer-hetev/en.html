If one claimant gives the identifying mark of the number of documents and the other gives the identifying mark of the manner in which they were bound, the Beit Yosef writes that it requires study to whom they should be given. The Darkei Moshe, however, writes that they are given to the one who gives the number, because that mark is superior. According to what I have written, the two marks are equal and the documents are given to neither. Thus the Sema. The Shach agrees with the Darkei Moshe and discusses the matter at length, explaining the sugya of the Gemara differently from many later authorities. He concludes: The rule is as follows. With two documents belonging to one borrower and one lender, even if both parties are before us, they are not returned in any case. Even if one gives the number and the manner of binding, while the other gives some other identifying mark, the documents remain deposited until Eliyahu comes. With three documents, if both parties are before us and one gives the number, or the binding or knot, and the other gives no identifying mark, they are given to the one who knows the mark. If one gives the number and the other gives the binding or knot, they are given to the one who gives the number. If one gives both identifying marks and the other gives the number, they are equal and the documents remain deposited. If both are not before us, and the second claimant gives both marks, namely the number together with the binding or knot, the documents are returned to him; otherwise they remain deposited. I explained above that for collection from encumbered property, an identifying mark does not suffice where there is concern that the borrower may have told the lender the mark or pretends not to know it, because we are concerned for collusion. Thus the Shach.
