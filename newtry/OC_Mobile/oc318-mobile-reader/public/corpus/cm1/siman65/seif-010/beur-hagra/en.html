(Likkut) But, and this applies specifically. The ruling was written according to the Tur's interpretation of Ramah, omitting the statement that if one claimant identifies the number and the other does not, the documents are given to the first. The Tur follows his own view that a single identifying feature suffices. Ramah, however, follows Rashi and requires both the manner of binding and the number. As the Shach explains, the primary identifying mark is the binding, for the Mishnah speaks of a bundle and Rashi explains that it is returned on that basis. The Gemara therefore does not announce merely that a bundle was found. The Gemara's discussion does not prove that a knot alone is ineffective. On the contrary, the conclusion that the documents were rolled together indicates that rolling is a stronger identifier than knotting. Since knotting is accepted as an identifying mark, rolling certainly is. The number is not the primary mark. Rather, where the same borrower and lender are involved, both marks are required because perhaps the debt was once paid and the documents were handed over together, or the reverse. We do not entertain such a combination of possibilities when both claimants are present and one knows one identifying feature while the other does not. In that case, one feature suffices, since both a knot and a number are recognized identifiers, and rolling is stronger still. The Tur's words, and the other did not take notice, are clearly a scribal error and a mistaken student's gloss. (End of supplement.) [Citation references: 12, 21, 32, 326, 636.]
