He need not state how they were bundled. The Mechaber begins by saying they were bundled one upon another because otherwise, even if they fell from the lender, he presumably already despaired, reasoning that they did not all fall in one place and therefore no identifying sign can be given based on the number. See Perishah. Nimukei Yosef writes that this applies specifically to a tight bundle that can be expected not to separate. If they could easily separate, it is like scattered coins, which have no identifying sign and belong to the finder. So cites Darkei Moshe, seif 11.
But the fact of bundling alone, etc. See the Tur, who writes this in the name of Ramah. In Perishah I wrote that Ramah holds that the number alone is likewise not an identifying sign when all the documents are between the same lender and borrower. I explained the reason there, and Beit Yosef likewise appears to understand Ramah this way. Rama here follows his own understanding in Darkei Moshe that Ramah did not intend to disagree with the Tur, but only to say that bundling alone is insufficient.
And the second one does not know, etc. If one person gives the identifying sign of the number and the other gives the identifying sign of the bundling, Beit Yosef leaves unresolved to whom they are given. Rama, however, writes in Darkei Moshe that they are given to the one who knows the number, because that is the stronger sign. According to what I wrote, the signs are equal and the documents are given to neither. See Derishah and Hagahot Darkei Moshe.
