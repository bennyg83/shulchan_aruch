That one collects with it. Baal HaTerumot, sha'ar 52, asks from Bava Metzia 19 regarding a get, where purchasers may say to the woman, "Bring proof of when the get reached your hand." This shows that even after the document has already been returned to her, she must bring proof. It appears that Rambam means only where the document was returned before its defect was seen by another beit din. Once its defect has been observed by beit din, return to the holder no longer restores its validity. The Gemara there is therefore properly understood, because it speaks of a return made by beit din, as is explicit from its language, "since the Sages returned it." Once the document stood before beit din in its defective condition, even Rambam agrees that regaining physical possession does not render it valid again, particularly because beit din returned it only for collection from unencumbered property and not from encumbered property. See Rosh to Ketubot 22, who cites Ramah that any seizure effected through beit din is not considered an independent seizure, because the same beit din that placed the property in his hands can remove it from him.
Or to his heirs. See Shach, seif katan 48, through his statement that the proof Baal HaTerumot brings for this rule from the case where the son died is a separate matter and does not relate to what was written earlier in explaining the Tur. His intent is that Baal HaTerumot needed the proof from the case where the son died specifically where payment was made to the heirs, because that is a seizure based on doubt. For that situation he needed a proof from the case where the son died, which is likewise a seizure based on doubt. Where payment was made to the debtor himself, however, no such proof was necessary. If the possessor makes a definite claim, his seizure is effective even if he seized without permission, as the Shach explains at length in many places and in his work Tekfo Kohen.
