Some say. There, "It fell from me and I found it." This shows that he may collect with it. See Tosafos to Bava Batra 76b, s.v. kani, where they explain why the case is needed.
And some say. Bava Metzia 19a, "When he comes to collect, we say..." even though the document is in his possession.
(Collected note) One who returned... The first opinion proves its view from 7a, "Two people..." and the lender says, "It fell from me..." But this proof is not decisive, because that case is specifically like the case in seif 2, "some say..." Here it is otherwise. Proof is from 12b, where the Gemara asks, "If so, all documents..." If possession by the lender itself sufficed, what is the question? Even with this very document, once it is in the lender's possession he could collect. Nor would the answer need to say, "all documents are not impaired..." because the issue would not be impairment at all, but merely possession. It should have said that all documents emerge from the lender's possession. Likewise at 19a, regarding a debt document, the language implies that if the debtor came and challenged it, his claim would have force. So writes Toras HaBayis at the end of siman 60. This is the second opinion. (End of note.)
And some say. They follow the opinion that in every case of uncertainty, seizure does not help, like the view at 10b that if a kohen seized, the item is removed from him. Although where beit din itself collected in error, as at 35a, that is different because of the law of appraisal, etc.
But if. Bechoros 49a: "If he gave... and if not..." The wording teaches that if he did not voluntarily give, then even seizure does not help. Thus the opening statement says that even if beit din collected it, etc., but... See Sefer HaTerumos.
