That one may collect with it. This follows directly from the rule above in seif 1. See what I wrote there.
Beit din returns, etc. Meaning that beit din collects the money from him against his will, not merely based on his admission. His prior possession is not treated as possession established before the doubt arose, which was effective above in siman 46, seif 33, because there the doubt concerned the validity of the document itself. Here the document itself is not doubtful. It was merely weakened because it had been lost, Shach.
But if he paid voluntarily, etc. Even though at first he stood and protested, once he ultimately paid we do not treat it as payment made under compulsion. Rather, we say that he has now admitted the debt and changed his mind. We therefore do not take the money back. Even if he paid the heirs and they themselves know nothing about whether the document had previously been paid, once they are in possession we do not remove it from them. This is true even though their possession arose amid doubt, as we learn from the law of redemption where, once payment was given, it is not returned even though the recipient's possession was uncertain. Shach explains this as the intent of Baal HaTerumos and Tur.
