Rather, he says: one document is forgiven to you, etc. Mahardakh compares this to the law of a sale in siman 218, but the comparison is questionable. There the seller remains in possession of his field, whereas here the recipient possesses the money, resembling repayment. For this reason, siman 42 rules that even a receipt phrased as a waiver is construed in favor of its holder against the debt document. This requires study. In any event, Mahardakh’s ruling applies only where it is certain, or the debtor admits, that the creditor gratuitously waived one document, because a purchaser or donee bears the inferior hand. If the creditor merely said, ‘it is forgiven to you,’ and the debtor certainly claims that the waiver was made because the creditor was obligated to release it, the matter resembles payment and the larger debt is deemed forgiven. The Rama abbreviated the case.
