And likewise. Here we do not say that the holder of the document has the inferior position, as we do regarding a sale in the Gemara there. Proof for this position is that waiver and emancipation share one rule, as Tosafot write in the first chapter of Sanhedrin and Sefer HaTerumot in gate 51. See there. The Tosefta in Bava Batra likewise teaches that one who says, Let Tavi my slave be made free, and one who says, Give, are governed differently from a promissory note, as explained above.
(Supplement) And likewise if. As stated in the Tosefta at the end of Bava Batra regarding emancipation, and the same applies to waiver, since emancipation is a form of waiver, as stated in the first chapter of Kiddushin. The Shach objects that with a receipt the language is construed in favor of the holder, as stated in siman 42, seif 8, and even regarding waiver, as stated in siman 43, seifim 25 and 27. This is not correct. Certainly the written language of a receipt is construed in favor of its holder, as stated there, 171b, where it was written without qualification, applying whenever the stated condition occurs. But when a person's right rests upon his oral statement, the language is construed narrowly, as stated in siman 71, seif 21. This ruling supports the Rif's decision there. See there.
