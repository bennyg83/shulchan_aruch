When the lender admits, etc. The wording deliberately says the lender admits. Then there is no concern that he is colluding to evade a debt he himself owes another creditor. This too follows the principle explained above in seif katan 19: since a valid receipt is before us supporting his admission, Chazal were concerned for collusion only where a creditor will certainly suffer a loss, as explained below in siman 86, seif 5. See what I write at the beginning of siman 47. But if the borrower admits that the debt remains unpaid, the document is not returned to the lender. Since it was impaired by having fallen, we suspect collusion intended to remove property from purchasers who bought from the borrower, as explained above in seif 6. See Derishah.
It is nothing, etc. We say that the lender prepared the receipt in advance to give the borrower when he would pay, but payment has not yet occurred, and the receipt fell from the lender. This applies obviously where another scribe wrote the receipt, because the lender may have thought: perhaps when the borrower comes to pay I will not find a scribe, and I will not have the promissory note with me to hand him; he may therefore withhold payment unless I already have a receipt. Even if the receipt is in the lender's or borrower's handwriting, so they could write it at the time of payment just as they wrote it now, we still say that the lender may have prepared it in advance in case the borrower comes to pay at twilight when there is no time to write a receipt. Even where it is in the borrower's handwriting, we say the borrower wrote it and gave it to the lender to sign, so it would be ready for immediate delivery when he paid and wished to be released from the debt.
Even if no witnesses are signed on the receipt. In Perishah I proved that this means even the lender's own signature is absent, and this too is included in 'no witnesses,' because his signature is like witnesses. Nevertheless, since there are two defects, namely that the promissory note was found among torn documents and there is also a receipt, we maintain the note as paid even if it contains a credibility clause. So write Baal HaTerumot, Beit Yosef, and Darkei Moshe, seif 25.
It is no worse, and they collect with it. Meaning, even the lender's heirs collect, which is why the plural language 'they collect' is used. The Tur explicitly writes this in seif 25. Although Beit Yosef says Baal HaTerumot records a dispute, he concludes that the Tur follows the first view. The reason is that whatever is found in the lender's house remains under his presumption, and we say the document happened to become mixed among the torn papers.
If witnesses are signed, etc. Meaning, the rule that a receipt found in the lender's possession is nothing because we say he prepared it in advance applies specifically where it bears no witness signatures. If witnesses signed it, they should be asked.
If it is authenticated, it is valid. It is not customary for a lender to authenticate a receipt. Rather, the borrower does so. We therefore say that the borrower had already paid, received the receipt from the lender, had it authenticated, and afterward redeposited it with the lender.
