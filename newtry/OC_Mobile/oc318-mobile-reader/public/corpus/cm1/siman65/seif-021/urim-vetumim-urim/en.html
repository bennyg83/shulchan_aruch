We act. As written in the receipt. Sema writes that this is repeated even though it was already stated in the preceding seif because where the lender is alive the law may depend on a circumstance in which he lacks a migo, whereas with heirs, even if they would have had a migo to conceal the writing, we nevertheless act according to what the receipt says. Shach writes that the novelty is that one might have thought the heirs have greater strength than the lender himself. Perhaps because the lender could have required the borrower to swear that he paid, we would make the same claim for his heirs and require the borrower to swear. The law therefore teaches that even an oath is unnecessary and we act according to the receipt.
