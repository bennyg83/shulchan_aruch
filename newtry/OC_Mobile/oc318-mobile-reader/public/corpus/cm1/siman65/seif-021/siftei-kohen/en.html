Reuven died, etc. We follow what is written in the receipt. Ir Shushan’s claim that the writing must have been found physically inserted within the document is unsupported and would omit the essential fact from the text. The Rosh’s responsum expressly concerns a receipt written on the document itself, and the Gemara makes no distinction between one placed inside the document and one found elsewhere. The Sema’s explanation that the novelty concerns a receipt written where it could have been cut away is also incorrect, especially because even the living creditor could not collect in that case. The Bach’s limitation to the creditor’s own handwriting is likewise unsound. A receipt written on the body of the document invalidates it even in another hand, because one does not ordinarily impair his own document unless payment occurred. The Mechaber cites the Rosh here to teach that the same applies to heirs. One might otherwise have argued that heirs are sometimes in a stronger position and could claim that their father would have explained that the receipt was written mistakenly, unlawfully, or merely as a notation. The ruling rejects that argument.
