It shall be authenticated by its signatures. Meaning, the signatures must be authenticated through witnesses. The Tur concludes regarding this: "Then the third party is believed to say that it has been paid." Meaning, after authentication, when the third party says that the debt has been paid, we believe him even if the debt instrument itself is not in his possession, by virtue of a migo that he could have returned the receipt to the borrower. So long as the signatures have not been authenticated, however, the receipt is as though it bears no witnesses at all. It has already been explained that in such a case the third party is not believed, because his position is no stronger than if the receipt were in the borrower's own possession. Therefore the Tur also concludes that if the third party dies, the receipt is not returned. The Mechaber abbreviated and did not explicitly write that even after the signatures are authenticated, we still require the third party to state that the debt was paid. He follows his own reasoning in the Beit Yosef, where he writes: "If the receipt has been authenticated by its signatures, why is the third party's credibility necessary? Perhaps this is what the text means: only when it is not authenticated does the credibility of the third party become relevant, etc." See the Perishah and Derishah, where I wrote that his words are difficult. One should also question the Rama, who did not gloss or add anything to the Mechaber's words here, even though he himself challenged the Beit Yosef in Darkei Moshe. See there.
