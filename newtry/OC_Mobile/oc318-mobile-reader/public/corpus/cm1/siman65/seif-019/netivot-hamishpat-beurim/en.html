The Shach's proof from the Tosefta depends on his interpretation of its phrase "among his father's documents" as meaning that the claimant found his own documents among his father's papers. The Shach found it difficult to explain the case as one where the son was supported at his father's table. Since such dependents do not establish ownership through possession against the head of the household, property found in their possession is treated as though it is in the father's domain, as explained in the gloss to seif 1.

It must be verified by its signatures. See Shach, seif katan 67, through his statement that this is contrary to what is written in Maadanei Melech. Ketzot HaChoshen challenges Maadanei Melech from the rule in Gittin that an agent must say, "It was written and signed in my presence," because rabbinic verification is required even though the agent functions as a depositary. This proves nothing. Maadanei Melech speaks only where the opposing party admits that the person is a depositary, or where that status is otherwise known. Certainly Maadanei Melech agrees that if a person appears with an unverified document and merely says, "I am the depositary," rabbinic verification is required. This is obvious.
