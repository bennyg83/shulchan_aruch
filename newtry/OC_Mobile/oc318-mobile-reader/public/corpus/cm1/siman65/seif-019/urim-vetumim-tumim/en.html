And specifically where the document is in his possession together with the receipt. See Urim, where I concluded like the Sema and Bach that even if the third party does not know whether the debt was paid, once both the promissory note and the receipt are in his possession the document is weakened. One should not ask, according to Rashi, who explains "a document was found and its status is unknown" as referring to a third party, why the Gemara did not explain the later clause, "if there is a receipt with them, follow the receipt," as continuing the same third party case. It would then be unnecessary to establish the receipt as found among torn documents. Baal HaTerumot, gate 52, part 3, law 10, resolves this from the wording. The Mishnah says "if there is with them," in the plural, rather than "if there is with it," in the singular, which would naturally refer back to the single document held by the third party. The Gemara therefore understood the clause as a separate case concerning the owner's group of documents, and properly asked how a receipt in the creditor's possession can have force.
