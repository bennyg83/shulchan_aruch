Even though. The Shach explains the Tur as speaking where the document is not presently validated, but can in principle be validated. Since the parties trusted the shalish, he is believed. This follows the Shach's approach in siman 56 and Nimukei Yosef. I have already written there that the Shulchan Aruch's rule is primary. The special credibility given to a shalish is no stronger than an express credibility clause given by a borrower to a creditor. Where the opposing party has a migo, he can sometimes contradict even such credibility. Therefore, if the document cannot presently be validated, the opposing party can deny the shalish through a migo of forgery. Nimukei Yosef can be understood as speaking specifically about a receipt. There the creditor's migo of forgery would be a migo to extract money, and Ramban does not regard the ordinary rule of migo with a document as a true migo to extract, because in the case of "another debt" the court is not adjudicating the document but the money already received. By contrast, where the shalish produces an unvalidated debt instrument and the borrower admits having written it but raises other claims, the borrower can contradict the shalish through a migo of forgery. My grandfather suggested that the Tur's reason is that document validation is only rabbinic, and where the matter is in a shalish's hands the Sages restored the Torah rule under which signed witnesses need no validation. But this is difficult from the first chapter of Gittin. A messenger who says, "It was written and signed before me," has the law of a trusted intermediary because the husband entrusted him, yet the Gemara still requires a special enactment to rely on his declaration where ordinary validation witnesses are unavailable. The Gemara explains that by Torah law signatures are treated as investigated testimony and validation is rabbinic, and only because of concern for abandoned wives did the Sages permit one messenger. If the status of shalish alone automatically restored the Torah rule for all documents, the Gemara's distinction would be unnecessary.
