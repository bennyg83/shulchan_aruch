(Collected note) Even if he died... This is compelled by Beit Yosef's question that otherwise there is no need for the receipt at all. Hagahos Mordechai writes that even if the third party is alive but does not know whether the debt was paid, the receipt is ineffective. This difficulty led Rashi to explain the Mishnah as listing separate cases, for otherwise the Gemara could not challenge from the Mishnah: if the receipt is in the third party's possession it should be valid. According to Rabbi Yehudah Barceloni, however, who explains the entire Mishnah as referring to a third party, even possession by the third party does not help merely because he is a third party. His special standing rests on the fact that the parties trusted him, and only where he knows the facts. The Gemara's question is therefore proper: when he does not know, perhaps the lender deposited the receipt with him. This also appears from the Tosefta, which speaks in the context of heirs and says "whether..." and gives the rule, etc., implying that only where it is found among torn documents is it effective, analogous to finding it in the marketplace, as in seif 8. Rambam, however, interprets "emerges from..." as meaning that the third party wrote that the document was paid, and therefore it remains effective even if he died, as stated in siman 56, seif 1, "and likewise if... this document..." (End of note.)

(Collected note) But if there are no... See Rashi in Bava Metzia there, who explains "there are witnesses" and "there are no witnesses" as referring to witnesses who can validate the document. He did not wish to interpret it as meaning no witnesses at all, because then it would be worthless, nor as where the document itself is also in his hands, because then no receipt would be needed. Yet his words are difficult: what does "it shall be validated through its signatories" mean, when the Gemara already established that the witnesses are questioned? The phrase "there are no witnesses upon it" likewise means there are no witnesses available to ask. Tosafos in Sanhedrin there, s.v. simpon and s.v. ein, explain similarly. Thus there are three approaches: Tosafos' two explanations, and Rambam's view that the third party wrote the receipt and died. Hagahos Mordechai in the name of Rabbeinu Tam explains that where the third party also holds the debt document, the receipt is valid even if he knows nothing. The Tur and Shulchan Aruch, however, require that he say it was paid, as the Gemara says, "for they trusted him." Likewise in the Mishnah, "he found a document among..." there are three approaches: Rashi, who treats them as separate clauses; Sefer HaTerumos, who refers it all to the lender; and Rabbi Yehudah Barceloni, who refers it all to a third party. This accords with the Tur and Shulchan Aruch, that he must actually say so. See Hagahos Ashri in Sanhedrin there. (End of note.)
