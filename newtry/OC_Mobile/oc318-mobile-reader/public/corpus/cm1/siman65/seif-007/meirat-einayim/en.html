Likewise, if the borrower has unencumbered property, etc. We do not say that perhaps he already paid and knows that he owes money to other creditors who will come and seize this unencumbered property, and therefore he asks that the document be returned to this creditor whose lien is earlier, a concern analogous to the case where Reuven sold a field to Shimon and Yehudah comes to challenge title. There, Reuven may not testify for Shimon even if he sold the field without warranty and even if he has other unencumbered property, as the Tur and Mechaber write in siman 37 seif 15. The cases are distinguishable. Here, since we have before us a valid document that supports his admission, the Sages were concerned only where there is a definite loss to purchasers. There, by contrast, he seeks to undermine the words of a challenger who presents testimony and proof against him. Likewise, where a deed of gift or sale is found in the marketplace, we say that it is not returned to the recipient or purchaser even though the seller or giver says to return it, as the Tur and Mechaber write below in this siman, seif 14. Those cases can also be distinguished as I wrote in the Derishah. See there.

If the obligor admits, it should be returned. So write the Rif and Rambam. The Maggid Mishneh questions why they required the document to bear an authentication, since it was written that very day and the obligor admits the debt, so there is no concern for payment or collusion, as explained. See the Derishah, where I offered a resolution. They hold that since an evident defect arose through the document's loss, there is reason to suspect that perhaps the creditor does not presently have money and will not lend to him until some later time, and that he is colluding with the borrower so that the debt will nevertheless bear the earlier date of today. The borrower may agree to this in order to save the scribe's fee. If the document has already been authenticated, however, meaning it bears a judicial certification, we do not entertain this concern. According to the opinion that the witnesses, by their signatures, acquire rights for the creditor from the time of signing, no authentication is necessary. Therefore the Tur did not write this requirement.
And there is an opinion, etc. The wording through "but if he is present, etc." is difficult, because the first view likewise holds this and adds an additional requirement, namely that the obligor must specifically admit the debt. The text should seemingly have stated the reverse: "And there is an opinion that only when the obligor is present and says, 'I paid it,' do we not return it, but ordinarily we do not suspect payment." It must be that the Mechaber adopted the wording of a responsum of the Rosh. In its original context the Rosh's formulation is appropriate. Nevertheless, this requires study, because the Rosh himself accepts Rav Kahana's interpretation, which specifically requires that the obligor admit the debt. See the Derishah, seif katan 21, where I wrote a resolution of this difficulty.
