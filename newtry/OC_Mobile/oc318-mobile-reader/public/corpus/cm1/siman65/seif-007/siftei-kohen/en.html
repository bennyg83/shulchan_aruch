Likewise, if the debtor has unencumbered property. The Sema explains that, since the creditor holds a valid document, we do not suspect that the debtor borrowed from others after the date of this document, because concern is limited to a definite loss to purchasers. The Bach similarly says that unknown creditors or purchasers caused their own loss by failing to investigate. This is astonishing. The entire first chapter of Bava Metzia, other passages, and the authorities plainly show that we are concerned for creditors and purchasers even when they are not presently known. The same is stated below regarding deeds of sale and gift. Perhaps they investigated and discovered that this document was invalid. Moreover, perhaps the debtor owes earlier creditors who will collect the property now in his possession, while this claimant will wrongfully collect from known purchasers. Rather, wherever a realistic concern exists, we take it into account. Maharshal says likewise. His interpretation that 'return it' means to issue a writ of collection only from the date it was found, or to write a new document, is strained. The correct meaning is that the property is known with certainty to be genuinely unencumbered, unsold, and not subject to any other debt. This may occur after public notice was made, or where local regulation invalidates every document not entered in the scribe's register, and similarly in other cases.
And it bears an authentication. The Maggid Mishneh, citing the Rashba, asks why authentication is required. The Sema's answer is unsatisfactory. Nor is the answer of Maadanei Melech correct, namely that without authentication we suspect the debtor wrote it but never borrowed and now they collude. The Gemara shows that such concern exists only where the document bears an earlier date and might wrongfully reach purchasers, but not where its date is the same day and the debtor admits the debt. The Rashba and Maggid Mishneh are therefore correct. Tosafot agree, and Baal HaMaor likewise argues that according to Rav Kahana authentication is unnecessary; the Ramban does not answer this point in Milchamot. This may also be the Tur's view, and Rabbeinu Yerucham concludes so in the name of most authorities. The Rama in Darkei Moshe also appears to accept Tosafot's position, contrary to the Rambam.
There is an opinion. The wording is defective. It should say that this applies specifically when the debtor is present, but when he is absent we do not suspect repayment made that same day. Even so, this requires study, because the Rosh wrote this according to Rav Assi, whereas the halachah follows Rav Kahana, as the Rosh himself rules. The document should therefore be returned only when the debtor admits the debt. The Sema and Bach likewise challenge the Mechaber.
