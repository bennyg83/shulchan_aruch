(Seif 19: If it is not authenticated, etc.) This concerns a case where only the receipt, not the loan document, is held by the escrow holder. The Beit Yosef asks why the escrow holder’s credibility is needed once the receipt is authenticated. The answer is that authentication established before us proves payment, but where authentication is now being sought, the document would be ineffective in the creditor’s possession because he could say it had merely been prepared for future delivery. The escrow holder is therefore needed to say that the debt was paid. If he merely displayed an unauthenticated receipt and said nothing, its placement in escrow would itself show that some dispute existed. An escrow is different from an ordinary deposit, because the parties resort to it precisely because of disagreement. This also explains why even the debtor’s admission may be suspected as collusive.
