And court validation is written upon it. The early authorities ask why validation matters. It appears to me from Rambam's law of a get that if a person cancels the get and says, "It is mere pottery," he cannot later use that same get again. Before delivery, writing alone remains verbal preparation and can be canceled. The same principle can apply to monetary instruments. If a document was written but not yet delivered and the obligor intentionally discarded or canceled it, the original writing can lose its force. Therefore a found document from the present day can create concern that the obligor canceled it before delivery. Court validation solves this by showing it had already entered an operative legal stage. If one holds that the borrower's witnesses acquire for the lender from the time of writing, then later delivery can relate back and cancellation after that point may not help. But Tosafot limits that principle within the day itself. If a document was written for one person and transferred to another that same day, the second can acquire first. Thus within the day the original party does not necessarily gain retroactive title. A document found on the day it was written can therefore still raise a cancellation concern even under that doctrine. The Gemara's broader discussion of found debt instruments does not usually suspect same day cancellation because such circumstances are uncommon. But where the document is actually found that very day, the concern becomes real. Ritva writes similarly. My grandfather in Pilpula Charifta suggested that validation is needed because perhaps it was written for a loan that never occurred. The Shach objects that the Gemara worries about delayed borrowing only where there is a purchaser priority gain, not when the date is the same day. That objection is not decisive. If there is no validation and the loan never occurred, there is another possible gain: avoiding the scribe's fee. Tosafot says explicitly that concern for the scribe's fee exists where no loan was made, even when the priority date creates no advantage. Thus the requirement of validation can be explained either through cancellation before delivery or through concern that no loan ever occurred.
