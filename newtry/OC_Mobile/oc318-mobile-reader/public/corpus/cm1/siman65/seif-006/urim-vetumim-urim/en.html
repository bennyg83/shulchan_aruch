There is a hole, etc. Both borrower and lender can know identifying marks in the deed. The borrower saw it before delivery, and even a non-kinyan loan deed that is not written until lender and borrower are together can be seen by the borrower. The lender knows it from having held it before repayment. Therefore an identifying mark such as a hole is not enough, even if one side gives a mark and the other does not. The latter may simply not have noticed it. See Tumim. This applies even where there is no property responsibility and no concern of collusion. But if there is no responsibility clause, the borrower says he wrote the deed intending to borrow but never did, and he gives a mark while the lender also gives one, then it should be given to the lender. The borrower's own account does not explain how the lender would know the mark. This resembles a get, where when both parties give identifying marks it is returned to the woman. Although the borrower has a migo that he possessed it and repaid, perhaps that migo is against the equivalent of witnesses once we rely on the lender's knowledge of the mark.
