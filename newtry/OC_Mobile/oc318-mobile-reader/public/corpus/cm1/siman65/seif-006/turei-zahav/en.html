This is difficult in light of siman 49, seif 9, where the Tur and Mechaber require, regarding two people with the same name, that the receipt be authenticated and that the documents also be found among the torn documents. It appears to me that there both factors are specifically required because we seek to invalidate two documents held by the lender against two people of the same name. There the invalidation is treated as certain, such that we regard both documents as definitely paid, not merely doubtful. The practical consequence is that even if the lender seized from one of the two and argues, "Since I am in possession I may say that you are the one who did not repay me, while the receipt was written concerning the other person," we remove it from him because we regard the documents as certainly paid. Authentication of the receipt alone would not suffice for that result. The second factor, namely that the documents were placed among the torn documents, proves that both were paid. Yet that factor alone also does not suffice when the receipt is unauthenticated. One might argue that since both documents lie among the torn documents and one is certainly paid because a receipt is found in the lender's possession, the other is also paid. But one can argue the reverse: one of them is necessarily unpaid because there is no receipt concerning it, despite its location among torn documents. Thus this man evidently places even valid documents among torn ones, so perhaps the receipt is worthless, as the Gemara says that a receipt found in the lender's possession is like a mere joke and does not invalidate the document unless the document is also among the torn ones. Here that combination alone is weakened because we see that this person places valid documents among torn ones. Therefore, from doubt we may not invalidate the two documents in the lender's possession, and the receipt must also be authenticated. Once authenticated, we know at least one document was paid, and its placement among the torn documents then supports the conclusion that the second was paid as well. This is why siman 49 requires both factors. Here, where there is only one borrower, either factor suffices: an authenticated receipt or the document being found among the torn ones. In either case, the receipt in the lender's possession becomes effective. This appears to me correct after careful analysis, and I have seen many err on this point.
