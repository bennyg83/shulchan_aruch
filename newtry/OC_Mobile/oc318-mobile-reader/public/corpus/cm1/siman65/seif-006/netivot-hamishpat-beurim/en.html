Because we are concerned for payment. If the debtor claims that he never borrowed, the creditor still does not collect from him. Although in that event we are no longer concerned that the document was paid, the debtor is nevertheless believed in his claim that he never borrowed. Even according to the view that we do not write a document for a borrower in the lender's absence, Rav Asi still holds that we are concerned that it may have happened exceptionally and been written, as explained in Bava Metzia 14. Even according to Abaye, who is not concerned that it may exceptionally have been written, the debtor is nevertheless believed to say that he never borrowed through a migo that he could have claimed payment, just as with an unauthenticated document he is believed to say that he never borrowed through a migo that he could have claimed forgery. If, however, the creditor provides a distinctive identifying mark, the document is returned to the creditor, because the reason given by Sema, seif katan 18, does not apply. One cannot explain Sema as referring to a debtor who says, "I never borrowed," and who claims that the witnesses delivered the document to him and that he then delivered it to the creditor in trust, which would explain how the creditor knows the identifying mark. Wherever the debtor is believed to claim payment, he is likewise believed to claim that the document was given in trust, as explained in siman 50, and such a claim does not render the witnesses wicked. That explanation cannot be correct, for regarding a handwritten instrument the debtor may likewise claim payment, yet if he claims that he never borrowed and the creditor produces his handwriting, he is established as a denier, as explained in siman 79 and in chapter two. However, if the debtor claims, "I paid you in Nisan, received the document from you, and it fell from me," and the creditor proves that the document was seen in his own hands in Iyar, the debtor is still believed concerning the payment. Although he has been contradicted concerning his statement that he received the document and lost it, he has not been contradicted concerning the payment itself. Had he claimed, "I paid you and left the document in your hands, and it fell from you because I was not careful about it," he would have been believed. Therefore his additional statement that he received the document is treated as an incidental and unnecessary statement, as explained above. The same applies where Reuven claims, "You borrowed one hundred from me on the strength of your handwritten instrument," the debtor says, "I paid you and received the handwritten instrument from you," and Reuven then produces it. Although the debtor is contradicted concerning his statement that he received the instrument, he is not contradicted concerning payment and remains believed. Any contradiction that does not concern the substance of the disputed matter is treated as an incidental statement of the sort people do not remember precisely. If the debtor had already been established as a denier and the document was later lost, the law depends on the timing. If he was established as a denier before the document was lost, for example he claimed that he never borrowed, the creditor produced the loan document, and beit din ordered him to pay, then although he cannot afterward claim payment even on an oral loan because he has been established as a denier, once the document later falls and is lost he is nevertheless believed to claim that he paid afterward. Tosafot in Bava Metzia 16, s.v. mishum dehuchzak kafran, write this. But if he makes a claim after the document has already fallen and is established through that claim as a denier, he is no longer believed even to say that he paid afterward, because the fall of the document gives no support to a payment allegedly made after the fall. All the more so, if he initially claimed that he never borrowed, he cannot later say that he had already paid, because by his own first claim he admitted that he had not paid. This is evident from Bava Metzia 17 in the incident involving the daughter in law of Rav Shabtai son of Rav Marinus. The matter requires no further elaboration.
