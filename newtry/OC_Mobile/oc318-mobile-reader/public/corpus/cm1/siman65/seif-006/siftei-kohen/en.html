Even if the debtor or creditor says that it has a hole. This means that the debtor does not admit the debt, but says that he paid it, that the document fell from him, and that he can identify it by this mark. Even so, it is not returned to him. The same mark given by the creditor is insufficient even to collect from the debtor himself. A different, highly distinctive mark is effective for collection from the debtor, but no mark is sufficient to collect from encumbered property where there is concern that the debtor disclosed the mark to the creditor, or that the debtor also knows it and pretends not to know it, since even where both admit we suspect collusion.
It has a hole. The Baal HaTerumot explains that the document remained with the creditor long enough for him to know the mark, while it may nevertheless have fallen from the debtor after payment. Conversely, the debtor may have observed the mark while the document was initially in his hands and it may later have fallen from the creditor. His wording is not exact, because a non-kinyan document may not be written for the debtor in the creditor's absence. The essential reason is simply that the document was initially also in the debtor's possession.
