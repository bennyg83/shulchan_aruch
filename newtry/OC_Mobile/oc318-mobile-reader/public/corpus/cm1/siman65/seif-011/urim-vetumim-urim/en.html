Without an identifying mark. We do not say they fell from a lender, because they belong to three different lenders, and there is no reason they would be together. Nor did they fall from beit din, since they are already validated, and ordinarily each person immediately takes his document after validation, so there is no reason for one person's document to be together with another's. See Tumim. If the documents say, "to you and to every bearer," which below in siman 66 we rule does not require writing and delivery for transfer, there is concern that one person may have purchased all three and they fell from him. Therefore they should be returned to the borrower only with an identifying mark. See there. We do not ordinarily suspect that the three lenders all deposited their documents with one custodian and they fell from that custodian, because it is uncommon that precisely those three lenders would all deposit documents against the same borrower with one person.
If they are not validated, etc. Sema writes that the identifying mark must specifically be number or wrapping. An identifying mark in the body of the document, such as a hole next to a particular letter, does not help, as above in seif 6. In Tumim I distinguished. Where the borrower gives the identifying mark, Sema is correct. Where the lender gives an identifying mark in the body of the document, that mark can be effective.
Except to the one who gives an identifying mark. There is no difference between two documents and three documents. Wherever an identifying mark is effective, it works. With two documents, a wrapping mark can be effective where there are two lenders and one borrower, or the reverse, even according to Ramah, who held that wrapping alone is insufficient with three documents belonging to one lender and one borrower. See Tumim.
It is given to the lender even without an identifying mark. See Tumim, seif katan 18. If the borrowers are before us and say that the documents fell from them because they had put them together in one bundle or the like, it is possible that they are mutually believed as witnesses for one another, as above in siman 37, seif 5. See Tumim.
If they are in the handwriting of one scribe, etc. Then perhaps they fell from the scribe when he wrapped them together, or perhaps they fell from the lender. An identifying mark is therefore required. See Tumim, seif katan 19. If the documents bear different dates and there is no concern of postdating, it may be that we should not fear that they fell from the scribe. Since we do not rule that witnesses automatically acquire for the lender from the moment of signature, a scribe would not retain a document after its date passed, because it would become antedated and invalid. See there, where I left this requiring study.
To whoever gives an identifying mark. If the documents are validated, they certainly fell from the lender, because a borrower does not validate a document against himself, and certainly a scribe does not ordinarily validate documents he writes. This is Sema's conclusion, and it is obvious.
