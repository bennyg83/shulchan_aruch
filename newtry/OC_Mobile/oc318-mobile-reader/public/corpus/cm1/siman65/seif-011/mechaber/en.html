If one finds together three authenticated promissory notes against one borrower, each owed to a different lender, he returns them to the borrower even without an identifying mark, and does not give them to any lender even if that lender provides an identifying mark. If the notes are not authenticated, he gives them only to the person who provides an identifying mark. If he finds together three notes from three borrowers who borrowed from one lender, and they are written in the hands of three different scribes, he gives them to the lender even without an identifying mark. If they are all in the hand of one scribe, he gives them only to whoever provides an identifying mark. The same rule applies to two documents from two borrowers or to two lenders.
