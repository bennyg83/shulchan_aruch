(And now I shall answer.) I shall address the Shach’s objections to the Rosh one by one. The Shach argues that the Mishnah’s mention of binding proves that binding itself is the identifying mark. There is no proof. The Mishnah had to mention binding because without a secure bundle the documents might not have fallen together, the owner would despair, and no announcement would be required. The fact that “a bundle” is listed first likewise proves nothing. Had the Mishnah said “a bundle or group of three documents,” one might have thought that both the manner of binding and the number were required. It separated the clauses to teach that the number is the identifying mark, while the binding merely preserves the group and prevents despair. The Gemara’s question whether a knot is a sign arose before the conclusion and assumed that each clause supplied an independent sign; it does not refute the Rosh’s final interpretation. The Shach further asks why the Gemara, which elsewhere doubts whether number is a sign, does not prove it here. Tosafot already answer that the Mishnah could be read as requiring both number and arrangement. The Rosh speaks according to the final law that number is a sign, not as a proof from this Mishnah alone. Nor should the finder announce the binding itself, because an announcement should disclose only the category of the object, not its identifying marks, lest a deceiver exploit them. These points fully vindicate the Rosh.
