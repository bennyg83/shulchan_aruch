And if they are authenticated, he should return them to the borrower, etc. The reason is that a creditor commonly goes to beit din to authenticate his document, and once it has been authenticated he ordinarily takes it from beit din immediately rather than leaving an authenticated document there. Since these are documents of three different creditors, we cannot say that they fell from the creditors' hands, because if so, who bound them together? Rather, it is certain that the one borrower who borrowed from all three paid all three, took his documents from them, bound them together, and they then fell from him. If they are not authenticated, however, it remains possible that the creditors went together to beit din to authenticate their documents and beit din bound them together until it could authenticate them, and that they fell from beit din before authentication. If so, they still belong to the creditors. It is also possible that they fell from the one borrower, either before he borrowed from them or after he borrowed and repaid them. Therefore, they are returned to the person who gives an identifying mark. This identifying mark does not mean a mark on the body of the document, such as a hole alongside a particular letter, because the Tur and Mechaber already wrote in seif 6 that a document is not returned on the basis of such a mark, for the reason I explained there. Rather, it means the identifying marks of number and binding discussed above. Although there are three creditors, they can say, "We saw that beit din bound all three documents together when we went together to have them authenticated." See the Perishah for what I wrote further about this.

Written in the handwriting of one scribe, etc. In that case it is possible that after the scribe wrote the documents for all three borrowers, he bound them together until the borrowers would come and he could give each one his debt instrument, and they fell together from his possession. It is also possible that they fell from the one creditor who lent to all three.
To the person who gives an identifying mark for them. At first glance it appears that this applies when the documents are not authenticated. If they are authenticated, one cannot say that they fell from the scribe, for who authenticated them? This is not conclusive, however, because it is possible that all three borrowers, together with the creditor, asked the scribe to act as their agent and take them to beit din for authentication, and told the agent not to deliver the documents to the creditor after authentication until the creditor actually lent the money to the borrowers. This is strained, however, because in that event the scribe would know the identifying mark equally well.
And the same applies to two documents, etc. Meaning, the Gemara and authorities do not mean specifically the case of three documents of three creditors, or three documents of three borrowers, as discussed above. The same law applies to two documents. See the Perishah, where I wrote that it is possible that the Gemara and authorities who specifically wrote three hold that even in the cases where the documents are returned to one who gives an identifying mark, the required marks are specifically number and binding. For an identifying mark based on number, three documents are specifically necessary, as explained above.
