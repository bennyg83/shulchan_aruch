The reason is that a lender ordinarily goes to beit din to authenticate his document, and once it has been authenticated he normally takes it from beit din rather than leaving an authenticated document there. Since these are documents of three different lenders, we cannot say that they fell from the lenders after authentication, for who would have bound them together? Rather, the one borrower must have repaid all three, taken his documents from them, bound them together, and then lost them. If they are not authenticated, however, there is room for doubt. Perhaps the lenders went together to beit din to authenticate their documents and lost them before authentication; or perhaps they fell from the borrower either before he borrowed or after he repaid them. Therefore the documents are returned to whoever gives an identifying mark. Here 'identifying mark' does not mean, for example, a hole next to a particular letter, because the Tur and Mechaber state in seif 6 that a document is not returned on such a mark. Rather, it means the number and the manner of binding. Although there are three lenders, they can say that they saw beit din bind the documents together when they went to authenticate them.
