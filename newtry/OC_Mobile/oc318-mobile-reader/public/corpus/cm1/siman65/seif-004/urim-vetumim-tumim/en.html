But we do not make that claim for them. Shach asks how Mechaber can state this without qualification. Rosh's entire reason is that the claim, "Perhaps the documents were held as collateral," is uncommon, and we do not make uncommon claims on behalf of heirs. Rosh follows his general rule that beit din does not plead an uncommon defense for heirs. But below in siman 108, Mechaber cites two opinions whether we do make even an uncommon claim, such as unavoidable loss, on behalf of heirs. According to the opinion that we do, why not also plead here that perhaps the notes were held as collateral? It appears that Mechaber distinguishes between two kinds of situations. When the heirs are already in possession and another person seeks to extract property from them, we may plead even an uncommon possibility in their favor. The reason is that the burden of proof rests upon the claimant. The heirs need only enough of a possible defense to preserve possession. But where the heirs themselves seek to extract property, everyone agrees that beit din does not invent an uncommon claim for them. This is similar to the rule that we do not follow majority probabilities in monetary law when doing so would extract money, but a majority can sometimes support retention. Here the physical document belongs legally to the lender. The heirs seek to take it on the theory that perhaps it had been pledged as collateral to their father. That is an extracting claim. Even when one possesses ordinary collateral, claiming payment up to its value is treated as an extracting claim. This is why the one who collects on collateral must take a severe oath, like one who swears and collects, as all the Geonim rule. All the more so with a loan note, whose physical paper is not itself money and which cannot ordinarily be sold without written transfer and delivery. Treating it as collateral in favor of the heirs is an extracting position. Therefore, even according to the view that we plead uncommon defenses for heirs who are merely retaining property, we do not plead an uncommon collateral claim here. This is why Mechaber could state the rule generally.
