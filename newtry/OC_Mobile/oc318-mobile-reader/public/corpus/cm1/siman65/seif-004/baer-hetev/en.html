We. The Rosh writes in a responsum that lending money against documents that cannot themselves be collected is uncommon. Accordingly, if the documents are written in a form that enables collection, then even if we know that they did not belong to the father, we argue on behalf of the heirs that he took them as collateral. Likewise, according to the authorities who hold that we make claims for heirs even on the basis of uncommon possibilities, as stated in siman 108, seif 4, siman 237, and siman 361, the same should apply here. This requires study. Thus the Shach. At any rate, with our mamram documents, we argue for the heirs that their father took them as collateral.
