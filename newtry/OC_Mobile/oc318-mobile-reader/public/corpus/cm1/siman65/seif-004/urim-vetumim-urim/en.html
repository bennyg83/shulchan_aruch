He took it as collateral. The Shulchan Aruch follows Rosh's wording. Rosh holds that even against the lender the holder cannot say, “I had a valid written assignment and lost it,” and therefore the heirs may claim only that perhaps the document was held as collateral, as stated above in siman 64. But according to the Shulchan Aruch's own adoption of Rambam, they could also claim that perhaps their father actually purchased the document and had a written assignment that was lost, because that claim can be believed.
We do not plead this for them. Such an acquisition is uncommon, because one does not usually take as collateral a debt document that cannot be collected without a written assignment. Shach adds that with an instrument that requires no written assignment under siman 66, we would plead for the heirs that perhaps their father acquired it. See Tumim, where I answer Shach's question from the opinion that even uncommon claims may sometimes be pleaded for heirs.
