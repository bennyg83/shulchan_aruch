And even partially. This is the Rambam's language. It appears that he means that part of the receipt is written opposite the debt instrument and part below it. This excludes a case where the entire receipt is written below the instrument, in which case it is ineffective because the creditor could simply cut it off and leave the debt instrument intact. This applies specifically when the creditor himself is alive and makes a definite claim that the debt was not paid. If he has died, however, then even if the entire receipt is written below the instrument in a place where it could be cut off, collection may not be made with this debt instrument. This resolves the difficulty in the next seif, which states, "Reuven died, etc.; we act in accordance with what is written in the receipt." What novelty does that teach, since even if the creditor were alive and made a definite claim that the debt was not paid, the law would seemingly be the same? Rather, the novelty there is that even when the receipt is written in a place where it could be cut off, we act according to it because the claimants are orphans who can only claim uncertainty. We do not advance for them the argument that the receipt should be disregarded. When the creditor is alive and makes a definite claim, however, the rule stated here requires that the receipt be written in a place from which it cannot be cut off. According to this, the phrase "whether on its front" must concern a case where it was written in a manner that cannot be cut away, such as between the lines. Regarding a notation found in his ledger, we presume the debt to have been paid because everything written in his ledger is as though he himself testified that it is so, as I wrote in the Derishah. I later saw that Rabbeinu Yerucham writes, "and it was found written in his ledger," meaning that others found it so written. This is even more satisfactory, because there is then no migo. It therefore appears that the Rama's text should be emended to read "and it was found" instead of "and he found." See what I write in the next seif.
