And even on part of it. Sema holds that this rule applies only where the receipt is written in a place from which it cannot be cut away. If it could have been cut off, the creditor is believed to say the debt was not paid because he has a migo that he could simply have cut off the receipt. Shach cites Hagahot Rosh that no migo applies because it is a migo against a chazakah. It is true that Hagahot Rosh says this, but he cites R. Baruch of Regensburg as disagreeing. It appears that Tosafot in Sanhedrin 31b follow Sema. The Gemara challenges the second version, which requires a migo for a third party, from the Mishnah: if there are no witnesses on the receipt and it emerges from the hand of a third party, the third party is believed. Tosafot say one cannot explain that case as one where the document had not yet been established before beit din, because the clause is parallel to the next clause concerning a receipt written after the signatures. Why not establish both clauses before the document was judicially established? The answer is that if the note had not yet been established before beit din, then where the receipt was written on the document itself the creditor would have a migo that he could cut it off. He could then deny payment. Thus the Mishnah's novelty would disappear. We must therefore be speaking after the document was established before beit din, when cutting the receipt would create a retroactive migo and would not help. This strongly indicates that Tosafot accept Sema's migo. I likewise found in Shitah Mekubetzet to Bava Metzia, in the name of Maharam of Rothenburg, that even a receipt written immediately after the witnesses' signatures is valid only if it lies so close to the signatures that cutting it off would make the document visibly suspicious or require attaching another sheet. If it could be removed cleanly, the concern remains that the creditor could cut it away. Thus the matter is genuinely disputed, but Sema has substantial support.
