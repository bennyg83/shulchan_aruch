No. This too is because we are concerned for collusion. His intention may not be that the recipient truly acquire the property, but only to remove it from one to whom he later gave or sold that field after the date of this deed. Therefore, if the recipient or purchaser brings proof that the deed came into his possession on the date written in it, it is returned to him. With a promissory note, however, it is not returned to the lender even if he proves that it reached his hand on the date written, because a loan document is subject to repayment and we must suspect that it was later paid. What the Tur and Mechaber state in siman 43, seif 15, that it is returned when proof is brought, which there appears to apply even to a promissory note, refers to a case where the lender himself found it after it was lost. Thus the Sema. See siman 43, seif katan 26, where the Taz challenges the Sema on this point.

A deed of present transfer. In that case it is a gift by a healthy person and the recipient acquires immediately upon the writing of the deed, even though it has not yet reached his hand. Thus the Sema. The Shach writes in the name of Maharshal that this ruling does not accord with practical halachah, because we rule that the witnesses acquire for him through their signatures. Therefore there is no distinction between a deed of present transfer and other deeds; whenever the obligor admits it, the document is given to the recipient. This is the primary ruling, contrary to the Bach. Thus the Shach.
