One who finds. It shall not be given to him. Maharshal says this ruling follows the Rif and Rambam but not Tosafot and the Rosh, who follow Abaye and hold that when the grantor admits, the document is returned, except in the special cases of a dying person and a healthy person. This is correct. The Bach's concern that perhaps the grantor wrote it intending to give it, never delivered it, and discarded it, so that it became worthless, is unsupported by logic or the Gemara. The Rosh initially copied the Rif's language but later indicated that Rabbi Abba bar Mammal's statement cannot be reconciled with Abaye. The Tur may likewise have relied on the dispute already recorded at the end of siman 39.
It shall not be given to him. We suspect collusion: perhaps he wrote the deed of gift or sale but did not deliver it, later gave or sold the property to another, and now retracts in order to harm that person. If the recipient or purchaser proves that the deed was delivered, the proof is effective, unlike a debt document where repayment remains possible. The document is not returned merely on condition that they later prove when it reached them, because purchasers may rely on the court's return and assume that the court already investigated timely delivery.
He did not come near him. P. A.D. is known that he has not come near him and the drunken and known are now protested to give him ap, and he will return, and that he will be called "T. S.C."
