(Supplement) And if it is. This is the Tur's language. See Beur HaGra there, following Rabbeinu Chananel. The phrase, a deed of present transfer, means a deed that expressly states that title is transferred immediately. This is unlike the Beit Yosef, who understood the Tur to follow the dissenting authorities. That reading is difficult, because the language does not imply it and it makes the Tur's express explanation unnecessary. Beur HaGra says that Rambam follows Rabbeinu Chananel, as the Beit Yosef inferred from Hilchot Zechiyah 9. Maggid Mishneh, however, writes that Rambam follows the dissenting view, and explains the case there as a gift of all one's property. Rivash likewise proves this from Rambam's warning in Hilchot Zechiyah 8:18. Maggid Mishneh writes that Ramban, Rashba, Ri Migash, and other authorities agree. Beit Yosef himself rules accordingly at the end of siman 39. The Tur in siman 65 says that a found deed of gift is returned only if it is a deed of present transfer, as I explained, meaning that it explicitly states immediate transfer. The Shulchan Aruch omitted these words according to its approach, but that omission is difficult because the opening clause should then also have been omitted. As a matter of law, the dissenting authorities are correct. Rashi on Bava Metzia 13a supplies an unanswerable proof, and the ordinary language of the Gemara there and on 16b supports him. Rivash and Rashba likewise write that Rashi's position is compelled by many proofs. Tur and Shulchan Aruch rule this way in siman 243, seifim 12 and 13, and the Rama in siman 49, seif 6, and siman 250, seif 25. The Rosh explains at length that the same applies to a sale and similar transfers, except for a promissory note, where payment may already have occurred. Rabbeinu Chananel's proof from Bava Batra 135b was rejected because that passage concerns a deathbed gift. (End of supplement.)
(Supplement) And if it is a deed. Bava Metzia 13a and 16b state that one who finds a deed of present transfer returns it. Even Rabbi Yochanan disagrees only regarding a promissory note, where payment may have occurred. Here the only concern is that it was written but never delivered. (End of supplement.)
(Supplement) And if it is a deed. Rambam follows Rashi that delivery to the recipient is unnecessary once the deed is executed. Rashba likewise rules this way and proves it from Bava Metzia 16a, where Shmuel's statement would otherwise remain vulnerable to the concern that the instrument never reached the recipient, the very concern entertained according to Abaye on 13a. The opposing authorities cite Bava Batra 77a regarding three types of documents and priority of delivery, but that passage can be read according to Abaye's view that witnesses create the transfer through their signatures. (End of supplement.) [Citation references: 40, 100, 161.]
