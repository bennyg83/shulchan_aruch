A gift document of a healthy person, etc. This concerns a gift without a kinyan. If there was a kinyan, the recipient acquired immediately and there is no concern. Without a kinyan, we suspect that perhaps the donor had the document written intending to give it but never actually delivered it. In the meantime he may have sold the field or given it to someone else, and now he colludes with the intended first recipient to defeat the later purchaser or recipient. The donor instructs that the found document be returned, and the intended recipient will later claim that his gift preceded the later transfer. Therefore, if the recipient brings proof that the gift document was already in his possession before it was lost, it is returned to him, just as with an emancipation document that may be used once he proves it had been in his possession before the loss, as explained above regarding emancipation and as I wrote there.
It should not be given to him. See Tumim. I established that even according to those who hold that witnesses acquire for a recipient through their signatures, they agree that a gift document should not be returned. This is why Rama added no gloss here.
It should be given to him. If the document is validated, Bach nevertheless holds that it should not be returned. See Tumim, where I wrote that this does not appear correct. If it is validated, it should be returned to the recipient, because the fact that the recipient had it is certain, since it is normally the recipient who validates the document.
From today, if I do not retract, etc. In that form there is no concern for collusion. If after the date of this gift the donor gives the property to someone else, the second gift remains effective because the later transfer itself constitutes a retraction from the first gift. Nor need we fear that after the date of this gift the donor incurred a debt to another person, and that the present recipient will later produce the earlier dated gift to defeat that creditor's lien. The creditor may collect from the property because the donor retained the power to retract. The creditor's right therefore has effect against it, and the donor's indebtedness is treated as a retraction. So writes Asifas Zekeinim in the name of the poskim. See what I wrote in Tumim, seif katan 26, in the name of Ran and Rambam, who hold that the Sages did not suspect this type of scheme to defeat a creditor. According to them the difficulty does not begin. It is also possible that Asifas Zekeinim speaks only of a documented loan. An oral creditor may possibly be unable to collect from a recipient of this kind of gift. Many authorities hold that even from a gift of a person on his deathbed an oral creditor cannot collect, and those who permit collection do so because the Sages treated such a recipient like an heir. That reason does not apply to a gift of a healthy person of this form. Nevertheless, there is no difficulty why the found document may be returned because perhaps an oral debt exists. Why would collusion be necessary? Even if the donor simply sold the property today, the oral creditor's lien would be defeated. Therefore, regarding an oral loan the matter requires study.
Who specified that he transferred it to him immediately. The Mechaber follows the Tur's wording, which follows Rosh. Rosh understands the talmudic term "documents of acquisition" not as referring to an ordinary kinyan, but to a kinyan accompanied by an express statement that the obligation should take effect whether or not the money is later lent. Therefore here too the Tur requires an express statement that the gift was transferred immediately. According to the Mechaber's own approach, an ordinary kinyan itself has that effect and no express statement should be necessary. Perhaps the wording is needed because of the later clause concerning a gift of a person on his deathbed. With such a gift, an ordinary kinyan where the document never reached the recipient does not suffice, as explained below in siman 250, seif 25. If a person died with a testamentary deed lying on his thigh, even if a kinyan had been made, it should not be returned because perhaps he changed his mind. The heir's instruction to return it would therefore still be ineffective, since perhaps his father had retracted and the heir is colluding. Thus an express kinyan is needed, stating that the recipient acquires even without delivery of the document. Such an express kinyan is effective even for a person on his deathbed. The case below in siman 250 concerns an ordinary kinyan.
A gift document of a person on his deathbed, etc. He may retract from such a gift, so it resembles the previous case where the donor expressly retained the power to retract. There is therefore no concern.
Even though his son says, etc. Sema explains that perhaps the son himself gave this field to someone else and now wishes to cancel his own gift. He therefore admits that his father's earlier gift should be returned, so that his own later transfer will be void. He is colluding. According to that reason, if the later transfer was made by the heir himself, one might think the document should be returned because his own transfer would in any event remain subject to whatever rights existed. But one can instead say that perhaps the father had already made an earlier gift to a different person, and this found document represents a later gift he intended but never delivered. Returning it now would improperly validate the later gift and defeat the first one. Therefore even at the heir's request it should not be returned. See Tumim, seif katan 26, for why the Gemara states the particular concern cited by Sema.
