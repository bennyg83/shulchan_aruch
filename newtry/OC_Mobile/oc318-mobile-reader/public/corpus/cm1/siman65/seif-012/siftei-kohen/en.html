The Rambam rules that a found marriage contract is not returned to the woman even if both spouses admit, lest it was paid or waived and the husband later sold property, and they now seek to collude against purchasers. The Maggid Mishneh calls this the ordinary law of debt documents, but the matter is explicitly disputed in the Gemara. Rabbi Yose says that while the woman remains married the contract is returned to her, whereas after widowhood or divorce it is returned to neither. Some understood the Rambam as following Rabbi Yose, but that cannot be correct, for he does not distinguish between a married and a divorced woman. Rather, the Rambam follows Rav Pappa's reading that the Sages suspect repayment even while she remains married, because the husband may have given her money or security, or she may have waived the first contract upon receiving another. The phrase 'this marriage contract' is precise: it may have been paid or waived when a replacement was written, since one may not remain married without a marriage contract. The Rif and Rosh, who do not separately record the law of a marriage contract, likewise appear to treat it like any debt document and rely upon their general ruling that it is returned to neither party because of concern for repayment and collusion.
