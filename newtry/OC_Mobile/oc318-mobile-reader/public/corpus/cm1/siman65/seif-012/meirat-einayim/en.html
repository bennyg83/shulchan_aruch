He found appraisal documents. This is the wording of the Mishnah and Rambam. The Tur writes: if one found appraisal documents by which beit din appraised a borrower's property for the creditor, support documents concerning an obligation one accepted to support his wife's daughter, or documents recording that beit din sold land for the support of a wife and daughters, or final transfer documents by which beit din vested a borrower's property in the creditor. See the Perishah for my explanation of these documents.
Such as a final transfer document. See the Perishah, where I wrote in the name of the Baal HaTerumot that appraisal documents refer to cases where beit din sold land, and likewise with all the documents mentioned above, when both borrower and creditor are before beit din. In that case the court appraises, announces the property, and writes for the creditor a formula such as: "So and so came before us, etc.; we descended to appraise it, announced it, tore the loan document, and placed so and so in possession of such and such field, etc." A final transfer document, by contrast, applies where the borrower is not before beit din, in which case the formula is written differently. One should not explain "appraisal documents" as the initial appraisal made by beit din, followed by thirty days of public announcement, with a final transfer document written only at the end, as explained below in simanim 103 and 109. Regarding such an initial appraisal document, the Gemara would not have said that if the debt had been paid the borrower would have torn it up. He is concerned to tear only the document by which the land itself is actually taken from him.
