(Seif 20: Whether on its front or its back.) This means whether the payment notation is written on the face of the document, between its lines, or on the reverse opposite the writing. Even a partial notation in the margin creates a presumption of payment. We do not say that the creditor merely prepared it for the debtor, because that concern applies to a receipt on separate paper that might be needed if payment occurred at twilight when the creditor lacked the document. It does not apply to a notation on the instrument itself. The Sema says that a notation in a place that can be cut off is ineffective. This is astonishing, for Bava Metzia states that a receipt written after the signatures is valid, even though it can be cut away. The Mechaber’s later reference to heirs teaches only that even an orphan’s document is impaired by such a notation and we do not say the father wrote it merely to avoid appearing wealthy. Common practice likewise records payments on documents and mamarniot even where the notation could be cut off.
