This is the Tur's formulation from the Rambam. The Rambam derives it from the plain meaning of the Mishnah: if one finds a document among his documents and does not know its status, it remains until Eliyahu comes; if receipts are found with them, one acts according to the receipts. The Gemara establishes this where the document was found among torn documents. This also follows from the Rambam's commentary to the Mishnah, unlike Rashi, who treats the clause concerning receipts as an independent ruling. The Lechem Mishneh objects that the Gemara challenges Rav, who invalidates a receipt found in the creditor's possession, from this Mishnah, implying that it concerns a creditor. This appears to be what compelled Rashi. The Bach offers a strained reconciliation. In my view the Rambam is clear. The clause applies to all the documents mentioned earlier, whether found in the marketplace or among one's own documents without knowing their status. If receipts accompany them, one follows the receipts. The plural wording, 'receipts' and 'with them,' supports this, as does the Tosefta. The Gemara's challenge is therefore sound: if a receipt in the creditor's possession were invalid, why follow it when the finder does not know from whom it fell? The answer is that the document was found among torn documents. The Tur may therefore be understood entirely in accordance with the Rambam.
