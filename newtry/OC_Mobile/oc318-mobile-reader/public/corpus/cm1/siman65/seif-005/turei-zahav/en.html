(Seif 18: One who finds a receipt, etc.) When the creditor admits the receipt, why return it? Perhaps it was written in Nisan but delivered only in Tishrei, while in the interim the creditor collected land and sold it, and the debtor will now use the antedated receipt to recover that land improperly. The answer is that the receipt is returned only to discharge the debtor from the creditor, not to collect from purchasers, just as the Gemara distinguishes concerning a bill of manumission. A purchaser can say that the document was returned merely to free the debtor from the creditor, not to extract property from others. Nor does the creditor’s admission prejudice his own creditors, because even without it they could not collect from the debtor while the debtor claims that the receipt fell from his possession. Thus the admission creates no new loss to third parties.
