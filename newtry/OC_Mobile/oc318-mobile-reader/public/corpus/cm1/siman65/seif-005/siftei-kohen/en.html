"Since he is bound up with his name, and he will return to him as he does, and he will return to him, as he does, and he will return to him, and he will return to the vessel, and he will not be able to see him as a third of them." Avan who has been in touch with all of his triangular things together with him and his sages
We presume that she deposited it. Meaning, the gift has not yet been acquired by her husband. The Sema says this applies only to a deed of gift accompanied by a kinyan, in which case the documents must be tied together; without a kinyan, siman 243, seif 13, rules that even an isolated deed is returned to the donor. In my view this is incorrect. No kinyan was mentioned, and the Gemara proves that even a deed of gift without a kinyan is not returned to the donor. Siman 243 concerns a case where it is known that the donor deposited it. Nor is the Sema's claimed distinction stated by the Rama in siman 250. Moreover, where a deed contains a kinyan, it is returned to the recipient even when it is known to have come from the donor, because the donor obligated himself from the time of the kinyan, as the Rosh writes. See also the end of siman 250 and siman 56, seif 5.
