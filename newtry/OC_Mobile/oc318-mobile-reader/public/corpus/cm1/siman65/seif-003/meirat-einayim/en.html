The Rosh and Tur write that we are concerned for collusion only with a document that was lost, where it can be said that because some defect had arisen in it the owner was not careful to guard it. With a deposited document, however, no such defect has arisen; rather, the bailee simply does not remember on whose behalf it came into his possession.
And if both of them wish, he shall act according to their instructions. This is also the language of the Rosh, cited by the Tur. The Tur explains: if they reach a compromise between themselves that one will rely upon the other's oath, since the creditor can require the borrower to swear, etc. In the Perishah I wrote that it appears to me that the Tur offered this explanation because the Rosh's wording was difficult. He should not have written "if both of them wish," since one of them certainly wishes, as he is asking for the document. Rather, he should have written, "if they agree with one another." Nor should he have written, "he shall act according to their instructions," but rather, "he shall act according to his instruction," meaning the third party shall act as directed. Although the Mechaber writes "he shall act," the phrase "according to their instructions" remains difficult. Therefore the Tur explains that the reference is to an agreement involving an oath. In that case the consent of both is necessary: the consent of the one who agrees to rely on the other's oath, and the consent of the second to swear rather than telling the first, "You swear that your claim is true and collect." Know that the Tur concludes there: "Therefore, so long as they have not compromised, it shall remain deposited." This wording is difficult, because what novelty is there in saying that it remains deposited? That seems obvious. I explained that he teaches that even according to the Rosh, if they have not agreed that one of them will swear, the document remains deposited and is not returned without an oath even if the other party consents. Although the Rosh writes that here, where there is no defect of loss, we do not suspect collusion, he means only that the document is not to remain permanently deposited to the point that even an oath would be ineffective, as the first opinion holds. Some concern nevertheless remains, requiring one of them to swear; otherwise it remains deposited. According to this, the Mechaber, who records the Rosh's view, should not have abbreviated his words, but should have explained that at least an oath is required.
