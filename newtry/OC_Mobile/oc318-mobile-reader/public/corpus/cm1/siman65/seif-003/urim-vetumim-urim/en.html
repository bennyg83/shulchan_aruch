Because we suspect collusion. The debt may actually have been paid, while the borrower now says it was not paid so that the lender can improperly collect from purchasers. Therefore we do not even return the intact document to the borrower, lest he later hand it to the lender. The dissenters hold that collusion is suspected only where the document was lost and therefore weakened. When it was deposited with a third party and there is no such defect, we do not suspect collusion. It appears to me that even according to the first opinion, this concern applies where the third party is alive but does not know the status of the document, because that itself is some indication of irregularity. But if the third party died and his son simply does not know the status, while both lender and borrower agree what should be done, everyone agrees that we need not suspect collusion because there is no defect at all. In any event Rashba holds that where the son has inherited the deposit, he returns it to the place from which it came. We should not extend the first opinion beyond its stated case. Otherwise no person could ever safely deposit a document with a third party, because after his death no one could recover it. This is clear.
If both of them wish. The third party should act in accordance with their agreement. This is the language of Rosh's responsum: he should not release the document until the truth is clarified, or until they compromise by agreeing that one will trust the other subject to an oath. If the lender says to the borrower, "Swear and be exempt," the borrower must swear. If he refuses, the document is returned to the lender. If he asks for time, he is given the normal time of beit din. If the borrower says to the lender, "Swear and collect," the lender must swear. If he refuses, the document is returned to the borrower. If each party says to the other, "You swear," see below in siman 87. But if both agree to resolve the matter without an oath, Shach holds that there is still concern for collusion and the third party may not release the document to either one without an oath. The later authorities objected to Sema on this point and hold that according to Rosh there is no concern for collusion at all. The third party should therefore act according to whatever compromise they reach, whatever its terms.
