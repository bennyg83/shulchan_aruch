In accordance with what they say. This is the wording of the Rosh. The Tur explains it to mean that if they compromise between themselves so that one will trust the other upon an oath, since the lender can require the borrower to swear, then they may proceed accordingly. According to this, the Mechaber should not have abbreviated the Rosh's words, but should have explained that at least an oath is still required. Thus the Sema. The Shach disagrees and writes that the Rosh means that if both parties wish, the document may be returned to the lender even without an oath. The Bach likewise explains the Tur this way and challenges the Sema. Only when the lender says to the borrower, 'Swear to me,' must the borrower swear, or they may compromise as to who will swear so that the document need not remain deposited. See Responsa of the Rama, siman 33. The Taz also challenges the Sema and explains the Tur differently; see there.
