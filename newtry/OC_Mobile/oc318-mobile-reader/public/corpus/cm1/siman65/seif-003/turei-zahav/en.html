(Seif 7: and similarly if the borrower has unencumbered property.) Maharshal asks why we do not fear that there is a later creditor to whom these encumbered properties are liable, and that by returning this document to the present creditor he will now become earlier in priority, just as we are concerned later in seif 14 regarding a deed of gift or sale. He answers that when the Gemara says here, "it shall be returned," it does not mean literal return. Rather, the creditor collects only from this day onward, the first document is torn up, and if another creditor later comes to collect, he can properly collect from him. What was stated above, that it "shall not be returned," means only with respect to encumbered property. The Gemara distinguishes similarly in the first chapter of Bava Metzia regarding a gift. This explanation, however, does not fit the wording "it shall be returned." Baal HaTerumot, gate 52, also compares this law to a document lacking a kinyan, implying that the document is returned completely. The Tur likewise writes in his lengthy responsum in this siman, "but if the borrower has property, we are not concerned about collusion." It therefore appears to me that Maharshal's difficulty can be resolved differently. Since every document creates publicity, we presume that after this document was written no one else lent to him, because people do not generally know that the document was never delivered to the lender. Even if another person did lend to him, that creditor certainly relied on some other basis. This differs from seif 14, where we fear that he later resold the property to another and accepted responsibility to maintain the field in that buyer's possession, while possessing ample other property to secure that undertaking. See what I write in seif 14.
