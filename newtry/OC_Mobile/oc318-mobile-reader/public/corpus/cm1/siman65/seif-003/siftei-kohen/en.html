There is an opinion that we do not suspect collusion. We suspect collusion only regarding a document that was lost, for its loss impairs its credibility and suggests that, because it contained some defect, its owner was careless in safeguarding it. A deposited document, however, has suffered no such impairment; the depositary merely does not know on whose behalf it came into his possession. If both parties agree, he should act as they direct.
If both agree, he should act as they direct. The Sema cites the Rosh and Tur as explaining that they must compromise by having one trust the other upon an oath, and therefore criticizes the Mechaber for not stating that an oath is at least required. In my view, however, the Mechaber wrote precisely. The Rosh holds that, if both agree, the document may be returned to the creditor even without an oath. The Bach likewise explains the Tur this way and disputes the Sema. The Rosh writes simply that they should act as they direct, without mentioning an oath. The Tur also implies that the matter depends upon the debtor's demand. The creditor may say: swear a hesset oath, or let my document be returned to me. The debtor may instead require the creditor to swear that he was not paid, or demand return of the document to himself rather than leave it needlessly with a third party. The responsum of the Rosh states that the document remains deposited until the truth is clarified or they compromise as to which party will be believed on oath. If the creditor requires the debtor to swear that he paid, the debtor must swear hesset; if he refuses, the document is returned to the creditor. If the debtor asks time to bring witnesses, he is given thirty days, after which the document is returned if he fails. Conversely, if the debtor requires the creditor to swear that he was not paid, the creditor must swear even if the document contains a clause of credibility, since it is not in his possession. If he refuses, the document is returned to the debtor. Thus, when the parties admit to one another, their joint direction is followed even without an oath.
