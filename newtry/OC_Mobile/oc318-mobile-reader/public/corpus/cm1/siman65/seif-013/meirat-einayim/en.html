And in a place where the witnesses by their signatures acquire for him, etc. Here too, the document is a benefit for the slave because it frees him. This language of the Rama was also written by the Beit Yosef both here and regarding a found deed of sale or gift, whose law is explained in the next seif. The Rama also cites it in Darkei Moshe. One cannot explain the words here, "and in a place where the witnesses by their signatures acquire for him," as referring simply to the opinion mentioned in Darkei Moshe that holds this generally. If that were the meaning, he should have written, "according to the one who holds that the witnesses by their signatures acquire for him." Why did he write "and in a place"? Moreover, he concludes, "as explained above in siman 39," yet there the Shulchan Aruch does not record such a view at all. On the contrary, in seif 3 there it rules without qualification like the Rif and Rambam that the witnesses' signatures do not acquire for him. We must therefore say that the phrase means a case where the document expressly states that the acquisition is effective from now, as the Rama writes there, or where the witnesses immediately acquired possession of the document on behalf of the creditor so that it entered the creditor's legal domain at once, which is as though the creditor himself acquired it, as I explained there in the Sema. See there carefully. See also the beginning of siman 125 concerning an agent who acquires a document on another's behalf.
