To the slave. Since the document supports his admission, for the master admits it, we are not concerned for collusion. Sema.
Proof. Since the document was impaired by having been lost, there is concern for collusion, that he does not truly intend to free him but does this so that the slave may collect from purchasers who bought after the date of the writing. Therefore, it is insufficient for him merely to bring proof that it came into his possession before it was lost, for there would still be concern for collusion. Thus writes the Sema. The Shach writes: It appears from his words that even from the time it was seen in his possession he may collect only from the time of the loss onward. In my view, however, his words are incorrect, for the Gemara and poskim imply that whenever he brings proof that the bill of manumission was in his possession, he may collect from the time it was seen in his possession, since from then onward there is no concern for collusion. See there.
And where. The Sema writes that Rama's phrase, 'and where its witnesses,' does not mean according to the authority who holds this view, for if so Rama should have said, 'according to the authority who holds that the witnesses, by their signatures...' Moreover, Rama concludes, 'as stated in siman 39,' while there the Shulchan Aruch mentions no such view. On the contrary, there in seif 13 it rules without qualification that the witnesses do not acquire for him through their signatures. Therefore, it appears that Rama's words here, 'and where...' refer to a case where the document expressly states 'from now,' as Rama writes there in the gloss, or where the witnesses immediately acquired the document on behalf of the lender so that it should at once be in his possession, as stated there. See also the beginning of siman 125 regarding an agent who acquires a document on another's behalf. See also siman 39, seif 13, what the Shach writes there, and what was cited in his name briefly there in seif katan 26. The Taz writes: It appears that this means that if it is known that the bill of manumission once came into his possession before it was lost, a case in which the Tur writes in siman 39 that the witnesses acquire for him through their signatures, no further proof is required. For even if it did not come into his possession on the day it was written, he nevertheless acquires retroactively.
