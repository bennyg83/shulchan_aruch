However, if the slave comes. Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe say that the requirement to prove when the deed of emancipation reached the slave applies only according to the view that witnesses do not confer rights through their signatures; according to Abaye, the witnesses acquire for him from the time of signing. They therefore question the Rosh and Tur. In my view, the Rosh holds that deeds of emancipation differ from ordinary monetary documents. Ordinary documents are principally written to create monetary rights against purchasers, so according to Abaye the witnesses confer that right from signing. A divorce or emancipation document, however, is principally written to effect a change in personal status, not to collect property. Purchasers therefore assume that the holder must prove when it reached his possession. The Gemara itself distinguishes a divorce document from ordinary documents on this basis. Thus it is reasonable that even Abaye agrees that the witnesses do not confer retroactive collection rights through such a document until delivery. The Rif similarly limits Abaye's rule to liens, not gifts or sales. Nevertheless, as a matter of final law, it appears that the Rosh merely copied the Rif's language without adopting it, as he sometimes does.
He must bring proof. The Tur's reason is that the document was impaired by having been lost. This implies that with an ordinary deed of emancipation or divorce that was not lost, no such proof is required. Tosafot, the Rosh, Rashba, and Ran likewise reject Rashi's view that every such document requires proof of delivery before collecting from purchasers. A document delivered later than its date becomes publicly known, so purchasers are protected. See siman 43, seif katan 33.
To bring proof. The Sema explains that because the document was impaired by falling, we suspect collusion and require proof that the master truly intended emancipation. He appears to conclude that even after witnesses saw the deed in the slave's possession, collection begins only from the time it was found. This is incorrect. The Gemara and authorities imply that once there is proof of possession, the slave collects from that time onward. The only concern is that the deed may not have been delivered at its stated date. If witnesses establish when it reached him, there is no continuing concern of collusion. Indeed, where witnesses saw it in his possession before it was lost, the loss no longer impairs it, for emancipation had already taken effect. Even if it later fell from the master, that is irrelevant, since after emancipation the parties may do with the deed as they wish. Because a deed delivered after its date generates publicity, we ordinarily presume timely delivery. The Tur's phrase requiring proof that it reached him from the date written means that if he seeks collection from that date he must prove delivery then; otherwise he collects only from the proven date of delivery.
Where his witnesses acquire for him. This means where the document expressly says 'from now,' as the Rama explains in siman 39. According to what I wrote there, however, the Mechaber and those who follow Rav Assi draw no distinction based on 'from now.' According to Abaye, the witnesses acquire for him through their signatures here as well.
