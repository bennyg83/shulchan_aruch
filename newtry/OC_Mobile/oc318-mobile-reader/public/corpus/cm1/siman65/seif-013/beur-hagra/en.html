(Supplement) From the date. The wording is not exact. It was used because the earlier clause says, Nevertheless, and later says, after. The Gemara asks when the document reached his hand, but does not require proof that it arrived specifically on the date it was written. The Shach likewise explains this. His further proof is incorrect, because the Gemara's concern is not that the document was written and never delivered, but that it was written in Nisan with an earlier date. The holder may have failed to guard it because an antedated document is invalid, and this is the defect created by its having fallen. The same concern is stated regarding a promissory note on Bava Metzia 13a. Although one might still suspect antedating even after proof of delivery, an antedated document is invalid only by rabbinic decree, so such a remote concern is not entertained. With respect to purchasers whose acquisitions preceded the actual delivery, however, the concern is real. The phrase from the date is therefore merely the ordinary case. In any event he must bring witnesses showing when the document reached his hand. (End of supplement.)
