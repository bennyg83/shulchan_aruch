It should be returned to the slave. Sema says that because the deed supports the master's admission, we do not fear collusion. I do not understand his wording, because here there is ordinarily no independent collusion concern once we require the slave to bring proof of prior receipt. Perhaps Sema means that the slave may have owned property and owed another creditor, and backdating the emancipation would protect those assets from collection. But that too is included in the requirement that the slave produce proof, just like proof concerning sold assets or the master's creditors. Since the deed fell, everything remains presumptively under the master's ownership until proof is brought.
To the slave. Bach writes that even if the emancipation deed is authenticated, if the master does not admit delivery we should not return it, because perhaps the master authenticated it intending to give it and later changed his mind. I do not know what basis he has for this. Raavad in the laws of divorce says that even a torn get can be valid if authenticated because the woman herself authenticates her get, not the husband, and Maggid Mishneh derives this from the rule that a creditor authenticates his deed rather than the borrower. Rashba agrees. Rambam also appears consistent with this and Beit Yosef cites it in Even HaEzer 128. All the more should this apply to an emancipation deed. Bach's ruling therefore has no basis.

From the date written in it. Sema understands that proof merely showing the emancipation deed was in the slave's possession before it fell is insufficient, because perhaps he received it many days after its date and could wrongfully collect or defeat earlier rights. He requires proof that the slave received it on the very day it was written. Shach disagrees and says any proof that it was in his hands before it fell suffices. After lengthy analysis in Tumim I conclude like Shach.
Where witnesses' signatures acquire for him. Sema explains that Rama does not mean the authorities who generally follow Abaye that signed witnesses acquire automatically. Rama did not accept that as general law. Rather, he refers to cases described above in siman 39:13, such as where the grantor said “from now” or expressly instructed the witnesses to acquire for the recipient. See Tumim. The first example is not correct for emancipation, because “from now” does not free the slave until the emancipation document reaches him. The second is correct, and indeed if the master told the witnesses to acquire for the slave, then even if the master now denies, the deed is returned without further proof because the slave became free immediately and the master could no longer retract. I explain Rama instead regarding an oral creditor of the master seeking to collect fruits of property acquired by the slave between the written date and the later return of the deed. As to those fruits, all agree that the witnesses' signatures can operate retroactively in the relevant sense, so the slave need not bring additional proof. Thus even if physical delivery occurred only much later, the slave can be treated as entitled to the fruits from the earlier date. See Tumim.
