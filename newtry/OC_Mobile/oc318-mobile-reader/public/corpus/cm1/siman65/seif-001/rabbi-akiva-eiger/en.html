[Sema, subparagraph 58] We do not return it to the lender. Note: See Responsa Penei Yehoshua, siman 7, who concludes that even if the borrower does not admit the debt, the lender nevertheless collects through his document. The rule that the document is not returned means that perhaps it will later be established that the receipt is genuine. The Sema too speaks of this case: even where the borrower admits, the document is not returned, and the law remains as where the borrower does not admit, yet the lender collects through the document. See there. Compare above, siman 19, regarding a third party custodian, where the Shulchan Aruch implies that collection is not made through the debt document. According to Penei Yehoshua, one must say that the Sema too distinguishes between a document that came to a third party from the lender, where its validity is more impaired, and one that merely fell from the lender, as the Shach distinguishes below in subparagraph 69 according to his own approach.
