Not with him. Between the borrower and the mortgage bill, he sold and waited, and every bill:
It shall remain deposited. The shalish may later say, "I remembered," and then we follow his statement. If one witness knows what the matter is and testifies that the note belongs to the lender, the borrower is obligated to a Torah oath. If the borrower admits the witness's statement that at the time of the shalish arrangement it belonged to the lender, but claims that he later paid or that they subsequently made a new condition in the shalish's presence, he is one who is obligated to swear and cannot swear, and therefore must pay. If the witness testifies that it belongs to the borrower, the single witness is disregarded and the note remains deposited. See Beurim, seif katan 1.
He could have collected. The Sema writes that the lender must take an oath like that required against one witness testifying that a document was paid. The Shach disagrees. Since the shalish is not expressly testifying that the note was paid, his having once held it does not prove payment, because the note may still be valid. Moreover, the lender's denial is no worse than if he admitted the shalish's possession but asserted with certainty that the debt remained due, in which case he would not need to swear. All the more so where he denies the shalish's statement. The Shach leaves it as requiring study, and this is the primary ruling.
To return it. The Shach asks why the lender should not be believed in denying the shalish, just as in siman 58 one who faces one witness testifying to payment may claim that the money applied to another debt. He explains that here the lender himself does not make a definite claim and is uncertain whether payment occurred, so the migo does not apply. See Beurim, seif katan 3, for the correct rationale why the migo is unavailable here, and why this does not resemble seif 16.
A claim. Meaning, not a claim that he holds it as collateral, because perhaps the borrower deposited it with him only after the entire debt had already been paid. Nor may he claim purchase, because the note itself reveals that it initially came to him as collateral. This applies specifically to a non-acquisition note, according to those who hold in siman 39:13 that one does not write a note for a borrower in the absence of the lender. According to those who allow such writing, or with a note containing a kinyan, he may claim purchase after maintaining possession for three years, because perhaps the mortgage document was written before the actual loan. If the date of the note is later than the beginning of the three years of possession, and three years have not yet passed from the date of the note itself, the note functions as a protest and he cannot claim purchase. See Beurim, seif katan 4.
To the owners. Although he presently possesses the land, because there is a defect in his claim we say that land remains in the possession of its original owner.
Himself. Meaning, if the father claims that the borrower still owes him money, he may collect with the note. If he says, "I do not know," it was explained in siman 59 that he must return the note.
