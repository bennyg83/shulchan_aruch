Any claim. See Baer Heitev and Netivot HaMishpat. See also Dagul Merevavah, who writes: This applies where it is known that the land came into his possession as a mortgage. But if the possessor claims that it never came to him as a mortgage and that he purchased the land, then, if he has held it for the three years required for a chazakah, he is believed, and the existence of a mortgage deed does not weaken his claim at all, for perhaps the seller acted deceptively by writing a mortgage deed. This is also implied by the Rosh, who writes, ‘since it is known that it came into his possession as a mortgage’; the Shulchan Aruch abbreviated the point. It further appears to me that if there are no witnesses that it came into his possession as a mortgage, but he claims that afterward he purchased it outright, or he claims that the other party deposited the mortgage deed, whether he is believed through a migo requires distinction. In my view, the claim of outright purchase is believed through a migo; see siman 146, seif 25. Regarding the mortgage claim, I am uncertain whether this is considered a migo used to extract property, since he admits that the body of the land is not his. See below, siman 130, seif 4.
