But if the lender says definitely that the debt remains owed, Shach would believe him through a migo that he could have denied delivery to the escrow holder. He permits a migo that contradicts one witness where the doctrine of one who cannot swear and therefore pays does not apply, and he allows even an audacious migo in monetary law. Under his view, if the escrow holder returned the deed in the presence of witnesses, the issue depends on the dispute later in seif 16 where the lender makes a definite claim. In Tumim I conclude like Sema: even if the lender makes a definite claim, because the instrument had been entrusted to an escrow holder it must be returned and cannot be collected upon.
