(Collected note) Likewise... The Tosefta, chapter 1, states: "Documents of sale, sharecropping, and contracting, he shall return neither to this one nor to that one; judgments and prozbul documents he shall return, because they are written in his name, whether he found them in the marketplace or among his father's documents. The rule is: if there are receipts with them, he shall act according to the receipts." What is stated at the end of the third chapter of Sanhedrin applies specifically because she said it was paid, and there the question was returning the document to the borrower, unlike here. In siman 243, seif 13, where it says, "one who...," the case is one where it is known that the document was deposited by the giver, as explained in seif 5 of this siman and for that reason. Otherwise, not, as stated in seif 14 and in the Gemara there 19a regarding wills, etc. The same applies in the heir's possession, as stated in the Tosefta regarding wills, apotiki documents, and gifts, whether found in the marketplace or among inherited papers. All this concerns a gift without a kinyan. Where there was a kinyan, it is returned to the recipient, as stated in seif 14. This is the meaning of siman 243, seif 12 regarding a document, and siman 250, seif 25 in the gloss regarding a gift. (End of note.)

However, if... There, 7b, because a kesubah is not ordinarily paid while the marriage continues. Even according to the Rabbis, who are concerned for two kesubah documents, that applies when one was lost, since a woman does not remain married without a kesubah. Even according to the first answer there, which invokes concern for a bundle of money set aside for payment, Ritva explains that although generally we do not suspect such a bundle, as in Bava Batra 5b, with a kesubah we do because she will not remain without a kesubah and he may have designated property for her, as in Kesubos 7a. That does not apply here.
(Collected note) Nevertheless... See there 7b. Hagahos Maimoniyos, citing Ri, rules like Ravina's answer and like Rabbi Yose. Therefore we are concerned for repayment of a debt document, as in seif 6, and regarding a kesubah the ruling follows Rabbi Yose that while she remains married, etc. Nimukei Yosef, citing Rabbeinu Chananel, rules concerning a kesubah like the Rabbis, since the halachah follows the Rabbis against Rabbi Yose. Hagahos Maimoniyos raises this difficulty. Yet concerning a debt document, the sugya follows Rabbi Yose, whereas here the halachah follows the Rabbis because of the concern for two kesubah documents, as Rava also holds there 21a. Rambam, at the end of Hilchos Gezeilah, halachah 12, rules according to Rav Pappa's answer, the Rabbis, and Rav Pappa, because the sugya establishing that we are concerned for repayment works well according to him. He writes that even if both parties agree, it is not returned, because he follows Tosafos that the opposing view assumes no concern for collusion, which we do not accept. Alternatively, Ritva's explanation in the case of seif 6 applies. Thus, "nevertheless..." means that not only according to Hagahos Maimoniyos, but even according to Nimukei Yosef and Ritva in the name of Rabbeinu Chananel, who rule like the Rabbis, they explicitly write that the concern is not repayment itself but the possibility of two kesubah documents, specifically when one was lost because she would not remain without a kesubah. The same applies where a receipt was written. See there. They write that the rule of collecting in the debtor's absence differs because near death or departure one commonly sets aside property for payment. See the Rosh, first chapter of Bava Batra, siman 69, and the answer of Ritzba. Even according to Rambam, the concern applies specifically because the document was lost, as explained in seif 6 and at the end of siman 41, where he writes that if it was deposited, etc. This is the meaning of seif 5 regarding her dowry document, there for collecting from her father who had not yet given it, and the Rosh writes that it was given so she could collect whenever she wished.
