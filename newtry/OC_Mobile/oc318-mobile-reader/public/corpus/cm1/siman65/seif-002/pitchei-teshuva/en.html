If, however, the present possessor claims that he never held it as a mortgage but purchased the land, then if he has possessed it for the full period of chazakah he is believed, and the mortgage document does not weaken his claim at all. Perhaps the seller acted deceitfully in writing a mortgage document. This is also implied by the Rosh, who writes, 'since it is known that it came into his possession as a mortgage,' while Shulchan Aruch abbreviated the matter. It further appears to me that if there are no witnesses that it came to him as a mortgage, but he admits the original mortgage and claims that he later purchased the property outright, or alternatively claims that he himself deposited the mortgage document, the question is whether he is believed by virtue of a migo. In my opinion, a claim of outright purchase is believed through migo; see siman 146, seif 25. As to a claim involving continued mortgage rights, I am uncertain whether this is considered a migo that extracts, since he admits that the land itself is not his. See below, siman 130, seif 4.
