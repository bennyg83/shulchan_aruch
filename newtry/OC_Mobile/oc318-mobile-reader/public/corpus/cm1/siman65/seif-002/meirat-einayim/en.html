Likewise, the heirs of the bailee, etc. This teaches us that one might otherwise have thought that only when the bailee is alive, where there is an evident deficiency in that he does not know on whose behalf the creditor named in the document deposited it with him, do we apply this rule. With his heirs, however, who could not be expected to know, one might have thought that they should return it to the creditor. It therefore teaches otherwise. There is an additional novelty regarding heirs, as I wrote in the Perishah in the name of the Rashba. This requires study, because in siman 243 seif 13 the Mechaber rules concerning a deed of gift found with the heirs of a bailee that they should return it to the heirs of the giver. It is difficult to distinguish by saying that here it is not known from whose hands the document came to the bailee, whereas there it is known that the giver handed it to him but it is unknown in what manner. Perhaps the distinction is that with a gift it is possible that the recipient never acquired the gift and the deed never reached him, for the case there concerns a deed of gift without a formal act of acquisition. Therefore it is returned to the giver's heirs. If, however, the deed records an act of acquisition, it is returned to the recipient, as the Rama states explicitly at the end of siman 250. A loan is different, because the valid debt instrument before us indicates that the loan occurred, and our concern is only that perhaps the borrower subsequently paid, as the Mechaber states explicitly at the beginning of this siman. Therefore the heirs return it to neither party.

The correct reading is: Nevertheless, if the heir produces a ketubah document of a woman that had been deposited with the deceased bailee, etc. The word "deposited," in the masculine form, refers to the document.
He should return it to the woman, etc. The reason is that a ketubah is not ordinarily due for payment until after the husband's death. The Ritva concludes there that precisely this consideration shows that the heir is not believed to say that the woman sold him the ketubah by writing and delivery, even according to the Rif and Rambam, or through acquisition together with land, or that it was pledged to him. The Rosh, cited by the Mechaber below in seif 5, writes that a dowry document that is found has the same status as a debt instrument. It appears to me that he speaks specifically of the dowry, which she gave him when she entered the marriage, because that is more readily subject to payment. A ketubah is different, because he may not pay it to her during his lifetime and then remain married to her without a ketubah, lest it become too easy in his eyes to divorce her, as explained in Even HaEzer siman 100. See the glosses at the end of seif 2 there and siman 102 seif 5.
