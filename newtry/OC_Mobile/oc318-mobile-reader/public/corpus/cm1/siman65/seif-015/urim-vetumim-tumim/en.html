And some say, and so forth. Rambam and Rif omit this entire rule and simply hold that the parties always divide. Maggid Mishneh and Kesef Mishneh already wondered why and suggested that Rambam had a different text of the Gemara. Shach labored to reconcile the omission, but his explanation is strained. In my opinion Rambam follows Rashi's interpretation there. The holder of the essential part of the document has an advantage over the holder of the lower part because the date has value, even though the latter holds the signed witnesses. This assumes that one who admits having written the document need not authenticate it. Tosafot challenge Rashi, and earlier authorities cited by Or Zarua answer their objections. Rambam can therefore follow Rashi. Tosafot also explain that when we appraise the relative value of the two pieces, we consider only a feature whose removal does not completely invalidate the document. Thus the date can be removed and the document still remains valid, though it loses purchaser-facing force. But something whose removal destroys the document entirely is not separately appraised. Now consider the lower portion containing two witnesses. We cannot value the possibility of removing both signatures entirely, because that would destroy the document. But where the borrower admits the document, one could remove one witness and leave the other. The note would still be valid against the borrower, though it would no longer collect from purchasers. Rambam rules above in siman 51 that a note with one witness is effective against a borrower who admits the document. Thus the lower portion has exactly the same type of purchaser-facing enhancement as the upper portion containing the date. Removing the date from the upper portion loses collection from purchasers; removing one of the two witnesses from the lower portion also loses collection from purchasers. There is therefore no special extra value in the upper portion. That is our halachah, because we rule that one who admits having written a document must nevertheless authenticate it before using it against purchasers. But according to the view that admission of the document makes authentication unnecessary even against purchasers, the lower portion retains full purchaser-facing force despite removal of one witness, while removal of the date from the upper portion destroys that force. Then the date has a genuine additional value. Accordingly, the Gemara's valuation rule is stated according to Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, who holds that an admitted document need not be authenticated. Under our halachah, where authentication is still needed for purchaser collection, the special value of the date disappears. Rif, Rambam, and Baal HaIttur therefore omitted the rule. This is correct, and no other distinct enhancement is stated in the Gemara that would require a different result.
