(Seif 21: On one of his documents, in his own handwriting.) The mention of the creditor’s handwriting is a greater novelty. If the notation were in another person’s hand or the debtor’s hand, we would certainly follow it. Even when it is in the creditor’s own hand, though one might say he prepared it for later delivery or wrote it so as not to appear wealthy, the ruling teaches that the document is treated as paid. Maharshal says that another person’s handwriting is not followed, but this is astonishing. Once the instrument has been impaired in any manner, it is treated as paid.
