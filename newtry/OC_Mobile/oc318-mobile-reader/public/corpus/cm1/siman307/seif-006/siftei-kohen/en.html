The first view appears primary. The Sema distinguishes between an injury that arose by itself and other loss of use, but that distinction is incorrect. In both cases the issue is compensation for loss of use. The Rosh and Mordechai explain that according to the exempting view, the rule that emaciation caused by work is exempt must concern permanent emaciation; otherwise temporary emaciation would be exempt even when not caused by work. Rabbeinu Yerucham likewise states that the dispute regarding an injury applies equally to emaciation. Thus the Rama correctly understood that the Tur favors the first view, and Maharshal, Beit Yosef, and Bach read the Tur similarly. Nevertheless, the Darkei Moshe's statement that Maharam agreed with the exempting view is difficult; Maharam appears to impose liability. Maharshal also ruled in practice that one is liable, and the abbreviated Tosafot state this without qualification. Since Tosafot, the Rosh, and Ri did not decide the matter, it should be treated as unresolved law, and the burden of proof rests upon the claimant. Where there is only loss of use without bodily damage, such as locking the animal in a room so that it cannot work, he is exempt.
