The first view appears primary. The Shach leaves the issue unresolved because Maharam holds the custodian liable and Tosafot, Rosh, and Ri did not clearly decide. In my view Tosafot actually indicates plainly that liability exists. Tosafot in Gittin distinguishes a robber from a custodian. If an animal becomes temporarily weakened in the robber's hands and will recover, the robber can say, "Here is yours before you," because no lasting change occurred. But a custodian who accepted responsibility can be liable even for temporary weakening caused by negligence. Tosafot asks why a direct tortfeasor should not likewise be liable when he weakens another's animal even if it will recover. This demonstrates that Tosafot follows Rabbi Chaim's position that temporary bodily weakening is itself damage. The same is true of a custodian who negligently causes such weakening. Sema's attempt to distinguish weakness that occurred by itself from weakness caused by negligence is therefore unnecessary. Under Tosafot, where the weakening was caused through actionable conduct, liability exists whether by direct damage or custodial negligence. See siman 340.
