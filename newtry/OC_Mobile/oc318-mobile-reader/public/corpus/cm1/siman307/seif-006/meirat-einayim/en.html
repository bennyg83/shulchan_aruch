There is no payment for loss of labor with an animal. Of the five heads of damages applicable to injury of a person, three, pain, loss of labor, and humiliation, do not apply to injury of an animal. Only the fourth relevant category, actual damage, applies.
And some obligate him. The Tur explains that only with a person do we treat a temporary injury that will heal merely as loss of labor, because a person is not ordinarily standing for sale and therefore his monetary value is not reduced. An animal, however, is always potentially for sale, and if one were to sell it while it bears this injury it would certainly be worth less. Therefore the reduction is regarded as actual damage and the tortfeasor is liable.
The first view appears primary. It appears that Rama ruled this way because the Tur himself states without qualification at the beginning of siman 340 that where the flesh of an animal was weakened but will recover, the borrower is exempt. He apparently holds that the two laws depend on one another. The Mechaber likewise rules that way there in Shulchan Aruch, and Rama writes the same. In the Derishah, however, I wrote that in my humble opinion this conclusion is not compelling. Since the Tur cites the view that obligates here as the final opinion, how could he state categorically at the beginning of siman 340 that one is exempt in such a case? I distinguished there that siman 340 concerns weakness that arose by itself. Although it deals with a borrower, who is ordinarily liable even for accidents and for damage arising by itself, when the animal will fully recover this is regarded only as loss of labor, for which there is no payment concerning an animal.
