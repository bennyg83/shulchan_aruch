Shach, seif katan 2: and so it is stated in the Gemara; see the Yerushalmi. Note: This is according to the view that because a person does not want his deposit to be in another's hands, one who transfers it to another bailee is treated as negligent and is liable to pay. According to the halacha, however, although it is prohibited to lend another person's property without the owner's knowledge, one is not for that reason regarded as negligent for purposes of monetary liability. We can therefore properly explain the case even where he violated the rule and lent it without the owner's knowledge. This is explicit in Ritva's novellae, cited in Shitah Mekubetzet in the sugya of one who rents a cow, and likewise in the Rosh in the sugya of a bailee who transfers to another bailee. See there what he writes in the name of Rif. The same appears in Rambam's Commentary to the Mishnah.
