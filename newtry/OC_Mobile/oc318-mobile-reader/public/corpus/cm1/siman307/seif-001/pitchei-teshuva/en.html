One who rents a cow, etc. See Shitah Mekubetzet, Bava Metzia 35b, on the Mishnah concerning one who rents a cow and lends it to another. In the name of the Ritva it states that a gratuitous or paid guardian who has no right to use the deposit and nevertheless rents or lends it is liable even for unavoidable accidents, as one who misappropriates a deposit. This is why the Mishnah specifically speaks of a renter and a borrower. Beit Yosef cites this below in siman 342, as does the Shach there. Shevut Yaakov III:148 discusses one who gave an ox to a gratuitous guardian, who transferred it to a paid guardian, and it was stolen or lost. He argued from the Mishnah that Rabbi Yose concedes here, because the first guardian paid from his own pocket for superior guarding, and therefore any benefit is commerce with his own money. I find this surprising, for Tosafot, Bava Kamma 11b, explicitly state that when a paid guardian pays, he pays the owners, because the halachah follows Rabbi Yose that one may not profit from another's animal. Shitah Mekubetzet states likewise. Shitah Mekubetzet also discusses whether the owners must reimburse the first guardian for the fee paid to the second. Mahari Katz exempts the owners, since the enhanced guarding was undertaken for the first guardian's own convenience, even if the owners ultimately benefited. R. Moshe of Saxony, however, requires the owner to pay because the guarding was improved and may save him loss. Divrei Chaim, laws of guardians, end of chapter 7, likewise questions Shevut Yaakov from Tosafot and also questions requiring reimbursement where there was no actual theft or loss, since mere enhancement of guarding is no better than driving away a lion.
