Until he performs one of the modes of acquisition. In the chapter HaShoel it is stated that just as Chazal instituted meshichah for purchasers, they instituted meshichah for bailees. Rambam's position is that meshichah operates both to prevent the owner who rents or lends from retracting and to create the bailee's liability for safeguarding. Tosafos and the Rosh, however, hold that meshichah by bailees does not prevent the lender or owner from retracting, but the bailees become liable for safeguarding immediately once the owners withdraw from guarding the property. The reasoning of Tosafos and the Rosh appears to be that a bailee has the law of a worker: once workers begin their work, that beginning itself functions as an acquisition, as explained at the beginning of chapter HaOmanin and in Tur and Shulchan Aruch, siman 332. Nimukei Yosef in chapter HaShoel cites an interpretation that the institution of meshichah for bailees applies only to a borrower and a renter, who acquire a right in the body of the object, and meshichah is therefore meaningful for them. For an unpaid or paid bailee, who acquires no right in the body of the object, meshichah should not help. Ramban, however, holds that meshichah is effective because it is comparable to the beginning of work, which serves as an acquisition for workers, and therefore meshichah is effective even for an unpaid or paid bailee. This is difficult. If so, why is meshichah necessary? Workers need no separate acquisition once they have begun their work. Here too, once the bailees have begun guarding and the owners have withdrawn from guarding, that should itself constitute the beginning of their service, as Tosafos and the Rosh maintain. Perhaps Ramban holds that the essential beginning of guarding occurs only once they draw the object into their own domain, and that before they do so there has not yet been a beginning of guarding. In my opinion, however, meshichah by an unpaid or paid bailee, even though he acquires nothing in the object, is a formal act that obligates him for the object according to the liability of his category of bailee, just as a thief or robber becomes liable for unavoidable accidents from the time he performs meshichah even though he acquires no legitimate ownership. Ramban, however, follows his own position, adopting the latter version in the chapter Eizehu Neshech that bailees become obligated only from the time of negligence, as cited by the Shach in siman 341. They are therefore unlike a thief or robber, whose liability begins at the time of the theft. Meshichah can readily create liability in a thief or robber from the time of meshichah. But if a bailee does not become liable until a later act of negligence, an earlier meshichah is like saying, 'Draw this cow now, but you shall acquire it only after thirty days,' where the original meshichah has already ended. One must therefore explain the meshichah of a bailee as equivalent to beginning the work. According to Rambam, however, who follows the first version at the beginning of chapter Eilu Na'arot that bailees become liable from the time of meshichah, meshichah may straightforwardly be understood as the act that creates each bailee's liability according to his law, just as it creates the thief's and robber's liability for unavoidable accidents. Examine this carefully.
