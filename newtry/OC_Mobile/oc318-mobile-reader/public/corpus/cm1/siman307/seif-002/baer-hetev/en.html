Negligent. This is not precise. It is worse than negligence, because the damaging force is directly his and it is considered as though he damaged with his own hands. Otherwise, since houses and land are exempt even for negligence, as stated in siman 301, he would not be liable. Accordingly, the phrase "he rented a house" is also not precise; the same law applies to another person who is not a tenant. The point is to teach that even a tenant, who presumably rented the premises to use them as he wishes, is liable. This also resolves the Sema's distinction from one who breaks a fence before another's animal, who is exempt under human law, because there the damage comes by itself, whereas here the damage is caused by his grain and is regarded as his own force and direct agency. See Responsa Rashdam, simanim 266, 371, and 372. Shach.
