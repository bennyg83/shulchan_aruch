A renter may not, etc. The Tur writes: All the laws of a paid custodian regarding oaths, payment, and acquisition of double payment also apply to a renter. Just as a paid custodian may not transfer the item to another custodian, so too a renter may not rent it to another. If he does so, he is liable even for unavoidable accidents unless the second custodian has proof that would exempt the first. The meaning is that the second must have evidence that he did not deviate from the permitted use. The second is not believed by oath alone concerning that. The first renter, however, is believed by oath if he says, "I saw that the second renter did not deviate," and he is thereby exempt. The statement that the first is liable even for unavoidable accidents means that even if witnesses prove the animal suffered an accident while in the second person's hand, so long as there are no witnesses that the second did not deviate from the permitted use, we suspect that he did change the use and thereby placed the animal in a status resembling unauthorized use. Although we do not ordinarily suspect a person of deliberate unauthorized use without evidence, we do suspect negligence or deviation in use where death may have resulted from it. The Mechaber phrases the law as that of a paid custodian who transfers to another custodian because with a paid custodian there is an additional issue if he lowers the level of guarding by giving the item to an unpaid custodian. That issue does not exist in the same way where an unpaid custodian gives it to another unpaid custodian. See siman 291:27.
