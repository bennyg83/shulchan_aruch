The borrower pays the renter. See Shach, seif katan 4, who notes that sometimes the owner can end up paying several cows. At first glance this seems difficult, because when a borrower pays for a cow that died he deducts the value of the days for which it was borrowed, as Nimukei Yosef explains at the end of HaShoel. The distinction is between a rental of "a cow" generally and rental of "this cow." Suppose a cow worth one hundred is rented for one hundred days, with the full rental period worth ten, and then the owner borrows it back for ninety days. If it dies, the borrower pays ninety-nine, deducting only the one unit corresponding to the ten days that remained rented. But if the original rental was of a generic cow, the owner must also provide the renter with another cow for the remaining rental days, because the obligation was to supply a cow for the full term. If he pays the ninety-nine with another cow, that replacement cow becomes the renter's payment-property, and the owner must still provide an additional cow for the outstanding ten days of rental. If the original rental was specifically "this cow," there is no such further duty to provide another animal. One simply deducts from the death payment the value of the remaining borrowing days and pays the balance in money or movable property. Shitah Mekubetzet likewise indicates that the rule of multiple cows applies only to a generic-cow rental.
