Primary. The Sema explains that this ruling was apparently adopted because the Tur himself rules without qualification at the beginning of siman 340 that a borrower is exempt where an animal's flesh became emaciated but will recover. He understood the two laws to depend upon one another, and the Mechaber and Rama rule likewise there. Yet this does not appear correct to the Sema. Since the Tur here cites the opinion imposing liability last, how could he rule without qualification in siman 340 that one is exempt in such a case? Rather, one can distinguish: there the animal weakened on its own. Although the discussion there concerns a borrower, who is liable even for unavoidable accidents that occur on their own, temporary weakness that will heal is merely loss of use, for which there is no liability with respect to an animal. The Shach writes that this distinction is not correct, because in both cases the loss is ultimately loss of use. This is also evident from the Rosh in chapter HaShoel and the Mordechai in chapter HaChovel, who write that according to the exempting opinion the statement that weakness caused by labor is exempt must refer to weakness that will not heal. If the Sema were correct, the same qualification would be necessary even according to the opinion imposing liability. Rabbeinu Yerucham explicitly states that the same dispute governing a wound also governs loss of flesh. The Darkei Moshe therefore correctly understood the Tur in siman 340 as deciding in favor of the first opinion, as is also explicit in the Rosh's Kitzur Piskei HaRosh. However, the Darkei Moshe's statement that Maharam agreed with that view in the Mordechai is difficult, because Maharam there actually imposes liability. The practical law requires study, since it is difficult to disagree with Maharam, and Maharshal likewise ruled in practice that one is liable. Piskei Tosafot, chapter HaSholeach, also rules without qualification that one is liable. It therefore appears to be a doubt of law, and the burden of proof rests upon the claimant, especially since Tosafot, the Rosh, and Rabbeinu Yerucham did not decide the dispute. This is unlike the Rama, who writes that the first view appears primary. Maharshal further writes that pure loss of use without damage, such as locking the animal in a room so that it cannot work, remains exempt, as stated by the Mordechai at the beginning of HaGozel Kama; see there.
