A transaction made through a prohibition. The authorities ask why the rule that an act performed against the Torah is ineffective does not invalidate a sale conducted on Shabbat. The Shach distinguishes a prohibition intrinsic to the transaction from one where the same transaction could have been performed permissibly at another time. That distinction does not answer all the Gemara's cases, such as one who separated terumah before first fruits, where the same separation could certainly have been done permissibly in the proper order. Sema cites Tosafot that Rava's rule applies to prohibitions created by the Torah, not to a prohibition a person created upon himself by oath. Rama in Yoreh Deah nevertheless rules that if one swore not to sell and sold, the sale is ineffective. The Tosafot passage must be read more precisely. Even Rava agrees that a divorce given through a prohibition can release the woman from the husband's domain, because divorce is compared to death. The issue is whether the prohibited act must be undone or reversed. In the case of a rapist who divorces the woman he was required to retain, the get removes her from him, but the Torah commands him to take her back. Rava's principle governs that remedial consequence. If a person merely created a prohibition for himself by oath, once he violated the oath and the prohibited act has already been done, there is no continuing Torah command requiring the act to be reversed. It is like one who swore to eat a loaf on a certain day and allowed the day to pass. The oath was violated, but no continuing duty remains that can invalidate the accomplished result. Thus Tosafot did not mean that self imposed prohibitions are irrelevant to every legal act. They meant that where the act is inherently capable of taking effect, a self imposed prohibition does not create the continuing remedial structure found in Torah prohibitions such as the rapist's divorce. In an ordinary sale, however, where the legal effect itself is the point in question, there remains room to say that Rava's rule can invalidate the prohibited transaction even when the prohibition arose from an oath. This supports Rama's ruling.
