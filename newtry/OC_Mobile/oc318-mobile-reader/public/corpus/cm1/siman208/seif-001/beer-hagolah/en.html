The language of the Tur. The Rosh writes so regarding Rava's statement, 'we take the cloak from him,' Bava Metzia 65a. This implies that it is only because people should not say that he is wearing the proceeds of interest that we make him return it. Otherwise the sale remains valid even though it was performed through a prohibited act. The Maggid Mishneh at the end of chapter 8 of Hilkhot Malveh likewise writes in the name of the Ramban and Rashba.
This view holds that the sale is valid as a transaction performed through a prohibited act. Although Rava states that whenever the Torah says not to do something, if one does it the act is ineffective, Temurah 4b, Tosafot there, 6a, explain that this applies specifically where one violated a prohibition written in the Torah. Where the person himself created the prohibited circumstance, Rava did not say that the act is ineffective. See Yoreh Deah siman 230, where it is written that the sale has no effect, following the Mordechai in the glosses. The Sema discusses this at length. The Bach writes that in his view this is not a definitive ruling, but it should be taken into account so as not to remove property from the current holder, whether the seller holds the goods or the recipient and buyer has already drawn them into his domain.
This excludes a case where he gave only money, in which case he has not acquired except for the law of Mi Shepara. If he refuses to fulfill his words, we do not curse him because the transaction was done through a prohibited act. The Maggid Mishneh likewise writes in the name of the Ramban and Rashba citing Rav Hai, and other poskim likewise write in Rav Hai's name.
