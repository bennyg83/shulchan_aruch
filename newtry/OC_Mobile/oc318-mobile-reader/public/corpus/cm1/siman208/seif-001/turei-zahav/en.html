(Seif 1: if he swore.) According to the Sema's conclusion in seif katan 3, I find difficulty from the end of siman 195 and the end of siman 235, where a person who performs a kinyan on Shabbat nevertheless effects a valid sale. We do not say that because he acted unlawfully the act is ineffective. It therefore appears to me that the proper distinction is this. Whenever the act is intrinsically prohibited, we say that if he performs it, it is ineffective, as in Perek HaIshah Shenaflu, where the Sages said not to sell the brother's property and the Gemara rules that if he sold it, the sale is invalid because whatever the Sages prohibited does not take effect. By contrast, when the underlying act is permissible and the prohibition arises only from the time or some external circumstance, the act remains effective. Thus a sale on Shabbat is prohibited because of the day, not because sale itself is inherently forbidden. Similarly, in Perek Merubah 70, where one says, "Throw me your stolen property on Shabbat and my courtyard will acquire it," the acquisition works even though the actor incurs capital liability. For the same reason, if one swore not to sell his property, the prohibition stems from his oath rather than from the sale itself, and the sale is valid after the fact. Likewise, where a sale was made through a prohibited pricing arrangement, the prohibition does not lie in the essence of sale but only in the added money, so if he acted, the sale is effective. This is a sound distinction. Only where there is a defect in the essential act itself do we treat it as not done. The Rama's ruling here is therefore primary, especially since the Beit Yosef also agrees with him here. Moreover, this is the principal place in the laws of sale, whereas Yoreh Deah mentions the issue only incidentally in the laws of oaths.
