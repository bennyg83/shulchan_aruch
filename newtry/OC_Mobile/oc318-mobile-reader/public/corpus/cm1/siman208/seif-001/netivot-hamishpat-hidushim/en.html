And likewise if he swore. This is surprising, because in Yoreh Deah siman 230 Rama rules that the sale is void under Rava's principle that whatever the Torah says not to do, if one does it, it is ineffective. This is not comparable to the sale made through a prohibited method discussed in this siman. There the parties ultimately give and receive only at a permitted price, so the prohibited element falls away and we do not penalize the permitted portion because of the prohibition. But where he swore, the transaction cannot be upheld at all without violating the oath. Although Tosafot write there that the principle of ineffectiveness may not apply to a prohibition that the person created through his own oath, it appears that since in Ketubot we rule that the principle applies even to rabbinic prohibitions, a self created oath should certainly be no weaker than a rabbinic prohibition. Tosafot used that rationale only to explain why the Gemara did not establish the dispute between Abaye and Rava in that situation. Practically, however, even Tosafot agree that where he swore, the transaction is void. Sema. Taz concludes as practical law that where he swore, the sale remains valid in accordance with Rama here.
