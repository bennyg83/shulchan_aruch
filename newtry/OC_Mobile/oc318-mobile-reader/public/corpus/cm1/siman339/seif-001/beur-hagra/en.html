(Supplement) But concerning, etc. See Be'er HaGolah. Its explanation is difficult, because it follows the first tanna of the baraita, whereas the tanna of the Mishna does not derive the law through the verbal analogy of 'hired worker.' Rather, he derives from 'with you' every labor product that is with you, which seemingly includes land. We rule like the Mishna, as stated in Yevamot, chapter 4, and Rambam rules accordingly. Even according to the baraita, it is not certain that 'in your land' excludes land itself. This requires study. (End of supplement.)
(Supplement) There is one who says, etc. This also appears from the Yerushalmi: 'in your land' includes animals and slaves, while 'in your gates' includes movable property. This implies that land itself is excluded. Slaves therefore required an inclusion, lest one think they are equated with land. (End of supplement.)
