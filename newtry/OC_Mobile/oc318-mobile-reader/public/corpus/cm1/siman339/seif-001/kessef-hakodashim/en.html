I wrote elsewhere in the name of the Sema that withholding payment does not violate the prohibition of oppression when one genuinely intends to pay later; the prohibition concerns an intention not to pay. According to the view that despair does not extinguish a debt, there is less danger that delay will legally erase the claim. According to Maharikk, however, one could imagine a moral danger where a debtor repeatedly delays while telling himself that he still plans to pay, until the creditor despairs. Yet Hashem probes all hearts, and a person knows whether his delay is a device to induce despair. If that is his intent, it is itself oppression. This may illuminate the verse describing Hashem as guarding truth forever and executing justice for the oppressed: one's delay must be genuinely temporary, not initially truthful and later converted into deceit. Moreover, even the Sema may agree that the debtor should provide reassurance through collateral, a handshake undertaking, a vow, or another reliable device so that the creditor will not despair. Just as the Torah requires a debtor to bring out collateral to the beit din agent in the proper manner, the prohibition against oppression may include a duty not to cause the creditor to abandon hope. The same concern applies to desecration of Hashem's Name. Where there is a Torah-level doubt concerning conduct that may desecrate Hashem's Name, one must act stringently, and Hashem knows all hearts. There is also reason to say that society could not function if every uncertain act were interpreted unfavorably; therefore, where the facts are genuinely doubtful, one should not choose the interpretation that creates a desecration of Hashem's Name. This requires further study.
