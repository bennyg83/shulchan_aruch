Unless he demanded it. See Baer Heitev in the name of Sefer Chassidim, who warns that one should not delay on the assumption that the worker has not demanded payment, lest when he eventually does demand it the employer no longer have readily available money. This means that the employer may no longer have disposable funds because he will need them for other expenses. If he has no money at all, he does not violate the prohibition even without a stipulation, as explained below.
He did not demand it. Shaar Mishpat writes that the Zohar, Parashat Kedoshim, indicates that even if the worker says, "Let the wages remain with you," the employer should not retain them until after he has first actually paid the worker. The Mishnah's wording, "If he did not demand it, he does not transgress," also implies that only the formal prohibition is absent, but there remains an impropriety. Similar textual reasoning appears in the Gemara, Bava Metzia 90. See there.
Or he demanded payment and the employer had no money. Shulchan Aruch HaRav cites the Arizal that it is nevertheless a pious practice to borrow in order to pay the worker on time.
And he accepted upon himself to pay him. Beit Yosef writes that it somewhat appears that even if the worker did not consent to the assignment, once the third party accepted payment upon himself the employer no longer transgresses. Perishah cites this as well. See Nachalat Tzvi.
