He has the entire night to pay him, etc. The verse "the sun shall not set upon it" cannot refer to a day laborer, because wages are payable only at completion of the work, which for a day laborer occurs at sunset. How then could the Torah warn that the sun not set before payment? It must therefore refer to a night laborer, whose wage becomes due at the end of the night. The Torah gives the employer the entire day to obtain the wage money and warns that the sun must not set before he pays. The verse "the wage of a hired worker shall not remain with you overnight until morning" refers to a day laborer. The employer has the entire night, but must pay before morning light. This is the Gemara's explanation and Rashi's. See the Perishah.
Because of "on his day you shall give his wage." This is the language of the Gemara and authorities. It appears they cite the beginning of the verse, immediately followed by "and the sun shall not set upon it," intending that he violates both the positive commandment and the negative commandment written consecutively. See above, siman 97, and Yoreh Deah at the end of siman 28, where I wrote that the Gemara sometimes cites the beginning of a verse while intending its conclusion. All the more so here, where the intent includes both clauses. This is straightforward.
