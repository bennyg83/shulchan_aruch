As long as the garment remains in the artisan's possession, etc. In that situation the prohibition of delaying a worker's wages does not rest upon the owner, because the artisan is holding the owner's property as security for his wage.
Because of "you shall not leave overnight," etc. This is the language of the baraita and Tur. It is difficult, for one would expect it to say that he violates "on his day you shall give his wage." In the Perishah I explained that most day laborers do not violate "on his day you shall give," as explained above, but rather "you shall not leave overnight." Therefore the sources use the familiar general expression and mean that the ordinary prohibition of delayed wages begins here as soon as the sun sets. One cannot explain that Rambam and the Tur cite "you shall not leave overnight" because they hold that in every case one violates all five prohibitions, as discussed above in seif 2. I already explained that even according to Rambam and Tur one violates all five only when he deliberately withholds the wage with no intention to pay the worker at all. Here the case is not one of outright withholding. This is straightforward.
Because contracting work is treated like hired labor. This excludes the opinion that an artisan acquires the enhancement he creates in the object. According to that opinion, when he improves the object he creates the enhancement for himself, and when he later hands the object together with the enhancement to the owner, it is as though he sold the owner something belonging to himself. The money would then be the price of a sale rather than wages. The ruling teaches that we hold that an artisan does not acquire the enhancement of the utensil, as explained above in siman 306, seif 2, and therefore the payment is wages with respect to the owner.
