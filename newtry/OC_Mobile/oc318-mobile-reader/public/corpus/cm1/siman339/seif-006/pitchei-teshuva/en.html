If the garment remains in the craftsman's possession, the employer does not violate the prohibition. Sema explains that in such a case the worker's labor is not presently imposed upon the employer in a manner that creates the prohibition, because the craftsman is holding the employer's own property as security against the wage. Rabbi Shneur Zalman of Liadi therefore writes in his Shulchan Aruch that if the employer gives a pledge to the worker, he does not violate the prohibition even if he has cash available. Levush, however, explains differently: because the craftsman does not return the garment, he is understood to waive immediate payment of his wage for as long as the garment remains with him. According to that explanation, one cannot derive the same rule for a different pledge. Shitah Mekubetzet on Bava Metzia 112a cites Rabbi Yonatan that so long as the garment is in the craftsman's possession, even if he gave the employer permission to take it home and stated that he does not wish to retain it because of the wage, there is no violation while a pledge remains under his control. Ketzot HaChoshen, siman 72 seif katan 23, discusses this at length and derives a novel rule: where an employer gave a craftsman a pledge for his wage, even if the craftsman later returned the pledge, once he had acquired a lien in the pledge corresponding to his wage, the prohibition of delaying wages no longer applies.
