As wages. This excludes the opinion that an artisan acquires the appreciation he creates in an article. According to that view, when he improves the article, the added value becomes his, and when he later hands the article together with its appreciation to the owner, it is as though the worker sold the owner something belonging to the worker. The money is then not regarded as wages for labor but as a debt arising from the sale. The ruling therefore teaches that we hold that an artisan does not acquire the appreciation in the article, and the amount due from the owner is considered wages. These are the words of the Sema. See Even HaEzer, siman 28, seif 15, where two views are cited. Rabbi Yehudah's view that the artisan acquires the appreciation is considered there because, with respect to kiddushin, we take his view into account and she is doubtfully betrothed. In monetary law, however, the ruling is not in accordance with Rabbi Yehudah, as the Beit Yosef likewise rules there in seif 2. The Tur's words in the name of the Rosh require study, however, for they appear contradictory. See the later commentators.
