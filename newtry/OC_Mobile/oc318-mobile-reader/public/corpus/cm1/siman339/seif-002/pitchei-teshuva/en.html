When the garment is in the artisan's possession, he does not transgress. Sema explains that the employer does not bear the operative wage debt for purposes of the prohibition because the artisan is holding the employer's property as security against it. From this, Shulchan Aruch HaRav of Rabbi Shneur Zalman of Liadi infers that if the employer gives the worker collateral, he does not violate the prohibition even if he has cash available. Levush, however, gives a different reason: since the artisan does not return the item, he is deemed to waive immediate payment so long as it remains in his hands. According to that reason, no inference can be drawn to unrelated collateral. Shitah Mekubetzet, Bava Metzia 112a, cites Rabbi Yonatan that as long as the garment remains with the artisan, even if the artisan gave the employer permission to take it home and stated that he did not intend to retain it for his wages, the employer still does not transgress because the artisan continues to hold a pledge. Ketzot HaChoshen above, siman 72, seif katan 23, discusses this at length and derives a further rule: where an employer gave collateral to an artisan for wages, even if the artisan later returned the collateral, once he had acquired a lien in it corresponding to his wages the prohibition of delayed wages no longer applies.
