It is as though he takes his life. The Gemara derives this from 'for to it he bears his soul.' One opinion understands it as the life of the person whose wages are withheld, and another as the life of the wrongdoer.
He violates five prohibitions and one positive commandment. The Gemara counts: 'You shall not rob,' 'You shall not oppress a hired worker,' 'You shall not oppress your fellow,' 'It shall not remain overnight,' 'The sun shall not set upon it,' and the positive command 'On that day you shall give his wages.' Although some verses are stated regarding a day worker and others regarding a night worker, the Rambam and poskim understand the general law of wages as one category, so the relevant prohibitions apply correspondingly.
