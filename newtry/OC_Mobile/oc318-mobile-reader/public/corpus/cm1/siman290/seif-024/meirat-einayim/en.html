He is not required to swear. The Raavad writes this and derives it a fortiori from a guardian appointed by the father. There, one might say that had the father not benefited from this person he would not have appointed him, yet we nevertheless exempt him from an oath lest people refrain from serving. Certainly, where the orphans attached themselves to someone on their own initiative, we should not require an oath. The Rosh disagrees. He is concerned that an unsuitable person may attract the orphans to himself and consume their money precisely because he knows he will not have to swear at the end. Nor can we answer that he will refrain from helping if required to swear, because no one asked him to take this responsibility. If he is an upright person acting for the sake of Heaven because he sees that the orphans have no helper, he will not refrain merely because of the oath.
A minor who attached himself to his mother. See Mordechai in HaNizakin concerning a widow who remarried and brought into the new marriage property from her first husband, while continuing to support her son from the first husband. The widow died, and the orphan argued that his mother had never sworn concerning her ketubah. The ruling was that if the property did not exceed what the new husband had spent supporting the orphan, he was exempt. The source discusses the matter at length. See also the cited responsa concerning diminution of the estate before the widow's oath and repayment of gentile debts from the husband's property.
