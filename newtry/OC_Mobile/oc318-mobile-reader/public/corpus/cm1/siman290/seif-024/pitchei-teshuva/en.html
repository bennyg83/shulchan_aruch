Be'er HaGolah cites Bach in the name of Ramah that the rule applies where the minor is nine years old, or younger if especially sharp, whereas ordinary reliance by a younger child is ineffective. The wording may suggest that Bach accepted this as law, but Bach was actually explaining the Ramah cited by Tur, while the Rosh disagrees and holds that there is no age distinction and the person becomes a full guardian even for a very young child. Shulchan Aruch's unqualified wording appears to follow the Rosh. Responsa Chacham Tzvi, additional responsum 3, discusses partners Reuven and his two sons, Shimon and Levi. Levi died leaving young children, and Reuven and Shimon continued the partnership for the benefit of the orphans without formal appointment by beit din. Chacham Tzvi holds that their status as guardians is not doubtful. They are no worse than householders upon whom orphans rely. Even the stricter view about age concerns a person who would not otherwise be fit to serve as guardian. Here the adults were fully suitable. Nor does kinship disqualify them where the estate consists of money and movables, since relatives and nonrelatives are treated alike in that setting. Indeed, in such a continuing family partnership, one should not ordinarily appoint outsiders, because the deceased father had expressly consented to the partnership during his lifetime and it is evidently beneficial to continue it. See there also concerning their authority to compromise a debt.
