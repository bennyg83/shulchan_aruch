Chet) There, seif 31, from the Mishnah in the fifth chapter of Gittin 52a. The Maggid Mishneh wonders why this law is not mentioned by Rambam.
Tet) From the incident there involving orphans who had attached themselves to an elderly woman, etc.
Yod) There in the name of the Raavad. The Rosh writes that the Ramban rules in accordance with the Raavad and argues a fortiori: if a guardian appointed by the orphans' father is not required to swear because we fear that otherwise people will refrain from accepting the appointment, even though one might say he would not have accepted unless he derived some benefit, then certainly a person whom the orphans attached themselves to voluntarily should not be required to swear, lest people refuse to help them. Requiring such a person to swear would repay good with harm. The Maggid Mishneh likewise writes in halachah 11 that the Ramban and Rashba rule this way, as do the other commentators.
Kaf) There in the name of the Rosh. He explains that there is reason to fear an unsuitable person who might draw the orphans to himself and consume their property if he knows he will not be required to swear. He concludes that if the person is upright and acts for the sake of Heaven because he sees that the orphans have no helper, he will not refrain merely because of the oath. The Bach cites the Ramah that the minor must ordinarily be at least nine years old; if especially sharp, attachment from age six onward can be effective because at that age he has authority to sell movable property. Below that age, mere attachment is not ordinarily effective. The Sema cites the Mordekhai in HaKotev concerning a widow who remarried, brought into the second marriage property from her first husband, and supported her son from the first husband. After the widow died, the orphan claimed that she had never sworn concerning her ketubah. The ruling was that if the property does not exceed what the second husband spent supporting the orphan, he is exempt.
