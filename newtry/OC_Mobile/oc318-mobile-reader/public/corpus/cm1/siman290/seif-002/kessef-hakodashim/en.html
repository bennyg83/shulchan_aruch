Minors who relied upon a householder. The precise boundaries of the rule that orphans who rely upon a householder confer upon him the status of a guardian are not clearly explained, and I have not found an exact definition among the earlier authorities. Since we follow the Rosh that such a person may even sell land on their behalf, though the Beit Yosef cites authorities following the Ramah who limit the rule, it seems that the reliance must be substantial. The orphans must have no other person responsible for their essential needs, such as food and other necessities, and the householder upon whom they rely must in practice conduct the full range of those essential affairs. Only then does Chazal's guardian status arise. Mere reliance upon him for a nonessential matter, or simply for selling their property, should not suffice. Indeed, even with movables, the minor's own consent cannot be the source of the sale, because a minor cannot appoint an agent. Even according to the Ramah, who distinguishes movables from land, the effectiveness regarding movables is not because the minor authorized the sale. It is a rabbinic measure for the minor's welfare: because this person manages the minor's essential affairs, Chazal assign him guardian powers. The Ramah's limitation may rest on the fact that the arrangement arose through the minor's own choice, when another caretaker could perhaps have been selected with the assistance of beit din. He therefore requires a child of sufficient age and limits the power to movables. According to all opinions, however, the householder should be one upon whom the minors truly depend for their essential affairs. If beit din expressly sends word to such a householder that he may not sell the orphans' property, then even if the orphans continue relying upon him and he continues acting for their welfare, a sale made contrary to that restriction is ineffective. If beit din authorizes him to sell only certain movables, sales of other movables may likewise be void, because the explicit restriction removes the general status of guardian with respect to sales. The fact that he was expressly authorized for certain items does not prove that he retains a general guardianship for others. This requires further study. It also requires study whether the rule of orphans who rely upon a householder applies where he undertakes their care for compensation rather than gratuitously.
