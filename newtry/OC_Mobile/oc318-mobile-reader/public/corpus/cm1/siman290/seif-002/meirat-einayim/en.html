Not a woman. Because women do not ordinarily go out and come in to conduct such affairs; nor a slave, because an ordinary slave is not trustworthy; nor a minor, who lacks legal understanding.
Who is presumed suspect. For an ignoramus is not presumed sin-fearing, and an am ha'aretz is not presumed pious. He may justify to himself that he is merely taking payment for his effort. This is Rashi's and Rif's language. Rambam limits the disqualification to an am ha'aretz who is ignorant both of Mishnah and ordinary proper conduct. Perhaps that is also the Mechaber's intent in saying "an am ha'aretz who is presumed suspect," meaning specifically where he is in fact suspect because he lacks mitzvah observance and proper conduct. This explains why the Mechaber gives a reason for the am ha'aretz more than for the other categories, and also explains the difference between the Tur's plural "they are suspect" and Rambam's singular "he is suspect." The Tur concludes there, "If the father appointed him, he has authority." In the Perishah I noted that some texts read "they have authority," meaning that if such people were trusted by the father during his lifetime, beit din may appoint them guardians after his death. The Beit Yosef writes that this law is correct even without that textual reading.
To appoint their relative. It appears this means beit din may appoint a relative of the judges themselves. It cannot mean a relative of the minor, because the Mechaber already cited Rashba, through the Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe, that a relative is disqualified only to judge his relative's right or liability. Where the relative has no personal right or obligation, why should he be disqualified from serving as guardian? See siman 285.
