And a perutah. As in the law of a Torah oath. See the beginning of simanim 88 and 93, where these laws are explained. At the end of seif 1 there, the Mechaber writes: "None of these swears on a claim of uncertainty (this also refers to a guardian) unless the one requiring the oath suspects him of two silver ma'ah." It must mean that the suspicion and denial concern two silver ma'ah, aside from an admission of at least a perutah, as the Tur and Mechaber write here. This is the Sema's language. But the Beit Yosef in siman 93, and the Kesef Mishneh, chapter 9 of Hilkhot Sheluchin, do not imply this. This therefore requires study. See what I wrote in siman 93, seif katan 3. Shach.
And he rolls onto him. Meaning, if there is a definite claim that he is withholding their property and he denies it, so that he is subject to a shevuat heset instituted for one who denies an entire definite claim, then one may also roll onto him the guardian's oath, even where the underlying claim is less than two silver ma'ah and a perutah.
