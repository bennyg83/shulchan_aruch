Two silver ma'ah and a perutah. This is the amount required for a Torah oath. See above, at the beginning of simanim 88 and 93, where these laws are explained. At the end of seif 1 there, the Mechaber writes: "None of these people takes an oath on the basis of an uncertain claim," referring there also to a guardian, "unless the person imposing the oath suspects them concerning two silver ma'ah." One must understand that the suspicion and denial must involve two silver ma'ah in addition to the admission, which must be at least one perutah, as the Tur and Shulchan Aruch write here. Examine this carefully.
If he asserts against him a definite claim, etc. Meaning, if he has a definite claim against him that the guardian is holding his property and the guardian denies it, the guardian thereby becomes subject to a hesset oath, which the Sages instituted for one who completely denies a definite claim. He can then require him, through gilgul shevuah, also to take this guardian's oath even though the amount of the underlying claim is less than two silver ma'ah and a perutah.
