Minors and adults. Kiddushin 42a, as stated above at the beginning of siman 289.
He must, etc.; and if not, etc. The Mishnah in Gittin 52a.
For beit din, etc. The beginning of chapter 4 of Bava Kamma.
And if the beit din, etc. For we advance claims on behalf of orphans. See Rosh, chapter 13 of Kesubos, siman 12. This is why chapter 11 there says that where beit din sold without public announcement, etc., whereas with public announcement it is permitted. Ran in chapter 2 of Kiddushin writes that since the Gemara says "and they choose, etc." in the plural, it implies that it refers to beit din. The reason an administrator is appointed is that he should argue for their benefit, something it is inappropriate for beit din itself to do because of the prohibition against acting as counsel for litigants. This is the meaning of "nevertheless, etc."
And that which beit din, etc. As stated in chapter 3 of Bava Metzia, "an administrator for adult bearded men, etc.," because beit din is not required to trouble itself for them, as stated in siman 285:2.
And beit din means, etc. As stated in Bava Kamma there, Rabban Gamliel and his beit din, etc.
One, etc. As stated, "and Yosef was the ruler, etc.," and "you shall be over my house, etc."
One who, etc., that not, etc. For then the father appointed him for their needs and trusted him. This is the meaning of the Mishnah's phrase, "or if he appointed for them, etc."
