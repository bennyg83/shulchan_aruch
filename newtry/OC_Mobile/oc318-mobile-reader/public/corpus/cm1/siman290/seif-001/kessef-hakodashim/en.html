To appoint a guardian for them. If a father appointed a guardian and authorized him to appoint another guardian, even if the father named a particular person whom the first guardian might appoint, there is room to apply the rule that certain entrusted matters cannot be delegated through an agent. This may be so even before the father's death. Even if a guardian personally appointed by the father can in some circumstances appoint another in his place, authority arising merely because the orphans relied upon a particular person cannot automatically be delegated by that person to someone else, and this is certainly doubtful after his death. If a father appointed two guardians and they disagree, then if they voluntarily agree upon a third person to decide between them, that is proper. If they cannot agree, beit din should appoint a third deciding guardian for the immediate dispute, or perhaps as a permanent member. This is all the more so where the guardians themselves were appointed by beit din.
