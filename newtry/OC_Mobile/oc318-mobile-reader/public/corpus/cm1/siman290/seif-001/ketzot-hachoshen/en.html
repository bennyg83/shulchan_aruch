Mahari ibn Lev discusses a woman who appointed two guardians and one later died, and similarly a get conditioned on the father appearing before the woman and before witnesses. He asks whether words joining two requirements demand both together or can be satisfied by either one, invoking the dispute of Rabbi Yonatan and Rabbi Yoshiyah. Mahara Sasson argues that in the vernacular wording used in those documents, the conjunction clearly joins the two elements, so the Talmudic dispute over the Hebrew letter vav does not apply. In my view the dispute cannot be limited to the ambiguity of a conjunctive vav. Bava Metzia applies the same disagreement to the verses concerning whether the owner was present both at borrowing and at the later accident, even though no dividing vav is involved. This proves the underlying dispute concerns whether joined conditions naturally require both elements, not merely the grammar of one particular Hebrew conjunction. Shitah Mekubetzet, citing Ritva, offers another distinction: Rabbi Yonatan's leniency applies chiefly to prohibitory formulations, while where a person positively directs that something be done for two people, both are required. If so, appointing two guardians would naturally require both. Yet the application of that distinction to the borrower verses remains difficult and requires study. For the practical law, Rashba ruled that if one of two appointed guardians withdraws or dies, there is no need to appoint another in his place. He reached the result from accepted practice rather than from the Rabbi Yonatan dispute. Rama in Yoreh Deah rules accordingly.
