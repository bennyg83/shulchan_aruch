Seif 9, gloss. The law favors the orphans. Note: See the Sema citing the Mordechai that the rule 'consecrated property has only its present place and present time' applies only to consecrated property and not to other matters. This is very difficult in light of the Rosh at the beginning of Kiddushin, cited by Beit Shmuel, Even HaEzer 31, regarding an item that may be worth a perutah in Media. There the Rosh appears to hold that the same valuation principle applies generally. The Rosh's own words also seem contradictory. In Bava Kamma, concerning appraisal for a creditor, cited in Choshen Mishpat 101:9, he gives a different reason, namely that otherwise lenders would be discouraged, rather than invoking the rule that consecrated property is valued only according to its place and time. That implies agreement with the Mordechai. Yet in Kiddushin he appears to say otherwise. Perhaps only in kiddushin did the Rosh derive the rule from consecrated property because kiddushin themselves have a quasi-consecratory character, in that the woman becomes prohibited to everyone else like consecrated property. The matter still requires study.
