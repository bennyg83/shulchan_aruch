In order to store them, rather they sell them while still untithed. This is the Rambam's language at the end of Hilchot Nachalot. Since he concludes, "rather they sell them while still tevel," it follows that the prohibition against tithing "in order to store" includes even tithing simply so that the produce will stand ready for later sale in corrected form. Instead they sell it as tevel and the purchasers separate the required portions themselves. The baraita's language is different: guardians separate terumah and tithes in order to feed the orphans, but not in order to store the produce. Rashi explains that they do not tithe it merely to put it into storage, because when the orphans grow they should separate their own tithes and possess the associated right of discretionary benefit. The Maggid Mishneh gives that reason. The Ir Shushan mixed the two approaches. He explained "to store" according to Rashi, yet concluded in the Rambam's language that they sell it as tevel. He also supplied his own rationale, that if they tithe now, later normal shrinkage and mice will reduce only the remaining ordinary produce, whereas if it remains untithed some of the loss will fall proportionately upon the tithe portion. That reason is not stated by Rashi or the Maggid Mishneh. See also the Rambam, the Raavad's objection, and the Maggid Mishneh's resolution.
