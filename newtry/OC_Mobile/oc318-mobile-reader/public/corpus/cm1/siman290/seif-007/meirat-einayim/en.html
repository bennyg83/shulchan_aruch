And the movable property that was not sold. The Tur and Mechaber later explain that when a guardian is appointed over minors' property, certain movables are sold to provide for the minors, but not necessarily in order merely to leave cash idle. Accordingly, this clause refers to the movables that do not need to be sold and remain with the guardian. It is also possible that the later rule concerning animals, slaves, and land is limited to those categories, while ordinary movables may be sold initially and the proceeds invested for profit. Some movables nevertheless are not sold because of family dignity or because no practical sale is available. This second explanation appears primary. The present language comes from the Rambam in chapter 11 of Hilchot Nachalot, and earlier in that chapter the Rambam explicitly writes that all movables of the orphans are appraised and sold through beit din, and the proceeds are added to the orphans' money. The Mechaber copies that rule below in seif 9. The language clearly shows that ordinary movables are sold in order to invest their proceeds like the rest of the orphans' funds.
