Reuven says that he holds money belonging to Shimon and says that Shimon instructed him... I have been unable to understand Rama's view here. There are only two possibilities. If Reuven voluntarily admits on his own that he holds money belonging to the orphans, he is certainly believed without an oath. Why should this be worse than one who saw his father hide money and the father said, "It belongs to so-and-so," where he is believed if the money is in his power to take, as explained in siman 256, seif 8? Why should the orphans be able to impose an oath merely by making a definite claim? If, on the other hand, the orphans claim that they know he holds money of their father but do not know how much, how can he become obligated to swear against a doubtful claim? We do not plead on behalf of orphans in order to extract money. Nor is this naturally a matter of guardianship merely because the dispute came through a claim and counterclaim. It therefore appears to me that the case is as follows. Reuven admitted before witnesses that he held a specified sum belonging to the orphans, and he further said that their father had appointed him guardian to attend to their needs and spend for them until they reached adulthood. He says that he acted accordingly. In that case the orphans cannot compel him to swear. At the moment he first admitted holding their father's money, he was like one returning a lost object, and a guardian appointed by the father is not required to swear. But suppose that after making the original admission, when the orphans later demanded payment, he said that their father had instructed him to use that money only for some of the children and not all of them. The remaining children, toward whom he was not appointed guardian, can require him to swear if no balance remains in his possession. Although he was believed to say that the father instructed him to provide maintenance only to some of them until they became adults, there remains a possibility that money was left over in his hands, and that surplus belongs to all of them. But if, at the time of the original admission before witnesses, he already said that the father had instructed him to give the money only to certain children and never to give any of it to the others, those others cannot require him to swear. This is like siman 256, seif 8, concerning one who saw his father hide money and heard him identify it as belonging to someone else. This, in my view, is the correct explanation.
