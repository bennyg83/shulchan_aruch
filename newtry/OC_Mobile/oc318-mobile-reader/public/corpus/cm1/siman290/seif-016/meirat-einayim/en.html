When is this said? When beit din appointed him, etc. If beit din appointed the guardian, we are not concerned that requiring an oath will deter him from serving, because he receives substantial reputational benefit from being publicly trusted by beit din. If the father appointed him, that benefit is absent, and a mandatory oath could deter people from accepting such appointments. See the Perishah.
But he swears on a definite claim. The Tur cites the Rif: If the orphans grew up and made a definite claim against him and he denied it, he certainly swears. The Mechaber writes generally that he swears on a definite claim, including also a case where beit din itself makes a definite claim and he denies it. If two judges know the matter, they are believed against him even to extract money. If only one knows, he must swear against that person like against a single witness. The Rif mentioned that the orphans had grown because before then, if two members of beit din make a definite claim against the guardian, it is not really a case of making him swear on their claim. Their direct knowledge itself is enough to establish the matter against him. See the Perishah.
