He should endeavor. Meaning, he should seek a trustworthy person fit to receive the money. The guardian should give it to that person through beit din, because beit din has authority over the recipient's property vis-a-vis the orphans and can stipulate that he receive the money on terms that are close to profit and remote from loss. This is the wording of Rashi and the Tur; see there. See Responsa Ibn Lev, part I, siman 60.

For himself. Although at the beginning of siman 72 the Tur and Shulchan Aruch write, regarding a loan secured by a hoe or axe, that the lender may rent them to others but not to himself, following the principle that one may exchange money for others but not for oneself, and they rule similarly in siman 292 regarding a deposit, orphans are different. Beit din is their father. When the guardian informs beit din, it is as though the owner himself placed the money in his hands as an investment. This does not apply to the pledge and deposit discussed there, which concern adults rather than orphans, because beit din does not attend to the private affairs of adults and therefore cannot authorize such a transaction for them.
