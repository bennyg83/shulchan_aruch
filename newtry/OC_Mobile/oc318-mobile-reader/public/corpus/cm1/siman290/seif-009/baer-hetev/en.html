As with his own. The Sema writes that this applies specifically here, where there is a risk of loss on both sides, and therefore they permitted him to act as he would with his own property. Where there is only a risk on one side, however, the Tur writes in seif 11 that he may not say, 'I will act as I would with my own,' but must act under the direction of beit din, unless he is a guardian. The Shach disagrees and writes that the Rosh disputes the Rambam and holds that even here, where there is potential loss on both sides, permission of beit din is required. His reason is that Ravina himself obtained permission from Rav Ashi. The Rambam's reasoning is that since Rav Ashi permitted what Ravina had done, it follows that Ravina acted properly and no one else need obtain permission. The Shach found this stated explicitly in Responsa Rashdam, siman 46; see there carefully.
