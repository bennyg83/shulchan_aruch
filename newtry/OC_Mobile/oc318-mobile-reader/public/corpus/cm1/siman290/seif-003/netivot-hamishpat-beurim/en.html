For it is based on a definite claim. See Sema, seif katan 9, who explains this as referring to a claim of two silver pieces and a perutah. This is strained. Moreover, if a definite claim can be established from a written beit din record, then that writing can produce collection. If it cannot establish a definite claim, what benefit is there in requiring the record? The correct explanation appears from Taz in siman 93. Taz says one does not impose an oath merely because of a bare suspicion; there must be some meaningful indication creating grounds for suspicion. Accordingly, even a guardian appointed by beit din is made to swear only where beit din has some factual basis to suspect mismanagement regarding the relevant amount. If there is a written accounting showing how much the guardian received and how much he returned, that record can supply an evidentiary basis for beit din or the orphans to suspect him and require an oath. If no one knows at all how much money entered his hands, there may be no factual basis from which to suspect him. Thus the phrase "upon a definite claim" means a definite, evidence-based ground for suspicion, not merely a technical amount in dispute.
