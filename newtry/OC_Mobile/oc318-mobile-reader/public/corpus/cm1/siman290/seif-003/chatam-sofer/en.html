There, in the continuation, "and also against the Ramban." Note: One who studies the Ramban's novellae will see that his difficulty was as follows. If the case in the chapter HaMafkid concerning the father of the orphan girl means that even for negligence he is exempt, necessarily because beit din appointed him, then when the verse concerning a paid guardian says "to pay," the guardian should have been required to pay, since he is a paid guardian. To this the Ramban gives two answers. First, the guardian here was not a paid guardian over the ox, because he could have entrusted it to a shepherd. Second, the sugya does not retain that assumption in its conclusion. See there. The Rashba considers this answer forced and therefore explains the case as referring to the father of the orphans during his lifetime, who is liable for negligence in accordance with Rav Hai.
