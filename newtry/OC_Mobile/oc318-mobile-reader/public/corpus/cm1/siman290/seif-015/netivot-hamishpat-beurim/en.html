We assess charity against them. See Ketzot HaChoshen, who proves that charity creates a property lien. In my view there is no general property lien for charity except where beit din itself has authority to compel the payment, such as redeeming a close relative, or paying the fixed communal obligations for the charity fund and public food distribution. Those obligations arise independently of the person's own verbal undertaking. But charity that becomes obligatory only because of the person's own words is like a vow, and no property lien attaches to the assets.
