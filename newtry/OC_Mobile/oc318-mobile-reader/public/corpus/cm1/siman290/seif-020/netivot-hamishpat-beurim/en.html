A guardian who pleaded in beit din something he should not have pleaded. See Ketzot HaChoshen, who questions the case where, had the guardian pleaded according to Reuven's actual position, Reuven would have recovered without an oath, but because of the guardian's pleading the opposing party instead swears and is exempt. The ruling says the guardian is not considered to have caused direct monetary damage because perhaps the defendant would swear. Ketzot asks: if so, why in Shevuot, chapter Shevuat HaEdut, when one witness denies knowledge and another admits, is the denying witness liable for a korban? The defendant remains obligated to swear because of the one witness who did testify, so perhaps he will swear. Why is the denial treated as a denial of money? In my view the cases are not comparable. In the law of the korban for false denial of testimony, the Torah imposes liability when the denial concerns money. A single witness, even when he testifies, produces only a causal route to money because perhaps the defendant will swear. Causing money is not itself treated as money. But if the second witness also testifies, there would then be a complete monetary obligation. Therefore when the second witness denies, his denial itself concerns full monetary liability. The first witness by himself created only a cause toward money, not money itself; the second witness would have completed the monetary proof. When he denies, he denies actual money, and therefore he is liable. Here, by contrast, the guardian's liability would be only under dina d'garmi. Even witnesses who testify falsely are treated only under dina d'garmi, as explained in siman 38, and dina d'garmi is rabbinic. Now the statement that one witness creates only a cause toward money because perhaps the defendant will swear necessarily means that so long as one witness has testified there still remains monetary uncertainty. For dina d'garmi, liability requires that the damage be definite. Whenever the actor causes harm but monetary uncertainty remains, the damage is not definite and he is exempt. Therefore here too, where under the guardian's pleading there still remains monetary uncertainty because perhaps the defendant will swear, the guardian is exempt. The harm is not certain.
