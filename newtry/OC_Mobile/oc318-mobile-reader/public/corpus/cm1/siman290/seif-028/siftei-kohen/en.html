We do not extract from the orphans, etc. Responsa Rashdam, siman 172, writes that the wording plainly implies that only where the guardian's son seeks to extract from the orphans is he not believed, because they are in possession of their property. If, however, the assets were in the guardian's son's possession, he would be believed to retain what he holds in accordance with the ledger, even though he cannot swear that his father had not collected that debt. These words do not appear correct to me in light of what I wrote above, siman 75, seif katan 64, that seizure is ineffective where the claimant says, 'My father told me.' The same applies where he found the matter written in his father's ledger, for this is equivalent to saying, 'My father told me,' as I wrote there in seif katan 66. The phrase here, 'we do not extract,' means that the assets remain presumptively the orphans' property. Even if physically held by the guardian's son, we do not remove from the orphans property that is legally in their possession. This must be so, for if the case concerned property actually held by the orphans, then even if the guardian were alive it would be obvious that he could not extract from them merely on the basis of his ledger. Examine this carefully. See below, siman 91, seif 5.
