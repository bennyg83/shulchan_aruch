We do not recover from the orphans. See Shach, seif katan 30, through his statement that even if the guardian is alive the matter is obvious. See above, siman 91, seif 5. There it is explained that the guardian can swear on the basis of his ledger, and while the father was alive, if the guardian made a definite claim he was believed with an oath like one who expended money with authorization, since beit din authorized him to spend for the orphans' support. This is also explained in Responsa Ramban, siman 3. Perhaps Shach means a case where the guardian returned the objects to the orphans, which would amount to an admission by the guardian. See what I wrote above in siman 91, Beurim, seif katan 1, regarding one who expended money with authorization and died. If movable property of the orphans remains in the guardian's possession, then because a notation found in the father's ledger is like the father's statement, where the objects were not seen in the orphans' hands and their father would have been believed through a migo, the case resembles a pledge, where we advance for orphans the claim their father could have made through a migo. But if the objects were seen and it is known that they belonged to the orphans' father, the guardian is seeking to recover from the orphans. We do not recover from heirs merely because a notation was found in the ledger, even though it is like the father's statement. Since their father would have been believed only by taking an oath, and he never took that oath, the claim cannot be collected from them.
