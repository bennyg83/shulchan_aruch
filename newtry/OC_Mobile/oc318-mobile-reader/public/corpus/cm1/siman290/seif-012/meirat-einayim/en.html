And if he prevailed, the judgment stands. However, initially he may not enter into litigation on the assumption that if he prevails the judgment will stand, whereas if he is found liable the case will be reopened, for that would impair the fairness owed to the claimant. Therefore the Sages said that strict law does not permit the claimant to sue the orphans until they reach majority. If nevertheless the claimant chose to sue them and this one descended into judgment and prevailed, the judgment stands; but if he was found liable, the case is reopened, because the claimant sued them contrary to law. This is straightforward.
And the guardian is believed if it is known, etc. The Mordechai writes as follows: If the guardian lent money to another person, and the borrower says, "I repaid," while the guardian says that he did not repay, then if the borrower admits that the money belonged to orphans, the guardian is believed without an oath. But if he does not concede that it belonged to the orphans, the guardian must swear. For although one does not swear on the basis of an uncertain claim, he nevertheless swears on the claim that he did not repay. End of his words. This is why Rama was precise and wrote, "if it is known that the money belongs to the orphans," for otherwise he is not believed, since perhaps the money is his own.

If a guardian advances a claim, his claim is effective. This is one of the reasons that a guardian is appointed, because in this respect he is better than the orphans themselves. A guardian regularly advances every plausible uncertain claim that their father could have advanced had he been alive, provided that he does not deliberately advance a claim that he knows with certainty is false. See above, siman 124, where I wrote concerning this.
We advance claims. Meaning, beit din is obligated to advance on behalf of an heir every common claim that his father could have advanced, provided that the heir does not contradict it. That means that the heir does not make a definite claim against the benefit that beit din could advance for him. See above, siman 72, Tur seif 28, and the Mechaber there, seif 34, where it is evident that even if the heirs state a particular version, we regard their statement as asserting their possible right only out of doubt, unless they explicitly say, "We know with certainty that this is what happened."
