If a guardian advances a claim, his claim is valid. As I wrote in siman 108, all the more if adult heirs themselves raise an unusual factual claim, it can be considered. The rule that beit din does not invent an unlikely claim for heirs does not mean the heirs or their guardian may not themselves assert it. There is, however, a limitation. If the heirs say merely, "Perhaps our father later repurchased it," and the claim is an unusual one that beit din would not plead for them, their own uncertainty is equivalent to one who says, "I do not know whether I repaid you." Such a debtor is liable even where he had no way to know. The ordinary exemption of heirs from an oral loan rests on beit din pleading for them as though with a definite claim that the father may have paid. That judicial plea is treated like a certain defense. Where the suggested defense is too unusual for beit din to raise, the heirs' own doubtful assertion does not become definite merely because they themselves voice it. Therefore an adult heir's uncertain unusual plea may still be insufficient unless the guardian or heir states it as a genuine factual contention.
