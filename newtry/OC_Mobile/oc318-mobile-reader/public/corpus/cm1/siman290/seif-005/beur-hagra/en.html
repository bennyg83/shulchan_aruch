Beit din, etc. Necessarily the case is where their father appointed him. Otherwise, how could Rav Sheila say that we do not remove him? It would be necessary to select someone trustworthy, as stated in Bava Metzia 71a. If their selection was mistaken, it can be reversed, as stated in Kiddushin 42a and Kesubos 100a: "this is no difficulty; one case is where they erred, etc." The Rif, however, does not appear to understand it this way, for he writes, "the rabbis said, etc., and that which was ruled, etc."
Others disagree, etc. They hold that the statement applies in every case because the Gemara states it without qualification.
