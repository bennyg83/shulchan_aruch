A guardian who lends orphan money. See Sema, seif katan 13, citing Maharik that if the guardian cannot collect the loan he is negligent, even if the borrower was a respected and apparently trustworthy person. Sema abbreviates Maharik's case. There it was already known at the time of the loan that the borrower owed others more than the value of his assets. In such a case the guardian was certainly negligent, because orphan funds may be lent only where there is sufficient asset security, as explained in seif 8. But if the borrower's excessive debts were not known at the time of the loan, the guardian should be exempt. One should not say that every written debt has publicity and therefore the guardian is treated as though he knew. Maharik's own wording says that the excessive debt was known, implying that without actual knowledge the guardian is not automatically negligent merely because the debts were documented.
