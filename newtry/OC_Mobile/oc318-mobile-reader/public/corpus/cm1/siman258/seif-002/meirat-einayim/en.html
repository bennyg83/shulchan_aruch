We do not say that the date of the document proves his intent. The Ramah gives the reason. When one writes property to another to take effect after his death, death itself fixes an obvious future time. If the deed nevertheless contains a present date, the date must be intended to begin some aspect of acquisition from that time. But where the gift is to take effect after thirty days, the date is needed independently to determine from when the thirty days are counted. Therefore the date does not prove that the acquisition begins immediately. The Beit Yosef asks that he could simply have written, "My property is given to you on such and such day," which would also make the effective date clear without the present date. For this reason the Mechaber omitted the Ramah's explanation. In the Derishah I wrote that the objection is not decisive. Even if another drafting method could have avoided the need for a date, his failure to use it does not prove that his intent was to transfer from today. People often write "my property is given to you from today and after thirty days," in which case the date is needed simply to identify when the thirty days begin. Rama therefore correctly records the Ramah's reasoning.
