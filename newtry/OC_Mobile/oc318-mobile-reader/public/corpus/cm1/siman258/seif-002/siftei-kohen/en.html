But only orally. Meaning, the deed of gift was written without qualification and bears the date on which it was written. When he hands over the deed, however, he stipulates orally that the gift is to take effect only after his death. Even so, the date written in the deed gives effect to the oral condition, and it is as though the deed expressly stated that he transfers it from today and after death. This is evident from the Gemara in Perek Mi Shemet, folio 72b; see Rashi there. The condition in the case of a bill of divorce is likewise oral. This is also the view of the Ran. The Sema did not reach the depth of this matter and offered an unusual explanation; his ruling also requires study. See Even HaEzer, siman 144, where this is stated explicitly.
