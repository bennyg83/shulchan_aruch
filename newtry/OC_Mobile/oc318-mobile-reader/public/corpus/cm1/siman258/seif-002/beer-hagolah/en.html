There is a name in M.M. M.M. M. M. M. M. M. M. M. M. M. M. M. M. M. M. M.M.R.:
He explains that since death itself clarifies the effective time, why would the document need any other date? Necessarily the date is written to transfer from that time, unlike where he writes that it should take effect after thirty days, etc.
His wording there is: not only does the date of the document prove, regarding what is written in the document, that it takes effect from today, but even with orally stated conditions like this case, 'this is your get if I do not return within twelve months,' etc., the date of the document proves that it takes effect from today. But the law of a date when the condition was stated entirely orally is not mentioned there. I did not merit seeing the Darkhei Moshe on this point. The Sema explains it as a case where the donor instructed the witnesses to remember the date, for an oral date certainly cannot be stronger than a written date in a document. In my humble opinion this still requires study.
