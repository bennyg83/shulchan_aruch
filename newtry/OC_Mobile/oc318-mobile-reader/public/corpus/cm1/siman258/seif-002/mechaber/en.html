If the document includes a kinyan, the recipient acquires the principal immediately even if the deed contains no date. Rama: Every formal kinyan is effective from now. The rule that the date of a deed proves present effect applies when the donor writes "after death." If he writes "after thirty days," the date does not prove present effect. Therefore, if donor or recipient dies within thirty days, the gift is void, and the donor may retract during that period. The date proves present effect only where the donor instructed that a date be written or ordered the witnesses to prepare a proper deed. A memorandum of a will bearing a date merely for record does not have that effect. Some say the principle is not limited to a written date; an orally stated date may likewise prove present effect.
