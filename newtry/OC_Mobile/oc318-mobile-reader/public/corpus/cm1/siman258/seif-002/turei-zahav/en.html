(We do not say that the date of the document proves present transfer.) The reason is stated by the Tur in the name of Ramah. Where one writes that the transfer is to take effect after his death, death itself identifies the operative time. Why, then, was it necessary to write a date? Evidently, he intended to transfer ownership from the date written in the document. Here, however, the date had to be written in order to determine from which day the thirty-day period begins. We therefore do not say that the date proves present transfer. The Sema answers that perhaps the grantor did not know which calendar date would be thirty days from the present, and therefore wrote, “from today and after thirty days,” as people commonly do. In the Tur’s text Ramah concludes, “and in all of these we do not say,” meaning that even in all the cases listed immediately before, we do not say that the date of the document proves present transfer, as we do regarding a transfer after death. Nevertheless, if they were effected by kinyan, they possess an advantage similar to a gift after death.
