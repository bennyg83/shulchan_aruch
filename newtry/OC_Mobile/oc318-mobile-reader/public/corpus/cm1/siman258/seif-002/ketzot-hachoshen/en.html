The same is true that the donor can retract within thirty days. Tur cites Rabbi Meir HaLevi Abulafia that the rule, "the date of the document proves the intent," applies specifically when property is written to take effect after death. But if one writes a gift to another person or to his son after thirty days and dies during the thirty days, the recipient does not acquire. The same is true if the recipient dies first or if the donor transfers the property to another during the interval. Even if one says that the date of the document proves an intention to begin acquisition now, the result is no stronger than the express phrase, "from now and after thirty days." Tosafot rules like Rabbi Yochanan that this language creates a retained future component. The acquisition begins now and finishes at the end of thirty days. The object must therefore remain in a place suitable for completion at the final time, and if the donor retracts or either party dies before completion, the acquisition fails. The formula "from now and after death" is different because it naturally means the body from now and fruits after death. It is not merely a thirty day incomplete acquisition. Thus even if the document date proves present intent, a fixed later date retains a future element and can be interrupted before completion. This sustains Rabbi Meir HaLevi Abulafia's practical ruling.
