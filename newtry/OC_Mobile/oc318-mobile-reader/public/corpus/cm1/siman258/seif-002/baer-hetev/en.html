Acquires. The Tur's wording is, 'He acquires the corpus immediately,' meaning together with the writing and delivery of the deed into his hand. Since the deed states that he conveys it to him from today, it is obvious that so long as the deed has not reached the recipient, the donor may retract. Sema.
Immediately. The Tur's wording is, 'when the deed reaches his hand.' Although ordinarily we hold that an unspecified kinyan is equivalent to saying 'from now' and takes effect immediately even before the deed is written, here it is different because this gift mentioned taking effect after death. Therefore, the deed must reach his hand. This is what the Tur and Mechaber mean by, 'Likewise, if there is...' The word 'likewise' shows that its law is like the first case, where it is obvious that the recipient does not acquire the corpus until the deed reaches his hand. So it appears to me, and I have found no one who disagrees. Accordingly, the Rama's concluding statement that every kinyan takes effect from now requires explanation, since it implies that the recipient acquires immediately from the time of the kinyan. Ibid.
Thirty days. However, the Gemara in the chapter Mi She'achazo contradicts this law and indicates that even one who gives property 'from now until twelve months' has the time limitation take effect if he dies within those twelve months. The Bach reconciles the view of the Ramah, but his resolution cannot be said according to the Maggid Mishneh. Examine this carefully; it requires study. See Even HaEzer, siman 144, seif 2, which explicitly indicates unlike the Ramah. Shach.
Orally. Meaning, the deed of gift was written without qualification and bears the date of writing, but when he delivered the deed of gift he orally stipulated that he was giving it after death. Even so, we say that the date written in the deed gives effect to the oral stipulation, and it is as though the deed explicitly states that he conveys it from today and after death. This is evident from Gittin 72b. See Rashi there. The condition regarding the get is oral, as explained there, and this is also the Ran's view. See there. The Sema offered an unusual explanation here, and his words also require study as a matter of law. See Even HaEzer, siman 144, where this is stated explicitly. Ibid.
