Seif 1. Question: A deed of gift states, "I give Shimon all my property as a gift of a healthy person from today and after death, except that while I am alive everything shall remain mine, and after my death everything shall be his from today." Is this like an ordinary deed stating "from today and after death," so that the donor cannot retract, or is this formulation different? Answer: It appears clear to me that he may retract while alive. Ordinarily, "from today and after death" is understood to transfer the body from today and the fruits after death. Here, however, he expressly stated that while alive everything remains his and only after death shall everything belong to the recipient retroactively. He therefore revealed that he did not intend the body to pass immediately. Even if the phrase "from today and after death" ordinarily permits only the standard interpretation, his later words immediately contradict it, similar to a document stating one hundred above and two hundred below. The later wording resembles "acquire from now and after thirty days." Beit Shmuel, Even HaEzer siman 40, cites Rashi that this means the transfer is conditional upon the donor not retracting during the thirty days, and he may retract. Rashba and Ramban disagree, but Tosafot and Ran appear to support Rashi, and Rashba in his novellae also rejects Ramban and agrees with Rashi, contrary to his responsum. At minimum the issue is doubtful. The party in possession therefore prevails. Since the heirs are in possession, the property cannot be extracted from them. I discussed this at length in the first edition of Mishpat Tzedek and record here only the practical conclusion.
