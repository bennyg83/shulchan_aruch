(Seif 2: even if it has no date.) Our master's text concludes: since the transfer was made with a kinyan, he acquires immediately when the document reaches his hand. The Beit Yosef explains that this means that once the document reaches him, he acquires retroactively from the date it was written. The Rama likewise appears to explain it this way, because he writes that every kinyan is treated as effective from now. Although the Sema, seif katan 3, explains "immediately" as meaning when the document reaches his hand, the Beit Yosef's explanation appears primary. This also resolves our master's citation of Ramah as disagreeing with the first opinion specifically where the document contains a date: if it has no date, it requires the words "from today," yet even if it says "from today" he acquires only from when the document reaches him. The Beit Yosef understood Ramah not to accept the principle that the date of a document proves its intended effective time. This is difficult, because afterward our master himself cites Ramah saying that the date of a document proves its intended time. The Beit Yosef is forced to say that Ramah there speaks according to the authority who accepts that principle. In my humble opinion, however, Ramah accepts the principle here as well. When he says specifically that the document has a date, he means that a date is actually mentioned in it, as our master said at the outset. Our master cites Ramah's disagreement only regarding the additional point that Ramah holds that even where the document says "from today," acquisition does not occur until the document reaches the recipient.
