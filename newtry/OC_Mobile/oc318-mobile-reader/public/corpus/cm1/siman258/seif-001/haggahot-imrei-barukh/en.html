(Shach, seif katan 2) However, the Gemara in chapter Mi SheAchazo. Note: See Even HaEzer 144, Beit Shmuel seif katan 4, who also raises this difficulty. At first glance the matter is very surprising. The Ramah writes explicitly that the case concerns a document taking effect after thirty days. In such a case it is certainly necessary to state the document's date, so as to know when the thirty days begin and end. This differs from a condition, "if I do not return from here within twelve months," where the get is written without a special effective date and the condition is oral, as explained in Gittin 72. It is true that there is room to say that even when a document is written to take effect after thirty days we may infer the effective date from the date written in the document. Since he did not write explicitly the date after thirty days, although he needed only the effective date and not the writing date, the written date shows his intent. This is suggested by Rashi, Ketubot 2b, passage beginning "where there is no." But the Ramah may hold that because it was necessary in any event to establish when the acquisition would occur, he wrote the date of the document so that the thirty-day period could be calculated from it. Rabbi Yochanan's rule applies only where the date of writing serves no purpose, such as a case in which the condition is entirely oral. Here, however, there was a genuine need to identify the date. What difference is there whether the date is stated directly as the day the acquisition will take effect, or indirectly by writing the date of the document from which the thirtieth day can be calculated?
