A gift document in which it is written, etc. Meaning, a document of a healthy person's gift. The Mechaber later says "whether or not it contains a kinyan." Although every healthy person's gift requires a kinyan, the case is one where the document itself says, "My field is given to you after my death," because such a document can itself effect acquisition for a healthy person, as the Tur and Mechaber write above in siman 191. The novelty is that even though it says "my field is given to you after death," without the words "from today and after death," and a document cannot effect acquisition after death, the stated date causes the recipient to acquire the principal from today, so the giver cannot retract, while the produce belongs to the recipient after death. Otherwise, why was a date written? The unqualified language of the authorities implies that there is no difference whether the date appears at the beginning or the end of the document. See the Perishah.
And not that he does not acquire, etc. Meaning, we do not interpret his intent as saying that the recipient should acquire only after death, in which case the gift would be ineffective.
He acquires after death. The Tur's language is: "He acquires the principal immediately and the giver cannot retract." "Immediately" means upon the writing and delivery of the document to him, because the document's date operates from today. Obviously, until the document reaches the recipient, the giver may retract.
