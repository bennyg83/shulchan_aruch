He acquires the corpus immediately. See Baer Heitev in the name of Sema concerning the wording of the Tur, concluding that therefore we require the deed to reach his hand. See Noda BiYehuda, second edition, siman 47, who writes that Sema's words are difficult. If the acquisition is defective because he mentioned that it should take effect after death, how does delivery of the deed help when the deed itself expressly states after death? He cites what he wrote in the margin of his Shulchan Aruch to siman 258: see siman 195, seif 5, where the rulings appear contradictory. There, if the buyer says, "Acquire this kerchief and acquire the object for me after thirty days," he does not acquire it, because when the time for acquisition arrives the kerchief has already been returned. Here, however, with a deed stating that the acquisition should occur after death, it is ruled that if there is a kinyan he acquires the corpus immediately even without a date. He explains that here, since a deed was also given, the kinyan was otherwise unnecessary, so it must have been intended to strengthen the recipient's rights. This accords with Bava Batra 136, where the Gemara initially considered distinguishing whether the kinyan was mentioned twice, because repetition indicates strengthening. Although the Gemara ultimately does not distinguish, the underlying reason remains that because the kinyan itself was unnecessary, it was clearly added so that he acquire from now. In siman 195, by contrast, the case involves only the kinyan. Nor should one distinguish between movables there and land here. First, the source for the view that he acquires is Nedarim 48b, which refers generally to property without distinguishing land from movables. Second, there is no logical reason to distinguish. Just as with land one may say that the corpus is acquired immediately and the fruits after death, so too with movables, many of which are rented out, and the donor can retain their use during his lifetime. See there further regarding the particular case.
