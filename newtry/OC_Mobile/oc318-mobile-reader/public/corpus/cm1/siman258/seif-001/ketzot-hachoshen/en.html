The date of the document proves the intent. The Jerusalem Talmud asks why in a gift to one's son the words "after death" are written and answers that they reserve the fruits, while in a get the same phrase cannot reserve the woman's body because one cannot have the woman married to one man while her bodily status belongs to another. It applies the same concept to emancipation and to ownerless designation. According to the Sages, "from now and after thirty days" can remain doubtful whether it is a condition or a retraction. In ownerless property there is an additional problem: a field cannot be made completely ownerless while its fruits remain privately owned, because ownerless designation must resemble the complete abandonment of Shemittah. Mishneh LaMelech asked why the body could not simply be ownerless from now and the fruits retained for thirty days, like the body and fruit division in a gift. Rashba already answers that genuine ownerless designation requires full abandonment to all. Retaining the fruits prevents the complete abandonment. This raises a practical question about an animal rented or lent to a gentile over Shabbat. Tur permits making it ownerless when the gentile fails to return it before Shabbat. Since the gentile still holds the temporary use right, how can the Jewish owner make the body ownerless? One may distinguish between retaining fruits for oneself, which prevents full abandonment, and a prior lease or loan in favor of another person. The owner is not retaining the fruits for himself. Alternatively, the owner's abandonment can be complete as to his remaining title even while the prior user's independent right continues. The issue still requires study.
Tur in Orach Chaim cites Semag permitting an animal hired to a gentile to be rented by an inclusive arrangement and then declared ownerless, and Rabbi Peretz permits declaring it ownerless for Shabbat. Beit Yosef says no one else will acquire it that Shabbat because the gentile has a present right through loan or rental. This proves that an owner can make property ownerless even while another has a temporary use right. Mishneh LaMelech asks another question. The Sages are uncertain whether "from now and after death" is a condition or retraction. Why, then, in emancipation does the slave not prevail through his possession of himself? The answer follows what I wrote in siman 88. A slave's own body is not independent possession against the master. If he remains a slave, his hand is the master's hand and his body is like the master's ox or donkey. Thus the very possession is itself in doubt. Possession founded on the disputed legal status is not sufficient to prevail. The same principle appears regarding one of uncertain gentile status. Where gentile status itself would eliminate the ordinary monetary presumption, a person whose very status as holder is doubtful cannot invoke possession against the prior owner. All the more, a slave cannot use his own physical control of his body to defeat the master's prior ownership when emancipation itself is uncertain.
I still find it difficult to identify the practical difference between treating "from now and after thirty days" as a condition or as a retraction in emancipation and ownerless designation. Rashi explains "condition" as, in effect, "from now, provided I do not retract within thirty days." If so, even on the condition reading, retraction within thirty days prevents the effect. On the retraction reading, if no retraction actually occurs, the act should likewise stand after thirty days. For emancipation one possible difference is where the bill of emancipation is torn before the thirty days. If it was a condition, the original document worked from now and later destruction does not matter. If the first wording was retracted and the emancipation was intended only at the later time, the destroyed document cannot operate then. That still leaves ownerless designation difficult. Rashba gives another interpretation of the condition reading, namely that the thirty days themselves are the condition and there is no retained power of retraction. On that reading the difference is clear: if he retracts within thirty days, the act stands according to the condition interpretation but fails according to the retraction interpretation. The Jerusalem Talmud adds that with consecration everyone agrees, because speech to Heaven is like delivery to an ordinary person. This can mean that in true consecration the word "from now" cannot be treated as retracted through the later phrase, since sacred speech is immediately fixed and cannot be withdrawn even within the ordinary interval. Thus it is read as a condition. Our charitable dedications, however, do not have that Temple consecration severity. Rama's ruling elsewhere that true consecration "from today and after thirty days" is not consecrated at all therefore remains difficult under either side and must be understood with the qualifications discussed in siman 257.
