Maggid Mishneh notes that our Halakhot do present it as a dispute. (Supplement) Some say, etc. See Baer HaGolah. The first tanna does not accept Rav Ashi's rule that a slave is acquired by pulling because he is compared to land, while Rabbi Shimon holds that in this respect he is compared to movable property, and Rav Ashi agrees. The reason is as Tosafot, Bava Kamma 12a, explain: on Torah law a slave is like land, but for rabbinic law he is like movable property. Rosh, Ramban, and the other authorities rule this way, unlike Rif, as stated below in siman 363, seif 1. The Yerushalmi, first chapter of Kiddushin, states that the Mishna indicates slaves are like movable property, for it teaches how possession is made in slaves, and Rabbi Shimon says, etc. Although the Yerushalmi does not distinguish Torah law from rabbinic law, it shows that this is the point of disagreement. The Yerushalmi understands that the first tanna concedes to Rabbi Shimon, whereas the Babylonian Talmud understands that they disagree. (End of supplement.)
