And it is also acquired by meshichah. Pnei Yehoshua asks how a slave can be acquired by meshichah when a slave is compared to land. He answers that we do not derive what is possible from what is impossible, since land cannot physically be moved. But this answer is difficult, because siman 202 concerning a perforated flowerpot rules that the plants are not acquired through meshichah of the pot because land is not acquired by meshichah. This is explicit in Gittin 22 and shows that even where movement is physically possible, the law of land does not become meshichah acquisition. The first answer of Pnei Yehoshua is therefore primary. His further question, that the enactment of meshichah due to fear that "your wheat may burn" does not apply to a slave, is likewise no difficulty. Even where that particular concern is absent, the Sages did not abolish meshichah as an acquisition. They did not differentiate. This is evident from the cases in siman 198 where money acquisition remains effective because the concern of "your wheat may burn" does not apply. Surely meshichah still works there. The absence of that concern preserves money acquisition; it does not cancel meshichah.
