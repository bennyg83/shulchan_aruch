But if he did not know of the error. The Shach writes that even in a fixed price item, where agent and principal ordinarily divide, if the agent did not know of the mistake before giving the property to the principal, the entire gain belongs to the principal because the agent never acquired it knowingly. In my view that is incorrect under Rashi's reason. In a fixed price item, the excess is treated as a gift, and there is doubt whether the seller intended the gift for the agent. Where another person is transferring property, one can acquire even without knowing at the moment of acquisition. Lack of subjective awareness does not prevent acquisition in the same way it does with ownerless property or a found object. Thus even if the agent did not know, the agent can still acquire his share. The same follows according to Rif. If division is because the principal received benefit through the agent and the agent through the principal, that mutual causal structure exists regardless of the agent's actual awareness of the excess. Rama therefore states the lack of awareness rule specifically in the case of a gentile's error, which is treated like ownerless property. In ownerless property there is no transferring mind, and an unaware person may fail to acquire, especially where the benefit is unusual. Even there the matter remains disputed among the authorities concerning whether a person's hand or courtyard acquires something uncommon without his knowledge. Since several authorities hold that it does, if the agent later seizes his claimed share from the principal, it should not necessarily be removed from him. But with the ordinary fixed price addition, where there is a transferring mind, the parties divide whether or not the agent knew at the time.
