Seif 6: The market price was fixed. This means, for example, an item sold by measure where the seller added one unit. The Sema correctly writes that a judge may rule like the Rif and Rosh according to the circumstances. In my opinion, that is the principal legal view, because the Rif, Rosh, and Ittur follow one approach. Rabbeinu Tam and Ri likewise explain that the principal shares in the gain because the profit came through his money. Rashi is therefore a lone view. Indeed, even Rashi must concede part of the Rif's reasoning. Otherwise, why should they divide the gain rather than apply the rule that the claimant bears the burden of proof, as in other unresolved doubts? We must say that the principal has a definite claim because his money caused the gain, while the agent also has a definite claim because he dealt with the seller. Since neither basis is clearly primary, they divide, and possession does not decide the matter.
