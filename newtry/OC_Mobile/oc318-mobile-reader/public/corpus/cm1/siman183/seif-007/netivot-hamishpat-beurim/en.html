He sent his agent. See Sema, seif katan 21, who says that where the gain arose from a mistake in the transaction the parties divide when the price was fixed. The Yerushalmi cited by Tosafot indeed implies this. Nevertheless, the underlying reason why the parties divide still requires explanation.
Everything belongs to the agent. See Sema, seif katan 22, who cites Ramban's responsum but abbreviates it. Ramban's actual case is one where the agent claims that he personally benefited the non Jew in some other matter and that this is why the non Jew gave him the additional amount. On that claim the extra belongs to the agent even according to Rif and Rosh, who ordinarily explain division on the ground that the agent profited through the principal's money. But if the agent makes no such claim and merely says that the non Jew gave extra in connection with the transaction, then according to Rif and Rosh the added amount is divided.
But if he did not know. See Shach, seif katan 14. Rama follows Mordechai in placing this rule here. His point is that specifically in a mistaken overpayment, which resembles a found object, a person does not acquire without awareness when the gain is something unusual that does not commonly come to him, as explained in siman 268, seif 3, in the gloss. But in seif 6, where the seller intentionally gave an additional amount, if the agent did not know of the addition and witnesses later observed that it was in fact intentionally added, then even if the agent forgot and delivered everything to the principal, the principal must return the agent's share. An intentional addition resembles a gift. Where another person knowingly transfers a gift, the recipient can acquire even without awareness because there is da'at acheret makneh, as Rashba writes in Gittin 20. Shach therefore concludes that even in seif 6, if the case is not a gift-like addition but a mistake in the transaction, then where the agent was unaware, the entire gain belongs to the principal. A mistaken gain resembles a found object and there is no other person's conscious intent to transfer it. The agent therefore never acquired it.
