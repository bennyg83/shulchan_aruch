To the agent. This ruling is compatible even with the Rif and those who agree with him in the preceding seif. If the agent stole, robbed, or deceived someone, what connection does the owner of the money have to that act? The Beit Yosef writes this as well, and it is necessarily so, because the Rosh above rules like the Rif, yet here he holds that everything belongs to the agent. See Responsa Maharam Alshich, siman 1, and Responsa Maharashdam, simanim 26 and 27. Shach.
The principal. The Rama follows the Mordechai, who records this ruling here. It nevertheless appears to me that even in seif 6, with an item having a fixed price where the excess is divided, if the agent did not know of the seller's error before he delivered the merchandise to the principal, everything belongs to the principal, because the agent cannot acquire without awareness. If the principal says that perhaps the agent did not know, while the agent claims with certainty that he did know, the money remains in the principal's possession. So it appears to me. Shach. Editor's note: In my humble opinion, it is not so clear that this case can be compared to seif 6. There, one may say that the seller expressly conferred the excess upon the agent, as the poskim explain their reasoning. If so, the agent may acquire even without knowing of it, unlike here. This requires further analysis.
They divide it. Darchei Moshe writes that Rabbeinu Yerucham and the Rosh also agree here because both parties were involved. His words require study. See the Mordechai and the responsa printed with Maimonides concerning one who sends merchandise to another place and customs duty is certainly owed to oppressive authorities, but the agent, through ingenuity and skill, smuggles it past the customs officials. This is not merely removing an uncertain danger, because the customs loss was definite, and therefore the owner must pay the agent compensation for the benefit he produced. Numerous distinctions and unresolved questions in this law are found in Responsa Mahara Sasson, siman 201. See there. Shach.
