If the seller stated explicitly. Sema cites Ran that according to Rif's explanation, where the parties divide because the benefit reached the agent through the principal, even if the seller expressly says that he is giving the addition to the agent, the principal still shares in it. This is understandable only where the seller gives specifically to the agent, because the benefit reached him through the principal's transaction. But if the seller expressly gives the additional amount to the principal, then even according to Rif it belongs wholly to the principal. Surely one who gives a gift to another through an agent does not thereby give the agent a share. Ramban likewise writes that even under the explanation based on benefit reaching the agent through the principal, if the agent can establish that he himself provided the seller the benefit for which the addition was given, the addition belongs entirely to the agent. Thus the identity of the intended recipient remains decisive.
