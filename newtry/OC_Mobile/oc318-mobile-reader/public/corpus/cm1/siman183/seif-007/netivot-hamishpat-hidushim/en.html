Everything belongs to the agent. This is true even according to those who hold that profit on a fixed price item is divided because it arose through the principal's money. Here everything belongs to the agent, because if the agent stole, robbed, or deceived the gentile, what connection does the principal have to that? The agent could simply tell the gentile, "You made a mistake in the calculation." Shach. This applies specifically to a mistake in arithmetic. If the mistake concerns the value of the sale itself, then for an item with no fixed price the entire gain belongs to the owner of the money, while for an item with a fixed price they divide. The reason is that perhaps the seller mistakenly believed the item itself was worth no more than the amount received. Sema. Ramban ruled regarding a community that appointed Reuven as agent to pay a tax and the ruler favored him personally: if Reuven claims that he benefited the ruler through some separate matter, the entire benefit belongs to Reuven. If he makes no such claim, the benefit is divided according to those authorities who reason that profit created through the principal's money is shared. The Sema abbreviated this responsum.
