He sent an agent to receive money, etc. The Tur cites Rabbeinu Yitzchak and Rosh that this law applies specifically where the agent caused the non-Jew to err in an accounting, but not where the non-Jew erred in the sale itself and gave merchandise worth more than the price paid. The reason is that with a sales error, the non-Jew gives the extra merchandise in exchange for the dinar he received, because he mistakenly thought the merchandise was worth no more than that amount. His intent is therefore to transfer what he gives in exchange for the money. Chazal consequently say that where the merchandise has a fixed price, the extra is divided and the agent does not take it entirely for himself, because at least the seller intended to transfer it in exchange for the payment. In an accounting error, however, nothing is presently given to the non-Jew. He knows the amount actually owed, and the excess is a separate matter, comparable to money taken from him. Had the agent wished, he could have told the non-Jew that he had made a calculation error and returned the excess. This differs from Rabbeinu Tam's view, cited by the Tur, that with an error in the sale itself, if the merchandise has a fixed price, the entire extra belongs to the principal. It is surprising that the Mechaber and Rama omit the law of a sales error. Since the authorities dispute it, they should have explained whether there is any distinction between an error in the sale and an intentional bonus. According to Rabbeinu Tam the entire extra belongs to the principal in every such sale, while Rabbeinu Yitzchak and Rosh divide a fixed-price excess. See what I wrote nearby and in the Derishah.
Everything belongs to the agent. Ramban likewise rules in responsum 69 concerning a community that appointed Reuven as agent to collect taxes and pay a non-Jew, and the non-Jew personally benefited Reuven from communal money, that the benefit belongs entirely to Reuven. See Darkei Moshe, who discusses this at length.
Everything belongs to the principal. The agent cannot acquire an unsolicited benefit without the giver's intent being directed to him.
And he assisted him and caused the non-Jew to err, etc. Darkei Moshe gives the reason that because both parties were engaged in the transaction, they divide the resulting gain even though only one of them actually caused the non-Jew's error.
