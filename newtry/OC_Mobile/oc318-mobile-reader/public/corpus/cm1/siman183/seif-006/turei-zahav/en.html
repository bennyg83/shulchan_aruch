There, in the gloss: Reuven knew. The Sema asks why Reuven's initial express consent is ineffective here, whereas in siman 269 such consent is effective according to all. He answers that here Reuven claims he expressly retracted before the purchase and said that he was buying only for himself. This is difficult, because once he initially agreed to acquire jointly, the other person acquired through him as one who lifts a found object for his fellow. In such a case, siman 269 rules that the lifter is not believed to say, “I lifted it for myself,” even while it remains in his hand. If witnesses heard an express retraction, the rule is obvious and would not need to be stated. The Bach's distinction is also unsatisfactory. The Ittur's own ruling can be explained where the other party lacked funds, for then initial consent is ineffective, unlike a found object that both could acquire. The concluding clause mentioning Reuven's money is merely the usual case, because one who has money normally contributes his share. The Ittur therefore disagrees with the later view in siman 269 that consent is effective even without funds. The Rama's position requires study. In practice, we follow the rule at the end of siman 269 that express consent is effective.
