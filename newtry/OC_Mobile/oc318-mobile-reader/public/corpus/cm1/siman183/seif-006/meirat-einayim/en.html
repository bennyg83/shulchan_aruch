The price is fixed and known. For example, meat or wine is sold by measure or weight at a standard price, such as one dinar for a stated measure or weight. If the seller gives the agent more than the standard measure for that dinar, strictly speaking the entire excess should belong to the agent. But because the agent obtained this benefit through the principal's money, the Sages ruled that they divide the excess. This is the reason given by the Rif, Rosh, and Baal HaItur. The Ran writes that according to them, even if the seller explicitly says that he gives the excess to the agent personally, they still divide it. Rashi, however, explains that the law rests on uncertainty as to whom the seller intended to benefit, the principal or the agent, and therefore they divide it. Ramban writes that Rashi's reason is correct. Rama follows that approach when he writes that if the seller explicitly states to whom the excess is given, etc. Nevertheless, I have stated the reasoning of the Rif, Rosh, and Baal HaItur because, depending on the circumstances, a judge may properly rely upon them.
