The price was fixed. See Taz, who questions Rashi's approach. If the law depends on uncertainty, ordinary monetary rules would place the burden of proof on the claimant, not require division. It appears this is the intent of Tosafot in Ketubot 98, who write that the reason they give is primary because the possessor has the stronger hand and therefore the parties divide. Their wording is initially puzzling. Tosafot had already proved from the Yerushalmi that one cannot say the entire gain belongs to the owner of the money. The remaining uncertainty would seem to be only whether all belongs to the agent, in which case possession should not generate a fifty-fifty split. Their point can be that this itself disproves Rashi's uncertainty approach. If the matter were merely doubtful, possession would determine the result rather than equal division. Therefore the reason must be as Tosafot explain: the agent profited through the principal's money, and equitable allocation itself requires division.
