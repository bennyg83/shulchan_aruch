And its price is fixed. Meaning, for example, meat or wine is sold by measure or weight and a fixed amount of the measure or weight is sold for one dinar. If the seller gives more than the fixed measure for the dinar, strictly speaking the entire excess should belong to the agent. However, because the benefit came through the principal's money, the Sages ruled that they divide the excess. This rationale is given by the Rif, Rosh, and Itur. The Ran writes that according to them, even if the seller expressly says that he gives the excess to the agent, they nevertheless divide it. Rashi, however, explains that the reason is that we are uncertain for whom the seller intended the gift. The Ramban writes that this explanation is correct. This is the basis of the Rama's ruling that if the seller explicitly says to whom he gives it, the result differs. The Sema writes that nevertheless, depending upon the circumstances as assessed by the dayan, one may sometimes rely upon the first rationale. The Shach expresses surprise that the Rama ruled so categorically against the Rif and the other authorities cited above, and he writes that the Yerushalmi also follows the Rif's reasoning that the benefit came through the principal's money. See Responsa Maharashdam, siman 458. The Taz likewise holds that the primary halachah is that they divide the excess even where the seller expressly says he gives it to the agent. See there.
