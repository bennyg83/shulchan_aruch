An agent, etc., although, etc. Bava Metzia 10a states: but if he said to him, etc., because the fruits of his labor belong to the principal. See Rashi there, s.v. nikash. The amount is deducted, etc., and this is like converting it into a loan.
(Supplement) An agent, etc. Bava Metzia 10a states: but if he said to him, etc. Necessarily, this is where he lifted it for himself. If he lifted it for the employer, then even in the first clause it belongs to the employer, according to the conclusion that one who lifts a found object for another acquires it for the other. This is also clear from Rava's challenge to Rav Nachman, which is resolved according to the view that the other acquires it. Since the worker's labor belongs to the employer, it is like the employer's own money. (End of supplement.)
Nevertheless, if. There the Gemara states that if he said, 'Whatever,' etc., then when he retracts, etc. It applies specifically when he expressly said so, as stated in Bava Kamma 117a: here the case concerns a worker, etc. Rashba adduces proof from Bava Kamma 102b.
And some say. This is not comparable to the case in Bava Metzia cited above. There, when he retracted, the worker's labor no longer belonged to the employer. Here, however, the money belongs to the principal, as explained above in seifim 1 and 2.
