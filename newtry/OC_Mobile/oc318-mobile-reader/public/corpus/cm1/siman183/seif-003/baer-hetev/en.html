On credit. And there are witnesses to this. This appears to be the view of the Rambam and Mechaber, and the Maggid Mishneh likewise indicates that this is the Rambam's view. The Rama's wording is imprecise. See Responsa Maharashdam, simanim 175 and 177, and Responsa Rashach, part 2, simanim 103 and 174. Shach.
Who reverts. Because there is no greater unauthorized use than this. The dissenting opinions hold that even through unauthorized use the agent does not acquire the item unless he changes the transaction, such as where the principal instructed him to buy wheat and he instead bought barley for himself. In that case he acquires it through the change. Sema.
For himself. Maharashal likewise rules in chapter 9 of Bava Kamma, siman 31, where there was a change. It is possible that even the 'some say' opinion agrees in such a case, as the Sema writes. See Maharashal there, though his words are not compelling. See Responsa R. Moshe Alshich, end of the cited siman, and Responsa Mabit, part 1, siman 105. Shach.
The principal. It appears that this is the halachah, since the Rif and Rambam hold this view and the Tur cites their words and reasoning. Presumably we say that the principal certainly did not retract and prefers that his credibility remain intact. Taz.
