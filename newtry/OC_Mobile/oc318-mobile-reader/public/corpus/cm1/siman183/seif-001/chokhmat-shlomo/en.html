Seif 1. Note: I saw that Netivot HaMishpat concludes that an agent or partner who negligently fails to purchase merchandise must compensate even the lost profit when the market price was established. In my opinion this is incorrect. The Gemara's point is that the Sages treated the standard agricultural undertaking, “If I leave the field fallow and do not work it, I will pay the expected yield,” as though written because both the act and its resulting profit are ordinarily within the person's control and the situation is common. Purchasing merchandise is different. Since in most cases the resulting profit is not fully within his control, the Sages never instituted such an implied obligation, even in an occasional case where profit happens to be highly predictable. There is a further reason. If a low market price is stable, the availability of cheap goods is common but the later profit is not. If the low price is merely temporary, eventual appreciation may be likely, but the initial opportunity to buy cheaply is itself uncommon. Thus one can never say that both the opportunity and the profit are the ordinary, controlled situation contemplated by the rabbinic enactment. Therefore a partner or agent is not liable merely for preventing expected profit unless he expressly undertook such liability. In monetary matters an express condition is valid. This appears correct in practice.
