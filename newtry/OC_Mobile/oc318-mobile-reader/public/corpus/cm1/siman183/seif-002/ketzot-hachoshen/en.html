He purchased it ordinarily with his own money. Rabbeinu Yerucham writes that if one buys with his own money while intending to acquire for another person, the other person does not acquire, even if the buyer declares before witnesses, "I am buying with my own money for my fellow," unless he informs the seller. Beit Yosef challenged this from Rosh in Bava Kamma. The Shach writes that he does not understand Beit Yosef's challenge, because Rosh appears explicitly to agree with Rabbeinu Yerucham, particularly in the discussion of a dyer who dyed garments for another. Darkei Moshe likewise says that the Tur's phrase, "It is as though he lent him the money," does not contradict Rabbeinu Yerucham. There the principal expressly told the agent, "Go buy for me." That case is different. As explained below, where the agent uses his own money entirely on his own initiative, mere intention to acquire for another does not suffice without seller notification.
