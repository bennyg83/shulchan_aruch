One who gives money to another, etc. R. Yerucham writes in Netiv 28, part I, that if a person purchases with his own money intending to acquire for another, the other does not acquire, even if he declares before witnesses, 'I am purchasing with my money for my fellow,' unless he informs the seller. Beit Yosef cites this in Mechudash 3 and challenges it from the Rosh in the ninth chapter of Bava Kamma. I do not understand the challenge, for the Rosh there explicitly agrees with R. Yerucham, in his discussion explaining why the case of R. Abba concerning a dyer who dyed wool for himself poses no difficulty. R. Yerucham therefore states this law in the ninth chapter of Bava Kamma. Although it is not explicit in the Gemara, R. Yerucham regularly records a ruling when it is explicit in the Rosh, and this occurs frequently throughout his work. Darkei Moshe also writes that the Tur's statement, 'it is as though he lent him the money,' differs from R. Yerucham, who holds that even where one buys with his own money intending for another, the other does not acquire, even if he said before witnesses that he was buying for his fellow, unless he later informed the seller. Darkei Moshe adds that Beit Yosef understood the Rosh differently. This is incorrect, as I have written. Nor is there proof from the Tur's phrase, for that case is different because the principal expressly told him, 'Go purchase for me.' This appears correct. Yet the Rosh himself raises a difficulty. In the Gemara's case of one who purchases land in the name of the Exilarch, why should this be presented as depending on whether one follows the scholars of the West? Even those who disagree with the scholars of the West require that the beneficiary be the one whose money was used, whereas the Exilarch did not provide the money. The case should resemble a dyer who dyes wool for himself, where a person cannot transfer his own property to another merely through intent. It appears to me that there, because the purchaser instructed the witnesses to write the deed in the Exilarch's name, the witnesses act on behalf of the Exilarch. Examine carefully. The matter still requires study. See Responsa Rashdam, simanim 125 and 155.
