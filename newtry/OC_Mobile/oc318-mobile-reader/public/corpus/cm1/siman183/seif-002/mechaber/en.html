If one gives another money to buy land or movable property for him, and the agent leaves the principal's money untouched but buys the property for himself with his own money, the acquisition is valid for the agent, though he is counted among the deceitful. If the agent knows that this seller favors and honors him and will sell to him but not to the principal, he may buy for himself, provided he first informs the principal. If he fears another buyer will precede him, he may buy for himself and inform the principal afterward. Rama: If he bought unspecified property with his own money, he may afterward say, 'I bought it for myself.' This applies to unspecified land. Where the principal designated a particular property, because taking it for himself is deceitful, we presume that he originally bought it for the principal and he cannot later withdraw. If the agent acquired it through a method of acquisition without paying money, the principal acquires it, and even a later sale by the agent is ineffective.
