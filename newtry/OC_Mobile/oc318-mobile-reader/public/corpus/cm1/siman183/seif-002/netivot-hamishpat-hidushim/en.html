The Sema writes that even if the agent already knew, at the time the principal told him to buy, of some fact making the purchase especially suitable for the agent, but feared that if he informed the principal the principal would compete to buy it himself, the agent may buy it for himself and inform the principal only afterward. The Taz disagrees. Only if the new fact became known to the agent after the agency began may he buy for himself. If he knew it when instructed, he must immediately inform the principal. The Shach writes that the same applies where the item is more suitable for the agent than the principal, for example in a region controlled by violent people who fear the agent but not the principal. The agent may then buy for himself. The Shach further cites Rabbeinu Yerucham: if Reuven buys land with his own money intending to acquire it for Shimon but does not inform the seller, Shimon does not acquire, even if Reuven intended it as a gift to him, unless the seller was informed. But if Levi had said to Shimon, "Go buy it for me," then Shimon acquires for Levi because the arrangement is treated as though Levi had lent him the purchase money, as explained in seif 4.
