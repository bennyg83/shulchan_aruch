[Shach, letter 2] His fellow did not acquire it. Note: See Rashba's novellae to Bava Kamma 102b. He writes there that if one who received an investment misappropriated part of the investment and continues to conduct business with the remainder, then any profit from the remainder ordinarily belongs to the owner of the capital. If it decreases, the loss is the recipient's, because the entire investment came into his domain so that he became liable for unavoidable accidents from the moment he misappropriated part of it. If it increased, the increase belongs to the owner of the money, because he is presumed to be conducting the business on the owner's behalf. If he is a partner, the increase is shared. Even if he later returned the portion he had taken and profited from it, then even according to those who rule like Rabbi Akiva that return requires the owner's knowledge, any subsequent increase belongs to the owner of the money, or is shared if he is a partner, because he intends to acquire the gain for the owner of the money. A responsum of the Rashba is cited by Beit Yosef, siman 176, seif 36, in the additional rulings. See there.
