Seif 2: One who gives money to his fellow. Note: See Shach, seif katan 2, citing Rabbeinu Yerucham. Machaneh Ephraim discusses the issue at length. I propose the following distinction. There are two forms of agency. In one, the agent transfers his legal power to the principal, so that the agent's hand is treated as the principal's hand. In the other, the principal transfers his power to the agent, so that the agent remains himself but is additionally empowered to act with the principal's legal capacity. This distinction explains Rabbeinu Yerucham. If the agent is treated as transformed into the principal, then when Shimon purchases while the seller believes he is buying for himself and does not know he represents Reuven, the seller has conveyed only to Shimon as the apparent purchaser and not to Reuven. But if Reuven has empowered Shimon while Shimon remains the actual actor, the seller has certainly conveyed to Shimon, and by virtue of Shimon's agency Reuven acquires as well. Accordingly, the Bavli and Yerushalmi need not conflict. The Bavli can concern a learned principal who understands how to create the effective form of agency, and particularly minors, who cannot themselves transfer legal power to a principal; rather, the principal empowers them. Their redemption or purchase can therefore operate for him. The Yerushalmi explicitly concerns an am ha'aretz who tells a chaver to act. An unlearned principal does not ordinarily understand how to transfer his legal power to the agent, so the agency may instead function in the opposite direction, and if the seller was not informed the principal does not acquire. This may reconcile the Rosh and Rabbeinu Yerucham. Even those who say the principal does not acquire can be speaking where the agent independently purchases for another, or where an unlearned principal did not properly structure the agency. Where a knowledgeable principal expressly appointed an agent to buy for him, all may agree that the principal acquires. Even if this was not the original intent of the authorities, it appears to me the proper practical resolution.
