The one who supplied the money. Rabbeinu Yerucham writes: If a person purchases with his own money while intending to acquire for his fellow, the fellow does not acquire, even if the purchaser stated before witnesses, I am purchasing with my money for my fellow, unless he informed the seller. The Beit Yosef cites this and questions it from the Rosh in chapter 9 of Bava Kamma. I do not understand his objection, for the Rosh there explicitly implies the same as Rabbeinu Yerucham. See there regarding the statement that one should not infer from Rabbi Abba's case of the dyer who dyed wool for their sake. Likewise, the Tur's expression that it is as though he lent him the money is no proof, because there the principal expressly told him, Go buy for me. So it appears to me. The Rosh's own position, however, is difficult. If so, how can the Gemara say concerning one who purchases a field from the Exilarch that we should say the matter depends upon the dispute concerning the people of the West? Even according to the dissenting opinion, the buyer must be the one who supplied the money, whereas the Exilarch supplied no money. The case should therefore resemble a dyer who dyes wool for another, where one cannot confer ownership of his own property upon another without an act of acquisition. One must answer that because the buyer instructed the witnesses to write the deed in the Exilarch's name, the witnesses stand in the Exilarch's place. Consider this carefully. It still requires study. See Responsa Maharashdam, simanim 125 and 155. Shach.
