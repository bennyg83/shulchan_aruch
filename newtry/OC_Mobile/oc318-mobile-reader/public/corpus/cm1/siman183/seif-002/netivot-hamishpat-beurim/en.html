One who gives money. See Shach, seif katan 2, through his statement that the principal must say, "Go buy it for me." The matter requires explanation. Rosh says that in the dyer's case the principal does not acquire because the agent buys with his own money and the seller transfers to the agent; one cannot transfer his own property to another merely by private intent. Although zekhiyah is based on agency, we do not say that the buyer automatically makes himself the principal's agent to acquire directly from the seller. Why then does it help when the principal says, "Go buy it for me"? The distinction is that when one says, "Buy for me with your money," once the agent gives money to the seller pursuant to that instruction, the principal becomes obligated to reimburse him. It is like saying, "Give money to so and so and I will be obligated to you." Because the agent expends money on the principal's word, the money is treated as though lent to the principal for purposes of the purchase. Shach also questions Rosh from the incident involving the exilarch. Shach appears to understand the Gemara's question there, "Does he acquire?" as referring to the exilarch, meaning that perhaps the property becomes the exilarch's because the buyer did not inform the seller but only witnesses. Rosh's responsum, kelal 96, siman 3, also appears to read it that way, and on that reading Shach's objection is strong. According to the other commentators, however, the Gemara's question concerns the buyer, whether he himself acquires. A buyer can certainly acquire for himself. See Shitah Mekubetzet on Bava Kamma and Ketzot HaChoshen. On that interpretation there is no difficulty.
Regarding specified land. It appears that even where there are no witnesses to the agency, the agent is not believed through a migo to say he purchased for himself. The land enters the principal's chazakah immediately upon the agent's purchase, and the agent cannot remove it from that chazakah merely through a migo. With movable property the matter requires study. There a migo may allow the holder to retain, and migo can operate even where his claim makes him appear wicked. The reason he is generally not believed here, even to avoid paying money, is that an agent who behaves deceitfully is confronted by the strong chazakah that an agent performs his mission. But where he also has a migo, perhaps he is believed, since a migo used to retain property can prevail even against a chazakah. I later found Tumim in siman 83 expressly ruling, concerning one who lifts a found object, that he is believed through a migo to say, "I intended to acquire for myself." The poskim compare our case to lifting a found object, as appears in seif 4 in the gloss and in Taz. Therefore the same rule should apply here where the agent has a true migo.
