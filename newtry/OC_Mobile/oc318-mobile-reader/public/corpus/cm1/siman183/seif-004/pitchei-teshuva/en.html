The agent before witnesses. See Responsa Noda BiYehuda, Choshen Mishpat 30, section 15. In his case he writes that the argument of the judge, Rabbi Meir, that the officer must be charged because when it is known that he purchased with the sender's money the property may be taken from him against his will, has no force. First, that rule applies where witnesses know that he purchased with the sender's money. If the only knowledge comes from his own admission, and he is the very mouth that says he misappropriated the money and bought for himself, then according to the authorities cited in the Rama, siman 183:3, who hold that such a declaration is effective when made before witnesses, he is believed through the principle that the mouth that created the prohibition is the mouth that released it. Second, what relevance do "the sender's money" have there? The Rama in seif 2 says that even if the agent acquired without money, using another mode of acquisition, the sender acquires. That rule, however, concerns a specifically designated parcel of land, as is clear from the context, not unspecified land. Noda BiYehuda repeats this point briefly in section 19 regarding the rule at the end of siman 269, where Reuven tells Shimon to buy certain merchandise jointly for them. He writes that the rule applies specifically where witnesses know that the merchandise was purchased. His words show that he does not accept the Taz's absolute ruling, cited by Baer Heitev, seif katan 9, that in every case the property belongs to the sender. Rather, he regards the matter as a doubt of law, and because of that doubt the officer, who was in possession, could not be charged. See the next seif katan.
