In Reuven's name. The Sema summarizes: if the agent uses his own money without specifying the principal's name, he may retract before meshichah and buy for himself. If he uses the principal's money, even without naming the principal, he cannot retract before meshichah unless he informs the seller that he is withdrawing from the agency and buying for himself, and he must also designate different money for the purchase. The Taz disagrees and requires both advantages before the agent is prevented from retracting: specifically where he uses the principal's money and buys expressly in the principal's name. If either element is missing, he can retract before meshichah by designating other money. See Beurim for the Sema's rationale.
And even if the principal later consented. The Sema asks from siman 269, where he concludes that according to everyone, if the principal later consents, acquisition takes effect for both, and if Reuven had funds there everyone agrees he acquires. Here it sounds as though acquisition is valid only if bought with Reuven's money. He answers that here the agent retracted before the acquisition, while siman 269 concerns retraction afterward. The Taz objects because the case is compared to one who lifts a found object for another, and there the agent is not believed after lifting to say that he had retracted beforehand. He therefore rules that later consent is effective. The Shach distinguishes: here they never made a complete agreement about how and for what price the item would be acquired, while siman 269 concerns a fully settled arrangement. See Beurim: where the principal said, "Buy this merchandise for me," and it was not a bargain purchase, then even if the principal supplied no money and the agent merely promised to lend him funds, the agent does not become a wrongdoer merely by retracting from the promise to lend. But he is considered deceitful if he buys the merchandise for himself without informing the principal. Since such conduct is improper, he is not believed to say that he bought for himself, as explained in seif 2 and seif 4. With unusually cheap merchandise, comparable to a found object, buying it for oneself is not ordinarily called wicked. Therefore, if the agent had not received the principal's money and merely promised to lend his own, he is certainly believed to say that he retracted, because the presumption that an agent performs his mission does not apply when he may simply have discovered that the principal was not creditworthy. Even if the principal's money was in his hands but he bought with his own funds, the presumption of agency still does not apply because his conduct itself shows deviation. By contrast, siman 269 concerns a found object, where the presumption that an agent performs his agency does apply. The same is true of bargain merchandise there where the principal sent him only to perform the act of acquisition while planning to reimburse him. In those circumstances the agent cannot later say, "I bought for myself," just as with a found object. If the principal never said, "Go buy for me," but the purchaser himself said, "I am buying this for a partnership," he may later say, "I was joking with you." Beit Yosef in the name of Rashba.
