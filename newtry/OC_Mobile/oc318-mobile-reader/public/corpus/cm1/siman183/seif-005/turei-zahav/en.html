There: And he said that he was buying for himself. The Sema infers from the opening clause that if the agent paid his own money expressly on Reuven's behalf, he cannot retract even before meshichah. This is astonishing, since no acquisition has yet occurred. Although the Rema may follow his own view in siman 184 that an agent who buys for another cannot retract even expressly, there the agent had already paid the principal's money. Here he paid his own money, and until the other person acquires by meshichah he can certainly retract. Rabbeinu cites the Rema here as halachah, yet in siman 184 agrees with Rabbi Yeshayah that an express retraction is effective. The cases must therefore be distinguished as stated. In my opinion, the agent may retract before meshichah. The opening clause is phrased generally only to teach that without an express retraction Shimon is not believed to say, “I bought for myself,” and Reuven acquires. An express retraction is effective unless two advantages coincide: the money belongs to the principal and the agent names the principal before the seller. In that case he cannot retract until he informs the seller. The Sema's claim that Rabbeinu's phrase “on Reuven's behalf” is imprecise is itself difficult, because the opening clause likewise involved an unstated purchase on Reuven's behalf. Rather, both factors are needed. Although elsewhere the principal acquires in every case when his money was used, that is because the agent's later retraction cannot undo the principal's money. Here, at the time of retraction, the agent designates other money for Reuven. A further difficulty remains. The requirement to notify the seller follows the Western scholars in Bava Kamma, while Rabbi Yochanan disputes them and makes the matter depend only on the agent's intent. The Rif and Rosh omit the Western view, and the Rosh expressly rejects it. The Rema appears to rule like the Western scholars, and Rabbeinu should not have cited that view without qualification. The matter requires study.
