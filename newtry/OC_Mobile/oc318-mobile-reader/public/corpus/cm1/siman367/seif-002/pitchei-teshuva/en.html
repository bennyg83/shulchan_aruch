And he swore to him. Baer HaGolah cites Beit Yosef, who suggests that the requirement of an oath here may not apply to the preceding rule. Darchei Moshe already disagrees, as cited by Sema above, seif katan 9, and Bach says the same. Biur HaGra likewise writes that the oath is specifically required. Mishneh LaMelech, Hilchot Gezelah, chapter 3, law 5, also questions Beit Yosef and cites Rashba, volume 2, siman 308, who writes explicitly that whether the victim was the robber's father or a convert, the special obligation applies only if the robber swore falsely during the victim's lifetime while the stolen property remained in his possession. If he never swore, all agree that he acquires the stolen property that remains in his hands. Raavad likewise writes so. See there.
