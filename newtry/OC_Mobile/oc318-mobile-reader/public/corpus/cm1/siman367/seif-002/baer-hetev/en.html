He has fulfilled. This too is an enactment for those who repent, easing his burden so that he need not incur great travel expense. So explains Rashi. The item remains with the beit din's agent until the victim comes and takes it, and the robber's responsibility ends when he delivers it to the agent. So writes the Sema. The Shach writes that this applies specifically when he gives it to beit din with authority to send it to the victim whenever they wish, not merely for the agent or beit din to hold it until the victim personally comes. This resolves Tosafot, who holds that delivery to beit din is effective. See Maharshal as well. Tosafot's objection to Rashi concerns only the implication that the beit din's agent must keep it until the victim comes. Shach.
