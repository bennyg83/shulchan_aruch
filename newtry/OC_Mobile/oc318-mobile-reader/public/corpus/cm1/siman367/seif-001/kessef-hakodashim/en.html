See Shach, seif katan 3. The Shach writes in siman 367 that even where the robber has not sworn, he must notify the victim. It appears, however, that where the robber is uncertain which of several people he robbed, general notice to all of them may suffice. This seems supported by the dispute of Rabbi Tarfon and Rabbi Akiva in Bava Kamma 103b. If specific personal notice were always required, merely placing the stolen amount before the group would not help, because the true victim would not have been individually notified. Perhaps the distinction is that with respect to notice, each individual claim is one of 'I do not know whether I became liable to you,' while the robber nevertheless knows that he is liable to one member of the group. Tosafot explain that this knowledge imposes a duty to make restitution somehow. General notice to the entire group may therefore satisfy the notification requirement because the true victim himself knows that the announcement applies to him, while the robber possesses no more specific knowledge that he could convey. By contrast, where actual restitution to each individual is required by the verse, merely placing the property among them may not satisfy that duty. Thus general notice may suffice even though a more individualized payment rule would not. This requires careful study.
