The Buddha. A. A.A. who came out of Judea does not have to persecute him, so I will not hesitate to call him the K.C. and that when he does not admit that he has only come to witness that he does not have to persecute him only. “Third:
To pursue him. The Sema writes that this exemption is because of the enactment for penitents. This is not correct. The Gemara implies that by strict law the robber need not pursue the victim, except where he swore falsely. Since he then requires atonement through a korban and atonement is incomplete until the stolen property reaches the victim's hand, he must pursue him. The same applies today: although there is no korban, he does not attain atonement until it reaches the victim. The Rosh likewise writes this. Ibid.
And notify him. The Rosh and Tur imply that the robber must send notice to the victim wherever he is, and the Bach explains the Tur this way. The Sema also writes that this is the apparent implication of the Tur's wording, but says that the Rambam and Mechaber appear to disagree, holding that the property may remain in the robber's possession until the victims come on their own. According to the Sema, the Rama's addition, 'and notify them,' means only that when they come to the robber's city, he tells them what he robbed from them and that they should take it from him. The Bach similarly explains the Rambam. Maharshal and Tosafot Yom Tov also appear to say so. In my view, even the Rambam and Mechaber agree that the robber must send notice. They mean only that he need not personally pursue the victim. This is why the Rama adds, 'and notify them.' The Ir Shushan likewise implies this. In my view this is also indicated by the Gemara at the beginning of page 104, where it says that because the robber knows whom he robbed and admitted it, the law follows the stated opinion. This implies specifically that he admitted to the victim and informed him. Otherwise, according to all views he remains obligated at least to satisfy Heaven. Accordingly, he must do everything reasonably possible to notify the victim. So long as he has not done so, he has not satisfied his heavenly obligation according to Rabbi Tarfon, and according to Rabbi Akiva he remains legally liable. Ibid.
To claim it. This is the Rambam's wording, and the Semag writes likewise. It is difficult, however, why the Rambam introduced a new reason on his own when the Gemara expressly says that because the robber swore falsely and requires atonement, it is insufficient until the stolen property reaches the victim. The Rosh and Maharshal likewise write this. Accordingly, even where the robber knows with certainty that the victim did not despair, he must carry the property to him, and mere notice is insufficient. The Rambam, Sema, and Mechaber therefore require study. Tosafot Yom Tov writes that according to this, where the robber did not swear, he need not even notify the victim. This implies that he understands the Rambam to hold that after a false oath, notice alone suffices. Maharshal in HaGozel, siman 35, also understands the Rambam this way. This does not appear correct to me, for the Rambam writes that he must pursue him. Rather, I understand the Rambam to mean that even if he notifies the victim, the victim may still fail to claim it because he thinks that after the robber already swore and I despaired, he will not return it despite notifying me. Maharshal writes there that even if the robber notified him and the victim responded, 'Leave it in your possession for me,' nevertheless, because the robber swore falsely, it is insufficient until he performs a complete restitution. This is also implied by the Gemara, which says that even if the victim tells him, 'Let it remain with you,' since the robber requires atonement that is insufficient. This applies when the victim said so through a messenger. If he said it in the robber's presence, however, it is different, because when he leaves the object with the robber and departs, he is effectively depositing it with him. Tosafot to page 104a likewise write this. Ibid.
