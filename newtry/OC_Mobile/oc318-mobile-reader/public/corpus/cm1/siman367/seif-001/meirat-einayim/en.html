Although he denied it, etc. Meaning, one should not say that because the robber denied the claim, the victim despaired and will no longer pursue him, unlike the case the Mechaber discusses afterward where the robber already swore falsely.
He is not obligated to pursue him, etc. This too is because of the enactment encouraging repentance. See the Perishah.
And he should notify them. The Tur says: rather, he should notify him by saying, "I robbed such-and-such amount from you; come and take it." At first glance, the Tur's wording implies that the robber must send notice to the victim wherever he presently is, although he need not trouble himself to send the stolen property there or accept responsibility for the risks of transport. Rambam's wording, however, proves that he disagrees and holds that the property may remain in the robber's possession until the victims come on their own. Rama's addition, "and he should notify them," therefore means that when they come to the robber's city, the robber must tell them how much he stole and that they should take it from him, since he is prepared to give it to them. This is straightforward.
But if he swore, etc. The verse says, "He shall give it to the one to whom it belongs," and the context concerns one who swore falsely, as the same section says, "on the day of his guilt," meaning when he brings an asham for his false oath. Were it not for the enactment encouraging repentance, however, strict law would require the robber to bring the property to the victim's location even if he had not sworn, since Scripture demonstrates particular concern about restitution.
