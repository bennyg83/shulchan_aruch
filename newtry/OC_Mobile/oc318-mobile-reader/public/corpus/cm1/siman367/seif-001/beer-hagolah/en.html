a) The language of the Rambam, chapter 7 of Hilkhot Gezeilah, law 9, from the Mishnah in the ninth chapter of Bava Kamma 103a, and according to Rava's interpretation at the beginning of 104a: once the robber knows whom he robbed and admits to him, it is as though the owner told him, 'Let it remain in your hands as a deposit.' This follows Tosafot's explanation there that the admission was not made in the owner's presence.
b) Ibid., in the Mishnah. Rava explains in the Gemara that since the robber needs atonement, it does not suffice until the item actually reaches the owner's hand.
gimel) In my humble opinion this is explicit in Rava's statement there, according to Tosafot's view that he did not admit in the owner's presence. If he did admit in the owner's presence, then even if he had sworn falsely he need not pursue the owner to Media and can bring his atonement offering.
