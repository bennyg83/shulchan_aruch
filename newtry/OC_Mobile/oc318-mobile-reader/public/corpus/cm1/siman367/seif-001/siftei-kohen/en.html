If he later admitted it. Even if he wishes to fulfill his obligation toward Heaven, he is not required to pursue the victim. This is explicit in the Gemara in Perek HaGozel, Bava Kamma 104. All the more so, if he did not admit it but witnesses came and testified that he robbed him, he is not required to pursue him; he must only notify him.
He is not required to pursue him, etc. The Sema writes that this is because of the enactment for penitents. This is not correct. The Gemara implies that by strict law he is not required to pursue him. Only where he swore falsely, since he must bring an offering to obtain atonement, it is insufficient until the money actually reaches the victim. The same applies nowadays: even though there is no offering, he does not obtain full atonement until the money reaches the victim. The Rosh writes likewise.
Until they come, and he shall notify them. The Tur and Rosh imply that he must send notice to the victim at the place where the victim is. The Bach likewise explains the Tur this way. The Sema also writes that the language of the Tur appears to imply this, but he says that the language of Rambam and the Mechaber implies otherwise, namely that the money remains with the robber until the victims come on their own. According to him, the Rama's addition "and he shall notify them" means that when they come to the robber's city, the robber then informs them how much he stole and tells them to take it from him. The Bach similarly explains Rambam, and this also appears from Maharshal and Tosafos Yom Tov cited below. In my humble opinion, Rambam and the Mechaber also agree that he must send notice to the victim. They mean only that he is not required personally to pursue him. This is why the Rama added, "and he shall notify them." The Ir Shushan also appears to understand it this way. This is likewise implied by the Gemara at the beginning of page 104, where it says that since he knows whom he robbed and admitted it to him, it is as though the victim said, "let it remain with you." This implies that specifically where he admitted it to him and notified him is it treated as though the victim said, "let it remain with you." Otherwise, according to everyone he has not fulfilled even his obligation toward Heaven. Accordingly, he must do everything reasonably possible to notify him. So long as he does not notify him, according to Rabbi Tarfon he does not fulfill his obligation toward Heaven, and according to Rabbi Akiva he is liable even by strict law. Examine carefully.
And he shall notify them. The Bach writes that the Tur explains Rava's conclusion in the Gemara, at the beginning of page 104, "since he knows whom he robbed and admitted it to him, therefore if he swore," as applying specifically where he admitted to the victim before the victim went to Media. But where he denied the claim and did not admit before him, and only after the victim went to Media he admitted before beit din, although he need not bring the money after him to Media he must still notify him, saying, "I stole such and such from you." This is unnecessarily forced. One can say that the Tur discusses a case where he did not admit before the victim, but holds that although he need not bring the money after him to Media, he must nevertheless admit and notify him, as I wrote above. The Bach also appears to say that even if he admitted before the victim, if he had sworn falsely he must travel after him to Media. This is incorrect, and he overlooked Tosafos on page 104a, who write that the case cannot be one where he admitted in the victim's presence, because if so, even after swearing falsely he need not bring it after him to Media and may bring his atonement offering. Since he admitted to him in person, left the money with him, and the victim then departed, it is as though the victim deposited the money with him. The Gemara therefore must concern a case where he admitted not in the victim's presence, but before witnesses. See what I write below.
But if he swore, etc. The Sema, seif katan 4, explains this from the verse, "he shall give it to the one to whom it belongs," which concerns one who swore falsely. The Ir Shushan writes likewise. This is not the Gemara's conclusion. The Gemara says that because he swore and requires atonement, it is insufficient until the money reaches the victim. Perhaps they mean that the Gemara explains the reason for the verse, but it was unnecessary for them to introduce this.
Because they already despaired after he swore and no longer come to demand it from him. This is Rambam's language, and the Semag likewise writes it. Tosafos Yom Tov writes that according to this reasoning, if he did not swear he need not even notify the victim. He apparently understands that according to Rambam, after swearing it is enough to notify him. Maharshal in Perek HaGozel, siman 35, writes similarly regarding Rambam. This does not appear correct to me, because Rambam writes that he must pursue him. Rather, Rambam means that even if the robber notifies him, the victim may still not come to demand it because he thinks that once the robber swore and he himself despaired, the robber will not return it even if notified. Nevertheless, it is difficult that Rambam introduced a new reason, because the Gemara explicitly gives the reason that since he swore and requires atonement, it is insufficient until the money reaches the victim. The Rosh and Maharshal likewise write this. Accordingly, even if the robber knows with certainty that the victim did not despair, he must still bring it after him and notification alone does not suffice. The words of Rambam, Semag, and the Mechaber therefore require study. Maharshal further writes that even if he notified the victim, and the victim sent word, "let it remain with you," nevertheless, once the robber swore falsely it is insufficient until he performs a complete act of restitution. This also appears from the Gemara, which says that where he swore, even though the victim said, "let it remain with you," because he needs atonement it is insufficient until it reaches the victim. This applies, however, where the victim said this through an agent. If he said it directly to the robber in person, it is different, because since the victim left the money with him and departed, it is as though he deposited it with him, as I wrote above in the name of Tosafos.
