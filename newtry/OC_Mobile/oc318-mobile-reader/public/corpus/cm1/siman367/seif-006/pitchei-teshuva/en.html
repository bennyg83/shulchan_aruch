And he swore to him. See Baer HaGolah, who writes that the Beit Yosef understood the wording "and he swore" here to imply that in the preceding law the same may perhaps apply even if he did not swear. The Darkei Moshe already disagreed with him, as cited by the Sema above, seif katan 9, and the Bach likewise rules so. See there. The Beur HaGra also writes that the law applies specifically where he swore. See there. The Mishneh LaMelech, chapter 3 of Hilchot Gezelah, halachah 5, likewise questions the Beit Yosef on this point and writes that the Rashba says explicitly in responsa, part 2, siman 308: "This is specifically when he swore, whether to his father or to the convert, during their lifetimes. Since he swore while the stolen property remained in his possession, he is obligated to remove it from his possession. But if he did not swear, all agree that he acquires the stolen property that is in his possession. The Raavad likewise wrote this." See there.
