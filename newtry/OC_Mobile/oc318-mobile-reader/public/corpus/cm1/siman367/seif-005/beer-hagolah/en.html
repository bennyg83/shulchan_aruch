lamed) The language of the Rambam there, law 2, from the Mishnah. The Maggid Mishneh explains that the Rambam understands 'he pays principal and a fifth' to mean that he pays them, but he may retain from the other estate assets more than his ordinary inheritance share, up to the amount corresponding to the principal and fifth that he paid them. This is what the Rambam means by 'he makes an accounting with them.' If he does not wish to surrender his share, and when the Rambam says 'if he has none,' he means that he has neither brothers nor sons and there is no one fit to inherit, he may borrow and repay the debt from the estate. The Beit Yosef and Kesef Mishneh write that Tosafot explain like the Rambam that 'his sons' means the sons of the robber. The Rambam's reasoning appears to be that once he gave the entire stolen property to his sons or brothers, he fulfilled the mitzvah of returning stolen property, so there is no reason he may not then claim his inheritance share in his father's assets.
