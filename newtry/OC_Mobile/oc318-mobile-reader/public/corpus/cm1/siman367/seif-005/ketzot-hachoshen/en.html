He borrowed from others. Tosafos in chapter HaGozel Kama, Bava Kamma 109a, s.v. 'he borrowed and the creditors come and collect from him,' explain that he borrowed from others against his own land, even without designating it as an apotiki, because land is encumbered to creditors. He then gives that land to his sons or brothers in repayment for the stolen property. Since the stolen item itself no longer exists, the creditors return and collect that land even though he has other property from which payment could be made. I saw that Maharsha writes that the 'other property' must mean movable property, while he has no other land except the parcel he gave to his sons. Otherwise the creditors could not seize that land from them, because according to the Gemara's assumption the sons are like gift recipients, and a creditor does not collect from a gift recipient where free property remains available. I do not understand this distinction between land and movable property. The rule that one does not collect from encumbered property when free property is available applies even where the available free property is movable property. This is explicit in Shulchan Aruch, siman 104, seif 1.
