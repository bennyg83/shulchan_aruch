One who borrowed a Torah scroll. Ran writes that borrowing a sacred book may not create full borrower liability for unavoidable accidents, because the lender also performs a mitzvah and receives the benefit described by Rav Yosef. The borrower therefore does not receive all the benefit. Yet Maharam rules that borrowers of books are liable for accidental loss like ordinary borrowers. He may follow Rabbah rather than Rav Yosef. According to the Shulchan Aruch, which follows Rav Yosef, the present discussion of exemption for loss caused by use may merely teach that such a loss is certainly exempt. It does not necessarily prove that every other unavoidable accident is chargeable. The issue requires study.
If he lent it to others. The Shach correctly notes that the original borrower can become liable from the moment he transfers the borrowed object to another. Even according to the opinion that a custodian's liability arises only at the time of negligence, the beginning of the improper transfer can create the relevant contingent liability. This has practical consequence where the borrower later dies and the object subsequently dies in the hands of the third party. If the borrower's estate was already exposed from the moment he improperly transferred it, the owner's claim can relate back to that earlier act, even though the final loss occurred after the borrower's death. This is the point I discussed in siman 341.
