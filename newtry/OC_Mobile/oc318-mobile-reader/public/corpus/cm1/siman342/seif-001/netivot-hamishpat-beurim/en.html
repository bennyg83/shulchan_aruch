He borrowed a Torah scroll. See Sema, seif katan 2. The reason he may not hand it to another is that the owner does not trust that person. Perhaps the second borrower will abscond with it or deny possession and free himself through a false claim. This resembles Ran's responsum, siman 19, concerning a borrower of a book, cited above in siman 72 in the Sema, seif katan 21. See what I wrote there in my novellae.
And if he lent it to others. See Shach, seif katan 1. There is a textual error there, and the text should read: "But regarding the possibility that an unavoidable accident occurred, he does not trust him." The word "changed" is erroneous. The Shach's meaning is that perhaps only in the ordinary case of one custodian transferring to another custodian may he transfer to a person with whom the owner regularly deposits. The owner may trust that person not to combine two forms of wrongdoing, both failing to guard properly and then swearing falsely. Here, however, there may be concern that the animal simply died naturally and that the second borrower will swear falsely. There is only one suspected wrongdoing, false oath, because no degree of guarding can prevail against natural death. Perhaps the owner suspects him of that single wrongdoing.
He is liable. See Shach, seif katan 2. His intent concerns what the Shach writes in siman 344, seif katan 7, in the name of Nimukei Yosef citing Ra'ah, also cited in siman 291, regarding one who rented a donkey to ride. Even where the first custodian does not know how the accident occurred, he is liable because, as Nimukei Yosef explains, once the initial negligence of giving the item to another person begins and that negligence becomes the cause of the later accident, liability has already begun.
