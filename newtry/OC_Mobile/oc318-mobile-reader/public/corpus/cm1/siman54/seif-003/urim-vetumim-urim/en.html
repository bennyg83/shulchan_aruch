Even if he swore, etc. We are certain that his oath did not include an obligation to pay while his document remained in the other party's possession. Nevertheless, the matter requires consideration, because Rashba wrote this as a rule of law but not as a ruling for practice. See Tumim.
The law is with the borrower. Even if the money is already in beit din's possession, the borrower need not leave it there, Rashba.
To pay immediately, etc. If the borrower wants the lender to write a notation of payment between the lines or on the document itself, the lender must do so, Shach.
Where it does not state on it, etc. See below in siman 71.
When they write a receipt, etc. Mahara Sasson holds that an antedated receipt is valid, and Shach agrees. See Tumim, where I wrote that this requires study, and see what I wrote above in siman 65.
If they remember the date of the document, etc. Initially they should certainly write it. Shach holds that if they knew the document's date but nevertheless wrote a generic receipt, the receipt is invalid because it may improperly weaken the lender's rights in other loans he later makes to this borrower. He likewise holds that a postdated receipt is invalid unless the lender was informed. See Tumim, where I established that Shach has no proof for this. On the contrary, Tosafos, Baal HaTerumos, and Rosh indicate otherwise. Likewise a postdated receipt is valid, for at worst it has the status of an undated receipt, which may be written initially.
And they should not write, etc. If they know that the document does not contain an after acquired property clause and that such a clause is not supplied as a scribal omission, then a postdated receipt may create a problem and they may write the date. See Tumim.
Unless they remember the date of the loan. Meaning the date written in the document. What difference does the actual date of the loan make? The same concern remains that perhaps the document itself was postdated, Sema.
It nullifies every document, etc. See Tumim, where I wrote that even if the document is still within its due period, we do not say that the receipt certainly cannot refer to it based on the presumption that a person does not repay before the due date. See there at length.
Rather, all his documents, etc. "All" is not literal. If the lender has two documents, one large and one small, the larger is treated as paid and the smaller remains valid. The point is only that if he produces a single document, whichever one it is and whatever its form, it is presumed paid. This is the meaning of the word "all." See the end of siman 65, Sema and Shach.
