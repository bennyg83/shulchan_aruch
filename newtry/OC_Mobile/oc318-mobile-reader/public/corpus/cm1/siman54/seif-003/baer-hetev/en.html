Without specification. The Shach writes that initially the receipt should identify the document by its date. Although if the document's date was omitted from the receipt the receipt is nevertheless valid after the fact, as stated in siman 43, seif 24, where the date is known it should initially be written in the receipt. This also follows from the Gemara and the poskim. It appears that if it is known that the witnesses knew the date of the document and nevertheless wrote an unspecified receipt without the creditor's knowledge, the receipt is invalid, because they unlawfully weaken the creditor's position. It is comparable to a receipt bearing a later date, which I explain below is invalid. Thus concludes the Shach.
The loan. This means that they should state the date appearing in the document, showing when the loan was made. It does not mean that they should write today that they saw him make the loan, for it was already explained that we must be concerned that the loan date was recorded later in the document. So writes the Sema. The Shach writes that it appears unnecessary to inform the creditor that the receipt will be written without identifying the document's date, even though this weakens his position, because where the date of the document is unknown the creditor himself understands that the receipt will necessarily be general. Accordingly, if they knew the date at the time of payment and the witnesses later forgot it, they may not write a general receipt without the creditor's knowledge. We must be concerned that the creditor may later lend this same borrower the same amount, assuming that the receipt specifies the date of the original document, while the borrower will produce the general receipt and thereby cause the creditor an unlawful loss. Nor may they write merely that payment was made on a document dated before a certain point. For example, if payment was made on Rosh Chodesh Iyar, they may not write that the creditor received a certain amount on Rosh Chodesh Iyar toward a document dated before Iyar, because the creditor may have another document also dated before Iyar. The creditor may have failed to tell this to the witnesses because he assumed that, since they knew the precise date, they would state it expressly in the receipt. There is therefore no remedy until they inform the creditor that they are writing a general receipt without the document's date. Thus concludes the Shach.
