And all the more so if. Ketubot 16b, at the beginning of the second chapter: "For it is as though she concealed it before us." This implies that where she concealed it, according to all opinions a receipt is not written, even according to the opinion that generally permits writing a receipt. From this the first ruling of the Shulchan Aruch is also derived.
(Collected note) He swore, etc. He did not swear with this situation in mind, as explained in Bava Kamma, first chapter, and elsewhere; see siman 73.

One who owes, etc. Bava Batra 171b: for if you would think that there is no, etc., "a borrower would not," etc. This implies that only where there is some defect in the creditor's position is this required; otherwise not. This is also implied by Rashi there, s.v. "a borrower is a servant," etc., and by the Mishnah there, where Rabbi Yose said to him, etc., "and he should not impair," etc.
