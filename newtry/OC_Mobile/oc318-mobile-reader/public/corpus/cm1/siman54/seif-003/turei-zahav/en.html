(See there: If it contains unspecified language, etc., through “but with a receipt the reverse is true.”) The Rif, cited in siman 71, seif 34, says that the holder of a document has the lower hand. There, however, the creditor possesses an authenticated instrument and the debtor seeks to use ambiguous language to diminish it. The debtor is therefore the claimant, and his reading is treated restrictively while the creditor retains the benefit of the credibility clause. Here the creditor is the one seeking to extract money by force of the instrument, so ambiguity is construed against him.
