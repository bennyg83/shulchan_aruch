If the lender says, 'My document is not now with me because it is in another city, and I will write you a receipt,' some say he is not heeded. Since the document exists, the borrower need not pay until it is returned. {Rama: Certainly, if the lender has the document but refuses to return it, the borrower need not pay until it is returned; even if he swore to pay, he is released from the oath (Responsa of the Rashba, siman 1110). If the lender demands payment before returning the document and the borrower demands the document first, the borrower prevails. If the lender is not trusted, the document is deposited with a third party until payment. If a document obligates a debtor in one hundred, he admits fifty but disputes the other fifty, and the creditor demands immediate payment of the admitted fifty with a receipt while reserving proof on the rest, the debtor may withhold payment until the document is returned if the claimant can presently bring his proofs and litigate. See siman 24. If he cannot presently bring proof or litigate, no deceit appears, or the beit din needs deliberation, the claimant prevails: the admitted amount is paid immediately against a receipt (Responsa of the Rashba, siman 930). A receipt is effective even if the document states it remains valid until torn by beit din or unless a receipt is written upon it (Maharick, shoresh 89).} When writing a receipt, if the document's date is remembered, the receipt should identify that date. If it is not remembered, the receipt should identify only the amount and contain no date, lest the loan document have been postdated so the receipt predates it and the lender later collect with the postdated document. A receipt stating simply that one person paid another cancels every document the recipient holds against the payer. {Rama: Likewise, if witnesses establish that a person holds a paid document against another, he cannot produce a document and claim that a different one was paid; all his documents are presumed paid (Responsa of the Rosh, klal 18, siman 19). See also siman 113.}
