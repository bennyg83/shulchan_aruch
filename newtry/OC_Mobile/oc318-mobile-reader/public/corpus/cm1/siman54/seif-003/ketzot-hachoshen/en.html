He must pay what he admits. The Shach cites Agudat Ezov, which questions Rama and argues that since the document still exists no receipt should be written. In my view, where the creditor and borrower dispute whether the document can be returned, the borrower cannot delay payment merely because of the doubt. In a direct dispute, the borrower says with certainty that the document still exists and the creditor says it was lost. We do not delay payment; rather, the creditor takes a hesset oath. This proves that judicial delay applies only where the continued existence of the document is clearly established before beit din, not where it is merely doubtful. Here too, since the creditor says he cannot return the document and the borrower says he can, payment of the admitted amount is not delayed merely because of the doubt. The reason the creditor does not immediately take a hesset oath in this particular setting is that the issue is expected to be clarified through witnesses. See the Shach in siman 24.
