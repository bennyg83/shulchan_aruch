When a receipt is written. See Shach, who concludes that an antedated receipt is valid. Urim VeTumim objected from Bava Metzia, where the Gemara worries that a ketubah may have been sold and Tosafot ask whether it might have been transferred through maamad sheloshtan, while Maharshal asks about Rabbi Natan's lien. In my view there is no difficulty. To fear maamad sheloshtan here we would also have to fear collusion between borrower and lender, for the borrower knows of the transfer. We ordinarily suspect collusion only where an instrument fell or where some separate defect already appears. Even according to Shach, who in siman 39 sometimes suspects collusion without a fall, that is only where we already know the act prejudices another creditor. We do not invent both the possibility of another creditor and a collusive scheme. Likewise, concern that the lender may owe others is weak because he could simply tear up or return his debt document. So long as no such third-party right is known, the borrower may repay the lender and thereby discharge himself, as in siman 86. He therefore has a migo that he could have returned the document and said payment occurred now. This is why a receipt without a date may be written, and an antedated receipt is similarly valid. In Bava Metzia, where the instrument was found, the special concern for collusion does apply; but once the conclusion follows Shmuel that the relevant purchaser concern is removed, a receipt without a date is valid. The issue of maamad sheloshtan likewise requires an additional collusion concern that we do not assume.
If they remember the date. See Shach, who rules that if the receipt witnesses know the date of the debt document they must record it, so that the lender will not later be harmed by the borrower taking a new loan of the same amount and using the undated receipt. If they omit it in circumstances that can harm the lender, the receipt is invalid. Urim VeTumim sought proof against him from Tosafot in Bava Batra, but there is no proof. If we assumed Shmuel's rule did not apply, an undated receipt would indeed be forbidden because of purchaser loss. Once we rule otherwise, an undated receipt is permitted only where necessary to protect the borrower. Where the witnesses know the relevant date and can write the receipt in a manner that protects both sides, they must do so. Otherwise the document resembles a postdated sale deed, which is invalid because of the predictable later loss it may create. Still, Tosafot's concern about an undated receipt can arise where the witnesses remember the date on which the original transaction occurred but do not remember whether the debt document itself was written on that date or intentionally postdated. In such a case they cannot safely state the date written in the deed and must issue an undated receipt.
