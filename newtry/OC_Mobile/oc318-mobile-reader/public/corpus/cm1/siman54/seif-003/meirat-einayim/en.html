Even if he swore to pay, etc. We are witnesses that he did not swear with this circumstance in mind.
The law follows the defendant. At the beginning of siman 24 the Tur likewise writes in the name of the Rosh that in such a case we do not first attend to the claimant's demand.
Unless they remember the time of the loan. Meaning, the time written in the document, when the loan actually occurred, not that they should write today's date merely because they saw the lender make the loan today. The Mechaber already wrote that there is concern perhaps the document's date was intentionally postponed.
That So-and-so has a paid document, etc. The Tur and Mechaber write a similar law at the end of siman 65, where one says, 'One document among my documents is paid.' From there we learn that if he has two documents against one person, the larger is presumed paid and not the smaller. Although Rama concludes, 'all his documents are paid,' that wording is not literal; it means that even if he says merely, 'one document was paid,' we treat the present one as the paid document until the matter is clarified. This is evident from Responsa Rosh there. See also Tur and Mechaber in siman 63 concerning one seeking witnesses with which to forge a document.
