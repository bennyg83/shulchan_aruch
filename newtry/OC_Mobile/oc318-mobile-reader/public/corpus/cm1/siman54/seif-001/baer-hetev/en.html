A receipt. The Shach writes that this applies when the due date of the entire document has arrived. The choice is then the creditor's, because he may say, 'Pay my document in full and nothing further is required.' But if the due date of the entire debt has not yet arrived, such as where payment is due in two installments and the debtor now pays what is due at the first date, the choice is the debtor's. The creditor must exchange the document for a new one or record the partial payment on the document itself, if that is what the debtor wishes. Thus concludes the Shach.
The scribe. The Shach writes that Darkei Moshe cites this in the name of Nimukei Yosef in the chapter Get Pashut, but in truth it is not stated there regarding this case. It is stated only where the creditor lost the document. Here, where the debtor pays only part of the debt, the creditor has no need for a receipt at all, and therefore the debtor must pay the scribe's fee. Rama's further statement, 'and if the creditor has no document,' must mean that he never had a document, such as where he stipulated before witnesses that the debtor should not repay him except in the presence of witnesses, as stated in siman 70, or a similar case. Thus concludes the Shach.
