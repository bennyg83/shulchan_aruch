But the witnesses to the deed. The Tur says that they may not write a replacement dated from the original date. The Bach infers that they may write one dated from the present even without the borrower’s consent, because they are merely copying the original and have not exhausted their agency. This is incorrect. The Tur states above in siman 41 that witnesses without beit din may not rewrite a lost deed even from the date of its erasure. Here he means only that they cannot write from the original date even with the borrower’s consent, because it would be antedated. A replacement from the current date may be written only with the borrower’s consent. This is also the Mechaber’s view and agrees with the Rama.
If the lender wishes, he may write a receipt. Although the borrower must then preserve the receipt, the Sages gave the lender the choice. The Bach adds that the borrower cannot compel the lender to record the payment on the deed itself. This applies where the entire debt has matured, for the lender may say, “Pay the whole deed and nothing further is needed.” Where only the first installment has matured, the choice belongs to the borrower. The lender must exchange the deed or record the partial payment upon it if the borrower requests that protection.
The lender pays the scribe’s fee, etc. The Darkei Moshe attributes this to the Nimukei Yosef, but the Nimukei Yosef says only that where a husband needs a receipt because his wife collects a ketubah by force of beit din without possessing a written ketubah, he pays for it. Where written ketubot are customary and she lost hers, she pays. The same applies to ordinary debts. If the lender lost the deed, he pays the cost of the receipt. In a case of partial payment, however, the lender does not need the receipt, while the borrower does, so the borrower should pay. The Rama’s clause concerning a lender who has no deed must refer to a debt that was never documented, such as where the borrower stipulated before witnesses that payment must be made before witnesses. It is possible that the text should have placed the lender’s obligation to pay in the next seif, concerning a lost deed. The Sema, Ir Shushan, and Darkei Moshe, however, apply it to partial payment, and their words require study.
