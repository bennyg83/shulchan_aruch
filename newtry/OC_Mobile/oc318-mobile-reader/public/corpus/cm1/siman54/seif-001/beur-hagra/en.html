(Collected note) And they should write it from the original date. This is the Gemara's statement, 'from the original date,' like the Mishnah and Gemara at 168 and the rule in siman 41. It means that they are permitted to write from the first date when the original document contained an after-acquired property clause. This explains the Rama's words, 'but from the date, etc.'
Although, etc.; but, etc. See Hagahot Ashri, which records two explanations of the Gemara's statement, 'but witnesses may not,' and Tosafot there.
(Collected note) But from the date, etc. This follows from Bava Batra 172a, which indicates that with the borrower's consent the document may be rewritten, as stated in siman 53. See Tosafot there, s.v. 'the law is not, etc.'
(Collected note) The lender, etc. This is the Mishnah at Bava Batra 167a. The lender pays the fee, etc. The statement here and at the end of siman 39 that the lender pays is not stated exactly this way by the Nimukei Yosef or Maggid Mishneh. They explain that the husband pays where ketubot are not ordinarily written, or where he never wrote one. If he wrote one and it was lost, the wife pays. The Shach rules accordingly.
