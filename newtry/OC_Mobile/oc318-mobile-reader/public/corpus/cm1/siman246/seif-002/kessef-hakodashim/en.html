But not the support of his wife. A teacher was hired jointly for the children of two householders and they agreed to provide him with food. One householder supplied all the food for the entire period. Perhaps this resembles one who supports another man's wife without authorization, where the payer may have intended to waive reimbursement. On the other hand, the food and the cash ultimately owed to the teacher are components of one compensation arrangement. The one who supplied the food may therefore be viewed as having advanced money toward the joint obligation, and its value should be deducted from the amount otherwise charged to him at the end.
