If he gave it through a kinyan and strengthened his power, etc. It requires study why the Mechaber made this a separate clause. It would have fit at the end of seif 4, after the statement that if he conveyed by kinyan the recipient acquires, adding that some require an enhanced formulation and some say even that does not help. The Tur groups these opinions together in Rabbi Yonah's name. Rabbi Yonah begins by saying that some hold a kinyan causes acquisition and then explains that this refers to a case where the donor strengthened the recipient's power, such as saying, "Give all my property to so and so, and also write it." Even then some say the recipient does not acquire. If he said merely, "Write and give him all my property," without strengthening the recipient's power, he did not even appoint him as administrator, because the donor attempted to create the administration specifically through a deed and we fear he intended it to take effect only through that deed, while there is no deed after death. In the Perishah I proved that Rabbi Yonah holds that just as writing "my property is given to you" does not make the recipient a full owner even when the donor strengthens his power, so too no other kinyan should change the result. The Mechaber's wording, however, appears to distinguish. With a kinyan sudar Rabbi Yonah may agree that the son acquires, while with acquisition through the writing of a deed even an enhanced formulation may fail. The Mechaber therefore rules without qualification in seif 4 that a kinyan acquires, but here records a dispute in a kinyan strengthened by writing. If so, the word "kinyan" here means the documentary acquisition. This distinction is difficult, because a kinyan sudar given to take effect only after death likewise requires strengthened present language, since there is no kinyan after death, as will be explained in siman 250. I do not see a sound reason to distinguish them.
