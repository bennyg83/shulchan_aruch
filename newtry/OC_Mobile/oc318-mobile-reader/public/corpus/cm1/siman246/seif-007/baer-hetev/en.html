And strengthened. The Sema writes that it requires study why the Mechaber stated this as a separate clause. He should have written it at the end of seif 4, where he rules that if the giver made a kinyan, the son acquires, and then note that some authorities require that the kinyan include a strengthening of the recipient's power, while others hold that even this does not suffice. The Tur likewise places the views together in seif 6 in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah. In the Perishah the Sema proves that Rabbeinu Yonah holds that just as writing, "My property is given to you," does not help even when accompanied by language strengthening the recipient's rights to show that he was not merely appointed administrator, so too no other kinyan helps. Yet the Mechaber's wording indicates that he distinguishes between the modes of acquisition: even Rabbeinu Yonah agrees that a formal symbolic kinyan transfers ownership, and only a document does not. For this reason the Mechaber rules without qualification in seif 4 that a kinyan is effective, while here he records a dispute concerning a kinyan accompanied by strengthened wording. The "kinyan" here would therefore mean acquisition through the written document, not that he both wrote a document, made a symbolic kinyan, and strengthened the recipient's power. This requires study, however, because there is no sound reason to distinguish between them. A symbolic kinyan too requires language strengthening the recipient's power where the gift is to take effect only after death, since there is no kinyan after death, as the Tur and Shulchan Aruch explain in siman 250. The Shach writes that the Sema's words are correct, contrary to the Mechaber.
