If he wrote to a son among daughters. Tur writes that although it is unclear from the sugya whether the son acquires, it is reasonable that even if he does acquire we do not deprive the daughters of their support. Beit Yosef writes that this is obvious because a father who says, "My daughters shall not be supported from my estate," is ignored. Therefore, even if he gave all his property to his son, he cannot thereby remove the daughters' support. This is difficult. A person can certainly remove the daughters' support by giving the property to another person. The special rule that daughters can collect even after a shechiv mera gift applies because such a gift is treated more like inheritance for this purpose. Yet according to Ramah here, the case can involve even a gift by a healthy person. Beit Yosef in siman 252 expressly says that even a gift "from today and after death" is treated as a healthy-person gift regarding the wife's maintenance. Why then should the daughters collect? We must say that Ramah follows Mordechai, cited in Even HaEzer siman 100, seif 1, in the gloss, that what one gives to his heirs can retain the law of inheritance. See Chelkat Mechokek, Even HaEzer siman 93, seif katan 33. There is still a question why Mechaber needed to say at all that a gift to a son among daughters is valid. Even without a gift, the son inherits everything, while the daughters retain their maintenance. What practical difference is produced by the gift? It appears that because the rule whether daughters' maintenance is displaced depends on the dispute whether a gift to an heir is treated as inheritance or as a true gift, Mechaber needed to teach first that the transfer to the son is not merely an apotropos appointment. Once the son receives the property as a gift rather than merely as manager, the maintenance question follows the above dispute.
