(Seif 17: Eat with me.) In the name of Mahari, this is derived from the case of, "Break it," where he is liable even though he was permitted to break it. The Rama requires study, for in siman 363 he rules that one who says to another, "Live in my courtyard," is exempt from paying rent. That ruling is taken from a responsum of the Beit Yosef there. We must therefore say that he disagrees with the Maharik cited here, yet the Rama's own position remains difficult. As a matter of practical law, the ruling here appears primary, for its proof is clear. A further proof appears from the Beit Yosef in Yoreh Deah, siman 115, who explains that saying, "Live in my courtyard," means for rent.
