To his son it too is a gift. The Tur explains that had the father meant the son to be only administrator while the outsider received an actual share, he could have written, "All my property to my son and to so and so." Since he instead first gave a portion to the son and afterward the remainder to the outsider, the son's portion is treated as an actual gift. But if he wrote, "Two thirds of my property to my son," the same inference cannot be made. He could not have written, "All my property to my son and to so and so," because that would imply equal halves. Therefore, even though the son is mentioned first, he may still be only administrator over the two thirds. This is straightforward.
