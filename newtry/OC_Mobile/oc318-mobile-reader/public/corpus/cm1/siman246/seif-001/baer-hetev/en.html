That it is. The Sema writes that the phrase "all the more so" is literal: regarding a small amount, everyone agrees that the gift is valid. This is unlike the law of a dangerously ill person who gives all his property, where if he recovers he may retract, but if he retained something he may not retract, and the authorities dispute how much must be retained, as the Tur and Shulchan Aruch explain in siman 250, seif 4. Some require that he retain enough for his sustenance, each according to his circumstances. The present case is different, as explained there. The Taz proves that there is no distinction between the cases and that here too he must retain enough for his sustenance, contrary to the Sema. We follow an assessment of presumed intent only when something occurred that the giver had not expressly contemplated. If circumstances did not change, we do not reinterpret his words. Maharit, part I, siman 27. See Responsa Divrei Rivot, simanim 129, 130, and 131. One who gave Shimon the rental income of a house for his support because Shimon was poor, without expressly stating that this was the reason for the gift, made an absolute gift, and even if Shimon later became wealthy the gift remains valid.
