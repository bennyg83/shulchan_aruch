If a person writes all his property as a gift to one of his sons, whether he is healthy or deathly ill, Rama: some say specifically when he uses the language of gift, but if a deathly ill person uses language of inheritance, the son acquires, then even if that son is an infant in a cradle, the father intended only to appoint him administrator. The son shares the estate equally with his brothers. Even elaborate deed language such as "go, acquire, and take possession" does not change the presumption that the father intended the brothers to heed him. If the father retained any property, land or movable property, the son acquires the gift. Likewise, he acquires if the deed expressly states that the transfer is absolute and not merely administrative, authorizes him to sell or give to others, transfers movables together with land, includes a formal kinyan, or contains any other language demonstrating an absolute gift. Rama: If the document merely says that one son shall rule and control everything, it appoints him administrator and does not convey a gift.
