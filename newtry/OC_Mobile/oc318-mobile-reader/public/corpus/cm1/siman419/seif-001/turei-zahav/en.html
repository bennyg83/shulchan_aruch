Seif 1, collection is made from movable property. The Sema writes that the Gemara proves damages differ from an ordinary creditor, and he interprets the Tur accordingly. This is difficult, for the Tur's words “we assess,” and so forth, indicate that this is the primary rule, and his conclusion “rather, he shall give him movable property” implies that the tortfeasor is present. Moreover, the Sema overlooked the Nimukei Yosef at the beginning of Bava Kamma, who cites the Ramah that the tortfeasor may pay with bran or other goods only when he lacks cash. If he has cash, he must pay cash, and the same applies to an ordinary creditor. The Ramah further indicates that if the debtor has both movable property and land, he cannot force the claimant to accept land. Even if practical law does not follow the Ramah regarding cash, since we permit payment with movable goods, why should we not follow him in preferring movable property to land? Tosafot in Bava Batra 92b, the Mordechai, and Rabbeinu Tam hold that the tortfeasor may choose either movable property or land, unlike an ordinary creditor. Since there is no decisive ruling, the injured party cannot compel the tortfeasor to give movable property against Rabbeinu Tam's opinion.
