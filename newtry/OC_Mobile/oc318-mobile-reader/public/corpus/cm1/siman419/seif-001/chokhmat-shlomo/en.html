Seif 1. "When beit din attends to collection for the injured party, etc." Note: See Shach, seif katan 2, and see Ateret Tzvi, who challenges the Shach. His words are very correct, but he did not refute the proof the Shach brings from the Gemara's statement that beit din does not attend to collection. In my view the Shach's interpretation is itself difficult. According to his explanation, what was the Gemara's question from the verse "he shall return," which is expounded to include payment even with bran or other movable property? The Gemara could simply answer that this verse concerns a tortfeasor who has no land and therefore must pay with movable property, while if he has both land and movable property, beit din first looks to the land. Indeed, earlier the Gemara asks the same question against Rav Huna's statement that the injured party may receive either money or superior land, citing "he shall return" to include even bran, and answers that Rav Huna was speaking where the tortfeasor possessed the better form of payment. The Gemara could have answered exactly the same way here. Its failure to do so indicates, like Rashi, that the initial possibility was that movable property might never be collected at all, even where there was no land, and therefore the verse presented a real difficulty. Moreover, the Gemara itself proves the opposite of the Shach. If the Shach were correct, why was the Gemara forced to answer that the case concerns orphans? It could have given his distinction directly, that where the tortfeasor has both land and movables beit din chooses the land. Since it did not answer that way, it appears that even in such a case the choice belongs to the tortfeasor, and there is no basis to say that the injured party controls the form of payment. This appears correct to me. Examine carefully.
