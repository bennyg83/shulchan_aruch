When beit din, etc., and as long as it can find, etc. Bava Kamma 14b: the baraisa says "money's worth," etc., and another baraisa says "he shall return," etc. Thus the rule resembles collection by a creditor, as stated in siman 101 seif 2.
(Collected note) When beit din, etc. This is the Rambam's language. He learned from a creditor that when movable property is available, beit din does not resort to land. This requires study, for the cases are not identical. With a creditor, when money is available we do not resort to inferior land, and movable property is equivalent to money. Here, however, Rav Huna, who is stringent, says "either money or best land," indicating that the injured party may collect land even when money exists. See Tosafos there. This is even more difficult according to our ruling like Rav Pappa and Rav Huna son of Rav Yehoshua. The Rambam and Shulchan Aruch nevertheless write similarly above in siman 348 seif 5. The Rosh's language also implies the distinction applies specifically to creditors. This matter requires great study. (End of note.)
(Collected note) When beit din, etc. The Tur says that the damager may give movable property, meaning that the choice rests with the damager. The point is to reject Rav Huna's view that when he owns best land he may not substitute movables. Bach and Derisha u'Perisha explain so, unlike Beit Yosef. The Rambam's wording, however, does not make the matter depend on the damager's choice. It appears that he follows the Ramah's view that while money is available the damager may give neither other movables nor land, and likewise while movables are available he may not insist on land. See siman 354 seif 5. The Rambam did not separately say that when money exists he may not give movables because in his view movables are included in the category of money, as the Gemara says, "all these are money." He thus reads Rav Huna's "either money or best land" to mean: if money exists, give money; otherwise give best land. This differs from Rashi's reading and explains why the later Amoraim do not necessarily disagree with Rav Huna. (End of note.)
(Collected note) When beit din, etc. Nimukei Yosef likewise writes in the name of the Ramah that the rule parallels creditors, but he adds that where money exists one does not resort even to movables. That is not the Rambam's view. (End of note.)

(Collected note) And even if he has, etc. Unlike Rav Huna, who holds that only when he lacks land and money may he use lesser assets, and unlike Rabbeinu Tam who rules like Rav Huna. The Rif follows the later authorities Rav Pappa and Rav Huna son of Rav Yehoshua. The Rosh likewise rules so and notes that Rav Kahana on 10b also follows them. (End of note.)
