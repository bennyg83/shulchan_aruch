From movable property. Rabbeinu Ephraim objects to the Rif, arguing that tort damages are uncommon. The Geonim enacted collection from heirs' movable property for creditors because otherwise people would refuse to lend, and for a woman because of family formation, but not for tort victims. The Rosh responds that in these generations movable property is like land for all lien purposes even by Talmudic law, without an enactment, like the Arabian camels mentioned in Ketubot 67a from which a woman collects her ketubah because commerce centers on them. In my view the Rosh's proof is not compelling, because there the rule is specifically about a ketubah, where the woman relies on such property from the beginning because their commerce is primarily movable property. That reasoning does not apply to torts. Nevertheless, most authorities agree with the Rif, Rambam, and Mechaber that nowadays tort damages are collected from heirs' movable property. Maharshal likewise rules so, chapter 1 of Bava Kamma, siman 39. Shach.
Orphans. This implies even where they also possess land. That is the default rule. But if the heirs wish to satisfy the claim with land, they may do so, because in such a case the Geonic enactment did not require movable property, as stated at the beginning of siman 107 regarding a creditor, and all the more so here. So too in Shiltei Giborim, chapter Chezkas HaBatim. Ibid.
From inferior land. This implies even from adult heirs. This appears to be the Rambam's view, unlike the Sema's interpretation. See what I wrote at the end of siman 108. Ibid.
