From an oath. Since the liability is a penalty, the Tur writes that no oath is imposed. This is where the injured party says, "You know what happened." Otherwise, even with a forewarned animal the owner is exempt from an oath because he was not expected to know, as explained at the beginning of siman 400 and in siman 87, seif 25. Even where witnesses saw part of the goring and thereby established part of the liability, the defendant is not treated regarding the remainder as one who must swear and cannot swear, because the additional liability is a penalty, as Tosafot writes at the beginning of HaParah. Tosafot gives another answer as well, that the owner was not expected to know, and the Nimukei Yosef and Hagahot Ashri write similarly. He is not exempt by the doctrine of "here it is," because although siman 87, seif 1, and siman 88, seif 24, state that an item still physically present may constitute "here it is" even in a bailee's house, that applies only where the item already belongs entirely to the claimant and therefore remains legally in its owner's domain wherever it is. A tam ox, however, still requires appraisal before the claimant's share is determined, so it is not considered "here it is." See siman 100, seif 3. Shach.
