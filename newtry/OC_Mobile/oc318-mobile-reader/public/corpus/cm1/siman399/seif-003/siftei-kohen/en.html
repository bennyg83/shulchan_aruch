Even from an oath. Even where witnesses saw the goring and thereby established liability for part of the claim, the defendant is not treated regarding the balance as one obligated to swear but unable to swear, because the remaining liability is a fine. Tosafot state this at the beginning of Perek Shor Shenagach et HaParah. They, Nimukei Yosef, and Hagahot Ashri also answer that the defendant was not expected to know. He is not exempt under the rule of ‘here it is,’ because although that rule applies to an existing item even in a custodian’s house, it applies only when the item already belongs entirely to the claimant. A tam ox requires appraisal, so the defendant has not tendered the claimant’s property itself. See below, siman 400, seif 3.
Nevertheless, if he seized, etc. The Tur writes this in the name of the Rosh, unlike Ramah, who holds that seizure does not help in a fine even when made without witnesses. Nimukei Yosef likewise follows Ramah. Bach asks from another ruling of Ramah that seizure is effective for half-damages, which are a fine. The distinction is that the earlier case involved witnesses to the damage, so the defendant would not be exempt by admitting a fine. The Tur says that Ramah’s view did not appear correct to the Rosh, but I did not find an express disagreement. Still, the Rosh implies otherwise in Perek HaManiach: regarding two tam oxen, he says that if the claimant seized in the presence of witnesses he collects only according to the damager’s version, implying that if he seized without witnesses he may collect according to his own version through migo. Since the Rosh expressly treats half-damages as a fine, possession without witnesses is evidently regarded as equivalent to producing proof. Otherwise the claimant could not be believed through migo. His later admission is therefore like an admission after witnesses have appeared. Accordingly, even according to the Rosh and Tur, seizure with migo helps only if it preceded the admission. In our time, when we lack ordained judges and an admission is not treated as formal admission for a fine, seizure of a fine is effective whenever the possessor has a migo. This appears correct.
