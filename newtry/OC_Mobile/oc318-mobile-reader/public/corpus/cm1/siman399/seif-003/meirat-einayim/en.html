Even from an oath. Meaning, where the damager also makes a definite claim, or where the injured party says, "I am certain that you know, so swear to me that you do not know." In a case of a muad ox the owner would have to swear, as the Tur and Mechaber write immediately below at the beginning of siman 400. Here, however, since the case concerns a tam ox, no oath is required. The Tur explains the reason: we impose an oath upon a defendant in order to induce an admission, but here the liability is a fine, and if he admitted he would be exempt; therefore denial likewise does not generate an oath. This is also explained above in siman 87, seif 25, and hinted by the Mechaber at the beginning of siman 400.
However, if he seized, etc. The Tur writes this in the name of Rosh, unlike Ramah, who holds that seizure is ineffective even without witnesses, because where the liability is a fine there is no enforceable obligation without witnesses.
