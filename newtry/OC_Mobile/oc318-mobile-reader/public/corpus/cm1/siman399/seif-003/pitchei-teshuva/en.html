See Baer Heitev, who cites the Shach that even according to the Rosh and Tur, seizure accompanied by a migo is effective only if the seizure occurred before the defendant admitted. Dagul MeRevavah objects: if according to the Shach seizure is equivalent to witnesses, then even if the defendant admitted first, this is a case of one who admitted to a penalty and witnesses subsequently came. Since his admission itself created no liability at all, the later equivalent of witnesses should obligate him. See Rambam, chapter 3 of Hilchot Geneivah, halachah 9, and Mishneh LaMelech there at the end of halachah 8. Dagul MeRevavah then notes that Yam Shel Shlomo, chapter Merubah, siman 26, writes that with every penalty, even where the admission obligated him in nothing and witnesses later came, he remains exempt, except in the special case of an admission concerning slaughter. See also Ketzot HaChoshen above at the end of siman 1.
