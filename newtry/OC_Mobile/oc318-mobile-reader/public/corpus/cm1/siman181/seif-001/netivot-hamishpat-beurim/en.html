He saved it for himself. See Sema, seif katan 1, who explains that the others had despaired of recovery. At first glance this is difficult. Despair alone does not transfer stolen property, even when the robber is a non Jew. A change of domain is also generally required. Siman 341, seif 5, rules that when property is taken from a robber against his will it is not a change of domain. What then effects acquisition here? A military band or raiding force is different. Property captured by such a force is like property swept away by a river, lost to everyone and effectively ownerless. This is not ordinary robbery. The Mishnah in Bava Kamma groups an armed force and robbers together, but that rule will be explained in siman 361. Because property seized by a raiding force is like an object swept away by a river, even unknowing despair can be effective. Therefore, if one partner is absent, we do not say the absent partner retains the property merely because he could not consciously despair. But where recovery remains possible with difficulty, the property is not lost to everyone. In that situation the one who rescues cannot acquire the absent partner's share merely through unknowing despair.
