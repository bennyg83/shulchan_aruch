Armed men came and seized it. See Responsa Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat, siman 100, who writes that this is the reading in all editions of the Gemara, Bava Kamma 116b, in the second baraita: "and they seized it." This shows that the property must already have been seized, and only afterward did one of the partners rescue it from their hands. In that case he may acquire it for himself as ownerless property. But if it had not yet been seized and had not come into their hands, there was not yet despair of recovery, and he cannot rescue it for himself. The Tur, however, and the abridged rulings of the Rosh compiled by the Tur read, "to seize it," which implies that even before the property reached their hands, if one partner stood up and drove them back, like Yochanan ben Yonatan the lion mentioned at the end of Yevamot, he may rescue it for himself. If he saved it by fleeing, however, even the Tur agrees that he did not acquire it for himself, because the remaining partners did not despair of the portion that he managed to carry away. Nevertheless, it appears to me that although he does not acquire it for himself by virtue of ownerlessness, if he is a partner he may divide the partnership without their knowledge even where he fled and saved the property.
