He saved it for himself. See Baer Heitev. Responsa Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat 125, discusses this law, and his words correspond to Netivot HaMishpat, seif katan 1. In responsum 100 the Chatam Sofer writes that Maharshal in Yam Shel Shlomo, Bava Kamma 10:42, distinguishes a numerous military band from ordinary robbers. Netivot HaMishpat at the beginning of siman 181 apparently did not have these words before him. In my view, however, no such distinction is necessary. The essential distinction is between an armed band in the wilderness and one in an inhabited place. In the wilderness there is neither law nor judge, the attackers have come from afar and will return there, so there is despair of recovery. An ordinary armed band in settled territory, even if numerous, is merely a band of robbers, and the matter depends on the circumstances. See also Responsa Beit Efrayim, Choshen Mishpat, end of siman 58, which explains that the ruling here, that when the partners cannot save the property and one of them saves it he saves it for himself, is the strict law. By enactment, however, what he saves is divided among them. See also what I wrote below, siman 259 seif 4, seif katan 3, and siman 264 seif 5, seif katan 6.
