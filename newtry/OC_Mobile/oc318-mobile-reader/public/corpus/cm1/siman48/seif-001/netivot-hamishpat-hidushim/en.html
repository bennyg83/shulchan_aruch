Its lien was waived. According to the Sema, he may still collect from unencumbered property. See Beurim: the borrower is not believed to say, "I paid." The Taz and Shach hold that the document is mere worthless paper. With the borrower's own handwriting, and likewise with a document that contains no property lien, he may borrow again upon it, because the concept of a waived lien does not apply. This is specifically where the handwritten document was not delivered through delivery witnesses. If it was delivered through witnesses like an ordinary document, the rule of waived lien applies. The Sema writes that if the renewed loan is on the following day, even a handwritten obligation may create a concern of antedating, because an earlier oral loan can have priority over a later written loan. The Shach writes that this is only where there is no concern of collusion, meaning where there is no suspicion that the handwritten document was written today but falsely antedated.
