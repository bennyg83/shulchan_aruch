(Shulchan Aruch, seif 1: Its lien was forgiven.) The Sema disputes the Levush and holds that collection remains possible from unencumbered property. Though I am unworthy to decide, my view follows the Levush. The Rosh cited in siman 55 actually supports that view. Under his second answer, only a prior stipulation that the money given be a gift can preserve collection; without it the document is invalid even against unencumbered property. Even under the first answer, the creditor acquires by delivery only according to the opinion that documentary rights transfer by delivery without writing. Since we rule that writing and delivery are required, without a new transfer from the creditor or escrow holder there is no basis even for collection from unencumbered property. The Rambam calls a document whose lien was forgiven “like a shard,” implying that it has no use at all, and the Rama rules expressly at the end of siman 55 that only an original gift stipulation permits collection. Bava Batra likewise treats such a document as entirely ineffective. Therefore the Levush is correct. The Sema also disputes the Levush’s requirement that the signature be aligned with the amount written on a mamarni. Here too the Levush is correct. If the amount is written below the signature, the holder may cut it away and write above the signature, “I, the undersigned, owe so-and-so,” for a signature anywhere below the top of the sheet can be misused. Even when the amount is above the signature, two blank lines might remain between them and permit forgery. The amount must therefore be aligned directly with the signature. A mamarni containing signatures alone and no stated obligation cannot support collection once presented to beit din in that defective form, because the holder could write whatever he wishes above the signatures. Even if the borrower trusts him, he may owe others on similar instruments and prejudice them. Although the document could have been corrected before presentation, once it comes before beit din invalid it cannot be rehabilitated, as stated in siman 45.
