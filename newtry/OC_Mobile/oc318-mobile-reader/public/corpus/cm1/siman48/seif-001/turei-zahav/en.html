Shulchan Aruch, seif 1: "whose lien was released." Sema disagrees with Levush regarding collection from unencumbered property and holds that one may collect from it. Although I am unworthy to decide between them, my view inclines to Levush. I am surprised at Sema's proof from the Rosh, cited by Beit Yosef in siman 55, because that passage is a proof for Levush. According to the Rosh's second answer, which explains R' Yose to mean that the creditor first says that the money he gave should be a gift, it is obvious that without such a stipulation the document is invalid even for collection from unencumbered property; there is no source for distinguishing. Even according to the Rosh's first answer, which explains R' Yose as allowing collection from unencumbered property, he concludes that the reason is because the creditor acquires through delivery of the document, according to the view that letters are acquired by delivery without a written transfer. For that very reason the Rosh later gives another answer, since we rule that both writing and delivery are required. It follows that without a new delivery from lender to lender, or from the lender to a third party, no authority holds that one can collect even from unencumbered property. The reason actual delivery does not allow collection from encumbered property is presumably that there are not two witnesses to the delivery; if there are two witnesses, it becomes a complete document, as Rama writes immediately below. All the Rosh's discussion there concerns R' Yose, who holds that an asmachta is binding. We learn from it that where there is no asmachta, such as where the stipulation was made before an important beit din, the same rule applies. Rambam's language in chapter 27 of Hilchos Malveh is likewise that a document whose lien has been released is like a shard, implying that it has no utility at all. Rama explicitly writes at the end of siman 55 that only if he stipulated from the outset that the money should be a gift may he collect from unencumbered property; otherwise he may not. There is also proof from Bava Batra 32b, in the case where one said, "Give me the money I paid to the lender for you; here is the document," and the borrower said, "You did not pay for me," while the guarantor responded, "Did you not then return and take the money from me?" The Gemara concludes that this is a document whose lien has been released and that the guarantor cannot collect at all from the lender. Therefore, in my humble opinion, Levush is correct that a document whose lien has been released cannot be used to collect even from unencumbered property.
Sema also disagrees with Levush and rules that the signature on a mamrani need not be positioned directly opposite the written obligation. In my humble opinion, Levush ruled correctly. If one wishes to write the amount below the signature, it is certainly invalid, because there is a concern that one will cut away the amount and write above the signature, "I, the undersigned, owe so-and-so such-and-such," and the fact that the signature is near the bottom of the paper will not prevent this. The Gemara says that a person should not display his signature except at the top of a scroll; it does not say "or at the bottom." This shows that anything lower than the top of the sheet can be forged into a handwritten obligation. Even if the amount is written above the signature, one must be careful, because sometimes there may be a blank space of two lines between them and a document can be forged in that space. Therefore the amount must be written aligned directly with the signature. This appears correct.
Regarding a mamrani on which no amount or obligation was written at all, but only the signatures, it appears to me that nothing can be collected with it once it is brought before beit din in that invalid form. One could forge it by writing whatever he wishes above the signatures. Even if the borrower trusts him, the borrower has no right to prejudice others, for perhaps he owes other creditors on mamranos and lacks sufficient assets, causing them loss. Although the holder could have corrected the instrument before bringing it to beit din, that does not help once it came before beit din in an invalid form, as stated in siman 45, seif 4. I discussed this matter at length in siman 104, seif 10. Levush likewise invalidates it here, though he gives a different reason. Sema disputes that reason, but according to the reason I have written there is no basis to validate it.
