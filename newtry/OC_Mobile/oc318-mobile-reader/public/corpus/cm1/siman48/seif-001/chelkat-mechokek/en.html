One who says to a woman, "I betrothed you." The Tur writes, "I betrothed you before so-and-so and so-and-so." This implies that if he merely says, "I betrothed you," he may later explain that it was done without witnesses. The Rosh likewise writes in a responsum concerning Reuven's concubine, who said, "Reuven betrothed me," that her words are of no effect until she says, "Reuven betrothed me before two witnesses." There is room to doubt whether that was said specifically about the concubine in that case. Perhaps an ordinary man, and especially a Torah scholar who knows that kiddushin require witnesses, who says simply, "I betrothed you," is presumed to mean valid kiddushin and cannot later claim that he meant kiddushin without witnesses.
