The same applies to other custodians, such as a borrower who says it died, etc. The language continues, "even though they are liable to pay." This might suggest that even the unpaid custodian mentioned first says, "I am paying because I was negligent," rather than claiming a defense. But that is not exact. The same rule applies if they claim grounds that would exempt them, such as a borrower saying the animal died from use, a paid custodian saying it died through unavoidable accident, or an unpaid custodian saying it was stolen, but they do not want to swear and instead choose to pay. They must first swear that the item is not in their possession. The Tur begins the siman explicitly with an unpaid custodian who says the object was stolen or lost and refuses to swear. The Mechaber states the greater novelty. Even where the custodian says, "I was negligent" or the paid custodian says, "It was stolen," such that his stated facts themselves obligate him and there is less reason to suspect that he is lying because he wants the object, we nevertheless require the oath that it is not in his possession.
