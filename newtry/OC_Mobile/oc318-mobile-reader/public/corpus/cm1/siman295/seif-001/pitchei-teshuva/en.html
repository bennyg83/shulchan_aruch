He paid. See Baer Heitev and Responsa VeShav HaKohen, siman 50, who discusses this at length. He concludes that according to Rif and Rosh, with a borrower one specifically requires actual payment, even if he had stipulated to have only the liability of an unpaid custodian. For the other classes of custodians, however, even if they stipulated to have the liability of a borrower, it is enough that the custodian says, "I will pay." According to Rashi, however, the first version of the Gemara does not establish whether the governing reason is that of Rav Zevid or Rav Huna, and perhaps both reasons are valid. If so, even another custodian who stipulated to be liable as a borrower may require actual payment. He suggests that Rambam may hold this as well. Thus it remains doubtful whether a nonborrower who undertook borrower liability acquires the usual subrogation rights merely by saying he will pay, or only after actual payment. This remains practically relevant even nowadays with respect to subsequent appreciation. See there.
