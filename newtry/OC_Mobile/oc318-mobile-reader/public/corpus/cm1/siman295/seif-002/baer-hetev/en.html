He paid. This is not literal. As soon as he says, "I hereby pay," his legal status is the same as one who has paid. See the Sema and Shach, who discuss this at length.
He wished. This applies specifically where he paid voluntarily. If the judge compelled him to pay, such as where he admitted negligence but refused to pay, or said, "I do not know where I placed it," which itself constitutes negligence, and the judge collected from him against his will, he does not acquire the subsequent appreciation. So appears in the Gemara, chapter HaMafkid, and the poskim. The glosses to the Mordechai cite Riva as inferring from the Rif and Raavan that if collection did not have to be made against his will, he is not considered to have burdened the depositor. This also follows from Tosafot and the Rosh there, and the Beit Yosef writes that the Tur's language is precise in this respect. One should not object from the Gemara's question concerning one who first said, "I hereby pay," and then retracted and said, "I will not pay," where Tosafot and the Rosh write that even though he cannot legally retract, he does not acquire the double payment because he burdened beit din. There he had not yet paid, so his later refusal burdened beit din, and since he had not paid he does not acquire the double payment. Here, however, he actually paid. See the Rosh at the end of Shevuot regarding one who said, "I will swear," where the other party sought to roll additional claims into the oath, and he then said, "I hereby pay"; he nevertheless acquires the double payment. The same applies if he first took a custodian's oath and afterward paid: he acquires all appreciation arising from external causes, as Rava states in Bava Kamma 108, and all poskim rule. Yet if he first swore, a later statement alone is insufficient; he must actually pay, as Tosafot write in Bava Metzia 34a. The later phrase "the thief was identified" likewise includes a case where the item was later found: he acquires the increase in value. Indeed, the Gemara shows that even if it is later found in the custodian's own house, so long as he did not know this when he paid or said, "I hereby pay," he later acquires the appreciation. The Shach further writes that his acquisition of automatic appreciation applies only after he pays, as the Raavad writes, contrary to the Sema, who wrote generally that if the animal increased in value after it was deposited, the custodian need pay only its value at the time of deposit, following the Maggid Mishneh's objection to the Raavad. See there.
Appreciation. The Mordechai at the end of HaMafkid states that similarly, where a depositor paid the custodian for a gentile's pledge, he acquires the interest owed by the gentile. The Maggid Mishneh and Nimukei Yosef cite the Rashba that if a paid custodian has witnesses that the item suffered an unavoidable accident, or an unpaid custodian has witnesses that it was stolen, and nevertheless he wishes to pay, he does not acquire the double payment. From the outset the depositor did not contemplate that the custodian would pay where witnesses established facts that exempt him, and therefore he never transferred the double payment to him. It is then as though he sells the double payment only now, which is ineffective because one cannot sell a penalty before it becomes payable. Nimukei Yosef explains this in the name of the Yerushalmi. According to this, however, he does acquire any increase in market value, because such appreciation can be sold, provided he performed some act of acquisition, such as meshichah, since money alone does not effect acquisition. See Tosafot at the beginning of HaMafkid, s.v. "such as the fruits of a palm tree." The Rashba is further cited as ruling that if there are witnesses that an unpaid custodian was negligent or that a paid custodian's item was stolen, and they say, "We will pay," or pay voluntarily without compulsion of beit din, they acquire the double payment. The Maggid Mishneh writes that the Rambam and Ramban disagree, and in my view their opinion is primary. The Gemara indicates that he acquires the double payment only because he could otherwise have exempted himself. Shach.
From the body itself. This means shearings and offspring. It appears that even appreciation arising after the theft is not acquired by the custodian if it derives from the body itself. So writes the Maggid Mishneh according to the Rambam, and this also appears to be the view of the Rosh, Tur, and Rabbeinu Yerucham, who rule in accordance with Rava's first version. According to that version, one must say that it is as though the depositor stipulated, "except for appreciation from external causes [from the body itself]," as stated in the Gemara. I am close to saying that all poskim agree on this, since Rabbi Zeira appears in the Gemara to hold like the first version. See the Tur and Rambam for many laws concerning double payment, which have practical relevance even today if one seized property or where the item appreciated. Shach.
