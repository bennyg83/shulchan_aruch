He paid and did not wish to swear. Rama adopts the Mishnah's language. The Mishnah says: One who deposited an animal or utensils with another and they were stolen or lost, if the custodian paid and did not wish to swear that they were stolen and be exempt, and the thief was later found, to whom does the double payment belong? To the custodian. The Gemara explains that although one cannot ordinarily transfer something not yet in existence, here the depositor effectively says at the time of deposit: if it is stolen and you choose to pay me its value, my deposited item is transferred to you from now through the meshichah by which you took it into your domain. The Gemara asks why the custodian then does not also acquire the natural increase, such as wool and offspring, and answers that people transfer external gains such as double payment, and likewise later appreciation in value, in exchange for certainty of recovery of the principal. They ordinarily do not transfer growth arising from the body of the animal itself. Rabbi Yochanan says actual payment is unnecessary. Once the custodian says, "I will pay," he acquires the double payment if the thief is found. Most authorities rule accordingly. The Rosh and Tur, however, hold that where he only says "I will pay" without paying, he acquires the double payment only if he also swears that the object is not in his possession. The Beit Yosef questions their source. The Mechaber therefore omitted the disputed issue entirely. Rama states only the law of the Mishnah, which everyone accepts: once he actually paid and the thief is found, the custodian acquires the double payment. Nimukei Yosef adds in Rashba's name that if an unpaid custodian had witnesses proving unavoidable accident or theft and nevertheless chooses voluntarily to pay, he does not acquire the double payment. The depositor never contemplated that a custodian who could prove complete exemption would nevertheless pay, and therefore did not transfer the future double payment on that possibility. Any later transfer would be an ineffective sale of something not yet in existence.
