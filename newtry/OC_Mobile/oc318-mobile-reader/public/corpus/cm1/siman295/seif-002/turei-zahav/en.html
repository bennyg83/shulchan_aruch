(And that it is not in his possession.) The Sema's explanation is very forced. In my opinion the text should read, "and behold, it is not in his possession." This is also the reading in Rambam. Rambam's wording is: "that such and such occurred to it, and behold, it is in his possession." Clearly there is a scribal error, and it must read: "and behold, it is not in his possession." The same correction is required here. The explanation is as follows. The Gemara in the chapter HaGozel, page 107, states that there are three oaths: that he was not negligent with it, that he did not make unauthorized use of it, and that it is not in his possession. Rambam holds that "it is not in his possession" is not the exact wording of the oath, but a general description of the claim concerning which he swears. If he is an unpaid custodian who claims that the item was stolen, he swears that the event of theft occurred to it and that it is still not in his possession. In such a case he must state explicitly that it is not in his possession. A paid custodian who claims that the animal died, however, necessarily claims that it is no longer in his possession, and therefore need not state this explicitly. Thus, the oath counted by the Mechaber here is that such and such occurred to the item in a manner that removed it from his possession, each custodian according to the circumstance applicable to him. This differs from the Sema's explanation that the oath concerning possession is the second oath. The wording of Rambam before us in chapter 6 of the Laws of Borrowing likewise implies this. See there.
