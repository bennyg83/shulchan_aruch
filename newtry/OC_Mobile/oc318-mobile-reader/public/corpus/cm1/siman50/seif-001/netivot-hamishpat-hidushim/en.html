A receipt. This refers to a document payable simply to whoever produces it. If the document names the lender and also says "or whoever produces it," then because the holder derives his right through the named lender, as explained above, only a receipt supported by witnesses and stating that the payer comes through the named lender is effective, Sema and Shach. The Shach further rules that with a document payable to whoever produces it, if the debtor paid before witnesses without taking a receipt, or paid against a handwritten receipt, then even if the receipt was established in beit din before the purchaser acquired the document, the debtor may nevertheless have to pay the purchaser a second time. The purchaser can say that he did not know of the earlier payment, and because the document is payable to whoever produces it, it is as though the debtor originally obligated himself directly to the eventual holder, so the debtor caused his own loss. In addition, the debtor must at least reimburse the purchaser's purchase price because of the market protection enactment, just as one who buys an item later found to be stolen may recover the amount he paid. The market protection enactment also applies to negotiable debt instruments because they are commonly bought and sold like movable property. But if payment was documented by a receipt with witnesses, which has publicity, the purchaser is like one who bought from a notorious thief and is not entitled even to recover his purchase price. The Tumim rejects the Shach's first rationale and rules that only the purchaser's price must be reimbursed because of the market protection enactment, in accordance with the Shach's second rationale. See Beurim, seif katan 5, that a receipt helps only where the witnesses testify that the debt document was then in the creditor's possession when the receipt was written. Without that, it is ineffective. If such a bearer document is lost, the debtor need not pay unless the creditor provides a guarantee protecting him against future loss caused by the lost document, Responsa Tzemach Tzedek.
