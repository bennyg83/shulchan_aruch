A document signed by witnesses that says, etc. Any writing signed by witnesses has the status of a document even if it does not say, 'before us witnesses,' but is written in the form of a personal handwritten obligation. This is evident from the Tur, as I wrote at the end of siman 46 and below in siman 69, and so write Beit Yosef and Perishah.
The same applies to a judgment, etc. This follows the view that a person cannot say, 'I paid,' against a formal judgment, as explained above in siman 39, seif 10. Otherwise he could be believed to say, 'I do not owe you,' through a migo that he could say, 'I paid,' just as with a personal handwritten obligation.
Specifically where he comes by virtue of that named person, etc. Meaning, the new holder derives his right from that person's claim. Since the document names a particular lender, that named lender is primary, and the added phrase, 'or anyone who produces it,' is like writing, 'and those who derive rights from him.' See below siman 61, seif 4, and siman 66, seif 1.
If a receipt was written, etc. This refers to the opening case where no lender is named at all. But where the document says that the borrower owes a named person or anyone who produces it, so that another holder must derive his right through that named person, it appears that a receipt in the borrower's hand is not effective merely because the borrower says, 'The person who signed the receipt came by your authority and I paid him.' More than that, even if the receipt explicitly says that the signer came by authority of the named lender and received money from this borrower, it does not bind the holder unless it is known that the signer truly had authority from the lender. One person's admission cannot obligate another or impair that other's document.
