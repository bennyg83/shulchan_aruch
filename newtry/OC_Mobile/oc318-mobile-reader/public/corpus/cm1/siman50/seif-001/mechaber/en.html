A document stating, "I borrowed one hundred from you." This siman contains one seif.
If a witnessed document states, "I, so and so, borrowed one hundred from you," whoever produces it may collect with it. But if there are no witnesses and it is merely in the debtor's own handwriting, then since he would be believed to say, "I paid," he is also believed to say, "I did not borrow from you but from someone else, and the document fell from him and you found it." Rama: The same applies to a judgment stating that so and so became liable to "the opposing party"; he must pay whoever produces it. Likewise, if a document states that so and so owes so and so, or owes whoever produces the document, he must pay whoever produces it, provided that the producer derives his right through the named person unless the document explicitly states that collection may be made whether or not the producer derives his right through him. If a receipt was later written in anyone's name concerning this document, no one may thereafter collect on it. If a person wrote in a document that he obligates himself toward whoever produces that debt instrument, then even if it is known that he never previously owed that producer anything, he must nevertheless pay him because he expressly obligated himself toward him. This is as explained above in siman 40. See below, siman 61, seif 10.
