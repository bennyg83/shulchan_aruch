On its reverse. This excludes the Mordechai's view that one may wrap a separate paper around the document and write an identifying mark on it. The Maggid Mishneh likewise cites this in the name of a Gaon and objects that no identifying mark can be made that cannot be forged. So explains the Sema. The Shach writes that Heaven forbid that one reject the Gaon's ruling without a clear proof from the Gemara, especially since several authorities cite it as practical halachah. In his view, even the Rambam agrees. The Rambam speaks where there is blank space on the reverse of the document, in which case it is obviously preferable to write the authentication there. The Gaon speaks where the paper is written on both sides and no blank space remains. Then one wraps another paper around it and writes a distinctive identifying mark. Specifically, the judges should write something with their own signatures on the body of the document and state in the authentication what they did. If someone later forges it, the handwriting will not match. Other identifying marks, however, are indeed ineffective. Thus the Gaon's words are clear and well reasoned.
