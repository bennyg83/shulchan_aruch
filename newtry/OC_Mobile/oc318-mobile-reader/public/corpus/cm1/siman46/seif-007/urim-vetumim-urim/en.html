The fourth method, that witnesses come, etc. See Tumim. I concluded that Rabbeinu Yeruham means as follows. If the witnesses who signed the document themselves say, "This is our handwriting," and others say, "This is not their handwriting," the signing witnesses are certainly more credible. There is only a dispute whether the signing witnesses must be questioned again, lest their original statement have been based on an assumption, or whether no further inquiry is required. The law was not decisively settled. But everyone agrees that if they were questioned again and maintained that it was their handwriting, they are more credible than the other witnesses. If the document was already validated by beit din, the Tur, Even HaEzer siman 152, makes clear that two witnesses who then say it was forged do not prevail, because people do not scrutinize a matter after a beit din has validated it. See Tumim. If the signing witnesses say, "This is not our handwriting," while others say that it is, then if each signing witness says only, "This is not my handwriting," but does not know about the other's handwriting, everyone agrees that the other witnesses are believed, since one witness cannot stand against two. But if both signing witnesses testify that neither signature is theirs, and two others say that both are genuine, Ra'ah and Ritva hold that this is two against two and the document is weakened. I remain uncertain, because once the signatures were validated by others, perhaps a witness can no longer say that it is not his handwriting, under the rule that after testimony has been given it cannot be retracted. The practical law therefore requires study. See there.
And they testify. Even if the signing witnesses are present in the city, the document may initially be validated through other witnesses. Sema writes this in the name of Beit Yosef and Darchei Moshe. Bach sought to infer from the Tur's wording, which says that if the signing witnesses are not before us we validate through others, that when they are before us it is preferable to hear them because they are more familiar with their own handwriting. This may be rejected. The Tur may merely be teaching that even when they are absent we accept testimony about their signatures, despite the possibility that this testimony could later create liability for the absent witnesses through contradiction or disproof, and one might otherwise think that we may not disadvantage a person in his absence.
That this is the handwriting of these witnesses. Even if they recognize only most of the witness's name, or his father's name, and the like, that is sufficient, Itur as cited by Shach.
And they may testify, etc. See Tumim. Even Rama says this only when others state that this is the handwriting of the witnesses. But when the signing witnesses themselves testify regarding their own handwriting, they cannot testify to it from a written memorandum. Likewise, if a witness is mute and cannot testify orally, he cannot testify from writing, because he must at least be fit for oral declaration.
And they need not testify, etc. Nevertheless, this is classified only as testimony and not as beit din validation. A practical difference is that if those witnesses die before their written testimony reaches beit din, it is ineffective, because they must still be fit for oral testimony when the matter comes before beit din. This is unlike a beit din validation, which remains valid even if the judges die. Likewise, if others merely testify that they saw these witnesses' written testimony, that is ineffective as testimony from one witness through another, whereas testimony that they saw an already validated document is effective. There are many similar differences. This also resolves why, where two judges recognize the signatures, they testify orally before the third and cannot suffice with their written testimony. Written testimony would be testimony, not a beit din validation.

We do not validate from it. Because it is in his possession, he has time to imitate its form and create a forgery. Accordingly, even if those comparison documents are now held by others, if it is known that he keeps in his home another document bearing signatures of the same witnesses, we cannot validate by visual comparison of the documents.
