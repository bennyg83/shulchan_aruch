A document is authenticated in one of five ways: the judges recognize the witnesses' handwriting; the witnesses sign before the judges; each signatory appears and says, 'This is my handwriting, and I witnessed this matter'; other witnesses testify that the handwriting is that of the signatories, and they may give this identifying testimony from a written statement without testifying orally (Rivash, simanim 382 and 413); or the handwriting is established from other documents, and the beit din compares the handwriting. Authentication from other documents requires two documents concerning two fields whose owners possessed them openly and properly for three years without fear of any claim, or two marriage contracts. The comparison documents must be produced by someone other than the person seeking authentication, lest he have forged all of them. {Rama: Even if both comparison documents are themselves authenticated, they are not used if produced by the person seeking authentication (Tur and Beit Yosef in the name of Rashi). Others disagree.} A document may also be authenticated from a single document that was challenged and then upheld in beit din. {Rama: Some say even an authenticated document that was never challenged may be used, as stated below in seif 37.} Some say a signature may be authenticated from a book the witness wrote, but not from a letter. Others say even a book may be used only if the writer signed his name at its end, in which case authentication is made from the signature. See also siman 136.
