There is a dispute in the Tosefta where the document witnesses say, "This is our handwriting," while others say, "This is not their handwriting," whether additional authentication is required. See Beurim. Where authentication is based on visual recognition of handwriting, we follow the majority of opinions. If the document witnesses say, "This is not our handwriting," and two others say, "It is their handwriting," then if each document witness testifies regarding both his own signature and that of his fellow, it is two against two. The Shach writes that according to the Mechaber, who understands the concern to be that perhaps the holder forged all the documents, the requirement that the comparison documents emerge from another person's possession eliminates the concern once they are authenticated. The other opinion, however, is concerned that because a document is in this person's possession he may have learned how to imitate the handwriting from it. According to that view, even if the comparison documents emerge from another person's possession, where it is known that the litigant also has a specimen in his own possession one may not authenticate by visual comparison alone.
