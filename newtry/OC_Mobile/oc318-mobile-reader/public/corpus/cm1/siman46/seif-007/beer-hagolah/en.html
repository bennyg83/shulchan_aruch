m) The wording of the Rambam there.
n) From what we say there, Ketubot 21b: "Learn from here that judges who recognize," etc. Although Rav Ashi rejects the proof there, we do not abandon what the Gemara regarded as obvious merely because of Rav Ashi's rejection.
s) This is straightforward from the law where they recognize the signatures; all the more so where the witnesses signed in their presence.
o) The Mishnah, "this one says, this is my handwriting," etc., following the Sages there, who hold that they testify concerning the maneh in the document, 20b.
p) This is found in many places in Mishnah and Gemara. One example is Rav Yehudah's statement in the name of Rav concerning two signatories when one died, etc., there 21a. The same applies if he did not die and is before us; the Gemara merely states the usual case, as the Kesef Mishneh writes there.
tz) From the Mishnah, "or his handwriting was established from another place," there 18b.
k) The Nehardeans said, etc., there 21a.
r) Like the statement of Rav Shimi bar Ashi there.
sh) So it appears in the Rambam there.
t) According to those who explain that we suspect perhaps he forged from among genuine signatures.
a) According to the view that we suspect perhaps he forged the entire document. If the comparison document is itself validated, one may validate from it. So writes the Kesef Mishneh there. However, I saw in Tosafot there, 21a, s.v. "a witness and a judge combine," that even from a validated document we suspect perhaps he forged. This is as stated above in seif 6. In my humble opinion, this is the Rama's explanation of the Rambam, and therefore he did not begin the gloss with "some say."
b) Rambam there, from the baraita at the end of 19b and Rav Assi's statement there 20a, according to the textual version before us and that of the Rif, as the Kesef Mishneh explains. So too writes the Tur in the name of the Ramah in seif 24.
c) There in the name of his father the Rosh, who explains that the Gemara merely states the usual case, because they were not accustomed to validate a document unless someone had challenged it.
