From their writing. See Baer Heitev in the name of the Shach, who asks why the Rama distinguishes here when elsewhere he follows the Mechaber, concluding that even in a rabbinic matter there should be no distinction between a mute person and another witness. See Even HaEzer, siman 142, seif 7 in the Rama, who writes that a mute person cannot act as the agent bringing a get. Beit Shmuel there, seif katan 11, cites Rivash, responsum 413, who permits authentication on the basis of writing, as the Rama likewise rules here in Choshen Mishpat, siman 46, seif 7. Accordingly, writing the declaration that the get was written and signed in the agent’s presence should also be valid, since that declaration functions as authentication. Beit Shmuel therefore explains that Rivash’s responsum 241, which rejects written testimony, refers specifically to a mute person. On this basis, the Rama’s rulings in the two places are consistent, contrary to the Shach. Korban Netanel, chapter 1 of Gittin, letter 37, likewise strongly challenges the Shach for overlooking the Rama in Even HaEzer and Rivash, responsum 240. Knesset Yechezkel, responsum 82, answers the Shach’s difficulty in another manner, and Tumim gives the same answer, as cited in Netivot HaMishpat, seif katan 20. See also Beit Meir to Even HaEzer there.
