And one of them was found to be related. See Shach, seif katan 77, who writes that if the related person signed at the end, the document is invalid just as in other documents. According to what I wrote in siman 45, seif 12, the reason a document is invalid when the disqualified person signed at the end, even though he signed later and the qualified witnesses never actually joined with him in the act of signing, is that their essential testimony is completed only when the document is delivered. At that moment all the signatories are joined together. See there, where I discussed this at length. That reasoning does not apply to judges. Moreover, even according to the reason stated by the authorities, that when a person signs in a place where the document could be validated on the basis of his signature we infer that he intended to sign as a witness and the entire testimony is nullified, this does not apply to judges. We can assume that three other qualified judges actually sat and adjudicated, and the principle that if one of the group is found related or disqualified applies to witnesses, not in the same manner to judges, as explained by Mishneh LaMelech and as I cited above in siman 28. One cannot argue that the document should nevertheless be invalid lest someone later validate it using the related judge's signature. If that were the concern, even related persons who signed among the first signatures would create the same problem, because where only three signatures appear, the document can be validated through any two of them, as explained in the gloss.
