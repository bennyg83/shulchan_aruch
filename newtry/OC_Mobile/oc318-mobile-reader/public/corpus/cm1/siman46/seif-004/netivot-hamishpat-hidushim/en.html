With two, it is not judicial validation. The Shach concludes that if the validation consists of others testifying that the handwriting is that of the witnesses, or the witnesses themselves say so, or the borrower admits before the two that it is their handwriting, this does not create a beit din validation, because testimony recorded before only two is testimony from writing and the two are not a beit din. Nevertheless, according to Rama below in seif 7, such evidence can be valid testimony for purposes of establishing the document. There are many practical differences between judicial validation and evidentiary validation. This is unlike the Sema and is the correct ruling. The Taz writes that although such written testimony is effective, the beit din that later judges on its basis must themselves recognize the handwriting of the validating witnesses. It is insufficient that other witnesses identify the handwriting of those validating witnesses. This is primary. If the document is in the borrower's own handwriting and two people validated it, see Beurim: this helps only if the borrower instructed them to validate it.
Rabbinic. See the Shach, who discusses at length whether validation is Torah law where the borrower claims the document is forged. See Beurim: if the document came before beit din before he claimed forgery, everyone agrees validation is rabbinic. If the lender says he does not know at all who the witnesses are or from where they came, everyone agrees validation is Torah law. If the witnesses themselves say, "We did not sign," then if each says that neither this handwriting nor his fellow's is his, validation is Torah law, but if each testifies only about his own handwriting, validation is rabbinic. If a document of acquisition is found and the lender claims it is his, validation is likewise rabbinic. By contrast, if a kiddushin document is found and the husband makes no claim, but beit din seeks to establish her as married because of the document, validation is Torah law. See Beurim for all these details.
