(Seif 15: If two authentication judges, etc.) The Sema writes that the Tur and Mechaber mention “the judge and another with him” in seif 14 only as an added novelty, and Maharshal likewise says that in truth no additional witness is needed. This is very difficult. The Gemara states that Shmuel holds a witness and a judge may combine, and the Rosh explains that a judge must be joined by another person. Rav Yonah appears to dispute this, holding that the authentication judges are like the original document witnesses, and therefore according to Shmuel a judge would need no associate. Yet the Tur’s wording, “and Rav Yonah wrote,” implies that Rav Yonah does not disagree with what preceded. The correct explanation is that the judge testifies to the authentication, not merely to the handwriting, but he is not believed by himself to identify the handwriting appearing in the authentication. With an original witness, the rule that he testifies to the debt in the document allows us to regard him as though he expressly testified to the loan without relying on the writing. A judge, however, cannot be regarded as though he omitted all reference to the written authentication, because there is no authentication without a written judicial record. His advantage therefore depends upon the record. Once the authentication is established as genuine, he is considered to testify to the debt in the document, but he still needs another person to establish that his authentication signature is genuine. When two judges each testify to his own signature, each assists the other, because each thereby attests to the signatures of both original witnesses. Here, however, the second original witness’s signature is authenticated only by the judge’s assertion that this is his own judicial signature, and he is not believed without another person joining him.
