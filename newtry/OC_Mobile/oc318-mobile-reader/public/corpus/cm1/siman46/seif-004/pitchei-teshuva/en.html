If they authenticated it with two. See Baer Heitev in the name of the Sema, through his statement that if the borrower himself signed the document and admits before two witnesses that it is his signature, it appears that this suffices for authentication. See the Taz as well. Rabbi Akiva Eiger writes in his glosses that this requires substantial analysis. The Rama in seif 7 permits testimony from writing because, as Rivash explains, authentication of documents is rabbinic. But with a handwritten obligation, which the Sema above in siman 35 and the Shach here in seif katan 10 hold requires authentication by Torah law, testimony from writing should not suffice. Netivot HaMishpat likewise raises this difficulty and answers that the case is one in which the borrower instructed the witnesses to sign and authenticate, so their signed attestation is treated like any other document made at the direction of the obligated party.
