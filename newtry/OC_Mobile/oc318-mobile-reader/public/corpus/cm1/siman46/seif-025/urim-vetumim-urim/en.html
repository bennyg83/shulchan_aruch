Rather, each one, etc. See Tumim. I explained that according to this approach the case must be one where each time only one witness testified before one judge. Had two testified before one, or one before two, then since in document validation a witness may become a judge, they would combine into a panel of three. See there.
Received testimony, etc. But if they received the testimony together, even if each later signed separately, there is no problem, Sema. So too in the responsum attributed to Ramban.
Invalid. This means where the judges' handwriting is recognized only through these witnesses. Otherwise the validation is not invalidated, rather we do not collect with it and we do not tear it up, as below in seif 37. See Tumim.
And the judges, etc. They are forbidden to do this, and therefore a person cannot render himself wicked by claiming that he did so.
Some validate. See Tumim for what I wrote regarding Rosh. Beit Yosef, cited by Sema, writes that according to this opinion the validation should state only, "We were a panel of three when this document came before us," because otherwise the judges would be writing a falsehood, Sema. If they did not write at all that they were three, but wrote simply, "This document was clarified to us as bearing the witnesses' signatures," it is also valid, and then there is no need for that formula. See Tumim. Regarding Maharsha's statement that when the matter was seen at night witnesses must at least repeat their testimony by day so that there is a valid declaration, see Tumim, where I wrote that this too does not appear from the poskim.
