They were not all in one sitting, etc. This differs from the preceding seif, where two judges testify before the third. There the two did not need the authentication testimony to be formally received before themselves, because they personally recognized the signatures. When they later testified before the third, it was as though all three received the evidence together, as explained there.
Rather, each received testimony separately, it is invalid, etc. Nevertheless, when they sign the certification they need not sign together. So writes Darkei Moshe, seif 21.
And the judges themselves are not believed. Because they would thereby admit that they originally acted unlawfully, and a person may not render himself wicked.
And there is one who validates it. Beit Yosef writes that according to this view the authentication should state: 'We were sitting as a panel of three when this document was produced before us, and when it became clear to us that these were their signatures, we approved and authenticated it as proper.' Although they were not together when the testimony concerning the signatures was heard, they must nevertheless be together to inform one another that all three know that these are the witnesses' signatures. So writes Darkei Moshe, seif 21.
