They infer that testimony concerning the witnesses’ signatures need not be given before all three judges together, and that the authentication here is invalid only because the judges signed a false statement. In my view, the first opinion is correct. Otherwise, why do the Gemara and authorities repeatedly use the formula, “We sat together as a court of three”? Likewise, they repeatedly speak of “three who sat to authenticate a document,” indicating that authentication is a judicial act requiring all three to be assembled together. Many passages in the Gemara and authorities imply that three are required for authentication just as for adjudication, meaning that all must be present together. This is explicit in the Ran’s citation of the Raah in the second chapter of Ketubot. Rabbi Zeira’s principal teaching is that because only two judges sign, the authentication must state, “We sat together as a court of three.” Had all three signed, there would be no need to write that formula, because when all three sign we do not suspect that they were not assembled together. The Beit Yosef cites this in section 23. The Rivash, siman 318, writes similarly in the name of the Ramah. It follows that if they were not assembled together, the authentication is invalid. This is also implied by the Rivash in simanim 382 and 413 and by other authorities. The same is demonstrated by the rule below in seif 26 that after two judges have signed, the third may not sign with them because their original joinder has ended, according to the Rif, Rabbeinu Chananel, and those who follow them. This shows that all must convene together. Although I write below, seif 26, seif katan 65, that Rashi’s explanation of the Gemara is primary rather than that of Rabbeinu Chananel and the Rif, their statement on this point is nevertheless correct. The Rosh and Tur themselves also record it. The Rosh’s inference that the authentication is invalid only because the two wrote falsely is not compelling. Since two judges recognize the signatures and testify before the third, and a witness may become a judge in document authentication, it is regarded as though testimony were given before all three together. Thus, the two signatures are invalid precisely because all three had to be present in one assembly and the two signed without the third, as the Ran writes above in seif katan 58.
