Seif 30. In the gloss: some say it is invalid. See Shach, who asks that nothing follows from the Rosh's language to prove this first opinion. Perhaps Rama's intention is to the view of Ramah cited by Shach in the preceding seif. Ramah holds that when the formula stating that one witness was absent was not written, we fear that the absent witness may have been a relative or otherwise disqualified, and that this was why he did not sign. According to that view, even where we do not actually see that he was disqualified, the irregularity creates a concern that he was. Certainly here, where one of the witnesses was in fact found to be a relative or disqualified, we cannot simply attribute the failure to sign to an innocent cause. Rama therefore understood Ramah to disagree here as well. Examine this carefully.
