Seif 13. In the gloss: all the more so if there are witnesses who can authenticate the signature of the surviving witness. See Shach, who writes that if the witness himself already testified first, all remaining assistance is thereafter treated as though removed. I am uncertain where his first testimony was given outside the presence of the litigant, or under another circumstance in which ordinary testimony would not be effective, but it was accepted only because the Sages were lenient with authentication of documents. If afterward there are no witnesses concerning the second signature, and this witness now writes his signature on a shard or others testify to his signature so that he together with another can testify concerning the second signature, does that work? On one hand, perhaps once he testified initially he can no longer participate in this second form of authentication, as Shach writes. On the other hand, his initial testimony was not valid under ordinary evidentiary law and was accepted only because the Sages were lenient in document authentication. That leniency was enacted for the benefit of the document holder, not to create a stringency against him. Wherever reliance on the leniency would now produce a disadvantage, perhaps we restore the ordinary law. Since the first testimony was not intrinsically valid testimony, money was not yet extracted through his mouth, and others may therefore authenticate his signature and allow him to join in authenticating the other signature. Or perhaps the Sages established the leniency as a complete legal acceptance, and once his testimony was accepted it has all the consequences of accepted testimony. The matter remains doubtful.
