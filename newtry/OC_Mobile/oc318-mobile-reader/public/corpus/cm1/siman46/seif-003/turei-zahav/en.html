(Seif 14: A scribe’s writing and one witness.) The Sema writes that collection may nevertheless be made from unencumbered property. This is very surprising. How can money be extracted from a borrower who denies the debt on the strength of the scribe’s writing and one witness? Gittin 88 implies that the scribe’s writing alone has no force. Rabbenu Yerucham, cited by the Beit Yosef, writes that collection from unencumbered property is possible “according to the Yerushalmi,” meaning the case of one witness in writing and one orally. The Yerushalmi asks how they can combine, since one written witness is ordinarily nothing, being testimony from writing. It answers that another witness is also signed in the document but his signature is not authenticated. The witness whose signature is authenticated can then impose an oath, and together with an oral witness may even support collection. The Maggid Mishneh, cited in siman 51, explains that we do not rule this way; one written witness alone obligates an oath just as two witnesses obligate payment. Rabbenu Yerucham followed the Yerushalmi and therefore meant collection only where an additional oral witness exists. If there are merely two signatures in the document and one is not authenticated, no authority can allow collection when the borrower denies. The Rama later suggested that the scribe’s authentication is ineffective only when the scribe is absent, whereas if he appears and testifies to his writing he is no worse than another oral witness. This too is very difficult, for why should his presence add more than recognition of his handwriting?
