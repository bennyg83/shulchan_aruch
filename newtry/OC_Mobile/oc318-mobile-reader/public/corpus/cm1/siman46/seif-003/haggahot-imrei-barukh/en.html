(Sema, seif katan 2) If so, this is like a migo against witnesses, etc. Note: See the language of the Itur cited in Baal HaTerumot, gate 13, part 2, ruling 3. He gives this reason only where the document states, "You are believed against me like two witnesses." He then adds that even without such a clause the debtor is not believed by a migo, because his present claim contradicts his earlier admission, and because a person is careful with such a document and would not pay until it was returned. Baal HaTerumot disagrees, reasoning that so long as the document has not been authenticated it is like a mere shard. The lender therefore relies on the debtor's own admission, and the same mouth that prohibited is the mouth that permitted. Gedulei Terumah notes this against the Beit Yosef.
