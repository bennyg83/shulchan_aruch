See Baer Heitev in the name of the Shach, who asks that if so the Rama is difficult, since there the Mechaber states the rule without distinction, etc., and therefore even in a rabbinic matter there should be no distinction between a mute person and one who is not mute. See Even HaEzer, siman 142, seif 7 in the Rama, who writes that therefore a mute person cannot bring a get. Beit Shmuel there, seif katan 11, writes: See Rivash, siman 413, who validates authentication on the basis of a writing, and so too the Rama writes in Choshen Mishpat siman 46, seif 7. According to this, a person should likewise be able to write, 'It was written and signed in my presence,' since this is a form of authentication. Necessarily, Rivash's statement in siman 241 that testimony from writing is ineffective refers specifically to a mute person. On this basis the two rulings of the Rama, there in seif 7 and seif 36, are consistent, unlike the Shach there. End of quote. Korban Netanel, first chapter of Gittin, entry 37, likewise strongly questions the Shach for not having seen the Rama's words in Even HaEzer and the Rivash in siman 240. See there. See Responsa Knesset Yechezkel, siman 82, who resolves the Shach's difficulty in another manner, and Tumim, whose resolution is cited in Netivot HaMishpat, seif katan 20. See also Beit Me'ir in Even HaEzer there.
