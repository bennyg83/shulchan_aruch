Seif 5. Not in the presence of the litigant, see Sema seif katan 11. If witnesses execute a certification document but it does not state that the party told them to write it, there is room to say that this is nevertheless a Torah level authentication of his handwriting, analogous to authenticating a borrower's signature. Such authentication does not permit collection from minor heirs merely on the strength of the borrower's signature. By contrast, where a document is authenticated through signatures that match signatures on other properly executed documents, beit din itself performs the authentication, and there is room to say that this does permit collection even from minor heirs. In the present era, when documents in foreign script and language are common, omission of wording equivalent to "he told us to write" tends to indicate that no such instruction was given, since these documents are not prepared with the same care as documents written by trained Jewish scribes and witnesses in Hebrew. Many people sign without being able to read and rely on a Jewish scribe. Even if that procedure was mistaken, it does not automatically invalidate the document, because perhaps it was read before them. If the witnesses themselves say that they relied on the scribe, the principle that a person cannot render himself wicked may not apply, because they may not have understood that the practice was prohibited.
