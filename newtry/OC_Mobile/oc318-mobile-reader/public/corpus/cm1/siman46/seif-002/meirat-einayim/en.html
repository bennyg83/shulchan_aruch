If it contains a credibility clause, he is not believed. The reason is that whenever the borrower granted the lender credibility, it is as though witnesses testify that he did not repay him so long as the lender retains the document containing that clause. A migo in the face of witnesses is ineffective. The Bach and Baal HaTerumot disagree and hold that such contractual credibility is not as strong as witnesses, because sometimes a borrower leaves the document in the lender's possession for some reason even after repayment. It is therefore not a migo against witnesses, especially here where the document is not authenticated and the borrower can say that he left it with the lender relying on his ability, if sued, to claim that it was forged. See Perishah.
And this is the more reasonable view. The Tur and Mechaber likewise rule without qualification at the beginning of siman 82.
