But if it contains a credibility clause, he is not believed. This is difficult. The Gemara in Ketubot 19 discusses Rav Yehudah in the name of Rav, who says that one who claims a document is a document of trust is not believed. The Gemara asks: if the borrower says this, it is obvious, for he cannot invalidate the creditor's document, and answers that the case is where the handwriting cannot be authenticated from another source, while Rav holds that one who admits that a document was written need not have it authenticated. How did the Gemara know to establish the case that way? Why not say that the document contains a credibility clause, in which case according to the opinion cited here he is not believed even if the document still requires authentication? One cannot answer that the Gemara did not want to force the unqualified phrase, "one who says it is a document of trust," into a case involving a special credibility clause. Later in the same passage it establishes the statement according to the possibility that the creditor himself says it is a document of trust and that doing so harms others. That case necessarily concerns a document containing a credibility clause, because otherwise even where other creditors would be harmed, the creditor is believed, as stated explicitly below in siman 47. We must therefore say one of two things. Either the authority cited here disagrees with the rule below in siman 47, as the Shach writes there that the matter is disputed among the authorities, and holds that even an ordinary document without a credibility clause does not allow the creditor to be believed where others would be harmed. Or else the Gemara could indeed have answered by establishing the case as one involving a credibility clause, but because the Gemara already knew that Rav in fact held that one who admits a document was written need not authenticate it, and Rav Huna expressly transmitted that explanation in his name, it gave the true explanation rather than an alternative possibility. See Tosafos there, s.v. "and according to Rav Huna." This also resolves their difficulty. Examine carefully.
