(Seif 5: He stands and protests, etc.) In Even HaEzer, siman 42, the Rama writes that if a document is found stating that a certain man betrothed a certain woman, but no witnesses are signed, authentication does not establish concern for the betrothal. This is difficult. Tosafot, the Tur, and the Mechaber rule here that even when the litigant stands and protests, authentication is required only rabbinically, as the Beit Yosef explains. By Torah law the document is treated as authenticated. Even according to the Mordechai gloss cited by the Sema, that denial of the signature makes authentication a Torah requirement, the Rama should not have ruled categorically that there is no betrothal. He should have limited the leniency to a case of explicit denial. This requires study in practice, especially because Maharik, responsum 74, required a bill of divorce. See also what I wrote in siman 110 concerning authentication of handwriting.
