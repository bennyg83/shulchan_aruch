It contains a clause of credibility. The Sema says that according to the view believing him through a migo, the clause of credibility is not absolute because a borrower may sometimes leave such a document with another under circumstances where he still relies upon the ability to claim forgery. In my view that is not the essential rationale. The true reason is that an ordinary contractual credibility clause can itself be overcome by a valid migo. But if the document grants credibility equivalent to two witnesses and the borrower admits having written it, then a migo cannot help, because that would be a migo against witnesses. This follows the rules in siman 71 concerning a migo against a clause granting credibility equal to two witnesses.
