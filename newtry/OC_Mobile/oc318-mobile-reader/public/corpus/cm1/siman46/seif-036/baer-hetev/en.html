The Shach notes that the Mechaber's words imply that a person who became mute cannot sign a document. The Mechaber's reason is based on what he concludes in siman 28, seif 15, in the name of Rabbeinu Tam, that testimony may be sent in writing to beit din, and that only a mute is excluded. According to that approach, there is no distinction between written testimony and a formal document. But according to the other authorities in siman 28, seif 11, who hold that everyone is forbidden to send testimony in writing, while nevertheless a formal document is not included in that prohibition, even a document signed after one became mute should be valid. Still, according to the Rambam, who holds that testimony in a document is only rabbinically effective in order not to close the door before borrowers, perhaps where healthy witnesses are available the Sages left the Biblical rule intact. According to what the Shach concluded there, however, that the primary view is that testimony in a document is valid by Torah law and is not subject to the requirement 'from their mouths and not from their writing,' the signature of a mute should also be valid. Perhaps a distinction can be drawn, though with difficulty. This requires study.
