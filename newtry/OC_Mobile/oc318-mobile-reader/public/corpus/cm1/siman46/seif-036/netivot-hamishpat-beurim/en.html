He was healthy and then became mute. The Shach infers that a mute person cannot sign a document. He asks that according to Mechaber, who does not apply the ordinary defect of written testimony to formal deeds, a signature made after he became mute should be valid, and in any event why can he not authenticate his own handwriting through writing? Urim VeTumim answers that when a witness authenticates his own signature he is effectively testifying to the debt in the deed. Ketzot HaChoshen gives the stronger explanation: a mute person is disqualified by the verse, 'If he does not tell,' which excludes one unable to give oral testimony. Although writing can sometimes count as communication, testimony is specifically restricted by 'from their mouths and not from their writing.' Thus at the time of seeing he was not capable of giving the legally required form of testimony. That is a personal disqualification at the beginning of the testimony, and later recovery cannot cure it. This is correct.
