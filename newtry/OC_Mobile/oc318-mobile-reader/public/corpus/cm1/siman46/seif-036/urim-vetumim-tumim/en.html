He signed while healthy and then became mute, etc. Shach, seif katan 93, asks why a mute person cannot sign a document. We do not follow Rabbenu Tam's view that written testimony is valid only when the witness remains capable of oral testimony because "anything fit for mixing" is treated as mixed. Rather, ordinary testimony from writing is generally invalid, yet a formal document is valid by a special Torah rule. Why then should a mute witness be excluded from that document rule? The law is nevertheless clear from Gittin 71a. The Gemara asks regarding a mute person, who is generally unfit for testimony, from a baraita saying he is examined for "testimony and inheritances." The Gemara is forced to interpret "testimony" as testimony concerning a woman's death and "inheritances" as a special firstborn case. If a mute person could give ordinary testimony through a formal document, the Gemara could have answered simply that the baraita speaks of written documentary testimony, or of a written testament concerning inheritance. Evidently a mute person cannot create ordinary testimony through a document. The reason is that the Torah's special recognition of documentary testimony applies only to a person who is generally fit to give the testimony orally. The writing is an alternative legal form for a testimony that could otherwise be stated by mouth. Someone wholly incapable of oral testimony does not enter the special document exception. Razah explains document testimony differently. Because the witnesses write by command of the borrower, their writing is treated like the borrower's own writing, and an agent acts as the principal. One might ask that a mute borrower can certainly create obligations through his own writing, so why should his witness-agents need speech? The answer is that the witnesses must at least be capable, at the time of signing, of testifying orally that the borrower appointed them and that they are acting as his agents. If they are incapable of giving that oral testimony at all, their written assertion of agency itself is merely testimony from writing and cannot establish the authority on which the document depends. Thus even under Razah's theory, the witness must be one who is personally fit for oral testimony at the time of signing.
