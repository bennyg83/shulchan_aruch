Some validate. The basis is that document validation is rabbinic. My brother Rabbi Yehudah asked from Bava Batra, which says that if a person knew testimony before becoming the son in law and afterward became related, he cannot personally testify to his handwriting, though others may identify it. This seems to show that a relative cannot participate in validation. The answer follows the Geonic position cited by Knesset HaGedolah: the lenient view allows a relative only where one validator is related and another is unrelated. Two relatives are not accepted. Their proof is an a fortiori argument from a minor, whose testimony can help in validation if accompanied by an adult. By the rule that the derived law cannot exceed its source, a relative too must be accompanied by a qualified unrelated person. In the son in law case, when the signed witness testifies concerning his own signature, he is effectively the sole source for that half of the document. Any additional witness about that same signature is treated as superfluous. Therefore his personal related testimony cannot be repaired by adding another. This also resolves the proof from the case where a stranger cannot combine with the father or brother of one signed witness to validate the other signature. The problem is that the relevant half of the validation would effectively rest upon relatives alone. The lenient view requires each side of the validation to contain qualified testimony. In practice, however, the accepted law is not to validate documents through relatives even when accompanied by an unrelated person.
