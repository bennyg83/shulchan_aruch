And the authentication judges, and so forth. The Rama’s words imply that even one who validates related authentication witnesses nevertheless disqualifies related authentication judges. The Ir Shushan states explicitly that authentication judges related to the lender or borrower are invalid according to all opinions. Their words are astonishing. It is obvious that one who validates related authentication witnesses likewise validates related authentication judges, for there is no reason to distinguish them. The validating opinion relies upon the fact that authentication of documents is rabbinic and the Sages were lenient, leaving the matter at its Torah law, under which no authentication is needed. That reasoning applies equally to authentication judges, and there is even greater reason to validate judges than witnesses. The Ran and those who follow him disqualify judges related to the lender or borrower, but they also disqualify authentication witnesses related to them. Conversely, one who validates the witnesses certainly validates the judges. This is explicit in a responsum of the Rashba printed among the Ramban’s responsa, siman 112. He writes that authentication witnesses or authentication judges related to either lender or borrower would appear invalid because money is extracted through relatives. He then raises proofs from Rabbi Chiya’s teaching that witnesses do not sign a document unless they have read it, and from the first chapter of Gittin concerning before how many persons a get is delivered. His conclusion is that authentication judges and authentication witnesses are valid even though they are relatives of the husband or wife. The wording in the printed Rashba responsa, siman 969, is somewhat corrupt, but his intent is clear from the version printed among the Ramban’s responsa. The same is explicit in the Rashba’s novellae at the beginning of Gittin, in the discussion beginning “Before how many does he deliver it,” folio 7b. The Rivash’s statement in siman 382, citing the Rashba as disqualifying authentication witnesses related to the lender or borrower, requires study. Perhaps the concluding words of the Rashba were absent from his copy. In any event, the Rama’s and Ir Shushan’s rulings require study.
