From the witnesses' possession, etc. If the deed is already in the litigant's possession, it is valid and he need not prove it had been delivered before the kinship arose. We say that since it is found there now, it had already been there. Otherwise this case would be identical to one where a robber had signed and later became disqualified, and there would be no difference between a robber and a son-in-law. It also appears that if the deed emerges from the possession of the second signatory who is not related, it is valid. Since a one-witness document can have legal force, the related witness's prior testimony can be treated as judicially fixed before the kinship arose, even though the second witness's testimony has not yet been received. See Tumim. Beit Yosef built his ruling on a certain reading of Rif, that signed testimony is not treated as judicially received until the deed leaves the witnesses' possession. I demonstrate that this is not the correct reading of Rif. Therefore, just as a deed held by the witness remains valid after that witness dies according to Rif, the same should be true when he later becomes a relative.
