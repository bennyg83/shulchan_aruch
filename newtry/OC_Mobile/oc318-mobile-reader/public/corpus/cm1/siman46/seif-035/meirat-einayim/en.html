And we make a cut, he cannot testify. And if he had known him in Klanin until he was not made, he would not sign what he had done
Even if he did not see him until after he became his son-in-law. The reason is that the witness is not suspected of forging a signature. His only disqualification is kinship. When the original witnesses signed, it was as though their testimony had already been examined in beit din at that time, and then he was not yet a son-in-law. Therefore one may rely on their signatures even after the relationship later arose.
But if it emerges from the witnesses' own possession, no. This follows Rif, who holds that when a document remains in the possession of its witnesses, we do not treat it for any purpose as though the testimony had already been examined in beit din. See Darkei Moshe, seif 8.
