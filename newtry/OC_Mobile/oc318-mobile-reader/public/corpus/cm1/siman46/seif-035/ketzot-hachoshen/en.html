From the possession of the witnesses. Beit Yosef writes that according to Rif, a document remaining under the witnesses' control is not treated as though the testimony had already been examined in beit din. If they become relatives before delivery, the document is therefore invalid. Rif explains that while the paper remains under their control they could suppress it, so their testimony has not yet become fixed. Once it leaves their hands, the signed testimony becomes like testimony already examined. With this, This can resolve Tosafot's difficulty in Gittin concerning the messenger who says he is the second signed witness. The messenger's own signature still requires full validation because the document is under his control and his own testimony has not yet become fixed. The other witness's signature, however, is already beyond that other witness's control and can benefit from the rabbinic messenger rule. This creates the mixed problem of one part validated through ordinary document validation and one through the special messenger enactment. Nevertheless, this approach is difficult against Ramban, Raavad, and Ran, who explain Rif differently. They hold that ordinary documents do not lose their status merely because they remain physically with the witnesses if they were intended for delivery. The special case in Yevamot concerns a writing that was never intended to function as a transferable document at all, but merely as the witnesses' private memorandum. Only such a record is considered "under the witnesses" in the disqualifying sense. This distinction is also necessary to preserve ordinary commercial practice, where scribes and witnesses often hold signed agreements for some time before handing them over. If mere delay under the witnesses' control invalidated them once memory faded or status changed, countless documents would fail. Therefore Beit Yosef's broad reading of Rif requires further study. The better rule is that a true completed document intended for delivery has documentary status even while temporarily entrusted with its signers, whereas a private memorandum retained by them for recollection does not.
