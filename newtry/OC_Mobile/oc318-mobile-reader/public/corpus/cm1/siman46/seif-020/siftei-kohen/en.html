This is the view of the Ran, Rivash, and those who follow them, cited above in seif 19, who disqualify authentication witnesses related to the borrower or lender. The judges must therefore inspect the document sufficiently to identify the parties. The same purpose is served if the authentication states the names of the witnesses who testified to the signatures. The Ritva writes in the chapter Shenei Dayanei Gezerot: “When we say that judges may sign a document even though they did not read it, this means that they did not examine it to learn its substance. They must, however, know the names of the litigants so that they do not authenticate it through relatives of the parties. Alternatively, they need not read it at all, provided that the authentication expressly records the names of those who testified concerning the document witnesses’ handwriting.” The Beit Yosef cites this. The Nimukei Yosef similarly writes at the end of Yesh Nochalin that the Gemara concludes that one beit din does not scrutinize the acts of another. The Ramah therefore wrote that when judges authenticate a document they must expressly record the names of those who testified to the signatures, or read the names of the litigants. Otherwise they might authenticate it through relatives, and the later beit din would not investigate the first beit din but would collect with the document. This explains the statement in Shenei Dayanei Gezerot that judges may sign a document without reading it, meaning without reading all of it, provided they read the parties’ names, or without reading any of it if they expressly identify the authentication witnesses. The “some say” recorded by the Mechaber, that the judges need not read the document at all, is the Rashba’s view in the responsum printed among the Ramban’s responsa, siman 112. His reason is explicit there: authentication witnesses and judges related to the lender or borrower are valid. This is the validating opinion in seif 19. According to that opinion there is no need to read the document or to record the witnesses’ names. The Rama in his gloss and the Ir Shushan confused these two positions, as will be explained.
Provided that they write, and so forth. The implication of the Rama and the Ir Shushan that according to the latter opinion they must record the names is incorrect. Likewise, their implication that according to the first opinion recording the names is ineffective is incorrect. According to the first opinion, just as it suffices for the judges to see who the lender and borrower are, it likewise suffices for them to record who the authentication witnesses are. According to the latter opinion, they need neither read the document nor record the authentication witnesses’ names, as I wrote nearby. The Rama and Ir Shushan combined the two positions incorrectly. They should not have written here, “provided that they record who the authentication witnesses are.” Rather, this should have been stated as part of the first opinion, after the Mechaber’s words, “Nevertheless, they must see,” as follows: “Or they may record who the authentication witnesses are.” So it appears to me.
