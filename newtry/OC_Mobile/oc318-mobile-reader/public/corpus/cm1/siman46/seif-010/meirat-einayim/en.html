Rather, they testify to the money in the document. For this reason the witnesses need not each testify to the other's signature, as the Tur and Mechaber write in seif 7. Rambam holds that just as this principle produces a leniency, it also produces a stringency. If the witnesses have forgotten the loan and their signatures are not authenticated from another source, then just as we trust them when they say these are their signatures, we also trust them when they say that they do not remember anything about the loan. Therefore, even if each also testifies to the other's signature, their testimony is of no effect.
And they are like deaf-mutes. Meaning, the signed witnesses, with respect to the testimony in this document, are treated like a deaf-mute who neither hears nor speaks, who is disqualified from testimony according to all opinions, as explained in siman 28 regarding a mute and in siman 35 regarding one who cannot hear. Maharik quotes Rambam with the singular wording, 'it is like a deaf-mute,' which would refer to the document itself, meaning that the document is regarded as inert like earthenware. The Mechaber writes similarly below in siman 51, seif 3, likewise adopting Rambam's wording. At the end of siman 47 he writes, 'the document is like a deaf-mute.' Even the plural 'they are like deaf-mutes' can be explained in this figurative sense, meaning that their signatures in the document are as lifeless as earthenware. See Perishah.
And we disregard their words, etc. Rambam writes that we are concerned they are retracting and seeking to invalidate the document. It is as though they said, 'We were minors,' which they are not believed to say once the signatures are authenticated independently.
Since they testify that this is their handwriting, the document is authenticated, etc. This view holds that their testimony is no worse than that of ordinary witnesses who recognize the signatures. Thus if each testifies also to his fellow's signature, or a third witness joins them and testifies to both signatures, the authentication is sufficient, as the conclusion states.
We require that they remember the testimony only with one witness, etc. It appears that the meaning is as follows. Where only one witness is signed on the writing, it does not have the legal status of a formal document that can simply be sent to beit din in order to impose an oath or to combine with another oral witness to create monetary liability. Rather, the defendant can swear that the event never occurred, or that he paid, and is exempt. Therefore the signed witness must remember the testimony from the writing and come to beit din to testify orally. The Mechaber and Rama rule in siman 28, seif 11, that a witness ordinarily may not testify orally from his own writing, even if he is not mute, unless two witnesses signed a formal document, where this was allowed for the needs of society. A single signed witness does not create such a formal document. This is what chapter 2 of Ketubot teaches: 'A person may write his testimony in a document and testify to it after many years, provided he remembers it,' and according to one explanation, even if he remembers it through the document, as Tosafot explain there. The phrase 'or with two witnesses for purposes of authenticating the document' means that where two witnesses are signed but neither recognizes the other's signature, they must also remember the substance of the document. Then we say that both testify to the money in the document, as the Tur writes in this siman and Tosafot likewise state.
But two witnesses signed on a document, etc., and if the document was authenticated, etc. Meaning, if it was properly authenticated, with two witnesses testifying to each signature, they need not remember the event, and the document is effective even if it was not originally written in the ideal manner, as the conclusion states. Mahari Weil writes similarly in the name of Hagahot Maimoniyot citing Maharam, distinguishing between one signed witness and two for this purpose. See Mahari Weil, siman 94. Understand this carefully. See also Rama in Darkei Moshe at the beginning of siman 39 and his gloss to Shulchan Aruch there, seif 3.
