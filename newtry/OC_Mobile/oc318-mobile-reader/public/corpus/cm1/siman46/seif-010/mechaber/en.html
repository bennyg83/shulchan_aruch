If a signatory comes to beit din, recognizes his handwriting with certainty, but has no recollection whatsoever of the underlying testimony or of any loan between the parties, he may not identify the signature in beit din. In identifying his handwriting he is effectively testifying to the debt recorded in the document; the handwriting serves only to remind him. If he does not remember, he may not testify. Accordingly, if a document is produced and the signatories say, 'This is our handwriting, but we never knew this testimony and do not remember any loan or sale,' the document is not authenticated. They are like deaf persons until they remember the testimony. If their signatures can be authenticated independently, or other witnesses identify them, the document is authenticated and their statement that they do not remember is disregarded, because authentication does not depend on them. For this reason, documents are ordinarily authenticated without requiring the signatories to appear and state whether they remember. These are Rambam's words. Others disagree and say that even if they do not remember the loan or ever signing this document, their identification of their handwriting authenticates it. Under that view, when the signatories themselves authenticate their signatures but do not remember the loan or signing, two witnesses are needed for each signature: each signatory must identify both his own and his fellow's signature, or a third witness must join them and identify both. {Rama: Recollection of the testimony is required only for one witness, or for two witnesses authenticating a document. But where two witnesses are signed on a document, even if they remember nothing and the document was not drafted with all the formal laws of a document, their signatures still constitute witness testimony. Once authenticated, the document governs all that is written in it. Even if it lacks the status of a complete legal instrument, it is a document at least for purposes of avoiding testimony from writing, as explained above in siman 28, seif 12 (Responsa of the Rashba, siman 1209; see also siman 111).
