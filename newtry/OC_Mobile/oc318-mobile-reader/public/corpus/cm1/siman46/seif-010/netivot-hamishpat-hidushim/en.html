They are like deaf people. Even if one additional witness identifies their handwriting, it does not help, because the original witnesses have a migo that they could have remained silent and are therefore believed to say they do not remember.
We disregard them. They do not have power to invalidate the document when their handwriting is established from another source, just as when they say, "We were minors." See Beurim for the distinction between witnesses who are present and say they do not remember, and witnesses who died or became deaf.
Except with one witness. The Sema explains that there is no legal document status at all until he gives oral testimony in beit din. The Shach challenges this. See Beurim: ordinarily we presume he remembers and the document is valid, and if he dies it remains valid. Only when he is before us and says he does not remember is it invalid. But if it was once validated in beit din, it is valid, because with one witness the testimony is not treated as investigated in beit din from the time of signing, but only when the document comes before beit din. With two witnesses, by contrast, it is treated as examined from the time of signing.
