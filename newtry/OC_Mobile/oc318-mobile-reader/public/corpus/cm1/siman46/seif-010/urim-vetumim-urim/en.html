He is forbidden to testify, etc. Even together with another witness who testifies regarding his handwriting. Since he could have remained silent, he has a migo and is believed to say that he does not remember, and according to Rambam the document is then invalid. See Tumim.
Only regarding the money stated in the document, etc. For this reason the Sages said that two witnesses are not required for each handwriting, because the signing witnesses testify regarding the money stated in the document. Just as this principle was applied leniently, it is applied stringently as well, Sema. This seems to imply that according to Rebbi, who holds that they testify regarding their handwriting, they need not remember the underlying matter. See Tumim, where I wrote that this does not appear correct, because they still have the same migo. Rambam wrote the point only for additional clarity.
But we never knew, etc. See Tumim. I concluded that beit din should initially ask the witnesses whether they remember what is written in the document. If, however, they happened to leave before beit din asked them, we nevertheless presume that they remembered, and the validation is not invalidated.
They are like deaf persons. According to this view, even if one additional witness testifies regarding the handwriting, it is ineffective, because the signing witness had a migo to remain silent and is therefore believed to say that he does not remember, Shach. Responsa Darchei Noam, siman 5, writes that he need not remember every detail mentioned in the document, only the essential substance of the transaction.
We disregard, etc. They do not have the power to invalidate the document once their handwriting is independently established, just as if they said, "We were minors," Sema, and so writes Rambam. One may ask: if the witnesses died or became mentally incapable, when we know they cannot remember, why is the document still valid? See above in siman 28, where I wrote at length that Rambam regards this as an enactment of the Sages. Otherwise all written loans would become impossible, because who would lend on a document if the witnesses might die or become incapable the next day and the document would be void? See there.
Only with one witness. Sema explains that a document signed by only one witness has no documentary status at all until that witness testifies orally before beit din, because with one witness the rule is that testimony must come from his mouth and not from his writing. See Tumim, where I established that this is not correct, as is clear below in siman 51 at length. Ordinarily we presume that the witness remembers and his testimony is valid in every respect, even if he has died and his handwriting is independently recognized. But if he comes before beit din and says that he does not remember, then a single witness is believed even if his handwriting is independently established, and according to the first opinion the document is invalid. See what I wrote below in siman 51. Shach challenged this, and the practical law requires study.
