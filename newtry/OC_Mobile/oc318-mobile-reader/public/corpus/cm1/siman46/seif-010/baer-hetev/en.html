One. Meaning, it does not have the legal status of a document so as to impose an oath on its basis or to obligate payment if one additional oral witness joins with it. Rather, the defendant may swear that the matter never occurred, or that he repaid, and he is exempt, unless the signed witness appears before the beit din and testifies orally, as the Mechaber states. The Rama in siman 28, seif 11, writes that testimony may not be given through writing even by one who is not mute, except where two witnesses are signed on a document, because of the needs of orderly society, and this is not such a document. So writes the Sema. The Shach, however, writes that it is not persuasive to distinguish between one witness and two witnesses, for we rule in siman 51 that one witness in a document is effective just as two witnesses are. This appears clearly correct to me. The Rama's statement here, made without qualification, that with one witness the witness must remember his testimony, requires analysis.
Above. The Shach writes: See there, where I explained that there is no dispute. The Rashba also agrees that when a document was not written in its proper formal manner it may nevertheless be entirely valid. Rather, if it was written without the borrower's knowledge it is wholly invalid, because it was not authorized to be written and does not constitute testimony at all; the witnesses must come and testify orally. Mahari Weil likewise holds this view, and it is the primary ruling. The Rama's words here are not well founded. Examine carefully.
