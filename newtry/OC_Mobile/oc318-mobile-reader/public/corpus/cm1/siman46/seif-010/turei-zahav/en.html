(In the Tur, seif 24: Only in a case such as this, etc.) The Tur teaches that one should not mistakenly reason as follows: since we suspect that a signature was forged by copying other signatures, whenever two documents emerge from one person’s possession, both signed by the same pair, and one is authenticated, perhaps he forged the second from the first, so even visual recognition of the signatures should not help. The Tur therefore teaches that forgery is suspected only when authentication depends on comparing the scripts. If the signatures are independently recognizable, so that anyone familiar with them identifies them by visual recognition without comparison, we do not suspect that they were forged.
