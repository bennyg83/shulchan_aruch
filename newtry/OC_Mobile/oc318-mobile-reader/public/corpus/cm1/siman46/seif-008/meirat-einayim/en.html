Even if they did not specify by which method, etc. Rambam and the Tur conclude: 'They need not state by what method it was authenticated, whether the signers themselves testified to their signatures or others testified concerning them, because we do not suspect the beit din of error. The custom, however, is to write,' etc. Meaning, we do not suspect that the judges erred regarding the differences between methods of authentication. If the original witnesses themselves testify to their signatures, each need not testify to the other's signature. If other witnesses testify to the signatures, each must testify also to the other's signature, or a third witness must join them, as the Tur and Mechaber write in seif 9.
And what it says, 'the custom is to write the method,' etc. This too appears in Rambam and the Tur. It seems to me that although we do not suspect a beit din of error, stating the method may enable the borrower to discover that the authentication was in fact defective, for example by proving that the authentication witnesses were disqualified or because of some other defect. This differs from where the original document witnesses themselves testified to their own signatures.
