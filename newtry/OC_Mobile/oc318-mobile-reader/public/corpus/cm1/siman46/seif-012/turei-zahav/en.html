(See there: Just as there is reason to suspect, etc.) The Ramah’s words are clear as the Tur understood them. Although the Beit Yosef reads them as concern that the comparison documents themselves are forged, the Tur rejected that reading because the validity of two field documents rests on the fact that their holders consumed the produce in the owners’ presence. There is no difference whether two different people possess two fields or one person possesses both. Rather, the Ramah means that just as a single field document in one person’s possession may have been seen and copied by the forger, so too two field documents in one person’s possession may have been used to forge the third. The Gemara’s statement that one cannot coordinate so extensively means that ordinary signatures vary somewhat from occasion to occasion. If the questioned writing resembles both authentic signatures, including their slight variations, this is the strongest authentication and therefore requires two documents. The Gemara asks why two documents in the same person’s possession are different, since the forger may have seen both and fashioned a third from them. It answers that he cannot coordinate so much when the two exemplars are not together in one person’s possession. Even if the first document had left his possession before the second arrived, we remain concerned if both were at some time available to him. The Tur, however, does not suspect that he went elsewhere to inspect and forge from documents not readily available. Its proof is that one authenticated document may be used when it is not in his possession. The printed reading “even if it emerges from his possession” is erroneous; old editions omit it. The correct sense is “if it does not emerge from his possession,” as explained.
