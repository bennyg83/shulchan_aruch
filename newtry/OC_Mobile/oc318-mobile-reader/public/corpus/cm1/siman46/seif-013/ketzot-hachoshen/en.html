Witnesses to validate. Nimukei Yosef writes that if the surviving signed witness has already testified first concerning his own signature, additional testimony regarding that signature adds nothing, because his own testimony already has the force of two as to his signature. The Shach therefore says that if he first writes his signature on a shard for comparison, later testimony about it should likewise not help. In my view this is not so. If he formally testifies concerning himself, then further testimony about the same signature can indeed become superfluous. But if he merely writes his signature before beit din so that the judges themselves can compare and recognize it, the judges' own recognition is not a redundant later witness. Their sensory recognition directly supplies the needed validation. Therefore writing before beit din can help where the judges themselves recognize the signature from that sample. What does not help is writing before other witnesses and then having those witnesses testify to the comparison, because once his own act is the foundation, the later witnesses add only derivative testimony. Thus the correct distinction is whether beit din itself acquires the recognition.
