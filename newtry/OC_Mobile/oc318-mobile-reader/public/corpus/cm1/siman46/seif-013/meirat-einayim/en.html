Even on earthenware. Although an ordinary legal document is not written on earthenware, as the Tur and Mechaber write at the beginning of siman 42, because writing on it can be altered, nevertheless for purposes of immediately authenticating this signature one may sign even on earthenware. The phrase 'even on earthenware' implies all the more so on parchment. Although the Gemara says one should not sign on a blank sheet lest an unscrupulous person find it and write a promissory note above the signature, that refers to signing in the middle of a sheet. He may sign at the very top of the parchment, where no document can be written before the signature. Writing after the signature is ineffective because it is later writing, as the Tur and Mechaber explain below at the beginning of siman 69.
All the more so if there were witnesses, etc. In such a case the surviving witness need not sign on earthenware, because other witnesses have already testified to his signature.
