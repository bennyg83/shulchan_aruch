The witnesses to the validation must not be relatives of the judges of validation. Sema explains that if those witnesses were later disproved through hazamah, the judges, being their relatives, could not accept the testimony establishing the hazamah. This reason applies where the validating witnesses testify that they saw the original witnesses sign in their presence on a particular day, for hazamah can then apply. But if they merely say that they personally recognize the signatures as the witnesses' handwriting, hazamah cannot operate. We must therefore say that the rule was made without distinction. Ran gives another reason: Scripture decreed that relatives may not testify in a matter affecting others. What difference does it make whether there are two related witnesses or witnesses related to the judges? In either case, another person is made liable through the testimony of relatives. Shach gives the same explanation.
Some validate. This is Rashba. He follows the first reason cited by Sema, that in ordinary monetary law witnesses may not be related to the judges because of the possibility of hazamah. With document validation this concern is uncommon, because validation is ordinarily based on personal recognition of the witnesses' handwriting, where hazamah cannot apply. It appears from his words that even where the validating witnesses happen to testify that they saw the signatures being made before them, the relationship is still permitted, because the disqualifying circumstance is uncommon and the Sages did not impose a decree for it. Sema writes the same.
