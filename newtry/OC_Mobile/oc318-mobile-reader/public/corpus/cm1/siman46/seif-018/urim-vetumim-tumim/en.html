And the authentication witnesses should not be relatives, etc. This is puzzling. Rama already stated explicitly in seif 12 that authentication witnesses may not be relatives of one another, and that rule comes directly from the Gemara. Perhaps Rama adds a different case here. Suppose three witnesses testify to authenticate the signatures, and one of the three is related to one of the others. One might have thought that because document authentication is merely a disclosure of handwriting and is rabbinic, we could disregard the related witness and retain the two remaining valid witnesses. Rama therefore teaches that authentication follows the ordinary law of testimony: when a group of witnesses joins in one testimony and one is found related or disqualified, the entire testimony can be invalidated. Thus the whole authentication fails. The matter still requires study.
