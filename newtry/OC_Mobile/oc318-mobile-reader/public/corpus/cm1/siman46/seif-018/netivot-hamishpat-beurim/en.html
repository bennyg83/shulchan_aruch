The authentication witnesses are relatives. Ketzot HaChoshen asks from the rule that a witness who signed and later became the obligor's son-in-law cannot himself testify. There is no difficulty. In that case the original witness is testifying not merely to handwriting but ultimately to the one hundred written in the deed. Even if he phrases his statement as authentication of his own signature, his testimony also confirms the debt itself. If he is now a relative to a party, part of his testimony is invalid, and partial invalidity defeats the whole. Here, by contrast, outside authentication witnesses testify only to the judge's signature or other handwriting, and their own kinship can be evaluated independently. The suggestion that the relative witness is simply treated as removed does not help in the first case, because the remaining authentication is incomplete. Likewise, relatives to each other certainly cannot form the required pair of authentication witnesses. Several textual corrections are needed in the Shach in this area, as noted.
