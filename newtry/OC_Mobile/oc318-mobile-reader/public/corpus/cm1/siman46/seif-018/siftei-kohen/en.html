That the authentication witnesses not be relatives. The Sema explains that testimony capable of being refuted as false witnesses is required, and these judges would not accept testimony of refutation against their relatives. Some authorities indeed give this reason. The Ran, at the end of the second chapter of Ketubot, rejects it and proves from the end of the chapter Zeh Borer that we do not follow the Yerushalmi, which invalidates related witnesses because of the law of refutation. Rather, the disqualification is a Scriptural decree derived from “Fathers shall not be put to death through sons.” Whenever the matter is concluded through relatives, whether both are witnesses or one is a witness and the other a judge, the two situations are equivalent and they are disqualified. He therefore disputes the Rashba, who permitted authentication witnesses to be relatives of the authentication judges on the ground that refutation is uncommon and the Sages did not take it into account in rabbinic authentication of documents.
