Scratches. Meaning that blank space remains visibly between the scratch marks, but the entire space should not be filled with ink, because that could raise concern of forgery. See the Sema. The Shach writes that this is a strained interpretation; rather, "blotting" means even if the entire area is filled with ink. See there.
For beit din. The Sema writes that this concern applies only to witnesses, as explained in siman 45, seif 6. It does not apply to judges signing a validation because we do not suspect that the holder erased wording unfavorable to him that had been written after the witnesses' signatures; people do not ordinarily write obligations against the document holder after the witnesses have signed. The Shach finds this explanation astonishing, because the discussion concerns scratch marks from which it is evident that no substantive wording was written there. Perhaps there is a textual error in the Sema's words. The Taz likewise writes that Rashbam explains simply that beit din does not ordinarily sign over such a feature, and questions why the Sema introduced such a remote explanation.
