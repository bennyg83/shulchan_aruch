Ink scratches. But not a complete erasure, lest there originally have been a document and witness signatures in the last line, which he erased. See Tumim, where I discussed this at length. If the validation explicitly states how many lines the document contained, there is no concern. Shach nevertheless writes that one must still suspect that a clause to the holder's detriment was written there and erased. See Tumim, where I wrote that this does not appear correct, because the holder could simply have cut it off. If the borrower was careless and thereby lost his protection, why should we be more concerned for him, when the holder could have cut it off before bringing the document to beit din for validation?
We are not concerned, etc. If there is a space of two lines between the text of the validation and the judges' signatures and that area was filled with ink scratches, Shach holds that the validation is invalid. See Tumim for what I wrote.
Some say we are concerned. This view holds that Rif and Rosh understand R' Yochanan as disagreeing and holding that even beit din may sign over an inked area. Shach and Sema in Derishah rule like the first opinion, and that is unquestionably correct. See Tumim, seif katan 34, where I reconciled the dissenting opinion.
