Disqualified witnesses, meaning because of kinship. See Baer Heitev in the name of the Sema, through his explanation that the loan itself was genuine. In Tosafos Rabbi Akiva Eiger to Mishnah, chapter 2 of Kesubos, entry 19, he writes: According to this, the case is only where they say that the loan was genuine and merely seek to disqualify the document by saying that they were relatives. Even as oral testimony it would be ineffective, because their beginning was in disqualification. The Taz, however, writes that they relied on the assumption that their kinship would be publicly known and no one would adjudicate on the document. According to that explanation, perhaps they are believed even if they say, 'We were relatives and the loan itself was false.' In my opinion, however, this is contradicted by Tosafos in our sugya, s.v. chazakah. End of quote. See there further.
They are believed. See Baer Heitev, through his statement that they are believed even if their explanation is strained. See Responsa Rabbi Moshe Rothenburg, siman 8, who writes that this applies specifically to witnesses, since the Torah grants credibility to witnesses as long as they do not retract their original testimony. If they can explain their first words in a manner that does not contradict them, the explanation is full testimony. Litigants themselves, however, certainly cannot reinterpret an admission through remote and strained explanations. See there.
Or there are witnesses that this is their handwriting. See Netzach Yisrael regarding what he writes on this matter.
They are not believed. The Shach writes that other witnesses are believed, and Rabbeinu Yeruham writes that the Geonim likewise ruled. The Beit Yosef cites this in Choshen Mishpat 36. This implies that even regarding a condition, where the signatories themselves are not believed once their signatures are independently authenticated, other witnesses are nevertheless believed. The Beit Yosef explicitly writes this at the end of siman 29 concerning Responsa Rashba about one who mortgaged a vineyard. See there. Tumim concludes that this applies only where the second set testifies generally that there was a condition. We then say that this is not a contradiction of the witnesses to the document, because they may not have known of the condition while the second set did. If, however, the second set testifies that the condition was made in the presence of the witnesses to the document, this is a contradiction of two against two. See there. Shaar Mishpat reaches the same conclusion and writes that this is also clear from the Beit Yosef in Even HaEzer, end of siman 145, in the name of Responsa Rashba, and Responsa Lechem Rav, siman 81, likewise rules this way. This is the primary law. See there.
Except to say, 'A protest was delivered before us.' See Shaar Mishpat regarding whether one witness signed on the document who says that a protest was delivered before him alone is also effective, since he thereby uproots his own testimony and there remains only one witness to the sale, leaving one witness against one. He discusses the matter at length and concludes that the practical law requires further study. See there.
