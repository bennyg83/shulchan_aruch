If two signatories died, their handwriting cannot be authenticated elsewhere, and two witnesses say, 'This is their handwriting, but they were minors or disqualified,' those witnesses are believed and the document is torn up, unless its holder claims that he had been granted time to authenticate it. If other witnesses identify the handwriting, or it can be authenticated from another document that was challenged and upheld in beit din, it is not torn up. Some say a prior challenge is unnecessary; authentication in beit din alone suffices. {Rama: This applies when the signatories have died. If the handwriting cannot be authenticated elsewhere and the signatories say, within the brief period for correcting speech, 'We signed under mortal coercion,' 'We were minors,' 'We were related but are no longer related,' 'A prior declaration nullifying the transaction was made before us,' or, 'The matter was conditional and we did not see the condition fulfilled,' they are believed. Even if one says there was no condition and the other says there was an unfulfilled condition, the latter is believed and only one witness remains. If the document states that the witnesses attest there was no reservation, they cannot say that a condition existed, because a witness may not retract. They may explain, however, that the statement of no reservation referred only to the substance of the sale and was not intended to negate the condition. They may likewise explain any wording without contradicting it. If they say they were coerced financially, that the document was a document of trust, or that they were disqualified through transgression, they are not believed, even if they say they later repented. Although they cannot invalidate the document, they are believed against themselves and must reimburse the borrower for the loss their signatures caused. If they say they did not know when signing that it was a document of trust and learned this only later, they are believed. If their handwriting is independently authenticated, they cannot invalidate the document on any ground except by saying that a prior declaration was made before them. See also simanim 40 and 50.}
