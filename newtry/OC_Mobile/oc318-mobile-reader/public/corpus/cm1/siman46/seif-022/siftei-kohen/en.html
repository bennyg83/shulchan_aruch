The authentication judges must be unrelated, and so forth. Just as the judges adjudicating the loan must be unrelated to the document witnesses, because although the witnesses’ role ended when they signed, the loan judges must rule upon the money through their signatures and money would thus be extracted through relatives, so too it is proper to require the loan judges to be unrelated to the authentication judges, because they must rule on the basis of their authentication. One might distinguish that the document witnesses testify to the loan itself, whereas the authentication judges address only the signatures, but this does not appear sufficient, because the loan judges still adjudicate through their relatives. One might then ask that the authentication witnesses should likewise have to be unrelated to the loan judges, requiring the authentication to identify them so the loan judges can avoid relatives. This is not the practice, for authentications are written without their names. The answer is that authentication witnesses are unlike authentication judges. Once the document has been authenticated by judges, the loan judges decide through the authentication judges, not through the authentication witnesses. Even if the authentication witnesses are relatives of the loan judges, when the latter collect upon the document the witnesses do not join with them, because only the authentication judges are needed. Although the relatives caused the eventual collection, mere causation without a combination of relatives is irrelevant, as taught regarding three brothers and one outsider, who form three testimonies but one for refutation. It follows that authentication witnesses may be related to the document witnesses and to the loan judges once the document has already been authenticated through others. Care must nevertheless be taken that authentication witnesses are not related to the authentication judges, and that authentication judges are not related to the loan judges. These are the Ran’s words.
