Each one testifies, etc. The reason the Mechaber wrote above in seif 14 that one judge who testifies regarding his own handwriting does not suffice unless another person testifies with him was only to teach the stronger point that even when another joins the judge, the judge still cannot combine with the signing witness, Sema and Shach. See Tumim, where I concluded, as appears from Beit Yosef, that a distinction should be made. One judge who testifies regarding his own handwriting requires another witness to join him. But where two judges each testify regarding their own handwriting, no additional witness is required, because it is as though each one testifies, "This is my handwriting and this is the handwriting of my colleague." See there for the reason. According to Sema and Shach, one must instead distinguish between a judge who remembers the substance of the validation and one who has forgotten the entire matter and knows only that this is his handwriting. See Tumim.
The document is validated. We do not suspect that the signature of the third judge was forged. Even if the two signatures are genuine, without the third there would be no valid beit din validation, since validation requires three. We do not ordinarily suspect forgery, and therefore we presume that the validation is genuine even if it does not state that the judges sat as a panel of three, Ran and Ritva. See there.
