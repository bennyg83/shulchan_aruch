The document is authenticated. We do not say that each judge is testifying concerning his own handwriting, such that each would also have to recognize the signature of his fellow judge, or that another witness would have to join him in testifying to their signatures. Rather, each judge testifies to the authentication of the document. Likewise, when two witnesses testify to the signatures of two judges who signed the authentication, the document is authenticated if three judges are signed upon it, even though they do not recognize the third judge's signature. We do not suspect that there were only two judges and that the third signature was forged, even where the authentication does not state, “We sat as a court of three,” and so forth. The Ran and Ritva write this, see there, and the same appears in Baal HaIttur, folio 35a.
