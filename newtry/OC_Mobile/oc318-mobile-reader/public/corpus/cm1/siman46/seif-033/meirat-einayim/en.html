Perhaps the authentication was distant, etc. In Perishah and Derishah I demonstrated that Rambam and the Mechaber had to state that perhaps the authentication was separated by a gap. They did not simply invalidate because perhaps another document and witnesses had originally been written where the present erased document now appears, later erased, and replaced with a different document and witnesses while leaving the first authentication in place. If the original witnesses had not also been erased, the new document would obviously be invalid, as explained at the end of siman 45:22. From the form of the ruling we therefore see that Rambam and the Mechaber speak even of a document written on ordinary paper rather than parchment, where one cannot erase the original writing and write a new document without the alteration being visible. Even so, we are concerned that the original document may have been written above at some distance from the authentication, with the space between filled by scratches. Those scratches could then be erased easily and a new document written in their place. I also offered another explanation there.
