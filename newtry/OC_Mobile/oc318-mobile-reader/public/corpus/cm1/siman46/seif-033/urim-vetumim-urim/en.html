Lest the validation had been distant, etc. See Tumim. I concluded that if the validation was written on the back of the document or in the side margin, there is no concern that an area was first filled with scratches and later a valid document was written over it, provided that the validation states that this document was written over an erasure. There is then no concern. Certainly there is no concern if the validation itself states the names of the lender and borrower, the date, and the amount. Likewise, if the paper is of a type that does not permit writing, erasing, and writing again, the document is plainly valid. See Tumim.
Some validate. These are the authorities who, in the preceding seif, are concerned about leaving blank space between the validation and the judges' signatures and therefore require filling it with ink scratches. Once that is done, there is no further concern, provided it is one of the forms described in the preceding seif katan where there is no concern that he will erase and rewrite, such as when the lender's and borrower's names, date, and amount are stated, or one of the other methods I described. See Tumim for the reasoning of these authorities.
