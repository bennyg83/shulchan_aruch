[" A document, etc. The Tur writes this entire seif in the name of Rambam, and Beit Yosef wonders why, since it is an explicit Talmudic rule. Bach labors greatly to resolve the matter. It appears to me that there is an additional difficulty in Rambam himself: why does he need the concern that the authentication may have been far from the text? Even without any blank space, we should be concerned that the holder erased the entire document and wrote another in its place. Sema, seif katan 81, answers that Rambam and the Mechaber are discussing even a document written on paper rather than parchment, where one cannot erase a large area without leaving scraping marks. This does not appear correct. An unqualified rule concerning a document applies to every type. Moreover, Rambam immediately before this states that a document whose text and witnesses are written over an erasure is valid, because a surface erased once differs from one erased twice. He then immediately says that such a document may not be authenticated from the signatures of the judges who authenticated it, lest the authentication have been written at a distance from the document. The preceding law necessarily concerns parchment, since the entire document may be erased. Rather, the same difficulty exists in the Gemara itself. The Gemara asks that perhaps the holder cut off the upper portion, erased the ink blot, wrote whatever he wished, and obtained signatures; Rav then said that a document whose text and witnesses are on an erasure is valid. This works according to Rav Kahana, who states the teaching in Shmuel’s name, but what can be said according to Rav Tavyomi, who states it in Rav’s name? Tosafot ask: even according to Rav Kahana, how is the problem resolved? A person could erase any authenticated document and turn it into another document. Tosafot answer that this is less difficult because where writing originally existed, he cannot erase it without the letters remaining perceptible, whereas an erased ink blot is not noticeable. Although later the Gemara asks, ‘Perhaps he will erase again,’ despite the fact that writing had existed there, Tosafot say that this later question reflects the final conclusion that there is no distinction between an ink blot and writing. Lechem Mishneh, chapter 27 of Hilchot Malveh, explains that Rambam did not want to abandon the Gemara’s original concern, which was substantial even according to the questioner. This is forced, and the Mechaber’s language likewise does not support it, as I write below in seif katan 90. Rather, Rambam rejected Tosafot’s answer because it is strained, especially their statement that the later question, ‘Perhaps he will erase again,’ is based on the final conclusion. That question is not raised by the Gemara’s anonymous voice, but by Rav himself. The difficulty would therefore return against Rav, and likewise against Shmuel if Shmuel is the author of the ruling. Once he himself says, ‘Perhaps he will erase again,’ he plainly recognizes that we are concerned with erasing writing too, so why not suspect this in every document? Even apart from this, once a document whose text and witnesses are on an erasure is valid, we see that the remnants of the erased letters either can be fully removed or do not invalidate the document even if somewhat visible. The original difficulty therefore remains: why not suspect that every authenticated document was erased and converted into a new document whose text and witnesses are all written on the erased area? It therefore appears to me that the Gemara assumes we do not ordinarily suspect that the holder will erase both the document and its witnesses. If he did not erase the witnesses as well, the document would plainly be invalid, as explained above in siman 45, seif 22. The reason is that if he erased the witnesses too, the forgery might later be exposed by the judges who authenticated the original document, who could testify that they never authenticated signatures written over an erasure, but rather signatures on clean paper. He would then lose his document entirely and would no longer possess a document signed by witnesses. We are concerned only that he cut away the original body of the document above the authentication, while retaining that original signed document in his possession, so that if the forgery were later discovered he could still produce the genuine body of the document bearing the witnesses’ signatures. This explains Rambam’s precise wording, ‘and he cut the body of the document,’ rather than the Gemara’s phrase, ‘he cut above it.’ He means that he cut away the body of the document but retained that original body. Later, by contrast, the concern ‘perhaps he will erase again’ refers to erasing the document without erasing the witnesses. Accordingly, where it is known that the authentication was not written at a distance from the document, for example where the certification itself states, ‘We, the beit din, wrote this authentication immediately adjacent to the document,’ or similar language, the document is valid. This is why the Tur writes the law specifically in Rambam’s name. Semag, at the end of positive commandment 94, likewise writes in accordance with Rambam. This appears to me the primary ruling:"]
[" And some validate authenticating it, etc. Their reason is as Beit Yosef writes in the name of some poskim: even judges are presumed to sign only after ensuring that no suspicious ink blot or scraping stands before their authentication. Accordingly, scraping marks before an authentication would invalidate it, and therefore they certainly were not present when the judges signed. This itself proves that even Rambam is not concerned that the holder may have erased the entire document and written another in its place. For if that were a concern, how could the Mechaber infer that some authorities permit authentication from the judges’ signatures? Even if a beit din does not sign after an ink blot, perhaps the original document was perfectly valid and had no scraping marks, and the holder later erased the entire document and wrote another in its place. Rather, the rule must be as I wrote above in seif katan 88. Examine this carefully:"]
