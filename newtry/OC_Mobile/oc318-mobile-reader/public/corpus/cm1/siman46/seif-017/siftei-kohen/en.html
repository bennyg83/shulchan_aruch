He may combine with another competent witness. This means that he recognized the signature after reaching adulthood, and he is competent to join this witness who recognized it while still a minor. The two persons testifying may, however, both be relatives of the witnesses signed on the document. The Ran writes this, as does the Ritva in the name of several authorities, adding that this appears correct in law. The Sema writes the same in seif katan 45. This applies only to testimony concerning a relative’s signature as a document witness, because witnesses signed on a document are treated as though their testimony had already been examined by a beit din, and on a Torah level no authentication is needed. Concerning the borrower’s signature, however, it is obvious that a relative is not believed to impose liability upon him. The Sema writes this in siman 35, seif katan 60, and it is self evident.
Some say, and so forth. Above, siman 35, seif 4, the Mechaber likewise records this rule as “some say.” The Sema there, seif katan 10, wonders why he uses that formulation, since no authority disputes it. He overlooked the Mechaber’s own words in Bedek HaBayit here. Regarding the Rosh’s derivation from the Yerushalmi, the Mechaber writes that the other authorities omitted the Yerushalmi because the Babylonian Gemara did not make this distinction, indicating that they do not accept it, or because they explain the Yerushalmi differently. The Yerushalmi’s phrase, “provided that these are matters with which they are familiar,” may mean that the same applies to other persons who are familiar with such matters. According to the Rosh, it should instead have said, “because they are familiar with them.” It therefore means that the same rule applies to others, provided that the matters are familiar to them. I also saw that the Ritva in the second chapter of Ketubot explicitly disagrees. He writes that since the Mishnah mentions a brother, who does not possess either special basis for knowledge, the same applies to any unrelated person. The Mishnah mentions those persons only because they are ordinarily found with him and know his writing.
