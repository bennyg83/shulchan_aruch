They are not believed. The Shach writes that other witnesses are believed, and Rabbeinu Yeruham writes that the Geonim likewise ruled. Beit Yosef cites this in Choshen Mishpat 36. This implies that even regarding a condition, where the signatories themselves are not believed once their signatures are independently authenticated, other witnesses are nevertheless believed. The Beit Yosef explicitly writes this at the end of siman 29 concerning Responsa Rashba about one who mortgaged a vineyard. See there. Tumim concludes that this applies only where the second set of witnesses testifies generally that there was a condition. We then say that this does not contradict the witnesses to the document, because those witnesses may simply not have known of the condition, while the second set did. If, however, the second set testifies that the condition was made in the presence of the witnesses to the document, then this is a direct contradiction of two against two. See there. Shaar Mishpat reaches the same conclusion and writes that this is also clear from the Beit Yosef in Even HaEzer, end of siman 145, in the name of Responsa Rashba. Responsa Lechem Rav, siman 81, likewise rules this way, and this is the primary law. See there.
