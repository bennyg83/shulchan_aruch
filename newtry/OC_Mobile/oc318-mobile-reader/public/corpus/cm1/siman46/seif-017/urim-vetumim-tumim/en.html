Relatives of the document witnesses. Ran, at the end of the second chapter of Ketubot, cites Razah's explanation. Ordinarily relatives cannot testify together because the testimony must be capable of hazamah, and if one witness is a relative of the other, full hazamah of the witnessing unit can create improper punishment through the kinship structure. But regarding a document, Razah holds ordinary hazamah does not operate in the same manner. Therefore relatives can participate in authenticating the signatures of relatives. This raises a question. The Yerushalmi, cited by Rif in Sanhedrin, explains that relatives may not testify together for another person because witnesses are not punished as zomemim unless the entire witnessing group is hazemed. If one relative's punishment would thereby be caused through another relative, the structure is defective. If that is the entire reason, then in documents, where Razah says hazamah does not apply in the ordinary way, why should two brothers not be allowed to write and sign a document together? The same rationale Razah uses to permit relatives in authentication would seem to permit related document witnesses themselves. The necessary answer is that Chazal or the Torah excluded related witnesses from ordinary written testimony because otherwise people would confuse document testimony with oral testimony. If relatives were routinely permitted to sign documents, people would come to permit them as ordinary witnesses as well. Authentication is different. Authentication exists only in relation to documents and has no ordinary oral-testimony analogue. There is therefore less concern of confusion, and relatives can be accepted in that specialized procedural role. Still, the distinction is somewhat forced and requires study.
