Another. Meaning together with another person who saw the signatures after reaching adulthood, even if that second person is also related to the signatories. Sema.
His brother. All of this concerns testimony regarding the signature of a relative who was himself a witness. Since witnesses signed on a document are regarded as though their testimony had already been examined in beit din, the requirement of later authentication is only rabbinic. But regarding the borrower's own signature, it is obvious that a relative is not believed to authenticate it so as to impose liability on him. The Sema writes this in siman 35, and it is straightforward. Shach.
And some say. The Shach notes that in siman 35, seif 4, the Sema wondered why the Mechaber records this law as "some say" when no posek appears to disagree. The Sema apparently had not seen Rama's own discussion in Darkei Moshe on this siman, where he writes that the poskim omitted the Yerushalmi, from which this law is derived, because the Bavli makes no such distinction. This indicates that they either disagree with the law or interpret the Yerushalmi differently. The Shach adds that Ritva in Ketubot chapter 2 expressly disagrees. See there.
