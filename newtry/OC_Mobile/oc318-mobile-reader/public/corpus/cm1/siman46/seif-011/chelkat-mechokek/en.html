We are not concerned for the kol, because people commonly call bridal gifts "kiddushin." The Rama's words are unclear. If there was a full kol that a certain woman was betrothed and it was established before beit din in the manner explained at the beginning of this siman, why should we ignore it merely because people sometimes call bridal gifts kiddushin? Even if the report was entirely false, according to the view that an established kol is not canceled she would require a get because of the report itself. If there was no full kol, but only a report that a certain man sent bridal gifts and people mistakenly called those gifts kiddushin, this was already explained at the end of the preceding siman, based on Mahari's rulings, that we do not treat an ordinary report of bridal gifts as kiddushin. One could force an interpretation that after a time an explanatory report emerged that the original event was only bridal gifts and not kiddushin, and that this explanation undermines the first kol where beit din finds it credible. But that is not the natural meaning of the wording. Moreover, one who studies the Rosh's responsum will see that it does not prove this rule. There the report was that a woman who had merely been matched was an "arusah," and the Rosh explains that people commonly use the term "arusah" for a matched woman even before kiddushin. That is not a clear kol requiring a get. The Rama similarly writes in the previous siman that the terms bride and groom can be used of a merely matched couple. But if a definite kol says that a woman was betrothed to a certain man and the alleged witnesses then went overseas, even if it is later clarified that the event was only bridal gifts, according to the view that an established kol is not canceled she requires a get unless a public explanatory report likewise spread that the event was gifts and not kiddushin.
