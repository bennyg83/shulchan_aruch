(See there: Provided they emerge from another person’s possession.) This plainly applies also to the authenticated document mentioned above: it too must be in another person’s possession. If it were in the present holder’s own possession, we would suspect that he copied and forged the handwriting from it. According to the Rambam, cited by the Rama as a dissenting view, we do not suspect that one copied handwriting from other handwriting; the concern is only that he forged the entire document. Therefore an authenticated instrument may serve for comparison even from the holder’s own possession. But according to the Tur, who does suspect copied handwriting, the same concern applies even to an authenticated document. The Sema writes that a person is afraid to forge from a document authenticated by beit din, but I know no source for this distinction. The Tur’s phrase “because it is not readily available to him” implies that if it is available, forgery is suspected, certainly when it is actually in his possession.
