Ketzot HaChoshen resolves the doubt cited by Beit Yosef in the name of Tosafot regarding a wicked person whose wickedness does not involve monetary dishonesty, namely whether we are concerned that he may have forged the document. Ketzot suggests that only one whose wickedness involves theft or monetary dishonesty is suspected of lying for financial benefit. A wicked person whose transgression does not involve money is disqualified only by Scriptural decree, and should therefore have the same law as one who later became the witness's son in law, discussed in seif 35. He considers this obvious from Nimukei Yosef, who writes that a wicked person whose transgression does not involve monetary dishonesty and whose disqualification is only by Scriptural decree can be valid for certain testimony concerning a woman. Yoreh Deah, siman 127, Shach, seif katan 20, explains that even in matters of prohibition such a person is not believed to prohibit, and is believed to permit only because of the principle that a person does not sin without benefit. Relatives, by contrast, may be believed. This shows that every wicked person is suspected of falsehood. Even Nimukei Yosef agrees that for testimony concerning a woman he is not generally believed where there is concern that he may have set his eyes upon her, as explained in Yevamot and Even HaEzer, siman 17. Any person disqualified by Torah law is disqualified for such testimony in the circumstances stated there.
