It does not help, etc. Maharit, Even HaEzer part 1, siman 9, writes that although the witness-and-judge combination does not create full document authentication, they can still combine regarding an oral-loan claim. His proof is that we rule that testimony to a loan and testimony to an admission can combine even though each witness describes a different event. In my view, this works only according to Ritva's explanation. Ritva says the problem with a witness and a judge is not that only one witness exists regarding the second signature, because in rabbinic document authentication one judge's confirmation can suffice in that respect. The problem is only that one person testifies to one type of matter and the other to a different type. If so, Maharit can argue that for ordinary oral testimony, where loan and admission testimony combine despite describing different events, the witness and judge can also combine. But Tosafot and most other commentators explain the Gemara differently. The defect is that as to the second document witness there is only one person, namely the judge. There are not two witnesses establishing that signature. Under that explanation, the same defect exists even for an oral-loan claim. There are still not two witnesses on the necessary point. Since Mechaber below, seif 16, follows Rashi and Rosh rather than Ritva, Maharit's ruling should not be accepted as practical halachah.
