One may ask what practical purpose remains in the Gemara’s statement that later witnesses are not believed to invalidate the document, since collection can never result. It appears that if the borrower himself delivered the property before the later witnesses arrived, the Ramah concedes that the delivery is effective. Only a seizure by beit din is reversed, because the court says, “we raised him to possession and we remove him,” and certainly a unilateral seizure by the creditor is ineffective. Alternatively, the Ramah interprets “they are not believed” with respect to a receipt: later witnesses seek to disqualify the original witnesses, and we do not extract money on the strength of the later testimony. This explanation, cited by the Beit Yosef from the Hagahot Ashiri, is very sound.
