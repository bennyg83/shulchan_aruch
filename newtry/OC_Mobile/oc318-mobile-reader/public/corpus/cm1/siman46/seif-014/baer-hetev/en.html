Maharam of Rothenburg, however, writes that a witness and a judge may combine, for example where at a later time they recognize the signature of one of the witnesses and the signature of one of the judges; according to this, he writes, those who are relatives should not sign the authentication. The words of Maharam are primary, and this is the accepted practice: judges do not authenticate the signatures of witnesses who are their relatives. Nevertheless, if this occurred after the fact and the signatures of all three judges, or even of two judges, are recognized, that is sufficient. Shach.
The scribe. The Sema notes that the Beit Yosef writes that nevertheless one may collect from unencumbered property, and Rabbeinu Yerucham says likewise. The Shach objects: how can one collect even from unencumbered property merely because the scribe's handwriting is recognized? Who says that the scribe is a witness? The Gemara in the chapter HaMegarresh explicitly states that a scribe's writing together with one witness is entirely invalid. He discusses this at length and proves from the authorities against Rabbeinu Yerucham. The Taz also objects and writes that Rabbeinu Yerucham follows the Yerushalmi, whereas we do not rule that way. He further notes that a responsum of the Rama raises the same difficulty from HaMegarresh, and that the Rama's answer there is very puzzling.
