One witness signed in a document. The Shach asks from siman 51, where one signed witness can require an oath. As explained above, one witness in writing is not disqualified merely as testimony from writing, but his signature is not treated as though judicially examined from the moment of signing. Until the document is produced in beit din he can still retract, much as Rabbeinu Chananel allows written testimony to be withdrawn before it reaches the court. Once it is presented in beit din, he can no longer retract. The Shach also challenges Responsa Rama, who validated authentication of a receipt, saying that one rationale applies only to oral testimony and not to authentication of the receipt. The practical difference is important for purchaser rights because authentication also establishes the date written in the receipt. Oral witnesses may testify that payment occurred, but without authenticating the dated receipt they may not establish that payment preceded a later sale. The fact that a sold debt can sometimes later be waived does not remove all such differences, especially where the legal situation remains as it was at the time the receipt was written. There are therefore several practical distinctions between oral proof of payment and authentication of the receipt itself.
