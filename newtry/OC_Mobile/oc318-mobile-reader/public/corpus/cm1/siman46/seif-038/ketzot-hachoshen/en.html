One witness signed on the document. Rama, in responsum 110, upheld a receipt on several grounds. One was that a cantor who remembered the sudar kinyan could join as an oral witness with one signed witness. The Shach objects that this may prove the receipt only orally and not establish it as a document against purchasers, leaving concern that the wife could later sell her ketubah and wrongfully defeat buyers. In my view this objection is not decisive. If oral witnesses establish that the ketubah had in fact been paid before the woman sold it, then the purchaser acquired nothing. The need for publicity applies to liens, where the debtor still owns the underlying property and purchasers need notice. It does not create title in an object the seller no longer owns. A purchaser of a debt instrument is not protected by the market overt enactment in the ordinary way. The Talmudic concern about returning a found receipt even when the wife admits it is different. There we fear the receipt may have been written in Nisan but not delivered until Tishrei, while the ketubah was sold in between. If witnesses establish actual payment before the sale, no purchaser can collect merely because that payment lacked publicity. Therefore Rama's second ground is sound. The Shach's broader concern confuses loss of title with hidden liens.
