They are not believed. This is not because of the presumption that witnesses do not sign unless they know, for a migo can overcome such a presumption, as below in siman 82, where a borrower is believed to say that he was a minor through a migo that he could claim forgery. Rather, the reason is that the witnesses make themselves wicked. They should not have signed unless they had clarified that he was an adult, and they are not believed to render themselves wicked, just as with witnesses who claim they signed under monetary coercion, because a person is considered related to himself. The borrower, however, may be believed through a migo even though his claim renders the witnesses wicked. Therefore the witnesses are not believed even where their handwriting is not independently established.
