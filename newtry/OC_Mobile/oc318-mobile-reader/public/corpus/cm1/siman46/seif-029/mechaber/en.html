If three judges sat to authenticate a document and one of them died, the remaining two must write, "We were sitting as three, and one is no longer present," so that an observer will not say that a beit din of two authenticated it. Even if the document says "in beit din," an observer might think they mistakenly regarded two as a beit din. If the language otherwise indicates that there were three, this additional statement is unnecessary. Some say that even if they did not write that one is no longer present, the authentication is valid. In any event, according to all opinions, if it says both "beit din" and "we were sitting as three," nothing more is needed, and two of them may sign. Rama: If all three sign, it is unnecessary to write "we were sitting as three." Even if it says only, "This document was properly authenticated," that is sufficient.
