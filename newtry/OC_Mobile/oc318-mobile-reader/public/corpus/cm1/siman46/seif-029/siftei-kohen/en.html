[" And one of them died, etc. The same applies if he is alive but cannot presently be found to sign, as the Mechaber writes in the following seif, and as is implied by the poskim:"]
[" They must write. According to Rivash, siman 382, and the Rama above in seif 7, who hold that testimony concerning authentication may later be given from writing, the requirement here to write ‘we sat as three,’ etc., is needed in order for the document to have the status of formal authentication by beit din. Nevertheless, it can still constitute the testimony of witnesses. Although the wording indicates that they sat as a beit din, two are not a beit din, but they are still witnesses. This applies where their writing itself constitutes direct testimony, such as where they state that they recognize the signatures, that the witnesses signed before them, or that the document was otherwise authenticated before them. But if the authentication states, ‘So and so testified before us concerning their signatures,’ then since only two judges were present they could not receive testimony, and the result is testimony from the mouth of another witness. Nimukei Yosef in Chezkat HaBatim writes this, and Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe cite it above at the beginning of this siman. Rashbam likewise writes so in Chezkat HaBatim. The Rama and Sema above in seif 4 likewise say that authentication by two is nothing because it is testimony from the mouth of another witness. In such a case it is entirely void and does not even constitute witness testimony. There are other practical differences between formal authentication by beit din and witness testimony. One concerns what I wrote above in seif 16, seif katan 45, in the names of Ran, Raah, Ritva, and Maharam, that a witness and a judge may combine. Another concerns a single expert judge, or a person whom the parties accepted to have the authority of a single expert. Such a judge may adjudicate alone on the basis of a document formally authenticated by beit din when he recognizes the authentication, but he cannot adjudicate alone merely because he recognizes the written testimony of witnesses. Receiving testimony cannot be performed by a single expert, as I wrote above in siman 3, seif katan 8, and in this siman, seif 4, seif katan 8; three are required. There is another practical difference as well. Where the writing amounts only to witness testimony and a later beit din must receive that testimony from their writing, as I explained above in seif 7, seif katan 16, it appears that the witnesses must still be alive at the time the later beit din receives their testimony from the writing, because at the time testimony is received they must be fit to give it orally. This appears correct to me; examine it carefully:"]
[" Even if it stated therein ‘in beit din,’ etc. Rivash, siman 413, writes that nowadays it is already universally known that a beit din consists of no fewer than three, and therefore whenever the document uses the language of ‘beit din,’ nothing further is required. Darkei Moshe and Beit Yosef, new material 24, cite him. Nevertheless, it appears that everything depends on the identity and status of those whose signatures appear:"]
[" And if it has an implication that there were three. For example, if it says, ‘So and so said to us,’ or, ‘We said to so and so,’ and that third person is known not to be related or disqualified. Otherwise, the Mechaber immediately afterward brings an opinion that invalidates unless the certification says, ‘one of the three is no longer present,’ even though it already states, ‘we sat as three.’ Rivash, siman 413, cites this in the name of Ramah. The concern is that there were indeed three, but one was related or otherwise disqualified and therefore did not sign, lest his disqualification become revealed. If, however, they write ‘beit din,’ it implies that all were valid judges. The only remaining concern is whether the authority follows Shmuel, but if both ‘beit din’ and ‘we sat as three’ are written, the phrase ‘one of the three is no longer present’ is unnecessary. This concludes the cited view. According to Ramah, then, what is gained by merely implying that there were three? The explicit words ‘we sat as three’ are the strongest possible implication that three were present, and even so he requires the phrase ‘one of the three is no longer present,’ lest the third have been related or disqualified. The same concern would apply here. Rather, the Mechaber must be discussing the case as I explained, namely that the identified third person is known to be valid. Examine this carefully:"]
[" An implication that there were three, etc. Such as, ‘So and so said to us,’ etc. Ir Shushan writes that if there is another indication that three sat, for example, that it says, ‘So and so, who is an expert recognized by the public, told us to sit and authenticate the document,’ or that the authentication was made in his presence, then the phrase ‘one of the three is no longer present’ is unnecessary, because that expert certainly would not err by calling two judges a beit din. Sema, seif katan 68, challenges him that according to Rambam the third judge need not be an expert. In my view there is no challenge. Ir Shushan saw that Rashi disagrees with Rambam’s interpretation and that Rashi’s reading of the Gemara is compelling, and therefore he wrote in accordance with Rashi. It is also possible that Rambam agrees with Rashi as a practical matter. Rambam discusses a case where the named person is himself one of the three judges, while Rashi and Ir Shushan discuss even a case where he is not one of the three:"]
[" Nevertheless, according to everyone, etc. Where the authentication states both ‘beit din’ and ‘we sat as three,’ nothing further is required. Since it explicitly says ‘we sat as three,’ there were three, and there is no concern that one was related or disqualified, because the term ‘beit din’ implies valid judges:"]
