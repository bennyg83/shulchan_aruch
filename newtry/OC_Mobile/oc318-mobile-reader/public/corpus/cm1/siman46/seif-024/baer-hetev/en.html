And he signs. The Sema explains that the third person who hears the testimony from the other two signs together with them. Although those two had themselves been witnesses who testified before him, they may also become judges, because the Sages were lenient with respect to authentication of documents. The Shach notes that the Beit Yosef and Darchei Moshe cite Rabbeinu Yerucham that a witness signed on a document may not judge that same document, whereas the Rashba writes that there is room to validate him as a judge. This seems to be a dispute, but in truth it is not. Rabbeinu Yerucham speaks of judging the monetary claim contained in the document. The Rashba speaks only of serving as a judge for the authentication of the document. Regarding adjudication of the claim itself, the Rashba certainly agrees that he may not serve as judge.
Before him. Neither those two nor others may testify only afterward. Although we generally are not concerned that an act merely appears false, here there is actual falsehood, because when they signed and thereby authenticated the document there was not yet a beit din of three, so the document was authenticated improperly. The Shiltei Giborim writes that from this one might say that after two judges have written and signed the authentication they can no longer testify before the third so that he may sign with them. He himself argues that even after they wrote and signed, they may testify before the third and he may sign with them; likewise, judges may write and sign an authentication even before testimony is given before them, provided it does not leave their possession until testimony has been given before them. The Shach agrees with the latter point, since we rule that we are not concerned with the appearance of falsehood, and they were sitting as three at the time of the signing. See siman 39, seif 13. But the first assertion is not persuasive, for it contradicts all the authorities and the implication of the Gemara. Perhaps it means that all three will sign again together, and we do not say that their earlier signatures make them interested parties. This requires study.
Primary. Even though the authentication states, 'We were sitting as three and it became known to us that this is the handwriting of so-and-so,' whereas at the time they wrote it all three did not yet know this, the Sema holds that this is not considered an appearance of falsehood because once the third learns it and signs with them, the text is no longer false in appearance. The Shach writes that at first glance this seems to mean that it is forbidden to write and sign the authentication before the document has actually been authenticated. In truth that is not so, for in siman 39, seif 13, he proved that even signing in advance is not prohibited merely because of the appearance of falsehood, and the Shiltei Giborim says likewise. Rather, the point is to exclude a case where two receive testimony and sign, and afterward testify before the third so that he signs. That procedure is ineffective, even if they later testify again before all three together, because their first signatures were made falsely and they can no longer join. Although this reading is somewhat strained, it is compelled by the Gemara and appears to be the primary practical ruling.
