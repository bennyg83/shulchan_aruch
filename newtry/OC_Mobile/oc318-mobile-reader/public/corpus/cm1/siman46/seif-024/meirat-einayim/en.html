They testify before him and he signs. Meaning that the judge who hears their testimony signs the authentication together with them. Although these two people were witnesses who testified before him, they may themselves become judges as well, because the Sages were lenient in document authentication. For the same reason, although ordinary reception of testimony requires three judges, here one judge receives the testimony of the two, because the two witnesses themselves become judges at the time of their statement and it is as though they sat with him to receive testimony from others. The same applies that a witness signed on the original document may become a judge to receive testimony concerning the second witness signed with him, as Rashba writes in responsum 969.
His ointment does not imply that at least, before their signatures, they will be found to be conscious and entangled together to be in the D.C. to observe and write:
It is permitted to write the authentication on the document, etc. Although the authentication formula states, 'We were sitting as three and recognized that these are their signatures,' which appears false because when it was written not all three yet knew the signatures, Rif and Rambam hold that we do not concern ourselves with an appearance of falsehood in such a case. Once the third judge also learns the facts and signs with them, the document no longer appears false. The dissenting view holds that because this is a judicial act, we are concerned even with a temporary appearance of falsehood. See Hagahot Maimoniyot for more.
