A witness testifies. The judge testifies to the deed witnesses' handwriting, and the other witness does the same. By contrast, the deed witness himself who signed the original document is treated as testifying to the monetary obligation in the document. If the deed witness no longer remembers the transaction, under the opinion above requiring an additional person with him, then if the judge joins him and that same deed witness also identifies the signature of the second deed witness, the combination can be valid. Both are now testifying to the two signatures and both function within the authentication. See Tumim.
They testify concerning the judge. One additional witness must also testify concerning both deed witnesses' signatures, so that each signature has two supporting witnesses: the additional witness and the judge. If no such additional witness testifies to both signatures, and the two witnesses who identify the judge also identify only one deed witness, then the second signature is authenticated only through the judge, equivalent to one witness, and does not suffice. Shach is more lenient and holds that even one judge can suffice in authentication because the Sages were lenient. He attributes this to Ritva and perhaps Ran. See Tumim. I show that Ritva has almost no support beyond Maharam, while Tosafot, Raah, Ran, and certainly Rosh disagree. We follow the majority. I also conclude that, apart from Rosh's unique position, judges of authentication should not be relatives of the deed witnesses, because the legal possibility of combining judge and witness could otherwise produce improper validation among relatives. If two witnesses testify to the handwriting of one authentication judge and two other witnesses to the handwriting of a second judge, see Tumim. I remain uncertain whether Rif would validate or whether the case becomes one of incomplete testimony.
