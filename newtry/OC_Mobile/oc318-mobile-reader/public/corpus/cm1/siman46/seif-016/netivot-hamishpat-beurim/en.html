Concerning what he testifies. The Shach's formulation is difficult because a practical defect can still emerge. For example, the living witness may later testify personally to his own signature, two witnesses may authenticate the judge's signature, and one additional witness may authenticate the second witness's signature. That produces a disproportionate structure in which three quarters of the authentication rests on the larger group. There is no realistic concern that the judge himself will later appear together with the living witness, because if they appear, the court will know the true number of witnesses and can evaluate the authentication accordingly.
They testify about the judge. Ran writes that once the judge's signature authenticating the deed has itself been authenticated, we do not additionally suspect that the underlying other witness's signature was forged. Ketzot HaChoshen objects from siman 45 concerning a document with text on one line and signatures on another. There is no comparison. Ran speaks of an otherwise valid document where the only concern is the ordinary rabbinic suspicion of forged handwriting. Once the judicial authentication itself is established, we do not manufacture a second forgery concern against the remaining signature. In siman 45, however, the signatures are not legally treated as witnesses to the document at all because of the structural concern stated there. Authenticating those signatures cannot cure the fact that they were never validly connected to the text.
