They must be unrelated, etc. This is because the principal loan depends on the witnesses, and also because hazamah could not be accepted against them, Sema. This applies according to the opinion in the following seif that validates judges of document validation who are related to the judges of the underlying loan. According to that view, although money ultimately leaves one party's possession through relatives, that alone is not disqualifying, and therefore the reason here, where the Mechaber cites no dispute, must be the inability to accept hazamah. According to Ran, however, who disqualifies judges of validation who are related to the judges of the loan because money would thereby be extracted through relatives, that same reason applies here. Sema stated a reason applicable according to all opinions, since on this point there is no dispute. This also resolves Shach's question against Sema. A practical difference between the two explanations arises when witnesses gave oral testimony before one beit din and another beit din later adjudicates based on that recorded testimony. According to the first reason, the second beit din may judge even if related to the witnesses. According to the second reason, it may not, because ultimately money would be extracted through relatives. See Shach, seif katan 53.
