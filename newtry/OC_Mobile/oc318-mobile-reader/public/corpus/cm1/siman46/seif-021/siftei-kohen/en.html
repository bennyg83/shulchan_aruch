They must be unrelated to the witnesses signed on the document itself, just as they must be unrelated to them when the witnesses testify orally before them. Although witnesses signed on a document are regarded, from the time they sign, as though their testimony had been examined in beit din, how can a beit din deciding the monetary claim adjudicate through the testimony of its relatives, who testify concerning the money itself? These are the Ran’s words. It follows that if witnesses testified orally before one beit din and that beit din recorded their testimony, another beit din whose judges are related to those witnesses may not adjudicate the loan on the basis of the testimony previously given by their relatives. This is also implied by the Ran’s words cited below in seif katan 53. The Ran further writes that we do not follow the Yerushalmi’s reason, which bases the disqualification upon the possibility of refutation. Rather, since the Babylonian Gemara derives from the verse concerning fathers and sons that relatives may not testify together, the same reason prevents one relative from testifying before another. Whenever the result is produced through relatives, whether both are witnesses or one is a witness and the other a judge, it is the same. The Ritva at the end of the second chapter of Ketubot similarly writes that the Yerushalmi attributes the rule to refutation, but that is not the approach of the Babylonian Gemara. Refutation is a separate event that comes from elsewhere, as shown in Sanhedrin concerning three brothers joined by one outsider, who form three separate testimonies but one testimony for purposes of refutation. The disqualification of relatives in our Gemara is a royal decree that a matter not be concluded through relatives, who are considered one body. Related witnesses are therefore considered one witness, and where the witnesses are related to the judges, it is as though the witness himself became a judge after testifying, leaving no complete judge. This is unlike the Ir Shushan, who explains that the matter affects the witnesses personally, namely whether they signed lawfully. The Sema, seif katan 52, likewise gives the reason that the judges could not accept refutation against them. The correct reason is the one clearly explained by the Ritva and Ran.
