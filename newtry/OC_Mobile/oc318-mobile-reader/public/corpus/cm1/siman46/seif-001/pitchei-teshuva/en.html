And if they authenticated it with two. See Baer Heitev in the name of the Sema, through his statement that if the borrower himself signed the document and admits before two witnesses that it is his signature, it appears that this is sufficient authentication. See Taz as well. In the glosses of Rabbi Akiva Eiger to Shulchan Aruch it is written: Nevertheless, where the document bears the borrower's signature and witnesses testify that the borrower admitted before them that it is his signature, which the Sema and Taz say is effective, there is much room to question this. The Rama's ruling in seif 7 that witnesses may testify from a writing is based on the Rivash's reason that authentication of documents is only rabbinic. Accordingly, regarding a person's own handwriting, which the Sema writes above in siman 35, and the Shach writes here in seif katan 10, requires authentication by Torah law, testimony from a writing should not be effective. End of quote. Netivot HaMishpat likewise questions the ruling, and answers that the case is where the borrower instructed them to sign and authenticate, so their written testimony has the status of ordinary witness testimony in a document executed at the direction of the person becoming obligated.
