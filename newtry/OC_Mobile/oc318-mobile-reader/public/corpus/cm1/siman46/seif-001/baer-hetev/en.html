A trust document. At the beginning of chapter 2, the Beit Yosef explains that a trust document is one regarding which the maker says: “I did not give it to him in order to borrow upon it. I merely prepared it so that if I later need to borrow, he will lend me on the strength of it, and I trusted him not to demand payment until I actually borrow.” The phrase “I wrote it in order to borrow...” means that I never delivered it to him; rather, it fell from me and he found it. See below, seif 37, where witnesses are not believed to say that a document was merely a trust document, because participating in such an arrangement is improper and a person cannot render himself wicked. The Mechaber does not say this where the witnesses state that it was written in order to borrow, because in that case they do not render themselves wicked. They can say: “We signed permissibly, because at the time he intended to borrow immediately, but afterward some circumstance arose and he did not borrow.” Sema.
