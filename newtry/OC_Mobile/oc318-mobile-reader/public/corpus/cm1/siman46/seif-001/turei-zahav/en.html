(Gloss to seif 4: Authentication by two is not authentication.) The Sema writes that if the two witnesses recognize the signatures, or if the borrower admits before them that the handwriting is his, their authentication is effective. This is very surprising. The Gemara states that document authentication requires three, and the Rambam’s five methods of authentication, cited in seif 7, include recognition of the signatures. Moreover, the Beit Yosef cites the Rivash, who ruled before the Ran that two witnesses may write that they recognize the signatures, but a beit din must still receive their written testimony, sign, and thereby authenticate the document. Thus two witnesses alone are insufficient. Their writing can serve as testimony, as the Rama states in seif 7. If two persons write that they recognize the signatures, whether because the signatories signed before them or because the borrower acknowledged the signatures before them, and the beit din adjudicating the document itself recognizes the handwriting of these two authenticating witnesses, that is effective. There is no difference between another beit din receiving their written testimony and the present beit din receiving it. But if the present beit din does not recognize their signatures and only two other witnesses identify them, the instrument has not acquired the status of an authenticated document. The first two were merely witnesses, not a beit din, and testimony identifying them would be testimony from hearsay. By contrast, where three authenticated the document, it is enough that two witnesses identify the signatures of two judges, because the act already bears the status of a beit din. Perhaps this is also the Sema’s intent: authentication by two is effective only when the judges themselves recognize their signatures, but it does not create an authenticated judicial record whose signatures may later be established by others as in a panel of three.
