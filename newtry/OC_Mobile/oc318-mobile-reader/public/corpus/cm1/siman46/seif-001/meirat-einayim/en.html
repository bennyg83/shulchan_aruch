It is an accommodation document, or I wrote it intending to borrow, etc. The Tur here writes only, 'It is an accommodation document,' but at the beginning of siman 82 he writes, 'It was an accommodation document, or I wrote it intending to borrow and did not borrow,' matching the Mechaber's wording here. Beit Yosef there explains that an accommodation document means that the borrower says: 'I did not give it to him in order to borrow with it. I merely prepared it so that if I would later need to borrow, he could lend to me on its basis, and I trusted him not to demand payment with it so long as I had not actually borrowed.' 'I wrote it intending to borrow,' means: 'I did not deliver it into his hand at all; rather, it fell from me and he found it.' See the Tur and Mechaber later in this siman, seif 37, where witnesses are not believed to say, 'It was an accommodation document,' because writing such a document is improper and a person may not render himself wicked. They do not say the same where the witnesses say, 'He wrote it intending to borrow but never borrowed,' because in that case they do not render themselves wicked. They can say that they signed permissibly because his intent was to borrow immediately. One may write a document for a borrower even if the lender is not present when the borrower intends to borrow immediately, and only afterward some circumstance arose and he did not borrow. See what I wrote above in siman 43, seif katan 12. This differs from witnesses saying that from the outset they wrote it merely as an accommodation document.
