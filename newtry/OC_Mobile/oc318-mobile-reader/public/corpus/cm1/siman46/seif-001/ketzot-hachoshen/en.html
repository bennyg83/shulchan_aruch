I wrote it in order to borrow. The Sema explains that the borrower claims he wrote the document to obtain a loan but never delivered it, so it fell from him and the present holder found it. This is difficult from Even HaEzer, where a woman who finds her get may rely upon it and the husband is not believed to say it merely fell from him, even though he has a migo of forgery. Similarly, in siman 50 the defendant can sometimes claim that a document fell from another person and was found by the claimant, but there the concern is a fall involving many people. I therefore distinguish between an individual fall and a broadly plausible fall. We do not suspect an individual fall even where a migo exists. In a crowded setting where multiple people could have lost an instrument, the concern is greater and a migo can make the claim credible. Accordingly, the present clause of "I wrote it in order to borrow" should not be understood as a later accidental fall. It means the document was initially prepared for the prospective loan and never became an operative loan document. Likewise an accommodation document was created from the outset in that manner. In such cases the witnesses need not be wicked, because they may properly prepare a document for an intended loan where the borrower has not yet received the money. But a later claim that this operative document merely fell from the borrower is not believed merely through a migo. This also supports the Sema in siman 69 against the Shach regarding an ordinary individual fall.
