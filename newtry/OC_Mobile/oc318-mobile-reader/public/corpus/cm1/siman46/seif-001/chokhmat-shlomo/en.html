Seif 1. One who produces a promissory note against another: so long as it has not been authenticated, the borrower may claim that it is forged. See my work on Orach Chaim from the year 5605, laws of Rosh Hashanah, in my Talmudic novellae there, where I proved from Tosafot to Gittin 11, concerning the discovery that a Samaritan witness was trustworthy, that when the borrower claims forgery, authentication is required by Torah law. See also the Yerushalmi there, which implies that even when one witness's signature was authenticated by others, that witness himself cannot join with another person to authenticate the second signature, contrary to the Bavli and the law we follow. I explained the Yerushalmi's reasoning in those novellae; examine it carefully.
