In order to make it easier, etc. Perhaps one of the judges will leave and they would otherwise have to find him again so that he too can sign. Therefore they initially wrote, "and one is absent."
Some say it is valid, etc. The case is where the validation states that they sat as three and as a beit din. Otherwise, if merely writing that they sat as three while only two signatures appear causes Ramah to invalidate, lest the third have been a relative and therefore refrained from signing, then certainly where we actually see that the third was a relative the validation would be invalid. Rather, Rivash concerns a validation that expressly states both that they were three and that they constituted a beit din. We can then neither say that they followed Shmuel, who permits two, because they wrote that they were three, nor that they mistakenly thought a relative was fit, because they wrote that they were a beit din. Therefore the presence of the relative does not invalidate, since we say that the valid judges themselves constituted the panel. This is explicit in Rivash.
Some say it is invalid. See Tumim. According to Rosh it is invalid in every case, even if four or five judges signed the validation. If a relative signed anywhere among them, at the beginning, middle, or end, it is invalid. See there.
Valid, etc. We say that they themselves were the witnesses who recognized the handwriting and saw it at night, in a manner where the rule that sight may not become judicial action would otherwise apply. Nevertheless, because with document validation a witness may become a judge, they wrote in the language of witnesses. But if the validation states, "It was clarified before us," this reasoning does not apply, because that wording means that they actually received testimony from others, and the validation is then invalid. Rivash, siman 382, says this explicitly, Shach.
