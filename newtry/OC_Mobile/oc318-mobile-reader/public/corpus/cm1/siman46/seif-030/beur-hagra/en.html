One who signed. For he testifies regarding the maneh itself, etc. See Tosafot there, s.v. al, etc. Rambam explains the Jerusalem Talmud cited by Tosafot, s.v. veRabbi Yochanan, as teaching that where the witness remembers nothing at all, Rabbi and the Sages do not disagree and he cannot testify. Just as Rabbi requires at least that he remember through seeing the document, so too the Sages.
(Gloss) See the Rif there, who explains the statement in Ketubot that a person may write, etc., as referring to testimony regarding and authentication of his handwriting before beit din.
(Gloss) See Yevamot 31b and Tosafot there, s.v. dechazu. The difficulty there, and likewise the passage in the second chapter, etc., are resolved by the Rif. Rambam therefore distinguishes between Yevamot and Ketubot, where the witnesses themselves testify, and Yevamot, where they remember nothing even after seeing the document, as Rashi explains in Ketubot, while in Bava Kamma other witnesses testify.

Rambam, however, holds that the Babylonian Gemara disagrees with the Jerusalem Talmud because it makes no such distinction.
