The Rivash’s wording is that the authentication is valid because we assume that the relative signed on his own afterward, or that the qualified judges left a blank space and he signed above them. The Beit Yosef cites this in section 26. The Rivash follows his own position in simanim 413 and 415, agreeing with the Ramban that whether the relative or unfit person appears first or last, we assume that he signed afterward on his own. According to the Rama’s ruling in siman 45, seif 12, and according to what I established there as the primary law, the document is valid only when the unfit signature appears first. We then say the qualified judges left room for an elder and the unfit person independently signed in that space to complete the line. If he signed last, we do not make this assumption. The same distinction applies here. This appears to be the Rama’s intent in his gloss here, where he writes that the third person, who was a relative or unfit, signed afterward, “as explained above in siman 45, seif 12, concerning other documents.” He means that, as with other documents, this is said specifically when the unfit person’s signature appears first.
Some say it is invalid. The Rama understands that the Rosh, in responsa, rule 60, siman 4, disagrees with the Rivash. He writes similarly in Darkei Moshe that the Rosh’s responsum implies the authentication is invalid when a relative signed. In my humble opinion, the Rosh does not disagree. He speaks where it is known that there was no other third judge and that the only judges were the three who signed. Review the responsum and this will be evident. The Rosh never states that we may not assume three other qualified judges were present. This appears clear to me.
