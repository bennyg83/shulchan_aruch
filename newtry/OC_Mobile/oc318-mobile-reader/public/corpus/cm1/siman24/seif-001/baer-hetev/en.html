The Shach writes that many explanations have been offered, but the primary explanation appears to be this: when the parties come to court and the claimant wants his words heard first while the defendant wants to speak first, there is a practical difference because the defendant may admit part of the claim. A partial admission obligates a Torah oath only when made after the claimant has asserted his claim, as stated below in siman 88, seif 15. Therefore beit din first attends to the claimant. If the defendant then admits part, he becomes obligated in a Torah oath, for he should have paid before being sued. But if the defendant's property has declined in value, such as where assets are presently cheap or he would have to sell them at a loss because of a forced sale, and for that reason he did not pay before being sued, beit din attends first to the defendant. If he admits part before the claimant states his claim, he is not obligated in a Torah oath.
