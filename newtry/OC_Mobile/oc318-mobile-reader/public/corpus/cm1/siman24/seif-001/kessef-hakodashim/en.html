Seif 1. We attend first to the claimant. My practice where each party has a claim against the other and each must swear in order to be exempt is that the original defendant swears first. Thus, if Reuven first sued Shimon and Shimon then asserted counterclaims, Shimon swears first even if his counterclaims are larger and more numerous. An exception is where only Reuven bears a Torah oath, in which case that more serious oath should be taken first. Where both oaths are Torah-level or both rabbinic, the original defendant takes precedence. In practice, with Hashem's help I have almost never imposed actual oaths, because compromise is arranged according to the weight of Torah law. Nevertheless, the order matters greatly because the party whose oath comes first often bears the greater compromise burden. I have not seen the phrase "we attend first to the claimant" explicitly interpreted this way, since the Gemara discusses circumstances where property may decline in value. Even so, the ruling follows sound logic, and independently from that phrase it follows from the rule that a case arriving first receives priority. Just as the earlier case is decided first, the practical fulfillment of its ruling, including the oath generated by that ruling, should ordinarily be performed first. Under the common compromise practice this can benefit the original claimant, but that is the reward of having acted diligently. The rule that where both parties must swear, the person holding the deposit may sometimes swear first lest he later produce the deposit and create a desecration of Hashem's Name does not contradict this. That concern protects Divine honor and therefore overrides the ordinary procedural priority, even if the other party willingly agrees to swear first. The ordinary priority rule is for the benefit and orderly peace of the earlier litigant and can be waived by him. The concern that a hidden deposit may later be produced cannot be waived in the same way.
