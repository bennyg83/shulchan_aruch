The Claimant Is Attended to First. One seif. The beit din attends to the claimant first. If delay will cause the defendant's property to decline in value, it attends to the defendant first. {Rama: For example, Reuven makes a claim against Shimon regarding which Shimon cannot say, 'I paid,' and Shimon responds, 'You possess an equivalent amount of mine,' but asks for more than the beit din's standard thirty-day period to bring witnesses. Even if we know that he has witnesses who know the matter, the claimant is attended to first, and Shimon must pay immediately, unless his property will decline in value and delay will harm him. The same applies if the claimant asks that his words be heard before the defendant's, asks the beit din not to issue judgment until he chooses, or is owed an oath by the defendant but wants the oath administered only when he chooses. Similarly, if he says that he has many claims but does not wish to present them now, the claimant is heeded unless the defendant's property will decline in value or the judges see that the defendant will suffer harm. See also simanim 5, 526, and 527.}
