We attend first to the claimant. This is the Gemara's language. The poskim offer different applications, all of which are legally correct. The Mechaber therefore states the principle without limiting it, while Rama lists several illustrations and concludes 'and anything similar.'
In a manner that he cannot say, 'I paid.' For example, the loan is documented by a note signed by witnesses. If the borrower would be believed to say 'I paid,' he would also be believed when he claims, 'You hold property of mine,' through a migo that he could have said 'I paid.'
You have the same amount of mine. The Tur gives the example where Shimon responds, 'You seized something of mine, return what you seized,' or, 'I had collateral in your possession and its value diminished.' Even if the claimant admits some depreciation and they dispute only the amount, the procedural rule remains the same.
More than thirty days. If Shimon asks only the standard thirty-day period of beit din, his request is heard even if his assets are not depreciating. If he asks for more than thirty days and beit din has no basis to know that relevant witnesses exist, his request is denied even if his assets will decline. The case addressed here is where beit din knows there are witnesses connected to the matter but does not yet know what they will say. Then there is a distinction depending on whether the defendant's assets will depreciate.
And he must pay him immediately. If he has cash and asks for time only to prove an offsetting claim, he pays immediately once he cannot produce his proof within the ordinary thirty days. There is no reason to postpone payment merely to grant thirty idle days. If he lacks cash, he receives the ordinary time needed to obtain money.
Unless his assets will depreciate. Meaning he lacks cash and cannot borrow or sell except at a distressed price because people will know he urgently needs money. In such a case we attend first to the defendant and give him the time reasonably needed, even beyond thirty days, to produce proof that may eliminate the claimant's demand and prevent a forced discounted sale.
And this causes damage to the defendant. The Tur adds another consequence: even if the defendant says he can bring evidence within thirty days, beit din may first issue the ruling on the claimant's case. If the defendant later brings proof, the judgment is reopened. If he does not, the thirty-day payment period has already been running from the original ruling.
That they hear his words first. Mordechai explains that merely hearing the defendant's claims first may materially assist him. In such a case, where he is not asking to receive money before the claimant but only to be heard first, the statement about depreciating assets means that he is independently trying to sell property and fears that if people first hear the claimant suing him they will assume a forced sale and offer less. If he is heard first, they may think he is the claimant.
Or if he requests, etc. The Tur explains that perhaps today or tomorrow the claimant will find witnesses or the defendant will admit. Darkei Moshe cites Rashba that even after making a kinyan to litigate before a particular beit din and showing his documents, a claimant may still say, 'I do not wish to proceed now,' unless the delay is depreciating the defendant's assets.
Who does not want him to swear now. See siman 16 for the practical effect even though a later oath can be reopened if witnesses are found. There the statement that after thirty days the defendant may swear against the claimant's will concerns a case where delay harms the defendant's assets, though thirty days are still granted because the claimant specifically says he has witnesses who will show that the defendant cannot swear. Here that factual assertion is absent. Thus when assets are not declining the claimant may defer the oath until he wants it; when they are declining the delay is curtailed, though ordinarily some limited time may still be allowed.
And all similar cases. Tosafot and Mordechai give another application: if Reuven sues Shimon for bodily injury, beit din first extracts the injury payment from Shimon even though Shimon says Reuven holds his property and he wishes to bring witnesses within thirty days. No extended time is granted for injury claims in that way.
And he does not wish to plead now, we listen, etc. Rama below in siman 87 writes that where a claimant has many claims, beit din tells him to present them together so that the defendant can discharge them with one oath. There is no contradiction. That rule belongs specifically to the laws of combining oaths and does not mean that beit din always compels a claimant to advance all unrelated claims now. Even there Rama concludes that if the claimant refuses and the defendant asserts that he owes nothing at all, he may swear and be exempt. Here the topic is the priority of hearing claims, not merely consolidation of oaths. If delay will materially depreciate the defendant's assets, however, beit din can require the claimant to clarify the outstanding claims or otherwise remove the damaging rumor.
We listen to the claimant, etc. Darkei Moshe cites Mahari Weil that where one sued another who refused to answer because the claimant was violent, and later that defendant brought a different claim against the original claimant, he too need not answer the new claim until the original claimant first responds to the earlier one.
