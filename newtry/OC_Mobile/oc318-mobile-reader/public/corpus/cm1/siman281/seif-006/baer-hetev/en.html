And he increased. The Tur writes: where he increased one son's share or made the firstborn equal to the others, Rabbi Yosef HaLevi writes that just as his words are ineffective with respect to the firstborn, so too they are ineffective with respect to the ordinary sons, and the entire estate is divided anew as though he had said nothing. For those whose shares he increased must make up the firstborn's double portion from that increase, with the result that the testamentary division is undone because they receive less than he directed. Others say that the division among the ordinary sons remains in effect, because in this respect the firstborn vis-a-vis his brothers is like a creditor who collects from them proportionally according to what their father gave each of them. These words explain the Mechaber's ruling. The Tur and Shulchan Aruch state the law of a creditor against brothers at the end of siman 107. Sema.
