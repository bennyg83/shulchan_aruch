(A creditor does not acquire collateral.) Above, siman 278, seif 7, I cited Tosafot that the rule “a creditor acquires collateral” applies where the collateral was taken after the loan, not at the time of the loan. Nevertheless, even collateral taken at the time of the loan creates a strong lien and is considered presently held rather than merely prospective. Here Mahari Weil writes, in siman 109, that a creditor does not acquire collateral. His meaning is that the creditor does not acquire it for every purpose. He may use it to betroth a woman, as stated in the first chapter of Kiddushin, and the lender is a paid guardian, but he is exempt from unavoidable accidents. His ownership is therefore incomplete. This creates a difficulty from siman 278, where we say that the firstborn takes a double share because the collateral is considered presently held, whereas here the books are not treated as belonging to the creditor. One cannot answer that inheritance requires only that the father’s lien be presently held rather than prospective, whereas here the books must belong to him completely. If so, why does siman 278 say that collateral belonging to a gentile is not considered presently held? The creditor certainly has a secure lien and will not return the collateral until payment. Evidently, the collateral itself must be acquired by him. Why then do we say here that it is not acquired? Moreover, why should this be less than betrothal, for which the creditor may use it, as Mahari Weil himself writes? Levush likewise expresses surprise: “It has already been explained in siman 278 that a creditor acquires collateral. This requires study.” The Sema says Levush was imprecise and distinguishes: there, the collateral is considered presently held rather than prospective, so the firstborn takes a double portion. Here, however, the daughter’s share depends not on present possession but on complete acquisition by her father, so she does not take a share in it. There must be a scribal error in the Sema’s final words, for precisely because it was not fully acquired by her father, she does take a share in it. I do not understand the basis of this distinction, as stated. See siman 211, seif 2, where I elaborated concerning two documents and the inheritance of one’s father-in-law.
