A deathly ill person, etc. Maharam explains the statement there 130a, "alternatively, the whole Mishnah follows Rabbi Yochanan ben Berokah, etc." If you ask from the statement 126b, "he increased for one, etc.," that case includes a firstborn and is invalid because he equated the firstborn, etc. Rif likewise writes that this applies specifically where there is no firstborn. Where there is a firstborn, if he uses language of inheritance, his words are ineffective, as the Mishnah states, "he increased for one, etc." The baraisa of Abba Chanan likewise teaches this.
Others say, etc. Rashbam holds this view. See there 130a at the end of s.v. "halachah" and s.v. "melo, etc." The Rosh likewise writes that it applies specifically to the shares of ordinary sons, etc., following their approach that the Mishnah "he increased, etc." is according to the first Tanna.
