A certain man, my firstborn son, etc. See Minchat Targuma by R. Yeshayah Pick, on Parashat Ki Tetze, who cites the Ramban's commentary to the Torah on the verse, 'He may not make the son of the beloved the firstborn.' The Ramban writes that this prohibition and law apply only during the lifetime of the firstborn. If the firstborn died during his father's lifetime, although the firstborn acquires his double portion in the grave and transmits it by law to his children, the grandfather may nevertheless say that his sons shall inherit specified portions and that the son of his deceased firstborn shall receive a specified portion, just as he could where there is no firstborn. Nor does he violate this prohibition by failing to recognize the firstborn after his death, for the expression 'in the presence of' is found with a living person. R. Yeshayah Pick observes that this explains the precise wording of the Mishna, which speaks of 'a certain man, my son, is a firstborn,' rather than simply 'my firstborn,' implying that the rule concerns his own son but not a grandson through a deceased firstborn. He adds that he had not found this novel ruling in other authors. In fact, Maharam Padua, responsum 44, also cites this ruling in the Ramban, and it is likewise cited in Avodat HaGershuni, end of responsum 61, discussed below in seif 7. Ketzot HaChoshen in seif 4 also cites the Ramban and notes that Maharit, Choshen Mishpat 71, did not rule this way, but since Maharit did not cite or apparently see the Ramban's words, it is reasonable that he would not have disputed them had they been before him. See also Ketonet Passim on Bava Batra 219a, who questions the Ramban from Midrash Rabbah, Acharei Mot chapter 20 and Bamidbar end of chapter 2, and Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat, end of responsum 150.
