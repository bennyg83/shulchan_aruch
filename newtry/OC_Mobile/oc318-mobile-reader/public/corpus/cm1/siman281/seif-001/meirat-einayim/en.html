And while dangerously ill he said, "So and so my brother, etc." His words are effective. We learn from "on the day that he causes his sons to inherit" that the Torah gives a father authority to distribute inheritance among his sons as he wishes, and the same principle applies to other heirs. The Mechaber gives the example "so and so my brother shall inherit me." The same law applies if he says so regarding a son, as the Tur writes. With a son there is a distinction whether he wrote or merely said it orally, as the Mechaber later explains, whereas according to the Rif and Mechaber there is no such distinction regarding a brother or daughter. Specifically a dangerously ill person can alter the normal inheritance through inheritance language, not a healthy person. The Tur says so in the names of Rav Hai, Rif, and Rosh. Conversely, at the beginning of the siman, where one seeks to uproot inheritance entirely or bequeath to someone who is not a legal heir, the statement is ineffective even if made while dangerously ill.
