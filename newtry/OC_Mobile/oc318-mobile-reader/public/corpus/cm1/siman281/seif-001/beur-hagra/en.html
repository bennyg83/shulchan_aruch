One may not, etc. As stated below, "so and so shall inherit me, etc."
Nor, etc. As stated below, "therefore, etc."
Therefore, etc. The Mishnah 126b.
"So and so shall inherit me," etc. The Mishnah 130a. Rabbi Yochanan ben Berokah concedes in these cases, as the Gemara says there: alternatively, the whole Mishnah follows Rabbi Yochanan ben Berokah, etc. The first clause is likewise his view, as Maharam explains. The Mishnah's statement, "one who says, 'so and so my son shall not inherit, etc.,'" can also follow Rabbi Yochanan ben Berokah. He disagrees only where one says "he shall inherit me," for then the other heirs are automatically excluded, and similarly where one says "so and so shall inherit such and such and so and so such and such." But if he says "he shall not inherit," although this implies that the remaining sons will inherit, because he did not explicitly say that so and so and so and so shall inherit, he has not displaced the inheritance by conveying it to them. It therefore falls before the excluded son as well, and it is as though he said that he shall not take his own money, etc. See there and Rashbam 126b, s.v. "lo amar, etc."
When he is deathly ill. Specifically, as stated in seif 5. This is why the first clause says, "whether he instructed, etc."
Or he increased, etc. See Rashbam 126b, s.v. "ve'im amar, etc.," and likewise there 130a, s.v. "veyesh," s.v. "vechi teima," and s.v. "ve'iba'is eima, etc." See what I wrote in seif 6.
Whether, etc. The Mishnah says "one who says," and the same applies if he writes, as stated there 133a: "a deathly ill person who wrote, etc.; thief, etc." Likewise in the first clause, whether orally, etc., as in the Mishnah 126b, "one who says, etc.; if he wrote, whether, etc." The latter clause, "but if he said, etc.," follows Rabbeinu Chananel and the Rif, as above.
