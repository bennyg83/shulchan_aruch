For the principal matter is the debt. Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat 144, discusses a report that R. Yirmiyah had ruled that a half-male-share debt must be paid specifically in silver coin and not in the banknotes then in use, because the standard document required payment in currency 'current with merchants,' meaning acceptable in all lands, whereas those banknotes circulated only within the imperial territories. Chatam Sofer explains that this ruling concerned an earlier period when the currency change had only begun, most commerce was still in silver, banknotes traded at a discount, and the original dowries and obligations had been made in silver coin. Although by the time of collection banknotes had legal currency, the evident intent was repayment in the same type of currency as at the time of the obligation. Even if the debt is said to take effect only one hour before death, the document still contains an admission that the daughter had advanced him the gold coins current at the date of the wedding and document. Had the instrument not expressly required currency 'current with merchants,' legal tender rules might have allowed payment in banknotes under the law of the kingdom. But where the contract itself required broadly current money and banknotes then circulated only at a substantial discount, R. Yirmiyah's ruling was correct. Chatam Sofer adds that in his own time economic conditions had changed, and therefore the practical ruling could differ.
