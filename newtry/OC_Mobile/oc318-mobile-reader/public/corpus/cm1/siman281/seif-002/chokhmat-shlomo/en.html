Seif 8. The expression 'he shall take possession of the property' is language of gift, etc. Note: See Ketzot HaChoshen regarding the view cited from Maharit, and see my fourth edition on Choshen Mishpat, responsum 52 to the community of Elikhov, where I wrote that we do not rule in accordance with that view. Ketzot HaChoshen challenges Maharit from the Ramban, whose words imply that if a firstborn son dies during his father's lifetime he has no firstborn share. Yet the Gemara itself appears to support Maharit. In Yesh Nochalin the Gemara asks why the verse 'he shall recognize' is necessary, since the father could simply give the purported firstborn all his property as a gift. But what is the question? If he gives him all his property and the son dies during the father's lifetime, the son's own son would retain that gift. If the father merely declares, 'This is my firstborn son,' then if that son dies before him he would, according to the Ramban, lose the firstborn share. What migo is there? It seems, therefore, that even if the firstborn dies during the father's lifetime, his firstborn right persists. Still, this can be rejected, because the father has a migo to give him all the property on condition that if he dies during the father's lifetime the gift is void. Accordingly, the matter remains unresolved.
