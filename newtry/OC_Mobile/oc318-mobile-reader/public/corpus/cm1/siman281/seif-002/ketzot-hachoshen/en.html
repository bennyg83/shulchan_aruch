His words stand. It appears to me that once the Torah granted a person authority to designate one of the legal heirs to receive a greater inheritance share, that person's designation takes effect as inheritance. Afterward the testator cannot revoke that already created inheritance merely by saying that the heir shall not receive the added portion. Once the additional portion was validly assigned, the beneficiary became a Torah heir to that extent. A later statement, 'he shall not inherit that increase,' is a stipulation against Torah law and is ineffective. If, however, he initially increased one heir and reduced another, and then changes his words by reducing the first and increasing the second using language of inheritance, he can do so. The second person is also a legal heir, and the Torah allows the testator to designate which legal heir shall receive the larger share. Even if he initially made one son among several inherit the entire estate, he may later designate a second son to inherit the entire estate, because the second son remains a legal heir. His earlier statement has legal force only insofar as he said, 'So-and-so my son shall inherit everything.' The accompanying statement that the other son shall not inherit is itself ineffective, as Tur writes in the name of Ramah, and is treated as though it were not said. The second son therefore remains legally fit to inherit, and the father may later say that the second son shall inherit everything. But he cannot undo the first son's already acquired increased inheritance merely by saying, 'he shall not inherit the increase.' Once the first son was made an heir to that amount, such a negative exclusion is a condition against Torah law. If the parties are still engaged in the same matter, however, he can retract the increase by saying that the beneficiary shall not inherit it, because even an ordinary kinyan can be retracted while they remain engaged in that same transaction. Since the designation took effect suddenly through his words, the same rule applies. Once they have left the matter, he can no longer revoke the first increase merely with negative language. He can only redirect the inheritance by positively designating another qualified heir and thereby reversing his earlier designation through valid language of inheritance, as explained. This follows from our discussion. See siman 253, seif katan 1.
