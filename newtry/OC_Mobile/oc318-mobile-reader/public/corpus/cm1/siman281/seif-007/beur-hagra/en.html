When he is deathly ill. For a healthy person requires a formal transfer. Maggid Mishneh.
And he increased, etc.; and if, etc. This follows Rambam's teacher, as stated in seif 6.
Although, etc. This too follows his teacher, that even with two fields and two recipients it works whether the language of gift appears at the beginning, etc. Reish Lakish's example using the middle is stated for novelty, as Maggid Mishneh explains. This is why here it says "although," teaching that the phrase "gift in the middle" there is not exclusive, unlike Rashbam there, s.v. "Reish Lakish amar, etc." See Maggid Mishneh.
(Collected note) Within the time required for speech. See Beer HaGolah, who explains the measure, etc., in Nazir 22b and there 21a, and Makkos 6a. See Tosafos to Bava Kamma 73b, s.v. "ki, etc.," who write that this is the halachah, etc., even, etc. The Rosh and other poskim likewise rule so.
When does this apply, etc. Reish Lakish's statement concerns only the dispute of Rabbi Yochanan and Rabbi Elazar regarding two fields and two people. Rashbam likewise writes so there.
Others say specifically, etc. See Gittin 87a.
One who writes, etc. As stated 133a, "now, etc.," and 135b, "what is a gift, etc."
And all the days, etc. This is not comparable to "from today and after death" in 136a, because when he says "like one of the heirs according to what remains to them," he can give their share, and the same applies to his own share.
And the daughter does not, etc., for there is no, etc. As stated 128a regarding present property. See Tosafos 131a at the end of s.v. "u'shema minah": "and in the commentary he explained, etc.; this does not appear correct, etc." Likewise there, "it was necessary regarding property, etc." If it were otherwise, he could give it to them when it comes into the world. See Eruvin 21a, where it says, etc., and "but, etc."
And descendants, etc. As in the phrase "all the descendants of Yaakov, etc."
A creditor does not, etc. As stated in chapter HaOmanin and chapter HaDayanin: "say that Rabbi Yitzchak said this when the loan was not made at the time, etc." Even when not at the time of the loan he is only a paid bailee, and the pledge does not become his so as to make him liable for unavoidable accidents, as Tosafos write there and in chapter Kol Sha'ah and Shitah Mekubetzes. Although in chapter Kol Sha'ah we say that if he lent against a pledge he transgresses, that is only because a Jew acquires a pledge from a non-Jew, etc. Tosafos there write that this is specifically regarding chametz, and likewise shemitah, as Tosafos write in the chapter on interest, but he does not acquire it completely. See Mahariv, whose words are very difficult.
But if, etc. As stated there, if he lent on condition that if the borrower does not redeem the pledge it will belong to him from now, he acquires it. See above siman 207:9.
