(The books were absolutely transferred.) Mahari Weil writes there that an absolute transfer need not already have occurred. It is enough that at the time of the loan they stipulated, “If I do not redeem them by such-and-such date, they shall be yours from now,” and the date passed without redemption. The deceased then acquired the books, and the daughter has no share in them. If the heir asserts with certainty that the borrower said “from now,” the daughters receive no share, and the heir swears in order to retain the books and deny them a share. But from doubt alone we do not presume that he said “from now,” because doubt cannot remove money from the borrower’s possession. This is extremely difficult. Why do we not plead for the orphans every claim their father could have made? How is this different from Ketubot 109: had their father been alive he could have claimed that only one furrow was involved? It has already been explained in siman 72, seif 35, that where orphans are uncertain about the amount of a loan secured by collateral left by their father, the borrower cannot remove it from them merely through his claim. This is straightforward both from authority and reason. Moreover, even an uncommon claim such as unavoidable accident is pleaded for orphans according to one opinion cited in siman 108. All the more should we plead this common stipulation. The Rama writes in siman 290, seif 12, that a guardian may advance even an uncommon claim, obviously even on the basis of uncertainty. Why then may the guardian or the orphans themselves not make this claim here? The only possible limitation is a case in which the father himself could not have claimed that the books were purchased by him and not returned. If there were a dispute between borrower and lender in such circumstances, the borrower would swear and recover, as stated in siman 72, seif 18. Accordingly, against the orphans as well, the borrower swears and recovers. Perhaps Mahari Weil discusses such a case. This appears possible, but the matter requires study.
