All these limitations apply when the father uses language of inheritance. If he uses language of gift, his words are effective. Therefore, a deathly ill person who orally distributes his property may increase one heir's share, reduce another's, equalize the firstborn, or give to one who is not an heir, provided he speaks in terms of gift. If he says it is by inheritance, the disposition is ineffective. If he says, 'So-and-so shall inherit this field and it is given to him as a gift,' or the reverse, his words are effective because gift language accompanies the inheritance language.
