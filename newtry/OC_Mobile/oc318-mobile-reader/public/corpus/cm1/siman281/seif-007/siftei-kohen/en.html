He increased one heir's portion and reduced another's, etc. The Sema explains that this applies even where he used the language 'shall not inherit,' such as saying, 'My son shall not inherit.' Otherwise, the law has already been stated that even when he uses language of inheritance rather than gift, his words are effective. This is astonishing to me. Above, siman 253, seif 2, the Mechaber writes that 'shall take' is language of gift. Why, then, does the Mechaber write here in seif 1 that one who says, 'So-and-so, my firstborn, shall not take a double share,' has said nothing? If this is language of gift, it should be effective in every form. Perhaps one may answer that a firstborn is different. A modification concerning him is effective only through affirmative language of gift, such as saying, 'My firstborn shall take only the share of an ordinary son.' If he says negatively, 'He shall not take,' it is ineffective, whereas such negative language is effective regarding an ordinary son. A firstborn must necessarily have a stronger status than an ordinary son, as explained in seif 4. Nevertheless, the practical law requires study. Examine this carefully. See Responsa Mabit, part I, siman 118.
A creditor does not acquire collateral, etc. See Sema, seif katan 15, through the words 'and this is clear.' His words are correct. Nevertheless, regarding the practical law it appears to me that collateral taken after the loan is fully acquired by the creditor, as I concluded above, siman 72, seif 3, seif katan 9. Although Responsa Mahari Weil plainly implies that even collateral taken after the loan is included in the daughters' share, as the Sema writes, this is because he followed the Rosh and those who agree with him, who hold that even collateral taken after the loan is not fully acquired, except for purposes such as betrothing a woman with it. According to what I concluded in siman 72, however, the primary view is that of Rashi and those who agree with him, that the creditor acquires it fully. Even if one argues that it remains an unresolved question of law whether he acquires it fully, the heirs are in possession and the daughters bear the burden of proof. See Migdal David and Responsa Rama, siman 77, folio 148.
