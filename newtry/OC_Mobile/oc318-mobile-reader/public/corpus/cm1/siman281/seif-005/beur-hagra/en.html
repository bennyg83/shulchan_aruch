(Likkut) If he was, etc. The Rif and Rav Hai Gaon write that the question was not resolved and therefore we rule leniently. The Rif's wording, cited by the Rosh, is that the Gemara's discussion proceeds in the language of 'perhaps,' and no definitive resolution emerged. The Rosh writes that Rabbeinu Chananel, however, says that since the Gemara concludes that the later beit din instituted it in addition to the earlier beit din, the question is necessarily resolved. Meaning: if two distinct courts instituted the two enactments, the Baraisa cannot be established according to the Sages as giving one person inheritance and the other a gift; it must follow R' Yochanan ben Beroka. Baal HaMaor likewise challenges the Rif. The Rif, however, holds that since the matter was stated in the language of 'perhaps,' it was not conclusively resolved, and one can say that a single beit din enacted both. Baal HaMaor further objects that the entire give-and-take cannot overturn the resolution of the question, which had already been established from Rav Nachman's words: had he not heard that R' Yochanan ben Beroka said even regarding a healthy person, etc. Ramban likewise wonders at this, and Rashbam writes similarly there, daf 130a, s.v. 'Rav Pappa said to him,' and daf 130b, s.v. 'actually.' The Rif, however, explains like Tosafos there, s.v. 'and infer from it,' that the entire discussion concerns whether the question was actually resolved. Ramban in Milchamos writes this as well. He further explains there and in his novellae that Rav Nachman spoke interrogatively: 'Have you taught,' etc.; he asked them, 'Do you think even regarding a healthy person?' The principal resolution comes instead from Rebbe's conclusion, who said 'they shall inherit,' and from the Mishnah, which necessarily follows this view. This is the meaning of 'Whom did you hear,' etc. If the proof were directly from Rav Nachman, all that discussion would be unnecessary, since Rav Nachman himself explicitly said, 'Have you taught,' etc. See there. End.
