(In all that he possesses.) This includes property he owns at the time of death even if he did not own it previously. It is customary to write in a half-male-share document that the debt takes effect only one hour before death. In that manner the obligation properly attaches at that time to everything then in his possession. This is difficult, however. We hold in siman 60, seif 6, that by way of personal obligation one may obligate himself even regarding something not yet in existence, provided a kinyan was made. In a half-male-share document, where the obligation takes effect only one hour before death, the kerchief used for the kinyan has long since returned to its owner, as stated in siman 195, seif 5. We must therefore say that it is treated like one who says, “from now and after a period of time,” which is effective under siman 195. If so, a creditor whose loan arose after the half-male-share document should have no priority over its holder, because the document’s lien begins immediately. Yet daily practice is otherwise: ordinary creditors are paid first, before the son-in-law receives the half-male share. The Sema likewise writes: “He must give him a share in everything he owns. Nevertheless, its law is like inheritance, so creditors and the ketubah are collected first, because they become exempt from the debt through giving the half-male share.” I have not understood his reason, “because they become exempt,” at all.
