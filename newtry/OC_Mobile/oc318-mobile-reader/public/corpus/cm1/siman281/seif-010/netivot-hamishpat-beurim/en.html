Ketzot HaChoshen asks that "from today" ordinarily means the body from today and fruits later. If so, the body should have returned to all four from the outset. He therefore concludes that "from today" here means a condition, "from today if I do not retract," so when the first sister dies the survivors acquire retroactively and revoke what they had given her. This is difficult for two reasons. First, what basis is there to interpret "from today" differently here from every other deed? Second, on his view, after the second sister dies one would have to prove that she had retracted from what she previously gave. In my view "from today" has its ordinary meaning here: body from today and fruits later. The answer to Ketzot's question is Sema's own premise: each woman gave only to the sisters who would still be alive after her death, not to a sister who had predeceased her. Thus the brother cannot inherit through any sister who dies first. The remaining question is why we presume each sister intended to exclude a sister who had already died, rather than giving to all three sisters, with a deceased sister's heirs taking her share. The reason is that an umdena can be used to prevent an act from defeating its evident purpose. They expressly arranged this structure so that the brother should not inherit. If the gift were interpreted as extending even to a sister who had already died, the deceased sister could acquire nothing personally and her heir would be the brother. That would be equivalent to an express gift to the brother, directly contrary to the stated purpose. We therefore presume each sister intended to give only to sisters still alive at the time her own gift would become effective.
