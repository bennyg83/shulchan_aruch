And he wrote to her and to her descendants, etc. See Nachalat Shivah, siman 21, in his formula for a half male inheritance document, where he writes, "I obligate myself to pay the stated sum to my daughter or to her descendants." In his explanation there, seif 4, he writes that one should not write "her heirs who are her descendants," but simply "her descendants," and certainly should not write merely "her heirs." He says the reason is clear from Mahari Weil as cited by Rama in Choshen Mishpat, siman 281. Ketzot HaChoshen already expressed surprise at this, writing that his intention is unclear. It is certainly more proper to arrange matters according to the normal order of inheritance, so that sons inherit rather than daughters. Mahari Weil did not instruct that one write "to my daughter and her descendants"; he merely ruled that where an inheritance document says "to her descendants," sons and daughters are equal. Initially, however, it is preferable not to structure the document so as to transfer an inheritance from a son to a daughter. Accordingly, one should instead write "to her heirs who are her descendants." Responsa Shev Yaakov, siman 20, likewise questions Nachalat Shivah and labors to reconcile his words. It therefore appears that one should not follow Nachalat Shivah's formula. This is also clear from Tzemach Tzedek, siman 114, cited in the preceding seif katan, who records the custom to write "and to her heirs who are her descendants." See the following seif katan as well.
