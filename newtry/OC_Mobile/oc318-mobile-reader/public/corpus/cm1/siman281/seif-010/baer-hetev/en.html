The last. The point that each one of them did not give much part because, before her death, he gave a gift to marry in a smooth life and what came to birth a gift from her sisters who had died before her, and since each of them had died, no one of my sisters had come to birth, no one of my sisters who died after her death had given me a gift from the fourth hand, and that she had not only one of her death Her animals from today and one hour before her death and so forth in the response of the Rashi and the column of the DAM did not say, can you return from waiting and then the brother will take them to the sisters who died. A:
Assessment of intent. This refers to what was stated at the beginning, that there is no gift at all and the brother inherits them, even though there was a clear assessment that they intended the brother not to inherit. The Rosh explains the reason, as cited by the Tur: whenever we follow an assessment of intent, we nullify an act that was performed and leave the situation as it originally stood. For example, one who heard that his son died and wrote all his property to others, and then his son returned, has his gift nullified. We assess that had he known his son was alive, he would not have written all his property to others. Therefore, when the son returns, we nullify the act and the property remains in its prior ownership, so the son inherits it. In the present case, however, although we know they intended to divert the property from the brother, they did not perform any legally effective act of diversion. Unexpressed intentions do not uproot a Torah inheritance. Ibid.
The preceding one. Rama is brief, but the matter is explained clearly in the responsum of the Rosh and in the Tur. The brother inherits whatever remained in the possession of those sisters who died. At times he inherits everything. This occurs when the first three sisters who made gifts died, and the surviving sister was the one who made her gift last. Each sister had given back, together with her own portion, what she had previously received from her sisters. Thus, when the final sister made her gift, nothing remained in her possession, and the brother therefore inherits everything upon the others' deaths. If, however, one of the sisters who had not made the final gift remains alive, the brother likewise inherits everything except whatever remains in that living sister's possession, in whatever amount results from the accounting. Ibid.
