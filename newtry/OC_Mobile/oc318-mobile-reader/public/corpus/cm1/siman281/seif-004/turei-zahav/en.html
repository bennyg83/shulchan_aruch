(That he had at the time of the gift.) Mordechai at the end of Perek Yesh Nochalin concludes: the rule concerns known property that Reuven possessed at the time of the gift, such as specific items known through witnesses to have been his then, but not property concerning which there is doubt whether it came into his possession only afterward. Darchei Moshe cites this here and writes that where it is not known, the recipient receives nothing. I am surprised by the Mechaber's ruling at the end of siman 250 that we say, "Here it was found, and here it was," regarding property, which is exactly this case. Necessarily these authorities disagree. I am also surprised that the Rama said nothing there, merely referring to another siman. I already wrote in siman 60 what can be said from there to reconcile the end of siman 250. But Mordechai's ruling here cannot be reconciled with it. One must say that Mordechai in Maharam's name disagrees with Rashba cited by the Beit Yosef at the end of siman 250. Therefore, as a matter of practical law, it appears that we should rule as Darchei Moshe writes in siman 251 in the name of Maharik, root 94, and Maharam Padua, siman 52: where major authorities dispute the matter, property is not removed from the heirs, and the recipient must bring proof. This is subject to what the Rama writes in siman 60, seif 6, in our master's name, where I gave distinctions, etc. The clear rule is that whenever a person admits that he made a gift, we assume he made it in a legally effective manner.
