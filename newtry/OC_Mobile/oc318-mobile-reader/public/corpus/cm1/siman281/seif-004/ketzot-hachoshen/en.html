But regarding a firstborn, if he said that he shall inherit as an ordinary son. Maharit ruled in a case where the firstborn son died during the father's lifetime and left a son. The grandfather later directed that the grandson of the firstborn and the surviving younger son divide the estate equally. Maharit held that the grandson still takes the firstborn double portion, because a father cannot alter firstborn rights merely through inheritance language. Ramban, however, writes in his Torah commentary that the prohibition against displacing the firstborn applies only while the firstborn son himself is alive. If the firstborn died during the father's lifetime, although his firstborn share can ordinarily pass through him to his descendants, the grandfather may now allocate his estate by ordinary inheritance rules as though no living firstborn remained. Ramban derives this from the verse's phrase "in the presence of the firstborn." Accordingly, in Maharit's case the grandfather's equal division should stand. Since Maharit did not cite Ramban's explicit ruling, it appears that had he seen it he would not have disagreed.
