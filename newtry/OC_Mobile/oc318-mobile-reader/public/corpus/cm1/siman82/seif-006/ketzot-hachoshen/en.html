He should pay and afterward make his claim. I was uncertain where the borrower says, "You hold property of mine equal to this debt," while admitting that the document itself has not been paid. Must the creditor first swear, even though the borrower does not plead repayment? Shiltei Gibborim, in the discussion of giving priority to the claimant's case, distinguishes. If Reuven sues Shimon on a document for one hundred and Shimon counters, "You have a particular object or parcel of mine," beit din says, "Pay the document you admit, and afterward sue him for your object." But if Shimon claims that Reuven owes him another sum of money suitable to satisfy the document, the matter is treated like a claim that the document has already been discharged to that extent. Then if he demands an oath, the creditor does not collect until he swears, just as with an ordinary plea of payment. Accordingly, where the counterclaim is a specific object or land, the creditor need not swear before collecting the document because the borrower's own claim does not constitute payment of the monetary debt. In an oral loan, however, even a claim to a specific object can be believed up to its value where the possessor has an appropriate migo, no worse than one holding collateral. See siman 59, seif katan 1, and siman 72, seif katan 29.
