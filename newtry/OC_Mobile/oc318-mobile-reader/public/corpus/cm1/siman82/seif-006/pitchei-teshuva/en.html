As though he said, 'I did not repay.' See Baer Heitev, which cites the Bach's question, what novelty is being taught here? See Responsa Panim Meirot II, siman 86, by my ancestor, which answers well. The novelty is that one might have thought that here, where witnesses testify that payment was made, the claimant should be made to swear. This differs from the chapter Shevuat HaDayanim and above at the beginning of siman 79, where the witnesses themselves say that he paid and therefore we cannot accept his oath that he did not pay against two witnesses who testify otherwise. It also teaches that here, after payment, he may impose a general ban even where he says that the alleged prior payment never occurred. Although in siman 79 he cannot impose such a ban, the reason there is that he cannot place a ban upon one who, according to the witnesses, unquestionably took the money lawfully; liability follows there only because a person's own admission is stronger than one hundred witnesses. Here the situation is different. See there.
