We do not remove it from him. See Tumim. I concluded that for an ordinary person, although we do not remove seized property, we nevertheless excommunicate him if he refuses the required oath. For a Torah scholar it requires study whether excommunication applies and whether he is called a transgressor. This special uncertainty concerns only this type of oath. For an oath concerning a partially impaired document, or one collecting from orphans, even a Torah scholar is excommunicated if he refuses according to all views. Likewise with a hesset oath, there is no distinction between a Torah scholar and any other person. I further concluded that seizure helps only if it occurred before beit din formally ruled that he must swear. After such a ruling, seizure does not help. For a Torah scholar, therefore, beit din should avoid issuing the oath ruling prematurely so that he still has an opportunity to seize. Every seizure mentioned here includes seizure in the presence of witnesses.
