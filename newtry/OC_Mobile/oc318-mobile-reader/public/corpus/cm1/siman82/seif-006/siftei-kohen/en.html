We do not administer an oath to him. This is so as not to demean his honor by suspecting that he seeks to collect with a paid note. If, however, he seized property belonging to the borrower, we do not remove it from him. We leave it with him without an oath and do not tell him, “You are obligated to swear”; once he seized it, his seizure is effective. Gedulei Terumah writes likewise at the end of page 103. Certainly we do not place him under a ban, as Bach also writes. With an ordinary person, however, although we do not descend to his property to remove what he seized, we nevertheless place him under a ban, as Tur writes below in siman 108, seif 6. This properly resolves the words of Tur, unlike Beit Yosef and unlike Sema, seif katan 22, who writes that even an ordinary person who seized and refused to swear is neither dispossessed nor placed under a ban, and that there is no distinction between a Torah scholar and another person. This is incorrect. Sema, Darkei Moshe, Beit Yosef, Bach, and the other later authorities overlooked the explicit words of Rosh in the chapter Chezkas HaBatim, siman 18: we do not remove the property because we do not descend to his assets, but we do place him under a ban. There is no doubt that Tur below in siman 108 follows those words of Rosh. Rosh in the chapter HaKosev, concerning the statement that a clever litigant can bring the matter to a Torah oath, also proves that even from an ordinary person we do not remove the property. Sema's further view that Tur would not place an ordinary person under a ban is likewise incorrect, as is clear from Rosh and Tur. Sema distinguishes collection from orphans, claiming that the law there is stricter for their benefit, but this too is untenable, because all these oaths share the same law. Gedulei Terumah and Bach explicitly rule that here too he is placed under a ban, and Beit Yosef implies the same. Ran in the chapter HaKosev also states explicitly that when she first seized the property, although it is not removed from her, she is nevertheless placed under a ban. This is the correct ruling. See below, siman 87, seifim 9 and 10, and what I write there. Every seizure discussed here is effective even when made before witnesses.
