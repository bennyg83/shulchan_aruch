But if he seized property of the borrower, etc. The Tur in seif 3 writes that even an ordinary person who refuses to swear and then seizes property of the borrower is not dispossessed, because the document retains its presumption of being unpaid even though the lender has not sworn. The Baal HaTerumot appears to agree, and the Beit Yosef writes that this may also be the Rosh's view. I demonstrated in Perishah and Derishah that the Tur treats this as the straightforward rule even for an ordinary person, and no ban is imposed upon one who seized. The distinction between a Torah scholar and another person is only this: if the borrower is prudent and demands that an ordinary lender either swear and collect or return the document so that he cannot seize property later, the borrower is heard. With a Torah scholar, however, beit din does not intervene even to that extent. He may retain his document, and if an opportunity later arises to seize the borrower's property, he may do so without an oath. It is possible that the other authorities agree. The Mechaber also indicates this view below in siman 87:10, where he cites an opinion that the same law applies to one who seized, without limiting it to a Torah scholar.
