Seized. The Sema writes: It appears to me that there is no distinction here between a Torah scholar and another person. Rather, if an ordinary person is prudent and the borrower claims that the lender should swear and collect or return the note so that he cannot seize the borrower's property today or tomorrow, we heed the borrower. With a Torah scholar, however, we do not entertain even this demand. If he wishes, he may retain his note, and when he is able to seize property of the borrower he may seize it and retain it without an oath. It is possible that the same law applies according to the other authorities as well. Although the Mechaber states the rule here without qualification, he revealed his view in siman 87, seif 10, where he writes that some say the same rule applies where one seized property, without mentioning a Torah scholar. See there. See the Shach and Taz for their discussion.
