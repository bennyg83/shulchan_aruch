[Shulchan Aruch] And this appears reasonable. Note: See the Taz, whose view is that the Shulchan Aruch rules this way only regarding the oath that a creditor has not been paid, which is a leniency because if the borrower does not demand the oath, beit din does not impose it. But with other claimants who swear and collect, such as one who admits partial payment on his document, even the Shulchan Aruch agrees that if the lender is disqualified from taking an oath, the borrower takes a hesset oath and is exempt. See there. The Shach’s view does not appear to be so, and see also the Sema there.
