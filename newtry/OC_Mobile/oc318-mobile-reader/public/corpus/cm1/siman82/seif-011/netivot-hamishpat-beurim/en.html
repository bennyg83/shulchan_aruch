This rule. Tumim derives the rule that a public report contradicting the claim does not defeat the migo from siman 150 seif 5, where one who possessed land for three years is believed to say that he purchased it even though there is a contrary public report. This is difficult, because there the claim of purchase is a strong claim even without a migo once he has three years of chazakah. But a claim that is not believed without a migo may perhaps not be sufficient to contradict a public report. Tumim nevertheless brings proof from Bava Batra 135, where even regarding yibbum the woman is permitted and we do not take account of the report. The Gemara says that only concerning a married woman do we take account of it because of the special stringency of that prohibition. This indicates that it is only a stringency in the law of a married woman, while in monetary law, at least for retaining possession, it appears that we do say a migo even against a public report.
