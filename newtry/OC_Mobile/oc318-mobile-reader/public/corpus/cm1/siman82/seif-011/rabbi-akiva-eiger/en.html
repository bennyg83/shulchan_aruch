[Sema, letter 34] The lender must take an oath. Note: Why is he not believed by means of a migo that he could claim there was no interest at all, according to those who hold in siman 236 that a migo may exempt one from an oath? Nor is this a migo used to extract money, since he is not collecting by virtue of the migo, but rather uses the migo only to avoid an oath. In my humble opinion, the answer is that if he were believed without an oath that the transaction was permitted, he would naturally prefer to make that claim, for once the borrower pays, the lender could not then be compelled to take a heset oath, since he would be believed without an oath by the presumption that a person does not abandon a permitted course and consume something prohibited. But if he advances the other claim, he will have to take a heset oath after payment, as the Shach writes in seif katan 23.
