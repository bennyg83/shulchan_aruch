Tumim writes that if one person seizes another's property and says, "You hold my deposit," while the other denies holding any deposit but has a migo that he could have claimed unavoidable accident, the possessor's migo should defeat the seizure. Tumim reasons that if the possessor had actually pleaded unavoidable accident, the seizer could not hold the property merely out of doubt. In his view the possessor's current denial should therefore remain effective because the seizer's possession is only by self help. In my view this is incorrect where the seizure itself occurred before witnesses. The reason a migo cannot ordinarily extract is the strength of the opponent's monetary possession. But one who seized another's property before witnesses does not acquire a legitimate monetary presumption through that act. He is a seizer whose possession is itself under challenge. The original custodian, who has a valid migo to maintain the prior legal state, remains stronger. This accords with the Shach in siman 90, seif katan 22. If, however, the depositor seized without witnesses, the result changes. The depositor now has his own migo that he never seized or that the object was his. The custodian likewise has a migo of unavoidable accident. Since both have migo and the depositor is presently in possession, we do not remove the property from him. Thus an unwitnessed seizure can be effective in the face of competing migo, but a witnessed seizure does not convert the seizer into the stronger possessor.
