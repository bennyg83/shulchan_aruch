Even though it contains a credibility clause, etc. In the glosses of Rabbi Akiva Eiger, of blessed memory: Although a claim of waiver is a weak claim and one is believed upon it only through a migo that he could have claimed payment, as stated above in siman 70, seif 1, in the Rama, that limitation concerns believing him so as to exempt himself. To require the other party to swear, a claim of waiver is effective even without a migo. Accordingly, even within the term he may require the creditor to swear that he did not waive the debt, because with respect to waiver there is no distinction between before and after maturity, as the Mordechai writes at the beginning of Bava Batra. The view of several poskim that one cannot require an oath on a claim of payment within the term and cannot rely upon a migo that he could have claimed waiver must therefore be explained by saying that we do not employ a migo merely to impose an oath, as Beit Shmuel, siman 96, seif katan 8, writes. See what I wrote in Pitchei Teshuva there, seif katan 4. Yet a difficulty remains according to Ramban, who holds here that one can require the creditor to swear that he did not waive even without a migo. Why then do heirs collect within the term without an oath? We should advance for the debtor the possibility that their father waived the debt, particularly according to Ramban's own view below in siman 108 that beit din advances even an uncommon claim. Since their father could have required an oath that he had not waived, we should advance that claim against the heirs as well. It is strained to say that they must in fact swear that he did not waive, and that 'collect without an oath' means only without swearing that the debt was not repaid. This requires study.
