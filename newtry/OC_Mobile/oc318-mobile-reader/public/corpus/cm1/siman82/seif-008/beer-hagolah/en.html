There in the name of the Rif, as he writes in a responsum, and in the name of the Rambam at the beginning of chapter 17 of Hilkhot Malveh VeLoveh, who holds that this is the primary ruling. Rav Hai likewise writes so in a responsum. The Maggid Mishneh writes there that this is the heirs' oath mentioned in the Mishnah, Shevuot 45a. The Gemara's conclusion there that heirs collect without an oath applies where the debtor did not demand, "Let them swear to me." If he does demand it, they take the heirs' oath, because had he made the same demand against their father, the father would have been required to swear. As for the Gemara's interpretation, "so too the heirs from the heirs," etc., it does not establish the Mishnah as a case where the borrower specifically demanded that the heirs swear, because the Mishnah's wording implies that they swear even without the opposing party demanding it, parallel to the cases of one who partially impairs a ketubah and one who collects from encumbered property, where they swear even without such a demand.
