We swore that we would not have commanded a father and that he did not tell us that this bar is wild and we were when he claims to be sued to me, but I would not tell her a book that enlightens their eyes, and that I called Cohen:
That the defendant swears, etc. This is treated like a Torah-level oath in which, when the primary party cannot swear, the opposing party swears. The same basic rule applies to rabbinic oaths. Why the resulting oath is hesset rather than a Mishnah oath is discussed above in siman 92.
And this is more reasonable. See Tumim. I concluded like the Taz that the Shulchan Aruch's decision here, that the creditor may still collect on the document when the debtor says, 'Swear to me,' applies specifically to this kind of imposed oath. But in cases of an impaired document and similar oaths stated explicitly in the Mishnah, Tur and the Shulchan Aruch in Even HaEzer rule that if the creditor cannot swear he loses the document and the opposing party swears. If both parties are suspected persons, then even for an impaired document and similar cases the creditor collects on the deed without an oath. This applies to an ordinary debt or to the additional ketubah amount. But for the principal one hundred or two hundred of a ketubah, if both are suspected she does not swear and loses. See Tumim for the reasoning.
