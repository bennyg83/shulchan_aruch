If. As stated in chapter Kol HaNishba'in. Likewise heirs, etc. Although the Gemara there says, "If we say the lender..." that refers to a case where the debtor did not demand an oath, as stated there: "now their father..." So too the Mishnah's wording, "and likewise the orphans," means that wherever their father would have had to swear, they too swear. It is not limited to collection from heirs. The same applies to partial repayment and the other cases, as stated there.
If the lender was... some say. As stated in Shevuos 41a: the practical difference concerns the opposing party, etc., and the enactment, etc.
And some say. They explain that the Gemara's reference to an enactment means that the Sages restored the creditor to the underlying law, so he collects without an oath. They disagree with Rambam only regarding the oath attached to collection on a document. Regarding other claimants who ordinarily may collect only by swearing, they agree with him, as stated in siman 92. See Rashi there, s.v. "the enactment..." Likewise in Bava Metzia 5a, in the incident of the shepherd, were it not for Rabbi Chiya's rule the claimant would have been entirely exempt. See there.
