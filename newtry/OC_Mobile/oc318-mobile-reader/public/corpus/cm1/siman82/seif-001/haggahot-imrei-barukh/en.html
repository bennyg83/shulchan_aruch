(Shach, seif katan 1) All of these are erroneous words. Note: I am ignorant and do not understand. Gedulei Terumah himself first wrote that from Rava we learn that according to the view that one who admits he wrote a document need not authenticate it, the borrower is believed to say, "the document was given in trust," whereas according to the view that it still requires authentication, just as the borrower is believed to say "paid," so too he should be believed to say "given in trust." Gedulei Terumah's dispute with the Beit Yosef begins only with the Beit Yosef's description of this as an explicit statement in the second chapter of Ketubot. It was not stated explicitly there; it is only inferred. Gedulei Terumah then questions whether, as a matter of law, even according to the view that a document acknowledged by the borrower still requires authentication, he might nevertheless not be believed to say it was given in trust, just as witnesses are not believed to say, "our words were given in trust," because witnesses do not sign upon wrongdoing. Likewise, according to the Ran, witnesses who say, "our words were given in trust," are not believed, even though the Ran holds that a document acknowledged by the borrower still requires authentication. See Tosafot there, passage beginning "say," and their question whether Rav Huna might be speaking of a protest or a trust document even according to the Ran. From this Gedulei Terumah proves that according to the Ran the status of the witnesses and borrower is the same: just as witnesses are not believed to say "given in trust," neither is the borrower. This is the substance of Gedulei Terumah's words. When he says the Ran's wording is awkward, his intent is merely that the Ran should not have written that this follows from the Gemara's conclusion, but rather that it follows from Rava, unlike the final establishment of Rav Ashi.
