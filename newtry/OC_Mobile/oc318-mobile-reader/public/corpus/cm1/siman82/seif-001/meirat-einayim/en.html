He is believed when he claims, 'I paid.' The Tur explains that an unauthenticated document is merely a shard of pottery.
The lender relies on the borrower to authenticate it, and the borrower's own admission gives the document whatever force it has.
Therefore he is believed through the principle that the mouth that prohibited is the mouth that permitted, with a migo that he could have said the document was forged.Even if it contains a credibility clause, etc.
This too follows from the same reason. He has a migo that he could have said the entire document, including its credibility clause, was forged.'It was given in trust,' or 'I wrote it intending to borrow,' etc.
'It was given in trust' means that the document was handed over conditionally and no actual loan occurred.
'I wrote it intending to borrow' means that it was prepared in anticipation of a loan that never ultimately took place.
In an unauthenticated document such defenses can be effective through the same migo.
