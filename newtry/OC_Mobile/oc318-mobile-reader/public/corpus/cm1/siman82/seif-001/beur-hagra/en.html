And even if... For he is believed even with the claim that the document was one of trust. That is equivalent to claiming that a document was paid where the creditor had granted him contractual credibility. See seif 10, and as stated there in Kesubos: if we say that the borrower says, etc.; rather, the case is where the borrower says, etc. This is the meaning of "the same law applies... or I wrote..." Meaning, although Bava Metzia 13a says that one does not write documents that do not effect an immediate acquisition, he is nevertheless believed here, because this claim is no worse than claiming that it was a document of trust, as stated there concerning witnesses who say, "It was a document of trust..."
Or upon a condition... This is certainly so, because even the witnesses themselves are believed to say this, as stated there in the question, etc. Likewise, the borrower is believed even when the document has been validated, as stated above in seif 12.
Or if he said... Meaning, although the Gemara there says there is a chazakah that witnesses do not sign, and the witnesses are not believed even where their handwriting is not otherwise verified, as explained in the Mishnah there, nevertheless the borrower's claim is no worse than a claim that the document was one of trust, as above.
And if... Meaning, although the borrower already took a shevuas hesses when he denied the entire claim, or a Torah oath when he admitted part, the creditor may nevertheless collect with the document, as stated in Bava Kamma 106a: "Now, Rav does not hold..."
He initially said... some say... As stated in Bava Metzia 17a in the incident of Shabtai, etc.
And he is not believed... This follows the law of one established as a denier, who is not believed even afterward. He likewise cannot claim that he had already paid even if he brings witnesses to that effect, as stated above in siman 79. See there.
And some disagree. Where he explains his earlier statement, he may change his claim, as stated in Bava Basra 31a. The first opinion holds that this is analogous to saying, "you have nothing of mine."
