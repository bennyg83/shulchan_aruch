(In the Shulchan Aruch, seif 1.) "I repaid," and he is believed. Meaning, he is believed through a migo that the document was forged, under the principle that the same mouth that prohibited is the mouth that permits. It appears obvious to me that this applies specifically when, at the very moment he admits that he wrote it, he says within the same immediate span of speech, "but I repaid." Otherwise it is a retroactive migo, which we do not apply, as Tosafos write in Perek HaKotev. See what I wrote at the end of siman 75. The borrower must swear. The lender need not first invalidate the document. On the contrary, the lender may later authenticate it even though the borrower already swore that he repaid, unlike what the Bach writes in this siman that the document must first be nullified.
