Believed. At the end of siman 46, witnesses who say, "Our signature was given on a trust document," or, "We were minors," are not believed because they would be declaring themselves wicked, and a person cannot make himself a wicked person. Here, however, it is the borrower who claims that the document was a trust document, and the document cannot presently be authenticated because the witnesses are absent. The borrower is believed through his migo. If the witnesses later appear and say, "This is our handwriting, and it was a trust document," the document becomes authenticated; their additional self-incriminating assertion does not invalidate the signature. The Taz adds that the borrower must make his alternative claim of payment immediately when admitting that he wrote the document. Otherwise it would be a retroactive migo, which is not recognized. He also rules that the borrower must swear, and the lender need not first cancel the document, because the lender may later authenticate it even after the borrower has sworn that he paid, contrary to the Bach. See also simanim 79 and 99 and the cited responsa.
And collects. Once authenticated, the lender may collect even from purchasers who bought property during the interim when the borrower was claiming that the document had been paid. Those purchasers caused their own loss by buying despite the possibility that the document would later be authenticated, and once authenticated its force is revealed retroactively. Sema. The Shach rejects the Bach's view that the lender must first cancel the document before the borrower takes the payment oath. See siman 87, seif 33.
I paid. The Sema explains that this includes a claim of payment made even after the document was authenticated, as stated in siman 79, seif 7. See the Shach.
Regarding the loan. If the borrower originally said, "There never was such a loan," as in seif 4, all agree that one who says "I never borrowed" is treated as one who says "I never paid." Sema. See also siman 69, seif 2.
