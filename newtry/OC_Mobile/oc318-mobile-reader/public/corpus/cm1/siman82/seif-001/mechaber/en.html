The law of an unauthenticated document, or an authenticated document against which the borrower claims that it was a trust document, was paid, or involves interest; the law of a condition in a document; and the oath. It contains 13 se'ifim.
If a lender produces an unauthenticated document and does not produce witnesses to authenticate it, and the borrower admits that he wrote it but claims, 'I paid,' the borrower is believed. Even a clause granting the lender credibility does not help. The same applies to any claim that would invalidate the document, such as, 'It was delivered in trust,' 'I wrote it in order to borrow but never borrowed,' or, 'It was made subject to a condition, and the condition was not fulfilled.' {Rama: He is also believed if he says, 'I was a minor when the document was written.'} If the lender subsequently finds witnesses who authenticate the document in beit din, it has the law of any other document and he may collect with it. {Rama: If the borrower first says, 'The document is forged,' and after the lender brings witnesses to authenticate it says, 'It was paid,' some say he has been established as a denier and is not believed. By saying it was forged, he was effectively saying that he never borrowed and therefore cannot later say that he paid. Others disagree and say he is believed, because he did not deny the loan itself; he only said the document was forged, meaning that the lender should first authenticate it and then litigate with him.}
