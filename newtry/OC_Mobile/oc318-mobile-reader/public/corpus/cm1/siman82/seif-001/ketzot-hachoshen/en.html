For example, an accommodation document. The Shach writes that the borrower is believed not only where he says the document was an accommodation unknown to the witnesses, but even where he says the witnesses themselves knew it was an accommodation. Although on his account the witnesses acted improperly, he is still believed because he has a migo. He seeks support from Nimukei Yosef concerning one who says he was a minor when the document was signed. This proof is not compelling. In the minor case the defendant need not say the witnesses knew he was a minor. He can say they failed to check and mistakenly assumed he was an adult. Nimukei Yosef itself explains that the ordinary presumption that witnesses do not sign until they verify adulthood is only a presumption, and a migo can overcome it because not all presumptions are equally strong and money remains with its possessor. But if the defendant says the witnesses actually knew he was a minor, he is accusing them of knowingly signing falsely. That is not merely contradicting a presumption. It is a claim against the very trustworthiness of two Torah witnesses. The same should apply to an accommodation document. If he says the witnesses did not know of the accommodation, his migo can be heard. If he says they knew it was an accommodation and nevertheless signed, he is accusing the witnesses of wrongdoing. This resembles a migo against witnesses, which ordinarily does not work, even where the identity of the witnesses themselves is known only through his own admission. We see similarly that if one says, "These signatures are genuine, but the witnesses are robbers," he cannot use a migo to defeat their testimony. One might distinguish that signing an accommodation document knowingly does not necessarily disqualify the witnesses from all testimony, as I wrote in siman 35, seif katan 4. But even if it does not create formal witness disqualification, it is still wrongful conduct. A litigant should not be believed through migo to accuse two trusted witnesses of such wrongdoing. Therefore only the claim that the witnesses were unaware, not that they knowingly participated, should be accepted through migo.
