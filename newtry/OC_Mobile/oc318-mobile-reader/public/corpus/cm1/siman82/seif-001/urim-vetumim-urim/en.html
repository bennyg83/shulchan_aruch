Even if a credibility clause is written in it. He still has a migo that he could have claimed that the document is forged. If the document is forged, the credibility clause within it is likewise void.
It was an accommodation document, and I wrote it intending to borrow. See what I wrote at the beginning of siman 46, where I explained these pleas at length. All this is only where he makes the qualifying plea immediately, within the permissible interval after acknowledging that the document itself is genuine. Once that interval passes, he can no longer raise a plea that destroys the document, because that would be a retroactive migo. Taz, and this is clear.
I was a minor. Although witnesses ordinarily sign only for an adult, he is nevertheless believed by virtue of his migo. See the general rules of migo concerning a migo against a presumption.
He is believed. Accordingly, even if he says that it was an accommodation document and that the witnesses knew this, he remains believed. See Tumim, where I brought proof from the Gemara.
It is like other documents. See Tumim. The principal ruling is that although the debtor is presently believed with an oath, he cannot say, "I will not swear until you tear up your document, lest tomorrow you find witnesses who authenticate it and I then be forced to pay." He must swear now. If witnesses are later found who authenticate the document, he must pay despite his prior oath; that oath does not protect him against subsequently established authentication. See there.
And he collects with it. He may even collect from purchasers who bought property after the debtor swore and was exempted by beit din, but before witnesses later appeared to authenticate the document. The purchasers cannot say, "We reasonably relied on the debtor's discharge," because they should have anticipated that evidence authenticating the document might later be found. They caused their own loss. Sema.
And he is not later believed to say, "I paid." If he brings witnesses who testify that he paid before he claimed the document was forged, their testimony does not help because by alleging forgery he effectively admitted that he had not paid. If they testify that payment occurred afterward, they are believed. If he has no witnesses and merely claims that he paid afterward, he cannot impose any oath upon the lender. Sema and Shach.
Afterward, etc. This limitation concerns the borrower himself. As against purchasers, however, if there are witnesses that the debt was paid, the lender cannot collect. The borrower's own statements cannot prejudice purchasers. Even an express admission by the borrower that he did not pay is ineffective against purchasers. Likewise, the lender must swear the ordinary oath required before collecting from encumbered property, even though the borrower himself was established as a false denier, because that personal status does not affect purchasers.
