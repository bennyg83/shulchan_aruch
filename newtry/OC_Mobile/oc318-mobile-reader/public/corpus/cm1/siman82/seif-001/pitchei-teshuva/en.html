Responsa Noda BiYehudah, siman 12, writes that according to the Shach’s distinction between this case and the case in siman 58 from the Ran, it would seem that where the claimant will later require seizure, the opposing party may withhold his oath until the claimant returns the document or otherwise prevents future seizure. Noda BiYehudah limits that implication. In the case of “I applied it to another debt” in siman 58, the issue is a genuine uncertainty of law and seizure is effective even in the presence of witnesses. The party can therefore say, “I will not swear until you return my document, so that you cannot later seize from me even before witnesses.” But with ordinary seizure without witnesses, which can always be effective in appropriate circumstances, it is obvious that one cannot postpone a required oath merely from fear that the other side may later seize secretly. Otherwise every hesset oath and even many Torah oaths would be nullified, since the opposing party could always potentially seize later without witnesses. We do not require a litigant to bind himself never to make such a seizure. See there further.
