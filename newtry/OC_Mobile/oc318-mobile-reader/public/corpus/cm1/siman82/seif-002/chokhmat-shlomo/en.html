Regarding the laws of migo, letter 9. Note: Concerning a claim made on the basis of an assumption that may be mistaken, see what I wrote, with Hashem’s help, as a general principle and as a resolution of Tosafot’s difficulty in Yevamot, chapter HaIsha Shalom, in my seventh-edition work on Even HaEzer, siman 56. See there carefully. There is also a difficulty in the sugya of Bava Batra concerning one who deposited property with another under a written document, where it appears that Rav Chisda agrees that we do not say migo against witnesses. As to the passage in the third chapter of Bava Batra, page 33, where according to Rav Chisda we do say migo against witnesses, see what I wrote to the community of Wengrow in the addenda to my sefer Stam. We rule that a migo asserted by two people together is generally not accepted. Why, then, in the case where an agent becomes a witness do we accept a migo involving two people? Also, regarding the doctrine of “the mouth that prohibited is the mouth that permitted,” whether it is based upon migo or is an independent rule, see my eighth-edition work on Even HaEzer, siman 124, where I discussed all these points.
