And if the document... if there is... As stated in chapter HaKosev, that even one who partially admits payment may collect without an oath. The same applies where the debtor says, "Swear to me," as stated in chapter HaDayanim. This is the meaning of "and even if..." Sefer HaTerumos writes that even a general grant of credibility suffices to exempt the creditor from an oath, because without an oath he would otherwise collect.
Nevertheless... As stated there on 19a: the case is where the lender says, etc., and for example where this would prejudice others, etc.
And the earlier creditor... As stated there on 93b: the first woman swears, etc., and the fourth, etc. Even Ben Nanas disagrees only where one field is discovered not to belong to the debtor, or because of the interests of orphans, as explained there in the Gemara.
And even if... Meaning, not only with a general grant of credibility, which exempts the creditor only vis a vis the debtor himself, as stated in chapter HaKosev regarding the language, "a vow..." but not vis a vis the debtor's heirs, until he expressly writes, "and not to those who come through his authority," etc. Rather, even if he expressly wrote language effective against heirs and successors, it does not help against purchasers and creditors, as above.
And this is... but... See Beer HaGolah.
Even if. As stated in Kesubos 87b, because one who repays is careful, etc. Although Tosafos there, s.v. "because one who repays..." write that this applies specifically to one who partially admits repayment, their point is only that the two cases are not entirely identical. Nevertheless the same rationale must apply even where he claims full repayment. Otherwise, why would the Sages impose an oath when the debtor says, "Swear to me"? Although at the end of Bava Kamma 118a the Gemara says, "the first clause too, where he did not..." that concerns an oral obligation. With a document we do not nullify it merely because of an uncertain concern, for we preserve its chazakah. The principle is, "Why is your document in my possession?" as stated in Bava Basra 70a.
