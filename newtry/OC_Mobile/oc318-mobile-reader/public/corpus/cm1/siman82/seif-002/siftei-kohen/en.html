If the instrument contains an express credibility clause, etc. The Tur writes in the name of Baal HaTerumot that where the first creditor has an express credibility clause, he need not swear. It appears that just as a credibility clause is ineffective against purchasers, it is likewise ineffective against a later creditor. This is also stated in Beit Yosef. I am surprised where this is found in Baal HaTerumot. To the contrary, at the end of gate 21 and in gate 44, part 4, Baal HaTerumot expressly equates purchasers and later creditors and rules that credibility does not prevail against either. He must be speaking even of an express credibility clause, because his proof is Rif’s statement that credibility helps only when the borrower explicitly trusted the creditor against his heirs, but not where another creditor, a wife’s ketubah, or a purchaser is involved. An ordinary credibility clause does not even prevail against heirs. Beit Yosef also writes that the Tur’s rule requiring an oath where there is a later creditor follows Ritva and Rif, while Rosh records dissent and recommends stringency because fraud is common. Yet the Tur at that point is discussing an ordinary credibility clause, since he introduces express credibility only afterward. Concerning ordinary credibility there is no dispute at all: everyone agrees it is ineffective against a later creditor, and indeed even against heirs. Rif and those who disagree with him must be discussing an express credibility clause, as their own words and those of Rosh, Baal HaTerumot, and the other authorities clearly show. The matter requires study.
