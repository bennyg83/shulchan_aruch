The case in which we say that a migo may extract. Where a document supports the claim, the authorities explain that the reason is that a document standing for collection is regarded as though already collected. Tumim explains instead that because the claimant possesses the chazakah created by the document, expressed in the principle "why is your document in my hand?", it is as though an independent chazakah supports him. Tumim asks against the authorities who explain the case of a woman claiming, "I was raped after betrothal," by saying that a migo may extract where one side is definite and the other uncertain: why is that rationale needed when her ketubah document itself supports her? I do not understand the question. Ran writes in the sugya of the open entrance that a ketubah is like a document containing a condition whose fulfillment the claimant must prove. Once the husband discovers an open entrance, she bears the burden of proving that the condition was fulfilled, because it is a condition concerning a past fact, as I explained above in siman 46, seif katan 25. A supporting document helps only where the document would remain fully valid even if the claimant said nothing at all, such as a document in the hands of heirs and similar cases.
