This rule. This is explained in Tosafot in Ketubot and Bava Batra 33 and in many other places, and it is also straightforward logically. If by the claim he presently advances he seeks to collect more than he could have collected under the alternative claim available to him, it is not a migo. Shach cites Riaz regarding one who says, "This is my son," that he is believed because it is within his power to divorce his wife. Although if he actually divorced her he could not thereby permit her to a kohen, while by this statement he does permit her to a kohen, this might appear to show that he accepts a migo for only part of a claim. That is not so. The case is different because Rosh writes in Gittin 54, regarding prohibitions, that a person is believed to say, "Your pure foods became impure," because it was within his power to render them impure. Rosh explains that this is not based on migo, for if he had actually rendered them impure he would have had to pay. Rather, since the matter was within his power, he is regarded as the owner. This is likewise the reasoning of the Shitah Mekubetzet. Similarly, when a husband says, "I divorced her," he is believed as to the future because it is within his power. This too cannot be because of migo, for there is an opinion that even when he says, "I divorced her previously," he is believed from now onward, even though he seeks to exempt himself from past support and has no migo. Further, if he actually divorces her she can produce her get and collect her ketubah, whereas if he merely says, "I divorced her," and she has no get, he can claim that he paid. Rather, when the matter is within his power he is believed because he is treated as the owner, as Rosh writes regarding prohibitions. Therefore, with respect to what is within his power, namely her status vis-a-vis an ordinary Jew, he is believed to say, "This is my son," because in relation to that Jew he is treated as the owner. With respect to a kohen, however, it is not within his power to permit her, so he is not treated as the owner and is not believed. The second case that I wrote in the main text is derived by Tumim from the words of Rashbam cited there.
