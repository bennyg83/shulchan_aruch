(Seif 12) “The borrower takes a heset oath and is exempt.” We do not say that the creditor should swear and collect, because according to his own account there is a defect in this note, namely that it was made subject to a condition. The force of the note has therefore fallen away. For the same reason the Shulchan Aruch later says that a migo is not used to extract money. Although in siman 58 a creditor’s migo is effective when he claims that a payment was applied to another debt, there the note remains fully valid according to the creditor’s own account. Tosafot make this distinction in Bava Batra, s.v. “He had a valid note and it was lost.” The Sema writes here, “See above siman 79 and below siman 93, where a migo is not accepted against a presumption, as explained there.” In siman 157 the Tur cites Rabbeinu Yonah concerning a migo against a presumption.
