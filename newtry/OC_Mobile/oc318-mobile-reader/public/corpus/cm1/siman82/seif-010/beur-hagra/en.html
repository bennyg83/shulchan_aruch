He produced against him... As stated in Kesubos 19a: it is obvious that it is not within his power, etc. It appears that even an oath is unnecessary, since the Gemara later establishes the case as one where the borrower says, etc. Since it does not establish the case as one where the borrower says, "it was paid," this implies that specifically a claim that it was a document of trust is ineffective. One must say that it is ineffective even with an oath. Otherwise the original question, "it is obvious," remains. One cannot say the novelty is merely that the document need not be validated, because then the Gemara should have taught the rule in a case where the borrower says it was paid, as above. The distinction is that a claim of repayment does not nullify the document, because documents are intended to be repaid and even where partial repayment is admitted the Sages imposed an oath only because a person who repays is careful, etc. That does not apply to these other claims. This is why the Mishnah says, "she reduces..."
And if he denied... This applies even where his defense is that the transaction involved ribbis, as stated above in siman 75, seif 8. Although a person cannot render himself wicked, and the borrower says, etc., that principle applies only to testimony, not to monetary claims. As stated at the end of chapter 3 of Kesubos, one who says, "I seduced..." or "I stole..." is liable, and we do not say that a person cannot render himself wicked. Likewise, regarding a claimant, if witnesses to his allegation would have obligated the defendant to pay money, the defendant must swear in response to the claim.
