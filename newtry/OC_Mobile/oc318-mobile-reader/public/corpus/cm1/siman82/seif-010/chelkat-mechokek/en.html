She can compel that the daughter remain with her. One who studies the responsum will see that the authority did not decide this rule absolutely in practice. He writes only that it is not so simple that the law always favors the mother. There is room to say that if beit din sees that it is better for the orphans to remain in their father's household, the court has authority to decide accordingly. The Gemara's rule that a daughter remains with her mother may apply only where the alternatives are equal. It is also possible that if the daughter herself does not wish to remain with her mother, she may choose otherwise. He infers this from Rashi's language. According to this, the Rama's statement here that the mother cannot compel means that she cannot compel the daughter if the daughter does not wish to remain with her. But if the daughter is indifferent, even if beit din thinks it better for her to remain in her father's household, one must deliberate carefully before uprooting the Sages' rule that a daughter remains with her mother merely on the basis of the court's assessment.
