(Ibid., “and careful scribes”) The Tur writes, “There is still room for concern.” The Sema cites the Maharshal that the note must expressly confer credibility against a claim of waiver. This is difficult, for the Tur should then have said that careful scribes indeed write this expressly, since they seek the greatest possible protection. It appears to me that even an express clause does not help, because the debtor can say, “You waived for me the very obligation by which I bound myself not to plead waiver,” comparable to a notice concerning the nullification of a prior notice. In truth, waiver is included in the phrase “in every matter relating to this debt,” but even that does not suffice. The Shulchan Aruch rules leniently because there are other dissenting authorities in any event, as the Rama states.
