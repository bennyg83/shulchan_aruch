A migo to extract. Rama in siman 138 rules that if one person says, "I rented it in front of so and so, who have gone overseas," he can be believed through a migo that he could have said he seized it from the other party. This appears to be a migo used to extract, because the disputed object is presently in the other person's possession. The rule is understandable because the possession there does not have the full strength of an ordinary monetary presumption. The parties had already come before beit din and had been told to divide the water, and the item is now in one person's hands only after that prior judicial posture. We therefore preserve the earlier legal presumption that it belonged to both, and an ordinary migo can operate against the present holder. Another explanation is that the prior judicial ruling itself has the force of a document. Every act of beit din is treated like a written instrument, and a migo can operate where a formal document already stands behind the claimant. Thus the case in siman 138 does not prove a general rule that migo can extract against an ordinary possessor.
