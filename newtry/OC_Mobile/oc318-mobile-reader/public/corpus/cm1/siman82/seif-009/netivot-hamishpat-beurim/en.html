It is explained. The Rosh writes in chapter 8 of HaKotev that she is not believed to say, "It was paid," by a migo that she could have waived it. He explains that even according to those who hold that she need not compensate the purchaser if she waives, not everyone is learned in the law. Shach in siman 66 disagrees, and his view is primary, because otherwise all migo arguments in the Gemara would be undermined by the possibility that a person does not know the law. In my view, even the Rosh agrees to this general principle. The case there is different, because the alternative claim is not a superior claim for her. Waiving the debt would be disadvantageous to her, for why should she place herself into a dispute among the authorities over whether she must compensate the purchaser? Moreover, her statement that the debt was paid is not made for her own benefit but as testimony for the husband, and she is therefore an interested party to the greatest degree. She is afraid to waive because of the dispute among the authorities concerning liability to compensate. But in an ordinary case where the migo rests on a genuinely superior alternative claim, we say migo for every person, in accordance with Shach.
