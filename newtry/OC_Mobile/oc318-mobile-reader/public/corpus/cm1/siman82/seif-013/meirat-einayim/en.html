The borrower claimed against an authenticated document, etc. The Beit Yosef in siman 75 cites Rashba concerning Shimon who produced a promissory note against Reuven.
Reuven claimed it had been paid and brought as proof that Shimon had not listed this debt in an inventory of all his property sworn to the tax collector. Rashba ruled for Shimon, because the omission proves nothing; perhaps he simply forgot to include the debt.And witnesses testify that it was entirely paid, etc.
Where the borrower's own position is inconsistent with the testimony offered on his behalf, the effect depends on whether his own admission legally precludes reliance on that testimony. As explained above, a person's own admission can sometimes be treated like a hundred witnesses against himself, so that evidence of payment may not help where his own words amount to an admission that payment did not occur.
