A migo to leave the land with the possessor. Knesset HaGedolah writes that even without three years of possession, a migo can operate to maintain the land with the present occupant. This follows the ruling that in land we follow Rabbah, who accepts a migo, in Perek Chazakat HaBatim. Tumim adds that the same can apply to the challenger, even though he is not presently occupying the land, because he has the original owner's presumption. I would go further. Even where the challenger lacks both physical possession and the original owner's presumption, a migo can still work in land. Tosafot at the end of Ketubot discusses one who had signed as a boundary witness and later said he repurchased the land. Where his handwriting is not otherwise established, Tosafot believes him through a migo of forgery. The Rosh and Rama in siman 147 rule the same. There the claimant does not necessarily possess the land and can no longer rely on an original owner's presumption, because by admitting that he had earlier designated it as another's boundary he has admitted it previously left his ownership. Yet the migo can still support his claim. The reason is that the rule rejecting migo to extract is driven by the exceptional strength of actual monetary possession. Land is never physically held in one's hand in that same way. Its relevant presumption is the original owner's presumption, which is weaker than actual possession of movables. Therefore a migo can operate against that presumption. As I explained elsewhere, land does not have the same "money under his hand" presumption because it is not literally under the possessor's physical control. The same should apply to movables lying in a public place where neither litigant actually holds them. If one side has only the original owner's presumption and the other has a migo, the migo can overcome that weaker presumption. The special bar against migo to extract applies where the money or object is actually under the opponent's hand.
