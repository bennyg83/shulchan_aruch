The reason. Rashba writes in Responsa, siman 1035, cited briefly by Shach in siman 66, that the uncertainty of a third party custodian is treated like the uncertainty of the owner. Since the person who entrusted the item relied upon him, he made him like an actual litigant, so that his uncertainty has the same force as the owner's uncertainty. Therefore, if only one of the parties, the one who lacks a migo, deposited the matter with him, then when the custodian is uncertain it is as though that litigant himself is uncertain, while the other party, who did not entrust it to him, makes a definite claim. This is a case of definite against uncertain, in which we say that a migo can serve even to extract. If, however, both parties entrusted the matter to him, it is as though both are making uncertain claims according to Rashba's principle that the custodian's uncertainty is equivalent to that of the litigant. It is established that when property is in the hands of a third party custodian, both parties are considered in possession of it, as Tosafot write in Bava Metzia 2, s.v. veyachloku. When both are considered in possession, a migo does not help, because it would be a migo used to extract, as Tosafot write there, s.v. zeh omer chatzyah sheli. See there.
