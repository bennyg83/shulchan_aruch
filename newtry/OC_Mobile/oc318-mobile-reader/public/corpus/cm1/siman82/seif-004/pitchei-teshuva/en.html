As though he said, 'I did not repay.' See Baer Heitev, which cites the Bach's question, what novelty is being taught here? See Responsa Panim Meirot II, siman 86, by my ancestor, which gives a cogent answer. The novelty is substantial: one might have thought that here, where witnesses testify that payment was made, the claimant should be made to swear, unlike the chapter Shevuat HaDayanim and above at the beginning of siman 79, because there the witnesses say that he paid and therefore we cannot accept his oath that he did not pay against two witnesses who testify that he did. The ruling also teaches that here, after payment, he may impose a general ban even if he says that the supposed earlier payment never occurred. Although in siman 79 he cannot impose such a ban, the reason there is that he cannot place a ban upon someone for taking from him unlawfully when, according to the witnesses, the recipient certainly took lawfully. He is required to pay only because his own admission is stronger than one hundred witnesses. Here the situation is different. See there.
