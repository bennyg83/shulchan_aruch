As though he said. The Bach asks what this teaches us, since it is an explicit rule in the Gemara and is explained at the beginning of siman 79. He answers that a written note is worse in this regard, and therefore in siman 79 the lender cannot even impose a general cherem, whereas here the principal novelty is that he may impose a cherem before payment. It appears, the Bach continues, that all the more so he may sue him after payment and require a hesset oath. The Shach writes that this is incorrect. Rather, he cannot require a hesset oath after payment, because by claiming, "It never happened," the borrower has admitted that he did not pay. The Taz likewise challenges the Bach on this point. This is also implied by the language of the Tur and Mechaber, and even more clearly by the Raavad, who write that he may impose a cherem upon anyone who took money from him, which plainly discusses a case after payment. No oath is required, only a general cherem. All this applies where he claims that he paid before he denied the transaction. But if he claims that he paid afterward, the lender may require him to take a hesset oath after payment. See siman 79, seif 8. Shach.
