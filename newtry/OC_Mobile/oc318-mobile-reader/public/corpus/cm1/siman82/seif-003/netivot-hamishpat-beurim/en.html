This rule. This is explained at the end of siman 56, in siman 348 seif 7, and in Responsa Radbaz, siman 134. As for what is explained in siman 108 seif 8, that an heir is not believed to say that the document has been paid even though he has a migo that, had he wished, he could have handed it to the borrower or burned it, it appears that there the case is one in which the document had already been established before beit din. He therefore has no present migo. Only if he had been a third party custodian would he be believed, because he once had a migo, as explained in siman 56. One who is not a third party custodian, however, is believed only when he presently has a migo. Once the document has been established before beit din, he is not believed. This also appears from Sema there, seif katan 29, who writes that here the document had been established before the public. Even if we were to say that the Rosh in his responsum holds that one is not believed even with a migo, as I wrote above in siman 56, nevertheless we do not rule like the Rosh on this point. What I wrote in the main text, that he is not believed to say that the other party owes him by virtue of a migo, is likewise explained in the responsum of Radbaz. Although Radbaz uses the expression "possibly," this is nevertheless the more reasonable conclusion. See what I wrote above at the end of siman 56, seif katan 18, and siman 108 seif 3.
