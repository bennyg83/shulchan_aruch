We tell him, 'Bring his money.' This wording implies that beit din raises this point for the lender even if the lender did not expressly make the argument. The Beit Yosef writes so in the name of Rambam. The Tur's wording, however, does not clearly imply that beit din argues it for him.
Since the Tur does not explicitly present a dispute between himself and Rambam, it appears that even Rambam may mean only a case where the lender is not demanding immediate payment but the borrower is instead demanding that the lender return his document. In that setting beit din can tell the borrower that if he wants the document returned, he must first bring the money that the document secures. This does not necessarily mean that beit din supplies a substantive claim that the lender himself failed to raise.
