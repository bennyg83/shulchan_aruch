We say to him. The wording implies that beit din itself raises this option, while the Tur sounds as though the lender must request it. See Sema. It appears that the matter depends upon the court's assessment of whether the borrower appears financially reliable.
Bring money. The Shach questions whether collateral can substitute for cash. It appears that if the borrower says he has no cash, collateral certainly suffices, because where he lacks cash he may discharge the debt with movable property, as explained above in siman 101, and we do not separately make him swear that he has no cash.
The borrower shall swear that he has none. The Taz questions this. The Shach agrees with the ruling. Since the lender holds a document and by strict law would collect without any oath, while the Sages imposed an additional oath upon him, it is proper that the borrower first swear that he lacks money. This is correct. By contrast, in the cases of a hired worker, a robbed claimant, and similar plaintiffs who swear and collect only by rabbinic enactment, until they take their oath they have no enforceable claim at all. Therefore the defendant need not swear that he lacks funds until they first take their own oath.
