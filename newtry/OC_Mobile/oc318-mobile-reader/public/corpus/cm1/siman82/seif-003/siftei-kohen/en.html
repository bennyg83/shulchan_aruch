["They tell him: bring his money, etc. This implies that even if the creditor did not request this, beit din makes this claim on his behalf. Beit Yosef, seif 8, writes likewise. See Sema, seif katan 17."]
["Bring his money. Below, siman 93, seif 18, the Tur cites a responsum of the Rosh from which it appears that collateral is likewise sufficient. At first glance, the same should apply here. It is possible, however, that there the matter is different, since he does not acquire the right to collect until he swears because the oath has been reversed upon him, whereas here he holds a promissory note and is required to swear only by rabbinic enactment. It may therefore be that he is not required to swear until the debtor specifically brings the money."]
["The borrower shall swear, etc. The Tur writes that the Geonim disagree with this, but I do not know his source. It appears that he derived it from Baal HaTerumot, Gate 2, end of Part 1, citing a responsum of the early authorities. Beit Yosef likewise writes that the Tur's statement in the name of the Geonim, that neither party swears, is written by Baal HaTerumot in Gate 2 in the name of a responsum of the early authorities. In my view, however, that responsum does not disagree with the Rambam. It states an independent rule concerning one of those who swear and collect, and it refers to other cases in which one swears and collects without a document, such as one who was robbed, one who was injured, or a shopkeeper relying on his ledger. In those cases, by strict law they would not collect even with an oath, but the Sages instituted that they swear and collect. Therefore, when the defendant says that he has nothing, since by strict law he is exempt even without an oath and only the enactment of the Geonim requires him to swear, it is certainly reasonable that neither party swears. Maharam of Recanati, ruling 274, likewise writes in the name of the Rif in accordance with that responsum and cites no dissent. This differs from a creditor holding an authenticated note, who by Torah law collects without an oath, while the Sages instituted that he swear. We similarly distinguish below, siman 92, seif 9, between a note and other cases of those who swear and collect regarding one who is suspected of false swearing, and the same applies here. Semag, positive commandment 94, page 177a, also rules like the Rambam. Therefore, the Rambam's ruling appears primary to me, and I believe that the Rambam himself agrees regarding other cases of those who swear and collect, as I have explained. Note carefully. What the Sema writes in seif katan 11, that the later authorities agreed with the Rambam and that Beit Yosef rules accordingly, is not stated that way in Beit Yosef. It appears that the Sema erred in his reading of Beit Yosef. See there."]
