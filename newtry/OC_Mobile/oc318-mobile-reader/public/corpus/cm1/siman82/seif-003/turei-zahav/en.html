(Seif 4) “If the borrower has been established as a denier.” The Tur cites this in the name of the Raavad. My teacher and father-in-law, of blessed memory, asks what this teaches us, since it is an explicit rule of the Talmud, as the Tur wrote at the beginning of siman 79. He answers that here the borrower was established as a denier through the note, and therefore he still has the power at least to impose an unspecified ban, as concluded here. By contrast, at the beginning of siman 79, where he became a denier through contradiction by witnesses, the court restrains the borrower even from imposing an unspecified ban; that is why the Tur did not mention such a ban there. In my humble opinion this is incorrect. Even where the note expressly provides that the creditor may not impose an unspecified ban, as stated in siman 71, seif 6, the rule remains as explained in siman 79. There the Tur writes that the Rif testified that Rav Hai retracted his earlier view that a proven denier is treated like a suspected person, so that the opposing party swears and collects; rather, the creditor has only an unspecified ban against him. This shows that an unspecified ban remains available against every denier discussed in the Talmud. It is therefore clear that every proven denier is subject to an unspecified ban. The Raavad’s principal innovation lies in the words, “and beit din obligated him to pay.” Those words at first appear unnecessary. They teach that even though judgment had already been entered against him, and one might think he could claim that he subsequently paid, he cannot do so. Even with an oral debt, where he first said, “I never borrowed,” and witnesses then testified that he did, he is not later believed to say, “I paid.” All the more so with a written note, he has no right to demand, “Swear to me.” This accords precisely with Sefer HaTerumot: “If beit din obligated him to pay, he cannot say, ‘Swear to me that I did not repay you,’ because we have already said that he is no longer believed to claim payment. How, then, can we administer an oath on a claim of payment?” This is exactly the explanation I have given.
