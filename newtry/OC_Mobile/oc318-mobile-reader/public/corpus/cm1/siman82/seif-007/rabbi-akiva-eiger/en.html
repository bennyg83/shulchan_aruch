[Shulchan Aruch] And the borrower cannot say, “Either swear and collect.” Note: If there is one witness who testifies that the later document has been paid, one may argue that the borrower can say that so long as the holder of the later document has not sworn, the single witness has the force of two, and thus there are effectively two witnesses that it has been paid. Yet one may counter that since the creditor is collecting and there is no Torah oath, the witness does not have the force of two, just as we say regarding land that the rule of the piece of silver of Rabbi Abba does not apply. The same should be true here. Even where there is no land, but there is a lien on movables together with land, the oath remains rabbinic because he is collecting. See below, siman 267, seif 10, and examine this carefully. This entire comment belongs above, seif 2.
