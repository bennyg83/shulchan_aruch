The Mechaber carefully writes 'or without,' because he holds that either formulation by itself is sufficient; it is unnecessary for the document to contain both the absence of conditions and the absence of reservations.

If the witnesses themselves say that there was a condition, and explain that the document's language 'without any reservation' was not intended to negate that condition but merely to say that there was no reservation in the body of the sale once the condition was fulfilled, they may be believed.
The same reasoning can apply here to a loan.
The legal effect therefore depends on whether the alleged condition contradicts the written wording or can coexist with it as an external condition to performance.The broader rule is that a party ordinarily cannot defeat a clear, authenticated document with a claim of an unwritten condition.
But where the document's own witnesses explain what the formula was intended to mean, their testimony may be accepted if it does not make the instrument internally false.
The distinctions parallel those developed above concerning whether witnesses can add or explain a condition after execution.
