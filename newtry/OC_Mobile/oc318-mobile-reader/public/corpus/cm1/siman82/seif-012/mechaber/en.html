If the borrower claims, 'The document was made on condition that I would be exempt if I fulfilled it, and I fulfilled it,' while the lender says there was no condition, then if the document states that it was made without any condition or reservation whatsoever, the borrower is not believed. If it does not state this, the lender swears and collects, even if the document grants him credibility. If the lender admits that it was made on condition but says the borrower has not yet fulfilled the condition, the lender must bring proof. If he has no proof, the borrower takes a heses oath and is exempt. {Rama: The lender is not believed through a migo, because a migo is not invoked to extract money. Others disagree and hold that a migo may be invoked to extract money; see Rivash 336 and note 9. If the borrower has witnesses that the document was made on condition, their testimony is accepted. Even the witnesses who signed the document are believed to say it was conditional, although their signatures can be authenticated from another source, and the borrower is exempt even without an oath. If one witness says it was conditional and one says there was no condition, the borrower takes a heses oath and is exempt.}
