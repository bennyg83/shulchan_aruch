["The creditor swears. He takes an oath resembling a Torah oath and then collects his debt, because this claim does not uproot the essential validity of the note and is comparable to a claim of payment. So writes the Tur in the name of Baal HaTerumot. It appears that they follow their own approach, for immediately afterward they write that witnesses are believed to say that the note was conditional even when their handwriting is authenticated from elsewhere, because a condition is a separate matter and resembles payment, as stated by Baal HaTerumot there. According to what I conclude below, seif katan 34, however, witnesses are not believed to say that there was a condition when their handwriting is authenticated from elsewhere, because they thereby come to uproot their own testimony. It is as though they say that no loan occurred, contrary to what they stated in the note, as the Ran in the second chapter of Kesubos implies according to these authorities. According to that approach, the creditor here likewise need not swear regarding the alleged condition. Note carefully."]
["And if the creditor admits, etc., and the creditor is not believed through a migo, etc. This ruling does not appear correct to me, although the Tur also records it, and it is from Baal HaTerumot, Gate 21. What difference does it make that the creditor admits that the note was made subject to a condition? According to the creditor's account, the note is presently a valid note. The case is exactly comparable to a claim that a payment was applied to another debt, where the creditor is believed even according to the view that a migo is not used to extract money. Let it be no better than a trust note. If the creditor were to say, 'It is true that the note was initially entrusted between us on condition that it take effect only when I lend you money, and I subsequently did lend you the money,' would he not be believed according to all views? We say below, at the end of siman 83, that a trust note is void only where the creditor admits that it remains merely a trust note and that he never subsequently lent on the basis of that note. But where he claims that he later lent the money on the basis of that very note, he is plainly believed. There is no greater condition than this, for until the loan is actually made the note has no effect. All the more so here. Moreover, a trust arrangement is treated more severely everywhere than an ordinary condition, as is clear above, siman 46, seif 37. Witnesses are not believed to say that the note was a trust note once their signatures are authenticated from elsewhere, whereas they are believed to say that it was conditional even when their signatures are authenticated from elsewhere, as the Tur writes here at the end of this siman. Plainly a trust arrangement is more severe because it uproots the note from its inception, whereas with a condition the note itself is a valid note and the condition is a separate matter. Therefore, if with a trust note the creditor is believed when he says that it was made in trust but that he subsequently lent the money on the strength of that note, all the more so with a conditional note he should be believed when he says that the condition was not fulfilled. I would not have had the confidence to disagree with Baal HaTerumot, the Tur, and the Mechaber. However, the Ramban, Rashba, and other later authorities already disagree with the very ruling regarding a forged or trust note stated below at the end of siman 83, and they hold that whenever the creditor has a migo he is believed even though he admits that the note itself was initially ineffective, as I explain there. All the more so here. Therefore it appears clear to me that here the law follows the creditor. See the responsum of Maharit, siman 70, and siman 105, pages 126a, 126b, and 127."]
["For we do not say a migo to extract money. In responsum Maharshach, volume 2, end of siman 228, he asks from Tosafos in the chapter Chazakat HaBatim, who write that even according to the view that a migo is not used to extract money, that applies only where there is no note. Where there is a note, a migo may be used to extract money, as they prove from the case in HaKotev concerning a payment applied to another debt. He leaves this requiring further study. He did not examine Baal HaTerumot, the source of this ruling. Baal HaTerumot himself writes in Gate 21, Part 3, that the creditor is not believed through a migo because a migo is not used to extract money, but that this applies only with a defective or invalid note; with a valid note a migo is effective, as he wrote in Gate 17 concerning a payment applied to another debt. Here too, since the creditor admits that the note was conditional, if the condition was fulfilled the lien never took effect, and the borrower claims that the condition was fulfilled, the note has no effect at all. Therefore the borrower swears and is exempt. Nevertheless, in the preceding seif katan I wrote that in my view this is in fact a valid note. See there. See also the responsum of the Ramban, beginning of siman 100, who likewise writes that although a migo is ordinarily not used to extract money, where there is a valid note it may be used even to extract money. It follows from this that one who is owed money orally in a situation where the debtor cannot claim payment, such as where the creditor said, 'Do not repay me except before witnesses,' or the like, cannot claim that a payment was applied to another debt if the debtor paid him without specifying that it was repayment, even though he could have claimed that the debtor gave it to him as a gift, according to the Mechaber and those who agree with him above, siman 58, seif 2. This is also clear above, siman 70. See what I wrote there in seif katan 16 and seif katan 18. Note carefully."]

["That it was made on condition, etc. The Bach explains that the borrower is prepared to fulfill the condition, or alternatively that there are witnesses that he fulfilled the condition. It appears more correct to explain that the case involves a condition whose performance is imposed upon the holder of the note through an affirmative act, and the borrower is believed to say that the holder did not fulfill the condition. This is also the necessary explanation of the words of the Tur and Mechaber above, siman 46, seif 37, who write, 'There was a condition, and we did not see that the condition was fulfilled,' as I explained there in seif katan 106, where I brought proof from Rashi, Ritva, and Ran. See there."]
["Even if their handwriting, etc. I am surprised that the Mechaber copied the Tur's ruling here without qualification. The Rambam, Ramban, and Rashba disagree and hold that once the witnesses' handwriting is authenticated from elsewhere they are not believed. This is stated in Beit Yosef above, at the end of siman 29, and the Mechaber himself rules this way above at the beginning of the present siman and in siman 46, seif 37. Although the Sema in siman 46, seif katan 63, writes that the Mechaber's rulings do not contradict one another, this is nevertheless strained, as I wrote in siman 46, seif 37, seif katan 115. See there. Moreover, even apart from that, it is difficult that the Mechaber rules without qualification against the Rambam, Ramban, and Raavad. I likewise concluded above in siman 29 that the law follows them, and I showed that Rashi, Rabbeinu Yerucham, Nimukei Yosef, and Ritva agree, and that even the Rosh does not disagree. See there."]

["On condition, etc. From the words of the Ran in the second chapter of Kesubos, concerning one witness who says that she was divorced, it is clear that only where one witness says that the note was conditional do we say that his testimony uproots the note from its inception, because according to him there was never an unconditional loan. For this reason we do not say that both witnesses are testifying to a valid note. If, however, one witness testifies that the debt was later paid, then according to all views both witnesses testify to a valid note, while the one who says it was paid is merely a single witness as to the later payment, even where their handwriting is not authenticated from elsewhere. This is explicit there. The words of the Ramah cited by the Tur at the end of siman 46 imply that even testimony of payment voids the note. Nevertheless, it appears that the Ramah said this only where the witnesses' handwriting is not authenticated from elsewhere. Where their handwriting is authenticated from elsewhere, or the note is otherwise authenticated, it is not reasonable to say that one witness is believed to declare it paid. As the Ran writes regarding witnesses who say, 'Our testimony was conditional,' in the name of the Ramban, it is impossible to say that one witness should be believed regarding an authenticated note to say that it has been paid, for then we would find one witness establishing a monetary result, while the Torah says that one witness rises only to impose an oath. See there."]
