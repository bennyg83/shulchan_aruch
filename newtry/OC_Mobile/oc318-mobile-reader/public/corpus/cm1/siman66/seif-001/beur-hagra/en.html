(Collected note) Documents... See Beer HaGolah. They also prove there from the end of the third chapter of Sanhedrin, where the Gemara asks, "According to whom, Rabbi?" This shows that the Amoraim there themselves do not hold his view. All the more so according to the alternative version there, "they did not trust him..." Moreover, the Gemara does not ask, "According to whom, not Rabbi?" In Yevamos 116a, "And according to Abaye, what concern is there...?" necessarily Rava holds that documents are not acquired by delivery alone. See Tosafos there, s.v. mai, and Kiddushin chapter 2, where the Gemara says, "Alternatively, all agree that Rabbi's view is not accepted, and here they disagree about Rav Pappa..." It follows that Rav Pappa does not follow Rabbi, and the halachah follows Rav Pappa. (End of note.)
Nevertheless he may. As above in siman 64.
Likewise if. From Rabbi Nasan, and specifically if he has no other assets. Bava Kamma 42.
Likewise if he accepted. As above in siman 40, seif 1.
Some say he is not obligated. Bava Batra there regarding "for wrapping," and "for wrapping." Sefer HaTerumos explains that the purchaser certainly intended to buy only as evidence, as in Bava Kamma, "a person does not throw away his money for nothing." Rather, "for wrapping" describes the seller's intention and the legal meaning of his sale. Since the seller did not sell it as evidence, the purchaser is not compelled to accept it.
(Collected note) And where... This is also Rashbam's view there, s.v. v'amar lei ein, as explained above. Rather it is a sale, etc., and in a voidable sale both parties may retract, as in Bava Metzia 50b. Although ona'ah does not apply to documents, because the seller intended merely "for wrapping," ordinary ona'ah applies. This is all the more so according to Sefer HaTerumos. This is the meaning of the later phrase, "rather it is a sale..." (End of note.)
Even the paper. The purchaser's intent was only to obtain proof. This is the meaning of, "rather it is a sale..." See Rashbam and Rosh there. This also explains the Gemara's extended language, "whether he wrote but did not deliver, or..." It could simply have said "until..." The extra wording teaches that he acquires neither the paper nor the debt. The same applies if he himself did not write it, and all, etc., as above.
Some say... through chalipin. Terumas HaDeshen follows its own view that every document can be acquired through chalipin just as through acquisition together with land. It expresses doubt there, but concludes that in this case it is acquired by chalipin. Nevertheless, he must orally say, "Acquire for yourself..." as explained above in seif 10. Here that may not be necessary because no writing is required at all, as will be explained. See the Shach and below, seif 4.
(Collected note) Some say... See Terumas HaDeshen, which questions whether every debt document can be acquired through chalipin and therefore concludes that in this case it can. Tosafos and most poskim, however, maintain that a document is not acquired by chalipin, as stated in seif 4, and the same applies here. See the Shach. (End of note.)
Or by delivery. Because whoever produces it may collect with it, as explained in siman 50, so it is equivalent to a document written to the purchaser, as above.
(Collected note) Or by delivery... See the Shach, who writes that this is as explained in siman 50 concerning a document stating, "So-and-so..." The same applies here. See what I wrote in seif 26. (End of note.)
But if. The purchaser is not designated, as explained above in siman 60, seif 9, and in this siman, seif 23 in the gloss. It is like the case of two people named Yosef ben Shimon in Bava Batra 173a and Yevamos 116a, where writing and delivery are required.
But if he said. Tosafos there 77b, s.v. hiknah. So too it appears from the Gemara's question, "and words cannot acquire words," meaning by delivery, as Tosafos there, s.v. osiyos, explains. The Gemara then asks, etc., implying that delivery alone suffices. It answers that acquisition together with land is different. Why does that answer help, when he must still say, "Acquire it and all its liens," as in seif 10? Evidently here that declaration is unnecessary. See Tosafos there, s.v. nikneh.
