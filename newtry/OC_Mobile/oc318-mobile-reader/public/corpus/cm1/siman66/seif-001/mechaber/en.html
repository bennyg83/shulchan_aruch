The Sale of Documents and Their Mispricing. Forty-two seifim. Rights represented by a document are acquired only through writing and delivery. {Rama: The buyer may retain the paper until the seller returns his money. If the seller cannot reimburse him, money is removed from the borrower and given to the buyer, as explained below in siman 86, even though the buyer did not acquire the document. If the seller accepted responsibility in the event that the buyer cannot collect the documents, the seller must fulfill that undertaking even though the buyer did not acquire them. Where the buyer failed to acquire and demands that the seller now write and deliver a valid transfer, but the seller retracts, some say the seller incurs no formal curse for retracting and may do so even after receiving payment.} Therefore, one who sells a document must write, 'Acquire it and every encumbrance contained in it.' Without this wording, the buyer acquires neither the debt nor even the paper for use as a bottle stopper; the transaction was mistaken, so the buyer returns the paper and the seller returns the money. {Rama: Some say a document stating, 'I obligate myself to you and anyone who comes through you,' is acquired by exchange or delivery without writing (Terumat HaDeshen, siman 351). If it lacks that wording and was merely written in the buyer's name from the outset, writing and delivery are required where it belonged to the seller but was drafted in the buyer's name. If the seller originally told the witnesses, 'Write the document for the buyer and give it to him,' the writing was primarily for the buyer and no separate writing and delivery are needed. See below, seifim 4 and 5, and siman 205.}
