Seif 10, in the gloss: some say a debt document is not acquired through agav. The proof cited by the Rosh and Shach is from the first chapter of Bava Metzia. Abaye answers there that the case concerns a ketubah document still in the woman’s possession, and the authorities ask why we need not fear that she transferred it through agav. In my opinion this is not a proof according to those who hold that forgiveness is ineffective when the creditor physically holds the document. They explain the rule that a seller of a debt document can later forgive the debt by distinguishing that case, where the instrument is in another person’s hands. The Gemara initially wished to infer the rule of Shmuel that one who sold a debt can subsequently forgive it, because the woman has a migo that she could forgive. Abaye rejects the necessity of Shmuel’s rule and says the case is where the ketubah document remains in her hand. His meaning can be that the very reason Shmuel’s rule is not generally accepted is that forgiveness does not work while the instrument is held, even if held by another. But here, because the ketubah document is in her own hand, she has a migo that she could forgive and return the document, in which setting even Shmuel’s principle would operate. If so, there is no difficulty even if she had previously sold rights through agav. Those who bring the Gemara as proof either hold that forgiveness works even when the instrument is held, or follow the Shach’s explanation that the migo is not literally “I could forgive” but that the claim of payment itself functions like forgiveness. Under the latter view, if the ketubah remains in her hand and forgiveness cannot operate, her claim of payment likewise cannot operate on rights already transferred through agav. Therefore they infer that a debt document cannot be acquired through agav. Examine this carefully.
