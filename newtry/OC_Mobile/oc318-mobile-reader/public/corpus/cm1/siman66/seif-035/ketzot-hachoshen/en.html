He does not collect from encumbered property. The Shach leaves this requiring study because tort obligations ordinarily have publicity. See what I wrote in siman 119.
From purchasers who preceded their waiver. Since we rule that under Rabbi Natan's lien the original creditor cannot waive, there is an additional consequence. If the seller waives a sold debt and thereby becomes liable to the purchaser for the loss, the debtor's obligation to the seller can immediately become subject to Rabbi Natan's lien in favor of the purchaser. Once that occurs, the waiver can no longer defeat the purchaser. Even if the exact amount of the seller's liability was not fixed at the moment of waiver, Rabbi Natan's lien can attach to a debt whose amount later becomes clarified, as the Shach writes in siman 86. The purchaser may therefore collect from the debtor himself. He can also collect from purchasers who bought property before the waiver. They cannot say, "Go first to the debtor," because after the waiver the debtor himself is also comparable to a purchaser who received the benefit of the released obligation. When both stand as purchasers, the creditor may collect from whichever property the law allows. See siman 119.
