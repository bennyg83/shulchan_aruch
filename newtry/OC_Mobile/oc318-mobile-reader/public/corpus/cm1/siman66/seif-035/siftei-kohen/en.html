It is correct only according to the opinion that damages are treated like an oral loan and cannot be collected from encumbered property unless the tortfeasor was first brought to judgment. Yet many authorities hold that damage claims generate publicity and may therefore be collected from encumbered property, as explained below in siman 119 and at the beginning of siman 419. The Ir Shushan and the Sema here, seif katan 83, also require study. Here they cite without qualification the Tur's statement that one cannot collect from encumbered property until the defendant has been brought to judgment, while in siman 119, seif katan 14, the Sema cites without qualification the Maggid Mishneh's view that damage claims are publicized, and the Ir Shushan rules likewise there. Regarding the forgiveness of a debt, one may perhaps say that even the opinion that damage claims are publicized concedes here, since the seller did not cause physical damage with his hands, or even through a complete causative act, but only by speech, and damage caused by speech does not become publicized. Nevertheless, the Ir Shushan and Sema remain difficult, for here they also cite the Rosh and Tur's case of one who sold a house with a warranty and afterward burned it himself, and rule that the purchaser cannot collect from encumbered property until the seller has been brought to judgment. That is direct damage by burning, yet below in siman 119 they rule without qualification like the Maggid Mishneh. This requires study.
