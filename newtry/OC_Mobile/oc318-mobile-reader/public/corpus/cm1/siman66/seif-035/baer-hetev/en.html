From purchasers. This ruling requires study, for it appears correct only according to the view that damages are treated like an oral loan and therefore cannot be collected from purchasers unless the tortfeasor was first brought to judgment. Yet many poskim hold that liability for damages creates public notice and may be collected from encumbered property, as stated in siman 119 and at the beginning of siman 419. See there. Nevertheless, as a matter of law one can perhaps say that even those who hold that damages create public notice agree in a case of forgiveness, because the creditor causes no damage with his hands, nor even through a complete causal act, but only through speech, and there is no public notice of such an act. Still, there is a difficulty in the Sema. Here he cites without qualification the Tur's ruling that if one sold a house with a warranty and afterward the seller himself burned it, the purchaser cannot collect from encumbered property until he brings the seller to judgment. This indicates that even direct damage by burning or the like does not create public notice. Yet in siman 119 the Sema writes without qualification that damages do create public notice. This requires study. Thus writes the Shach.
That preceded. The Shach explains that the reason is that purchasers should have taken precautions lest the lender immediately forgive the debt. This is comparable to what the Taz writes in siman 61, seif 7, that purchasers must be concerned that the lender may immediately have given the borrower his handwritten acknowledgment. See there.
