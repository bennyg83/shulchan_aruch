Since. There is a difficulty: even without this reason he should not acquire it, because there is no written transfer. Furthermore, the borrower should acquire what is already in his own possession immediately when the lender abandons the note, just as in the case of a convert who dies, as stated in siman 72, seif 37, and siman 278, seif 27. It appears that the case concerns a note expressly stating that the debtor is obligated to whoever produces it, so that no written transfer is required. As to the second difficulty, it appears that the note is one held against a non-Jew, where the non-Jew obligated himself to whoever produces it. Were it not for the requirement of another person's intent to transfer ownership, the person who pulled the note into his possession would acquire it, and no other Jew could then claim the debt from the non-Jew. See Responsa Maharam Lublin, siman 22, whose words do not appear correct. According to what I have written, that promissory notes are acquired by delivery, pulling, and lifting, the same rule applies where one abandons a note against a non-Jew stating that the non-Jew is obligated to whoever produces it, even without a written transfer, or where a convert dies leaving such notes against non-Jews. Whoever pulls them into his possession acquires them, and no other Jew can claim those debts from the non-Jews. But if he merely takes hold of them without pulling them, he does not acquire them, because delivery is effective only with the transferor's consent. See siman 198, seif 8. Thus writes the Shach.
