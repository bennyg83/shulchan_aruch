Levi claimed that he did not sell, etc. The wording implies that Levi makes this claim against Reuven and says, "It is true that Shimon paid me, but nevertheless I need give you nothing because I never sold you anything." The ruling would then be that Levi swears that his claim is true and is exempt. The Beit Yosef and Kesef Mishneh, however, do not explain it this way. They explain that Levi litigated with Shimon and said, "I did not sell this document to Reuven, and you still owe me; pay me." Shimon responds, "I already paid you, took the document from you, and it fell from me, whereupon Reuven found it." In that case Shimon swears and is exempt from both Levi and Reuven, in accordance with the Rambam cited by the Tur and Mechaber in siman 41, that whenever a debt instrument is found in another person's possession we believe the borrower, upon an oath, that it has been paid. See there. In the Derishah I explained why they interpreted the case this way. They hold that if Levi admits that he was paid and merely claims against Reuven, "I did not sell it to you," Levi would not even need to swear against him, because the burden of proof is upon the claimant. See there, where I discuss this at length.
That even regarding its acquisition he is not believed. The reason is that there is an evident defect in his claim. If the document had truly been transferred to him by writing and delivery, he should possess the written instrument of transfer. He is not believed to say that it was transferred to him together with land or in the presence of all three parties, both of which are also effective, as the Tur and Mechaber write below in seif 19, because the ordinary method of transferring a debt instrument is by writing and delivery rather than by acquisition together with land or in the presence of all three parties. See the Perishah. He is likewise not believed to say, "I had a written instrument of transfer and lost it," by virtue of a migo that he could have said, "Your document was never in my possession," because this is not a valid migo. He must produce the debt instrument in order to extract payment from the borrower. The Darkei Moshe writes similarly in the name of the Rashba. See there, and see what I wrote on this in siman 64, seif katan 6.
