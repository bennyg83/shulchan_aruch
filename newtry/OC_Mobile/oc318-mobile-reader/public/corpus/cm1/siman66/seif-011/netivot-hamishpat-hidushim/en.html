Levi claimed. The straightforward reading is that Levi says, "It is true that I was paid, but I never sold the document to you." He swears a hesset oath against the purchaser. The Sema, in the name of the Beit Yosef, adds that if the borrower says, "I paid and the document later fell from me," he swears and is exempt toward both Levi and Reuven because the document is no longer in the lender's possession. See Beurim. This applies only to the original lender, who cannot collect because the purchaser is believed regarding his acquisition and the lender is therefore treated as though he has no document. If the seller provides the purchaser with the required written assignment, the purchaser may collect on the document and the borrower is not believed merely to claim that it fell from him. As above. The same applies if he claims that the debt document was given to him as a deathbed gift. If it is a type of document acquired by delivery alone, he is believed even against the borrower. If there are witnesses that the document fell from its owner, then according to all opinions even a document normally acquired by delivery alone requires proof of a valid transfer, because possession proves nothing when it is known that the document was lost and may have been found. Ordinarily we do not suspect loss. Where two people have identical names and one holds a document against a borrower but admits that the document was not originally his, claiming that it was sold to him by the other person with the same name, he is believed against the alleged seller through a migo that he could have claimed original ownership.
