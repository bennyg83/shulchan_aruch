They cannot collect it. See Baer Heitev, who cites the Shach that this ruling requires study. See Responsa Naot Deshe, siman 68, who writes that the case must be one where the witnesses saw the note in the lender’s possession before he sold it to them. Ordinarily this is indeed the case, because a sale of a debt instrument by writing and delivery is generally performed before witnesses, so those witnesses saw the note in the lender’s possession before he transferred it to them. The Mechaber nevertheless disqualifies the note even in such a case because we suspect that from the outset they signed falsely with the intention of later acquiring the note. The Shach disagrees on the ground that two witnesses are not suspected of such conduct. But where they did not see the note in his possession beforehand, the note is certainly invalid. Since the witnesses are now financially interested, we suspect that they wrote it only now and intentionally antedated it. This appears clear.
