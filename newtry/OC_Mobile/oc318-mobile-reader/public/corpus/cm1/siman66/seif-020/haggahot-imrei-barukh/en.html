(Shach, same passage) The principal oath is that the item is not in his possession. Note: This requires study. The claimant has no certain claim here, while the guardian asserts certainty. Why should an oath be imposed on a doubtful claim? According to the Ran at the end of chapter Kol HaNishba'in and the Rashba's novellae to Kiddushin 8, one may answer that only with respect to the guardian's liability for accidents or loss is there no certain claim, because his monetary liability begins only from the accident or loss. But as to the possibility that the property remains in the guardian's possession, even though the claimant does not know this with certainty, his claim is treated like a certain claim because it is certain that he deposited the item with him.
