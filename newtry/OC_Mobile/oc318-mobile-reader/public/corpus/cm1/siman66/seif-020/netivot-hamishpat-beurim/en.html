The documents that I deposited with you. See Shach, who concludes that documents cannot be acquired through maamad sheloshtan, and that the words of the Mechaber therefore require study. In Ketzot HaChoshen it is argued from the words of Ran that maamad sheloshtan is effective for documents, and that only a writ of manumission is different because it relates exclusively to the slave, whereas with ordinary documents the depositary could say, since I could acquire it for myself by delivery, I can likewise acquire it for another through delivery. This is incorrect. The words of Ran in fact indicate that even ordinary documents are not acquired through maamad sheloshtan. A document requires writing together with the maamad sheloshtan, and once the written instrument specifies the purchaser's name, the argument that the depositary could acquire it for himself no longer applies, because the depositary himself could not acquire it under that writing. Likewise, what Ketzot writes concerning a borrower who deposited the promissory note and then, through maamad sheloshtan, instructed the depositary to give it to the lender, namely, that the lender does not acquire it because the rationale that the depositary could acquire it for himself does not apply, since writing and delivery apply only where the lender is the seller, is straightforward according to what I have written, because maamad sheloshtan does not apply to documents at all. Ketzot also writes that with respect to a ketubah, since Rambam rules that waiver is ineffective, maamad sheloshtan is likewise ineffective because the rationale of the depositary being able to acquire it for himself is absent. This is a serious error. The rule that waiver is ineffective with respect to a ketubah applies only where the husband wishes thereafter to continue the marriage, in which case her waiver is void and she remains entitled to a ketubah. But if he does not wish to continue the marriage, her waiver is effective, as Maggid Mishneh writes there, as is evident from chapter 17 of Hilchot Ishut. The same is true where the husband intends to write her a new ketubah. Accordingly, a wife can also sell her ketubah to her husband.
