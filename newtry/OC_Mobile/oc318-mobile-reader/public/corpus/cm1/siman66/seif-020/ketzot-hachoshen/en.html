Debt instruments that I deposited with you. The Shach leaves this law requiring study because Ramban holds that if debt instruments can be acquired by exchange, they should likewise be transferred by a three party arrangement, and the Tur elsewhere rejects acquisition of debt instruments through exchange. Tosafot in Gittin appears to hold that the special three party transfer does not apply to debt instruments because their bodies are not money. Ran, however, indicates that it can apply. The sources therefore reveal a real dispute. According to Tosafot, the three party enactment was made for money and ordinary claims, not for the documentary paper itself. A debt instrument requires its own transfer rules. According to Ran, the enactment can encompass the documentary right as well because it is a transferable monetary claim. The Tur's rulings are therefore difficult to harmonize without distinguishing whether the transfer concerns the underlying debt or the paper and lien together. Where the three party arrangement is used merely to redirect a debt owed by one person, it can operate upon the monetary obligation. Where the claimant seeks to transfer the legal instrument itself with all its documentary consequences, ordinary writing and delivery remain necessary. This distinction fits the sugyot better than equating three party transfer with exchange in every respect.
