Seif 21. But if he sold it to the two witnesses who are signed upon it. See the Shach’s difficulty. I already addressed this in siman 46. The Sema later effectively supplies the same distinction. Where the signer is a non-Jew or otherwise disqualified, the defect exists at the moment of signing, at the very inception of the testimony. Here, however, there was no defect when the testimony was created; the problematic interest arose only afterward. This distinction resolves the Shach’s question. It is surprising that the Shach raised the difficulty without addressing the Sema’s subsequent words.
