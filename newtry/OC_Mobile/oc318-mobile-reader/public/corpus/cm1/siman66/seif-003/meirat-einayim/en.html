One who gives his property to another as a gift while healthy, movable property together with land, etc. At the end of this siman, seif 42, the Rama writes that debt instruments are not included in the term "movable property." According to this, the Mechaber's words here, "movable property together with land, etc.," must mean that the transferor conveyed his property in the manner used to acquire movable property together with land. This explains his concluding phrase, "even though debt instruments are included in the term property."

Since he did not write for him, "Acquire," etc. This is the language of a responsum of the Rosh, cited by the Tur in this siman, seif 3. According to the Rosh's own position it is well understood, because he holds that a debt instrument is not acquired together with land. According to the Beit Yosef's ruling, however, following the Rif and Rambam that a debt instrument is acquired together with land, as the Mechaber writes below in seif 10, with which the Rama also agrees there, we must say that the word "write" here is not literal. Rather, it means that the transferor did not say the formula orally. The Rosh himself writes that even according to the Rif and Rambam, the formula must at least be stated orally, and the Mechaber likewise rules below in seif 10. Similarly, in siman 248 the Tur writes concerning this law that a gift by a healthy person is ineffective "since he did not write to him, 'Acquire it, etc.'" There too "write" is not literal, since the Mechaber rules here that an oral statement suffices. Some resolve the difficulty by saying that specifically where the debt instruments alone are conveyed together with land, the Mechaber rules in seif 10 that an oral statement suffices. But where the debt instruments are conveyed together with other movable property under the general expression "my property together with land," the Mechaber rules that the oral formula alone is insufficient and writing is also required. This distinction has no reason. On the contrary, the reverse is more logical, because the other items require neither writing nor this oral formula, and when debt instruments are included with them one should say that any sufficient form should be enough.
