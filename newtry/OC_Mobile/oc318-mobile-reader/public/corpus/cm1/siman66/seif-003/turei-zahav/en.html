(Seif 10: But he cannot transfer it.) The Sema says that unlike other property, which can be transferred as stated in siman 37, a debt instrument not yet written cannot be transferred. With due respect, this is mistaken. A person cannot transfer something not yet in existence. A clause pledging after-acquired property creates only a lien, not full ownership. Tosafot hold this, and the Sema himself cites it elsewhere. Here the issue is acquisition, and there is no difference between documents and other property. Documents are mentioned as a greater novelty: although the underlying transaction already occurred and a right exists, it cannot be transferred until the instrument itself is written. The Rivash states this expressly.
