There are those who disagree, etc. Responsa She'erit Yosef, siman 20, writes that these dissenters appear correct because the principle that the law follows the later authorities applies only from Abaye and Rava onward. In my opinion, however, the first view is primary. The Rosh objected to the Rif, who ruled like Rav Nachman because he was later and because the law follows Rav Nachman in monetary law, arguing that neither principle applies against Rav Nachman's teachers, Rav, Shmuel, or Rabbi Yochanan. This objection is not compelling. In Bava Metzia 108a, concerning one who bought land for two hundred when it was worth one hundred, the academy initially thought that the principal could say to his agent, 'I sent you to improve matters, not to distort them.' Mar Kashisha then told Rav Ashi that the scholars of Nehardea said in Rav Nachman's name that land is not subject to overreaching. Thus Mar Kashisha, Rav Ashi, and the later academy accepted Rav Nachman's ruling, although Rabbi Ami, Rabbi Yirmiyah, Rabbi Yonah, and Rabbi Yochanan disagreed and held that land may be subject to rescission for gross disparity. They clearly understood that the law follows Rav Nachman in monetary matters even against Rav, Shmuel, and Rabbi Yochanan. The same is explicit in Bava Metzia 110a regarding a deed stating an unspecified number of years, where the Gemara invokes the established rule that the law follows Rav Nachman in monetary law and cites his ruling that land remains in the possession of its owner, although Rav and Shmuel expressly disagreed in the chapter HaShoel. Rashi also explains the passage this way. It therefore appears that the Rosh overlooked this sugya when he criticized the Rif in the laws of one who deposits produce. It further appears that the Rosh himself later retracted, for in several places in Ketubot he cites the Rif's rule without objection even where Shmuel or Rabbi Yochanan disagreed, and the Tur follows this in Even HaEzer, simanim 54, 70, and 154. Likewise, in Ketubot concerning judicial appraisal the Rosh rules like Rav Nachman against Shmuel, and in Makkot concerning whether testimony survives through the remaining witnesses he again writes that the law follows Rav Nachman in monetary matters. Raavan explicitly states that although the law generally follows Shmuel in monetary matters against Rav, where Rav Nachman disagrees with Shmuel the law follows Rav Nachman, because Rav Nachman was attached to the Exilarch's court, where judges were frequently present. The Rosh himself also writes in the chapter HaMokher et HaBayit that the law follows Rav Nachman in monetary law, relying upon this very passage in Bava Metzia. The Rosh's alternative explanation that the case involved a price only slightly below two hundred is exceedingly forced. The straightforward reading is that Mar Kashisha and Rav Ashi rejected the agent's claim altogether because land is never subject to overreaching. The proof cited by Ri from Bava Kamma, that 'property worth money' refers to land, slaves, and notes, which are not subject to overreaching, is likewise sound. Nor is it necessary to say, as Tosafot and the Rosh do, that 'all money' is imprecise. The Beit Yosef's assertion that Rabbenu Chananel agrees with Rabbenu Tam is also incorrect. Rabbenu Chananel merely explains the Yerushalmi's phrase 'an extreme disparity' as up to one half, but he may still rule like the Rif that Rav Nachman rejects overreaching for land entirely and that the law follows Rav Nachman against Rabbi Yochanan. The Ramban in Milchamot and Tosafot Rid explicitly report Rabbenu Chananel this way. The latter explains that although the printed text says that rescission applies only to movable property and not to land, the Rif proved from many passages that Rav Nachman holds that even rescission does not apply to land, and the law follows him in monetary law. This is the decisive view.
