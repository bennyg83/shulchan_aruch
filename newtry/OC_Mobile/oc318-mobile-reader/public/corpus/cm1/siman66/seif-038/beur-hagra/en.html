Even if he sold. See the glosses, and Kiddushin 42b. See Tosafos Gittin 37a, s.v. ela.
Some disagree. See the glosses. The Gemara asks there, "Up to how much? Up to their value." This shows that the same rule applies to land, since it says "even..." Likewise Kesubos 99, "even if they sold..." and there 102, "up to their value"; Bava Metzia 108, "he sold for a maneh..." even though there is no ona'ah for land; and the Yerushalmi, which states that up to one-half there is ona'ah, etc.
When it exceeds one-half. So Tosafos write in Bava Kamma 14b. Although in Kiddushin there Tosafos were uncertain, the Rosh in Bava Metzia rules that at exactly one-half there is ona'ah. Tosafos in Kiddushin explain that the Yerushalmi's phrase "up to one-half there is ona'ah, but less than one-half..." need not mean precisely one-half, but somewhat more. All the proofs cited above indicate that at exactly one-half there is no ona'ah. See the Shach.
