Overreaching. Responsa Maharam of Lublin, siman 5, writes concerning one who purchased from his son-in-law the latter's half-male inheritance document that the sale is void. See there. His words are not clear, particularly according to what I conclude below, that the laws of overreaching do not apply to documents at all, even if one dinar was exchanged for a thousand, as written by the Rif, Rambam, and Mechaber. Shach.
By more. The Sema writes that the Tur cites Rabbeinu Tam that up to one half there is no law of cancellation of the sale, and that one half itself is not included. This proves that if he misled him by one half, the sale is void. Likewise, in siman 175, seif 14, the Tur writes, 'he acquired it for slightly less than two hundred,' and the same implication appears in siman 227, that at one half there is cancellation of the sale and one need not exceed one half, unlike what is written here. The Shach writes that Responsa She'erit Yosef, siman 20, states that these dissenting authorities appear correct because the rule of following later authorities applies only from Abaye and Rava onward. The Shach concludes that the first view appears primary, and see there where he proves this at length from the authorities.
