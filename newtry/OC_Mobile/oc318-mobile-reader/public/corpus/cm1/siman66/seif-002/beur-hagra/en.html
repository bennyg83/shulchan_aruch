Even. The rule that a kinyan makes it unnecessary to write a document applies only to an evidentiary document, because the transaction itself is not effected by that document. But with a deed of acquisition, the kinyan cannot substitute for the deed, because the acquisition here occurs only through the deed. So writes Sefer HaTerumos in the name of the Riem. A proof is from Kiddushin 22b: "A Tanna taught, even by chalipin..." If chalipin merely stood in place of a deed, it would simply be the same as a deed.
Rather, he must say. As with every deed of acquisition. Kesubos 55a: "He said to the witnesses..."
(Collected note) Some say... As stated at the beginning of siman 191, and in the first chapter of Bava Metzia, "Perhaps he wrote it intending to give..." and in siman 65, seif 14. Even with a kinyan it is ineffective here, because the kinyan itself does not effect the transfer, as above. (End of note.)
