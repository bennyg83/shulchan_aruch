In his own handwriting. I was uncertain about a debt instrument that has no signed witnesses and is not written in the debtor's own handwriting, but was delivered before witnesses. Can such a document itself later be transferred through writing and delivery? One might say no, because its evidentiary force rests upon the external delivery witnesses rather than upon the writing itself, and there would therefore be no lien "contained in the instrument" to transfer. It appears, however, that it still has full document status. According to Tosafot and Rosh, Rabbi Eliezer holds that delivery witnesses are the ones who effect a document, yet once the instrument was delivered before valid witnesses it remains legally operative later even when those witnesses are not standing there. A get given to take effect after thirty days and kiddushin by document effective after thirty days can work even though the original delivery witnesses are not present at the later effective moment. The original delivery strengthens the paper into a legal document. Therefore it can later be transferred through writing and delivery like any other instrument. I later found this explicit in Tosafot Rid regarding the sugya of appraising the paper in kiddushin. Even according to Rabbi Eliezer, who says delivery witnesses effect the document, a debt instrument can be acquired through writing and delivery.
