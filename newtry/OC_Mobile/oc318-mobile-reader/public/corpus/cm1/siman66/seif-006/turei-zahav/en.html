(Seif 17: Some say, etc.) The Sema, following Terumat HaDeshen, says that the Rosh and Tur require the seller’s knowledge and silence only to exempt the debtor, not to protect the purchaser who already received payment. I do not understand this. The rule is derived from fruits of a palm not yet in existence, where absent the seller’s later knowledge and waiver the fruits are recovered from the purchaser. Here too an oral statement by the seller cannot validate the acquisition without writing and delivery. If the purchaser’s possession were effective, the debtor would likewise be discharged, as the Tur and Mechaber state at the beginning of the siman. The Tur also says expressly that the purchaser must return the money. The Rosh’s phrase that the seller may again collect from unencumbered property does not prove otherwise. It teaches that the debtor cannot defend himself by saying that he paid the person who held the document in good faith; he was required to see a written transfer and delivery. Since the law is disputed, money should not be extracted even from the debtor absent a decisive ruling. Anyone who follows the Rosh’s view should require the seller’s knowledge and silence also before allowing the purchaser to retain payment.
