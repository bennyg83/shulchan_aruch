Only in pressing circumstances. This at least implies that in a pressing circumstance one may temporarily rely upon witnesses to a kinyan, like the Tur, Maharik, and Mordechai in the name of Semag. The Bach, however, writes that even in pressing circumstances one should not rely upon witnesses to a kinyan, but should instead place movable property in her possession as security. Likewise, if a ketubah is found invalid, movable property should be placed in her possession. He writes that this was the practice he saw from his teachers. Certainly one who is stringent is praiseworthy. But if the groom has no movable property available for temporary security, he may rely upon the Tur, Maharik, and Mordechai in the name of Semag. With a relatively light prohibition of this kind one should not be stringent to the point of canceling the mitzvah of marital relations and procreation, especially today when a husband may not divorce his wife against her will, so the concern that it be easy in his eyes to divorce her is diminished.
