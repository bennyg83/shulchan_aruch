Because he guarded properly. If the entrusted item was lost through unavoidable accident, he receives wages only for the time during which he actually guarded it. For example, if he was hired for a week for one dinar and the unavoidable accident occurred in the middle of the week, he receives only half a dinar. Likewise, if an agent was hired to carry a letter to a particular place and the letter was lost through unavoidable accident halfway there, he receives only half his fee. The first reasoning is primary. The Shach rules like the Rambam that the custodian is liable for negligence:
Who threw it into the river. If he merely reduced the level of protection, such as where a paid custodian transferred the item to an unpaid custodian, that is only negligence. But if the person to whom he transferred it actually burned it and cannot pay, or denies receiving it, the first custodian is liable:
And if they made a kinyan from him. Although an unpaid custodian may undertake the liability of a borrower without a kinyan, that case is different because he remains within the legal framework of custodians and merely changes the level of liability. Here, where the proposed obligation is outside the ordinary laws of custodians, a kinyan is required, Sema. The Shach explains similarly that there the custodian can say, "I accept upon myself the status of a paid custodian," or, "I accept the status of a borrower," whereas here no such existing category applies, and therefore a kinyan is necessary:
That he shall become liable. The Sema holds that even with a debt document such a kinyan is effective. The Shach rules that it is not. The primary view follows the Sema, because the custodian is making a kinyan to obligate himself to pay if he does not return the document.
