They made a kinyan. The Beit Yosef writes that this applies only to a paid custodian, who can obligate himself through a kinyan to make payment, but regarding an unpaid custodian, how can he obligate himself through a kinyan to swear? The Gemara too stated this concerning a paid custodian. The Darchei Moshe responds that he does not know what distinction there is, for an unpaid custodian can also stipulate and make a kinyan that he will swear or pay. The Sema likewise applies the ruling to an unpaid custodian. The Shach writes that they did not grasp the Beit Yosef's intent. Certainly, if an unpaid custodian stipulated that he would be liable to pay, the kinyan can take effect on that payment obligation. But if he stipulated merely that he would swear, upon what can the kinyan take effect? It is only a kinyan on words. This is explicit in Bedek HaBayit and Kesef Mishneh, and his words are correct and compelled by the Gemara. This is the primary ruling. See there. The Sema further writes that although the Tur and Shulchan Aruch state in siman 291, seif 27, and siman 305, seif 4, that an unpaid custodian who stipulated to be liable even for unavoidable accidents is liable by verbal undertaking alone without a kinyan, custodians are different because they already bear some custodial liability. Therefore they may assume greater liability even verbally. Documents, land, and the other excluded items, however, are not subject to custodial liability at all, and one can assume the law of a custodian over them only by a kinyan, as the Maggid Mishneh writes.
