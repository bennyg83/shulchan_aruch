If they made a kinyan from him that he would become liable, etc. This applies also to an unpaid custodian, as Darkei Moshe writes, unlike Beit Yosef. The Mechaber likewise writes below in siman 301, seif 4, that liability arises only through a kinyan. Rama at the beginning of siman 95, on the law that 'if he stipulated to pay everything, the matter follows his condition,' does not mean a mere verbal stipulation but one accompanied by a kinyan, as I explain there. Yet the Tur and Mechaber below in siman 291, seif 27, write that an unpaid custodian who stipulates to be liable even for unavoidable accidents becomes liable through words alone, without a kinyan, and they repeat this in siman 305, seif 4. I reconcile this in siman 301, seif 4. Ordinary custodians are already subject to some liability, and therefore they can assume greater liability through speech alone. But documents, land, and the other excluded categories have no custodial liability at all, and one cannot bring them within the legal rules of custodianship except through a kinyan. So writes the Maggid Mishneh. Understand this well.
