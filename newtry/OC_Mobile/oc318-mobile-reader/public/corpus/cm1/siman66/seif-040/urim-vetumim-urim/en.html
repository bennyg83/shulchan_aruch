He must pay. Sema and Shach agree that he must pay the full face value of the document, not merely what the purchaser paid for it. The Shulchan Aruch's language implies that this also applies to a gift, which necessarily means full value. See Tumim, where I asked why this is not merely prevention of a benefit, like withholding testimony. If we say the sale is treated as a mistaken transaction because it is self-evident that the purchaser bought on condition that the seller would swear if the borrower demanded it, then ordinarily the seller should have to return only the purchase price, like other mistaken sales. One must therefore say that when the seller refuses to swear, we presume the debt was in fact already paid. Why else would he refuse an oath and thereby reveal himself as one who sold a paid debt? Therefore he must reimburse the full loss. But if the purchaser himself admits that the debt had not been paid, there is room to exempt the seller even if he refuses to swear.
