The same applies if the security consisted of land, etc. The Baal HaTerumot, Gate 51, section 6, as cited by the Beit Yosef, makes clear that the first opinion holds that the creditor may collect from orphans without an oath, whereas the dissenting opinion requires an oath. Likewise, the Ritva, whom the Beit Yosef cites here as holding that the debt may be forgiven, requires an oath, as the Beit Yosef cites in his name at the end of siman 110. Consider this carefully, because at first glance there is no basis for distinguishing the issues. If no oath is required when collecting from orphans, the debt is treated as already collected, and it is obvious that it cannot be forgiven. The Mechaber's words therefore require study, for here he records a dissenting opinion, while below, siman 108, seif 12, he rules without qualification that collection from orphans requires no oath. As to the practical ruling, I conclude below, siman 108, seif 12, seif katan 25, that an oath is required. Even so, it appears that here the creditor cannot forgive the debt. This is also the Rashba's view in the responsum cited by the Beit Yosef below, siman 140, section 5, printed as responsum 939. There the Rashba requires an oath, while here the Beit Yosef cites his ruling that the debt cannot be forgiven. This is the primary halachah.
