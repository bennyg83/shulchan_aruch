A mortgage. The Baal HaTerumot, cited by the Beit Yosef, explains that this view holds that one collects from heirs without an oath, whereas the dissenting view holds that an oath is required. For if no oath were needed against heirs, the debt would be considered already collected, and it would be obvious that the original lender could not forgive it. Accordingly, the Mechaber's presentation is difficult: here he records the dissenting view, yet in siman 108, seif 12, he rules without qualification that one collects from heirs without an oath. This requires study. As to the practical law, I concluded there that an oath is required, yet even so it appears that here the original lender cannot forgive the debt, and this is the primary ruling. Thus writes the Shach.
