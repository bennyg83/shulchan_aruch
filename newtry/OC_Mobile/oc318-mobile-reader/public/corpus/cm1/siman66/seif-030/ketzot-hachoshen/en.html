A mortgage on land. Although Rabbi Yitzchak's rule that a creditor acquires a pledge applies specifically to movables and not land, once the land mortgage has actually reached the purchaser's possession the seller can no longer waive the lien. Raavad infers this from Rif's statement that with a deathbed gift the creditor cannot waive because the Sages treated it as though it had already reached the recipient's hand. This shows that once the right has actually reached the purchaser, waiver is ineffective. Nevertheless, with land this requires a valid written transfer. Delivery alone is insufficient because Rabbi Yitzchak's rule does not apply to land. With movable collateral, however, the creditor acquires the pledge under Rabbi Yitzchak, and delivery of the pledge itself can transfer that right. See siman 126.
