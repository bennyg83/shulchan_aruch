The purchaser may again collect from him. The wording implies that the purchaser may collect a second time from the borrower, because beit din told the borrower to pay the purchaser and he violated the court's ruling. The continuation, "but if the borrower paid first, etc., he is exempt," would then refer to a case where the borrower paid the seller before beit din ruled that he must pay the purchaser. In that case he is exempt, whereas after beit din ruled against him, the purchaser may again collect from him. In the Perishah, however, I wrote that even in that case the purchaser does not recover from the borrower, but rather from the creditor who sold him the document and received its price. See there.
And some say that the purchaser must return his document to the borrower. In the Perishah I proved that the Tur understands the Baal HaTerumot to have written this only according to his own position, namely that the purchaser acquires the physical paper of the document for the use of covering the mouth of a flask even if the seller did not write, "Acquire it and all its liens." Even so, he holds that the purchaser must return the document to the borrower when the borrower paid the seller. Accordingly, he likewise holds this when the purchaser acquired it by writing and delivery but the borrower first paid the seller. The Tur and those who agree with him, however, hold that even for the mere use of covering a flask the purchaser does not acquire the physical document without a proper transfer. According to them it can be said that when he did acquire it by writing and delivery, the purchaser need not return the physical document to the borrower when the borrower first paid the seller.
