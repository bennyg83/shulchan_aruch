This rule is derived from the Gemara's statement that if the seller had previously written the deed, once the purchaser acquires the land he acquires the deed wherever it is. The Gemara initially infers from this that debt documents can be acquired without writing and delivery, and answers that acquisition through land is different. From here the authorities learn that agav can substitute for the usual writing and delivery requirements. Rashba, in his chiddushim to Kiddushin and in responsa cited above in siman 55, derives a further rule. If a person instructed witnesses to write a note saying he owes Shimon one hundred, but said, "Keep it with you or with me until I later decide to give it to Shimon," and he subsequently decides to give it, Rashba holds that he then needs a formal writing and delivery. The document did not originally become Shimon's instrument. Rashba proves this from our Gemara. If there were a fundamental distinction between a document originally written in the transferee's own name and an ordinary debt note held against a third person, the Gemara could not have inferred anything about ordinary document transfer from the sale-deed case. The fact that it does shows that both require the same transfer structure. This is difficult for Mechaber and Rama, who accept the present agav rule but do not codify Rashba's further requirement. Indeed, in several places above, including siman 55, the law appears to be that once the obligor later decides to deliver a document written in the beneficiary's own name, no new writing and delivery are required. The resolution is that Mechaber does not interpret the Gemara's sale-deed case as Rashba does. Ritva in Kiddushin explains that the seller already possessed a deed from his own seller concerning the field. He now sells the field to a new purchaser. Once the new purchaser acquires the land, he also acquires that older deed held by the present seller. This is an ordinary transferred document, one never written in the new purchaser's name. The Gemara can therefore properly infer the law of document transfer: agav acquires even such a note without a separate writing and delivery. On this reading there is no proof whatsoever that a document originally written in the beneficiary's own name requires a later formal transfer if the writer initially delayed delivery. This also explains why Mechaber omitted the special law that if a seller had already written a deed to the purchaser, acquisition of the land acquires the deed wherever it is. Rambam codifies that case explicitly. Mechaber does not, because according to Ritva's interpretation the rule is simply an instance of the general law that documents are acquired through agav, which Mechaber already states. Rambam interprets the Gemara differently because he holds that sale and gift deeds may be written for the seller or donor even without a prior kinyan, as discussed above in siman 65. His system can therefore sustain the original-deed interpretation. Accordingly, the practical halachah follows the simpler distinction: an ordinary debt note transferred from one owner to another can be acquired through agav as stated here; but a document originally written directly in the beneficiary's own name need not undergo a new writing and delivery merely because actual delivery was delayed until the obligor later consented.
