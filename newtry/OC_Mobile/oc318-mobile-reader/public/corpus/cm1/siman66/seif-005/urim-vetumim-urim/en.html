He must fulfill, etc. Sema understands the seller's accepted responsibility as obligating him to pay the full value of the debt. Otherwise, if he merely had to return the purchase price he received, what special meaning would the responsibility clause add? Shach questions this and says perhaps the clause was written only to prevent the seller from later saying he would give a new written assignment instead of money; therefore full debt value is not owed unless expressly accepted. See Tumim. I explain that ordinary acceptance of responsibility for even the purchase price creates an interest problem, and even a conditional structure such as, “If I give you another valid assignment I bear no responsibility, but if I do not, I must pay the full amount,” can still resemble prohibited interest because the purchase money is effectively a loan in the seller's hands. Hagahot HaRosh in *Eizehu Neshech* appears to support this. Therefore either the case is a gift, where no interest concern exists and the seller plainly must pay the full document value, or it is a sale and he repays only the amount received. The responsibility clause then has practical effect by preventing the money from being treated merely as a deposit. Even though the original transfer arrangement may have involved a prohibited interest issue, the monetary obligation itself is not thereby cancelled. The point still requires study.
