"And he gave him permission to raise the banknotes in his name, and he would not receive them from his name, and he would return to his son and brother, without writing, he would not buy them from the world if he had given him permission to raise them in his name and his banknotes only that he would return and give them the notes to his son, that the banknotes were of Simone as an end of his name
He did not acquire it. Maharam di Lonzano rules that when partners divide debt documents written in both names, writing and delivery are required like any other transfer. His proof from Rashba is not conclusive. Where a document was originally written in both partners’ names, either partner can collect the entire debt from the debtor, as Rashba expressly rules, because each is treated as authorized by the other and the debtor undertook to pay whichever demands payment. Nevertheless, Maharam’s conclusion may be sustained regarding ownership between the partners. The division may not create an irrevocable proprietary transfer without writing and delivery, even though each partner has standing against the debtor. Thus one partner may retract from the division until a valid transfer occurs.
