Or witnesses testify to his undertaking and make a kinyan from him. Since an ordinary kinyan is presumed to be made for the purpose of writing, she may go to the witnesses and have them write the ketubah, and they need not ask the husband again. This is the straightforward meaning of Rambam according to our text, as understood by the Beit Yosef and Maggid Mishneh. Rambam explicitly equates one who writes the ketubah in a document with one whose obligation was witnessed and secured by kinyan. The Bach inferred from Rambam's concluding words, "until he has time to write," rather than "to write or make a kinyan," that a kinyan helps only in pressing circumstances. This inference is insufficient to displace Rambam's plain meaning. Because a standard kinyan is made with the expectation of writing, the purpose of the kinyan is ultimately that a written proof be placed in her hand. She may have that document prepared without further knowledge or consent from him. Rambam therefore speaks of having time to write, which is the natural completion of the kinyan. Semag, however, interprets Rambam as speaking in a place where ketubot are not ordinarily written, in which case witnesses to a kinyan are sufficient. In a place where ketubot are written, even if a kinyan was made she does not rely upon it, because the burden and expense of securing the writing is not hers, and perhaps her husband will die suddenly and the heirs will claim that the ketubah was already paid and torn up.
