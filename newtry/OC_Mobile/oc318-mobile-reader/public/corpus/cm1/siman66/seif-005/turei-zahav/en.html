(There: and some say that even regarding its acquisition, etc.) One cannot say that he should be believed by a migo that there never was such a transaction, as explained in siman 64, where in the absence of witnesses he is certainly believed through a migo that nothing occurred or that the item was lost through unavoidable circumstances. Here too the case concerns documents that were seen in his possession immediately before they came to beit din. Nevertheless, witnesses concerning the original deposit are unnecessary here. Specifically where he claims, "I seized them as collateral," which requires neither writing nor delivery, as stated in siman 64, one needs testimony that they were deposited with him together with current sight of the documents. Here, however, he claims, "I purchased them from you." Since there is no acquisition of documents without writing and delivery, it is enough that they are now seen in his possession. Nor can one say that he should be believed to say, "I purchased them," by a migo that he could have claimed, "They are in my possession as collateral," because he prefers the claim of purchase, which prevents the borrower from simply paying the original lender. This appears to me correct.
