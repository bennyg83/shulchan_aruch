Certainly. The Beit Yosef cites a responsum of the Ritva concerning one who claims, regarding documents, 'I returned them,' whether he must swear since he is believed through a migo that he could have claimed they were lost through unavoidable circumstances, and on such a claim he would not need to swear because one does not swear in response to an uncertain claim. His teachers disputed this. It appears from his words that the primary view is that he must swear, and this also appears primary to me and is implied by the Rambam. See below siman 95, seif 4. In any event, according to what I wrote above that he must at least swear that the documents are not in his possession, one who claims 'I returned them' certainly requires at least a shevuat hesset, for even had he claimed unavoidable loss he would still have to swear that they are not in his possession. The practical difference arises elsewhere, where a shevuat hesset is required because of a definite claim: even if he has a migo that he could have advanced a claim that would not require an oath because the opposing claim was uncertain, he must now swear a shevuat hesset. Shach.
