And some calculate the ketubah of a virgin as ten gold coins. According to this calculation, redemption of the firstborn son would be eight gold coins. A virgin's ketubah is twenty-five dinar and is valued here at ten gold coins, so twenty dinar, the five sela required for redemption, would be eight gold coins. A dinar would accordingly be valued at a proportional amount in Polish currency. It is surprising that the Rama did not mention this view in Yoreh Deah 305, where he gives a much lower figure for redemption. I examined Responsa Maharil, siman 80, and the Agudah, who explain the custom of writing a reduced sum in the marriage conditions on the basis of Rabbeinu Tam's interpretation that a zekuk of silver is one maneh, so two zekukim equal two hundred. At that time a zekuk of silver was worth five gold coins, so two zekukim were ten. Since Rabbeinu Tam holds the virgin's ketubah is Torah law, it follows that in his time one and one-half gedolim equaled a silver dinar and one hundred dinar equaled five gold coins. On that basis the redemption of the firstborn would require only one-fifth of a zekuk, worth a single gold coin. The Rama therefore did not cite this as a practical monetary figure in Yoreh Deah, because in our time the older coin is copper and the result would be far too lenient. Rivash, simanim 66 and 23, calculates each dinar as three argenin. Twenty-five dinar therefore equal seventy-five argenin. Sixteen argenin equal one ounce, so sixty-four are four ounces, leaving eleven argenin, approximately another half ounce plus three argenin. Hagahot Mordechai states that a ma'ah weighs sixteen barleycorns, so a dinar of six ma'ah weighs ninety-six barleycorns and a sela of four dinar weighs 384 barleycorns. A new Venetian ducat was weighed at forty-eight barleycorns, equivalent to three ma'ah. A virgin's ketubah of two hundred Tyrian dinar would therefore equal four hundred such silver ducats. At first glance this too is more than the Rama's calculation in Yoreh Deah 305. But in general one should not be exact using local weights or coins, because weights differ and coins are not uniformly pure silver. The sound method is to use pure-silver weight: according to those who hold the ketubah rabbinic, it is twenty-five pure-silver dinar weighing 2,400 barleycorns. The redemption of the firstborn is twenty pure-silver dinar weighing 1,920 barleycorns. Later authorities weighed this at about five local lot, with various small differences. Therefore one should rely not on local lot or coins, but on pure silver weighing 1,920 barleycorns for redemption and 2,400 barleycorns for the ketubah. See Maadanei Melech to Bekhorot, page 269a, who discusses the Prague weights at length and concludes that redemption requires five lot of pure silver by Prague weight, more than three Reichsthaler according to the detailed subdivision he records.
