If a person validly sells a debt document and later waives the debt to the borrower, the waiver is effective. {Rama: The buyer nevertheless need not return the physical document to the borrower; others disagree.} The buyer's remedy is to persuade the borrower to write a new document in the buyer's name, or to have the borrower formally undertake, through acquisition or an admission before witnesses, that the debt is now owed directly to the buyer; it then can no longer be waived. Even a condition that the seller may not waive does not prevent an effective waiver. Even if the seller entirely removed himself from the debt and emptied all his rights in the document to the buyer, his later waiver is effective. The seller's heir may also waive, though the heir may not waive to himself merely to harm the buyer. Thus, if Reuven lent his son money by document, sold it to Shimon, and died, the son cannot say, 'As heir, I waive my own debt and owe no damages because I intended only to exempt myself.' He must pay the entire debt to the buyer. Some disagree and permit even self-waiver. {Rama: Above, siman 60, seif 8, it was explained that if Reuven ordered his document written in Shimon's name, Shimon cannot waive it because the money is not his, but Reuven can because he is the true owner. Even if Reuven does not expressly waive this document but releases the borrower from everything in his possession, this document is included despite being written in Shimon's name (Responsa of the Rashba, siman 926). See also simanim 4 and 7.}
