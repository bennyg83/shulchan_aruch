This is so even if the note contains a credibility clause, even before its due date, and even if the borrower disqualified any witnesses who might testify to waiver. Some authorities explain that because sale of debt instruments is rabbinic, the acquisition is not strong enough to prevent the original lender from waiving. According to those who hold that sale of notes is Torah law, the seller can transfer only the property lien while the personal obligation remains with the original lender. When he waives the personal obligation, the property lien falls away automatically. Thus, even if Reuven holds a note against Shimon, sells it to Levi, and Levi himself owes money to Shimon, Reuven can nevertheless waive Shimon's debt. Shach, unlike a responsum of the Rosh.
