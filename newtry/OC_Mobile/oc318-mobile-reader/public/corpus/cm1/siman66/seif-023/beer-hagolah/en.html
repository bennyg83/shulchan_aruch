t) Statement of Shmuel, Ketubot 85b. The reason is as the Rif writes there: since documents do not themselves constitute money, they cannot be acquired by a complete Torah mode of transfer; rather, the Sages instituted a method by which they can be sold. So too writes the Rambam in chapter 6 of Hilkhot Mekhirah. Tosafot there likewise write this on Shmuel's statement in the name of Rabbeinu Yitzchak and Rabbeinu Tam. Maharik, shoresh 89, writes that even if the assignor initially wrote that he disqualifies every witness who might testify that he later forgave the debt, he can nevertheless forgive it. Beit Yosef, cited by the Sema.

s) Tur in the name of Baal HaTerumot, gate 51. He writes that he found in Sefer HaZechut that this is derived from the baraita: if one was owed money by his brother, etc., he may not say, "Since I am an heir, I have taken possession"; rather, it is removed from him, etc., Ketubot 21b. If one could forgive a debt to oneself through inheritance, why could he not say, "Since I am an heir, I have taken possession because I forgave it to myself"?
