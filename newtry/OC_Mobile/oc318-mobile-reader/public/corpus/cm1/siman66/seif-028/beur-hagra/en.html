Or an oral loan. The reason she cannot forgive is that the underlying obligation of the borrower becomes subordinated to him, even though it is not transferred by writing and delivery. The same therefore applies to an oral loan. See Kesubos 83a, "his hand..."
Or that they borrowed. As the Rosh writes in Yesh Nochalin, Bava Batra 125b, in such a case the husband inherits it because she was unable to sell it. See there.
Even if they were. Bava Kamma 89b concerning slaves that are nichsei melog, which belong to him rather than her. From there there is also proof for the preceding rule, even if they borrowed, because at 91 the Gemara discusses husband and wife. See Tosafos there, s.v. k'man. Emancipation is equivalent to forgiveness; consequently, just as she cannot sell the underlying right, so too she cannot forgive it.
