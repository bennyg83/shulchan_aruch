The principal ketubah is not included. This language implies that even if the principal ketubah was not mentioned in the document at all, but only the dowry she brought in and the additional sum he added, the beit din condition still obligates him to pay the principal ketubah. All the more so with our ketubah text, which first states that he gives her the mohar of her virginity, two hundred silver zuz fitting for her, and afterward records one hundred zekukim as dowry and one hundred zekukim as addition. Thus he must pay two hundred zekukim of silver plus two hundred silver dinar. At the end of the ketubah all three are again mentioned together: the obligation of this ketubah, this dowry, and this addition. They are therefore three distinct components, as Maharshal rules in Yam Shel Shlomo. Every virgin accordingly receives the two hundred zekukim, valued in different lands at four hundred or five hundred gold coins, plus two hundred dinar, calculated in the Rama's Yoreh Deah reckoning as twenty Rhine gold coins. I already noted that the later calculations there make the pure-silver amount approximately fifty lot or more, apart from the other ketubah components. I later saw a different calculation in a later pamphlet, but its words are not clear.
