(Seif 21: We are not concerned for suspicion, etc.) The Tur and Mechaber thereby answer several difficulties. Why is Shimon not regarded as interested in the testimony as in seif 22, and what does the second witness accomplish if only one witness remains valid? Here there was no legal invalidity when the signatures were made. Signed witnesses are treated as though their testimony had already been examined, and only an appearance of impropriety might justify stringency, like the rule that a sage who prohibited a woman to her husband may not marry her. The presence of another valid witness removes that suspicion. In seif 22, however, Shimon was legally disqualified at the moment of signing because he signed for his own benefit. A second witness cannot cure that defect.
