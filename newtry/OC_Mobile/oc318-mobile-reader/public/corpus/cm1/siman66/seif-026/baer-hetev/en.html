By virtue of you. This applies where the purchaser informed the debtor that he had acquired this document. The original lender can then no longer waive the debt. So writes the Rashba in a responsum. It implies that writing and delivery are nevertheless required, and even then the rule does not apply until the debtor is informed. Shach.
To waive. The Sema writes that the words of the Rosh prove that these authorities hold that in this case one need not write, 'Acquire it and all its liens,' and for this reason the Rama wrote at the beginning of this siman that where a document states, 'I obligate myself to you,' and the like, it is acquired by delivery alone. Nevertheless, they hold that the first creditor can waive it, since the debtor originally became obligated to him. The Shach disagrees and holds that even according to the authorities who maintain that he cannot waive it, he must still write, 'Acquire it and all its liens.' He further writes that the primary ruling appears to be like the authorities who hold that he can waive it, as is also indicated by the Gemara and by reason. See there at length, and Responsa Kol Bo, Responsa Mabit, Choshen Mishpat siman 128, and Responsa Mahari ben Lev, part 2, siman 24.
He is liable. The Sema writes that when the Rama states at the beginning of this siman, 'and likewise, if the seller has nothing with which to pay him,' this refers to the money the seller received from the purchaser for acquisition of the document. But where the seller waived the debt document, even the purchase money is not collected from the borrower, since the debt has already been waived to him, even if the seller has nothing with which to pay. See the Shach regarding this.

By means of agav. The Sema explains that this opinion holds that money is not acquired through agav except when it is a deposit, not a debt, as stated in simanim 122 and 123 and siman 203, seif 9. By ma'amad sheloshtan, however, it is acquired, as stated in seif 9. The Shach writes that this is indeed our ruling: a loan is not acquired together with land by agav, and there is no acquisition at all. Maharshal likewise writes this, unless the document concerns land, in which case it can be acquired by kinyan sudar, or it concerns deposited money, which can be acquired through agav with land. See there, Responsa Mabit, part 2, simanim 25 and 169, Responsa Maharashach, part 2, siman 102, Responsa Maharanach, siman 77, and below siman 211, seif 7 and siman 253, seif 20.
