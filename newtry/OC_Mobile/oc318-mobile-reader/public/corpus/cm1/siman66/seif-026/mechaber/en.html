Some say that if a borrower wrote to the lender, 'I obligate myself to you and anyone who comes through you,' and the lender sold the document to someone already alive when the loan was made, the lender can no longer waive the debt. Others say he may still waive it. {Rama: Some say a seller can waive a sold debt document only when he did not owe the buyer, or has other property with which to pay him. If he owed the buyer and has nothing else to pay, the borrower is now directly obligated to the buyer, as explained below in siman 86, and the seller cannot waive. Others disagree. See siman 86. If the seller transferred the debt itself together with land, some say he cannot waive, others say he can, and still others say no acquisition occurred because a debt is acquired neither incidentally with land nor by exchange. See siman 203, seif 9.}
