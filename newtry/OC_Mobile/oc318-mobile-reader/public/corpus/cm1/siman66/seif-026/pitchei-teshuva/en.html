And if he is a person on his deathbed. See Baer Heitev regarding whether he must say, ‘and all its liens.’ Below, siman 253, Shach seif katan 29, cited by Baer Heitev there, seif katan 42, the Shach himself rules that the primary halachah follows the Ran and Maggid Mishneh, that the gift is effective even though he did not use that formula. Responsa Noda BiYehuda, second edition, siman 41, already notes that the Shach’s rulings in siman 66 and siman 253 contradict one another. He writes, however, that all this concerns a person on his deathbed who merely says that the documents should be given to a certain person, as in the case of Rav Amram’s mother in Bava Batra 151a, who possessed a bundle of documents and said that they should belong to her son Amram. There she did not expressly say that the underlying debts should belong to Amram, only the documents. But in the testament before him, where she said that a certain person should take the debts, both those recorded in documents and oral debts, she transferred the debts themselves by a deathbed gift. It is obvious that a debt embodied in a document is no worse than an oral debt, which can be acquired by a deathbed gift. See there.
