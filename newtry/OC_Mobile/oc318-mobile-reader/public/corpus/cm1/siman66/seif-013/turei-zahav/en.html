If so, how does the Gemara in Bava Batra say that the baraita and the Mishnah disagree in the dispute between Abaye and Rava, and that the Tanna of the Mishnah, who holds that the documents are produced, follows Rava, who says that no proof need be brought? According to Rambam and the Maggid Mishneh, even Rava agrees that against the borrower proof must be brought. Why, then, does the Mishnah say that the documents are produced, rather than allowing the borrower to resist payment? The cited work attempts a difficult reconciliation, saying that the baraita and Mishnah dispute the borrower's position just as Abaye and Rava dispute the seller's position. There is another difficulty: what proof does Abaye bring there from the case of one brother among heirs who produces documents and must bring proof? Perhaps "must bring proof" there means only against the borrower, which Rava also concedes. Rava should have rejected the proof on that basis. Perhaps Rava answered with a stronger response. Even according to Rabbi Yosef ibn Migash and the Ran, Rava could have rejected the baraita concerning one brother by saying that it follows the opinion that documents are not acquired by delivery, whereas he follows the opinion that documents are acquired by delivery.
