[Shach, letter 72] If Reuven admits that he gave it to him. Note: I am astonished. Since the witness is interested in the matter, the written instrument, which is the instrument of acquisition, should be invalid, and the deed should not be acquired. What does Reuven's admission help, when acquisition cannot occur through this invalid writing? Clearly, one must explain that the defect of an interested witness is that he is suspected of lying, and we suspect that he testified falsely because the second party is more favorable to him. Once Reuven admits that Shimon signed truthfully, Shimon is restored as a valid witness and the writing becomes a properly executed deed. However, according to the rationale that an interested witness is like a relative, see the Shach above at the beginning of siman 36, the matter is all the more difficult. Since he receives a benefit because the second party is more favorable to him, he is like a relative and the deed remains invalid. The practical law requires study. What the Shach further writes, "and moreover, here witnesses are unnecessary," is certainly difficult, because witnesses are needed to render it a deed through which acquisition can be made. This requires very great study.
