Even the interest. See Shach, seif katan 27. Tumim explains that the reference is to interest that accrued after the sale, because the purchaser's money generated that return. In my humble view, this cannot be correct. If so, even a waiver by the seller could not affect it, since the gain belongs to the purchaser. Rather, one must say that the principal money itself was never transferred to the purchaser, because it is a loan. What the seller transfers to him by rabbinic enactment is only the lien upon property. But the underlying loan itself cannot be assigned. Since the interest was not written into the document, there is no lien upon property securing that interest. Therefore the seller cannot transfer the interest at all. Nor can he create a personal obligation for it against the lender. It is merely prevention of expected profit, as Shach writes.
