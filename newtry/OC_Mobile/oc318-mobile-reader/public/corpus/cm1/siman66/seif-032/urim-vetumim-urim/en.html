Some say a debt instrument is not acquired incidentally. In Tumim I explain at length that Rosh's responsum should be understood as follows. If a debtor encumbered movable property together with land and owns debt instruments, those instruments themselves become subject to the creditor's lien. If he later sells them, an earlier creditor can collect them from the buyer. I wrote similarly above in siman 60 that the marketplace enactment does not protect such a buyer. Rashba disagrees and holds that if the debtor sells the instruments or transfers them to a later creditor, the earlier creditor cannot take them, because debt instruments do not become encumbered incidentally even according to Rif, who allows their sale through incidental acquisition. See Tumim. If the obligation includes after-acquired property, or according to the view that such a clause is an omitted standard term and cannot be retracted, then all agree the debt instruments can become encumbered. Rashba is consistent with his own view that one can retract an after-acquired-property undertaking. Thus the matter depends on the dispute below in siman 112. See also what I write in siman 85 and at the end of siman 111.
