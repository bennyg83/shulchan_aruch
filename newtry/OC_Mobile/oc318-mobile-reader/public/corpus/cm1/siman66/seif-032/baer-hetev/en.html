His heirs. The Shach writes that very many authorities disagree with this and hold that with respect to liability for indirect damage, the penalty was not imposed upon one's son after him. Their words appear primary, and it is difficult why the Rama did not cite their view at all. See below siman 108, seif 2, siman 388, seif 2, and the beginning of siman 386.
The interest. The words of the Rama require study, for the Rashba himself was uncertain whether one can sell the interest at all. If so, even though it can be collected, this is merely prevention of profit, which is not called damage. We must therefore say that the case is where the interest was capitalized into the debt document as a loan, in which case it is regarded as already collected, as stated in Yoreh Deah siman 171. But the Rama's wording, that he must pay him the interest that accrued upon it, does not imply this. The matter requires study. See Responsa Maharam Mintz, siman 66. Shach.
That he gave. The Tur writes that this is unlike other sales where, if a creditor seizes property from a purchaser, the purchaser is entitled to appreciation. Here, since the seller has the power to waive the debt, and even his heir can waive it, the sale was defective from the outset. The purchaser should have had the document rewritten in his own name. Since he did not do so, he is entitled to recover only the money he paid. The Sema explains that this applies specifically here. But if a creditor comes and seizes the document from the purchaser, or another person claims that it is his, and similarly in the other cases written by the Tur and Shulchan Aruch in seif 34, all agree that the seller must pay the entire value of the document. There, the purchaser had no reason to contemplate that the seller would sell something that was not his, so the sale was not inherently defective. The Shach writes that the Mechaber's view appears primary, and that this is the view of most authorities. See there for the authorities and proofs he cites, and Responsa Ri MiBeit Levi, siman 27.
