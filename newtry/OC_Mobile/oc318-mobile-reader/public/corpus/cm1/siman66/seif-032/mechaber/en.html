When a seller or his heirs waive a sold debt, they must pay the buyer the entire amount written in the debt document from their best property, because they caused him to lose the document and are like one who burned it. The document is nevertheless appraised at its actual value, accounting for such factors as a violent borrower who refuses judgment or a borrower without property. If the seller dies, his heirs pay. {Rama: If the sold document was against a non-Jew and the seller waived it, he must also pay the accrued interest. If the debt was discovered to have been paid, the person who waived is exempt even though he might have collected it again from the non-Jew. All this follows some authorities. Many disagree and hold that whenever a sold debt document is waived, the seller need pay only the purchase price and the buyer's expenses. See also siman 40.}
