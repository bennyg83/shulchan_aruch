Seif 22. Reuven has a document against Shimon. See the Shach, who writes that whenever a witness is disqualified only because he has a financial interest, if the litigants admit the relevant facts his testimony can be accepted. This follows their position in siman 37 that an interested witness is disqualified because of the concern that he may lie. Where the parties admit the facts, that concern disappears and he can testify. In my opinion, however, if the disqualification of an interested witness is understood as analogous to familial relationship, then even the parties’ admission cannot make him a valid witness. A relative remains disqualified in every setting in which valid witnesses are legally required, regardless of the litigants’ consent to the underlying facts. The same should follow for an interested witness if his defect is one of personal relationship rather than merely suspicion of falsehood. Examine this carefully.
