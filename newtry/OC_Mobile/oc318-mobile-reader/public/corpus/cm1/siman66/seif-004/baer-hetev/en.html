Before. From the Tur's citation of the Ramban it is clear that it is also enough if the writing and delivery occur in one act. The Tur comes only to exclude a case where the document is delivered to the buyer afterward. The statement that if the document being transferred is not present means to exclude the Raavad's view that even without delivering the document at all, if he transfers it by another kinyan and writes the assignment formula, that suffices. Thus the Sema. The Shach challenges this and writes that nothing is proven from the Ramban's words. Moreover, it is impossible to make the writing and delivery literally simultaneous. Therefore it is preferable to deliver first and then write, 'Acquire it and all its liens.' See there.

We require. It appears that the case was as follows. The documents belonged to Shimon, but he had them written in Reuven's name with Reuven's consent, on condition that Reuven give him a written admission that the money represented by the documents belonged to Shimon. When Shimon later demanded this, Reuven said, 'You subsequently waived and canceled my admission and authorized me to collect the value of the documents.' On that understanding the use of waiver is appropriate. Otherwise, as explained in siman 73, seif 19, waiver applies to a monetary obligation owed by one person to another, but not to a tangible object, for which one must use a language of gift. All the more so here. This also follows from the Mechaber's next ruling that even the language of gift does not help without the written formula, 'Acquire it.' Thus the Sema. The Shach writes that the Sema's difficulty seems to be that if the subject were a tangible object, language of waiver would not help. But I do not understand his reasoning. If the documents initially became Shimon's, and only because the money belonged to Reuven Shimon had to transfer them back to him, then waiver certainly helps, for what difference is there between waiving money and waiving this claim? Why would Reuven not acquire them even without writing and delivery? If, however, the Sema means that the documents never became Shimon's and were merely written in his name for Reuven's benefit, then they are indeed like a tangible object, and waiver would not help in any case. Rather, the words should be understood simply. The Rama adopts the Rashba's wording because that was the actual case there, while the main point is only to teach that writing and delivery are required. Thus the Shach.
