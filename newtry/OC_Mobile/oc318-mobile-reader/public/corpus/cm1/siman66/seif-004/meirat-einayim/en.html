There is an opinion that the document must be delivered before, etc. See what the Tur writes in the name of the Ramban. It is clear from there that the document need not be delivered first; delivery together with the writing at the same time is also sufficient. It appears that this is also the view of Nimukei Yosef, who intended only to exclude a case where the document is delivered afterward. His statement, "and if the sold document is not present, etc.," comes to exclude the Raavad's view that even without delivering the document at all, one may transfer it through another act of acquisition together with writing the formula, "Acquire it, etc." See the Tur.
And Shimon forgave him regarding them, etc. The responsum of the Rashba, cited by the Beit Yosef later in this siman, states: Reuven executed a written acknowledgment in his own handwriting to Shimon that he held such and such documents belonging to Shimon. It appears that the actual case was that the documents belonged to Shimon, but Shimon had caused them to be written in Reuven's name with Reuven's consent, on condition that Reuven give him a handwritten acknowledgment that the money represented by the documents belonged to Shimon. When Shimon demanded them, Reuven said, "You later forgave this claim, etc.," meaning, you forgave and cancelled my acknowledgment so that I may collect the value of the documents for myself. On this understanding, the ruling that forgiveness is effective is clear. If the case were understood literally, it would be difficult, because the Tur writes at the end of siman 43, and the Mechaber there in seif 19, that the concept of forgiveness applies only where one person owes another money. When a tangible object belonging to another is in his possession, the language of forgiveness is ineffective; the language of a gift is required. All the more so here. Moreover, the law here would then follow a fortiori from the law written immediately afterward, that even language of gift is ineffective without a written instrument stating, "Acquire it, etc."
