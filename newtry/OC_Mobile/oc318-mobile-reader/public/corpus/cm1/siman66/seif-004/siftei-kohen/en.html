The document must be delivered first, etc. The Sema says the Ramban does not require delivery to precede the writing and that simultaneous writing and delivery suffice. The Ramban’s words do not establish this. His statement that the document was not written ‘at the time he transferred it’ means when it was taken from the seller, excluding reliance upon an ordinary kinyan on the theory that a kinyan is destined for writing. Nimukei Yosef and Ritva appear to require delivery first, because the document itself is primary and produces the lien. Exact simultaneity is also impractical. It is therefore preferable to deliver the document first and then write, ‘acquire it and all its liens.’

Reuven who has, etc. The Sema interprets Rashba’s responsum as involving documents that actually belonged to Shimon but were written in Reuven’s name, with Reuven giving Shimon a written acknowledgment of ownership; he then claimed that Shimon had waived the acknowledgment. The Sema does so because waiver ordinarily applies to a debt, not to a physical object in another’s possession. In my view, this is unnecessary. Where the documents were initially acquired by Reuven and then required a new transfer to Shimon, a waiver can operate upon Reuven’s obligation to transfer them. The case therefore concerns the enforceability of that obligation, not a waiver of ownership in a physical object.
