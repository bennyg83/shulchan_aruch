A borrower who paid a purchaser to whom the creditor delivered the document but did not write, "Acquire it," etc. In the Perishah I wrote that the Baal HaTerumot derived this law from the rule concerning one who sells another the future fruits of a palm tree, which are not yet in existence. Rav Nachman says that the buyer does not acquire them, but concedes that if the buyer seized and ate them, they are not taken from him. Just as possession is effective there with respect to an attempted acquisition of something not yet in existence, so too possession is effective for a debt instrument acquired without the required writing and delivery. There, the case applies even if the seller did not know of the buyer's seizure. The same is true here: the purchaser is exempt even if the seller did not know. The Rosh and Tur, who write that the seller must know, say so in order to exempt the borrower from liability to the seller as well. Mahari likewise writes this, as do Darkei Moshe and the authorities represented by the "some say" cited by the Mechaber here. Their requirement of the seller's knowledge concerns exemption of the borrower. The purchaser, however, is exempt in any event, as the Rama concludes: "And if the borrower cannot pay, the seller does not, etc." We must say that they hold that if the purchaser's possession were not legally effective, the borrower who paid the purchaser would not be exempt even if the seller stood there, saw the payment, and remained silent. Only where the law is that what the purchaser seized cannot be removed from him do we say that the seller should not have remained silent. This also explains the structure of the Tur and Mechaber. They begin, "A borrower who paid the purchaser, etc., is not liable," discussing the borrower's exemption, and conclude with the reason, "because if he seized, etc." At first glance this is difficult, because according to the Rosh and Tur the borrower is exempt even without that reason, since the case specifically requires that the seller knew of the payment. According to what I have explained, the wording is fully consistent.
And what Rama wrote in the gloss, namely, that this applies specifically where he did not write to him, 'Acquire it for yourself,' but if he said, etc. It must be explained that he holds that if he did not say, 'Acquire it for yourself,' etc., this is worse than the sale of the fruits of a date palm, for there he at least told him to acquire the fruits, whereas here he sold him nothing but the paper itself. Rama therefore holds that everyone agrees in this case that even the purchaser is not exempt unless he told him, 'Acquire it for yourself,' etc. Otherwise Rama should have presented this as an opinion introduced by 'there are those who say.' See the Perishah and Derishah, where I explained why the Tur and the Mechaber altered the wording of the Rosh and the Maggid Mishneh in the name of the Rashba. They stated this law in the case of a purchaser who collected first, whereas the Tur and the Mechaber stated it in the case of a borrower who repaid, etc. See there carefully.
