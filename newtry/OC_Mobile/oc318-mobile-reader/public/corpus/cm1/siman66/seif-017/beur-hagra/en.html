And there is an authority. Bava Metzia 67a says that the objection is overreaching, not a conclusive refutation of the transaction.
(Supplement) And there is an authority. As stated there: not overreaching. See Tosafot there, s.v. Hatam, and Hagahot Ashri, which raises many objections to Rabbeinu Tam's explanation. The Rosh explains like Rashi. Although we generally rule that an erroneous waiver is not a waiver, that applies when the party did not know the facts. Here both parties knew, and the waiver is therefore effective. A loan remains prohibited despite knowledge, as with all interest, as the Rosh writes there. (End of supplement.)
(Supplement) And there is an authority. This follows the Rosh's explanation that the Gemara's phrase, there it is a sale, means like Rashi: because the party knew and waived. In a loan, however, it remains prohibited. Hagahot Maimoniyot likewise rules this way in the third chapter of Bava Batra in the name of Ri. (End of supplement.)
And if he does not. This resembles one who says, Take one hundred zuz to so-and-so. Although this is not equivalent to acquisition on behalf of the recipient in a gift, if the agent delivered it, the gift is effective, as stated at the end of the first chapter of Gittin. This requires study, however, because there the agent should initially give it to the recipient, as the sugya indicates.
