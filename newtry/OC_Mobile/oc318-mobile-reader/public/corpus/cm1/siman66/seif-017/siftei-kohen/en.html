If the debtor cannot pay, etc. This ruling is not correct. If the debtor must pay again because the original transfer was ineffective, he may in turn recover from the purchaser, for the purchaser received money under a void transaction. Consequently, if the debtor lacks means, the seller may recover directly from the purchaser under Rabbi Natan’s rule. It is incoherent to say that the transfer is void as against the debtor but valid as against the purchaser. Terumat HaDeshen does not clearly establish otherwise; its case may mean only that the transferor must first proceed against the original debtor or depositary, not that the recipient may retain funds when the underlying acquisition was legally ineffective. The Rosh, Rashi, Tosafot, Nimukei Yosef, and related authorities indicate that where an acquisition is void, possession does not validate it absent knowing waiver.
