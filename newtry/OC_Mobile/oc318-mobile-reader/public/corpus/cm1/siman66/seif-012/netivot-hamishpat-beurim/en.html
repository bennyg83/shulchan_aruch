A woman who brings a debt instrument into the marriage. Chelkat Mechokek and Beit Shmuel in Even HaEzer siman 51, seif katan 2, write that if the debtor says he paid, the woman must swear, and if she refuses to swear the debtor does not pay. They appear to follow the Mechaber's position in Choshen Mishpat siman 47 that even where a creditor's admission would injure others and he cannot forgive the debt, collection still requires an express clause of trustworthiness. Without such a clause he cannot collect. The same would therefore apply where a woman brings a debt instrument into marriage. The Shach there, seif katan 4, however, holds that where an admission would injure others and the creditor cannot forgive the debt, collection is permitted even without a trustworthiness clause and even where the creditor cannot swear. The same should apply to a woman who brings a debt instrument into marriage. Since she cannot forgive the debt, collection is allowed even if she cannot or will not swear and the document contains no trustworthiness clause. This is also indicated by Rosh and Ran in the chapter Kol HaNishba'in. They explain the seller's oath by saying that the seller is believed to say the debt was paid through a migo that he could forgive it. This implies that where he cannot forgive the debt, so that there is no such migo and he is not believed to say it was paid, there is no basis for requiring that oath.
