The document must first be delivered. Sema writes that simultaneous writing and delivery also works. The rule excludes only a case where the writing precedes the delivery. Shach, seif katan 14, questions whether Sema has proof. But the preceding seif provides a clear proof. Where one gives all of his property through a land-based acquisition and the transfer document itself contains the phrase "the note and all its liens," the acquisition is effective even though the writing and delivery occur simultaneously. At the moment the transferee acquires the land, the note is also delivered through the same acquisition, and the written transfer language becomes effective at that very moment. Thus simultaneous writing and delivery must suffice. Shach's further practical recommendation, that because exact simultaneity may be difficult to establish one should perform the delivery first, can still be sensible. The preceding case proves only that legal simultaneity is sufficient where the structure of the transaction makes both elements arise together with certainty. But as a matter of principle, Sema is correct.
