And he accepts responsibility if they are lost. This implies that even if the woman agrees to accept the movable property at her own risk, it is not permitted. Ordinarily, if a husband places valuables in her possession for her ketubah and they are lost, the loss is his, as stated at the beginning of chapter Af Al Pi. One can distinguish, however, between this temporary security and an ordinary long-term placement of assets. In the ordinary case, if the assets were at her risk, there would be concern that the husband might say, "Take your ketubah and leave," because the assets would effectively already be hers. But with this temporary arrangement, perhaps that concern does not apply even if she accepts the risk. This may be why most authorities omit this requirement and it appears only in Semag. Semag himself may mean only that if she refuses to accept the risk of loss or depreciation, the husband must accept it, but if she willingly assumes it there is no objection in a temporary arrangement. After writing this I saw Tosafot, Ketubot 56b, who explain Rashi's statement that movable property is not designated for a ketubah because its value may depreciate or it may be lost. Tosafot reconcile the first chapter, where Rav Ami permitted first relations on Shabbat after they placed movable property in the bride's possession, by explaining that the husband accepted responsibility if it was lost or depreciated.
