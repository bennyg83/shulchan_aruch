Responsa Maharam of Lublin, siman 22, writes that if the non-Jew is a violent person and there is concern that he may retract and demand the debt from the borrower a second time, the purchaser or recipient of the gift must provide a guarantor so that the borrower will suffer no loss from the non-Jew. See there, and below siman 126, seif 23. See further regarding a Jew who comes by virtue of a non-Jew in siman 45, seif 17, siman 149, seif 15, siman 154, seif 19 and seif 20, the end of siman 155, and Responsa She'erit Yosef, siman 55. Thus the Shach.
To a Jew. Since we rule that sale of promissory notes is Torah law, the non-Jew's sale is effective and the non-Jew has the same legal power as the Jew who sold it to him. So writes the Ir Shushan in the name of the Ritva, and this is also the Mechaber's view. I have already written at the beginning of this siman that the primary ruling is that sale of documents is Torah law. Nevertheless, even if it were rabbinic, the sale would still be effective, because whatever the Sages instituted they instituted with full legal force. This is also implied by the Nimukei Yosef, who in chapter HaSefinah writes that sale of documents is rabbinic, yet in chapter Mi Shemet writes that a document can be sold to a non-Jew. See there.
