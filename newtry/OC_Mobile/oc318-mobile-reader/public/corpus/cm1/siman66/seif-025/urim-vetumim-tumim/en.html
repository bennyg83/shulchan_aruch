This is the view of many authorities who understand Rava's statement that the holder need not bring proof as applying only according to the opinion that documents are acquired by delivery alone. Since our halachah requires writing and delivery, they say the holder must prove his acquisition. At first glance there is a difficulty. Abaye challenges Rava from the baraita: one of the brothers who produces family debt notes must prove that they belong to him. The Gemara asks whether the same is true of any outsider. In the alternate version Rava proves the opposite: the baraita specifically speaks of brothers, implying that an outsider need not prove acquisition. But perhaps that baraita follows the Sages, who require writing and delivery. Then it would be obvious that the possessor must bring proof, and the whole dispute of Abaye and Rava, which concerns delivery-only acquisition, would not be implicated. The answer appears from Rama's responsum, siman 22. Where partners jointly own debt notes, transfer between the partners does not require ordinary writing and delivery. Since each already has an ownership interest and one partner can act for the partnership without formal authorization, delivery alone can suffice. Our baraita concerns precisely such notes: the brothers inherited them jointly from their father and held them in an undivided estate. Therefore, even according to the Sages who ordinarily require writing and delivery, the note in the hands of one brother is in a category where delivery alone can effect transfer between the brothers. Yet the baraita says that the brother must bring proof. This proves that even where the acquisition itself requires only delivery, possession alone does not necessarily prove that the transfer occurred. Conversely, in the alternate version, Rava infers that an outsider in an analogous delivery-only setting need not bring proof. Thus the Gemara's proof remains valid. This also gives strong support to Rama against Maharshal, cited by Shach, who holds that partners still need full writing and delivery. The very structure of the Talmudic challenge shows that partnership documents stand in a more lenient transfer category. According to Rambam, whom I explained above, one need not rely on this partnership distinction to resolve the passage, because his theory of deathbed gifts supplies another route. But for the authorities presently under discussion, Rama's analysis is compelling.
