And upon his death they divide it. See Responsa Tzemach Tzedek, siman 97, who writes that where brothers or partners divide debt instruments, the other party cannot later waive to the debtor the share that he formerly had in a particular note. Although brothers who divide an estate are treated as purchasers, so that the division might appear equivalent to one brother selling his former share in the note to the other, and ordinarily a seller of a debt instrument can waive the debt, there is a distinction between brothers or partners dividing debt instruments and an ordinary sale of a debt instrument. Otherwise, how could beit din compel division of debt instruments or apply the rule of ‘buy or sell,’ when one party could thereby expose the other to loss through a subsequent waiver? The distinction is that an ordinary purchaser of a debt instrument must collect by virtue of the original lender’s right, and therefore the lender retains power to waive the debt. By contrast, where brothers or partners jointly hold a debt, either one may collect the entire debt from the debtor without written assignment and delivery and without deriving his right through the other, as the Rashba writes in a responsum cited by the Beit Yosef in simanim 77 and 176. From the outset the debtor obligated himself to pay the entire debt to whichever one of them would demand it. Consequently, when they divide their rights, the other partner’s right in the assigned note is completely extinguished and he can no longer waive it. See there at length. See also Tumim, seif katan 72, who notes that the Rama had already ruled this way in Responsa Rama, siman 92, though for a different reason. We rule above, seif 28, that when a woman brings a debt instrument into marriage she can no longer waive it because the husband’s hand is like the wife’s hand. Thus, where both have equal legal standing, one cannot waive against the other; the same applies here. Tumim questions the proofs offered for these positions, but concludes that the elder authorities of the generation have already ruled this way, and who can raise his head against them? See there.
