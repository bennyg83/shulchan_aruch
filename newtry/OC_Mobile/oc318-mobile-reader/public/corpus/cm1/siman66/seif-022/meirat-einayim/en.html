And Shimon was one of the witnesses to the gift. The wording implies that the case is where the promissory note was conveyed before witnesses and he instructed them to write for him, in his name, the written formula of 'Acquire it for yourself, it and all its liens,' in the manner explained in seif 2, and Shimon was one of those witnesses. The seller now claims: It is true that I gave you the promissory note as stated, but you did not acquire it, because one of the witnesses to the writing of 'Acquire it for yourself' has a personal interest, so that witness is disqualified and the transaction amounts merely to words conveyed through words. However, since the conclusion says that he is considered personally interested, it implies that his disqualification is because we do not believe him and suspect that he wishes to testify falsely, like the interested witness discussed by the Tur and Mechaber immediately above. According to what I wrote, however, his disqualification would apply even if he is telling the truth, because the document itself is defective and the matter remains words conveyed through words. We must therefore say that here too the case is where Reuven denies the matter. The statements that he gave it to Levi in writing, and that Reuven then wished to retract, describe what appears to us from the written instrument in Levi's possession, which states that it was given to Levi. Since Reuven now denies it, it appears that he seeks to retract, not that the matter was false from the outset. The ruling is that even here Reuven prevails. This differs from the preceding law, because here the defect was apparent from the very time of the gift, whereas in the preceding case there was no defect at the time of signing. Once the witnesses signed, their testimony was already regarded from then as though examined in beit din, as I wrote there.
