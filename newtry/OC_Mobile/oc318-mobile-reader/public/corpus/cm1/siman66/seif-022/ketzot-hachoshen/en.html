Interested in the testimony. The Shach says that the witness is disqualified only where he denies having transferred the document. If he admits the transfer, then even though he has an interest, the acquisition document can remain valid, as explained in siman 191. In my view that is incorrect where the document is not in the grantor's own handwriting. Siman 191 speaks about the grantor's own writing. If the document was written by another, it requires either valid signed witnesses or witnesses to delivery. Here there are no delivery witnesses, and one of the signed witnesses is disqualified by interest. Therefore there is no valid acquisition document at all. This also explains why no Torah oath arises against the remaining qualified witness. Even if the grantor admits the underlying transaction, with only one valid witness there is no proper acquisition document. The later writers who say that admission by the grantor cures the disqualified signature have overlooked this distinction.
