"And if he took a break. It was written before that he sold that this trend was wild and that he had made a mistake and the rumors he had given in return:
And if the borrower was poor at the time of the sale, etc. Responsa Mabit, part II, siman 178, explains that this refers to a case in which the purchaser knew that the borrower was poor, whereas if he did not know, the sale is void as a mistaken transaction. The Bach similarly explains it; see there. In my humble opinion, however, since the Mechaber first wrote, concerning the preceding case, that it is a mistaken transaction, whereas here he wrote without qualification that it falls within the laws of overreaching, this indicates that whenever the borrower was poor the matter is classified as overreaching rather than as a mistaken transaction. This is also explicit in Baal HaTerumot, Gate 51, part 6, the source of this ruling. He initially raises the question whether, when the purchaser finds the borrower poor, the sale is void because the debt was sold on the presumption that it could be collected, or whether the purchaser should have investigated and specified the matter. He cites some support from the Yerushalmi concerning ma'amad sheloshtan, but distinguishes that case: there, the assignor says, 'Give this person my money,' and the debtor agrees, so if the debtor is poor and they acted deceitfully there can be no greater mistake. By contrast, when one purchases a note and accepts it, he may be content to have the other person become his debtor. Baal HaTerumot then records that he asked the Ramban, who answered that when the borrower was poor the matter is included in overreaching, and notes are not subject to overreaching. The Ramban plainly addressed the very case in which the purchaser did not know that the borrower was poor. This is further explicit from his conclusion: if the purchaser believed the borrower wealthy and he was poor, whether or not the assignor knew, the assignor is exempt, for the purchaser should have inquired about him. Thus there is no mistaken transaction here, because the purchaser should have investigated. Although the Ramban later cites a different explanation from Rav Hai regarding ma'amad sheloshtan, as discussed below in siman 126, seif katan 43, that applies specifically there and not to the sale of a promissory note. This is also indicated by Baal HaTerumot. See there and examine carefully. See also Responsa Maharshal appended to book II, siman 6.
