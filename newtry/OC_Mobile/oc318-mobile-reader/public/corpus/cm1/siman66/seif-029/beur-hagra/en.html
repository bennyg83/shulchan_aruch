One who gives. This follows from the cited sugya in Kiddushin, where the Gemara says that Rav's rule applies to a deposit. If a loan could always be transferred in the presence of all three parties, the woman's reliance would not be lacking.
(Supplement) One who gives. Many authorities disagree. They prove their view from the end of the first chapter of Bava Metzia, asking why the claim might not have been transferred in the presence of all three parties, and from the cited passage in Bava Kamma. See Tosafot there, s.v. Shema mina. Their answer is compelled, because otherwise the Gemara's resolution would not answer the difficulty. The same appears at the end of the first chapter of Gittin. The Shach disagrees with all of them and writes that a debt evidenced by a note cannot be transferred in the presence of all three parties. The plain sense of Kiddushin supports him. (End of supplement.)
