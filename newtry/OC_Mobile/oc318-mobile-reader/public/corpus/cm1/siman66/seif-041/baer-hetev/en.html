Buy me out or be bought out. See the end of siman 171, seif 14, where Rama writes that according to some authorities the rule of 'buy me out or be bought out' does not apply to property whose substance does not belong to the parties, such as a mortgage; rather, they divide the money. See there. The same should apply to a promissory note. If so, Rama's unqualified ruling here must be understood as relying on what he wrote there. It is also possible to distinguish: specifically with a mortgage and similar property, Rama ruled that the doctrine does not apply, because even while the land remains intact in their possession it is considered money for this purpose, since they can immediately divide its value between them in money. A promissory note is different. Thus writes the Sema. The Shach writes that the Tur and Shulchan Aruch follow their own view as stated in siman 171, but I have already written there that many poskim disagree and hold that the rule of 'buy me out or be bought out' applies only to brothers who inherited jointly, not to ordinary partners. See there.
