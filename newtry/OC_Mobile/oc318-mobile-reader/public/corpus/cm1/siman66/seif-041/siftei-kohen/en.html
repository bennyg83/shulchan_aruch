Or partners, etc. The Rosh and Tur follow their own view below in siman 171 regarding the remedy of 'buy me out or I will buy you out.' I have written there, however, that Ri Migash, the Raavad, Nimukei Yosef in the name of Rabbenu Yonah, the Raah, and other authorities disagree and hold that this remedy applies only to brothers who inherited jointly, not to ordinary partners. See there.
If it is a promissory note, etc. Rabbenu Yerucham, citing a responsum of the Rashba, writes that the rule of 'buy me out or I will buy you out' does not apply to notes. The same appears in a responsum of the Ramban. The Rama himself writes below in siman 171 that some hold this remedy applies only to property whose substance belongs to the parties, not to a mortgage or a future collectible right. His unqualified ruling here therefore requires study. The Sema's attempts to distinguish the cases are strained. A mortgage is not presently divisible into money, for the parties must wait until redemption, and the Ramban and Rashba explicitly say that a promissory note is all the more excluded. It appears, however, that the Rama cites the restrictive view below only as 'some say' but does not accept it as law, which explains his unqualified rulings here and in siman 176 that notes may be divided through 'buy me out or I will buy you out.' More than this, it appears that the Rashba himself would ultimately agree with the Rosh and Tur. His restrictive responsum reflected his earlier view that notes were not subject to collection in kind. In later responsa he deferred in practice to the leading decisors who treated notes as collectible property. Once notes are treated like movable property for collection and division, the remedy applies to them as well. This likely explains why the Mechaber ruled simply like the Rosh and Tur. The Beit Yosef and Bach likewise indicate that accepted practice follows the Rosh. This is the principal ruling.
