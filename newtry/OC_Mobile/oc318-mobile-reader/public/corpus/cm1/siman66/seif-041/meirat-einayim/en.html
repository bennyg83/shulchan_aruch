And if it is a single promissory note, etc. See siman 171 at the end of seif 14, where Rama writes that some say the rule of 'either buy me out or I will buy you out' does not apply to property whose body is not jointly owned by them, such as a mortgage from which they are ultimately to collect money. Rather, they divide the money, etc. See there. The same rule applies to a promissory note, as Rama writes here in Darkei Moshe, note 42, in the name of a responsum of Ramban, siman 73. He likewise cites the Rashba in Darkei Moshe to siman 171, seif 35. This is the meaning of Rama's phrase there, 'and the like.' If so, Rama's silence here, without commenting on the Mechaber, must be because he relied on what he wrote in siman 171. It is also possible that only with a mortgage and similar property did Rama decide that the rule of buyout does not apply, because even at present, since the land itself is before them, it is regarded as money for this purpose and can immediately be divided between them in monetary terms. A promissory note may differ.
