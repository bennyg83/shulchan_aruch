(Seif 26, gloss: He did not owe the purchaser.) If the seller already owed the purchaser another debt, this document would be pledged to him under Rabbi Natan’s rule, and the seller could not forgive what already belongs to another. The Beit Yosef suggests from a responsum of the Rosh that a seller of a debt instrument who lacks assets to compensate the purchaser cannot forgive the debt. This is imprecise. The Rosh’s case involved a preexisting debt owed to the purchaser, as the Rama states here. If no independent debt existed, the seller may forgive the instrument even though he lacks assets to reimburse the purchaser for the loss.
