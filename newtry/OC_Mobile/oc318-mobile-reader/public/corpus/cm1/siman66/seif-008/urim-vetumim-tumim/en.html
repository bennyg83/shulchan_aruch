And to whoever produces it. The same applies if this bearer transfers the note to another person, and that person to another, successively without limit. No additional writing is required at any stage. Likewise, where the note says it is payable to the lender "and to anyone who comes through him," the language extends through every subsequent purchaser, not merely the first transferee. Rashba writes this in the first chapter of Gittin. Ran's reasoning also proves it. He explains that the clause does not suffer from the problem of bereirah because the note can be transferred successively to one person after another. That reasoning necessarily assumes that the clause continues through all later holders. The point is therefore clear.
