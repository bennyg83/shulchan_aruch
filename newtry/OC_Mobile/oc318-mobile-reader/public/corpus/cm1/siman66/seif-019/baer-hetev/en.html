We compel. The Tur writes that the proof belonging to the transferee is in the lender's possession and one may not suppress another person's testimony or evidence. The Tur and Mechaber rule similarly at the end of siman 16. Thus the Sema. The Shach writes: According to what I concluded at length in seif 29, that a debt transferred by maamad sheloshtan transfers only the debt itself but does not make the transferee the holder of a written promissory note, none of the laws in this seif follow. The purchasers of the borrower's property are not obligated to Shimon and can say, 'You are not our litigant.' Levi may also say that he paid Shimon so long as Shimon does not possess the document through writing and delivery. Accordingly, the lender is not compelled to produce the document. Nevertheless, he may now transfer the document anew to Shimon by writing and delivery. Although ordinarily, once a debt has been transferred by maamad sheloshtan the transferor no longer has any claim against the debtor, here, because he left the document with the transferor, it is presumed that he did so with the understanding that the transfer would not become ineffective before payment to Shimon was clearly made.
