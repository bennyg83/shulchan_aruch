Some say. Tosafos there say that it must be together with the very land to which the document relates. Their proof is from Bava Metzia 21, where the Gemara establishes the case where the document, etc. If ordinary land sufficed, perhaps she transferred it together with land. The first opinion answers that a married woman has no independent land, as explained below in seif 28, and moreover such a case is uncommon. See Tosafos Yevamos 116a, s.v. osiyos, and Rosh at the end of the first chapter of Bava Metzia, seifim 48 and 49. The latter view holds that the Gemara speaks of every woman, including a divorced woman. See Tosafos Bava Batra 147b, end of s.v. hamocher.
(Collected note) Some say... So writes the Rosh at the end of the first chapter of Bava Metzia in the name of Rav Hai Gaon and Rabbeinu Chananel. Bava Batra there, however, implies that the dispute is only whether acquisition together with land alone suffices; when combined with delivery, it is effective and no additional delivery is needed. Tosafos there appears similarly. See the Shach, who disputes the Rosh. Tosafos Yevamos 116a, s.v. osiyos, nevertheless writes like the Rosh, and most poskim rule accordingly, as stated in Sefer HaTerumos and elsewhere. This is the principal view. Otherwise, what does the Gemara gain by answering in Bava Batra that acquisition together with land is different, since even then it would not suffice? Rather, in place of delivery it is effective, and writing is unnecessary there. See Tosafos. (End of note.)

But he cannot. An oral debt is not acquired by writing and delivery, as shown in Kiddushin 47 and 48, where the Gemara separately asks how they disagree concerning a documented loan and concerning an oral loan. Thus an oral loan cannot be transferred by writing and delivery. Likewise Bava Batra 147 asks whether Rav Nachman holds, etc. If an oral loan could be transferred together with land, the question would not arise. See Tosafos there 77b, s.v. Rav Pappa, and further.
