Because a document is not acquired by means of agav. The Rosh writes in a responsum, klal 75, siman 3, that nevertheless a document can become subject to a lien by means of agav. So quote Darkei Moshe and the Sema. The Rosh indeed writes this there. The Rashba, however, in responsum 914, writes the exact opposite, that even according to the view that a document can be acquired by agav, it cannot become subject to a lien by agav, because the body of a document is not money. The Beit Yosef cites this briefly below in his new material, seif katan 4. In the Rashba's responsum itself the point is stated even more clearly: "Further, even if he explicitly subjected them to a lien, he accomplished nothing, because documents are not themselves money and do not become subject to a lien together with land, etc. Even if you say that creating a lien is like a sale, nevertheless even for a sale he must write to him, 'Acquire it and all the liens contained in it,' etc., whereas Reuven did not write this here. According to what I have written, Reuven's subjection of movables together with land makes no difference with respect to debt documents he owns against others." The Rosh writes there that elsewhere we find a distinction between sale and lien, in that it is easier for a person to subject property than to sell it, because property later acquired can become subject to a lien even though a person cannot sell property he has not yet acquired. In my humble opinion this is not analogous. There, since the person himself is already obligated, immediately when he later acquires the property it becomes subject to the lien, and if he then sells it he sells property already subject to another's right. Here, however, the body of the document is not money. What is accomplished by subjecting the physical document to a lien? Can one subject the underlying debt claim itself to a lien? That claim is not acquired until he writes, "Acquire it and the lien contained in it," and delivers the document in a manner that transfers it completely. Even if he explicitly writes that he subjects the document and all its underlying rights to a lien, the concept of a lien cannot take effect upon that debt right in the same manner as transfer. Only by transferring the debt through the required writing, "Acquire it, etc.," does he fully remove himself from the debt right and convey it, and the recipient acquires it through delivery of the document. That is unlike merely subjecting it to a lien. Nevertheless, the view of the Tur, Mechaber, and other later authorities above in siman 60, end of seif 1, clearly implies that documents can become subject to a lien through agav. The same follows from Baal HaTerumos and the Tur and Mechaber below at the beginning of seif 34, and again from Baal HaTerumos and the Tur and Mechaber below in siman 111, seifim 22 and 23, and the later authorities there.
And regarding the practical halachah, we adopt, etc. It appears to me that the primary ruling is to distinguish as follows: agav with land is equivalent only to delivery, while the written formula of transfer is still required. Baal HaTerumos, Shaar 51, part 3, writes in the name of some authorities who prove that a document can be acquired together with land provided that the transferor writes, "Acquire it and all the lien contained in it, etc." Although Baal HaTerumos there disagrees and follows the Rambam and Rabbi Yosef ibn Migash, nevertheless the primary ruling appears to me as I have written, for the reasons I will explain. What appears from the Tur, that a document cannot be acquired through agav at all because agav does not even stand in place of delivery, is difficult. The same appears from Responsa Rosh, klal 76, siman 3, and explicitly from the Rosh at the end of the first chapter of Bava Metzia. There, regarding the Gemara's answer that the woman's ketubah document was still in her possession, he writes: if she had sold the ketubah, why would the document be in her possession? A debt document is acquired only by writing and delivery. One might ask that perhaps she transferred it together with land and kept the physical ketubah. He answers that purchasers would not rely on such a transfer unless she delivered the ketubah to them. Rav Hai Gaon writes, from this question, that when we say that once the buyer takes possession of the land, the document is acquired wherever it is, this applies only to the very deed of sale concerning that land, but not to an unrelated document. Rabbeinu Chananel likewise writes that letters, meaning documents, are not acquired by agav. End of the Rosh. This does not appear correct to me, because the Gemara in Perek HaMochel Es HaSefinah, Bava Basra 77b, clearly proves that any document may be acquired through agav. Otherwise, the Gemara's question there would not begin, and it would not answer, "agav is different, for a coin, etc." Rather, it could simply have answered that a deed concerning that very land is different. There is in truth no logical reason to distinguish between the deed of that land and another document. All the poskim understand that any document can be acquired through agav, and this is also the position of Tosafos and the Rosh himself in Perek HaSefinah. The Rosh's words at the end of the first chapter of Bava Metzia are therefore astonishing and contradict what he writes in Perek HaSefinah. His citation of Rabbeinu Chananel there is likewise surprising. I do not know where he found such words in Rabbeinu Chananel, or how Rabbeinu Chananel could disagree with an explicit Gemara. When the Rosh cites Rabbeinu Chananel in Perek HaSefinah as saying that documents are not acquired by agav, he means that they are not acquired by agav alone, but require writing as well. Baal HaItur, third maamar, Agav, folio 8b, writes: "Rabbeinu Chananel wrote that agav serves only in place of delivery, and they are acquired only with a document of transfer." The Maggid Mishneh at the end of chapter 6 of Hilchos Mechirah likewise writes that Rabbeinu Chananel held that agav stands only in place of delivery. This is also explicit in the Ramban's responsum in the name of Rabbeinu Chananel, cited by Baal HaTerumos, Shaar 51, end of part 3. As for what the Rosh writes in the name of Rav Hai Gaon, I found no such statement in any book or author. I did find in Rav Hai Gaon's Sefer HaMekach VeHaMemkar, Shaar 13, that where a person writes a deed of sale for a field and the buyer takes possession of the field, the deed is acquired wherever it may be because it is like movable property acquired with land. Perhaps the Rosh inferred from the fact that Rav Hai stated this specifically concerning the deed of that same land and not another document that Rav Hai, because of the question from the end of Bava Metzia, limited the rule to that case. But this is not a valid inference. Rav Hai merely adopted the Gemara's own formulation, and the natural implication is that there is no distinction. In any event, we have established that Rabbeinu Chananel holds like the authorities cited by Baal HaTerumos that agav stands in place of delivery but written transfer is still required. Although the other poskim disagree, and Baal HaItur writes that Rabbeinu Chananel's view is not convincing because the Gemara answers, "agav is different, for coins are not acquired by chalipin and yet are acquired by agav," implying that agav is like both writing and delivery since a coin is acquired through agav alone, nevertheless Rabbeinu Chananel's view appears primary. Why should agav itself be equivalent to both writing and delivery? The questioner in the Gemara thought that agav was an inferior kinyan and no stronger than other methods. The answer, "agav is different," establishes only that agav is a significant kinyan. With respect to the deed there, which had initially been written in the buyer's own name and therefore did not require a separate writing of transfer, agav could stand in place of delivery. The answerer never states that agav can substitute for the writing required to transfer an existing debt document. This understanding is also explicit in Tosafos there and in the Rosh there, for according to both the question and answer, agav functions only like delivery, while written transfer remains necessary. Baal HaItur's proof from coins is likewise no difficulty. The Gemara means that agav is a more significant acquisition than other methods, as we also find that a coin cannot be acquired by chalipin yet can be acquired with land. It does not mean that acquisition of a document is identical to acquisition of a coin. A coin itself is acquired by delivery alone, and therefore is also acquired by agav alone because agav substitutes for delivery. A document, however, is not acquired by delivery alone and additionally requires writing. Further, in my humble opinion, even what the Rosh and other poskim attribute to the Rif, that agav is equivalent to writing and delivery, is not compelled. It appears to me that the Rif's own view is the same as Tosafos and the Rosh's explanation of the Gemara. That is why the Rif writes: although documents are not acquired merely by delivery, they are acquired through agav, for coins are not acquired by chalipin yet are acquired with land. His meaning is that although documents are not acquired by simple delivery and require a significant kinyan, agav can effect that aspect because it is a significant kinyan, as shown by the law of coins. The Beit Yosef also writes that Baal HaTerumos, Shaar 50, cites the Raavad as agreeing with the Rambam and adds that physical delivery of the document is nevertheless preferable. I examined Baal HaTerumos there, Shaar 50, part 4, in the name of the Raavad, and it implies only that agav is equivalent to delivery. That is why he writes that actual delivery of the document is stronger than all the other methods. He may indeed hold like Rabbeinu Chananel, Tosafos, the Rosh in Perek HaSefinah, and the authorities cited in Baal HaTerumos who still require writing. The Beit Yosef further cites a responsum of the Rashba that "perhaps agav stands in place of writing and delivery, and so is the view of most commentators." This wording is also found in Responsa Rashba, siman 894. But he says only "perhaps," and it does not appear to be his practical ruling. I found in Responsa Rashba, siman 914, that he writes explicitly: even if documents are called property, so that one who writes and subjects all his property, movable property together with land, might appear to include his documents, this is not so. Even if one says that creating a lien is like a sale, nevertheless even a sale requires the transferor to write, "Acquire it and all the lien contained in it," as Rav Pappa says in Perek HaSefinah, and all the great poskim rule so, whereas here he did not write this. The Beit Yosef cites this briefly below in siman 104, new material 4. Thus it is explicit that agav stands only in place of delivery and that writing is required. This is the primary practical halachah. I later saw that Responsa Mahara ibn Chaim, siman 77, likewise agrees with this ruling. He writes that when the Sages said documents are acquired with land, they meant only the manner of their transfer, so that agav functions like delivery. But the additional requirement necessary for collection through a debt document, namely the formula, "Acquire it and all its liens," is not replaced by agav or by any other kinyan. He writes that the Rosh similarly states in a responsum, klal 76, siman 2, regarding a transfer of documents. The Rif's statement that documents are acquired through agav means that the transferor conveyed the underlying lien as well, and the Rashba also rules so in a responsum. Although his attribution to the Rosh is not precise, because the Rosh there says only that a statement of transfer is necessary, as the Tur cites him, but does not require a written instrument, and indeed the Rosh explicitly writes in that same klal, siman 3, that according to the Rif documents are acquired by agav without writing and delivery, nevertheless Mahara ibn Chaim's practical ruling that writing is required is correct, as I have demonstrated. It also appears that he overlooked the Rambam and many other poskim who explicitly write that agav substitutes for both writing and delivery, since he does not cite them there. Still, what he writes in the name of the Rashba's responsum and his practical conclusion that writing is required are correct.
A document that has not yet been written. The Sema writes that this differs from other objects, which a person can convey by saying, "Acquire with the land the cow or garment that I will acquire later," as appears in simanim 37 and 61, where one can subject later acquired movables to a lien. He was not precise. It is obvious that a person cannot convey property that he will acquire only later, as stated in the Gemara and all the poskim and explained below in siman 209. The cases above in simanim 37 and 61 concern liens, which are different: one can subject later acquired property to a lien, but cannot convey it by sale or gift, as is explicitly distinguished in the Gemara and all the poskim. Here, because this particular document concerns a loan that has already taken place, one might have thought that its status is stronger. So explains the Rivash in siman 210.
