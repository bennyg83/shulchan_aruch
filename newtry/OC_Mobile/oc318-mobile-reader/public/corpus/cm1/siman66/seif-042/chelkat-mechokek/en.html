Even though she shows the written marriage conditions between them. See Piskei Mahari, siman 89, where the written conditions contained no kinyan. This somewhat implies that had there been a kinyan, she could collect on the basis of those conditions. But see Choshen Mishpat 245, which rules in the name of Mahari Weil that a kinyan to "give" in the future is ineffective. A man who writes during the matchmaking stage that he will later provide his wife a ketubah, even if a kinyan is made, has only a future-giving kinyan and it is ineffective. Mahari's statement in the cited responsum that the ketubah is in fact written and signed pursuant to a kinyan and the husband's instruction, but that this accomplished nothing in the particular case, referred to a case where the ketubah had only one valid witness and the second was disqualified, as the Rama quotes here. Mahari further writes that the rule that a kinyan does not require witnesses applies to a kinyan made directly from the person conferring the right to the recipient, but not to a kinyan of this kind. These words are difficult. Why should this kinyan made on the wedding day, by which he encumbers his property for a large ketubah obligation, differ from any other kinyan? The Beit Yosef already writes in this siman that Mahari's formulation is difficult.
