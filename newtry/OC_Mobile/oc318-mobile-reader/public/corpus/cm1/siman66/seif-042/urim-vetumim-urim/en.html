Likewise if he admitted, etc. Even if the document contains a trust clause so that no oath would ordinarily be required, once the seller admits that he was paid he is believed and must compensate the purchaser. This appears to be the view of many authorities. Baal HaTerumos and Ra'avad, however, hold that where the document contains a trust clause he is not believed to say, "I was paid," even though he has a migo that he could have waived the debt. In Tumim I left requiring study whether the party in possession can say that he follows those authorities. If the document states that the debtor obligates himself to the lender and to anyone deriving rights through him, the Mechaber appears below in seif 26 to hold that the debt cannot be waived. In such a case the seller is likewise not believed to say, "I was paid," because he lacks a migo of waiver. See Tumim, where I concluded that even if the document contains no trust clause, so that the seller would ordinarily have to swear and cannot swear, nevertheless once he lacks the power to waive, he is not believed to say, "I was paid." What purpose would that oath serve? See there, and see above in siman 47 in Tumim.
