A person on his deathbed, etc. Even when he said only, 'My property,' we interpret his words as including the notes and all their liens. Maimonides and the Tur rule below that if a dying person says, 'Give this promissory note to so-and-so,' the recipient acquires the note as though it had been written and delivered, even though he did not physically take it. The Maggid Mishneh and Ran understand this to mean that the dying person need not expressly say, 'and all its liens.' Baal HaTerumot agrees. The Rashba, however, requires those words when a particular note is singled out, although he concedes that a general gift of all property includes the notes and their liens. Nimukei Yosef reports the Rashba's view. The wording of Maimonides is not conclusive, since he may merely have repeated the Gemara's abbreviated language, which the Rashba interprets as implicitly including the necessary phrase. Mahari Beirav therefore ruled that the inheritance should not be removed from the heirs unless the dying person expressly transferred the note and all its liens, because no clear proof establishes the broader reading. Mahari Ibn Sasson also leans this way and understands the Ran as agreeing with the Rashba. On the other hand, the Rosh clearly writes that a dying person's verbal direction has the force of writing and delivery, and in another responsum he explicitly rules that saying, 'Give this note to so-and-so,' transfers the note rather than merely the paper. The Bach follows this view. The matter therefore still requires study, because the interpretation of Maimonides and Ran remains uncertain, while Rabbenu Yerucham cites only the Rashba's restrictive rule. See also Responsa Maharam Alshich, siman 4.
Therefore, a husband who relinquished, etc. This is effective only while she is still betrothed, including the time immediately before entry beneath the chuppah. Maharik, Rivash, and the Maggid Mishneh all speak of that circumstance. After marriage, a mere relinquishment is ineffective. The phrase 'at the time of entry beneath the chuppah' refers to a woman who is still only betrothed, excluding our practice in which betrothal and chuppah occur together. Before betrothal, the relinquishment is also ineffective, as the Rashba, Ran, Mechaber, and Rama rule in Even HaEzer, siman 92. Thus, the Rama's words here must likewise be understood as referring to a writing made while she was betrothed. Under our practice, where betrothal occurs at the chuppah, such a relinquishment made only then is ineffective. Examine this carefully.
