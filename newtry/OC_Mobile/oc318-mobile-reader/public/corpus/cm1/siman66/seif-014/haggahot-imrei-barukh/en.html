(Netivot HaMishpat, same passage) And the defendant contradict each other. Note: This requires study. If so, why did Abaye say that if the benefit of discretion belonged to the husband, the witnesses could say to her, "What loss did we cause you? Your husband would have taken it from you"? This indicates that when the husband comes to take from her, we do not simply follow his admission that she is not his wife and therefore that he has no right to her benefit of discretion, a right that never came into his possession. Rather, the baraita states that she is presently living under him and serving him. It therefore appears that even according to the husband's account, he had divorced her and then remarried her, and before the second marriage she removed his rights from her nichsei tzon barzel. The witnesses, after being rendered zomemim, likewise testify to this, and are charged according to the estimated value explained in the baraita. Since even on the husband's version she is now his wife and he can inherit her, he also could have taken the benefit of discretion from her if the law were that this benefit belongs to the husband.
