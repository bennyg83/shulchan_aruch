Even though the underlying loan itself already creates liability and the debt instrument serves as evidence, the Sages had to create a mode for transferring the debt because otherwise there would be no effective acquisition and commercial lending would be obstructed. Several early authorities struggled with the question whether sale of debt instruments is biblical or rabbinic precisely because a debt instrument seems merely evidentiary. In my view a debt instrument is always also an instrument of acquisition or lien. Although the debtor already became obligated through the money, the document itself can impose an additional lien. A lien can attach upon an existing lien, much as Tosafot writes regarding redemption of the firstborn that property acquired later can become newly subjected at each moment even though the redemption obligation already existed. Personal obligations can likewise accumulate. This differs from acquisition of land. Once the land is already acquired, a second act cannot acquire the same land anew because one cannot transfer what is no longer his. A debt, however, can carry a renewed or additional personal or property lien through the instrument. Therefore the debt instrument is not merely evidence. Its legal content explains why it can itself be sold through writing and delivery.
