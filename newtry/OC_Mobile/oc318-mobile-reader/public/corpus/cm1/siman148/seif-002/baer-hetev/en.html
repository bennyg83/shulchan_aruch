I will return. We will be able to see Hashem’s promise, and even if He does not return His blood, and He will return to Him as a result of Hashem’s promise to him, and He will not give him any of the sins of Hashem’s name. Name:
Finally. For he who is now a husband who is a man of his head, but “If it is one and has one like him.” A:
Chazakah. The Maggid Mishneh explains the Rambam this way, and the ruling applies to all the details in this siman: once he has taken possession, we do not remove him. This is unlike the Kesef Mishneh, who explains 'has taken possession' as meaning that he forcibly seized it from the field owner, and whose words imply that the rule applies only to the immediately preceding case. Sema. In truth, that is indeed the plain implication of the Kesef Mishneh and Bedek HaBayit. It nevertheless appears to me that the rule applies to the entire siman, yet even so three years of chazakah are unnecessary. Since this person is presently in possession and asserts a definite claim, the others are the ones seeking to remove property from him. Because their claim is uncertain, they cannot remove it without clear proof. Shach.
