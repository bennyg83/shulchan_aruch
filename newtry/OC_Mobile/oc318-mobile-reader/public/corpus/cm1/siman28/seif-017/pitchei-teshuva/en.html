In the glosses of the Gaon Maharam, author of Tiferet LeMoshe, he writes: It is true that the Tur states that both factors are required, but from Tosafot in HaMafkid and in the second chapter of Ketubot, cited by the Beit Yosef, it appears clear that when the beit din already knows that the opposing party is violent, no separate proof is required that the witnesses are afraid. A violent person need not bring witnesses that he is not the claimant's brother; rather, he brings the witnesses whom his opponent says he is intimidating, and they testify in court that they do not know the vessels. This is the case discussed by Tosafot in HaMafkid. Where the court does not know that he is violent, proof is required, and that is the case in Ketubot. This is clear. Urim VeTumim, however, holds that even where the court knows he is violent, proof that the witnesses are afraid is still required. Yet once such proof exists, there is no need for the court independently to know that he is violent, since the witnesses' statement, 'Why should we bring trouble upon ourselves by testifying against him,' itself establishes the intimidation. Even if he is not objectively violent, the witnesses are in fact afraid of him, and therefore he must bring proof; see there.
