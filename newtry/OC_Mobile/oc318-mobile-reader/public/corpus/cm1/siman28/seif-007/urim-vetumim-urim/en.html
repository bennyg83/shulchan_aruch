And he is despised in the eyes of the one who hired him. The Gemara says that false witnesses are degraded even in the eyes of those who hire them. Sema asks that this seems identical to Rambam’s warning that false witnesses suffer shame in this world, and therefore Rambam states the shame but not the degradation in the hirer’s eyes, while Tur states the latter but not the former. In my view both are needed. If the hirers themselves are base people, why should the witness care about being despised by them? Rather, because even they despise him, they will ultimately publicize his disgrace and respectable people will learn of his wickedness. That is the shame in this world. The witness may initially think no one will know that he lied, so the court tells him that even those who hired him despise him and will reveal his conduct. Both statements are therefore true and necessary. From Ketubot it also appears that although witnesses are warned, we do not tell them explicitly that if they are discredited through hazamah they will pay, receive lashes, or be executed. Otherwise this would itself be the greatest possible warning, and the Gemara’s discussion that plotting witnesses require no prior warning would be impossible. Ritva likewise indicates this.
