[Shach, letter 6] We do not place him under a ban. Note: Nevertheless, we do not permit the witness to testify initially, because ultimately money will be taken from the Jew unlawfully on the basis of his testimony, since the Jew did not admit before their secular court. What we rule in seif 4, that two witnesses may testify, applies specifically to an ordinary loan, where there is a desecration of Hashem's Name. But where the gentile does not know of the debt, so that there is no desecration of Hashem's Name, the witnesses are not permitted to testify, because the Jew has acquired the money under the law governing a debt from which a gentile can be lawfully released. Nevertheless, the witnesses are not placed under a ban. If two Jews are quarreling and went to litigate in a secular court, and one Jew came and testified, causing money to be extracted, although he did not act properly among his people, because he made our enemies into judges and the gentile judgment was completed on the basis of his testimony, it is proper to punish him as the judge sees fit, according to the circumstances and cause. Even two witnesses should not testify without permission of the halachic authority and the communal leaders. Nevertheless, he is not placed under a ban. This is explained in Yam Shel Shlomo there. Responsa Rama, siman 52, writes that it is only in a dispute between a gentile and a Jew that one may testify where their law accords with our law. But if one Jew litigates against another, it is forbidden to testify, because this assists transgressors. Although the prohibition of placing a stumbling block before the blind does not apply where the transgressor could commit the prohibition without him, it remains rabbinically forbidden. See Responsa Zera Avraham, Choshen Mishpat, siman 12.
