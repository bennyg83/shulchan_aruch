Afterward they send everyone outside. This is Rambam's language, based on the mishnah in Zeh Borer, Sanhedrin 29. The Tur omits it. In any event, "everyone" is not literal, because ordinarily the litigants remain when testimony is given. At first glance one might say the witnesses first testified before the litigants and the others, saying generally that so-and-so owes so-and-so, and afterward everyone, including the litigants, is sent out while each witness is examined separately. The mishnah and poskim say they ask each one, "How do you know he owes him?" rather than asking him again to state the debt, suggesting the basic testimony has already been given. But this cannot be correct. The Gemara on Sanhedrin 30 asks, "afterward they bring them in, whom do they bring in? If the litigants, they were there all along," and Rashi explains that the mishnah never said the litigants were removed. Thus the Gemara takes it as obvious that "they send everyone outside" does not include the litigants.
“The G-d that owes it to it and if he says he must give me a name.”
He told me, "I owe him," etc. In such a case we plead for the debtor that he said it merely so as not to appear wealthy, as explained above and at the beginning of siman 32.
Or he says, "He admitted to him before me." The Tur writes that he must say, "You are my witnesses." The Mechaber follows his position in siman 81, adopting Rambam and Ramah, that a full admission made before witnesses can have the same force as saying, "You are my witnesses."
