She is not believed to say that initially she accepted it for the sake of kiddushin. This refers to the beginning of the ruling, where he betrothed her with property that he had stolen from her, without prior shidduchin, and she accepted it silently. She is not believed to say that she accepted it for the sake of kiddushin and consented to his act, because the Sages assessed a woman's intent as not wishing to become betrothed with her own stolen property unless there had been prior shidduchin or she expressly said yes, as explained by the Ran. But in the latter clause, where he gave her something silently, how could she say that she accepted it for kiddushin if he said nothing at the time of giving? It is possible that this gloss is a scribal error and should be placed at the beginning.
