m) Like the first Tanna against Rabbi Yosi bar Yehudah, who holds that standing while leaning from the side, meaning one cannot remain in that position without support, is not considered standing, Zevachim 16a. This also follows from the apparent contradiction between the verse "and I sat on the mountain," etc., Megillah 21a.
In Even HaEzer, Hilkhot Chalitzah, it appears that leaning is considered standing, for it says that they should stand the yavam near a wall or pillar so that he may lean upon it. It further writes that throughout the chalitzah proceedings the judges sit while the yavam and yevamah stand, because it resembles a judicial proceeding. It appears to me that there it does not mean true leaning. Examine the wording "to lean," and the reason for placing him near a wall or pillar is so that his leg can be pushed, but he must not lean so heavily that if the pillar were removed he would fall. So too writes the Rama there in the order of chalitzah, page 45.
