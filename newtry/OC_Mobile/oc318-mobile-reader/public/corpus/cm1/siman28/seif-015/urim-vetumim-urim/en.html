Not in the presence of the litigant. See Tumim, where I cited Kenesset HaGedolah that if the witnesses do not know the litigant, this is treated as testimony not given in his presence. This must mean that there is a reasonable possibility that they will recognize him in the near future. Otherwise, they may testify despite his absence, as explained there.
Although at first they testified by force of the cherem, etc. The Sema writes that the same applies if they swore to tell the truth. See Tumim, where I wrote that this rule requires study and discussed it at length. I concluded that if they swore that whatever they would say was true, they certainly cannot later retract and give different testimony. But if they swore merely that they would testify truthfully, one may say their intent was to testify where their testimony has legal effect, namely in the presence of the litigant. Even this requires study. See there.
And some disagree. Maharshal rules that even after the fact, testimony received outside the litigant's presence is ineffective. The Shach cites him.
It is effective after the fact. If the litigant expressly warns the court, "Do not receive testimony outside my presence," then all agree it is ineffective. Kenesset HaGedolah. In a matter of issur, such as testimony that a married woman committed adultery, since testimony outside the husband's presence is ineffective to establish the prohibition, it is likewise ineffective to cause her to lose her ketubah. Rama in a responsum.
