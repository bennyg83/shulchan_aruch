Testimony is not received outside the presence of the litigant against whom it is offered. If it was received in his absence, judgment is not rendered on its basis. {Rama: The witnesses testify again in his presence. If their second testimony differs from their first, the testimony given in his presence is followed, even if the first was given under a ban, because testimony outside his presence is like testimony outside beit din and may be restated (Beit Yosef in the name of Rabbi Menachem). Others hold that testimony received outside his presence is valid after the fact. Therefore, if the litigant is violent and the witnesses fear testifying before him, testimony may be received in his absence and judgment rendered upon it (Piskei Maharai, simanim 175 and 177). If he can be compelled as explained above in this siman, seif 5, that is preferable. Some say that the requirement to receive testimony in the litigant's presence applies only to monetary cases. In quarrels where testimony before the parties could cause them to fight with the witnesses and with one another, the Geonim enacted that testimony be received in their absence and that the witnesses' identities not be disclosed. It appears to me that this applies only when the beit din does not intend to punish or adjudicate either party under strict law, but merely to quiet the quarrel and direct matters as its circumstances require. If it intends to punish or adjudicate one party, testimony may be received only in his presence, as explained in Even HaEzer, siman 11, seif 4. See also simanim 1 and 12.}
