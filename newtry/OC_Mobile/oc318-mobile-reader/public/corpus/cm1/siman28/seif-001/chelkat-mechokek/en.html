And it became known that he acquired it. This is Rambam's language in chapter 5 of Hilchot Ishut. Its simple implication is that ordinary robbery does not create presumed ye'ush, and therefore Rambam says it became known that he acquired the stolen object, meaning there are witnesses that the owners despaired of recovery. But if at the time of kiddushin it was still unknown whether they had despaired, even if it later became known that ye'ush had already occurred before kiddushin, that resembles ye'ush without knowledge. Bach asks that here it appears ordinary robbery does not create ye'ush, consistent with Kiddushin 52a, which says no one follows Rabbi Shimon's view that ordinary robbery causes ye'ush. Yet Bava Kamma 114a records Rav Ashi that with Jewish robbers, who are subject to ordinary adjudication, owners generally despair. The sugya there and the dispute between the Sages and Rabbi Shimon over thief and robber are difficult to reconcile. Tur in Choshen Mishpat 368 cites two views. The first, Baal HaMaor, holds that among Jews the distinction between thief and robber remains, while among gentiles, who act by force, even a thief may not cause ye'ush. The second, which Tur attributes to Rosh, reverses the framework: among Jews, who are subject to adjudication, even armed robbery ordinarily produces ye'ush, and the dispute of Sages and Rabbi Shimon concerns gentile thieves and robbers. Bach attempts to prove that Rosh indeed disagrees with Baal HaMaor, but the proof is not compelling. Yam Shel Shlomo likewise challenges Tur and concludes like Baal HaMaor that ordinary robbery does not create ye'ush even stringently, so a woman betrothed with such property is not betrothed, in accordance with the straightforward Gemara here. With theft, however, there is a distinction between a Jew and a gentile. According to this, the Shulchan Aruch's grouping of theft and robbery together is difficult. Since the discussion here is about Jews, ordinary theft should involve ye'ush. We must therefore explain that the phrase 'if not' means where it is definitely known that the owners did not despair, not merely where ye'ush is unproven. Rambam in Hilchot Gezelah and Shulchan Aruch, Choshen Mishpat 368, appear to distinguish between a gentile robber and a Jewish robber in a way that tends toward Tur's view. Yet Rambam in Hilchot Kelim 24 distinguishes thief and robber in a manner implying that ordinary Jewish robbery does not create ye'ush, apparently contradicting his own Hilchot Gezelah. This requires study.
