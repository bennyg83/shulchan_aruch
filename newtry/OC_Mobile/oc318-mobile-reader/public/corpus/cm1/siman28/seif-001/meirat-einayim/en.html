Meaning even if, after initially withholding testimony and after the litigant already paid, the witnesses later admit that they knew testimony that would have exempted him, they are not liable in human courts. This applies, for example, where the claimant collected on a document and they knew it had already been paid, and all the more so where they knew testimony obligating the defendant and initially suppressed it. One might have thought that since their earlier failure caused financial loss, and after they have already stated in court that they knew nothing they cannot later retract because "once he has testified, he cannot testify again," they should be believed at least against themselves and be liable under the laws of causative damage. We find similar cases below in siman 29 and at the end of siman 46, where witnesses who say, "This is our handwriting, but the document was one of trust," may be believed regarding their own liability. The law here teaches that withheld testimony does not create such human-court liability.
