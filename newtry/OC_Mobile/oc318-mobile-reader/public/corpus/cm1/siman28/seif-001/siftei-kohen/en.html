“Anybody who knows the testimony of his son.” He said, “And he will not be able to tell him that he will not be given a secret that he will not be able to reveal it, and he will not reveal it to him.”
And he is liable under the laws of Heaven. Maharshal writes in Yam Shel Shlomo, Bava Kamma, chapter HaKones, siman 6: Whenever we say that one is liable under the laws of Heaven, if the injured party seized property, it is removed from him. This is so even according to Rashi, who explains in the chapter HaSocher et HaOmanin, regarding one who muzzles a cow and threshes with it, that although he is exempt because the greater punishment absorbs the lesser, he remains obligated to satisfy the demands of Heaven, and that if the injured party seized property it is not removed from him, as Or Zarua likewise rules. In the present type of case, however, all agree that it is removed from him. Where the rule of the greater punishment applies, the defendant is intrinsically liable, but the court does not impose two punishments and instead applies the more severe one. Therefore, where the severe punishment is not imposed, such as where the act was inadvertent, without warning, or in our time, logic supports that property seized should not be removed, since the monetary liability still exists. But where the matter is merely indirect causation of damage and there is no legal liability at all, except an obligation to satisfy the demands of Heaven, it is obvious that seized property is removed. The Rivash, siman 392, writes similarly. See below, siman 182, seif katan 1. Maharshal further writes there that one may not pressure him even verbally, without coercion.
