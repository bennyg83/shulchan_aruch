And fit. This excludes a relative or otherwise disqualified witness. Even according to the opinion that a communal cherem applies to relatives, so long as no cherem has actually been imposed they are not included in the sin of withholding testimony, because even if they testify they cannot impose monetary liability. Beit Yosef. Sema.
Benefit. This excludes a case where the witnesses did not see the loan but merely heard the borrower admit it without saying, "You are my witnesses." He can then say that he intended only to avoid appearing wealthy, as explained below in seif 8 and in simanim 32 and 81. Even if they heard him admit the debt in response to the claimant's demand, they need not testify merely so that their testimony will force him to swear that he had been joking. Even without their testimony, if the claimant says, "You admitted before so-and-so," the law is the same, unless the defendant denies ever making the admission. In that case too, their testimony gives no practical benefit because he nevertheless remains exempt from an oath, as the Tur writes at the beginning of siman 32. Thus the Sema.
With him. Even if the other witness is not presently available and he wishes to wait until that witness arrives so that together they can impose monetary liability, the law teaches that he may not delay; he must testify immediately. Sema.
Alone. Because one witness can impose a Torah oath, and perhaps the defendant will choose to pay rather than swear. The testimony can therefore produce a benefit. Sema.
Under human law. The Sema writes that even two witnesses who suppress their testimony are exempt under human law. For example, if a claimant sues on a promissory note and the witnesses know it was paid, and certainly if they know that the defendant owes money but initially withheld their testimony and later admit that they knew relevant testimony, they can no longer be believed because once a witness has given his testimony he cannot retract or replace it. One might have thought they should be believed at least to make themselves liable under dina d'garmi, similar to the cases in siman 29 and at the end of siman 46 where witnesses say, "This is our handwriting, but the document was issued on trust," etc. The rule teaches that here they are not liable. The Shach writes in the name of Maharshal that one should not pressure such a person even verbally where there is no legal compulsion. See the Bach above, siman 12, seif 4.
Heaven. If the claimant seized property on account of the witnesses' heavenly liability, it is removed from him. Shach in the name of Maharshal.
He should frighten him. The Gemara forbids this because of the verse, "Distance yourself from a false matter." Meaning, perhaps the defendant will settle out of fear even though he does not actually owe the claimant. Sema.
