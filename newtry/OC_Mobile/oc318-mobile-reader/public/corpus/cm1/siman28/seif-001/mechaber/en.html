How Witnesses Are Admonished, and Testimony Is Not Received Outside a Litigant's Presence. Twenty-six seifim. Anyone who knows testimony for another, is fit to testify, and whose testimony would benefit that person must testify if asked to do so in beit din, whether another witness is with him or he is alone. If he suppresses his testimony, he is exempt under human law but liable under the laws of Heaven. {Rama: A single witness should testify only in a monetary matter where his testimony will require an oath, or in a matter of prohibition where it can prevent a transgression. If the transgression already occurred, he should not testify, because he would merely defame the other person (Hagahos Maimoniyos, chapter 5 of Hilchos Edus). A person may not testify to something he does not know, even if someone whom he knows does not lie told it to him. Even if that person asks him merely to stand beside his one genuine witness, without testifying, so that the debtor will fear that there are two witnesses and admit the debt, he must not comply (Tur).}
