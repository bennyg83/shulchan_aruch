Regarding an appraisal based on estimation, perhaps the concern for withholding testimony does not apply in the same way, because they choose evaluators only when the matter is clear to them. Although the verse says 'or he knew,' an estimation is not the same as direct knowledge, and therefore the decision follows the majority of evaluations even against two or more. Before one has determined the nature of the matter, it is included in the rule that a person is not obligated to go observe an event in order to become a witness. Accordingly, where testimony is useful only because of accepted practice, such as in quarrels, and the person is not technically a valid witness but the parties would rely on him if he came forward, perhaps he has no formal obligation to testify. Nevertheless, by reason alone it appears that once he now knows the matter he should testify.
