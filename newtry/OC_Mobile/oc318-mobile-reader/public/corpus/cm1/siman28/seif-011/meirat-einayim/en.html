And this is the practice. Tosafot, Rosh, Mordechai, and Tur cite Rabbeinu Tam that a witness may send written testimony so long as he remembers the testimony and is capable of testifying orally. This excludes a mute person, who cannot fulfill the verse, "by the mouth of two witnesses." Rabbeinu Tam reasons that where a person is fit for the required act, actual performance of that form is not indispensable. Their wording implies that this is the accepted view. I have seen teachers of practical halachah avoid burdening a Torah scholar to attend court and instead ask him to write his testimony in his own hand and send it to beit din. In my view this is proper, because according to Rabbeinu Tam and those who follow him, even one who is not a Torah scholar may do so.
