If the lender sold it to another, and he used it for kiddushin. Meaning that the purchaser then used it to betroth a woman. This is explicit in Responsa Mahari Weil, siman 135, and is also evident from the continuation, 'and even if the lender himself...' One who studies the responsum will see that some, and perhaps all, of the reasons given there may apply even if the lender himself uses the pledge for kiddushin. This is not identical to actual robbery, but resembles evasion of a gentile debt. Although prohibited because of desecration of Hashem's name, perhaps the prohibition is like avak ribit and other rabbinic prohibitions, where kiddushin performed with a prohibited item nevertheless take effect. Moreover, perhaps one can evade the gentile through a stratagem, claiming that the pledge was lost, in which case its retention may be legally permitted and perhaps there is not even desecration of Hashem's name. If so, the words of Raavyah in the Mordechai, cited by Rama immediately before this, and the words of Mahari Weil contradict one another. It is difficult that Rama appears to uphold both positions.
