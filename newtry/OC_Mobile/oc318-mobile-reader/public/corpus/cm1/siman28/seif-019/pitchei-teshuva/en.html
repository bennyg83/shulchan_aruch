The language of the testimony. See Tashbetz, volume 1, end of siman 160. Among his criticisms concerning the rabbinic position of the Rivash in Algiers, he writes that the Rivash did not sufficiently understand the language spoken by the people of the country, and therefore could not properly receive testimony, because the court must hear testimony directly. He adds that this is not limited to the Sanhedrin, as shown by the Gemara's discussion of foreign-language speakers. Birkei Yosef above, siman 17, letter 13, observes that he could likewise have objected that, according to the Rambam, even the litigants themselves must be heard without an interpreter. This objection may be less compelling according to the Sema there, seif katan 14. Birkei Yosef, letter 15, further writes that Radbaz, siman 335, concludes that if there are three persons who know the language, they may receive the testimony and the judges may decide on the basis of that testimony. If there are not three such persons and no judge knows the language, the testimony may be received through an interpreter; see there.
