Not in the presence of the litigant. Dagul MeRevavah writes that concerning receiving testimony regarding a wife's nichsei melog or nichsei tzon barzel in the husband's presence but not hers, see Even HaEzer siman 85 and Beit Shmuel seif katan 7. Shaar Mishpat discusses the case of a claim against two partners where one partner is absent and testimony is received in the presence of the partner who is there. He notes that later authorities did not clearly address whether this receipt is effective against the absent partner, but that the matter is explained in the Rashba at the end of HaSholeach and in the Ran there, which the Beit Shmuel cites. Knesset HaGedolah, Hagahot HaTur, letter 70, writes that where the witnesses do not recognize the litigant, even if he is standing there the testimony is considered to have been received not in his presence. He derives this from Sanhedrin in the case of a murderer mixed among others. Shevut Yaakov, volume 1, siman 148, disagrees and concludes that for purposes of receiving testimony this is considered receipt in his presence. Tumim challenges Shevut Yaakov and agrees with Knesset HaGedolah, but limits the rule to a case where it is possible that the witnesses will recognize him within a short time, so that one should wait. If recognition will not be possible, testimony is certainly received, for this is no worse than where the witnesses are dangerously ill and testimony is received so that it not be lost. See also Noda BiYehudah, Even HaEzer, siman 42, and Pitchei Teshuva there.
