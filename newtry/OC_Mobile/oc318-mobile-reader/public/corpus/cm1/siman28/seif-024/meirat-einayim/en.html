He has not lost his right, etc. The same applies where successive groups of witnesses contradicted one another. See the end of siman 31.
Testimony is accepted only during the day, etc. Receiving testimony has the status of adjudication. Just as litigants can accept upon themselves judgment at night, as explained in siman 5, comparable acceptance can affect receipt of testimony.
We do not judge on the basis of that acceptance. This differs from Rama's citation above in siman 5 that according to some, if judges actually adjudicated at night the judgment stands. There it is a genuinely completed after-the-fact act. Here they still seek now to adjudicate based on testimony improperly received at night, so the present use of that testimony is not a completed after-the-fact situation. Ir Shushan's explanation based on different verses is unnecessary, because both the beginning of adjudication and receipt of testimony derive from the relevant daytime rules as explained in this siman.
