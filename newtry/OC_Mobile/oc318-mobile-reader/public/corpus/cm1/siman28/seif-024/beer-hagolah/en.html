t) The Sema writes that this is not comparable to what the Rama writes above, siman 5, seif 8, where some say that if they judged at night their judgment is valid, because there it is truly after the fact. Here, however, they wish initially to adjudicate based on testimony taken at night. The Bach writes that the two laws are equal, and even testimony accepted at night may be used for judgment, in accordance with the Rashbam. I found the same in the words of the Ramban cited by the Rosh in Bava Batra, page 207, siman 74, that according to the Yerushalmi, if night proceedings are valid for judgment, the same applies to testimony. Although I am not worthy to decide the matter, it appears to me that if the witnesses have already gone overseas, judgment may be rendered on that testimony. This also appears from the wording of Tosafot there at the beginning of 114a and Rosh Hashanah 36b, s.v. "if they wished." It appears to me, however, that there they discuss the beginning of judgment, and the opinion cited by the Rama that if they transgressed and judged at night the judgment is valid must be understood as a case where they began at night but concluded by day. Here, by contrast, the entire testimony was accepted at night. Examine this carefully.
