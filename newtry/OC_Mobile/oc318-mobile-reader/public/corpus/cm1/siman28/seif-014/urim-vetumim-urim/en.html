And if the witness is a Torah scholar, etc. According to Rambam this applies where the litigant is a Torah scholar. Sema says Tur accepts both formulations. Rama writes in responsum that just as we no longer apply the old fixed fine of a litra of gold for insulting a Torah scholar, so too we should not use the special rule allowing one to remind a witness because of present-day Torah scholarship. Although there is room to question this, for the Gemara itself speaks of a Torah scholar and I have elsewhere distinguished different rabbinic titles, Rama has ruled and one should not reject his decision without clear proof. In Tumim I further explain that even if the witness is present and beit din is questioning him, and he says he does not remember unless reminded, one may not prompt an ordinary witness. If, however, the litigant conveys the relevant facts to beit din or its agent and they then remind the witness, this can be permitted because the witness is being prompted by another party rather than directly by the litigant.
