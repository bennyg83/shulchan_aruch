She is betrothed only out of doubt. The reason is that she does not rely upon the debt because the lender may waive it to the borrower. Nevertheless, there is doubtful kiddushin because of the value of the paper. Tur writes that if the paper is worth a perutah she is certainly betrothed. The Mechaber, however, holds that even then there is only doubtful kiddushin because the parties intended the body of the debt, not the paper, which belongs to the borrower. The Mechaber's formulation proves that in the first clause he intended a definite ruling that she is certainly betrothed, and cited the 'some say' only to note that others disagree. Otherwise, if the first clause itself were only doubtful kiddushin, he should have written 'and likewise,' not 'but.' I cited above, siman 11, seif katan 11, Responsa Menahem Azariah that whenever the Mechaber first states a rule anonymously and then cites another view as 'some say,' he intends to rule like the anonymous first view.
