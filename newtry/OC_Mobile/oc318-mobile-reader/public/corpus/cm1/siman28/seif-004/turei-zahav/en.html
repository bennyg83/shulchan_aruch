See there “All the more so two witnesses,” etc. Maharshal infers from Tosafot that even two should not testify initially in gentile court, while Rosh and Mordechai imply that they may. The views can be reconciled. Rosh and Mordechai concern a case where evading the gentile debt is forbidden because of desecration of God’s name. Tosafot concerns a case without such concern, where testimony would deprive the Jew of a lawful opportunity to evade payment. Maharshal further rules that only two witnesses may testify where evasion is forbidden; a single witness may not, because gentile court would extract money on testimony insufficient under Torah law. If two Jews litigate in gentile court and one Jewish witness causes money to be taken, he acted improperly and should be punished. In my view he is also excommunicated when the collection was unlawful, even though another Jew benefited. The Gemara’s mention of testimony for a gentile is a greater novelty, teaching that even there two witnesses may testify when appropriate.
