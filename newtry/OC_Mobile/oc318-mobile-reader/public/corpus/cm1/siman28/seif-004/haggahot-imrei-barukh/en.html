(Seif katan 3) Gloss, etc., and what he brought as proof, etc. Note: One who studies the Mabit will see that he did not bring this as a proof. He cited it only to reject the scholar who wrote that witnesses become liable for an oath only if they explicitly say, "We swear that we do not know," or answer amen. The Mabit then adds, in the name of Mahari Beir citing the Ramban, that even if they remain silent they are liable. So too the Rashba writes in his novellae to Shevuot 31a in the name of the Ramban. His novellae to Bava Kamma 56a, in the passage beginning "it is obvious," also appear to say this. If so, however, that would contradict what he wrote in his novellae to Shevuot, where he disagrees with the Ramban.
