After ye'ush alone, she is betrothed rabbinically. I already wrote above, seif katan 2, citing Maggid Mishneh, that although ye'ush alone does not acquire for the robber, the transfer into the woman's possession creates a change of domain. At first glance Rabbeinu Yerucham's words here disagree. Perhaps he is discussing a case where he betrothed her with property he had robbed from her herself after she despaired, so there is no change of domain, as Tur writes in the name of the Ramah. See Bach.
