He may testify for him if he is sued. This is so even in a jurisdiction where a defendant is ordinarily required to swear upon denial and one supporting witness can exempt him from that oath. Although without the witness he might have paid rather than swear, testimony is still permitted, because under Jewish law too one supporting witness can exempt from a hesset oath.
All the more so two witnesses may testify. Meaning where the non-Jewish claimant extracts money through two witnesses.
Where money is not extracted. The correct text is: where money is not extracted on the basis of one witness, two may testify. The Rosh rules that even an important person, against whom a foreign court may extract on the testimony of one witness because he cannot easily evade the proceeding, may testify where appropriate. See the Derishah.
For under our law. The correct reading is: 'money is not extracted through them as through one witness.' Just as one Jewish witness may not testify in a foreign court that will extract money on the basis of his lone testimony when Jewish law would not, so too two witnesses who are relatives to each other may not testify in a system that will extract money on their combined testimony, because Jewish law would not treat them as two valid witnesses.
