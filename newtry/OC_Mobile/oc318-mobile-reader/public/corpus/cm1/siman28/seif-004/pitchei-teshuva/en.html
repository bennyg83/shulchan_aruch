Even without a demand, there is an obligation under 'You shall not stand idly by your fellow's blood' whenever a person knows evidence establishing his fellow's right and the latter will suffer loss because he refrains from testifying. The Rambam states this explicitly in Sefer HaMitzvos, mitzvah 297, in the name of the Sifrei. There is also an obligation of returning a lost object. See there. Shaar Mishpat likewise discusses this at length and questions the rule. He concludes that, were it not for the words of the Beit Yosef and Sema, he would explain the Rambam and Tur, who require that the litigant demand his testimony, as excluding only a case where a person knows evidence relevant to his fellow but the litigants have not yet begun litigation, or they are litigating but he does not know whether the case depends on his testimony. In such a case he is not obligated to go to beit din on his own initiative to testify. By contrast, in matters of prohibition or capital cases he must go on his own initiative. In monetary cases as well, it is obvious that he must testify when he knows that the litigants are presently litigating and that the matter depends on him. This can also be proven from the words of the Ra'ah in Sefer HaChinuch, mitzvah 122, who writes that in monetary matters one is not obligated to testify on his own initiative unless the litigant or beit din demands it. See there. He concludes that this is clearly correct in his view, unlike the Beit Yosef and Sema. See also Tiferet Yaakov and Haamek Sheelah on Sheiltot, parashat Vayikra, sheilta 69, regarding this issue.
