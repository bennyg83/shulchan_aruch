A consideration of separating another from a prohibition. See Birkei Yosef, letter 12, regarding a concern that the defendant may swear falsely in order to contradict the second witness brought by the claimant. He discusses whether a Torah scholar must testify before a beit din inferior to him, and his words imply three distinctions. Ordinarily the scholar need not go, because it is unlikely that the defendant will swear falsely when another witness already exists, since he fears that the scholar may later testify as well, leaving him liable to pay and subject to punishment and disqualification. If the possibility is evenly balanced, as in the case discussed in Masat Moshe, siman 63, where the defendant knew with certainty that the scholar would not go before the rabbi with whom he was in conflict, Masat Moshe inclines to require the scholar to testify even though it is only doubtful that the defendant will swear falsely, but does not decide conclusively. If it is close to certain that he will swear falsely, the scholar must testify; see there.
