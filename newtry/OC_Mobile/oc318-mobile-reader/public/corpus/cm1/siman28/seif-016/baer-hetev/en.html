According to this rule it is also difficult to understand what is gained by having already opened the case. If the defendant lacks a writ from the Great Beit Din compelling him to litigate locally, then even after proceedings have begun he may say, "I will go to the Great Beit Din." If he does possess such a writ, then even before proceedings begin he may be compelled, as the Gemara states explicitly. This is very puzzling. I examined Rabbeinu Yerucham, and his words imply that even after proceedings have begun the defendant may still say, "I will go to the Great Beit Din." The distinction between before and after proceedings have begun applies only between two batei din of equal rank. Examine carefully how this resolves the Gemara. See also the Taz, who cites Maharshal.
