Before the property falls to the orphans, etc. Raavad writes that even if the claimant says, “If you will not receive my testimony, at least take the orphans’ money into your custody until they grow up so they do not consume it,” we do not listen. Maharshal asks from siman 73, where beit din restrains one who is wasting his property. He answers that here the debt is not yet established because the testimony has not been received. Bach rejects that answer, because in siman 73 restraint can apply even where the debt is not yet fully established. It seems preferable to distinguish ordinary spending from destructive dissipation. In siman 73 the debtor is wasting assets abnormally, effectively damaging them, and beit din must prevent such destruction. Here the orphans are merely using their assets in the normal course for their needs. So long as no collection right has arisen, a debtor may live from his own property without abnormal waste. Another distinction is that the concern here is chiefly movable property of orphans, which by strict law was not subject to creditors at all and became so only by Geonic enactment. Restraining those movables would be an enactment built upon another enactment, which we do not institute.
