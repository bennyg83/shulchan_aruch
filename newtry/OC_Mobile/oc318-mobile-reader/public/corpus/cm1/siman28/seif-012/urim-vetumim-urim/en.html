With testimony in a document, etc. Shach discusses Ramban’s disagreement with Rambam and supports the view that documentary testimony is valid by Torah law. In Tumim I answered the difficulties against Rambam. Still, even Rambam can agree that a valid document is effective by Torah law while the witnesses are alive, because we presume they remember the matter and the testimony is potentially available orally. Once the witnesses have died, there is no longer any possible Torah-valid oral testimony, and the document’s force is then rabbinic. Sema cites Hagahot Mordechai as saying that in monetary law written testimony was accepted as a tradition from Sinai like other testimony. The intent cannot be that a person may simply testify by writing. Rather, a formal document has a special status, rooted in tradition, and is not included in the exclusion of “from their mouths and not from their writing.” Ordinary written testimony remains invalid.
