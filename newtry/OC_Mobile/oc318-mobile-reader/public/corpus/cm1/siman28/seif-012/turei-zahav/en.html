(There: and they inform him of the punishment for false testimony.) It is difficult why our master needed to write that the false witness is disgraced in the eyes of the one who hired him, as the Gemara cited by the Beit Yosef states. One can further analyze the Gemara's wording, "he will not pass by the craftsman's shop." Does the case concern only a craftsman? Rather, according to the conclusion, the witnesses may indeed be threatened with the warnings about clouds, wind, and rain. The questioner objected that the witness could say, "I have a good occupation, because the one for whom I testify will provide my food." The Gemara answers that hired witnesses are contemptible even to those who hire them, so the employer will not continue supporting him afterward. Thus our master's phrase, "and he is disgraced in the eyes of the one who hires him," means that this consideration strengthens the force of the warning concerning the punishment for false testimony, the threat involving clouds, wind, and rain, etc. This is the meaning.
