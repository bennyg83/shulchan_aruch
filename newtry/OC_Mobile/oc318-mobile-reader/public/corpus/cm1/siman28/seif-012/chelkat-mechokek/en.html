Even if he threw it into her lap, silence of this kind is nothing. At first glance this appears to apply only where he initially told her, 'Take this as a deposit,' and afterward threw it into her lap. But the Ramah's words imply a broader rule: whenever he throws kiddushin into her lap without prior matchmaking and without any indication that she desires kiddushin, she is not betrothed. Bach explains the Ramah to mean that after he threw them into her lap, she shook out her garment until the kiddushin fell and went away; if she had not done so, her silence at the moment of delivery would be effective, as Kiddushin 12b states. Rivash, siman 170, however, describes the woman extending her hand and taking the ring, implying that if she does not extend her hand and he merely throws it into her lap, she has done no affirmative act showing consent and there are no kiddushin. Nevertheless, where she did not immediately throw it away, it appears there is at least doubtful kiddushin. The Rivash's case was one where she threw it away immediately, and therefore he ruled that there were no kiddushin at all. If she did not, perhaps she feared to discard it because not everyone knows the law, and she would require a get out of doubt. See what I write at the beginning of siman 30 and in siman 42.
