But one who brings witnesses to exempt himself, etc. Beit Yosef cites this law in the name of Ran. Darkei Moshe adds that Rashba, responsum 1021, writes that a person in possession of land who brings witnesses to establish his chazakah can have their testimony received even though the protester is not present. If so, one would have to say that even where the protester has an ancestral chazakah, because the current possessor has three years of possession, the protester is treated as the claimant and the possessor as defendant. But then I do not understand Rama's proof below in seif 16 from siman 140, where testimony is received when there is concern that the evidence will be lost. There, too, the possessor has already held the property for three years through tenants. On this reasoning, he is simply a defendant preserving evidence for his defense, and testimony could be received even without the special danger of loss. It therefore appears that Rama in his gloss ultimately did not rely on the explanation he suggested in Darkei Moshe. Rashba's own responsum says explicitly that the testimony is received because the situation resembles witnesses about to go overseas and the evidence may be lost. Thus, where the protester has an ancestral chazakah, he remains the defendant for this procedural rule, and testimony is ordinarily not received outside his presence unless the special preservation-of-evidence exception applies. That is the correct understanding.
