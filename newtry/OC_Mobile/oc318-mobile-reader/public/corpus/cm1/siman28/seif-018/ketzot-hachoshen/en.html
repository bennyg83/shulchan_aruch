Witnesses for exemption. Maharit, Choshen Mishpat, responsum 32, writes that receiving testimony outside the presence of the opposing party was improper. The rule cited in siman 28 from Ran, that the restriction against receiving testimony outside a litigant's presence applies to a claimant bringing proof for his claim but not to a defendant bringing proof to exempt himself, does not mean that testimony may be accepted in the creditor's absence in order to extinguish his established monetary right. That would destroy every lender's security, for every borrower could produce witnesses outside the lender's presence claiming payment or waiver. The Torah says, "its owner shall be warned," and there is no distinction between directly extracting money and extinguishing an existing right. Ran means only that where the claimant is present through beit din or an administrator and presses the claim, the defendant may present proof in that proceeding to defeat the claim. If no valid claimant is present, no testimony is received merely to create an exemption. Maharit therefore rules that testimony brought by a debtor to defeat a debt instrument may not be received outside the creditor's presence. Knesset HaGedolah's challenge from Ran is thus resolved. Even according to Raavan and Raavyah, who give some after the fact effect to testimony received outside a defendant's presence, testimony accepted solely to extinguish the absent claimant's right is ineffective. The Shach seems to explain the rule merely as avoiding unnecessary burden on beit din because perhaps the claimant would admit. In my view it is a rule of substantive law. Testimony is not received outside the litigant's presence even where its purpose is exemption. This will be explained further in siman 110.
