But testimony brought by a person to exempt himself is accepted. See below, siman 108, seif 16. The Mechaber writes there that if a minor heir possesses a promissory note that belonged to his father, and after the father's death a receipt is produced against it, we neither tear up the note nor collect with it until the orphans become adults. Even if there are witnesses who remember the payment, their testimony is not accepted, because testimony is not received outside the presence of the litigant. Although the witnesses are being brought to exempt the debtor, that case is different because the debtor is effectively seeking affirmative relief, namely to nullify and tear up the creditor's note. This resolves the difficulty left by Gidulei Terumah, page 141b. Maharit, Responsa part 3, question 32, writes that the Ran's rule applies only when one seeks to extract from the borrower in the lender's absence. To exempt himself from an existing claim, the borrower may bring witnesses outside the claimant's presence. But when the lender is not presently making any claim, testimony certainly is not received except in his presence. Maharit brings proof from the rule below in siman 108, which originates in Ramban, and additional proofs. There is another proof from the Mechaber's ruling below, seif 25. Examine this carefully.
