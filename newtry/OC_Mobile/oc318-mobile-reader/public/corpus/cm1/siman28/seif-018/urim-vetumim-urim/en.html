But one who brings witnesses to exempt himself, etc. Shach asks from siman 108, where a minor heir holds a debt instrument and a receipt appears after the father’s death. The receipt is not torn up even if there are witnesses, because testimony is not received outside the presence of the opposing party. Yet there the testimony is being brought to maintain possession. Shach answers that there the holder of the document is effectively the plaintiff seeking to extract, and the party relying on the receipt is therefore not merely defending possession. Maharit says instead that defensive testimony can be received outside the opponent’s presence only when the plaintiff has already sued; before any claim is made, even defensive testimony is not received. Maharit’s distinction is strained. Shach’s explanation is preferable. See Tumim.
