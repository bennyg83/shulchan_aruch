Initially, they should be seated. See Shach, seif katan 11, and Netivot HaMishpat. Birkei Yosef, letter 14, notes that the responsum of Mabit cited by the Shach is in volume 2, siman 116, and that Rashba likewise writes in Responsa, volume 2, siman 64, that testimony must be received in the place of the beit din. From the Mabit's wording, as cited by the Shach, one might infer that if the judges sat in the marketplace the reception of testimony would be valid, since he invalidated only where the judges were standing. Nevertheless, Birkei Yosef concludes that outside the place of beit din, even if they are seated, it is considered testimony received outside court. Masat Binyamin, siman 106, says the same and proves it from the passage in Bava Kamma that Rabbi Yehoshua was not then sitting as a beit din. The same is implied by Maharshal and other authorities. He therefore explains the Rashba and Mabit consistently with this rule, even if their wording at first appears otherwise, so as to harmonize the authorities. See also Shaar Mishpat on this point.
