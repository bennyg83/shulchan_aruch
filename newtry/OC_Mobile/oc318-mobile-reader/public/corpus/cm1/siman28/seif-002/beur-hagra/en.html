A person. Like every doubtful claim established by the enactment of the Geonim, as the Maggid Mishneh writes. This is also in the Rif at the end of Kol HaNishba'in.
But. As with every doubtful claim, as above; Bava Metzia 26b and 42b. See Mordechai and Rosh there.
(Collected note) But he cannot, etc. The Rashba writes that he may impose an oath on a doubtful basis, from what is stated in Shevuat HaEdut: "all agree regarding a witness to a woman's death, etc." There she necessarily does not know; examine carefully. (End of collected note.)
However. See above, siman 15, seif 4; Sanhedrin 32b.
(Collected note) Some say, etc. For we rule that one who adjures a single witness is exempt, whereas with regard to testifying he is obligated even without an oath, as the Sema writes. (End of collected note.)
(Collected note) And even a litigant, etc. See siman 16, seif 13, and Sema. (End of collected note.)
And there are those who disagree. Shevuot 31 and 35a. The first opinion, as above, holds that the rule was said only regarding liability for an offering, like one who swears to annul a mitzvah. Proof is from 35a: one who stood in the synagogue, etc.; one who sent by another, etc. All these are practiced nowadays through a ban.
(Collected note) And this is the principal view. This also appears, at first glance, to be the view of the Tur and Shulchan Aruch in paragraph 1, though perhaps one can distinguish because of a ban. (End of collected note.)
