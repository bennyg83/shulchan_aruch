He cannot impose an oath upon them. See the Shach, who concludes that even regarding an oath imposed on witnesses, if they say, 'We do not accept this oath upon ourselves,' it does not become an oath. Only if they remain silent is their silence treated as admission and acceptance. See there. From the Sema, however, it appears that he can impose an oath upon them against their will through the law of one adjured by others. What he cannot do is require them to take an oath upon themselves, meaning to answer 'Amen.' See what I wrote in siman 96, seif katan 2.
And this is the principal ruling. Some judges have the practice that when a litigant accepts a person who is otherwise disqualified as one witness, they ask that person to testify under the penalty associated with the oath of testimony. They explain that once the litigant accepts him as a witness, he now has the legal status of a witness. This is incorrect. He does not acquire the legal status of a witness. The Yerushalmi states: where one witness was told, 'You are accepted upon us like two,' is he thereby obligated? The verse says, 'and he is a witness, whether he saw,' etc. The Ran likewise writes this in chapter Shevuat HaEdut in the name of Ramban. Nevertheless, although he is not liable under the prohibition of 'if he does not tell,' there is still a mitzvah for him to testify because of the obligation to return another's loss.
