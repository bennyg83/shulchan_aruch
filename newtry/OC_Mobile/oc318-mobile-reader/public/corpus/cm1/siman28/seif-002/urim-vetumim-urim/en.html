But he cannot require them to swear, etc. Meaning, he cannot compel them to say in beit din, "We accept upon ourselves an oath and imprecation that we know no testimony for you," nor may he say, "I adjure you, if you know testimony for me, to testify," and compel them to answer "Amen." The same applies to imposing a cherem in this fashion. Sema. According to Ramban's approach I do not understand the point. If he adjures them, "If you know testimony for me, testify," then even if they do not answer Amen and do not expressly say that they know no testimony, they are liable for the offering for an oath concerning testimony, as the Shach cites from Ramban in seif katan 3. What difference, then, does it make whether they themselves pronounce the oath or answer Amen? However, Rashi's words on the Mishnah concerning one who adjured witnesses five times imply that it is uncertain whether, when they heard the oath and gave no response, silence is treated as admission. Hagahos HaRosh in the name of Or Zarua likewise says that the matter is doubtful. Tosafos, s.v. "mashbia," on Shevuos 31b, also implies that according to the Rabbis one at least needs either an answer of Amen or an express statement, "We know no testimony for you." Kesef Mishneh likewise implies this. Accordingly, the claimant may wish to be stringent upon them and require an answer of Amen or an express denial of knowledge, so that the full force of an oath will certainly rest upon them. Rashba therefore writes that he has no power to compel them to do so. This is correct. Sema and Darkei Moshe cite Responsa Rivash, siman 170, that when the testimony concerns a transgression, such as where someone is suspected of an offense and there is concern that the witnesses may suppress their testimony out of fear, the practice is to administer an oath.
A need of the hour. Because people are accustomed to gentile courts, where witnesses are sworn to tell the truth. As a result, ordinary people may imagine that so long as witnesses have not been sworn there is no prohibition against false testimony. Beit din therefore commonly administers an oath to them when circumstances require it.
