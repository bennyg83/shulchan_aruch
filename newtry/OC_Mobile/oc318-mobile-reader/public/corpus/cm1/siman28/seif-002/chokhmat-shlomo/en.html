Seif 6. “The judges must be seated,” etc. Note: The Gemara states: “Was it not taught that the dispute is only at the beginning of the trial, but at the conclusion of the judgment all agree that the judges sit?” At first glance the question is difficult. Perhaps the incident of Rav Nachman occurred only at the beginning of the proceeding, when the rules are different. I found that Torat Chaim seems to have noticed this and explains that because Rav Nachman wished to honor her and therefore stood for her throughout the case, the Gemara asks what honor he could show at the final judgment, when he would have to sit. I do not understand this. Rav Nachman’s desire to honor her was itself based on a mitzvah. If Hashem commanded that a judge sit at the conclusion of judgment, then at that stage he is simply forbidden to honor her by standing. Why would the absence of that honor create any problem? The matter is better understood in light of Tosafot’s question why Rav Nachman did not seat her, since the positive commandment of honoring Torah should override her obligation to stand. Tosafot answer that this would inhibit the arguments of the opposing litigant. Maharsha asks why he did not also seat the opposing litigant. He answers that seating both is possible at the beginning of the trial, when the litigants’ standing is not an absolute positive commandment, but not at the final judgment, when the litigants must stand. Accordingly the Gemara’s question is precise. The incident must have involved the conclusion of the judgment. Had it been only the beginning, Rav Nachman would not have needed to stand at all. He could simply have seated both litigants, thereby honoring her without intimidating the other party, as the Gemara itself later explains. The fact that he instead needed a device to honor her proves that they were at the final stage, when he could not seat the opposing litigant because litigants must stand. If so, how could Rav Nachman himself stand, when at the conclusion of judgment judges must sit? The Gemara therefore answers with the special posture described there. This appears straightforward.
