[Shulchan Aruch] And if he withheld his testimony. Note: Although at first glance there is no proof for this from Rabbi Yehoshua’s statement that with one witness he is liable under the laws of Heaven, one could say that this applied specifically then, before the rabbinic oath of hesset was instituted. At that time, withholding the witness likely caused the litigant a loss, since most people do not swear falsely, and he would therefore pay rather than swear. Even so, there is no liability for a false oath offering, because he might swear, so the witness merely causes a monetary loss, as stated in chapter 4 of Shevuos, and Rabbi Yehoshua follows his own view in Pesachim that causation is not treated as direct monetary damage. Nowadays, however, even without the witness the defendant must take a hesset oath, so perhaps the witness causes no loss by withholding his testimony. This requires study. Nevertheless, he may still cause a loss, because the defendant might be more deterred from swearing, fearing that the claimant and the witness could later combine to disqualify him from testimony, or because he would not brazenly swear falsely in the witness’s presence. Still, it appears to me that this applies only to movable property, where the witness would impose a Torah oath. Regarding land, it does not apply, as implied by the Tur. Although the defendant might confess out of fear, since the testimony itself creates no legal obligation, because he is already subject only to a rabbinic oath, it is not legally considered testimony, and the witness is not liable merely for failing to frighten the other party. With movable property, however, where his testimony would impose a Torah oath, it is properly classified as testimony, and he is liable under the laws of Heaven for the possible loss caused by the defendant’s refusal to confess and his taking an oath. This is clear.
