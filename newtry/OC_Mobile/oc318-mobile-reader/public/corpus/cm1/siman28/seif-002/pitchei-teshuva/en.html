Benefit from his testimony. See Responsa Chavos Yair, siman 208, concerning a Torah scholar who was presumed poor and whom members of the community supported financially. Shimon spread a report that the scholar possessed money, which he lent at interest, sufficient to support himself comfortably. As a result, people stopped supporting him, and the community sought to require him to share in communal burdens. The scholar asked a neighbor who frequently visited him to testify under formal oath whether he knew of anyone who owed the scholar money. Alternatively, if Shimon denied having spread the report, the scholar sought to have the neighbor testify under oath concerning that statement. Chavos Yair questions whether the neighbor, or Shimon himself, is obligated to testify. Although the matter is not directly a monetary claim, the scholar derives a practical benefit from the testimony. He raises several similar questions and does not reach a definite conclusion.
