But he cannot make them swear. Meaning he cannot individually compel the potential witnesses in beit din to say, 'We accept upon ourselves an oath that we know no testimony for him,' or require them to answer amen to an oath he imposes. It appears the same applies to an individual cherem, because oath and ban operate similarly here. Only in the synagogue may he impose a general oath or ban on everyone who knows testimony, or address specified individuals in the communal form, as explained in Hagahot Darkei Moshe. Rivash adds that where witnesses to a transgression may be afraid to speak, they may be placed under oath as an extraordinary need.
An immediate need. Such as where the people are lax about false testimony but are still afraid of false oaths.
Even relatives must testify. Hagahot Ashri cites Or Zarua that a communal ban imposed to obtain testimony does not apply to relatives. Even if it says 'near and far,' a relative need not testify, because the declaration is made according to the law's intent, and the Torah does not regard a relative as a witness. He derives this from the Mishnah that an oath of testimony applies to unrelated but not related persons. Raaviah disagrees and says relatives are exempt only from the special sacrifice for an oath of testimony, but an ordinary oath does bind them, and an intentional violation can carry lashes. He says a court's declaration is intentionally broad in order to uncover the truth, although money is ultimately extracted only through qualified testimony. Mordechai reproduces this latter view. Darkei Moshe cites both and places Mordechai last, so it requires study why Rama here rules like the opposing view.
