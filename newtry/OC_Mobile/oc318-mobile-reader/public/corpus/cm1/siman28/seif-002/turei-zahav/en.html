There, seif katan 4: “Benefit from his testimony” The Sema says that if witnesses heard an admission and the defendant says, “It never happened,” their testimony brings no benefit because he is exempt from an additional oath. In my view he must swear. Tur in siman 32 requires one who claims he was joking to swear that this was his intent; if complete denial exempted him, he could avoid that oath through a migo. Rosh allows a migo to exempt from an oath, so the Tur’s ruling proves that even one who says “It never happened” must swear. Rav Pappa’s statement “exempt” excludes monetary liability, not the oath. Nevertheless, absent witnesses presently before us, a claimant cannot demand a speculative oath merely by alleging that an admission was made before witnesses. Beit Yosef’s statement that witnesses to “I owe” need not testify when it may have been said to avoid self-incrimination also conflicts with Shulchan Aruch 32, which requires them to testify so beit din can assess the context. A general ban addressed even to relatives may still be imposed, since a valid proof may emerge.
