Seif 13. For after he saw the writing, he remembered. Witnesses must actually remember the testimony; they may not testify or sign merely because they know that a note in their possession is accurate. The writing must awaken memory of the event itself. Arbitrators are somewhat different. Once a kinyan sudar makes their announced compromise binding, they may not change what they already fixed before the parties. If they know with certainty that their written note accurately records the compromise they announced, that knowledge can function like memory of the details, because their authority at present would otherwise allow them to formulate a compromise, but not to replace the one already fixed. There is therefore greater room to rely upon their record. There may likewise be added leniency for witnesses where the obligor left his signed blank authorization in their hands to write the document and include his signature. By doing so he trusted them like a depositary or third party, and they know that their written record does not deviate from the truth. Perhaps this also lessens the concern that witnesses must be able personally to read and understand every detail of the resulting document, because by leaving his signed authorization the obligor effectively accepted what the witnesses would properly write.
