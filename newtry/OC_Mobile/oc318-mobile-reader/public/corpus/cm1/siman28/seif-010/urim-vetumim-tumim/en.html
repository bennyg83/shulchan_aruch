Unless he went overseas, etc. Mordechai writes that Raavan disagrees with this qualification. Commentators ask that we do not find Raavan stating such a disagreement explicitly. The answer is that Raavan discusses a written record of testimony in which one witness testified in full and the second merely said, "I too testify like him." Raavan invalidates the record. Had there been a recognized circumstance in which such abbreviated testimony could be valid, Raavan should not have invalidated the recorded testimony categorically. We ordinarily presume that beit din acted properly, and perhaps the witness was in urgent circumstances such as imminent departure overseas. Raavan's categorical invalidation therefore implies that even in such circumstances he does not validate this form. Accordingly, where our practical halachah does recognize the exceptional circumstance, if a beit din record comes before us containing such testimony, we should presume the court acted properly and that the necessary circumstances existed. The record can therefore be upheld. This is straightforward.
