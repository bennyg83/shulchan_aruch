He is not obligated to pay. See Sema, who says that sometimes the witness is placed under ban until he pays when the falsehood can be clarified. That is difficult, because if we can establish that he testified falsely Rama already rules that we descend to his property and make him pay. The principal explanation is Urim VeTumim's: the case can be one where even if the witness's factual statement is true, the gentile law may impose liability that Torah law would not. For example, the defendant says, 'I repaid,' the witness testifies only that he originally borrowed, and the gentile court refuses to recognize a plea of payment. Then the witness has caused loss even if his statement about the loan was true, and he may be placed under ban until the damage is removed, like a shalish who improperly returned a debt document. If the injured party wants to impose an oath on the witness by claiming that he testified falsely and thereby damaged him, ordinarily he cannot. A valid witness whose testimony in Jewish beit din would have required the defendant to swear can say, 'I testified truthfully and you would not have been able to swear.' His conduct is only indirect causation, because who says the defendant would have sworn successfully? By contrast, a disqualified witness who testified before a gentile court and caused direct extraction can be subject to a stronger claim, because he has no Torah evidentiary status that overrides the existing presumption of ownership. If the injured party seized property from such a witness and has a migo, he can be believed to say the testimony was false and may swear to contradict him. But where a valid witness created liability in Jewish law, such as in a case of one who must swear and cannot, the witness has the force of two unless contradicted by the required oath, and seizure cannot overcome that testimony.
