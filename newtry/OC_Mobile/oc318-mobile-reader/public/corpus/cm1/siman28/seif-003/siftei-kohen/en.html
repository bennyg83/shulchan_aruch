They place him under a ban for thirty days. See Maharshal in Yam Shel Shlomo, Bava Kamma, chapter 10, siman 23, who writes that if he wishes to pay, the ban is lifted, and likewise he is not placed under a ban initially. See also the responsa of the Rama, siman 88, folio 181c.
But he is not obligated to pay. Yam Shel Shlomo rules likewise there. Maharshal also recognized the difficulty raised by the Sema from the Tur, Yoreh Deah, siman 334. See there.
They do not place him under a ban. Maharshal writes there that this applies specifically where he admitted an ordinary loan and denied it merely for profit, since public desecration of Hashem’s Name is involved, and therefore they do not place him under a ban. But where he evades repayment of a loan in a manner that will not become known to the gentile, so that no desecration of Hashem’s Name results, they do place him under a ban.
