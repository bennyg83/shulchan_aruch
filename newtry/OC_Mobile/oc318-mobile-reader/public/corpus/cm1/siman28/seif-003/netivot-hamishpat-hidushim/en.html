He is not required to pay. In Yoreh Deah, siman 334, it is written that they place him under ban until he pays. The Sema explains that there the case is one where it can be established that he testified falsely. The Shach writes that the ban lasts only thirty days. See Beurim, which notes the difficulty in this. The primary explanation follows the Tumim: there the case is one in which even if his testimony that the loan occurred was true, the defendant might nevertheless have been exempt under Jewish law, for example because he would have been believed to say that he repaid, while under non Jewish law he is not believed. Therefore, the witness is placed under ban until he removes the damage he caused. Here, by contrast, the case is one in which truthful testimony would not have caused any loss at all. See Beurim, seif katan 4, that one may not impose an oath on the witness. If the injured party seized property from the witness and has a migo, then if the witness testified in Jewish beit din and the testimony itself created liability, as in the case of the silver ingot of Rabbi Abba, seizure is ineffective. If liability was imposed only by non Jewish law, seizure is effective. A relative or disqualified witness who testified may be required to swear, and it is possible that he must also pay. See Beurim:
