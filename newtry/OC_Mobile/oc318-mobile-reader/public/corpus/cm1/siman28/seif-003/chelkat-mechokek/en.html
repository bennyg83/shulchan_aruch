He betrothed her with property robbed or stolen from a gentile. The simple wording implies that even if the gentile did not despair, the kiddushin are valid, because the woman need return the property to the gentile only because of sanctifying Hashem's name, not as a matter of strict property law. This requires study. Several Gemara passages imply that robbery and theft from gentiles are prohibited by Torah law, except that double payment is not required because Scripture says 'his fellow.' What basis is there to distinguish, and perhaps she must return the intact stolen object so long as the gentile did not despair? Darkei Moshe copied this rule from Responsa Mahari Weil, siman 138, but that case involved a gentile's pledge that someone sold and the buyer then used for kiddushin. That may be only rabbinic theft, because the pledge came lawfully into his possession and the sale resembles evading repayment of a gentile's debt. It is not proof regarding actual robbery, such as taking merchandise from a shop. The responsum itself discusses whether this is rabbinic robbery and therefore ineffective for kiddushin, or is more lenient and effective. It does not address Torah-level robbery. Even if some authority classifies robbery from a gentile as rabbinic, we do not rule that way, as in Choshen Mishpat 359. Therefore, in practice these are certainly not full kiddushin. If another man betroths her, she requires a get from both.
