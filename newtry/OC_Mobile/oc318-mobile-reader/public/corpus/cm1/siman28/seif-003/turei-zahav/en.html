(Seif 4.) "And the non-Jew has one witness." The Tur explains that under Jewish law a supporting witness exempts. It is difficult why this reason is needed, since one who denies everything is exempt in any event. This is indeed how Mordechai states the matter. If the concern is the oath that the non-Jew would have had to take under Jewish law, he is no better than a Jewish person disqualified from swearing, who is not subject to an oath. What loss, then, did the testimony cause under Jewish law? It appears that the case concerns partial denial. Under Jewish law the claimant would swear and collect, as explained in siman 87 where the defendant is disqualified from swearing and owes a Torah oath. Under non-Jewish law, however, the claimant loses because of the witness. We therefore need the reason that a supporting witness would exempt under Jewish law. Mordechai, by contrast, speaks of complete denial.
