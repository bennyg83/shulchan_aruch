He is obligated to testify. See Sema and Baer HaGolah, which cite Darkei Moshe in the name of Mahari Weil concerning a witness who says he may not reveal the matter because he undertook not to disclose it. Taz objects that even formal release should not be necessary, since Rama, Yoreh Deah 228:33, rules that if a communal ban requires disclosure, the witness must speak. Taz there likewise asks why any ban is needed when a witness whose testimony is required already violates 'if he does not tell.' He answers that Rama speaks where the witness has not yet been summoned to testify; if he was summoned, he must testify even without the ban. Ateret Tzvi and Shaar Mishpat attempt other resolutions, though their words require study. Tumim, seif katan 1, gives the main resolution. If the witness merely accepted orally not to disclose, Mahari Weil only meant that ideally the party should release him; if the party refuses or is absent, the acceptance is irrelevant and beit din orders the witness to speak, for he cannot suppress testimony. If he actually swore, however, distinctions apply. If he is a single witness and swore not to testify, the oath takes effect and requires regret and formal annulment, because a lone witness does not violate 'if he does not tell' and therefore the oath is not an oath to nullify a mitzvah. If there are two witnesses, an oath not to testify attempts to nullify a mitzvah and does not take effect. If he swore more broadly not to tell the matter to anyone, including beit din, the oath is a comprehensive oath: since it takes effect regarding disclosure to ordinary persons, it may also take effect regarding testimony to beit din, depending on Tosafot's two answers in Shevuot 24. Netivot HaMishpat states this briefly, and see Tiferet Yaakov and Yadot Nedarim. Bat Eini suggests another explanation for requiring the litigant to release the witness: perhaps the testimony will ultimately prove useless, so disclosure would not have been a biblical duty and the witness would have violated his oath for no purpose. We therefore compel the litigant to release him first. This requires study. See below, siman 34, seif 5, seif katan 14, regarding a witness who testified in beit din before receiving release.
