I testified truthfully. It appears to me that where he is a legally competent witness, although one witness is not believed like two, he still has the status of a witness and can say, "My testimony was true." Therefore he is exempt from paying the loss caused by his testimony. But if he has no legal status as a witness at all, for example he is a relative or otherwise disqualified, he is not believed merely to say that the content was true. Consider a person who forcibly takes an object from one person before witnesses and hands it to another who cannot return it. He must certainly pay and cannot say, "It really belonged to the recipient," when he has no migo. He is simply a robber who caused the owner's loss. The same should apply to one who has no status as a witness. His act is merely informing against another person, and he cannot defend himself by saying his words were true. I have not found this stated explicitly, but reason strongly favors it.
