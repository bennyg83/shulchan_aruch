But he is not obligated to pay. This requires study. In Yoreh Deah 334:43 the Tur and Mechaber list among the twenty-four grounds for excommunication one who testifies against a Jew in non-Jewish court and thereby causes money to be collected unlawfully, saying that he is excommunicated until he pays. It is difficult to say the present case is different merely because the non-Jews compelled him to testify, for Yoreh Deah should then have distinguished that circumstance. Perhaps there the wording is disjunctive: sometimes he is merely excommunicated, where it cannot be demonstrated that his testimony was false, and sometimes he remains under excommunication until he pays, where the falsehood can be established. One could also say, with difficulty, that "until he pays" means only that if he does not wish to remain under excommunication, he may escape it by paying. But that too is difficult. One who refuses a shevuat heset is likewise not subjected to execution against his assets; he is excommunicated for thirty days and afterward the ban is lifted and he is discharged. See the end of siman 87. Why should this witness differ? It is difficult to read "until he pays" as meaning only that payment can shorten the first thirty days.
