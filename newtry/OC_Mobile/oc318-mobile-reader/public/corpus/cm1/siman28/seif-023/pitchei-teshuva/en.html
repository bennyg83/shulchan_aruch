And if the witness is a Torah scholar. See Baer Heitev, who notes that the Rambam speaks of a case where the litigant is a Torah scholar. Responsa Rama, siman 44, states that nowadays there is no Torah scholar of the requisite status for this rule. Knesset HaGedolah, Hagahot HaTur, letter 63, disputes this, and Shevut Yaakov, volume 2, siman 147, likewise concludes that the law of a Torah scholar still applies today; he suggests that even the Rama did not state otherwise as a final rule, but only as an additional consideration. Tumim writes that although the Rama's ruling may be questioned, he has already ruled and one should not disagree without clear proof. Tumim further states that even if the witness is present and the beit din questions him, and he says that he did not remember the matter until reminded, this is prohibited for anyone other than a Torah scholar, contrary to the Beit Yosef's suggestion. The omission of this ruling from the Shulchan Aruch indicates that the Beit Yosef himself retracted it. Tumim also writes that if the litigant puts the facts into the mouth of the court or its agent so that they remind the witness, it is permitted, for ultimately another person is doing the reminding; see there.
