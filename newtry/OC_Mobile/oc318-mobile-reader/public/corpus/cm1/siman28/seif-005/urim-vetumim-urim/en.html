And there is evidence supporting his claim. Sema writes that everyone agrees in such a case, for otherwise every violent person could force his opponent to bring witnesses whenever sued. It appears, however, that when there is evidence supporting the claim, beit din need not independently know that the person is violent. The witnesses themselves say, in effect, “Why should we bring trouble upon ourselves by testifying against him?” That itself establishes the practical concern. Even if he is not generally violent, these witnesses are afraid of him, and without compelling him to bring the witnesses the matter cannot proceed. Tosafot and Rosh therefore did not require beit din separately to know that he is violent. Accordingly, Rama should be understood to mean that where there is such evidence, no further independent knowledge by beit din is needed.
