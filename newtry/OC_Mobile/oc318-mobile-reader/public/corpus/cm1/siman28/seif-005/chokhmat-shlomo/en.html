Seif 15. “And they return and testify in his presence.” Note: Ketzot HaChoshen, seif katan 7, cites two opinions whether we say that a person tends to maintain his earlier statement merely in order to preserve consistency. See my work on Even HaEzer, sixth edition siman 177, where I brought proof from the Gemara that we do apply the principle that a person may persist in his first statement, and therefore a later change of testimony is not necessarily effective. See also below, siman 33, seif 12 in the gloss, where this principle is expressly stated. I also discussed this in my Torah commentary to Parashat Vayakhel in the year 5608. Seif 15, in the gloss. “Some disagree and hold that if testimony was accepted after the fact not in the litigant’s presence, it is effective.” Note: See the Shach, who cites Maharshal as holding that even after the fact it is ineffective. I do not presently have Maharshal’s work before me to examine his proofs. At first glance, however, there appears to be proof against him from Bava Kamma 40b, regarding an ox that became forewarned while in a borrower’s possession and was later returned to its owner. The Gemara offers several explanations, including that the owner can say, “You have no authority to render my ox forewarned.” See Rashi there. Why does the Gemara need these answers? It could have explained simply that the testimony establishing the ox as forewarned was accepted before the borrower but not before the actual owner. With respect to the borrower’s liability, the borrower is the relevant owner and the testimony was received in the presence of a litigant. With respect to imposing full liability on the true owner after the ox is returned, however, the testimony was accepted outside his presence. Scripture states, “warning shall be given to its owner,” implying that the owner must stand over his ox when the warning testimony is received. If testimony received outside the litigant’s presence were absolutely void even after the fact, the owner should pay only half damages and the Gemara could have answered in this straightforward manner. The fact that it does not do so indicates that although testimony should initially be received only in the litigant’s presence, once it was actually received it remains effective after the fact. Since the baraita says the ox was already made forewarned in the borrower’s possession, the evidentiary act had already occurred and could therefore operate even against the owner. The Gemara needed another reason why the owner should not be fully liable. This supports the dissenting opinion cited by the Rama and not Maharshal. I am not presently occupied with this topic in depth. Perhaps Hashem will grant me an opportunity to elaborate elsewhere.
