Under the laws of Heaven. Baer Heitev, citing Shach in the name of Maharshal, writes that if the injured party seized property, it is taken back from him. Shach also cites Rivash, siman 392. Below, siman 75, Shach, seif katan 26, cites Ramban that seizure does not help where the claimant has no enforceable right and the other party is obligated only to satisfy Heaven, and Shach repeats this in simanim 76 and 182. Rabbi Akiva Eiger notes that Rivash proves this from rabbinic interest in Yoreh Deah 161. Yet Nimukei Yosef, in the discussion of a mortgage in a place where the borrower may redeem it, cites Rashba and Ran as holding that where one is obligated to satisfy Heaven, seizure is effective. The matter therefore depends on a major dispute among Rishonim. As a practical matter, perhaps the possessor cannot plead that he relies on Rashba and Ran, according to Mishneh LaMelech, who writes that where the effectiveness of seizure itself is the subject of dispute, one cannot invoke kim li. See Tumim on seizure and the discussion of kim li at the end of siman 25 and below in siman 75. Shevut Yaakov, volume 1, siman 146, distinguishes: with rabbinic interest and with indirect damages where one is biblically exempt even by heavenly law but the Sages imposed a heavenly obligation, seized property is returned. Where the heavenly obligation is itself biblical, such as a case of kim lei bid'rabbah minei or suppressing testimony, seizure is not undone. Tumim, seif katan 4, rejects this distinction and concludes that seizure is ineffective for all obligations enforceable only by Heaven, except in kim lei bid'rabbah minei, where seizure does help. Netivot HaMishpat agrees. See Responsa Chavot Yair, siman 44, Shemen Rokeach, volume 1, siman 80, Beit Shmuel Acharon, siman 10, and below, siman 75, seif 9.
