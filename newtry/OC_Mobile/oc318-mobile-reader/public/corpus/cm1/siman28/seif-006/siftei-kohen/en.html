To be seated when receiving the testimony. The Mabit writes in responsa, part 1, siman 116, that when testimony is received outside the judges’ regular place of sitting, such as in the marketplace, on the road, or the like, it is ineffective even after the fact if the judges were standing. See there. His words are not compelling. The responsa of Mahari ben Lev, at the beginning of volume 4, cites the Rivash that it is valid after the fact, agrees with him, and writes that no one is known to disagree. See there. See also below, siman 39, seif 7.
