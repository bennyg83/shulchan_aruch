An unstable wall or tree that falls and causes damage. It contains one seif.
If a wall or tree falls into the public domain and causes damage, the owner is exempt even if he abandoned the fallen material, because it is unlike a pit, since it was not originally made to cause damage. Some say that if he had time to remove it and did not abandon it, he is liable. If the wall or tree was unstable, beit din sets a time for removal. A warning by private individuals alone is insufficient; beit din must issue the warning. If someone digs into the wall and thereby causes it to fall, he is liable because the collapse is directly attributable to his act. The ordinary period allowed to cut the tree or demolish the wall is thirty days. If the danger is urgent and others may be harmed, no delay is granted and he is compelled to remove it immediately. If it falls within the allowed period, he is exempt; if it falls afterward, he is liable because he delayed. Rama: This applies where the wall was originally built properly. If it was improperly built and fell for that reason, the builder is liable for the damage.
