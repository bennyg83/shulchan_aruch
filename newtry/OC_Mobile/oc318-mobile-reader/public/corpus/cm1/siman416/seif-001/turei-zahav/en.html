Even though he abandoned them for free. The Sema struggles with the word "even." At the beginning of Bava Kamma, Rav and Shmuel dispute this. Rav holds that liability for a pit applies specifically where one abandoned the damaging object. Thus, regarding a wall or tree that was given a deadline for removal and fell within that period, liability would apply where he abandoned the debris. According to Shmuel, even without abandonment it can be considered a pit. But in the opening case, everyone exempts even if he abandoned it, because the collapse occurred through unavoidable circumstances and no deadline had been given. Therefore the phrase "even though he abandoned them" is entirely appropriate, since one might have thought that abandonment itself creates pit liability. My only difficulty is with the stated reason, "because its initial creation was not for damage." The Gemara says that this very factor would not necessarily exempt, because the law of an ox could prove liability. The essential exemption is that the collapse was unavoidable, making this a case of one who abandons his damaging property after an unavoidable accident. Why then does the ruling not conclude with that reason?
