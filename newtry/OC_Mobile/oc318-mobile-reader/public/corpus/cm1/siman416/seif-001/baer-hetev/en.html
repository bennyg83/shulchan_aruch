The time. The Tur's wording is: after the allotted time, if the wall or tree causes damage, whether while falling or after it has fallen, it has the law of a pit. The Rosh writes similarly and adds that this differs from one's stone, knife, or burden placed on a rooftop and blown down by an ordinary wind, where damage caused while falling has the law of fire, because those objects become analogous to fire through an external force, namely the wind that brought them down. A wall or tree, by contrast, falls of itself because of its weakened condition. Beit din warned the owner to take it down because it could no longer stand, not because it moves and causes damage. That fact does not make it less analogous to a pit, and certainly does not justify exempting the owner. It is no different from tying a stone to an animal's foot, after which the animal walks into the public domain and causes damage. And if one argues that the wall and tree were not originally made for purposes of damage, an ox proves otherwise. Sema.
