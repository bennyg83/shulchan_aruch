Tomorrow. The Beit Yosef writes in the name of the students of the Rashba that during the intervening night, if it is the borrower's usual practice for borrowed animals to remain overnight with him after the borrowing period, then here too he is considered a borrower for the night and is liable if an unavoidable accident occurs during the night. If borrowed animals ordinarily do not remain with him overnight after the borrowing period, then during the night he is only like a renter, exempt from unavoidable accidents but liable for theft and loss. These are the words of the Darkei Moshe. See Maharshal, chapter 10 of Bava Kamma, siman 3; what I wrote at length in siman 291, seif 26; and Responsa Rashach, part 3, siman 17. Shach.
