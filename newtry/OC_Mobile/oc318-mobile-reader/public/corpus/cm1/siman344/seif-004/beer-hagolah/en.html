This distinction is mentioned neither in the Tur nor Rambam, nor in Beit Yosef or Kesef Mishneh. Rambam's wording is: if there is no proof, the renter swears concerning the rented animal that it died, or that he does not know. In Bedek HaBayit it is written that one may wonder how an oath can be imposed on a claim of uncertainty. The answer is that he must take the custodians' oath, and by extension he also swears that it died during the period of rental. From this the Mechaber derived his distinction and wrote that where the lender says, 'I do not know,' etc. Sema discusses this at length, and the reader may judge.
Meaning, a shevuat heset. Kesef Mishneh writes so there. Even if the lender believes him that it died naturally, he nevertheless swears, as Tosafot write there, 97a, s.v. 'on the day it was borrowed.' See there.
Rambam writes: one says the borrowed animal died and the other says the rented one died; the renter swears that the rented animal died naturally, as he claims, and an oath is imposed upon him by extension that it was the rented animal that died. In my humble opinion, Rambam adopted the Gemara's establishment of the Mishnah on 98a, as is his way to preserve the wording of the Mishnah or Gemara. The Mechaber, however, wrote the law with respect to a shevuat heset, which was instituted in the days of the Amoraim, and therefore he is required to swear heset.
