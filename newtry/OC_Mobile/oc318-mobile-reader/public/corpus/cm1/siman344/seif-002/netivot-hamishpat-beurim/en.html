The broken remains were stolen. See Sema, seif katan 7, who writes that if the broken remains are stolen after the borrower says, "Take yours," the borrower remains liable. Darkei Moshe does not clearly state such a limitation, and Nimukei Yosef's use of the word "accident" need not exclude theft. It is not reasonable that after the borrower says, "Take your property," once the borrowing period has ended through the breakage, he should be forced to continue guarding another person's property against his will. Even with an ordinary debt, according to the view that involuntary repayment counts as repayment, one who says, "Take your money" can be discharged from theft, and some authorities exempt even from negligence, as siman 120 explains. A broken borrowed object is certainly no worse. Once it has broken, the borrowing has ended. By saying, "Take what is yours," the borrower has effected return for custodial purposes. He should not remain liable for subsequent theft.
