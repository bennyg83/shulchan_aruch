If they depreciated, etc. This follows Tosafos there, s.v. “zil,” etc. Likewise if, etc. Bava Kamma 11a, “We do not appraise,” etc., according to Tosafos, who explain it concerning depreciation. There it concerns an animal, in keeping with the subject of the passage, as Tosafos write in the name of the Yerushalmi from “he shall pay two living animals,” etc.; so too in Tosafos. If they were stolen or lost, the borrower, etc. This is the correct reading. Although Nimukei Yosef writes that he remains liable even for unavoidable accidents, while if the owner told him “Take yours” he is exempt specifically from unavoidable accidents, as the Beit Yosef cites in the name of Rabbenu Yerucham, the Rama disputes them, and his words are correct. Once the object broke, the period of borrowing ended, as stated in siman 345, seif 6, and he is only a paid bailee, as stated in siman 343, seif 1. If he says “Take what is yours,” he becomes an unpaid bailee, even in the case of a borrower, just as with a craftsman in chapter 2; see what I wrote at the end of siman 120.
