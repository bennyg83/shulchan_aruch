dalet) Ibid., seif 4, from the incident of the man who borrowed an axe, where the halakhah was ruled like Rav Kahana and Rav Asi that he returns the broken remains. Rashi likewise explains this, and the Gemara at the beginning of Bava Kamma also concludes that an appraisal is made for a borrower, Bava Metzia 97a.
heh) The Rosh writes likewise in his rulings, based on the law of an ox that gored where the carcass depreciated between the time of death and standing in judgment, concerning which Bava Kamma 15a states that according to all opinions that depreciation is borne by the injured party.
vav) At that point he is exempt from unavoidable accidents but liable for theft and loss. He explains, in the name of the Rashba's students, that he is like a paid guardian because he has received a benefit.
