(Seif 3: And be exempt) This is difficult, for according to his own claim he should at least owe the lowest possible amount. Why then does the text say that he is not considered one obligated to swear who cannot swear? It appears that the object was not entirely lost. The borrower returned it and said he did not know whether or how much it had depreciated. He told the lender: “According to your claim, swear how much it was worth and collect.” The lender answered: “You swear and be exempt; if you cannot swear, pay.” One might have thought that the borrower is an obligated defendant unable to swear and must therefore pay. The Rosh rejects this because there is no oath of partial admission: the borrower made no definite admission at all, and what he does admit he is presently returning, which is equivalent to “here it is.” On the balance he says only that he does not know whether any depreciation occurred. He therefore owes only a rabbinic oath that he does not know. The Sema's explanation is incomprehensible.
