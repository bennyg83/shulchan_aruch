He cannot stipulate verbally. Mordechai records a borrower who, after the loan term ended, agreed with the lender that the same money should remain with him as an investment. The money was then lost through accident. Mordechai ruled that because the money originally came into his hands under full borrower liability, he did not shed that liability merely by a new verbal description. Rama cites this as though once a person has already assumed a custodial obligation he cannot reduce it verbally. The rule must be limited. Where he expressly says, "I am exempt from this liability," verbal release can certainly work. The Gemara concerning "Tear my garment and be exempt" proves that an express waiver is effective even where the property was already in a custodial relationship. Mordechai concerns wording that merely indicates a different arrangement but does not clearly release the prior liability. In such a case, because the money already came into his hands under the stronger obligation, ambiguous new words are not enough. But where the owner explicitly says, "You shall not swear" or "You shall not pay," that is an express release and is effective without a kinyan.
