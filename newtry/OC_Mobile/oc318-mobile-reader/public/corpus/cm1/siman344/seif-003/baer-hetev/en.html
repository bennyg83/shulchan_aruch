The Sema writes that the case is where the lender himself claims only doubtfully that perhaps the article was worth that much and the borrower caused that loss, while it may be that the actual loss was worth less than a perutah. Even if the loss was worth a perutah, since the lender does not claim the amount with certainty, we do not apply the rule that one who cannot swear must pay. Alternatively, the case is where the borrower says: "I know that I caused a loss of such-and-such an amount, and I am giving it to you now; beyond that I do not know whether I caused any loss. Swear and collect." Therefore this is not a case of "since he cannot swear, he pays," because the lender himself does not make a definite claim regarding the excess, and as to what the borrower admits he says, "Here it is." These are the Sema's words. The Shach rejects them, as well as the Bach's explanation, and concludes: It appears to me that here Shimon says that he caused no loss at all and that the article was already in that condition from the outset. The phrase "and he damaged it" means that Reuven makes that allegation. Shach.
