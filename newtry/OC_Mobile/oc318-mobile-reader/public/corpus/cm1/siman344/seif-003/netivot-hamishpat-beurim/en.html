And he is not considered one who is obligated to swear but cannot swear. See Sema, seif katan 8, and Shach's objections. The ruling can be understood where the borrower says that the object was damaged through the permitted work and therefore he owes only the amount of the damage, but he does not wish or is unable to swear. He tells the claimant to swear to the object's former value and he will pay that amount. He is not treated as one who admitted part and is unable to swear on the balance in the classic sense. Even if a court descends to his property for the amount that he cannot properly deny, that compulsory payment does not make him a formal partial admitter on the remainder. Shach in siman 87 writes similarly that where one pays part only because of the rule "one who cannot swear must pay," that payment does not generate a new partial-admission oath on the balance. Nor does gilgul create a full oath here, because the claimant's principal allegation is itself uncertain.
