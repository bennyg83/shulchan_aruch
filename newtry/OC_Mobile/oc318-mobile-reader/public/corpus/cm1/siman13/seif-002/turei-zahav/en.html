Addenda to seif 7 in the Tur The Tur earlier distinguishes, regarding three lay judges, between a defendant who refuses judgment altogether and one who merely rejects the three chosen by the claimant. He then says an expert may judge coercively. Why then does he challenge Rambam, who makes a similar distinction regarding an expert? The Tur holds that the procedure of each party selecting one judge does not apply to a single expert. With three lay judges, selection by the parties does not diminish the tribunal. But if each selected an expert, two experts would sit together, undermining the expert’s special capacity to sit alone. Thus an expert compels in every case. The proposed answer is that an expert has this power only when no party first designated him as his selected judge. If one party first approached and selected him for a dispute with the other, his special status for this purpose is lost and he is treated like an ordinary selected judge.
