Seif 1. Note: See the Shach, who discusses at length whether age is measured by exact hours or only by calendar years, and see also Urim VeTumim. It appears to me that there is evidence from the Gemara that exact hours are relevant in calculating years. Our Sages expounded the verse, “I am unable to go out and come in today,” to mean: today my days and my years were completed. The commentators ask that our Sages also taught concerning the verse, “because you did not believe in Me,” that had Moshe and Aharon fully sanctified Hashem there, their time to leave the world would not yet have arrived. The answer is that “my years were completed” does not mean that a predetermined quota of life-years had necessarily expired. It means that Hashem completed his years from day to day, so that he did not die halfway through a year. But if so, why did the Sages use the doubled phrase “my days and my years”? It should have sufficed to say either “my days” or “my years.” Moreover, our Sages say that Moshe was born on the seventh of Adar and died on the seventh of Adar. How, then, could Moshe say on that day, “I am one hundred and twenty years old today”? At the very beginning of the night of the seventh of Adar, once the sixth had ended, he had already entered his one hundred and twenty-first year, especially according to the rule that even one day in a year can sometimes count as a year. It must therefore be that years are reckoned by exact time as well. Since he had been born at a particular time on that day, only when that time arrived did he become precisely one hundred and twenty full years old. He could therefore say, “I am one hundred and twenty years old today.” This is what the Sages meant by “today my days and my years were completed”: just as days can depend on a full twenty-four-hour period and on hours for various laws, so too his years were completed only at the corresponding hour today. They mentioned both days and years to teach that the same principle of exact time applies to each. The intent is not that a heavenly decree of years had just expired, but that his full years and his full days were completed today, not yesterday. This appears to me a clear proof, with Hashem’s help.
