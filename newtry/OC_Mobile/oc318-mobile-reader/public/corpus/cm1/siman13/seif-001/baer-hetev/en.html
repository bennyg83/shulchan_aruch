Willingly. The Sema writes that although the Mechaber has already stated several times that three lay judges, or one expert judge, may adjudicate a person against his will, without saying that initially each litigant should select one judge, the intent here is as follows: once each party selects one judge and records whom he selected, those two judges may themselves select a third judge even against the litigants' will and adjudicate the case. Thus, "these two judges" refers to the statement that they select a third judge, as though the text said, "these two judges select." The Bach infers from the Tur that if the parties agreed to a compromise through the procedure of each selecting one judge, the two judges may not select a third without the parties' consent. See Tosafot Yom Tov at the beginning of chapter 3 of Sanhedrin and Responsa Mahari Beit Levi, siman 39.
Merit. Each selected judge carefully examines the law in the case for which he was chosen. Even when he sees that the party who selected him should lose under the law, he does not rush to issue judgment, but waits to see whether the matter may become clearer and some merit may be found for him. The other judge does the same. Because both proceed deliberately, the judgment ultimately emerges truthfully. Sema in the name of Nimukei Yosef. The Mechaber's statement that each may choose whomever he wishes means, according to the Sema, whether someone greater or lesser than himself. The Shach cites Rabbeinu Yerucham that the selected judge must be learned, or must have been accepted by the public, for otherwise the other party may object.
