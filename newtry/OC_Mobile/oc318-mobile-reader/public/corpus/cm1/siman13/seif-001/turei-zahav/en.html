Shulchan Aruch, seif 1, in the gloss “They may agree among themselves,” etc. This was derived from Responsa Maharan 11, but examination shows that case differs, because from the outset the parties submitted to two judges. Maharik, shoresh 57, likewise implies this limitation. If one merely said, “You shall be my selected judge,” he did not intend the two selected judges to sit without a third, for litigants too prefer a larger tribunal. Rama’s wording likewise refers back to one who expressly said that a certain individual alone should judge him. Without such specification, two do not suffice when a party insists on three, even if the two agree. See the cited later authorities; one must distinguish an individual expert, who may compel even one willing to litigate, from three ordinary judges.
