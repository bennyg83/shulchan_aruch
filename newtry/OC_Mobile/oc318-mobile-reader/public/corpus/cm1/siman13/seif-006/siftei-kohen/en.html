For we require the litigants to be present, etc. The ruling below, at the end of siman 18, that monetary cases may be decided in the absence of a litigant, concerns a case in which the parties already presented their arguments before the judges, as the Sema explains there. Thus they already stood before the judges who are issuing the ruling. The Bach objects that the responsum of the Rashba cited by Beit Yosef below, at the end of siman 16, concerns a defendant who transferred his right to another person, and that other person presented no argument at all. He further argues that according to the Rashba's reason, Scripture specifically requires presence in capital cases from the verse, 'and its owner also shall be put to death,' but not in monetary cases, so no distinction can be drawn; rather, the Rashba must refer only to a ruling after the fact. His words are incorrect. The language of the Rashba in responsum 1118, 'Beit din may rule in the absence of a litigant, therefore they may decide his case today,' implies that this may be done initially. Rather, as I wrote, the Rashba discusses a case in which the parties had already presented their arguments, and therefore the judges may issue and finalize the ruling in his absence. Although the responsum cited by Beit Yosef below in siman 16 concerns one who transferred his right to another, he himself had presented his arguments before making that transfer. The Rashba's reason concerning capital cases applies to the completion of judgment, and on that point he distinguishes monetary from capital cases. This is also evident from responsum 1118: 'Although they disputed at the end of chapter Shor Shenagach Arba'ah VaChamishah whether an ox's judgment may be completed only in its presence, that applies specifically to the judgment of an ox, because Scripture states, The ox shall be stoned and its owner also shall be put to death; but in an ordinary monetary matter they may decide the case.' Thus his distinction concerns the completion of judgment. This appears correct to me.
