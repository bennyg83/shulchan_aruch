The same applies when a party refuses to litigate before the beit din already selected and insists on another beit din, while that other court refuses to take the case because he is a violent or difficult person. He can be compelled to litigate before the original judges. Sema, citing Maharik. If each party selected an arbitrator and those two arbitrators are disqualified relative to one another, and each litigant demands that the other select a replacement, Rama rules in responsum 104 that the defendant has the stronger position and the plaintiff must choose a different arbitrator.
