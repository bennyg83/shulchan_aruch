And they may consult, etc. Hagahot Ashri at the beginning of Zeh Borer, cited by Beit Yosef at the beginning of this siman, states: where the parties selected two judges and presented their claims before them, the litigants cannot tell those judges, “Do not ask such-and-such talmid chacham about my case, but ask another scholar,” because we rule like the Sages that the litigants’ consent is not required. The wording indicates explicitly that even if both litigants want to restrict whom the judges consult, they are not heard. Responsa Shevut Yaakov, part I, siman 149, notes that Responsa Maharanach, part I, siman 121, interprets Hagahot Ashri as speaking only where one litigant seeks to impose the restriction. Shevut Yaakov rejects that reading as contrary to the plain wording: even if both litigants agree, their stipulation is ineffective so long as the judges do not agree to it. The litigants cannot insist, “Consult only this scholar and not that one.” Conversely, where several scholars are available they may ask the judges not to consult a particular one, but even one litigant cannot prevent the judges from consulting another scholar whom they choose. Shevut Yaakov explains at length why litigant consent is unnecessary and how this fits Rabbi Ovadiah of Bartenura’s explanation of the Mishnah. See there and what I wrote above, seif 1, seif katan 2.
