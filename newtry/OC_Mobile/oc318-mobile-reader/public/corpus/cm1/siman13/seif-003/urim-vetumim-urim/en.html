They do not compel him, etc. They may not compel a litigant to submit his claims to beit din in writing even after he already stated them orally, and certainly may not compel him to give the opposing litigant a written copy. Nimukei Yosef and Sema. If both agree to put the pleadings in writing, and one party says, "Let my claims and yours be written in one document to reduce scribal expense," while the other says, "Let each side's claims be written separately because I do not want my claims and legal arguments permanently in your possession, for you are to me like a lion lying in ambush," this is the dispute in Bava Batra 167b between the Sages and Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel. The majority of authorities, including Rashba and Rosh, and apparently Rambam, rule like the first Tanna. They conclude, however, that this applies only where the reason for a joint document is to reduce the expense of writing. Where that consideration is absent, for example the party who wants separate copies says, "I will personally bear the additional scribal fee," all agree that he may prevent his opponent from receiving a written copy of his arguments, because he need not leave his legal strategy in the opponent's possession. See the responsa of Rashba and Rosh, klal 68, siman 20, at length.
A judge should not accept written pleadings, etc. Otherwise the other party must trouble himself to obtain a copy and prepare a written response. Moreover, beit din can distinguish truth from falsehood more effectively when hearing the litigants themselves. In writing, a party may present polished arguments prepared by others, attractively worded yet deceitful, and be trained to speak falsely. Rivash and Sema.
If both litigants wish to plead in writing, etc. Since neither objects that the other may formulate his position better in writing than orally, their agreement is comparable to accepting a related or otherwise disqualified judge. Sema.
And whatever they write. A party may not later retract a written claim even with an explanation for the retraction. For this reason the claims are not written except with both parties' consent, because one party may not wish to lose his ordinary ability to amend or explain an earlier argument. Beit Yosef and Sema.
