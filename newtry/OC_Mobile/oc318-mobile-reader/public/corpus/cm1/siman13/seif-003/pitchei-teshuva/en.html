And each judge should seek arguments, etc. The Sema explains that although a judge selected by a litigant may not simply act according to that litigant’s wishes, each selected judge should fully investigate the merits of the side that selected him. See the Tur’s quotation from the Rosh. Responsa Panim Meirot, part II, siman 159, writes that under halachah it is forbidden for a litigant to present his claims privately to his selected judge in advance. All the more is it forbidden to promise or pay that judge compensation for securing a favorable outcome, because in zeh borer lo echad each selected person has the full status and obligations of a judge. He sharply rebukes the corruption of this practice and concludes that communal leaders should remove this stumbling block from Israel. Since the community is obligated to support its judges, and the adjudication fees customarily taken among Ashkenazim are effectively communal funds used to support them, when a dispute requires zeh borer lo echad the communal leaders should fix the compensation of both selected judges and the third judge. They should prohibit both giving and receiving any additional gift beyond the communal rate. Neither party should privately arrange his arguments beforehand. Rather, when all three judges sit together, both litigants should present their claims. Nor should either party choose a friend or enemy as his judge unless both sides consent.
