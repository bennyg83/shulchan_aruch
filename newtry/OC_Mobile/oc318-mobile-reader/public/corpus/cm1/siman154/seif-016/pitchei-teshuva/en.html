However, some disagree, etc. Responsa Rabbi Akiva Eiger, at the end of siman 151, writes that although one may generally place substances such as olive refuse near the boundary before the neighbor has erected the vulnerable wall, opening a window toward the neighbor's ruin is different. The consensus of the poskim is that when the neighbor later builds, the window must be sealed even if it was opened earlier. A window causes a continuing renewed injury, and there is no clearly established authority permitting permanent reliance on prior placement. Rama mentions an opinion that even a window is not considered one's direct force except at the moment it is opened, but most poskim appear to reject this. Rabbi Akiva Eiger adds that even opening the window now on condition that it later be sealed is questionable, because the neighbor may fear that a future beit din will recognize a prescriptive right or follow the minority opinion and refuse to order closure. The exact basis for this concern requires analysis, but his practical conclusion is that the neighbor may object at the outset.
