But where one opens toward the public domain. This concerns two houses on opposite sides of a public domain less than four cubits wide. Even though in such a case, according to the Rosh in seif 14, the second may not open a window opposite the window of the one who opened first, he may nevertheless build on his own property and darken the first window. The reason is that one person cannot impose a servitude upon his fellow's property. The requirement to distance a cattle shed from a preexisting storeroom is not because the neighbor's property became servient, but because one may not actively damage the other. Here, however, the window also uses the airspace of the neighbor's courtyard. Therefore, if the neighbor wishes to raise his wall, he may say, "You may not use the airspace of my property without my consent." But if he wishes to open a window directly opposite the first window, the first may say, "Do not damage me through visual intrusion," just as one may not place a cattle shed opposite a preexisting storeroom. If the first owner could have protested when the window was opened and the opener then held it for three years with a proper claim, the second would have to distance four cubits. But where there was a ruin between them with the public domain intervening and no right to protest existed, no chazakah arises and no distancing is required. Sema. See Beurim.
