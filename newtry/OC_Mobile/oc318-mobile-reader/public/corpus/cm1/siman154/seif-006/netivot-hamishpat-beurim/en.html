Even though it is high. Ramban asks why this differs from a courtyard, where one cannot compel his neighbor to build higher than four cubits. It appears that Ramban's question is this: even a window opposite a window or opposite a courtyard, with a public domain separating them, does not take any of the neighbor's private airspace, yet it is still prohibited even above four cubits, as shown by the unqualified language of the Gemara and poskim. The prohibition must therefore be based on the possibility that a person will stand on a bench or similar object and look in. If so, why is a courtyard different, where above four cubits one is permitted? This also answers Ketzot HaChoshen's question on Ramban. Ramban answers that people do not ordinarily set up a bench in a courtyard merely to look over the wall. According to that answer, one may make a window in a courtyard wall above four cubits.
