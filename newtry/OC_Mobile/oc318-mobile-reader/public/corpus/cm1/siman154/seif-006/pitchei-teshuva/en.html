A window toward his fellow's courtyard. Knesset HaGedolah, Hagahot Tur, letter 5, cites Responsa HaGeonim, siman 235: if one constructed beams or a ceiling prepared for windows but did not yet form the window openings, the courtyard owner may compel him to remove the beams, saying, 'I fear that I may travel overseas and you will then open the windows.' If the courtyard owner is satisfied with a written undertaking, that is preferable; if not, the beams must be removed. The reason appears to be that the construction demonstrates an intention eventually to open windows. Although we follow Rabbi Yose that one may make use of his own property where he is not presently causing injury, here the circumstances show an intention to create the injury directly. By contrast, the present custom of forming a sealed decorative window in a masonry wall, where the wall is merely thinner at the shaped recess and the form is ornamental, does not prove an intention to open it later. The courtyard owner therefore cannot object.
