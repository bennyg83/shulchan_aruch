And the wall containing the window fell. This differs from the Mechaber's ruling in siman 153 concerning Reuven, who had established a right to insert beams into a wall known to belong to his fellow. If that wall fell and the owner rebuilt it, Reuven may not insert beams into the new wall. The Rashba, in a responsum cited by the Beit Yosef, explains the distinction. Whenever the physical object that became subject to the servitude still exists, the servitude is not canceled merely because a particular implement is replaced. If one has a right to insert a beam into his neighbor's wall and that beam breaks, the right remains, because the servitude was not to that specific beam but to the ability to insert a beam. Likewise, the courtyard is not made subject to one particular flow of water from a gutter, and a window made for light is not limited to the particular wall opening. Even if the wall falls, the easement has not disappeared, because the servitude is upon the neighboring courtyard to supply light to a window serving this courtyard. Thus the distinction is clear. In the beam case, the servitude was upon the neighbor's wall itself, and that wall is gone. In the window case, the servitude was upon the neighbor's courtyard and its light, and that underlying property remains.
