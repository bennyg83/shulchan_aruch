(Seif 18: Reuven has a house, and so forth). The rulings of the Mechaber and Rama require examination. Here we say that the rule concerning one who derives title from a gentile applies only to his detriment. Yet in siman 45, seif 17, regarding leaving two blank lines; in siman 66, seif 25, regarding a waiver that is ineffective; and in siman 67, regarding the release of debts in the Sabbatical year, the Mechaber rules that the Jew stands in the gentile's position. The Sema in siman 45 answers that there the borrower suffers no injury, but this distinction is not satisfactory. Rama's statement in seif 19 that “it has already been explained that some disagree” is also difficult, because the dissenters in seif 18 rule in favor of the second Jew. It appears that all can be resolved as follows. Whenever a Jew obligated himself to a gentile, the gentile's claim is not stripped of any of its legal incidents merely because it later came into a Jew's hands. Otherwise, one who purchased a note from a gentile could not collect it from the Jewish borrower. Clearly, the purchaser stands in the seller's place. This explains the laws in simanim 45 and 67. Here in seif 18, however, the Jew never became obligated to the gentile in any respect. Everything is therefore consistent.
