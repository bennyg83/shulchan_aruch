Reuven gave or sold simultaneously. The text carefully says 'simultaneously.' In that case, according to all opinions, one may build in front of the other's windows. If he sold to them one after another, however, there is a dispute, analogous to the case where one sells the courtyard but retains the house for himself, because the first purchaser receives all rights the seller possessed. The laws of two people who bought from one owner and later divided, or two who bought from two owners, or from two brothers who divided, are explained below in siman 173:3.
To build in front of them. The Mechaber follows his ruling in the preceding seif that the neighbor may build in front of the windows but may not demand that the window owner close them. Rama, who cites an opinion that he may say, 'Close your window,' would apply the same here. The Tur likewise writes without qualification that in this case the window must be closed, because he follows the Rosh's view in the preceding law. See Darkei Moshe for further details.
Some say he cannot say, 'Close it.' Here too Rama uses the language 'close it' in accordance with his view. According to the Mechaber the practical issue would be whether he may build in front of it.
Some disagree. The source concludes with the reason: although a seller generally sells generously, he does not sell away his right to be free of continuing damage. The window causes him visual harm at every moment. This applies where the damage already existed at the time of sale. But if he sold a house with a window opening toward his own vacant lot, even if that lot was expected to be built upon, since the buyer opened or possessed the window permissibly, when the seller later builds on the vacant lot he cannot force closure of the window.
