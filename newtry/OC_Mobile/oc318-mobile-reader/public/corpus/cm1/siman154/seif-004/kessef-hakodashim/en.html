Seif 6. (To build opposite it.) If two parties stipulated that neither would darken the other's windows opening onto the public domain, and their document states that there shall be no causation of damage or impairment to a particular basement window, it appears sufficient to maintain the four cubit distance fixed by the Sages. Even if some shadow remains, and even if the condition concerned a window opening into a courtyard where the law already requires four cubits without any special stipulation, one should not expand the condition beyond that established measure merely by inference. This is especially so in times and places where parties often write explicit contractual terms even for matters already required by Torah law, because enforcement of Torah based civil law may otherwise be uncertain. The Sages themselves fixed four cubits as the ordinary boundary for this concern, and the rule that documentary ambiguity is construed against the holder of the instrument supports limiting the condition to that measure. The matter nevertheless requires a little further study.
