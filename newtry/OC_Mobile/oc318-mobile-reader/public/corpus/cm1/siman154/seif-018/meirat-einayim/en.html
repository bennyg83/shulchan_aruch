After some years. Meaning, after he had possessed the opening for several years, as is clear from what follows.
Although the purchaser comes through a non-Jew, etc. Under non-Jewish law there is no permanent right to light. Each may open toward another's property, and when the neighbor later wishes he may block the light even after many years. So writes the Tur.
Only to his detriment. For example, where a Jew purchases a field from a non-Jew and holds it for several years, and another Jew proves that the field was formerly his. We say that one who comes through a non-Jew has the non-Jew's status and cannot establish chazakah over Jewish property without a deed, as the Mechaber writes in siman 149:14-15.
And the non-Jew could have blocked it, etc., he may block it. Meaning, he may block the light by constructing a building in front of the window. Without new construction, however, he cannot compel the window owner to close it, because he has no greater power than the non-Jew, and under their law one cannot force the other to close a window, only build in one's own courtyard in front of it when one wishes.
