Since it arose automatically. Sema cites another reason from the Rosh, brought by the Tur in siman 157: chazakah does not apply here. The reason chazakah ordinarily works is that the owner failed to protest and thereby loses his claim. That applies specifically where the possessor causes harm but suffers none, such as one who has a window open toward his fellow's courtyard. But where the possessor himself is harmed just as he harms the other, he can say, 'I did not trouble to protest because I harm you just as you harm me, and I waited for you to initiate a claim.' Moreover, visual intrusion is a constant form of harm and resembles smoke, unlike an ordinary window, which is made for light rather than to gaze into another's courtyard. A practical difference between the two reasons arises with visual damage between a roof and a courtyard. Siman 160 explains that the roof owner harms the courtyard owner, while the courtyard owner does not harm the roof owner. According to the first reason, chazakah can operate there because the possessor harms but is not harmed. According to the second reason, chazakah does not operate even between courtyard and roof, because the harm resembles smoke.
