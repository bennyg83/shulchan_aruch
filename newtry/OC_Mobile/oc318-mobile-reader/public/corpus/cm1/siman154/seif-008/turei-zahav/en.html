(Seif 15: This applies specifically in a place, and so forth). The Tur wrote this in siman 11 in the name of a responsum of the Rosh, concerning two neighbors, one of whom extends a projection from his upper story into the public domain opposite the airspace before the other's upper story. The one who acted first acted unlawfully. The Mechaber transferred this condition to the law of two houses separated by a public domain, although the wording is not exact here, for it should have said “opposite his neighbor's upper story,” not “opposite the airspace before his neighbor's upper story.” He merely followed the Rosh's wording. It appears that the Mechaber intended to rule like Rashba in seif 14, whose view he cites in seif 31, that the second owner may raise his wall although the first owner has no prescriptive right. This is why he writes there, “Rashba wrote,” indicating that the law follows Rashba here as well, just as in the rule stated in seif 15.
