Seal your windows. This appears difficult, because in seif 18 it was ruled that the purchaser may not even build opposite the window. One cannot answer that there the Jew's window had been open before the non Jew built alongside it, when the adjacent area was ownerless, because the Rama explicitly wrote there that all this concerns a case where it is known that the non Jew's courtyard existed first. It appears that the distinction is as follows. Seif 18 discusses a window opening onto a roof, where there is no visual damage from a roof, as explained in siman 159. The purchaser merely wishes to raise his own building, and the window indirectly prevents him from building. Since the purchaser suffers only indirect loss, we do not say that he acquired the non Jew's right to compel closure. Seif 19, however, concerns a courtyard where the window causes visual damage, which is treated like direct damage. Therefore he can compel the neighbor to seal it, and certainly may build opposite it. Chazakah does not help, because the possessor does not claim that he purchased the right from the non Jew. Even if he does, it does not help, because the non Jew had no right of protest. Where there was no ability to protest, there can be no chazakah. Sema. See the Beurim:
