Reuven who had... Meaning he opened a window toward a non-Jew's courtyard that predated the window, and under Jewish law the non-Jew could have closed it. The purchaser from the non-Jew argues that he may now close the Jew's window. This is the third view cited by Mordechai in the name of the Geonim, that a Jew who comes through a non-Jew has the non-Jew's status even to the Jew's detriment. Since the Jew could not protest against the non-Jew, he acquired no chazakah even against the Jew who later purchases from him.
Collected note: Reuven... the law... Hagahos Ashri, siman 24.
Even if he claims. A supporting claim without chazakah is insufficient where chazakah was legally unavailable. Bava Basra 41a and the Yerushalmi require both. Here belongs the earlier gloss that no chazakah helps where the original owner could not protest. That principle is evident throughout the laws of chazakah.
