(See there, gloss: Some say that he may not build). At first glance this is difficult, for it is a settled rule in the chapter HaMokher et HaBayit and in siman 214 that a seller sells generously. It appears that this opinion follows Tosafot, cited nearby, that one may never completely darken a house. Here the construction would eliminate all light, and in such a case we do not apply the rule that a seller sells generously. This also explains the Tur's statement in the name of Rabbi Yehudah of Barcelona: “The decisive consideration is that he need not distance himself, because we hold that a seller sells generously.” The Tur had already cited this proof earlier, so what was added by calling it decisive? He means that the settled rule that a seller sells generously ordinarily applies in every respect, whereas Rama nevertheless cites the dissenting opinion for a case of total darkness.
