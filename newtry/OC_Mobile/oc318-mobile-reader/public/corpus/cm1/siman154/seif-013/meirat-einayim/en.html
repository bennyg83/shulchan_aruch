He cannot prevent him. The reason is that we compel a person not to act with the trait of Sodom, as the Tur explains.
To another small opening. Meaning, in a different place. This refers to the case where he wishes to change the location of his window, as explained above at the end of seif 7.
He may demolish it whenever he wishes. Meaning, he may demolish the wall for his own purposes and tell his fellow: after I remove my wall, we will build another wall at our joint expense to eliminate visual damage. One cannot object from Rama's gloss below at the end of seif 16, citing the Rosh, that Reuven had windows opposite his fellow's courtyard and when the wall fell he had to close the windows even though they had originally been opened permissibly. There, once the wall fell, Reuven looking from his window would be the sole source of damage to the garden owner. Here both parties contribute equally to the visual damage, as I explain nearby in Sema, seif katan 23.
