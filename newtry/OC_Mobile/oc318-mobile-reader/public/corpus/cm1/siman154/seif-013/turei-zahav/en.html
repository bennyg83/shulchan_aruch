(Seif 28: And the windows were open). In the Tur the text read, “and the windows,” with the definite article, and that is the correct reading. The practical consequence is that these were the principal windows through which this house received light. Thus the buyer may entirely block the house's light, yet he is still entitled to do so, as is evident from the Tur's failure to distinguish this case. Although the Tur and Rama distinguish nearby in seif 27 concerning additional light, there the owner of the house paid the owner of the courtyard an increased price, and presumably the value of the light was included in that increase. Here, however, perhaps the house was sold to him without any right to light. This follows from the Rosh at the beginning of Bava Batra: “Even according to Ri, where the house has no other source of light except the window overlooking the other property, when they appraised the houses and upper stories against one another, their intention included the enhanced value of the light.” This implies that in other cases, such as one who sells simultaneously to two purchasers, it makes no difference that one purchaser completely darkens the other's house. This is also the Tur's view, and Tosafot there appears to agree.
