Or he narrows the top of the wall, etc. Meaning, he slopes or sharpens it so that one cannot sit on it and peer over. Rosh, Rashbam, and the Tur require both conditions: the wall must be four amot high and its top must also be sloped and narrowed. See the Derishah for the reason for their dispute. Darkei Moshe cites Maggid Mishneh as ruling that Rambam's approach is primary, and therefore the Mechaber and Rama omit the view of Rashbam and the Tur.
This tefach is measured from the window. The Tur writes that unspecified amot in the Talmud contain six tefachim, and each tefach contains four thumbbreadths. Teshuvot Maimoniyot at the end of Sefer Kinyan, siman 14, writes that the thumb is measured at its first joint. Darkei Moshe cites this. I do not know why the Mechaber omitted these details.
And not from the splayed edge. Meaning, where the wall around the window is beveled inward or outward so as to widen the field of view from a distance. See Mordechai, siman 695.
He may splay it afterward. Meaning, once he is entitled to make the window, he may later bevel it without needing new permission from his neighbor. Therefore this bevel does not create chazakah preventing the neighbor from later building in his own courtyard alongside the wall where the bevel extends.
