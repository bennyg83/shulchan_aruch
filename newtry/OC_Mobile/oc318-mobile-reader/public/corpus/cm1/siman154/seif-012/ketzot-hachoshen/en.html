And immediately it is deemed a waiver. Sema writes that the Rosh and those who follow him agree here because of the Mechaber's reason: a person does not ordinarily allow his light to be blocked and remain silent unless he has waived his right. Rambam likewise gives this reason concerning blocking a window, although Rambam holds that other forms of nuisance can also become subject to chazakah immediately. Sema explains that in other nuisances this is only when we know that the injured person noticed the harm and remained silent, whereas here the blocking itself proves that he noticed it, since ordinarily a person immediately senses the loss of his light.
Sema's statement that in other nuisances we require affirmative knowledge that the injured party noticed the harm is not precise. Rambam writes explicitly in chapter 11 of Hilchot Shechenim that where one has established a nuisance and the injured party says, 'I noticed it only now and did not previously know,' the burden of proof is upon the injured party. Shulchan Aruch rules likewise in siman 155. When Shulchan Aruch says in seif 4, 'or he knew of the harm and did not protest,' this excludes a case where it is affirmatively established that he did not know. Ordinarily we presume he knew, and he is not believed merely to say that he did not, just as one in a three-year chazakah case is not believed merely to say, 'I did not know,' as Maggid Mishneh writes in chapter 11 of Hilchot Shechenim. The distinction between the immediate chazakah of other nuisances and the immediate waiver created by blocking a window is different. With other nuisances, although three years are unnecessary, some period is still required, long enough that the nuisance could reasonably have become known to the injured party. After that period he is no longer believed to say he did not know. With blocking a window, no waiting period is needed at all, because the harm is immediately apparent. So Rivash writes in siman 171, and so I wrote in siman 153, seif katan 1.
