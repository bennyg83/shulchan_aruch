If Reuven acquired an ownerless ruin and built houses and upper stories there with windows and doorways, and Shimon later also acquired adjoining ownerless property and built houses and upper stories opposite him, so that the second is injured by the first through invasion of privacy and demands that the first remove the harm, the second party's claim has no substance. The first person acquired his right, and the second cannot restrain him. Moreover, the second may not himself create an invasion of the first person's privacy, such as by opening a window toward the first person's courtyard or directly opposite it. See note 200.
