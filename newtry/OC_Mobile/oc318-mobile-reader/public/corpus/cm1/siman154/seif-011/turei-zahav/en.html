(Seif 20: The purchaser may further say, and so forth). The Sema answers in seif katan 48 that here there is no visible indication, but this is exceedingly difficult, for the case is precisely one in which the window remains visible from the interior as before, and that itself proves that it was not permanently sealed. It appears instead that in seif 12 the owner of the window had a complete and established right, and the question was whether the sealing diminished that right. Here, by contrast, he never had a clearly established right, for perhaps the window was sealed immediately, and even if it was not sealed immediately, perhaps the gentile later sealed it and thereby established a right against him. Since the original right is uncertain, the purchaser may raise all these claims. I found in one corrected copy of the Tur: “How do we know that it had been open and was later sealed? Moreover, there is reason to suspect, and so forth.” This reading is correct.
