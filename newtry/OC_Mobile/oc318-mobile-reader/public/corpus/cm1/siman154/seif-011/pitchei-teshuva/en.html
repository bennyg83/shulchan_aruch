And the courtyard owner cannot build opposite it. Responsa Shevut Yaakov I, siman 159, considers windows in Reuven's house facing Shimon's garden, near which Shimon later planted trees. Reuven demanded that the trees be cut because they darkened his windows. There is no prohibition of wanton destruction when a tree is cut to prevent injury to oneself or another. The principal question is whether the claim of darkening applies to a tree as it does to a building. Shevut Yaakov concludes that this depends upon the circumstances: if the tree is thick and large and substantially blocks the light, the neighbor may object as he could to a building. He there cites Chavot Yair, siman 195, as supposedly requiring the tree to be cut, but the cited responsum actually concerns a tree shading its owner's own window and merely rules that he may cut it. The wording 'must cut' is therefore evidently a scribal error for 'may cut.'
