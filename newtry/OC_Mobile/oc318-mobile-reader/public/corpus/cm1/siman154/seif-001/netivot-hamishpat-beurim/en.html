Only as he originally built it. See Taz, who asks from the Rosh concerning two people who purchased a ruin. Even if one initially built his upper story with light materials, he may later rebuild it with heavy materials, because he can say, "Had I wanted, I could have built it heavily from the outset, and I did not forfeit my right." Why then should the rule here be different? In my view this rule applies specifically to an opening. The space opposite an opening is not ordinarily available for the neighbor's use, both because each entrance may have four cubits of frontage and because of privacy damage. When the parties originally build, each may place openings into the courtyard according to local custom or in proportion to the number the other party opened. If one then chooses an opening in a particular place and the other likewise chooses his opening elsewhere, where four cubits belong to each entrance this resembles partners dividing by directions. Once one acquires his designated side, the other acquires the corresponding side. They have effectively divided where each person's four cubits of entrance space will be, leaving the remainder jointly owned. Even where there are no four cubits belonging to each entrance, as discussed later in siman 288, the area before an entrance is not naturally fit for use. When one party makes his opening in a particular place and the other uses a different area, that use amounts to an act of acquisition in the place he occupies. By contrast, with roofing an upper story in cedar rather than sycamore, the neighbor's supposed right arises only because the first builder initially chose lighter roofing. That is like seif 8, where mere inaction without any affirmative act does not create chazakah. The builder can say, "At the time I did not have cedar, so I used sycamore. Now I do." The neighbor did nothing to acquire a right preventing heavier roofing. The same therefore appears true where two people purchase a ruin and one builds a house with an upper story while the other builds only a house. The second may later add an upper story opening into his own house. The fact that neither party performed any act regarding that unused vertical right does not create chazakah. This is comparable to the rule of cedar and sycamore in siman 164, seif 4. An opening is different because where a person originally had a right to make two openings but made only one, failure to make the second resembles sealing an existing opening, which can create immediate chazakah because he had the right to do it and affirmatively left it unused.
