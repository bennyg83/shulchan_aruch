(There: two people who jointly own, etc.) The Sema's second ruling in seif katan 5, that one partner may place others in his own stead, is certainly correct, because he could sell his share. But this creates a difficulty with the first ruling there, that even if the partner himself does not live in the house he may not bring many other people into his portion. Why should we not say there too that he could have sold his share to many people? It is obvious that a partner may sell his share even to several purchasers. One cannot answer that Mordechai forbids authorizing others to use his share despite his ability to sell it. Mordechai there concerns a case where the partner himself continues living there and also permits outsiders to use the premises. The objection is not that the outsiders are numerous. Even one outsider is prohibited when the partner himself has not withdrawn from the house and his name remains associated with it, because he may not add a stranger who has no share in the house. Although the question in Mordechai says, "one partner authorizes many others," that wording is not essential to the answer. Indeed, the Rama omits the word "many" and simply says that he may not allow a person from the market to use the premises with him. But if he withdraws and puts others in his place, I see no prohibition at all. That is equivalent to a sale, and we do not fear that the house will be damaged merely because of the replacement occupants. This appears correct to me.
