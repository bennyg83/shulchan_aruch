A person may not open a window facing his neighbor's courtyard. Even one of the partners in a courtyard who wishes to open a window from his house into the courtyard may be stopped by the other partner because of visual intrusion. If he opened it, he must close it. If the courtyard partners gave him permission to open a window or doorway, he may do so, provided that he does not open a doorway directly opposite another doorway or a window directly opposite another window. Rama: A doorway directly opposite a doorway does not acquire a chazakah, and the same applies to a window opposite a window. Beit Yosef in the name of Rashbam. Some say that a window opposite a window can acquire a chazakah. Tur in the name of Ramah. If both parties seek to open facing doorways or windows at the same time, and one suffers no loss while the other benefits, the first is compelled under the rule against the trait of Sodom. If both would suffer loss, they should compromise. Nimukei Yosef, chapter Chezkas. Rather, one should offset his opening at least slightly from the other's. If the opening faces another courtyard and permission was given to open a doorway or window, he must move it far enough from the neighbor's opening that he cannot see into it at all. A person may, however, open a house doorway opposite a house doorway, or a window opposite a window, across a public domain if the openings are not higher than four amot, because he may say, "I am no different from the members of the public domain who can see you." Rama: Regarding one who builds first on an upper story, and whether the person opposite can object by saying, "Today or tomorrow I too will open an opening and you will then object to me," see seifim 14 through 17 of this siman. With respect to an alleyway that has at least three houses farther inside it, some say that it is treated like a public domain; others say that anything not open from end to end is not treated as a public domain. If one opens toward a public domain in a manner that creates visual intrusion into the courtyard across the public domain, the neighbor may object. Nimukei Yosef and Mordechai, first chapter of Bava Batra, and this follows from close reading of the authorities. Nevertheless, a person may not open a shop directly opposite his neighbor's doorway or window, because this creates constant visual intrusion: passersby in the public domain come and go and do not constantly look inside, while the shopkeeper sits in his shop all day and looks toward the neighbor's doorway or window. He must therefore distance the shop sufficiently that he cannot see inside at all. Some say that a person must take care not to gaze into his neighbor's house, to the point that such looking makes him regarded like a thief when he has no legitimate reason for doing so. Nimukei Yosef, chapter Chezkas.
