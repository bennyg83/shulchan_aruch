He may not open. This is stated in the Gemara there.
Even... This is the Mishnah there.
Because... This is the Gemara's reason.
If they gave permission. See Beer HaGolah. The Yerushalmi says the same, as explained below.
Regarding an opening opposite... Rashbam explains the Mishnah as a case where a right had already been established, because otherwise even an opening not directly opposite would be forbidden, and an unlawful opening cannot acquire a chazakah.
Some say. They explain the Mishnah as a case where permission was explicitly given, as above. See Sema.
If... they compel him. Bava Basra 59a.
If both... Bava Basra 6b concerning two houses facing one another. Radvaz explains that the case is where both opened at the same time, because otherwise the second would have to construct the entire protective barrier as stated in the Mishnah.
Rather, he must distance it. Rashbam explains so. Ramban must be understanding the Gemara's phrase, "until now, in the courtyard..." according to Rashbam's second interpretation. In that approach, the objection is not that any possible view is forbidden, but that a direct facing opening causes constant observation. Once the owner has consented to ordinary visibility, the remaining prohibition is against a directly facing opening or window because of its constant intrusion.
If it is... There he cannot see the other at all because he has no right to enter the courtyard, as stated in the case of two houses. The required distance is enough to prevent all viewing, as in siman 159.
If they are not... Ri Migash and Ramban infer from the case of the two houses that this concerns walls below four cubits, the ordinary measure for visual privacy. This is unlike Rashbam and the Rosh.
Into an alley through which the public... The apparently conflicting inferences are resolved by saying that "public domain" here includes any route through which a public passes. Three houses can constitute a public for this purpose, following Rav Nachman.
Some say that every... A through-alley certainly has the law of a public domain, as the Rif writes. A closed alley, however, is not treated as a public domain. The Yerushalmi distinguishes between an alley and a courtyard, and explains that in an alley permission can matter, whereas in a courtyard ordinary privacy rights remain stronger. See Nimukei Yosef.
An opening to the public domain... The Gemara says that the other can answer, "In any event you must..." But where the opening itself causes a distinct injury, it remains forbidden, as in Bava Basra 6b and the Tosefta cited below.
Therefore he must... As above. If it faces another courtyard, the same rule applies.
