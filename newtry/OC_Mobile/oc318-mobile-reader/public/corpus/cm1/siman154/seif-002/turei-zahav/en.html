(Seif 2, gloss: Lodgers and residents). It appears to me that Ramah also agrees, as the Tur writes in his name in siman 156, seif 6, that if one wishes to rent his house to several people who will reside in it, this is permitted. This is unlike Beit Yosef, who writes that according to Ramah one may not bring outside residents into his house. In connection with this, I will explain Ramah's words, which require clarification. Ramah understands that the Mishnah's permission to divide one room into two, as the Tur explains, was not intended to permit placing additional residents there, meaning people who will live in the added room. If he does not create a separate room, however, he may take people into his own household, and they are considered lodgers. Ramah says that the prohibition against adding people who will live in a separate room applies even where no new structure is added, but one merely divides one room into two. The Mishnah permitted such division only for creating a storeroom, not for housing additional people. Adding a new structure is prohibited even if he does not intend to place residents in it, because he can use the additional room adjoining his house for commerce or some other activity beyond what he previously possessed. The amount of passage through the courtyard would therefore be substantially greater than before. Ramah then explains his statement that adding residents is prohibited even though no new structure is built. The prohibition applies only after he adds rooms by dividing them, but without dividing the existing rooms it is entirely permitted, since he is not adding, and so forth. The term for “residents” written with a final mem throughout Ramah's discussion means people. The term written with a final nun means rooms. At the end, the correct reading is, “There is no distinction whether the number of rooms increased,” and so forth.
