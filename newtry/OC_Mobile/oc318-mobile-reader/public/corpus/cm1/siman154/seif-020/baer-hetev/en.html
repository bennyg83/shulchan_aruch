To say. This is unlike the Rama's ruling in seif 12 that if the owner of the window says, 'I sealed it,' the burden of proof rests on the owner of the courtyard. Here the non-Jew is not before us, and we may say that perhaps he could have produced proof that he had sealed it. More plausibly, there the case is where the jambs still remained within the window, which demonstrates that he did not intend to seal it permanently, whereas here it was not known whether jambs had ever been there. This accords with the opinion cited by the Rama in seif 11 that local custom is followed, so that where the custom is to acquire a chazakah even without jambs, it is a chazakah. This is the Sema's language. The Taz writes that this answer is exceedingly difficult, because here too the window is visibly intact from the inside, and that itself proves that it was not permanently sealed. He therefore explains that in seif 12 the owner of the window had a complete chazakah, and only the later sealing was alleged to weaken his right. Here, by contrast, he never had a definite chazakah, since perhaps the opening was sealed immediately; and even if not, perhaps the non-Jew sealed it and thereby established his own chazakah. Since the original chazakah is uncertain, the other party may make this claim. The Taz adds that he found a corrected text reading, 'and it was sealed, and there is further concern,' which is correct.
