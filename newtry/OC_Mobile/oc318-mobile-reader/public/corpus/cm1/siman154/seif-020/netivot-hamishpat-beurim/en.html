Who says that it had been open and was later sealed? See Sema, seif katan 45, who asks from seif 12, where the window owner is believed, and answers with great difficulty. In my view that case is different because the window owner is treated as holding the chazakah of the prior owner, as Shach explains in siman 114. That applies specifically between two Jews. When a Jew opens a window toward another Jew and establishes a right, the result is that he acquired or received waiver concerning the airspace. The airspace then becomes effectively his, because the other Jew may not build opposite the window. Since it was once his, he is treated as the prior owner, and later doubts are resolved in favor of that prior chazakah. Between a Jew and a non Jew, however, opening the window does not make the airspace belong to the Jew. The non Jew remains permitted to build opposite it. The Jew can open the window only because there is no actionable privacy damage against the non Jew, under the principle that one benefits and the other does not lose. Even according to the opinion cited by Rama that the Jew may not later build opposite the non Jew's window, the airspace still does not become the window owner's property in the same sense. Since the air was never his at the moment of opening, he is not treated as the prior owner. This also clarifies the wording, "Who says that it had been open and was later sealed?" Meaning: who says that it had been open through a valid acquisition in a manner that made the right yours, and was only afterward sealed? Since you have no prior ownership chazakah in the window right, we must also consider that perhaps the non Jew himself sealed it. There is also no difficulty from the rule stated earlier that sealing immediately creates chazakah. One might ask that a non Jew is treated as a robber and cannot establish chazakah. According to what I have written, however, the airspace never became the Jew's property through the opening. He merely had permission to open because his use did not legally deprive the non Jew. If the non Jew later closed it, even forcibly, this is like taking back his own airspace. When he later sells to a Jew, the airspace passes to the purchaser, and reopening the window is treated like opening a new window from the beginning. Alternatively, the case can concern a country with functioning courts where dina d'malchuta governs. It would then resemble siman 236, seif 9, where we presume that the original owner has despaired of recovery because the governing law has conclusively placed the right elsewhere.
