Although Rama wrote in seif 12 that in a similar case the owner of the courtyard must bring proof, there the gentile is not before us, and one may say that if he were present he would bring proof. It further appears that there the frame of the former opening was still recognizable, whereas here it was no longer recognizable whether such a frame ever existed, and the chazakah rests only upon local custom mentioned by Rama in seif 11. Sema. See Beurim: the essential distinction is that there the dispute is between two Jews. Once one has chazakah in the window, the airspace becomes his right because the other Jew cannot build opposite it. Therefore we preserve the window holder's status as prior owner. Here the dispute arises through a gentile. As long as the property remained in the gentile's hand, the airspace never became the window holder's property because the gentile could build opposite him. Therefore the window holder was never the prior owner of that airspace.
