Furthermore, the purchaser can, etc. This is not comparable to Rama's ruling above at the end of seif 12, that if the window owner says, "I closed it temporarily," the courtyard owner bears the burden of proof. Here the gentile is no longer before us, and one may say that if he were present he could bring proof that the window had in fact been permanently closed. More likely, however, there the window still retained its doorposts or frame, which is evidence that the owner did not intend a permanent closure. That is the case discussed there. Here it is not known whether any such frame remained. This resembles the opinion cited by Rama in seif 11 that local custom determines whether an opening can remain established without visible framing. See the Perishah.
