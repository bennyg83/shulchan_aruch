Seif 15. (Like a jointly owned courtyard.) Although an opening ordinarily requires only a four cubit separation in certain contexts, a new opening into a private courtyard can be prevented entirely because of the additional intrusion and foot traffic it creates, as explained at the beginning of siman 154. Seif 15 further teaches that where each party has a right to project beams or structures into a common airspace, that airspace is treated like a jointly owned courtyard and one party may not open a new window into it. The same reasoning applies to an open area in front of a person's entrance where, by local law or accepted building practice, he has the right to extend construction or otherwise use the area as an owner would use the space before his own doorway. That area is then functionally comparable to a jointly owned courtyard. The neighbor may not create a window or entrance there that causes any new impairment to the first person's use. Even if the neighbor already had an opening, if he reconstructs it in a manner that causes significantly greater interference according to the reasonable assessment of the neighbors, the affected party appears entitled to object. Although the Bach is cited as disagreeing with the rule in seif 15, I do not presently have his work before me for careful review. The Taz appears to follow Beit Yosef rather than the Bach, and the Sema also appears to accept that approach. The Shach's brief citation of the Bach does not necessarily prove that he adopted the Bach's conclusion; it may simply direct the reader to examine the Bach. On reviewing the language of the Sema and Taz, it may even be that the Bach does not dispute the case where the party truly has a legal right to project structures and windows into the airspace, in which event the area is indeed comparable to a jointly owned courtyard. The Bach's exact position requires further study.
