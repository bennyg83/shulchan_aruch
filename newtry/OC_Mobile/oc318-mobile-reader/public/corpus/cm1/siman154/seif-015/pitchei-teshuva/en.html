And even according to the view that he acquires a prescriptive right, etc. See Sema, who states that Rabbeinu Yonah and the Rivash hold this way. Responsa Mayim Chaim, Choshen Mishpat, siman 7, discusses Reuven, who opened a window toward an area belonging to his fellow that had for many years served as a boundary used by the public. Reuven claimed to have one witness who had seen that the area was formally designated as a public passage. If the witness so testifies, the neighbor plainly cannot object to the window; moreover, the witness assists Reuven and exempts him from an oath. Even without that witness, Mayim Chaim maintains that under the Taz's understanding of Rama in siman 155, a right of this sort is acquired immediately. Since the Mechaber in siman 154:16 allows the neighbor to object even when the window presently causes no visual injury, and Rama adds that this remains so even according to those who recognize a prescriptive right, Reuven may assert the views of Rabbeinu Yonah and Rivash that the right arises immediately where objection was possible. No special claim is required where the right is based on immediate waiver. Beit Yosef, citing Nimukei Yosef, likewise indicates that according to Rambam even a window that presently causes no visual injury acquires an immediate right. Thus Reuven may rely on those authorities.
