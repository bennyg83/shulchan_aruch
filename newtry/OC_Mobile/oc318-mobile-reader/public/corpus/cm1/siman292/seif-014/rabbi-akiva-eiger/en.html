[Rama] It is presumed to have separated from the majority. Note: Responsa Maimoniyot and Responsa Rashba, siman 893, imply the opposite. Their statement that the reason is the rule governing a fixed item implies that without that consideration we would follow the majority. I saw that Terumat HaDeshen likewise infers that this was Maharam's view. However, he understands Maharam's reasoning to be that where the majority is physically before us, one follows the majority even in monetary law. This is unlike the view of Mordechai in the first chapter of Ketubot, that even where the majority is physically before us we do not follow the majority in monetary law. According to that view, the same rule would apply here. However, Responsa Maharit, Choshen Mishpat, siman 124, writes that Maharam's reason is that the presence of other people's money in the mixture has been established, while the money now found has no prior presumptive status of ownership in the possessor's favor beyond the fact that it was found in his possession. In such a case, the majority is stronger. According to this, all authorities would agree that where a person mixed his friend's rings with his own, we follow the majority.
