Produce. The Rosh concludes in the chapter Shevuat HaDayanim that even property worth less than a perutah is termed "produce." His words there clearly indicate that he understands the Rambam to be speaking where the custodian expressly says, "I know the produce was worth at least one perutah more, but I do not know how much more." The Tur cites this at the end of siman 87. In my humble opinion, this is not the Rambam's view. Rather, even where one admits generally that he received produce, his admission is not presumed to concern less than a perutah. I have already written at length above, siman 72, seif 12, challenging the Rosh on this matter. See there, Responsa Rashach, vol. 2, siman 17, and Responsa Rashdam, siman 69. Shach.
Negligent. Meaning, because of this, if after he mixed the produce the depositor comes and says, "Return my deposit, which contained such and such an amount," and the custodian says, "I do not know," the rule applies. The Rambam begins there with this law and writes: "A custodian who became liable to pay and says, 'I do not know how much,' while the depositor makes a definite claim, the depositor collects without an oath." This is the law recorded by the Tur and Shulchan Aruch at the beginning of siman 298. Although the Rosh disagrees there, he agrees in this case, as the Tur writes, because here the custodian mixed the produce with his own by hand, which is greater negligence. Sema. The Maggid Mishneh makes the same distinction, cited by the Beit Yosef. It further appears to me, and this is the principal explanation of the Rosh, that he disagrees there only when the deposit was given in a sack, because it is common that a custodian does not carefully ascertain what is inside sacks and bundled packages, unlike here. See what I wrote above, siman 72, seif 12. As a matter of practical law, however, I already concluded there in accordance with the Rambam. Shach.
