Gold coins. As explained in siman 292 concerning identified gold coins, one who is in possession may sometimes say, 'Yours was the one that was lost,' rather than dividing the loss proportionally. The same applies to banknotes of different denominations, because a larger note is individually distinguishable from smaller ones. Likewise, if funds of several persons were commingled and exchanged for currency when exchange rates fluctuated, the possessor may claim that his own funds were the portion exchanged at the favorable rate. Even though at the time of commingling he did not know which exchange would later prove advantageous, once he selected a portion and declared privately that he was first exchanging for himself, since equivalent coins may be separated and allocated, he may designate them to himself or to whomever he chooses. Those who sent money through him for exchange may nevertheless impose upon him the oath of partners or a member of the household. If he can establish that the money held for another was exchanged during the low-rate period, there may be no basis even for a formal ban, because at the time he could not know whether later rates would rise or fall. His advantage resulted automatically from possession and identification, not from selecting in advance for his own benefit. This still requires study. Nor does he necessarily forfeit his fee merely because he delayed and the exchange later became less favorable, where the delay was not negligent, such as when exchange was not readily available then or because of the urgency of preparing for Shabbat.
