Seif 4. If he says that he wishes to make unauthorized use of the deposit. Note: See Sema and Shach regarding agency for wrongdoing. In my fourth edition on Yoreh De'ah I brought a strong proof for the Sema from Sanhedrin, Perek Ben Sorer UMoreh, that even where agency applies to a prohibited act, the agent himself remains liable; the agency rule only adds liability to the principal. I also proved there that although a non-Jew ordinarily cannot serve as an agent, where the act is compelled and he acts under coercion, agency can apply. I addressed the Shach's objection to the Sema in my work on Orach Chaim. I also suggested there that the Torah's special rule establishing agency for wrongdoing in me'ilah and slaughter or sale of stolen property may apply only where neither actor could accomplish the act alone. Where each could accomplish it alone, perhaps even in those cases there is no agency for wrongdoing, because it resembles two persons jointly performing an act that either could have performed alone. As to the Shach's conclusion in the name of his father, that even where agency exists for wrongdoing it makes the principal liable but does not exempt the agent, I brought a proof in my work on Avodah Zarah, part 2, addenda. Yet the Shach's own ruling here, that the Torah merely added liability to the principal while the agent remains liable as well, seems difficult from Kiddushin 43. Rava says that Shammai concedes where one tells another, 'Eat forbidden fat' or 'engage in a prohibited sexual relation,' that the actor is liable and the sender is exempt, because we have never found that one person derives the benefit while another incurs the liability. The wording, 'the actor is liable,' implies that ordinarily, according to Shammai when the sender is liable, the agent is exempt. Otherwise the novelty here would only be that the sender is exempt, and Rava need not have emphasized that the actor is liable. Moreover, if both could ordinarily be liable, from where did Rava know that Shammai exempts the agent? Shammai only said that the sender is liable. He could agree with the Sages that the agent is also liable and merely add liability to the sender. It must be that Rava understood that it is not reasonable to impose full liability on both for one act, comparable to the case where each could perform the act alone and two do it together. The dispute is therefore which actor is treated as primary: the Sages make the agent primary, while Shammai makes the principal primary. But both cannot be fully liable. This appears contrary to the Shach. Understand this carefully.
