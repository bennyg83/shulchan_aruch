Mordechai writes there that he does not bear the law of a borrower but only that of an ordinary custodian, and a custodian's intent is deemed equivalent to the owner's intent. Meaning, if the custodian borrowed an article from the deposit in his possession and returned it to the place where it had originally rested, it is as though the owner knew of this. Mordechai further writes that even according to Rabbi Akiva, who holds that the custodian's responsibility has ended and the owner's knowledge is required upon return, that applies only to a thief, whom the owner no longer trusts. A borrower, however, remains trusted, and his custodianship has not ended. This is difficult, for here too he is termed a robber because he borrowed without the owner's knowledge. We should likewise say that the owner no longer trusts him. It appears that the Rama and Mordechai discuss one who borrowed without permission, but nevertheless he is not comparable to a thief. If the owner knows that this person stole, he will no longer trust him. By contrast, people do not regard one who borrows without permission as so clearly a robber, just as the Gemara states in the first chapter of Bava Metzia that ordinary people understand the prohibition "You shall not covet" as applying only when no payment is given. Yet the Rama's ruling is very difficult in light of seif 6 of this siman. There, if the custodian moved the item for his own need, thereby becoming a borrower without permission and thus a robber, he remains liable even if he later returned it to its original place, because he had already become a robber. This is also implied by the Rosh cited by Beit Yosef in seif 11 of this siman, who writes that even according to Rabbi Akiva, because he used it with permission, implying that without permission he remains liable even as a borrower. See there. It therefore appears to me that Mordechai does not discuss one who borrows without permission, for such a person is certainly exactly like a thief. Rather, he discusses one who borrowed from the owners an article that was already in his possession as a deposit. In that case he ruled that it need not be returned with the owner's knowledge, because the owner still trusts him.
