And moved it for his own need. This is also the language of the Mishnah and the poskim. The Gemara infers from the word "moved it," rather than "took it," that the case applies even where he did not remove it from its place in order to steal it or make unauthorized use intending to diminish it. He merely moved it so he could climb upon it as a ladder and take young birds from a dovecote, intending afterward to return it intact to its place. Even so, he becomes liable for unavoidable accidents because he moved it for his own use. For this reason the Rambam and Mechaber did not write that he "made unauthorized use" of the barrel. Although the Tur does use that expression here, in the Perishah I explained that it is not literal. Since householders ordinarily object to a barrel being used this way lest it break, the conduct has some resemblance to unauthorized use. This is unlike the Ir Shushan, who twice writes that the case requires intent to diminish the object.
