There the Gemara asks regarding theft, "what are the circumstances? Always, etc." This is unlike Rav Sheshes, who establishes it according to Rabbi Yishmael, for then the halachah would follow Rabbi Yishmael under the rule that where a Mishnah states one view anonymously and a baraisa records the dispute, etc. See Rif. Semag in the name of Ri, Hagahos Maimoniyos chapter 4, and Hagahos Ashri chapter 1, however, rule like Rabbi Yishmael. See Rashi there, s.v. "but he took them, etc."
He moved it, etc. His obligation of guarding has not yet ended even according to Rabbi Akiva, as stated there. The special rule applies only when he took it with intent to misappropriate it or use the item itself as an unauthorized borrower. See Rashi there, s.v. "interpret it": "for if, etc." Although we do not rule that way, this nevertheless teaches the law in such a case.
And if an occurrence happened, etc. See Beer HaGolah. This is the meaning of the Mishnah there, "for its needs he is exempt." The reading is not "not for his own need." Bach.
Because of, etc. See siman 291:9.
And if not, etc. Rabbi Akiva necessarily disputes Rabbi Yishmael only where he took it to steal it or to use it. In a case such as this he concedes to Rabbi Yishmael.
