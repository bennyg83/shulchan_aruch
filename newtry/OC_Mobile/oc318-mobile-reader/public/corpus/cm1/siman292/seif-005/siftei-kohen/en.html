He is deemed a robber, etc. The Tur writes that one who misappropriates a deposit is treated like any other robber and pays according to the item's condition at the time of payment. Thus, if he misappropriated an ewe while it was empty and it later became laden or pregnant in his possession, and he sheared it or it gave birth, he pays only according to its present state. Even if it was laden or pregnant when he took it and he later sheared it or it gave birth, he pays according to its present state. These words are astonishing, for they directly contradict the Gemara and the Rosh at the beginning of Perek HaGozel Kama and the end of Perek HaMafkid. The Gemara and Rosh establish that all robbers pay according to the time of the robbery, not the time of payment or claim. Therefore, one who misappropriates a deposited ewe pays according to its value at the time of misappropriation. If it was empty then and later became laden or pregnant, he pays only its value when he took it. According to Rav Pappa, as followed by the authorities and the Rosh, becoming laden or pregnant itself constitutes a legal change, even if it was not subsequently sheared and did not give birth. The Tur is therefore difficult in two respects. First, he says that payment follows the item's current condition, whereas it follows the time of misappropriation. Second, he makes the rule depend upon shearing or birth, although the Rosh holds that becoming laden or pregnant alone is a change. The same difficulty applies to Rabbeinu Yerucham, who writes that one who misappropriates a deposited animal and it later becomes laden or pregnant and is sheared or gives birth pays only according to the time of the robbery. This conflicts with his own conclusion elsewhere that no further change is required after the animal becomes laden or pregnant. The matter requires study, and it is surprising that the later commentators did not address it. As to the practical ruling, Maharshal, Perek HaGozel Kama, siman 4, holds that when an empty animal becomes pregnant or laden in the robber's possession, he acquires only the increase and not the body of the animal.
