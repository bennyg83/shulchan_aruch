Even if he does not intend, etc. 41a, "he placed his staff, etc.; he holds, etc."
(Collected note) Even if he does not intend, etc. This entire seif is the Tur's formulation, and we have not found this approach in Rashi, Tosafos, or any other posek. Every case of misappropriation in the Mishnah 43b and the Gemara there concerns intent to take from the deposit. According to the opinion that misappropriation does not require actual diminution, even if he did not yet take anything, once he lifted or pulled it with intent to take from it he is liable. Likewise there 41a, "explain it, etc.; explain it, etc.," is such a case. If he intends to steal the entire object, all agree that even if he has not yet taken from it he is liable. If he intends to take only part, according to the view that misappropriation requires diminution, he is liable only after he actually takes it; according to the view that it does not require diminution, he is liable even before he takes it. In either case, however, he intended to take the property. See Tosafos there, s.v. "shenatalah, etc." There is another approach that always requires intent to take the entire item; see Ra'avad's glosses. The Tur apparently derived his rule from the case there of the shepherd, etc., where he intends only to use it. See Rashi there, s.v. "but he did not, etc." Rashi's words are difficult: is an animal weakened through use any better? Maharam likewise objects. The Tur therefore explains that one becomes a borrower only once he actually uses the item, whereas with a living animal he becomes liable for misappropriation even before using it. The statement "he placed his staff, etc." must then be understood as Tosafos explain, s.v. "but he did not, etc.," that with that staff he struck the animal so that the staff would rest on it, and before he actually placed it a wolf came, etc. This is the meaning of "although he had not yet, etc.; but if, etc." Yet all this is strained, because "he placed" ordinarily means that it was already placed. It is also difficult why one should force such an interpretation rather than say it had already been placed and liability was as a borrower. The plain flow of the sugya, "he moved it to bring, etc.," also implies that even before actual use he is considered a borrower, analogous to "he took it, etc." Rashbam in Bava Batra 48a does explain "to measure" as meaning that he actually measured with it, but that is not the plain implication here. The repeated "explain it, etc." is also very difficult, since the Gemara seemingly needed only to explain that he placed it in another location. Rather, Rabbi Yochanan explains the Mishnah as a case where he had already stolen it and removed it into his own domain. This is why the Gemara says, "whose view is this? Rabbi Yochanan's, for it was taught: one who steals, etc." Accordingly, "he placed it" means that afterward he returned and placed it in its place. The answer "explain it that he took it, etc.; that he moved it, etc." means that he had not yet used it and then reconsidered and placed it down; one can say he placed it somewhere other than its original place. Certainly Rashi's words elsewhere prove that Rabbi Shimon's explanation "he moved it, etc.; and he holds, etc." is not based on misappropriation. Likewise there 43b. This must follow Rashi's own distinction, namely that ordinary use that does not diminish the object is borrowing, while use that diminishes it is treated as misappropriation, such as intending to take from it. In all such cases liability begins even before actual use. The phrase "he placed his staff, etc." is necessary only because otherwise the animal returned to its place, as stated there in the discussion.
(Collected note) See what is stated there. The Tur learned this from Rashi's difficulty. Ramban distinguishes whether he removed it from the owner's domain; see there. His explanation is strained. Baal HaMaor's explanation, however, is very sound: certainly he is a borrower, but any unauthorized borrower who is a bailee is termed one who misappropriates. The first explanation holds that misappropriation requires actual diminution, and without diminution there is no liability, and certainly an unauthorized borrower would not be liable. The second holds that misappropriation does not require diminution, but an unauthorized borrower still would not be liable. Rabbi Shimon holds that even a borrower who does not wish to diminish the item, and did not diminish it, is liable, and all the more so one who misappropriates intending to diminish it need not actually diminish it. This is why the Gemara says, "conclude from this, etc." Since he holds that even a borrower has the law of a thief, it follows that actual diminution is unnecessary. This accords with what was stated there, that in all cases, whether a borrower or one who misappropriates intending to diminish the item, liability begins even before actual use because all are treated under the law of misappropriation.
