The Mordechai speaks of a custodian who borrowed one object from a deposit in his possession. He borrowed only part of the deposit for his own use without permission, so his original custodianship did not entirely terminate because he continued guarding the remainder. Thus, when he later returned the borrowed portion to the place where it had been kept, it was returned to the owner's domain, for he still stood in the owner's place with respect to the rest. Even according to Rabbi Akiva, who holds that a thief's custodianship ends, that applies to theft, even of part, because the owner no longer trusts the thief to guard anything and a new delivery with the owner's knowledge is required. A borrower without permission, however, is still trusted by the owner. Although the Sages give him the legal status of a robber for taking without permission, the owner does not thereby suspect him of dishonesty, since he did not steal or appropriate the item. Hence his custodianship did not end entirely. The case of the barrel is different because he moved the entire barrel for his own purpose, so no aspect of his prior custodianship remained and he was no longer standing in the owner's place. See there at length, and see Shefer Etz Chaim and Netivot HaMishpat.
