There, Shach seif katan 4, at the end of his words, "and this is clear." Note: Do not challenge this from Rashi in Kiddushin 42b, s.v. "shall we say," where he writes, "his agent should be liable, not the principal." That statement was made before we knew the principle that "the words of the Master and the words of the student, whose words should one heed?" But according to the conclusion, even if there is agency for a transgression, the agent would also be liable because he should have heeded the words of the Master. It requires study whether, where an agent intended to misappropriate entrusted property or actually misappropriated part of it, the agent becomes liable for all of it, since he himself is not the guardian. My revered father-in-law, the gaon, asked me about a woman who told a man, "Round the hair of a minor for me." Does his hand become like the woman's hand so that he may perform the prohibited rounding? In my humble opinion, anything that he himself cannot permissibly do cannot become permitted by making him an agent. His hand does not become like hers, because he himself has no permitted way to perform the rounding. This is clear.
