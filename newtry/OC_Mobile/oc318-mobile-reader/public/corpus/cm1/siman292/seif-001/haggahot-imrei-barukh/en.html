(Mechaber, seif 1) He is like a robber. Note: Study is required where the object was worth one dinar at the time of unauthorized use and two dinarim when the unavoidable accident occurred. Is he discharged by paying one dinar, like a robber who pays according to the value at the time of robbery? At first glance this may depend on a dispute. According to Ramban and Ritva, cited in Shitah Mekubetzet to Bava Metzia 43, the reason a robber does not pay according to the time of the accident like a borrower is that immediately upon robbery the object leaves the owner's domain with respect to consecration and sale, since stolen property not yet despaired of cannot be conveyed by either party because it is not in the owner's possession. This applies to one who steals with no intent to return, but not to an unauthorized borrower, for the property never leaves the owner's domain and the owner can still consecrate or sell it. It would therefore follow that an unauthorized borrower is liable for unavoidable accidents according to the value at the time of the accident if the object appreciated after the unauthorized use. If it depreciated, he would still pay according to its value at the time of use under the law of robbery. See also Shevuot 41b, Rashi, passage beginning "it is a deposit in my hand," who writes that he is only an unpaid guardian with respect to it.
