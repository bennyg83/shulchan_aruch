If he made unauthorized use of it, etc. Scripture says, "or it died, or was broken, or was captured, with no one seeing, an oath of Hashem shall be between them, if he did not put his hand to his fellow's property, and its owner shall accept and he shall not pay." This implies that if he does not swear to this but admits that he made unauthorized use, he is liable even for death or breakage that occurred by itself, which are complete unavoidable accidents, even if he later returned the object to its place. The reason is that from the moment he made unauthorized use intending to diminish it, it entered his responsibility and he became obligated to pay for it according to its condition at that moment, like a robber. The Mechaber states this immediately below in seif 5.
It is not necessary for the purpose of Hashem’s wrath that is not yet made of Hashem’s glory
Only from the time of use, etc., until "he is a borrower without permission." The wording "without permission" is precise. An authorized borrower becomes liable for unavoidable accidents from the moment he pulls the object from the owner's possession, even before actually using it or lifting it for use. Tosafot explain that when he borrows with permission, intending to use it tomorrow or on another day, once he borrows it and pulls it from the owner's domain it is as though he has already used it. Here, however, the object was deposited with him to remain unused. Until he actually uses it, it remains under the owner's responsibility because it was not standing for use. See the Perishah.
