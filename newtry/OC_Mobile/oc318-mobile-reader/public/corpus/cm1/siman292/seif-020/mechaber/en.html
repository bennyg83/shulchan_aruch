One entrusted with a Torah scroll rolls it once every twelve months. If, while rolling it, he opens and reads, it is permitted, but he may not open it solely for his own reading. The same applies to other books. If he opens, reads, or rolls for his own benefit, he has misappropriated the deposit and becomes liable for unavoidable accidents. Rama: Just as reading is prohibited, copying even one letter is prohibited. This applies to an unlearned person. A Torah scholar who lacks such a book may read and copy from it, because the depositor presumably entrusted it on that understanding. Where Torah study would otherwise cease because books are unavailable, beit din may compel an owner to lend his books for study, provided borrowers pay for resulting wear.
