A month. This means for the scroll's benefit, so that it not become moldy. If he rolls it within thirty days, it is not for its benefit but for his own benefit, to study Torah. This is the Sema's language. The Shach writes that the Sema merely followed the Tur's wording, for according to the Mechaber he should have said: if he rolls it within twelve months. He further writes that although regarding a lost object, in siman 267, seif 20, the Mechaber rules thirty days, a depositor is different, since he knowingly entrusted it and did not state that it must be rolled every thirty days, so once in twelve months suffices. The Tur, however, holds that here too it must be rolled every thirty days. The Mechaber follows his view in the Beit Yosef that the Rif and Rosh agree with the Rambam, but in truth their words do not prove this, as the Bach writes. Rashi likewise holds that a deposited scroll is like a lost object, and the Mishnah follows Rabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov. This appears primary, because we rule according to the anonymous Mishnah and according to Rabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov, whose teaching is precise and reliable. So rules the Bach, and this also appears to be the view of the Ma'adanei Melech.
Permitted. See siman 267 for the distinctions concerning when reading it is permitted, including that one may not read from it material he has never studied before, etc. It appears that the same applies here. The Mechaber did not enter into those details, but came only to teach when the custodian is deemed to have made unauthorized use so that the item stands in his domain regarding unavoidable losses. Sema.
With him. This is comparable to depositing money with a moneychanger or storekeeper in seif 7. It follows that if the depositor sealed and tied the book with an unusual knot, thereby showing that he objects to its being read, then he certainly does not consent, and reading it constitutes unauthorized use, all the more so copying from it. The Mordechai, however, writes at the end of chapter HaMafkid, cited by the Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe, regarding the rule that a Torah scholar may copy from it and study from it: perhaps the reason is the Midrash on Mishlei, "people do not despise a thief," meaning one should not despise a person who steals words of Torah by copying them. According to this rationale it would appear permissible even where we know the owner objects. Yet the Rama omitted this and instead wrote, "certainly on this understanding," which proves that if we know he objects, it is forbidden. The Mordechai itself wrote only "perhaps." The Ir Shushan mixed the rationales and contradicted himself. This requires study. This is the Sema's language. See the Shach, who agrees with the Ir Shushan and holds that even where the owner objects to the copying, it is nevertheless permitted because of "people do not despise a thief." He writes that this appears primary and discusses it at length.
