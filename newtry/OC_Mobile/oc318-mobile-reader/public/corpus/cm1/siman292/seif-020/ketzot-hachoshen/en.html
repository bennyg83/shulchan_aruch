If, while rolling the scroll, he opened it and read from it, it is permitted. Sema writes that if the custodian rolls the scroll within thirty days, he may not read from it. Shach objects that under the Shulchan Aruch the custodian need roll a deposited scroll only once every twelve months. Sema is correct. The physical need of a scroll does not change merely because it was deposited knowingly. The law of a found scroll requires rolling once every thirty days because that is beneficial maintenance for the scroll. A depositor who did not instruct the custodian to do so is treated as waiving the burden of that more frequent maintenance, not as changing what counts as maintenance. Therefore, if the custodian actually rolls it at thirty days for the scroll's benefit, he may read while rolling, because the action is a legitimate act of preservation. If he rolls it sooner than thirty days, the act is not needed for maintenance, as shown by the law of a found scroll, and reading then is unauthorized use. This remains true even though the depositor's ordinary required schedule is only once in twelve months.
