This teaches that although the verse says, "for every matter of trespass," one should not think that he becomes liable merely through speech, for the verse also says, "if he did not put forth his hand," requiring actual unauthorized use. The Gemara derives from "for every matter of trespass" that if he instructed an agent to make unauthorized use of a deposit and the agent did so, the principal is liable, as the Tur and Shulchan Aruch write in the following seif. The Sema writes that this would appear to apply only where the agent did not know that the property belonged to another, or was not legally accountable, or had no means to pay, as stated at the beginning of siman (172) [182]; otherwise, the rule is that there is no agency for a transgression. The Shach writes that all the later authorities challenged the Sema on this point. The Tosafot Yom Tov at the end of HaMafkid and the Bach also objected that he had overlooked explicit Gemarot at the beginning of HaIsh Mekadesh and in the first chapter of Bava Metzia. The Shach's father, however, defended the Sema's words with sound reasoning and wrote that the critics had not grasped his full intent. See there at length.
