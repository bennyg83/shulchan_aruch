Who wishes to misappropriate the deposit. See Sema, seif katan 10. Here my master and father showed the greatness of his sharpness, for all the later authorities attacked the Sema without reaching his intent. The Tosafot Yom Tov and the Bach argued that the Sema overlooked the explicit Gemara at the beginning of Perek HaIsh Mekadesh, which derives the general rule that there is no agency for a transgression precisely from misappropriation, slaughter and sale, or misuse of sacred property, because these are two verses establishing agency for a transgression and therefore do not teach other cases. They further argued from Bava Metzia 10a that one who tells a woman or slave to steal remains exempt, even though the agent presently cannot pay, because each is legally liable and may later acquire assets. Thus, inability to pay does not make the sender liable. Yet my master and father explained the Sema correctly. The Sema certainly knew that misappropriation is exceptional, for he himself derives the sender's liability from the verse, 'for every matter of trespass.' His question is rather why the verse is needed when the agent is himself able and obligated to pay. Ordinarily, the agent cannot excuse himself by saying that the sender instructed him, because there is no agency for a transgression. The verse must therefore address circumstances in which, without it, the depositor would suffer a loss. There are disputes among the authorities whether agency for a transgression exists when the agent did not know that the act was forbidden, and whether it exists when the agent is not personally subject to the relevant liability. In addition, when the agent lacks funds, all agree that ordinarily the sender remains exempt. The Sema therefore means that the verse imposes liability upon the sender in one of these exceptional settings, where the agent was unaware, was not personally liable, or cannot pay. But where the agent knew, was legally liable, and has funds, the agent certainly pays under the rule that there is no agency for a transgression, and the sender is exempt. The verse is an inclusion imposing liability upon the sender; it does not exempt the agent. If the agent can fully compensate the depositor, there is no reason to impose an additional payment upon the sender. Although this may not be the most obvious reading of the Sema's words, my master's explanation is sound and vindicates the Sema as a matter of law. This is especially so according to what I concluded above, at the beginning of siman 182, that even where the agent is not personally subject to the liability there is no agency for a transgression, and below, at the end of siman 348, that the same applies even where the agent was unaware. Accordingly, the special verse here applies precisely where the agent was unaware, was not personally liable, or lacks funds. Where none of these circumstances exists and the agent pays, the sender is plainly exempt. This is clear.
