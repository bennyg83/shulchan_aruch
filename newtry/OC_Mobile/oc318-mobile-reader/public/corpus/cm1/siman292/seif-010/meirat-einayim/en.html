He should not mix them, etc. Then, even if they diminish greatly, he can say, "Here is yours before you," on the basis of the principle of present tender.
And he calculates the normal diminution of the deposit, etc. See the Perishah, where I explained that one calculates the normal rate of diminution for all the measures. If the actual diminution is no greater than what ordinarily occurs, as explained afterward, he is exempt without an oath. If it diminished beyond the normal amount, he must swear that the excess loss occurred without his negligence, and he gives the depositor his fruit in proportion to the respective measures belonging to the depositor and to himself.
At his responsibility, etc. Meaning, at the depositor's responsibility. Terumat HaDeshen, siman 314, says this explicitly, and it is also evident from the context.
The loss according to the proportion, etc. This teaches us not to think that it resembles one who deposited a lamb with another and the bailee placed it among his own lambs, where the bailee can say, "Yours died," or one who deposited rings, as Rama writes afterward. There, each person specifically wants his own property, and from the outset each individual item was clearly identified as belonging to its owner. Since the bailee is in possession, he can therefore say to the depositor, "Yours died" or "Yours was stolen," and the burden of proof rests on the claimant. Fruit and coins are different. They share one generic identity, and had none been stolen the bailee would have returned the depositor's amount by measure or number without any concern about which particular units were his. It is therefore as though everything constituted one mass, and the deficiency is borne proportionately by all. Terumat HaDeshen, siman 314, discusses these distinctions at length.
If two people deposited, etc. This refers specifically to the concluding case, where they deposited rings. If they deposited money, the loss is likewise divided proportionately among all of them. See Terumat HaDeshen.
Whatever separates is presumed to have separated from the majority, etc. Meaning, if an item separates on its own and is then lost, we follow the majority. If a person takes from the rings, however, we apply the rule that anything fixed is treated as half and half, as explained in Orach Chayyim simanim 439 and 328. In this respect there is no distinction between a case in which each item was individually recognizable and one of mixture, except in the laws of prohibited and permitted mixtures, because of the concern that one might take directly from the fixed location, as explained in Yoreh Deah simanim 110 and 112.
