Even beforehand. See Baer Heitev, who concludes that he may instead give other money. Shach already writes this in siman 47, seif katan 9, siman 73, seif katan 25, and siman 75, seif katan 15. Chacham Tzvi, however, in his glosses to Taz, siman 73, seif 19, strongly disputes Shach and writes that Tosafos proves the opposite. According to our ruling that the bailee is a paid custodian, if the depositor demands the money before the bailee has spent it, he must return those very coins and may not substitute other money. Baer Heitev cites this in siman 73, and Tumim there agrees in practice. Rabbi Ozer likewise challenges Shach in his gloss printed in Baer Heitev above, siman 47. Responsa Beit Ephraim, Choshen Mishpat, siman 49, also rules against Shach. He indicates, however, that this applies where the owners are present and insist upon receiving those particular coins. If they are absent, the holder needs the money, and he does not wish to take it as a loan but instead credits the owners with equivalent money in its place, that is permitted under the principle that one benefits and the other suffers no loss, since the owners have no particular interest in those coins. His words imply that this is so even where the holder has no general permission to use the deposit, such as one who is neither a money changer nor a merchant. In my humble opinion this requires study. See Nachalat Tzvi.
