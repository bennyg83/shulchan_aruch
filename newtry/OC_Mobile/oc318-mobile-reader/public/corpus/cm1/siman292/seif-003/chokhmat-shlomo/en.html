Seif 8. One who deposits money or utensils with another, etc. Note: See Shach, seif katan 19, regarding a custodian who handed the deposit to robbers. In my view there is a scribal error in his words. They should read: 'Even if the robbers would have found it in any event, he is liable, and this is evident from Maggid Mishneh, who concedes this point to Rashba.' The meaning is as follows. Rashba explains the cited gloss as a case where the robbers could have taken the item themselves, and nevertheless the custodian is exempt only where he was not known to possess such property. This implies that where he was known to possess it, the first clause, he is liable even though they could have taken it themselves. Maggid Mishneh disagrees only by saying that where he was not known to possess it he is exempt even if the robbers could not otherwise have taken it. But on the converse rule, that where he was known to possess it he is liable even if they could have taken it themselves, Maggid Mishneh appears to agree. This is, in my view, the Shach's intent.
