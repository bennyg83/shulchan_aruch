To use. Since the depositor knows that a storekeeper or moneychanger needs money, and he did not seal it or tie it with an unusual knot, he certainly does not object to their using his money. Sema.
Before. Tosafot, Bava Metzia 29a, indicates that even if the depositor demands the money before the custodian has spent it, the custodian need not return those very coins but may give him other money. See there, and what I wrote at the beginning of siman 39, seif 17. Shach.
For unavoidable losses. Regarding proceeds of a lost object, where the finder is likewise permitted to use the money and is liable for unavoidable losses even before using it, as the Tur and Mechaber write in siman 267, seif 25, the Raavad explains that here the custodian is in a weaker position because he is afraid to buy merchandise with deposited money lest the depositor suddenly arrive and demand his deposit. By contrast, with proceeds of a lost object he is confident the money will remain with him for a long time. For the same reason this is unlike an authorized borrower, who becomes liable for unavoidable losses before using the item, because there he has explicit permission to use it and is not concerned that the owner will suddenly demand it. This is the Sema's language. See the Shach's explanation of the practical difference whether the custodian is considered a borrower of the money or a robber.
To their owners. I already wrote above in seif 1, in the gloss, that one who borrows a deposited article without permission is liable for unavoidable losses even after returning it to its place. Nevertheless, the Rosh had to distinguish the present case, because here the unauthorized use of the money was with permission, since he is permitted to use it. See the Rosh, where this is explicit. Shach.
With. See the Shach, who cites the Beit Yosef's question on Rabbeinu Yerucham, who wrote that he does not become a paid custodian by stipulation, etc., and resolves his words at length.
He profited. See Responsa Rashdam, siman 155; Responsa Ibn Lev, part I, siman 51 and part II, siman 26; Responsa Maharam, siman 3; Responsa Rabbi Yosef of the House of Levi, siman 27; and Responsa Rashakh, part II, siman 111.
To give. If he wishes to give him a share of the profit, he may do so, and there is no prohibition of interest, since the money did not come into his possession as a loan. So writes the Mordechai there. See the end of siman 81 and what I wrote there. Sema. See Responsa Shevut Yaakov, part I, siman 64.
To give. Maharshal, chapter 9 of Bava Kamma, siman 30, rules that even if the depositor sued him in beit din and said, "Give me my deposit, for I have a definite profitable opportunity," and proves his words, but the custodian withholds it, the custodian is exempt, because withholding another's capital from profit is only indirect damage. See further there regarding this law. See Nimukei Yosef, chapter HaMekabel, in the discussion of ordinary contractual language, citing the Yerushalmi that one who prevents another's capital from earning profit is exempt. See also the Yerushalmi and Sefer Imrei Emet, p. 103, col. 4. Shach.
The custodian. Since he spent the money with permission, he may say: If you want a share of the profit, share with me in the loss as well. Sema.
He disclosed. See Responsa Ibn Lev, part III, siman 112.
