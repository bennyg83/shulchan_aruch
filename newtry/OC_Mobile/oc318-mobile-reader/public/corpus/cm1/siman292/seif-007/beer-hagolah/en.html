Ayin) This is the language of the Tur, seif 11. Rambam likewise rules there, halachah 6, from the Mishnah at the beginning of Bava Metzia 43a.
Peh) Following Rabbi Yehudah there in the Mishnah.
Tzadi) According to Rav Ashi's explanation of Rabbi Yehudah there.
Kuf) According to the latter version in the name of Rav Mari. The question was unresolved, and the Rif and Rosh therefore write that he should not use the money where the manner in which it was tied is unusual.
Reish) Following Rav Nachman there, because the halachah follows him in monetary law. The poskim likewise rule so.
Shin) The Rif likewise writes that because the money has the status of a loan in the custodian's possession, he is liable accordingly. Rav Hai Gaon likewise rules so.
Tav) The Tur cites the Rosh's explanation of the Rif: since the custodian has already benefited from the money and continues to anticipate further benefit if an opportunity for merchandise arises, he bears the corresponding liability.
Alef) This is explained by what was written above.
Bet) There in the Mishnah.
Gimel) Rambam likewise writes this there, halachah 7.
Dalet) The Tur there in the name of Rabbenu Tam. The Mordekhai writes that nowadays, when ordinary livelihood is not based on fields and vineyards but on interest-bearing investments and commerce, everyone has the status of a moneychanger for this law. Maharik, shoresh 151, writes that there is no distinction between a place where merchants are commonly present and one where they are not, nor between a wealthy person who does not need this money and a poor person. The Sema cites this from the Darkei Moshe.
Heh) According to the Rama's concluding gloss in the name of the Rashba, or where use of the money is forbidden, one must explain that the case is where the custodian revealed before witnesses that he was taking the money for his own profit. But the Mordekhai in HaGozel and at the end of HaMafkid states explicitly that even without such a declaration before witnesses, the profit belongs to the custodian. The Rashba in a responsum likewise required an express declaration before witnesses only in a case where the custodian's conduct showed that he was investing for the depositor, since he had previously given the depositor all the profits. Thus further profits are presumed to have been earned on the same basis unless he declares before witnesses that he is now investing for himself. This point requires study, as the Sema also questions it.
Vav) The Mordekhai writes that where the custodian lent the money to a gentile on trust and thereby acted negligently, etc., when the depositor later demands his money, if the custodian first pays the principal he acquires the right to the interest. But a mere statement, "I will pay," does not transfer that right, just as with a borrower who receives all benefit. We rule like Rav Zevid in HaMafkid, Bava Metzia 34a, that a borrower does not acquire the double payment merely by declaring that he will pay, because all benefit is his. The interest is an incidental gain comparable to the double payment. The Sema cites this rule.
