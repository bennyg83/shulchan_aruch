And not tied, etc. See Beer HaGolah, and all the more so according to the first version.
And if he already, etc. Rav Nachman necessarily disputes only where he had not yet used the money. Rabbi Akiva likewise disputes Rabbi Tarfon only where it had not yet been used. Once it was used, everyone agrees that he becomes a borrower, even according to Rabbah. See the sugya 29a. This is stated explicitly here where Rav Nachman was challenged, etc., proving that once he spent it he concedes. See Beer HaGolah. The Yerushalmi in Demai likewise says in the name of Rabbi Yose: this teaches that one who gives money to his fellow and the latter exchanges it, and it is then lost, is liable for it. Hagahos Maimoniyos there.
Even after, etc. The Rosh writes that otherwise this law would not need to be stated. The novelty is specifically that even though returning it does not require the owner's knowledge according to Rabbi Akiva where possession was initially authorized, he nevertheless remains liable. See Beer HaGolah and the Rosh there, who asks why the Rif here rules like Rav Nachman but in chapter 2 rules like Rav Yosef regarding money from a lost object. His first answer is that there, without the permission to use it, he would already be a paid bailee. Maggid Mishneh writes likewise. See siman 19 in a similar case. We say below 81a that even after the borrowing period he remains liable as a paid bailee because, etc. Therefore, through the benefit that if an opportunity arises, etc., he becomes a borrower, as above.

And if he profited, etc. The Sages said only that he becomes responsible for the money. This is a case where one benefits and the other does not lose. Although the Tosefta chapter 5, cited by the Rosh there, teaches concerning one who gives money, etc., "and if there are witnesses, etc.," as stated above siman 183:1, there the money was given specifically for investment, as stated in the Yerushalmi there concerning one who idles another's money, etc. See Tosafos 29a, s.v. "vehavi, etc." Certainly where he had no permission to use the money he is a thief and pays according to its value at the time of theft, as stated 43a to 43b. See Tosafos 28b, s.v. "al korchach, etc.," and Bava Kamma 96b, "at the end of theft, etc."
Nevertheless, if, etc. This too is a case of idling another's money. Mordechai there.
But if, etc. Meaning, the depositor does not wish to share any loss and says, "give me either my money or only the profit." This is unlike Bava Kamma 101b, "if they decreased, etc., in the middle," because there the agent deviated from instructions, whereas here he did not. This appears to reconcile the Rama's words, and the Sema likewise explains so. Mordechai there, however, does not contain this ruling. On the contrary, it implies that if the depositor wants his money, the depositary must return it, though Mordechai writes that in such a case the profit belongs to the depositary. He then cites Rav Hai Gaon that where the depositary refuses and delays him, he must share the profit. Darkei Moshe likewise cites this. It appears that the text should read "the law is with the depositor," meaning that his money must be returned, because it came to the depositary as a deposit. See the Rosh in the sugya here, who cites Ra'avad's answer that the benefit of use, etc., is limited because perhaps the depositor will come, etc. This is the meaning of "and if he invested, etc.," corresponding to the third opinion there. Even though the depositor does not share the loss, it is like one who gives money to an agent because he is idling the depositor's funds when the depositor wants his money. This is not prohibited interest through a one-sided profit arrangement, because the money was not given as a loan or sale and no such condition was made. See Mordechai there. The phrase about "for the owner's needs" is a separate matter from a responsum of the Rashba and refers back to "if he profited, etc.": if he invested for the depositor's benefit, the profit belongs to the depositor, as stated in Bava Kamma 102b, "and if they increased, etc.," even if he did not act as the depositor's agent. See Rashi there, s.v. "who informed him, etc." We do not rule like the scholars of the West. What was written above, "the law is with the depositary," does not mean he may retain the principal, but only that he need not give the profit, according to the first view in Mordechai. This is also implied by Darkei Moshe, who rules like the first opinion.
(Collected note) The law is with the depositary. The text should read "with the depositor," because certainly his money must be returned. One could read it as saying that the depositor is entitled to the profit according to Rav Hai Gaon in chapter HaGozel, siman 124, but the wording does not imply this, since the depositor does not want profit. Rather the text must read "with the depositor." Yet even this does not fit well, because Mordechai did not mention such a rule and the present discussion is not about that issue. The main reading should therefore be "the profit belongs to the depositary." The following words, "and if he invested, etc.," refer to the entire preceding discussion, even the profit, as stated in the Rashba's responsum there.
