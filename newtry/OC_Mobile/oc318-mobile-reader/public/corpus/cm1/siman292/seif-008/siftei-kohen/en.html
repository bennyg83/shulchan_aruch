One who deposits, etc., the presumption is that they did not come, etc. The Maggid Mishna writes that the Rashba limits the exemption to a case in which the thieves already had access to the property and would have found it upon searching. Otherwise the custodian is certainly liable, for he is no better than one whom coercers compelled to bring another's money and who did so, who is liable so long as they did not already control it, as explained below, siman 388, seif 4. The Baal HaMaor likewise rules at the end of Perek HaGozel Batra. The Maggid Mishna suggests that the Rambam distinguishes this case because the money was already in the custodian's domain. Tosafot at the end of Perek HaGozel Batra and the Rosh explain that a custodian accepted the deposit with this contingency in mind. The Mordechai there cites a responsum of Maharam to the same effect, and Maharshal so rules. Ramban likewise writes in Milchamot that there is a distinction between a custodian and another person. One who is coerced to bring another's property has suffered a heavenly calamity unrelated to that property and may not save himself with it; once he hands it over, he is treated as a robber and liable even for accidents. A custodian, however, stands in the owner's place. His hand and domain are treated as the owner's, and it is as though the owner himself were present when the thieves arrived. The owner would certainly have surrendered the property rather than be killed, for the deposit was entrusted on that understanding. The Yerushalmi and the case of the silver cup in our Gemara follow this principle. The Nimukei Yosef reaches the same conclusion in the name of the Raah. In siman 388, seif 4, however, I conclude that one who is compelled to bring another's money is exempt; according to that conclusion, this case is certainly resolved.

They came only because of the report, etc. The Sema writes that this rule applies specifically to a deposit, not to one coerced to bring another's money, because the custodian's possession of the deposit caused him to acquire a reputation for wealth and brought the coercer upon him. The Beit Yosef's wording about acquiring a reputation for wealth, however, refers to the later case and not to this one. The true explanation here is that he accepted custody with this contingency in mind, in accordance with the Ran. The Sema appears to have written otherwise inadvertently.
