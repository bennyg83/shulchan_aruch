In the Shulchan Aruch, in the gloss. "Even though he has no migo." At first glance, this statement seems unnecessary, because where he has no migo he must certainly relinquish the item, as the Sema writes. It appears to me that the Rama can be explained as interpreting the Tur and Shulchan Aruch as speaking where the debt itself is not known, since they do not mention that it is known. Because he seized the item during the debtor's lifetime, when he had a migo that it was lost through unavoidable circumstances, since it was not then seen in his possession, that seizure remains effective even now when he has no migo at all. He may therefore say, "I seized it during his lifetime as collateral." According to Rashba, however, that earlier seizure is ineffective once he presently has no migo, and therefore the debt itself must be known. Rashba could not otherwise establish a case in which he is believed to say, "I seized it as collateral," except where the debt is known.
