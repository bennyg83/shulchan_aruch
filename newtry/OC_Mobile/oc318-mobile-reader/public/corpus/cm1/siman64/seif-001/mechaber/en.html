A Custodian Who Wishes to Retain Deposited Documents as Security for His Debt. One seif. If documents were deposited with a person and the depositor died, and the custodian claims, 'During his lifetime I seized them as collateral for a debt he owed me,' for he could not collect them without a written transfer, then if witnesses testify that the depositor demanded them during his lifetime and the custodian refused, this is valid seizure; otherwise it is not. This assumes witnesses establish both the original deposit and that the documents are presently in his possession. Without such testimony he would be believed through the defense that they were lost through unavoidable accident or were never deposited. This is the law of the Gemara. Under the later enactment permitting collection from orphans' movable property, he may collect from their documents even without lifetime seizure. {Rama: Even though he has no alternative defense and cannot claim he purchased them, if he seized them during the depositor's lifetime he may say, 'I seized them as collateral,' contrary to the view requiring independent proof that the depositor owed him.}
