He must rejoice with her for seven days. In siman 61, seif 7, the law of the blessing was explained: it applies throughout all seven days of the wedding festivities, whether a bachelor marries a widow or a widower marries a virgin. The blessing was instituted for the joy of the man's heart, and a bachelor who has never married has greater joy even if he marries a widow. All the more so, a widower who marries a virgin has greater joy. Therefore there are seven days for the blessing. But the rejoicing of eating and drinking, cessation from work, and business activity is all an enactment for her benefit, because the Sages were diligent concerning the welfare of Jewish women. For a virgin they instituted seven days of rejoicing, and for a woman who has previously been married three days, because a virgin requires greater appeasement and rejoicing than a widow. Therefore, even a bachelor who marries a widow needs only three days of rejoicing with her, although he recites the groom's blessing for all seven days.
