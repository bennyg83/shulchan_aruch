So write the Rif and Rosh there. The Rivash discusses at length in siman 392 and proves that nowadays seizure after death is effective. Hagahot Mordechai in chapter 1 of Bava Kamma likewise writes so, and this also appears from the Rambam, who after writing the cited law in chapter 11, law 10, writes the enactment of the Geonim at the end of that chapter.
This refers back to the case where there are witnesses to the deposit and witnesses who now saw the documents in his possession. Although the debt itself is not known, he is believed because while the depositor was alive he claimed that he did not wish to return the documents and was holding them for his debt, and he had a migo that he could have said, 'I returned them,' or 'They were lost through accident,' even though he could not say, 'I purchased them,' as stated nearby. The Rashba, however, writes in a responsum that the incident involved a known debt. Otherwise he would not be believed, neither by a migo of accidental loss, because that is a weak migo and would prevent him from collecting with the documents, nor by a migo of purchase, because letters are not acquired by delivery alone and proof of written transfer is required. This appears to me to be the Rama's intent. The Sema and Bach object that Tosafot and the Rosh necessarily speak only where the debt is known; otherwise he is not believed once witnesses saw the documents before litigation, as explained in simanim 72 and 133.
