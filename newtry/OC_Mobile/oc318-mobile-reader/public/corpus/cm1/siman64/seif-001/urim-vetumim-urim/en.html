I seized them during his lifetime, etc. This follows the talmudic law that movable property of orphans is not subject to a creditor's lien. Therefore he must specifically claim that during the borrower's lifetime he declared that he was seizing the documents as collateral. Since the documents had originally been entrusted to him as a deposit, mere possession is not considered seizure unless he expressly states during the owner's lifetime that he now holds them for himself.
As collateral. Meaning that the document owner will not be able to collect his debt until he pays what he owes the custodian, who holds the physical paper as collateral. By holding the paper itself, he can compel the lender to pay him.
For if he seized them in order to collect, etc. Even if during the depositor's lifetime he expressly stated before witnesses, "I seize these documents toward my debt in order to collect them," that is ineffective and does not constitute legal seizure, because acquisition of a debt document requires writing and delivery. See Tumim for what I wrote.
The case is where there are witnesses, etc. See Tumim. The Shulchan Aruch follows its own view, like Rambam below, that as against the lender himself a purchaser of a debt document need not prove the acquisition with witnesses, and therefore the custodian can rely on a migo. According to Rama below, however, who requires proof even against the lender, most authorities hold that the documents remain presumptively the lender's or depositor's property and no migo helps, even if there are no witnesses at all. See Tumim at length.
Through a migo that he could say they were lost through unavoidable circumstances. Shach holds that no oath is required because the subject is documents and the depositor's claim is uncertain. Sema requires an oath. See what I wrote above in simanim 66 and 133, and see Tumim.
Even though he has no migo, etc. There are witnesses both to the original deposit and to current possession, so he cannot make any alternative claim. Nevertheless, he is believed to say, "I seized them as collateral."
Only if it is known that the other owed him. Rama's wording implies that even if the debt is not known he may still be believed. Sema and all the later authorities already objected. See Tumim. I explained that Rama means a case with witnesses to current possession but not witnesses to the original deposit. Where there are also witnesses to the deposit, Rama too agrees that he is not believed without proof of the debt. As practical law, the later authorities are correct. See Tumim.
