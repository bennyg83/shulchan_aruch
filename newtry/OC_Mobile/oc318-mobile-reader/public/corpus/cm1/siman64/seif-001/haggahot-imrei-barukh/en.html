(Netivot HaMishpat, seif katan 2) At the end of the passage: since he subjected movable property to him. Note: I am astonished, for the movable property collected by the heirs is not regarded as though it had been in their father's possession. There is no indication that their father had a lien on the debtor's movable property together with land, and it is therefore as though the orphans purchased that movable property. This is clear to one who studies chapter Kol Sha'ah, Pesachim 31a, and Tosafos there, s.v. 'meshabed,' as well as Shach, siman 85, seif katan 23. See the explanation of this author there.
