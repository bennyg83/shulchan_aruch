In the Tur: Rabbi Yehuda Albargeloni wrote, etc. One may wonder why he remained uncertain, since everywhere the Jerusalem Talmud is set aside in favor of the Babylonian Talmud. There is a further difficulty with the first opinion, which disqualifies a judge until age eighteen and proves this from King Yoshiyahu. Shmuel states at the end of the chapter Bameh Behemah that Yoshiyahu is praised by the verse, “There was no king like him who returned to Hashem,” because he repaid every judgment he had rendered before the age of eighteen. If the law requires every person to do this, what special praise belongs to Yoshiyahu? The expression “an additional stringency” also requires explanation, for it should simply have said “a stringency.” It therefore appears that the first opinion agrees that Yoshiyahu's conduct was praiseworthy beyond that of ordinary people. Its view is that his principal praise lay in returning money after he had already rendered judgment. Initially, however, every person is forbidden to judge before eighteen. The Jerusalem Talmud, by contrast, implies that any ordinary person may initially judge immediately after age thirteen, and that Yoshiyahu adopted an additional stringency. He accepted two stringencies: others may judge initially, whereas he even reversed judgments already rendered. Rabbi Yehuda Albargeloni therefore writes that it requires study whether the halacha follows Shmuel. That is, does Shmuel's age of eighteen establish the rule for everyone, with Yoshiyahu's added stringency consisting only in repayment after the fact, or does the halacha follow Rabbi Abahu that even initially it is permitted, while Yoshiyahu accepted both stringencies? We must still explain the Tur's first opinion, which requires both eighteen years and two pubic hairs. What is the source for the requirement of hairs? It appears that there is uncertainty regarding why Yoshiyahu reversed his judgments until eighteen. Perhaps age alone is determinative even if he had not yet developed two hairs, or perhaps he developed two hairs only then and physical maturity is determinative. We therefore rule stringently that both eighteen years and two hairs are required. Rabbi Yehuda Albargeloni, however, insofar as he follows Shmuel, holds that hairs are irrelevant and that eighteen years alone is required, since Shmuel did not mention hairs. This is clear.
