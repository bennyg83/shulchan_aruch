A woman is disqualified from judging. Tosafot in Niddah 50b discuss at length whether a woman may judge. At the end they cite the Yerushalmi: "The Yerushalmi disqualifies a woman from judging. Some derive it from 'the two men shall stand', 'two' by a gezerah shavah from 'by the mouth of two witnesses'; and some derive it from 'two men remained in the camp.'" Maharsha understood Tosafot as following the Yerushalmi in Yoma, where "the two men shall stand" is linked by gezerah shavah to "by the mouth of two witnesses," and another view derives "two witnesses" from "two men remained in the camp," where men and not women or minors are intended. He therefore emended Tosafot's text accordingly. But in that Yerushalmi the conclusion is "from here a woman does not testify, and a woman does not judge." Maharsha therefore asks why Tosafot needed the Yerushalmi at all regarding testimony, when our Bavli at the beginning of Shevuot HaEdut already excludes women from testimony through the same gezerah shavah of "two." He leaves this difficult. The truth is that Tosafot follow the Yerushalmi in Shevuot HaEdut, whose wording matches Tosafot exactly and requires no emendation. There the Yerushalmi asks why Scripture says "two men." It answers that "two" is available for a gezerah shavah: "two" is written here and "two men remained in the camp" is written there. Just as there the term means men and not women or minors, so too here. It then says explicitly, "We have learned that a woman does not judge; consequently a woman does not testify." R. Yosi gives another derivation from "by the mouth of two witnesses." The text is therefore correct. The passage must be understood carefully. Why is a gezerah shavah needed merely to learn "men and not women or minors"? The verse already says "two men." That exclusion is self-evident. The real uncertainty is whether "the two men shall stand" refers to the witnesses or to the litigants, because one might say the litigants stand in fulfillment of the principle that the honor of a king's daughter is inward, as discussed explicitly at the beginning of Shevuot HaEdut. What does the gezerah shavah from "two men remained in the camp" add? The answer is that this opinion already assumes that "the two men" are the witnesses, like one of the Bavli's interpretations. The gezerah shavah is not needed primarily to exclude women from being witnesses. It extends the male requirement to the judges before whom those witnesses stand. In "two men remained in the camp," the selected men were among those designated for the judicial leadership of Israel and for the Great Sanhedrin. There, the chosen judges were men and not women or minors. The gezerah shavah therefore teaches that here too, the judges before whom the two witnesses stand must be men, not women or minors. That is why the Yerushalmi first says, "We have learned that a woman does not judge." Only afterward does it say, "Consequently a woman does not testify," because the plain verse itself excludes women from the witness-role. This is the principal content of the gezerah shavah: just as the men in the camp were judges, so the tribunal before whom "the two men" stand must consist of men. This is exactly why Tosafot cite this Yerushalmi. Their entire discussion concerns the validity of a woman as judge, not her well-known disqualification from testimony. The Bavli in Shevuot discussing female witnesses therefore would not have supplied what Tosafot needed. Ir Shushan says almost precisely this. Sema calls his explanation unusual, but Ir Shushan was evidently following Tosafot and the Yerushalmi's straightforward meaning.
