Some say from age thirteen. Responsa Mishkenot Yaakov, siman 5, brings proof for the first opinion and concludes that, at minimum, one should not appoint a permanent judge who is younger than twenty. Responsa Shevut Yaakov, part I, siman 140, concludes that for judging alone it is proper to be stringent until age forty, unless there is no one greater than him in the city. To join a panel of three, however, he is certainly no worse than one of three lay judges. I cited this also in Pitchei Teshuva to Yoreh Deah, siman 242, seif katan 16. See Urim VeTumim on this matter.
