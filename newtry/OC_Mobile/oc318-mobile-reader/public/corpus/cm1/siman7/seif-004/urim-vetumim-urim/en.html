A woman is disqualified from judging. This is derived from the law of testimony, not from the unusual reason suggested by Levush. Sema. One cannot ask why the same derivation was not used above for a person blind in both eyes, since blindness disqualifies a witness. Blindness is disqualifying in testimony because the witness cannot see the event. That reason does not necessarily apply to judging, where mental clarity is what matters. A woman, however, is excluded from testimony by scriptural decree independent of such functional limitation. Therefore the analogy from testimony to judgment is proper. In Tumim I show support from Tosafot for Levush's broader approach. Sema's criticism that Levush's reason is foreign is therefore misplaced.
