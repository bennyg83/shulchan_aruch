He is disqualified from judging, etc. Meaning in monetary cases. This is derived from the doubled wording, 'you shall surely appoint a king over yourself from among your brothers,' from which Chazal derive that every appointment over Israel must be from among your brothers, excluding a convert and even the descendants of converts from such appointments. Rambam writes likewise in chapter 1 of Hilchot Melakhim. This applies specifically to an appointment 'over you,' but the verse does not exclude one convert judging another convert.
Unless his mother was Jewish. Then he too is included in 'from among your brothers.'
Or his father, etc. If his father was Jewish the case is all the more valid. Tosafot likewise indicates that a Jewish father is stronger. See the Perishah.
A convert may judge his fellow convert. Darkei Moshe writes that Mordechai in chapter Mitzvat Chalitzah implies that this is only without coercion. I did not find that implication there. The Rosh, and likewise the ruling in Yoreh Deah at the end of siman 269, states explicitly that a convert may judge another convert even by coercion. Without coercion, he may even judge a born Jew if the parties accepted him. See the Perishah.
