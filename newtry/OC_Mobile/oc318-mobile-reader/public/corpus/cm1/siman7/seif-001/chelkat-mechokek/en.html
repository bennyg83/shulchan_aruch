A relative is believed for this testimony. The Gemara in Ketubot 27a teaches that in a city conquered by siege, every kohenet found there is disqualified unless there are witnesses, and for this purpose even a slave or maidservant is believed, though a person is not believed concerning himself. The Gemara discusses whether her own maidservant is believed, and compares this with the law of seclusion after a conditional get, where even a slave or maidservant may serve as witness except for her own maidservant, whose familiarity with her makes the testimony suspect. Rav Pappi answers that the Sages were lenient regarding a captive woman. Rav Pappa distinguishes between her maidservant and his maidservant, and the Gemara then analyzes whether her maidservant is treated like herself. Rav Ashi gives another resolution, and the Gemara cites a dispute of baraitot: one teaches that everyone is qualified for testimony concerning a captive woman, including father, mother, brother, and sister, but not son, daughter, slave, or maidservant; another teaches that everyone is believed except the woman herself and her husband. According to the Rambam, whose language the Mechaber copied here, a relative is believed without qualification. Since he did not exclude son and daughter, they are included. The Rif likewise appears to hold this way, since he omitted any disqualification of son and daughter and mentioned only that her maidservant is not believed. It appears that although they rule like Rav Pappa that her own maidservant is not believed, they do not accept the first baraita in full. They reject it regarding son and daughter and follow the anonymous mishnah that excludes only testimony concerning oneself. Rav Pappa disputed only her maidservant, whom he treats as equivalent to herself. The Rosh, Ran, and Maggid Mishneh, however, rule fully in accordance with the first baraita, so that son and daughter are like her maidservant and are not believed. It is therefore surprising that the Mechaber copied only the Rambam's general language and did not cite these other authorities. See also below, siman 148, seif katan 2, regarding seclusion after a conditional get. There too the Shulchan Aruch excludes only her maidservant and minor son from witnesses of seclusion, not an adult son. Perhaps that case differs: even if an adult son is not believed regarding captivity, a woman is embarrassed to engage in relations in his presence, whereas a captive woman may have been coerced.
