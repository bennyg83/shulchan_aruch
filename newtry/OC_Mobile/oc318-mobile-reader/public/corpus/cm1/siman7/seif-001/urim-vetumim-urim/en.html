A convert is disqualified, etc. This concerns coercive jurisdiction over a born Jew. Without coercion, a convert may adjudicate even between Jews. A convert may also adjudicate another convert even coercively. Rosh uses this to explain the statement in Sanhedrin that "everyone is fit to adjudicate monetary cases," which includes a convert, meaning without coercion. One must understand what novelty remains if the litigants came voluntarily. Above in siman 5 I wrote that voluntary appearance is treated as acceptance. If so, even a relative or otherwise disqualified person can judge when accepted, and even a non Jew could theoretically be accepted were it not for the impropriety of having "our enemies as judges." Certainly a convert could. The answer is that acceptance of an actually disqualified judge can ordinarily be withdrawn before final judgment under certain conditions. A convert, by contrast, is substantively fit but lacks coercive authority over a born Jew. Once the litigants voluntarily accept his jurisdiction, they cannot simply withdraw before judgment in the same way, because the defect is not in his personal qualification as a judge.
Unless his mother was Jewish. If one litigant is a full convert and the other has a Jewish mother, the full convert may not coercively judge the latter. A person whose mother is Jewish is treated as a full Jew for this law. What some authorities write, that a full convert may judge one whose father was Jewish, is difficult. If he cannot judge one whose mother was Jewish, all the more so should he not judge one whose father is Jewish in a case where paternal lineage gives an additional recognized Jewish family connection. See Knesset HaGedolah.
Or his father was Jewish. There is an important distinction. If the mother is Jewish, even if the father cohabited with her before conversion, the child is Jewish because status follows the Jewish mother. If a Jewish man had a child with a non Jewish woman, however, and afterward the woman and child converted, the child is a full convert and is not halachically related to the Jewish father. Knesset HaGedolah. If respected communal leaders deliberately appoint a convert as judge because of his great wisdom and stature, this may be treated as communal acceptance and permit him to serve. Knesset HaGedolah. See Tumim, where I questioned whether this can be applied without qualification.
