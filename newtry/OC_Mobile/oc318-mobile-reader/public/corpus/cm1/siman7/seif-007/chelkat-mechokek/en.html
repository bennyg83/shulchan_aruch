But not against two witnesses. Hagahot Alfasi explains that she does not have the power to prohibit herself to her husband who is a kohen when two witnesses say she remained pure. If she were unmarried, however, she would be believed to disqualify herself from marrying a kohen even against two witnesses, because a person is believed to prohibit something to himself more than one hundred other people. One should ask, however, that while she remains married, where there is one witness saying she is pure, why should she be believed to tell her husband that she became defiled? How is this different from an ordinary wife who tells her husband, 'I am defiled to you,' where we suspect that she has set her eyes upon another man? Logically, a captive woman with one witness saying she remained pure has an even stronger position than an ordinary woman who says she was raped, yet even there we are concerned that she seeks another man. All the more so here, where the witness contradicts her in her presence and says she remained pure. Although one could draw a forced distinction, it does not appear convincing.
