His student. All the more so may he judge the father of his student, even if the father pays him tuition. Sema. The Shach writes that he may likewise judge his host, as well as laborers and hired workers. See Responsa Mahari ben Lev, part 3, siman 96.
To appoint. Maharik writes that even one who, as an act of piety, is stringent upon himself not to judge a particular case is nevertheless qualified to participate in selecting the judges. Mahari in his responsa writes that the custom is not to disqualify any beit din from arranging the composition of the court. Although one should ordinarily investigate the basis of a custom, he writes that great authorities followed this practice, and who would change their custom? For this reason the Rama carefully wrote, "one who is disqualified from judging because of love or hatred," rather than simply "one who is disqualified from judging." Mahari had questioned the custom but did not wish to alter it. Thus concludes the Sema. See Responsa Rabbi Yosef Kohen, siman 30.
