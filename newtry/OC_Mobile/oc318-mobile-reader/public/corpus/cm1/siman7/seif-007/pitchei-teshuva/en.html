But if they saw it at night. See Baer Heitev, seif katan 12, who writes that the same applies if they saw the event on Shabbat or Yom Tov. The Taz disagrees and holds that this is comparable to the Rama's rule that in a rabbinic matter a witness may become a judge. Responsa Knesset Yechezkel, at the beginning of siman 88, rules simply like the Bach and brings proof from Tosafot at the end of the first chapter of Makkot. Urim VeTumim discusses the issue and concludes that the Bach's ruling has no proof and that the Taz's view appears correct. Ketzot HaChoshen likewise concludes that one may be lenient, and that judges may adjudicate on the basis of what they personally saw on Shabbat or Yom Tov. This is the primary ruling.
