Meaning his close wedding companion. Sema interprets this as the seven wedding days and cites Rashi. Rashi's words "during his wedding" do not conclusively establish all seven days. The Gemara itself records disagreement, and Rabbeinu Yerucham rules in the name of Rav that disqualification applies only on the first day. In our time, when unfortunately wedding joy is not maintained with the same intensity throughout all seven days, it appears that all agree that from the second day onward the former wedding companion may judge. The special affection and closeness have already diminished and each person has returned to his own affairs.

For each one chooses his friend. The purpose is that the chosen judge will investigate every legitimate argument in favor of the party who selected him, and through this the truth emerges. Levush says one should not deliberately choose a friend, though this became customary. Sema permits it from the outset. Both are correct in their proper context. If the selected judge searches for his party's merits but does not stubbornly cling to them, accepts truth, and does not distort halachah, this is an excellent method of clarifying the law. But in our sins, when a selected judge struggles with all his strength to justify the party who chose him whether right or wrong, manipulates legal reasoning to distort judgment, or coaches the party in false claims, the practice desecrates Torah and corrupts justice. Levush's warning was directed precisely at such conduct, and he wrote correctly.
