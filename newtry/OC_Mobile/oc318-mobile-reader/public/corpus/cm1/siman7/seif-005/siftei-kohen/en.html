Even if they directed their observation for the purpose of testimony, etc. Although below in siman 36 I conclude that intent alone makes one a witness, even if he never later testifies, so that if one of the group is a relative or disqualified the entire testimony is void, and although the Ran equates that law with this one, as cited by Beit Yosef, the distinction given initially by Tosafot and the Rosh in Bava Batra may be accepted. Only with respect to the rule that if one witness is a relative or disqualified the whole testimony is void does joining the observation suffice, because that is a Scriptural decree. By contrast, the rule that a witness may not become a judge is based on the requirement that 'the two men,' meaning the witnesses, stand 'before Hashem,' meaning before the judges, or because judges cannot accept testimony that would render themselves liable as conspiring witnesses. These reasons do not apply merely because one intended to testify. See Imrei Binah, page 99, column 3.
