Intended. The Shach writes: although in siman 36 I concluded that intent alone makes one a witness, even if he did not later testify, with respect to the rule that if one member of a group of witnesses is related or disqualified the testimony is void, that rule is a special Scriptural decree. Here, by contrast, the reason a witness cannot become a judge is either the requirement that "the two men shall stand," etc., or because the judges cannot receive hazamah against themselves. Mere intent to testify does not invoke either reason, and therefore does not disqualify him from judging.
Rabbinic. The Mechaber similarly writes in siman 46, seif 12, that one who saw an event at night may become a judge for the rabbinic proceeding of validating documents. See siman 46 for further details on this matter. Thus the Sema. The Shach writes in the name of the Bach that likewise, if the judges saw the event on Shabbos or Yom Tov, they may not become judges in that case, even though the prohibition against judging then is only rabbinic. He concludes that the practical law requires study.
