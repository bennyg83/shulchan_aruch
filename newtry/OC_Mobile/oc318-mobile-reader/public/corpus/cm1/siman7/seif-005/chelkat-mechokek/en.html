Speaking innocently helps only to be lenient, but not to be stringent. Meaning, if one gentile speaking innocently says that the husband is alive and another gentile speaking innocently says that he died, she may marry. This rule is derived from a responsum of the Radach, who cites it in the names of the Rashba and the Mordechai, the latter citing Rabbi David son of Rabbi Kalonymus. Yet one who examines the Mordechai at the end of chapter HaIshah Batra will see that Rabbi David's opinion is rejected there. The Mordechai expresses great astonishment at his lenient ruling and cites Rabbeinu Tam and a responsum of Rabbeinu Gershom prohibiting the woman. The Shulchan Aruch likewise rules stringently below, siman 17, seif 37, and so rules the Ran in responsa. See what is written there.
