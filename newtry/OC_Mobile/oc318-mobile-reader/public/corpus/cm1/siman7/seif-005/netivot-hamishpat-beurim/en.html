If one of the judges testified, etc. It appears that if three judges received testimony and one later departed, the remaining two may add a third judge and relate to him the testimony they received. The two may then sit as judges together with him. The rule that a witness cannot become a judge does not apply here. Where judges personally saw the underlying event outside a judicial setting, if they later judge on the basis of their own witnessing, they are functioning as witnesses and judges on the same matter. Here, however, the two remaining judges do not adjudicate on the basis of their personal witnessing of the event. They adjudicate on the basis of testimony that was formally received before beit din. Their knowledge is judicial knowledge. This resembles siman 30, where a member from one beit din can combine with others on the basis of a prior judicial act. If the two remaining judges had written the received testimony as a court record stating that one judge is absent, another court could rely on it. Ordinary witnesses could not create such force through writing because that would be testimony from writing. The difference proves that the two remaining members speak with the status of beit din, not as ordinary witnesses. Therefore their oral report to the added third judge should be no weaker than the written court record they could produce. When the added judge hears them, it is as though he sees a valid court record, and the three may then adjudicate. It may even be unnecessary to add two judges merely to 'receive' what the remaining judges say, because their statement is not ordinary testimony requiring reception by three. Urim VeTumim in siman 30 writes that where two remaining members came from two different courts and need to testify before a third, they cannot themselves become judges. That does not contradict this case. There, each speaks only as an individual concerning what occurred in a different court, and neither can create a court record on his own. Here the two were members of the same original beit din and together retain the status of that judicial act.
