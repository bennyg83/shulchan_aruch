And they deliberate among themselves. Meaning they adjudicate the matter among themselves. The case is where the local judges are regarded by the scholar as too lowly for him to litigate before. They gather the more distinguished scholars of the city, and he litigates before them. However, the wording "they deliberate among themselves" can sound like compromise rather than formal judgment, meaning that they first see whether they can reconcile the parties. Alternatively, the Gaon's original wording, as cited by the Tur, may use "deliberate" in place of "adjudicate." All this applies when the scholar has a legitimate objection to the court. If the court is adequate and the only issue is that it is demeaning for a Torah scholar to litigate with an ignoramus, siman 124 explains that the judges send scribes to the Torah scholar, record his claims, and then rule upon them.
