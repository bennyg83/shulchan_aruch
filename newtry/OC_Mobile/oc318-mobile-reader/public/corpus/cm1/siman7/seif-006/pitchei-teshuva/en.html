Monetary laws. Knesset HaGedolah, Hagahot Beit Yosef, letter 3, raises a difficulty from Midrash Rabbah, Parashat Naso, which indicates that even in monetary cases an intoxicated person is forbidden to judge. He writes that perhaps Tosafot holds that our Gemara disagrees with the Midrash, since the Gemara uses the expression “he should not issue a ruling,” and therefore we follow our Gemara. Responsa Shevut Yaakov, part I, siman 140, writes that Responsa Bach, siman 41, rules that an intoxicated person is forbidden even to judge, and in any event one who is stringent is praiseworthy. Sefer HaYirah of Rabbeinu Yonah likewise states that after eating and drinking one should not issue any ruling, whether in a monetary law or in matters of prohibition and permission. Responsa Mishkenot Yaakov, siman 6, concludes that there is no room for leniency, for according to the prohibiting authorities this is a Torah prohibition just like issuing a halachic ruling. See Urim VeTumim on this matter.
