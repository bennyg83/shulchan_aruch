(In the Beit Yosef, s.v. A judge may not judge his friend, etc., until: since it did not say invalid, etc.) This is difficult, because Rambam explicitly rules in chapter 13 of Hilchot Edut that a friend or enemy is therefore valid for testimony but invalid for judging, and the term "invalid" implies even after the fact. The same appears from his words in chapter 16 of Hilchot Edut, where he compares a friend and enemy to a convert whose mother is not Jewish, as the Kesef Mishneh notes there, and that case is invalid even after the fact, like a eunuch or mamzer. It therefore appears that the Tur's position is primary, and Rambam too distinguishes between one degree of enmity and another. See chapter 6 of Hilchot Rotzeach, where Rambam defines an enemy as one who did not speak with the other for three days, etc. This also resolves the Kesef Mishneh's difficulty at the end of chapter 6 of Hilchot Edut. Examine there.
