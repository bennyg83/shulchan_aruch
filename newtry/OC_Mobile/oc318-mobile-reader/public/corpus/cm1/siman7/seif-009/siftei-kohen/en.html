Because of kinship, etc. Maharshal writes in Yam Shel Shlomo, Bava Kamma, chapter 10, siman 34: If an expert rabbi is approached by a person who wishes him to adjudicate a claim against the rabbi's relative, the rabbi may judge the case. Since the claimant brought the case before him, he thereby accepted him as qualified, as in the incident in which Ravina adjudicated a matter involving his father in law. Although this is clear law, some rabbis refrain from doing so as an act of piety, and as a result justice is impaired. Therefore I have written this.
