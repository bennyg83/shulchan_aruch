To compel him to go before him. See Birkei Yosef, entry 15, who infers from this wording that the Gaon holds that if the defendant wishes to go and litigate before a judge of lesser stature than himself, he may do so. This is not degradation, but merely a lack of honor, and a sage may waive his honor. This also resolves what is implied by the Gaon's wording, that the judge may summon him but cannot compel him. One might ask why we do not erect a barrier before this lesser judge so that he not degrade him, and forbid him even to summon him initially. What then is meant by saying that he cannot compel him? It should have said that the summons has no force from the outset. According to what was stated, however, that if he wishes to go he may do so, one can say that for this reason the lesser judge may summon him. This is not deemed a degradation of his Torah stature, since if he wishes he may come, and if he does not wish, the choice is his. He discusses this at length.
