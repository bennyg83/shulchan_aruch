Two Torah scholars who hate one another, etc. Nimukei Yosef holds that if they judged, the judgment is valid, even though regarding a judge who hates a litigant he follows Rosh that the judgment is invalid after the fact. Bach asks why these cases should differ. Both are derived from verses, the judge who hates the litigant from "and he is not his enemy," and judges who hate one another from "and does not seek his harm." Bach therefore rules that if they are genuine enemies who have not spoken to one another for three days because of hatred, their judgment is invalid even after the fact. See Tumim, where I reconcile Nimukei Yosef and answer Bach's question.
