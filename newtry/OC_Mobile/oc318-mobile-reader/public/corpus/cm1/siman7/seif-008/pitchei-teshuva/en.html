Before a judge who is lesser than him. Birkei Yosef writes that this does not depend on chronological age. “Lesser than him” means lesser in wisdom. Even if the judge is older in years, he cannot compel a greater Torah scholar to appear before him. Knesset HaGedolah similarly explains below, siman 28, Hagahot Tur, letter 27, and there discusses a case in which one is exceptionally old while the other is not exceptionally great in wisdom. This is straightforward, as Bava Batra 142b states, “Does the matter depend on seniority of age?” Birkei Yosef further questions whether this rule applies only when a single judge is presiding, whereas if there is a panel of three, even if the defendant is greater than each of them individually, the authority of a three-member beit din as a body may prevail; or perhaps there is no distinction and the Gaon mentioned one judge merely as an example, with the same rule applying to a panel of three. He writes that earlier scholars, the rabbis of Egypt, disagreed on this issue in a manuscript collection of responsa. He discusses the matter at great length and ultimately cites Responsa Radbaz, part I, siman 119, from which it is evident that there is no distinction between a single judge and a panel of three. See there, and see what I write in seif katan 10.
