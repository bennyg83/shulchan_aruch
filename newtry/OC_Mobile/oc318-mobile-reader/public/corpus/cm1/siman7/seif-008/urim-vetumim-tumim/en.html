Scholars who hate one another. Nimukei Yosef holds that if they judged, their judgment is valid. Bach asks why this should differ from an enemy judging a litigant, whose judgment is invalid, as discussed in Urim. Perishah appears to answer that the verse "and he is not his enemy" is only an asmachta rather than a full Scriptural derivation. This does not appear correct. The Gemara and Tosafot treat the verse as a real derashah. In my view Nimukei Yosef's position can be explained differently. What harm results merely because two judges hate one another? Each will insist upon his own understanding. But in civil cases even one judge can be valid d'oraisa according to the position discussed above. The third judge decides the majority and resolves the case. Even if the two enemies refuse to concede to one another, there remains a deciding judge, and the tribunal can still function. The verse concerning one who "does not seek his harm" appears in the law of exile, as Rashi states explicitly there. That is a setting of capital-type adjudication requiring twenty-three judges. There, if members of the court hate one another and each refuses to acknowledge the other's correct reasoning, the panel's effective number is diminished. The required court cannot function properly. Thus in capital cases their judgment would indeed be invalid even after the fact, while in civil cases Nimukei Yosef can validate it after the fact. One difficulty nevertheless remains. How do we find Torah scholars who hate one another at all? Would they not violate "you shall not hate your brother in your heart"? If each claims that he saw the other commit a transgression and is therefore permitted to hate him, another problem arises: how can he sit in judgment together with a person whom he regards as a sinner, when below, seif 10, one may not sit in judgment with such a person? The matter can perhaps be reconciled with difficulty, but it requires further study.
