Or she married before beit din and they did not object. This wording implies that if she married privately without beit din's knowledge and afterward witnesses came that she had been taken captive, she must leave the marriage. Just as a married woman against whom witnesses of captivity later appear must leave, so too a captive woman who married must leave unless she married by authorization of beit din or in their presence without objection. In truth, however, there is room to distinguish. A married woman is not believed because she is personally interested in remaining with her husband. A captive woman who has already married does not abandon a permitted option and deliberately choose a prohibited one, for she was free to marry any Israelite she wished. Responsa Radach, house 8, writes that even if she married privately without beit din's knowledge she need not leave. The Radach also writes that even if her husband knew before marriage that she had been captive, so long as the fact was not established before beit din by witnesses she remains permitted to him, because she had a migo that she could have married another man. It appears, however, that if a married woman was taken captive and her husband knew that fact, she is not believed to say she remained pure, because she has no migo to say she was never captive. This is also implied by Responsa Rosh, rule 32, siman 5: once the husband knows that she was among the gentiles, there can be no greater testimony of captivity than that.
