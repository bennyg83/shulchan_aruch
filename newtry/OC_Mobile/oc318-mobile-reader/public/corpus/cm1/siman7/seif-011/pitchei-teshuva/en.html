For one whom he loves, etc. Birkei Yosef, entry 20, writes that there is room to question whether a judge may judge two people whom he loves, or two whom he hates, who come before him for judgment, since his mind is drawn toward or repelled from both of them and the two litigants are equal in his eyes. At first glance, it appears that even if both are his friends or both his enemies, he should not judge them, because presumably his love for them, or likewise his hatred for them, is not equal in degree, and his mind will be more drawn toward the one he loves more, or more repelled from the one he hates more. As the Rosh writes in the chapter Zeh Borer, even a small degree of love or hatred inclines the heart. There is also concern that the case before him may branch into several issues, and because both are his friends or both his enemies his heart may incline to favor one on one issue and rule against him on another. This appears correct by reason. Some support may also be brought from Tosafos in the chapter Ba Siman, Niddah 50a, and also some support from Responsa Mahari ibn Migash cited in Shitah Mekubetzes at the end of the chapter HaChovel, Bava Kamma 92.
