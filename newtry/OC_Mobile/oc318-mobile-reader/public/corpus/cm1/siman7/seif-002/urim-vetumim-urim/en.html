Even if all three are mamzerim, etc. Likewise, three converts whose mothers are Jewish may adjudicate born Jews, because they are treated as full Jews for this purpose. Sema.
Blind in one eye is valid. Since ordinary people also cannot see at night, just as a person blind in one eye lacks full vision by day, and final judgment is valid at night, Rif reasons that such a person may even begin a case by day. Sema.
But one blind in both eyes is disqualified. Since everyone is disqualified to begin a case at night, and for this person daytime itself is like night. This is unlike Ir Shushan, who explains the disqualification through the comparison of disputes to tzaraat, according to Rabbi Meir. Sema says the halachah does not follow that derivation. A practical difference is where a judge saw at the beginning of the case and became blind before final judgment. According to Sema he may complete the case, because final judgment is valid at night. According to Ir Shushan he remains disqualified even for final judgment by the scriptural comparison.
