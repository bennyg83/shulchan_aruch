If they saw the matter by day, etc. The Tur does not say “by day” here, but rather “at a time fit for judging.” From this my father-in-law derives that the same applies if they saw it on Shabbos or Yom Tov. Although judging is rabbinically forbidden then, the Sages fashioned their enactments in the pattern of Torah law. This is astonishing. In siman 253 Rabbeinu himself writes that they saw it by day. Moreover, what source did the Tur have to extend the rule beyond daytime? The decisors mention only daytime, which is fit for judgment by Torah law, and had the broader rule been intended, the greater novelty should have been stated. Further, if rabbinic law in this matter is patterned precisely after Torah law, why do we say that in a rabbinic matter a witness may become a judge, as the Rama writes in seif 5? Also, what the Sema writes in seif katan 20 in the name of the Maharik, that a relative or one otherwise disqualified by Torah law is excluded, is imprecise. Certainly all disqualified persons, even those disqualified rabbinically, are invalid as judges selected by the litigants, as stated in the mishna at the beginning of the chapter Zeh Borer: “When does this apply? When they are relatives or disqualified.” The Maharik intended only to permit selecting a friend as one's judge, lest one think that a friend is included among the disqualified persons.
