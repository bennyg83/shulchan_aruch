And even women who are not believed, etc. This requires study. The Rosh is stringent and does not accept a gentile speaking innocently as valid testimony for a captive woman, as stated in the gloss to the following seif. He therefore holds that the testimony required for a captive woman is stricter than that required to establish a husband's death, where a gentile speaking innocently is believed, as ruled below in siman 17. If so, all the more should those women who are not believed to testify to a husband's death, such as a mother-in-law and the others listed there, be disqualified from testifying concerning a captive woman. Why, then, is their testimony accepted here?
