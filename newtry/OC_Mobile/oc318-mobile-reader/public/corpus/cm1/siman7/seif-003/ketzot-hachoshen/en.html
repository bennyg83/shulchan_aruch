A Kohen Gadol is entirely outside the ordinary rule of being judged, since the Mishnah says one does not judge him. A minor, however, is certainly judged in many matters. If he stole an object in our presence, beit din orders its return. The same applies if he stole land. Thus the minor is subject to judgment. The rule does not require that he be legally judged in the identical subject matter in which he himself judges. It is enough that he is subject to judgment in some matter. Tumim concludes that his judgment is valid regarding movable property but not land, because a minor's purchase of land is ineffective and therefore the principle of first judging oneself is absent. This is not convincing. The matter does not depend on his being subject to judgment in precisely the type of case he adjudicates, as explained.
