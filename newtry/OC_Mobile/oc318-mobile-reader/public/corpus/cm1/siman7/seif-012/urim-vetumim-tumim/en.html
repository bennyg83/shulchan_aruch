Another Torah scroll. See Sema, who explains that if there is another Torah scroll in another synagogue, and the witness goes to that other synagogue, he may testify. Likewise, in a dispute concerning a synagogue, if he goes to another synagogue he may judge and testify. It must, however, be that the other synagogue is large enough to accommodate all the townspeople so that they can pray there and hear the Torah reading. Otherwise, how could he testify concerning the synagogue or Torah scroll? The townspeople may compel one another to build a synagogue and purchase a Torah scroll, as explained below in siman 163. Therefore, if the town loses this Torah scroll or this synagogue, even though it has another synagogue containing a Torah scroll, if that synagogue cannot accommodate everyone, they will be obligated to contribute toward building another synagogue and purchasing a Torah scroll, and this witness will have to pay his share. He therefore benefits financially if the townspeople prevail with respect to the present Torah scroll or synagogue. We must therefore say that the second synagogue is sufficient for the entire town, so that there is no obligation to build a new synagogue or purchase another Torah scroll, because everyone can enter the remaining synagogue and hear its Torah reading. Even where there is another Torah scroll in the synagogue to which the judge goes, the Sema appears to imply that no divestment is required because the judge derives no benefit, since there is already another Torah scroll. This is open to question. We rule in Yoreh Deah that the seven communal trustees, acting in the presence of the townspeople, may sell a Torah scroll for communal needs. If so, he does derive a benefit from the disputed Torah scroll remaining in communal possession, because in a time of need it may be sold and the proceeds used for the town, thereby reducing what he personally would otherwise have to contribute. Possibly the Sema follows his own view in the Derishah to Yoreh Deah, siman 252, that a communal Torah scroll may not be sold for any need. According to the later authorities there, who rejected his view, the present ruling requires study. Indeed, Responsa Rosh, rule 3, siman 12, implies that divestment is required. Where there is no other Torah scroll, divestment is ineffective; but where there is another Torah scroll, divestment is effective, meaning that he obligates himself that even if the disputed Torah scroll is later sold for some communal need, he will contribute his share toward that need as though the scroll had not been sold and as though the town had no Torah scroll at all. This appears correct. Although the Shulchan Aruch here states the rule without qualification and may appear to require no divestment, it may be that it relies upon siman 37 below, where it appears that divestment is nevertheless required. The Ir Shushan writes likewise.
