But if the other Torah scroll is in another synagogue, so that they would have to exchange scrolls and read from the other scroll in this synagogue, or the judge would have to attend the other synagogue, removal can be necessary. Likewise, when litigating over a synagogue and another synagogue exists in town, the judge must remove himself and consistently attend the other synagogue. If he continues attending the disputed synagogue, even though he could attend the other one, he is considered to derive benefit. Sema. It must also be possible for the entire city to attend the other synagogue. Otherwise the judge remains interested in the outcome, because residents can compel one another to build a synagogue where everyone cannot be accommodated, and he may ultimately have to contribute. The case must also be one where the Torah scroll cannot be sold for another communal need through the seven trustees of the city. Otherwise the judge has a financial interest. Urim VeTumim.
