Unless they have another Sefer Torah. Meaning that if there is another Sefer Torah available, even the judges themselves may read from the Sefer Torah that is the subject of the litigation. This is not considered a benefit because they could have read from the other one. This applies only if the other Sefer Torah is in the same synagogue where the judges pray and read. If the other scroll is in a different synagogue, they must remove themselves from benefit from the disputed scroll entirely and read only from the other. The same applies when they adjudicate ownership of a synagogue. Even if there is another synagogue in the city, they may judge only if they remove themselves from using the disputed synagogue. Rabbeinu Yerucham writes this. It is unlike the Ir Shushan, who compares two synagogues in one city to two Torah scrolls in one synagogue.
