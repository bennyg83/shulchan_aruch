A judge may not adjudicate any matter in which he has a potential benefit. Therefore, if a city's Torah scroll was stolen, judges from that city may not hear the case unless the city owns another Torah scroll. If a person says, 'Give one hundred zuz to the poor of my city,' the matter may not be adjudicated by that city's judges. {Rama: See above, siman 4: if one person withholds the community's property, the community may enforce its own rights. See below, siman 37, seifim 19 and 20, regarding these laws.} Consequently, tax matters may not be adjudicated by judges of that city because they or their relatives have an interest in them. {Rama: If it is a particular tax, such as one imposed only for a fixed period, some members of the community may renounce their interests and any resulting benefit and then serve as judges. Responsa of the Rosh, klal 26, siman 18, states that renunciation is ineffective, while klal 99 states that it is effective; one must distinguish a particular tax from other taxes. See also siman 136.} If the community enacted a regulation, or the local custom is for the city's judges to adjudicate even tax matters, their ruling is valid. {Rama: See below, end of siman 37, seif 22, and siman 40.}
