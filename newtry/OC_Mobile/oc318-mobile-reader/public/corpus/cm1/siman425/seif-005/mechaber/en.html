A Jewish heretic, including one who practices idolatry or commits transgressions specifically to provoke, even if he eats neveilah or wears shatnez for the sake of provocation, is considered a heretic. Likewise, Jews who deny the Torah or prophecy are included in this category. Under the law stated here, there is a mitzvah to remove such persons when one has the power to do so openly; if one cannot do so openly, one may bring about their removal indirectly. For example, if one sees such a person who has fallen into a pit and there is a ladder in the pit, he may remove the ladder and say, "I need it to bring my son down from the roof, and afterward I will return it to you," or use similar means. But with respect to non-Jews with whom we are not at war, or a Jewish shepherd of small livestock in a place where the fields belong to Jews, and similar persons, one does not actively bring about their death, though under the law stated here one is not obligated to rescue them. See the cited source; some disagree. See above, siman 34, seif 13, and siman 409. This distinction regarding a Jewish transgressor applies when he persists in wickedness and repeatedly commits it, such as a shepherd of small livestock who is heedless about theft and continues in his wrongdoing. But a Jewish transgressor who does not persist in rebellion and sins only for personal benefit, such as one who eats neveilah out of appetite, must be rescued, and it is forbidden to stand idly by while his life is in danger. See the cited source and Yoreh Deah, siman 158.
