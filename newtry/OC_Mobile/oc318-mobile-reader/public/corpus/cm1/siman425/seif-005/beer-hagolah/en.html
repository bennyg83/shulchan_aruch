peh) The language of the Rambam there, chapter 4, law 10, from a baraita in the second chapter of Avodah Zarah 26b and the Gemara's discussion there. The Beit Yosef records another textual version, copied in Yoreh Deah siman 158, seif 11; see there.
tzadi) So appears in the Rambam there. The Kesef Mishneh writes that in the dispute of Rav Acha and Ravina the Rambam rules according to the view that one who sins in order to provoke is an apikores. Rabbi Abbahu and Rabbi Yochanan likewise hold this view there.
kuf) Ibid., in the baraita, as the Amoraim explain it there.
reish) This means that the statement taught at the end of Kiddushin, 'the best among the gentiles, kill,' refers to a time of war, as Tosafot write in the name of the Yerushalmi.
shin) Chazal said this only regarding the gentiles of their time, who worshipped stars and constellations and did not believe in the Exodus from Egypt or creation of the world. But the nations under whose protection the Jewish people presently live and among whom we are dispersed believe in creation, the Exodus from Egypt, and the fundamentals of religion, and direct their intent toward the Creator of heaven and earth, as the poskim write and the Rama cites in Orach Chaim siman 126 in a gloss. Not only is it not prohibited to save them, we are even obligated to pray for their welfare. The author of Maaseh Hashem explains this at length in his commentary on the Haggadah to the verse, 'Pour out Your wrath upon the nations that do not know You': King David prayed against those nations that do not believe in creation or in the signs and wonders Hashem performed for us in Egypt and at Matan Torah. The nations under whose protection we live believe in all these matters, as stated, and we remain steadfast in continually praying for the welfare of the government and its ministers, for their success, and for all the lands under their rule. The Rambam writes that the halakhah follows Rabbi Yehoshua in Sanhedrin 105a, chapter Chelek, that even the righteous among the nations have a share in the World to Come.
tav) The Kesef Mishneh explains that the phrase means one who does not persist continually in wickedness, excluding a shepherd of small livestock. Although such shepherds act for their own benefit out of greed for money, they remain constantly engaged in wrongdoing. It also excludes one who eats neveilot deliberately to provoke, for even if he does not constantly persist in that wrongdoing, he is included among the apikorsim, as explained at the beginning of the seif.
