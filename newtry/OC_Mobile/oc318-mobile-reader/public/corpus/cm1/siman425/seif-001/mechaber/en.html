How persons liable to judicial execution are treated nowadays. It contains five seifim.
Rama: Nowadays we lack authority to administer lashes, exile, execution, or corporal punishment to those liable to penalties imposed by beit din. Instead, they are placed under a ban and separated from the community. This is the ordinary law. Where beit din sees an urgent communal need and a necessity to reinforce observance, it may impose whatever punishment the circumstances require, as explained in siman 2. This limitation applies to capital cases requiring adjudication by beit din. Persons who may be killed without a judicial proceeding are governed even today by the rules explained below. If one pursues another in order to kill him and continues after being warned, even if the pursuer is a minor, every Jew must save the pursued person by injuring one of the pursuer's limbs if possible. If the victim cannot be saved otherwise, the pursuer may be killed even before he has killed. Rama: One who breaks into a house to steal is likewise treated as a pursuer. If it is known that he came only for property and would not kill the owner even if resisted, it is forbidden to kill him. A person who endangers the public, such as one who counterfeits currency in a jurisdiction that severely punishes such conduct, is treated as a pursuer and may be reported to the authorities, as explained in siman 388.
