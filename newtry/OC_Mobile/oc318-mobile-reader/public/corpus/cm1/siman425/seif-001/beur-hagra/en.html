To administer lashes to them. Ordained judges are required for Torah lashes. The Rambam writes in chapter 16 of Hilchos Sanhedrin that Torah lashes in any place require three ordained judges. The lashes administered by judges outside Eretz Yisrael are therefore only rabbinic disciplinary lashes, just as the Gemara in chapter 8 of Bava Kamma limits agency to common monetary cases involving financial loss.
Or to exile them. We have no cities of refuge today. Tur. Sifrei derives that the command to designate cities applies after inheritance and settlement of the land, from the verses concerning Hashem cutting off the nations, etc.
Or to execute them. Sanhedrin 52b, and even forty years before the destruction of the Beis HaMikdash capital jurisdiction ceased, as stated there 41a.
Or to confine them. Bach emends the text from "strike them" to "confine them," referring to the rule that one who kills without sufficient witnesses may be confined, which is a death procedure. Rabbinic disciplinary lashes, however, may be administered, as the Tur writes, together with excommunication and other sanctions. The Rambam also discusses such measures, and the Gemara frequently says that a person was flogged. The Rama omitted disciplinary lashes because of the corrupted Tur reading "strike them," but that reading is incorrect.
(Collected note) Or to confine them. The correct text is "confine them," as in Hagahos Mordechai at the end of Sanhedrin. (End of note.)
Rather, etc. As found in many places, one may impose excommunication even for a minor rabbinic violation, as discussed in Yoreh Deah siman 334, and disciplinary lashes are likewise found in chapter 4 of Pesachim.
And all this, etc. As stated in chapter 3 of Sanhedrin concerning Rabban Gamliel ordering punishment, etc. See siman 2.
Specifically, etc. At the end of Bava Kamma, Rav Kahana imposed severe punishment in an exceptional case; likewise with a pursuer at the end of Berachos in the incident of Rav Sheila, and with a burglar in Sanhedrin 72b, where Rav says that anyone coming upon him through a tunnel may be treated accordingly. This is the basis of the language concerning a pursuer and one who comes, etc.
And they warned him. Sanhedrin 72b: the Gemara seeks support from a case where he was told, "see," etc. See siman 388 seif 10 in the Shulchan Aruch and Rama.
Even though, etc. As in the incident of Rav Sheila. See Beer HaGolah and siman 388 there.
And if it is known, etc. Sanhedrin 72a and 72b.
One who endangers, etc. Even if his intention is not to endanger, as in the incident involving a donkey at the end of Bava Kamma.
