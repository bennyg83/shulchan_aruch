(Seif 5: when there is no war between us and them.) The Sema writes that this means there is a mitzvah to lower them into danger and bring about their death, and says Tosafos writes likewise. It appears there is a textual error here. In my Turei Zahav on Yoreh Deah, siman 158, I wrote that the Beit Yosef explains in Tosafos' name that the phrase, "we do not raise them and we do not lower them," means there is no mitzvah to lower them. I wrote there that he overlooked an explicit Gemara showing that there is actually a prohibition against doing so. The Sema also writes: see Shevet Yehudah, which gives an answer to sectarians on this matter. Meaning, it explains the saying, "The best among the non-Jews, kill," as referring to a praiseworthy legal quality among them, namely that they execute a person clearly liable to death and do not employ as many avenues of rescue as Jewish law, where we say perhaps the person killed was already mortally wounded, and similar defenses. A certain sage gave this answer to a pope.
