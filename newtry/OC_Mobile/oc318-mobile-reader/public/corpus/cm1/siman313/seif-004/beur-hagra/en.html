Even those of others, etc. Tur cites Ramah, who follows Rashi's view that rental grants the tenant possession. Since the premises are rented to him and the items stand in the courtyard, the tenant acquires them. Bach explains similarly. The gloss is difficult because elsewhere it follows Rambam, yet here rules like Rashi. Tur consistently follows Rashi and Rosh, holding that the tenant has no right in the courtyard except passage to his house, while the manure belongs to him. Rosh explains that mere passage does not make the courtyard his for acquisition. Ritva likewise derives that a courtyard in which one has only passage rights does not acquire for him. See Shach, siman 198. Tosafot here appear to disagree, and Shach overlooked their words. His attribution of Rambam's view to Tosafot is also incorrect. Tosafot's question means that even without a formal rental the owner normally allows such use. Nevertheless, the gloss may be supported by Tosafot's distinction that the tenant's right differs depending on the purpose of the courtyard. Sema likewise distinguishes the cases, as in siman 200:1 in the gloss.
(Supplement) Even, etc. This follows the preceding gloss and parallels the Mishna's law of manure. (End of supplement.) See 260.
