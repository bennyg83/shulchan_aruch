The ash that comes from the oven and stove, etc. The Tur explains that the oven and stove are standing in the courtyard and are rented to the tenant for cooking. The novelty is that even if others also bake and cook there, since the oven and stove are rented to him, the ash belongs to the tenant. This is what Rama means when he concludes, "even if others, etc." One may ask why this differs from manure left in the courtyard by other people's animals, where the Rambam holds that even if the courtyard is rented to the tenant, the manure belongs to the landlord. The distinction is that ash from the oven and stove mixes with the tenant's own ash, because he too cooks and bakes there. Therefore the landlord does not ordinarily retain his claim to it.
