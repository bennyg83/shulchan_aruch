One who rents a house to another in a large building, etc. This is the Gemara's language. Rashi explains that a "large building" is a long structure divided internally into many rooms and small chambers. If he rents one of them to this tenant, the tenant may use the holes in the exterior wall along its length for four cubits even outside the area directly opposite his room, the projecting ledges from the walls, and the top thickness of the wall, for example where he rented an upper story in the building. Nimukei Yosef likewise explains that use of the wall thickness concerns one who rented the upper story. Regarding the Gemara's phrase, "in a place where they are accustomed to use what is his," he explains that where people are not particular about such use, this renter too may use those places. This applies not only to the wall of the building in front of the room entrances, but also to the wall of the pleasure garden, and to the open area behind the houses when his room also opens there. Rashi likewise mentions the garden wall. Their explanations imply that they permit use of the garden wall, not necessarily of the garden itself. Regarding the rear open area, however, they do not limit the use to its wall, implying that use of the open area itself is permitted. It therefore requires study why the poskim wrote the law without this distinction. In the Perishah I reconciled the Tur by saying that he relied upon what he writes afterward: one who rents a house in a courtyard has in the courtyard only a right of passage, not a right to use the body of the courtyard. Certainly he has no right to use the garden itself, which is even more private and specially designated than a courtyard. But this reconciliation does not work for the Rambam and Mechaber, who do not write here the rule that one who rents a house in a courtyard has only a right of passage. It therefore appears that the Rambam holds that the Gemara permitted use of the airspace of the courtyard just as of the rear open area. Perhaps the Tur too holds this, since he wrote the rule without limiting it to the garden wall. Examine carefully.
