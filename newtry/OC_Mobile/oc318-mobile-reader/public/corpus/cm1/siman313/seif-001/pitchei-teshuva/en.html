Rented in the possession of others. See Baer Heitev, who concludes that practical law appears principally in accordance with Rambam. Shach further writes that although Tosafos in the first chapter of Bava Metzia require that the landlord's own house open into the rented courtyard so that he is considered as standing beside his field, Rambam, Semag, Mechaber, and other decisors do not appear to require this additional fact. Rabbi Akiva Eiger, Responsa siman 133, answers that Rambam's silence is not decisive because Rambam often states the Gemara's rule without its contextual details. The question actually depends on two interpretations of Rambam in chapter 6 of Hilchot Mechirah regarding a buyer's house that is rented to the seller, where Rambam says meshichah is unnecessary because the purchased item is already in the buyer's domain. Therefore Rambam's view here cannot be conclusively determined. Rabbi Akiva Eiger adds that according to Tosafos, the landlord's house opening into the courtyard helps only for an item lying in the courtyard itself. Since the landlord goes in and out and can supervise it, he is treated as standing beside his field. But for an item lying inside the rented room, it does not help that the landlord lives in another room of the same building. The tenant has the right to lock his room and prevent the landlord from entering, so the item is certainly not guarded for the landlord. He further cites Beit Meir, who questions Shach's basic ruling in favor of Rambam and prefers Rosh and Tur. See there, what I wrote in Pitchei Teshuva to Even HaEzer, siman 102, seif katan 4, and Responsa VeShav HaKohen, siman 47.
