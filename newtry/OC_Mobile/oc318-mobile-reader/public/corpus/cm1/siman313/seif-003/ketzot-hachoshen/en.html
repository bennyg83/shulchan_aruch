The ordinary rule is that a rented courtyard acquires for the tenant, as seen from Rabban Gamliel and the elders, from acquisition through rented space, and from the laws of divorce. The present rule concerning manure is exceptional. Because the landlord did not rent the stable, he retained the benefit produced by it and with it the necessary acquisition power in the courtyard for that manure. Ordinary found property in the rented courtyard belongs to the tenant. This also explains why the Gemara infers that if the stable had been included in the rental, the manure would belong to the tenant. Courtyard acquisition follows present possessory use, whereas the hand in divorce is expressly designated by Scripture.
