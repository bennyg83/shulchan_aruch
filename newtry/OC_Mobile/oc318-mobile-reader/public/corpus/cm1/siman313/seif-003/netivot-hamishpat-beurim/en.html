Even though it is leased. See Shach, seif katan 1, and see siman 200 where I discussed this at length. His statement that because it is guarded and is his courtyard requires explanation. Siman 200 indicates that even without a partition separating the owner from his produce, the courtyard is considered unguarded. One might distinguish that with respect to the tenant the courtyard is unguarded against the owner, whereas with respect to the owner it is guarded against the tenant, because the tenant is like a guest relative to the owner, as Shitah Mekubetzes writes in Bava Metzia 11. In truth, however, Shitah Mekubetzes says this only where the tenant has merely a right of passage. Where he is a full tenant the distinction is strained.
