Others. This is the Rambam's view, but Rashi does not agree, and the Tur's language likewise does not imply it. The Tur states this law regarding one who rented a house in a courtyard, not one who rented the courtyard itself. Maggid Mishneh also records dissent from the Rambam. For the same reason, the Rosh, cited by the Tur in siman 260 regarding a lost object such as an injured deer that enters a courtyard in which both tenant and lessor live, rules that both acquire it. All the more so would that seem true here where the tenant alone lives there. The Sema therefore wonders why the Mechaber rules without qualification in siman 260, seif 4, like the Rosh, yet here rules without qualification like the Rambam. See the Shach, who discusses this at length and concludes that for practical law the Rambam's view appears primary.
