The manure in the courtyard belongs to the renter; therefore he must attend to removing it. Rambam likewise writes this, and it contains two rulings. First, if both parties want the manure, it belongs to the renter. From this follows the second law: if neither wants it, the renter bears the burden of removing it.
Where the animals that produced the manure belonged to the renter. The same applies to manure he removes from his house into the courtyard, as the Tur writes.
The manure belongs to the owner of the courtyard. Ordinarily the owners of animals passing from place to place abandon the manure, and the courtyard acquires it for its owner without his knowledge.
Even though it is rented to others. This is Rambam's view. Rashi disagrees, and the Tur's wording likewise does not imply this, because the Tur states this law concerning one who rents a house in a courtyard, not one who rents the courtyard itself. The Maggid Mishneh also writes that some disagree with Rambam, though he notes that the language of the Gemara somewhat supports Rambam. For this reason the Rosh, cited by the Tur above in siman 260, seif 3, rules regarding a found object, such as an injured deer that enters a courtyard in which renter and owner both live, that both acquire it. All the more so where the renter alone lives there. It is therefore surprising that the Mechaber there in seif 4 rules without qualification like the Rosh, while here he rules without qualification like Rambam.
If the renter caught it in a vessel, etc. In the Perishah I proved that this means specifically where the renter placed the vessel beneath the animal before the waste left its body, so that it was never destined to rest in the courtyard. See there. It appears, however, that this is only according to the Tur and those who explain the case as involving the owner's courtyard, as above. The Mechaber states the law according to Rambam, who applies it even where the courtyard itself was rented to the renter. According to that view Rambam may concede that if the renter receives the manure in his own vessel, he acquires it even if the vessel was not in place before the waste emerged. Perhaps even if the renter's vessel rests on the ground of the owner's courtyard, since the renter rented the courtyard and is entitled to place his vessels there, his vessel acquires the manure. Rama copied the Tur's wording onto the Mechaber's ruling, and the words must therefore be understood according to each authority's approach.
