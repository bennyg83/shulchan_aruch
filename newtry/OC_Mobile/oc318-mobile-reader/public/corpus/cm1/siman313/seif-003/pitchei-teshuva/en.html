Rented in the hands of others. See Baer Heitev and Shach, who incline to the Rambam. Tosafot require that the landlord's own house open into the courtyard so that he is considered standing beside his field, whereas the Rambam and other codifiers do not state this qualification. Rabbi Akiva Eiger writes that the Rambam's silence is not decisive, since this may depend on two interpretations of his wording in Hilchot Mechirah. Even according to Tosafot, an opening into the courtyard helps only for an item lying in the courtyard, because the landlord enters and leaves and can guard it. It does not help for an item inside the rented room, which the tenant may lock and from which he may exclude the landlord. Beit Meir questions the Shach's basic ruling and favors the Rosh and Tur. See also Petach Teshuva, Even HaEzer 102, and Responsa VeShav HaKohen 47.
