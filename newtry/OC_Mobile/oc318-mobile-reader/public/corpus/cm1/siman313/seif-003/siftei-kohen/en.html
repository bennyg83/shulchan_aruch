Although it is rented to others. Tosafot indicate that a courtyard can acquire for the landlord even while a tenant uses it. They ask how the landlord acquires manure deposited there by outside animals when the tenant uses the courtyard, and answer that the landlord's own house also opens into the courtyard, so he is regarded as standing beside his field. The Agudah says likewise. The Rambam, Semag, Mechaber, and other authorities do not appear to require that the landlord's house open into the courtyard. In their view it remains a guarded courtyard for the landlord because it is his protected property. The Sema notes an apparent contradiction between the Mechaber's ruling elsewhere like the Rosh and his ruling here like the Rambam. The Tosefot Yom Tov attempts to distinguish that the courtyard was not rented for keeping cattle and producing manure, but that distinction is unpersuasive. The primary law nevertheless follows the Rambam, as ruled by the Semag and indicated by the Maggid Mishneh, Nimukei Yosef, Tosafot Yom Tov, and Tosafot. The principle that “rental for a day is a sale” applies to onaah, not generally. Tosafot and Terumat HaDeshen state this expressly. Even those who describe rental as a sale mean that the tenant acquires the use, not the body of the property. Since the tenant does not acquire the courtyard itself, the courtyard does not automatically acquire ownerless property for him. The Raavad's proof from one who rents the place occupied by goods can be answered. There another person intentionally transfers the goods to the renter, whereas here the property is ownerless. Alternatively, in the sale case the object was placed there with the renter's knowledge, and the place was rented for that very purpose; it is therefore treated as the renter's domain for the deposited object. This differs from a chance find or ownerless property. The Ritva also rules that where a courtyard is rented only for passage it does not acquire for the renter, though the Rambam's present ruling appears to concern a courtyard rented for general use.
