But, etc., for his courtyard, etc. See Rashi there, s.v. 'in the landlord's courtyard.' Raavad objects that the Mishna says one rents its place and that place is rented to him, as stated in siman 198:5 and many other places. Rental effects acquisition, and a daily rental is considered a sale. Rosh likewise writes this. If Rambam meant a courtyard not included in the rental, the Gemara should have said so explicitly. Moreover, how could Rav Ashi infer from this that one who rents, etc.? Lechem Mishneh's explanation is very strained. See what I wrote above in siman 260:3, where the ruling follows Rashi and Rosh.
