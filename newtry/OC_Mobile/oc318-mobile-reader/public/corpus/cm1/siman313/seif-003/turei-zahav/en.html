(Seif 4: Even if it belongs to others, etc.) This is unlike manure belonging to others, concerning which the Sema raised a difficulty and answered strainedly. Ashes from an oven are certain to be produced, for that is the oven's normal function, and the tenant therefore relies on receiving them when renting the courtyard. Manure from animals is uncertain: perhaps animals will not enter, and even if they do, perhaps no droppings will fall there. This also resolves the Sema's objection from siman 260 regarding a found object. The courtyard owner acquires manure because the tenant did not rely upon it, whereas a disabled deer is more commonly found in a field and the tenant may rely upon such finds. This appears to be the Mechaber's view. The Rambam himself, however, appears to follow the Tosafot cited in the preceding seif.
