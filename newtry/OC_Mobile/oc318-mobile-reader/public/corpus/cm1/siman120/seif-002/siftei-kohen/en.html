Here are your funds, tied and ready. See the Sema, seif katan 10. Although above, siman 74, seif 3, the Rama writes that a borrower is not discharged merely by saying, “Come and take your money,” that case is different. There the borrower did not tell him that the money had been tied up and set aside; the Rama merely states that it was tied, and he therefore remains liable for the reason explained there, namely that had a business opportunity arisen he might still have used it. Here, however, once he expressly tells the creditor that the money is tied and ready, it immediately enters the creditor’s domain and the borrower may no longer use it, so he is exempt. This also answers the difficulty raised from siman 198, seif 17. So too writes Raviah in the Mordechi, chapter Mi She’achazo. This exemption applies only where the money is actually available for immediate return and he placed it before the creditor, or in his house, and the creditor or depositor then departed. If he does not have the funds presently in hand and merely says, “I will bring you your deposit,” he is not exempt. Although Raviah holds even a depositary liable where the money is not presently available, he may agree that where the depositary says, “Take what is yours,” he is exempt regarding a deposit, as the Rama writes in siman 74. See Responsa Rabbi Shlomo Kohen, vol. 2, siman 99.
Some authorities disagree with this. Meaning, they exempt him even if he was negligent. This is also the view of Raviah in the Mordechi there, and the Sema evidently understood it this way, which is why he challenged the apparent contradiction between the Rama’s rulings. The Bach, however, writes in siman 74, seif 5, that “some authorities disagree” means that they hold the borrower liable, which is the view of Riva in the Mordechi. On that basis the Rama ruled in siman 74 that the borrower is liable. This is difficult, for Riva himself was uncertain, whereas Raviah was certain. Moreover, the Rama should have written here that the primary ruling follows the dissenting view, since in siman 74 he ruled that way without qualification. Rather, the cases differ, as explained above. As a matter of law, Maharshal also rules like Raviah that he is exempt even for negligence.
