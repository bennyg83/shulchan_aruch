[Shulchan Aruch] For he authorized him to do so. Note: If another person comes first and acquires the money, the matter requires analysis. Seemingly one may say that he acquires it as ownerless property, because the borrower has withdrawn his control from it and removed it from his possession, while the lender has not yet acquired it through any kinyan that would make this money his. This may be compared to the law below, siman 194, seif 2. I later found in Shitah Mekubetzet, Bava Metzia 71b, and in the Berlin edition page 126a, that he writes in the name of Rashba: If a Jew said to a gentile, “Place the money on the ground and be exempt,” and the gentile placed it there, the gentile withdrew from it and the Jew did not acquire it. If a second Jew then took it, he acquired it from ownerless property. See there. The practical law requires study.
