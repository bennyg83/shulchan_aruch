It is as though he threw it into a place of loss. Why is this reason necessary, rather than simply saying that since he told him, "according to the law of a get," and regarding a get she is not divorced according to all opinions? It may make a difference regarding a deposit. If he said, "Throw my deposit according to the law governing a deposit," and he threw it in this manner, and it was then lost through an unavoidable accident from there, if the reason he was not exempt were merely that this is ineffective under the law of gittin, the item would remain in the guardian's domain and he would be exempt for an unavoidable accident. Therefore the reason given is that it is as though he threw it into a place of loss. It is thus a case whose beginning was negligence and whose end was an unavoidable accident, for which he is liable. This is well understood.
