Your money is tied and ready. See the Beurim. During the term of a loan, even though the borrower may have the right to force early repayment upon the lender, he remains liable for the money. With loose deposited money, however, where the custodian is a paid custodian because he may use it, if he says, "Your money is tied and ready," he is exempt from theft and loss, though still liable for negligence. After the circumstances described below, he is exempt entirely. This applies specifically where the lender refused to accept the money and the borrower then said, "If so, I will take it home and leave it there tied." If he remained silent after the lender refused, he remains liable. Likewise, if he merely says, "Your money is tied, come to my house and I will give it to you," he remains liable even if the lender refuses. Only where he says, "Prepare yourself to receive it and I will bring it to you," is he exempt after the lender refuses to accept it.
