When does the preceding rule apply? When the lender said nothing and did not reveal that he refused to accept the money. Therefore, if the borrower threw it to him and it was lost, the borrower remains liable, because the lender may say, 'Had you handed it to me, I would have guarded it.' But if the borrower said, 'Here is your money,' the lender refused to accept it, and the borrower threw it before him in a place where repayment may properly be made, the borrower is exempt. He need not bring it to beit din, because payment made against the creditor's will is valid payment. Similarly, if he says, 'Your money is tied up in my house; come and take it,' and the lender refuses to accept it, then if it is stolen or lost, the borrower is exempt unless he was negligent.
