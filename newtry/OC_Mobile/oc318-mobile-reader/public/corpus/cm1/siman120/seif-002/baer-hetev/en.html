Was negligent. If the lender tells him, "I no longer wish to be even an unpaid bailee," he is exempt even for negligence. So writes the Mordechai, cited in Darkei Moshe. Sema.
They disagree. Meaning that according to the dissenting view the debtor is exempt even if he was negligent. This is the view of Raavyah and appears to be the Sema's understanding. The Bach, however, writes in siman 74, seif 5, that the dissenting view means the debtor is liable, following Riva, and therefore the Rama ruled in siman 74 that the debtor is liable. The Shach objects, because Riva himself was uncertain whereas Raavyah ruled definitively, and further, if the Rama intended to follow the dissenting view he should have stated here that it is the primary ruling, since he ruled without qualification in siman 74. Rather, this case differs as explained above. As a practical matter, Maharshal rules that the debtor is exempt even where he was negligent. Shach.
Of understanding. The wording specifically says a daughter of sufficient understanding, because with a borrower it is not enough to return the borrowed item to the lender's children or household members, as stated in siman 72, seif 31. A monetary loan appears comparable to borrowing an object because in both cases the borrower receives all the benefit. Therefore the phrase means a daughter who actively conducts household business. The Rashba states this explicitly. See siman 340, seif 8, and above siman 73, seif 8. Sema. The Shach writes that Maharshal disagrees and rules that with a monetary loan, even a daughter who conducts household affairs does not discharge the borrower. See Responsa Maharam Alshich 39.
