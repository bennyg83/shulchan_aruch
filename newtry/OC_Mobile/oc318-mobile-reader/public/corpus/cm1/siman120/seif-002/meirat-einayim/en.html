Your money is bundled in my house, etc. In the Perishah and Derishah I explained that this ruling must be reconciled with siman 198:17, where the Tur, and by implication the Mechaber, rule that when a seller retracts from a sale, the purchase money he received remains in his hands with the law of a loan. Even if he tells the owner of the money, 'Come take your money,' and the money is then lost through unavoidable circumstances, he remains liable. The same siman concludes in the name of the Ramah that a complete act of return is required, as with a loan. This shows that merely saying, 'Come take your money,' does not discharge a borrower. Rama likewise writes above in siman 74:3 that a borrower is not discharged by saying, 'Come and take your money.' Some explain that Rama there speaks only where the borrower says this before the due date, based on the words 'and it is within the time.' That cannot be correct, because then a custodian should likewise remain liable, while Rama there says that a custodian is exempt. Rather, the words mean that the term has ended and repayment has become due, yet the borrower remains liable for unavoidable loss. It therefore appears that the exemption here applies only where the borrower first offered the money directly, saying, 'Here is your money,' and when the lender refused to accept it, the borrower said, 'Since you will not receive it, I will take it home, keep it bundled there, and you may come after me whenever you wish and take it.' Thus the Tur and Mechaber's statement, 'and similarly if he said, Your money is bundled in my house,' refers back to the case immediately before, where he offered the money, the lender refused, and he threw it before him, thereby becoming exempt. Here too, instead of throwing it before him, he took it home after the lender refused. Alternatively, when they say, 'Your money is bundled in my house, come and take it,' they do not mean, 'Come to my house to fetch it.' Rather, they mean, 'Prepare yourself to receive it and I will give it to you, for your money is bundled in my house and I will bring it whenever you wish.' Only where the lender refuses such an offer is the borrower exempt.

The text deliberately says 'competent' because with a borrowed object it is not enough to return it into the hands of the lender's children or household members, as explained above in siman 72:31. I wrote there that a loan should be compared to borrowing an object, because in both cases all the benefit belongs to the borrower.
