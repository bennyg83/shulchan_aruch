If the creditor said, etc. This follows the rule concerning one who tells another to throw an item into the sea or into a place of loss. Liability depends upon whether the recipient accepted the risk or acted merely as directed. See citations 74 and 198.
And some say, etc. The dispute concerns whether directing the debtor to cast the money to a designated place is equivalent to receipt, and whether the creditor bears the resulting risk. The authorities compare the laws of a borrower, guardian, messenger, and one who destroys property at the owner's command. The ruling depends upon the precise language, whether the creditor said 'give it to me,' 'throw it,' or expressly accepted responsibility. See citations 40, 50, 73, 74, 130, 198, and 306.
But if he said, etc. Where the creditor expressly undertook responsibility, or the debtor acted only after a clear instruction that transfers the risk, the debtor is discharged. The distinctions follow the laws of agency and guardianship discussed in the cited passages. See citations 2, 72, 111, 129, 291, and 340.
Reuven, etc. This follows the rule on 93a. An item that originally came into one's possession for safekeeping does not become one received for destruction merely because the owner later told him to tear or destroy it. Likewise, if one accepted personal liability initially, a later stipulation does not undo that liability.
