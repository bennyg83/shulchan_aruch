Tur, seif 4, in the name of Ramban. Maggid Mishneh likewise cites him as having written so in a responsum; that responsum is cited by Baal HaTerumot in Shaar 50. He concludes that Hillel needed to institute depositing the money in a chamber only when payment was made outside the creditor's presence, as in the Mishnah in Arakhin 38b. But in the creditor's presence, payment is effective by delivery, following Rava's second version, Arakhin 32a, where all agree that forced payment is valid payment, etc. Rabbeinu Yerucham writes similarly. Tur adds that the same applies to a deposit: if one returns it against the owner's will in an inhabited place, the return is effective.
From the incident of the sesame seeds in Bava Metzia 49a, as Rif explains there. Maharshal objects in his glosses. Sema also brings proof from several places that with a loan it is not enough to say, "Come take your money." Sema therefore explains that the Tur and Mechaber's statement that the debtor is exempt refers back to the previous case, where he said, "Here is your money," the creditor refused to accept it, and the debtor threw it before him. They then add that the same applies if he did not throw it before him but brought it to the creditor's house and said, "Here is your money, tied up," etc. I likewise saw Mordechai in the name of Riva explain the case this way.
