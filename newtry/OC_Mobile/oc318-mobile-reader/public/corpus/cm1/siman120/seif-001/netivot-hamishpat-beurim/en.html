Throw me my debt. See Sema seif katan 1, who writes that it is unreasonable to say that the creditor meant that the debtor should send the money and accompany it on the road to guard it. This is difficult. According to Sema, if the creditor said, "Throw it into the sea," where one likewise cannot interpret his words as "throw it and guard it," he should not need to add "and be discharged." Likewise, if a minor was standing beside him and the creditor said, "Give it to this minor," the debtor should remain liable because the creditor supposedly meant "give it and guard it," which is wholly implausible. Moreover, if Sema's reason is that he means "throw it and guard it," then in a case of unavoidable accident the debtor should be exempt, yet the implication is that all the law of a borrower remains upon him until the money reaches the creditor. In my view the matter is simple. If he says, "Throw it into the sea," or "Place it in a public ownerless place," he must add "and be discharged," as is clear in Bava Kamma at the end of HaChovel, because when the item came into his hand under an obligation of safekeeping, he needs an express discharge. See also what I wrote in Even HaEzer siman 30 seif katan 18. For this reason, if a woman says, "Throw a maneh into the sea and I will become betrothed to you," or "and I will become obligated to you," it is ineffective. The rule of "give a maneh to so and so" operates through the law of an arev, and an arev does not become obligated where the money never reaches any person. It therefore appears that if one says, "Give it to a minor, deaf mute, or mentally incompetent person, and I will become obligated to you," or "and I will become betrothed to you," it is effective, because the money has reached a person. Tosafot in Kiddushin 19, s.v. Omer Adam, provide clear proof, writing that even if he said, "Give a maneh to so and so and I will become betrothed," it works, all the more so with his minor daughter. Thus if he says, "Give it to such an incompetent person and I will become obligated," he is certainly liable. In the case of an ordinary debt, however, if he merely says "Give a maneh to so and so" without saying "and I will become obligated," he has accepted no new liability. But if he says "Give my debt to so and so," he is certainly discharged, since his words themselves make clear that he accepts the transfer, and this is stronger than an express undertaking. The Shitah Mekubetzet in Bava Metzia 98 likewise explains sending through such persons by the law of arev, and states that the same applies when returning the item. Therefore, on return as well, sending through such persons works through the law of arev. See further what I wrote in siman 121 seif katan 4. By contrast, saying "place it in an ownerless place" does not work for kiddushin, and for discharge of a debt it likewise does not work unless he adds "and be discharged." The poskim distinguish between throwing into the sea, which is an ownerless place, and giving a maneh to a person, where the money remains guarded. Tosafot in Bava Kamma 9, s.v. VeL'Rav Yosef, also write that a minor, deaf mute, or mentally incompetent person is considered a guardian.
And he threw it. See Sema seif katan 12 through the words "or alternatively, for kiddushin and as a stringency." Meaning, this is where he says, "and I will become betrothed to you," as explained there in Beit Shmuel, Even HaEzer siman 30 seif katan 18.
