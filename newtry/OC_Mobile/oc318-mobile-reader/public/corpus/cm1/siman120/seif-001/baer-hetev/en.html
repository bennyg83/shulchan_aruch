See Responsa Rashach II, beginning of siman 11, and siman 101. As long as the debtor has not yet thrown the money, the lender may say, "I do not want you to throw it." Maharit, Choshen Mishpat 118. Knesset HaGedolah.
To guard it. Regarding gittin, the Tur and Shulchan Aruch there write that we do not rely upon the fact that she can guard the get when it lies outside her four cubits. Regarding kiddushin, however, we do rely upon that ability and she is betrothed, as stated in Even HaEzer 30. Although kiddushin too concerns personal status and may have lenient consequences if another man later betroths her, the present determination that she is betrothed is itself the stringent ruling, and therefore the law follows the underlying legal rule that the object is considered in her domain when she can guard it. Sema.
Can. Meaning either each one can guard it independently, or specifically both together can guard it while neither can do so alone. This appears from Tosafot. Sema.
Are liable. Meaning that the loss is borne by both, each for half. Sema.
To guard it. Meaning even together they cannot guard it. All these detailed rules apply even if the lender did not say, "and thereby be exempt," but merely told the debtor to throw it to him according to the law of a get. The same law applies according to all views, unlike the Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe. If he said, "Throw it according to the law of a get and thereby be exempt," then even where it cannot be guarded he is exempt. Sema.
