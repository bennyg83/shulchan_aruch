How so, if he threw it to him, etc. Tosafot discusses the relation between Rav, Rabbi Yochanan, and Shmuel concerning an item thrown near one party. Rambam distinguishes a Torah-invalid get, a rabbinically invalid get, and doubtful divorce. He understands Rabbi Yochanan not to dispute Rav. Shmuel's standard that the woman must be able to bend down and take the get is a stringency in divorce, not the general definition of proximity in acquisitions or debts. Rav's four-cubit rule remains independent, and Rabbi Yochanan addresses a case outside both parties' four cubits, where ability to guard determines proximity. If the husband entered first, even an item within her four cubits may remain close to him. Rosh treats Shmuel's statements as stringencies and applies them to doubtful proximity, not to an item solely within one party's four cubits. In kiddushin and debt, Shmuel's divorce stringency does not apply. See citations 3, 4, 30, 49, 50, 139, and 150.
