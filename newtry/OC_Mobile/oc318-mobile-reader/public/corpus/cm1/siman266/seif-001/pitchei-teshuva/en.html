As it is stated, "your brother's lost property." Rabbi Akiva Eiger, novellae to Bava Metzia 22b, considers a Jew who was guarding property of a non-Jew and accepted liability for its safekeeping, and the object was then lost. He writes that at first it seemed obvious that one who finds it must return it, even though the object itself belongs to the non-Jew, because by restoring it he saves the Jewish guardian from having to pay. According to Rabbi Shimon, who regards property that causes monetary liability as monetary property, the object itself is treated as the Jew's monetary interest. Even according to the Sages, who disagree with Rabbi Shimon, that only means the object is not legally the guardian's property; nevertheless, returning it saves Reuven's assets from liability. Rabbi Akiva Eiger records that his colleague Rabbi Moshe Mordechai Yisrael questioned this, arguing that according to the Sages the object may still not qualify as "your brother's lost property." He sought proof but leaves the matter requiring study.
