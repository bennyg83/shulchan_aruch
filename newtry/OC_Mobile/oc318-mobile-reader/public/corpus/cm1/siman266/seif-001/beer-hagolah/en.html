Alef) This is the language of the Rambam, chapter 11 of Hilkhot Gezelah VaAveidah, halachah 3, from the statement of Rav Chama bar Gurya in the name of Rav in the tenth chapter of Bava Kamma 113b.
Bet) A statement of Rav Yehudah in the name of Rav in Sanhedrin 76b, derived from the verse concerning adding the satiated to the thirsty. Rashi explains that one who returns a gentile's lost object for no valid reason presents the mitzvah of returning lost property as though it were merely a personal moral practice rather than a command of the Creator, since he performs it even toward one regarding whom he was not commanded. From Rambam's reason, that doing so improperly strengthens the gentile, it appears in my humble opinion that he understood Rav's statement in Sanhedrin as applying specifically to idolatrous gentiles, and not necessarily to contemporary gentiles who acknowledge the Creator and whose civil law requires return of lost property.
Gimel) From the incident of Shimon ben Shetach in the Yerushalmi, second chapter of Bava Metzia, and several similar incidents there. Midrash Tehillim, siman 12, likewise relates an incident involving a pious man who returned a valuable lost object to a noblewoman, who responded with praise of such integrity.
Dalet) From the baraita of Rabbi Pinchas ben Yair in Bava Kamma. The Beit Yosef explains that the case is where the object was found in a place with an Israelite majority and the gentile owner might think that an Israelite had taken it. In my humble opinion the text should read that an Israelite found it.
Heh) Yerushalmi in HaNizakin.
