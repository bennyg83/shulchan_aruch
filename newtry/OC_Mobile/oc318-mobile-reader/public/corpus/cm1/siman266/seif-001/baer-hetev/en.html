Permitted. The Tur writes: It is not only that so long as the lost item has not come into his hand he is not obligated to trouble himself to pursue and return it, but even if it came into his hand it is permitted to retain it. This is derived from the verse, 'for every lost item of your brother... and you find it,' which implies that it has already come into his hand, yet the verse specifies the lost item of your brother. Sema.
They bring in. The Tur writes: 'Everywhere, they bring in their utensils from fear of thieves together with the utensils of a Jew, for the sake of peaceful relations.' It appears that he holds that the Torah prohibited only retaining a lost item that fell from its owner unknowingly, when the owner does not know where it fell or into whose hands it came. But if the owner deliberately left his movable property and utensils in his courtyard or a similar place, never imagining that they would be lost from there, and a Jew then learns that thieves are coming to the city and will steal them, the objects do not yet have the status of a lost item. Therefore he may bring them into a protected place for the sake of peaceful relations. For this reason the Tur writes that it is permitted 'everywhere,' meaning even where there is no concern of desecration of Hashem's name and no opportunity for sanctification of Hashem's name. Although even regarding a Jew, if he deliberately left property in a designated place one need not return it, as stated in simanim 260 and 262, it was explained there that this applies only while it remains protected there, unlike a case where thieves subsequently arrive. The general rule is that according to the Tur, whenever the act is done for peaceful relations and indeed serves peaceful relations, it is permitted. Ibid.
