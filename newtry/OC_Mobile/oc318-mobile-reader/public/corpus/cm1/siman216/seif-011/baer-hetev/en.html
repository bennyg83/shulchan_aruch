Half. Maggid Mishneh explains Rambam's reasoning here and in the following law, where the seller retained two trees. Rambam holds that whenever one acts to reserve something for himself, he reserves generously. Therefore we raise his right by one level in each case. Had he sold three trees to another, the purchaser would acquire the land beneath them, the land between them, and an area around them of the prescribed width. When the seller retains the trees for himself, we enhance his reservation and say that he retained for himself half the field in which they are planted. Where two trees were sold, the purchaser acquires no part of the land itself. If the seller retained the trees for himself, however, we say that he also retained the land needed for them, namely the land beneath them and the related area. The 'some say' hold that even when he reserves the trees for himself, his power is not enhanced to the extent of giving him half the field. They agree, however, that even where he retained only two trees, he also has all the land needed for them, including beneath them and between them, because he retained them for himself. Ibid.
