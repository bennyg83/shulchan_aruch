One who sells land, etc. From here to the end of the siman is the Rambam’s wording. He understands the statement on 37a, “half the land,” literally as half the land for purposes of sale. Likewise the Gemara’s “obvious, etc.” there also refers to half the land, because it is placed next to that statement. He explains the case as one of three trees, following the Rif at the end of chapter 4. Rav Huna’s statement on 71a he interprets as granting only the land needed for the trees, from the phrase “although they said, etc.” This indicates that the novelty is not a greater rule for three trees but only that even two trees receive what is needed. The phrase “although, etc.” therefore means that specifically with three trees there is an independent right to land. In Hilchos To'en chapter 12, however, the Rambam writes concerning chazakah that one acquired only the land needed for the trees, as codified above siman 141 seif 19. Likewise regarding one who sold trees and retained the land, the Rambam writes that the owner of the trees receives the land appropriate for them, “as we explained,” which must refer to three trees. Since he also interprets the Gemara’s “obvious” case as three trees, Rashbam’s difficulty remains: with three trees everyone agrees that there is a right to land.
