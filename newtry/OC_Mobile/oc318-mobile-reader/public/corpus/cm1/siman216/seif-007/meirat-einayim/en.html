For they are two, etc. This is also Rambam's wording. It is stated as a reason: since the trees stand in this configuration, the plow cannot pass between them, and therefore the land is sold together with the trees. Rashi writes likewise.
From four cubits up to sixteen. Meaning, sixteen is included. The reason is that if they are less than four cubits apart, they do not have enough space for their roots to draw nourishment and are considered as though uprooted. If they are more than sixteen cubits apart, the intervening land is not subordinate to them because they do not require so much area for nourishment. What was written in the preceding seif, that the purchaser acquires the trees between them, refers to trees at least four cubits away from the three trees he purchased. Otherwise they are regarded as uprooted. This is from Nimukei Yosef. See the Derishah.
From the broad base. The Tur writes that the four and sixteen cubits are measured from the point where the trunk emerges from the ground, where it is thick. See the Perishah.
