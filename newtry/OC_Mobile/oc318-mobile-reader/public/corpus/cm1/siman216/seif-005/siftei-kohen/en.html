Land and palm trees, etc. See the Sema, seif katan 14. His apparent intent is that otherwise one could ask why the Rambam omitted the Gemara's law that if the seller owns palm trees he must give them, and therefore the Sema was forced into his interpretation. But how can one say that the buyer acquires all the palm trees? The Gemara says that if the seller has palm trees, he gives him two. According to both Rashbam and Tosafot he gives only two. Rashbam holds that he may give two from elsewhere, while Tosafot requires him to give two from this particular land. Although ordinarily a sale of 'palm trees' includes all of them, this case is weaker because the seller mentioned palm trees separately. The Ir Shushan's words are correct. The Rambam also follows Rashbam, that if the seller owns palm trees he must give two even from another place. By writing that even if he had no palm trees, he may purchase them if he wishes, the Rambam necessarily implies that if he has them elsewhere he must provide two. This law was obvious to the Rambam, and his principal novelty was that when the seller has none, he may buy them and thereby maintain the sale. In any event, the Rambam cannot follow Tosafot's view that this case is uniquely limited to palm trees on this land, for then the novelty should not have been omitted.
