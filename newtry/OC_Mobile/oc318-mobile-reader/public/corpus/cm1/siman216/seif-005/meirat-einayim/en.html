Land and palms I sell you. It appears that the Rambam, who is the source of this law, holds that if the land already contains palms, the seller need not give the buyer additional palms elsewhere. His statement “land and palms” is not superfluous even though palms are ordinarily included in sale of the land, because the case is where he did not show the buyer the land at the time of sale. He therefore had to say that he was selling land and palms. This differs from the Ir Shushan, who writes that even under this opinion the seller must give two additional palms. If the land contains no palms, however, the purchaser cannot say that the sale was mistaken because he thought he would receive land containing palms. So long as the seller did not say “land with palms,” he may satisfy the sale by giving land without palms and two palms elsewhere. If the seller does not provide two palms elsewhere, the Rambam holds that the purchaser may annul the entire transaction. The alternative opinion cited by Rama disagrees on two points. It treats “land and palms” as two separate sales. The land is sold as it stands, whether it contains many palms or none at all, and the additional phrase “and palms” means that the seller must give two palms in addition to the land. If the seller owns two palms elsewhere, he is compelled to deliver them. If he does not own palms, it is proper for him to keep his word and purchase two from others, but he cannot be compelled, because a sale of property not in his possession resembles a sale of something not yet in existence. Nevertheless, failure of the palm sale does not allow the purchaser to rescind the land sale. The value of two palms is deducted from the price and the balance remains payable for the land.
Canan, in any case, said P.D., “The ground of the palms of the palms of the palms of the earth shall not be exalted in any matter that he said to him, even though he has a lot of light, and that he is written in his name, that he may not be cursed, but that he does not want to do so.”
