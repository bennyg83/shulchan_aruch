Lamed) There, halachah 13, based on the Gemara on Bava Batra 69b.
Mem) The Raavad writes in his gloss that the seller must purchase two palm trees. He states that this is the primary explanation of the Rif. The Maggid Mishneh cites him that one who sells to another an item commonly available for purchase, even if he does not currently possess it, is obligated to acquire it for the buyer. We likewise rule that one may contract at the market rate even when he does not presently possess the goods, and this is also stated in the Tosefta. The Baal HaIttur writes similarly in the name of Rabbenu Nissim. The Kesef Mishneh distinguishes Rambam's ruling: when the seller has no palm trees, the subject is something not yet in existence and the buyer therefore acquires nothing; we cannot compel the seller to purchase palm trees and thereby turn the transaction into one concerning an existing object. By contrast, when the seller later gives him two palm trees, he is in effect saying, "I am fulfilling my original stipulation." Since the trees now exist and are in his possession, it is as though they had been in his possession at the time of the original sale, and the buyer must uphold the transaction. Nevertheless, it requires study why the Mechaber did not record the view of the Geonim above as a "some say" opinion.
The Tur, seif 7, and the Maggid Mishneh write likewise in the name of Rabbenu Shmuel.
