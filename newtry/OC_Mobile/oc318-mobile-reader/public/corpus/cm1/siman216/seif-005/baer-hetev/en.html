And palm trees. It appears that the Rambam holds that if this field already contains palm trees, the seller need not provide additional palm trees elsewhere. The reason he nevertheless said, 'land and palm trees,' even though palm trees ordinarily pass with the land, is that the seller had not shown him the land at the time of sale, so he needed to say that he was selling land and palm trees. If the land contains no palms, the buyer cannot rescind on the ground that he thought he would receive land containing palms. Unless the seller said 'land with palm trees,' the seller may give him land without palms plus two palms elsewhere. If the seller does not provide two additional palms, however, the Rambam holds that the buyer may cancel the sale entirely. The Tur cites him this way. The dissenting opinion disagrees in two respects. It holds that when the seller says, 'I sell you land and palm trees,' these are two separate sales. The word 'land' refers to the land in its present condition, so whether or not palms stand upon it, the buyer takes it as it is. The phrase 'and palm trees' means that in addition to the land the seller must provide two palms. If the seller owns two palms elsewhere, beit din compels him to fulfill the sale and give them. If he owns none, it is proper that he keep his word and purchase two from others, but he cannot be compelled because they are not in his possession and it resembles selling something not yet in existence. Although the palm-tree sale is not fulfilled, the buyer cannot therefore rescind the land sale, because they are separate sales. Instead, the price is reduced by the value of two palms. Thus far the Sema. The Taz likewise writes that according to the Rambam these are not two sales. Accordingly, if one person sells two items together for a single aggregate price and it later turns out that one was not in his possession, reason dictates that the second sale is also void, because the buyer can say: Since I included them in one price, it was one sale, and perhaps the missing item was especially valuable to me and I do not wish to take the other according to ordinary appraisal. All the more so with land, which is not subject to onaah. A case came before us where someone sold another two seats in a synagogue for one price and it later emerged that he owned only one. We ruled that this is a dispute between the Rambam and Rashbam, and we lack power to extract money, so the entire sale is void. Taz. See the Shach on this, Responsa Mahari ben Lev, part 2, siman 37, and Imrei Binah, folio 96a.
Manner. Meaning, even if he said 'land of palm trees,' this is no worse than selling land without qualification, where we rule that all trees standing in it are included. It also teaches that he takes them regardless of how many palms are there, even if there are many. This excludes the view of Rabbeinu Yonah cited by the Tur that by saying 'and palm trees' the seller meant to limit the buyer to only two palms. See there. Sema.
