One who sells an orchard to his fellow, etc. This is the Rambam's language in chapter 24 of Hilchot Mekhirah. The Gemara in HaMokher Et HaBayit says more generally: one who sells land to his fellow should write, “Acquire the palms, fig trees, branches, and shoots,” and although the purchaser would acquire them even without such wording, it beautifies the deed. It appears that the Rambam specifically chose an orchard to teach a greater novelty. Even though an orchard is especially designated for trees, it is still advisable to write explicitly that all its trees are sold with it. All the more so with ordinary land or a field, which is not devoted exclusively to trees, although the trees in it are also sold unless excluded by the special rules concerning a grafted carob or sycamore stump. The Gemara and Rambam's wording might imply that all kinds of trees should be itemized. The Tur, however, says at the beginning of this siman that one who buys a field would do well to state in the deed that he buys it “and all the trees in it,” implying that this general phrase is enough. The Tur precisely says “it is good to specify,” teaching that the Gemara's “he must write” is not an absolute requirement but sound advice for the buyer. The Rambam and Mechaber likewise conclude that it is merely beautification of the deed.
It is beautification of the deed. The Gemara likewise calls it beautification of the deed. Rashbam explains that even if the matter later comes before an inexperienced beit din, they will award the trees because they are explicit in the document. One might ask that even now an inexperienced beit din may err and award the purchaser a grafted carob or sycamore stump, because the deed broadly says that the seller retained nothing. The answer is that they are unlikely to err so far. Alternatively, inexperienced courts tend to rule conservatively. Whatever the deed does not clearly prove was sold, they place upon the claimant the burden of proof. Thus, even if the deed says “all the trees,” they may conclude that a sycamore stump and similar important items are not included. See the Perishah for further discussion.
