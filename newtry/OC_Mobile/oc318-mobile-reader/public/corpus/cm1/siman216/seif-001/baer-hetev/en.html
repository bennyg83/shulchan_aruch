Rashbam explains that even if the matter comes before an erroneous beit din, they will award these items to him because they are expressly written in the deed. One might ask that even now there is concern that an erroneous beit din will rule that the purchaser receives even a grafted carob tree and a mature sycamore stump, since the deed simply states that the seller retained nothing for himself. One may answer that even an erroneous court would not err to that extent. Alternatively, courts that are not expert in the law generally rule stringently, and regarding anything not proven from the deed to have been sold, they generally rule that the burden of proof rests upon the claimant. Therefore, even if the deed says that he sold all the trees, they would say that a sycamore stump and the like are not included. Sema.
