(Seif 4.) The reason given is that a seller sells generously. This is difficult, for why is that reason necessary? Even without it, he retained only the vines, since vines are more naturally included in the term “trees,” as the Rashbam states explicitly. The Sema in seif katan 7 explains this way even according to the Rambam, while in seif katan 9 he writes that the principle of generous sale is invoked to explain why we do not say that the buyer acquired both species. According to that approach, whenever one sells an item under a general designation and owns two things, one more naturally included in that designation than the other, we should say that he must give both because a seller sells generously, just as here with trees, even though one species is more naturally called a tree. This is certainly incorrect. The Gemara and siman 215, seif 6, state explicitly that this rule applies where the seller should have specified what he intended. We do not say there that generous sale causes the buyer to acquire everything. Generosity affects only an item that is certainly sold, causing it to be acquired with all its appurtenances, as the Sema himself writes. Here, therefore, the buyer should acquire only the species more naturally included in “trees,” namely vines. It consequently appears to me that the Rambam does not accept the premise that vines are more naturally called trees than palms. Rather, both are equally included in that designation. There was therefore room to say that the seller retained both, and the principle that a seller sells generously teaches that he retained only the lesser species, namely vines, because palms are more valuable. Although the Gemara in Bava Kamma says, “a vine buys a palm, but a palm does not buy a vine,” Tosafot explain there that in one respect the palm is superior; see there. Accordingly, if one says, “I sell you trees,” and the property contains vines and palms, he must indeed give both, because both share one designation. According to the Rashbam, however, vines are more naturally included in “trees,” and even one who sells “trees” need not give the palms. Generous sale does not alter that result. This is a dispute between the Rashbam and the Rambam.
