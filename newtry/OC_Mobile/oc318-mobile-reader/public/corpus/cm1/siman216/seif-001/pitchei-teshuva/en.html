See Responsa Avodat HaGershuni, siman 26, who writes that even if the deed explicitly states that the seller reserved a right of passage for himself, he nevertheless did not thereby transfer the other carob trees. Rashbam there writes that where there is nothing else for the extra wording to include, we do not say that because there is nothing to add, the speaker intended instead to diminish what would otherwise be excluded. One might suggest that this applies only under the Talmudic rule that the holder of the deed has the weaker hand, whereas nowadays our deeds state that they are not to be treated as asmachta or as ordinary boilerplate, and that in every uncertainty the holder of the deed has the stronger hand, as stated above, siman 42, seif 10.
