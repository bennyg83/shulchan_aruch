Dalet) There, chapter 24, halachah 12. The Maggid Mishneh writes that the laws through the principle that a seller sells generously are explicit there on Bava Batra 69b without dispute.
Heh) Rav Yosef ibn Migash explains that grapevines are considered more significant than palm trees, as appears at the beginning of Bava Kamma 32a, and therefore we follow the seller's intent and assume that he reserved the grapevines because of their importance.
Vav) This gloss belongs here.
Zayin) This is the correct text, as appears in Rambam and the Tur.
Chet) The Maggid Mishneh explains that Rambam gives a reason for the case where the property contains palm trees and ordinary trees, or grapevines and ordinary trees. We do not say that every tree was reserved; only what can ordinarily be called simply "a tree" is reserved, because a seller sells generously.
Rabbenu Shmuel explains that the case concerns a grafted carob tree.
Tet) This is explicit there at the beginning of 70a.
Yod) The Maggid Mishneh writes likewise in the name of Rabbenu Shmuel and Rav Yosef ibn Migash, bringing proof that when there are no ordinary trees but only palm trees, we say that since there are no other trees, the stipulation necessarily referred to those palm trees.
Kaf) This too is stated there in the Gemara.
