In order to reserve a path for himself. See Responsa Avodat HaGershuni, siman 26. He writes that even if the deed expressly states that the seller reserved a right of passage for himself, that does not imply that he acquired or retained other ruined areas not otherwise included. Rashbam explains that where there is no additional matter to which surplus language can apply, one does not infer that the wording was intended to diminish the purchaser's rights. Nor can one argue that this was only the Talmudic rule because ordinarily the holder of a document has the lower hand, whereas contemporary deeds commonly include clauses strengthening the holder's interpretation in cases of doubt. Rashbam's discussion regarding "land and palm trees" shows that the same interpretive rule is applied even in favor of the purchaser. See there.
