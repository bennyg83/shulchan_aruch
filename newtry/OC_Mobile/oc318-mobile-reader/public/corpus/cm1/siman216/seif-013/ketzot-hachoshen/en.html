And this one took possession of the trees while the other took possession of the land. In chapter Chazakat HaBatim, Bava Batra 37, the Gemara states: one person took possession of the trees and another of the land. Rav Zevid says the first acquires the trees and the second the land. Rav Pappa challenges: if so, the owner of the trees has no right in the land, and the landowner can tell him, 'Uproot your trees, take them, and go.' Rav Pappa therefore says that the first acquires the trees and half the land, and the second acquires half the land. Rashbam explains that this is not a case of three-year chazakah. Rather, one seller sold the land to one person and the trees to another, and each performed an act of chazakah on his portion, such as making a small improvement in the land and pruning the trees. Tosafos ask that if so, why does the Gemara speak specifically of chazakah rather than document, money, or chalipin? They further ask that Rav Zevid himself agrees that while the tree lives, the tree owner has rights in the land just like one who purchases two trees. Rav Zevid means only that when the tree dies he has no land in which to plant another. Why, then, does Rav Pappa challenge that the landowner can say, 'Uproot your tree and go,' when during the tree's life the tree owner unquestionably has land rights? Rashbam explains Rav Pappa's statement as referring to after the tree dies, but that still leaves the question: what is Rav Pappa challenging, when Rav Zevid himself holds exactly that? Tosafos therefore offer another explanation.
Rashbam's explanation can be defended through a question discussed by Mishneh LaMelech, chapter 1 of Hilchot Mechirah. Earlier scholars were uncertain about a person who rented his house for a fixed term and afterward wished, during that term, to rent or sell it to another person effective after the first rental ends. Can the second person acquire through chazakah of locking, fencing, or breaching, given that the house and all its present use belong to the first tenant until the term ends? Does the first tenant's permission enable the second to acquire through chazakah? They connected the issue to Responsa Rivash, siman 510, cited by the Mechaber in siman 312. Maharam Mintz, siman 39, appears to take it as obvious that such a chazakah does not work. He writes that if the property is presently leased to Reuven until the end of the year, the city owners cannot presently convey to another person by the latter's living there for the following year. Even if the city said, 'Acquire it from now,' and its ownership of the body of the house enables such a transfer in principle, an ordinary private purchaser who must make an act of chazakah cannot presently perform chazakah in something that is not now available to him.
Accordingly, Rav Pappa's challenge to Rav Zevid is clear. If the tree owner has a right in the land until the tree dies, how did the person who performed chazakah on the land acquire the land beneath the trees for the period after the trees die? That ground is not presently his to acquire; it presently belongs for use to the tree owner. His act of locking, fencing, or breaching cannot acquire a future right in land that is not presently available to him. Thus, if you say that the person who performed chazakah acquired all the land, including the land under the trees after their death, it necessarily follows that the tree owner has no land right at all even during the trees' lives. If so, the landowner could tell him even now, 'Uproot your trees and go.' This also explains why the Gemara specifically uses chazakah rather than money or document. Those other acquisitions can operate even where the land is not presently available to the buyer but will become his later. Rav Pappa's difficulty arises specifically with chazakah, because an act of chazakah works only on a right presently capable of acquisition.
