He is the owner of the elites whose infiltration is his own and is exported like him, but he rises from heirs under the ground even though he drew from his melan from abroad out below the ground and the land is of the seller:
Because it has no trunk. The Mechaber's wording is taken exactly from the Rambam in chapter 24 of Hilchot Mekhirah. From the beginning of his discussion it is clear that what emerges from the ground is called the trunk. Accordingly, when he later says “because it has no trunk,” he means that a branch does not ordinarily grow from the trunk, meaning from the stock of the palm. The Maggid Mishneh likewise explains that no new trunk growth rises from the stock of a palm in a manner that would belong to the owner of the tree; anything that rises is from the roots and belongs to the landowner. His phrase must therefore be understood as referring to a branch rising from the stock. Even if such a branch unusually emerges from the palm's stock, it does not belong to the purchaser, because his original intent did not include such growth. Rashbam explains the Gemara similarly. This appears to apply specifically to one who purchased one or two palms. One who purchased three palms acquires the land around and between them, and therefore all growth from them belongs to him.
