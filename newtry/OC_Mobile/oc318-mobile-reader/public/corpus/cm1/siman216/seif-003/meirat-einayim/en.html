And the rest belong to the purchaser, etc. The general rule is that when one sells a field, all the palms and trees in it are sold with it, as explained above. When he says “except,” he retains whatever he expressly excluded. Therefore, if the palm he excludes is a good palm, we can say that he intended to reserve only that one and the rest are sold. But if the palm he excluded is a poor one, we assess his intent differently. He did not mean only that tree, but rather intended to reserve all trees at least as good as it, as though he had said, “Even this poor palm I reserve for myself, and certainly those better than it.” We likewise say that he intended to reserve even trees somewhat inferior to it, because he did not descend to such precision as to identify the very worst tree. The alternative opinion cited by Rama holds that since he specifically said “except” regarding this tree and did not say so regarding the worse ones, he did not reserve the inferior trees, and even trees equal to it were not reserved. Since by law all trees were included in the sale, whenever there is any reasonable basis to say that a tree was not reserved, it remains included in the sale.
