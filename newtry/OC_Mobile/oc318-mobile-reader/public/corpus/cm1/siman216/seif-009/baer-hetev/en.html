Another explanation is that there, where the branches had grown larger in the ordinary way, the buyer who claims that they did not grow bears the burden of proof. Here, however, a new branch growing close to the ground and then becoming covered with earth is uncommon. If the parties litigate, the buyer's claim that he originally purchased three trees will be more plausible, because such low growth is unusual. Sema.

Although shade is harmful to an irrigated field, since the buyer owns no land, the seller has subjected his field to all needs of the trees, and the buyer purchased them on that understanding. This is unlike one who bought three trees and acquired land with them. There the seller did not subject any additional part of his land, so he may compel the buyer to cut branches that spread beyond their extent at the time of sale, since their shade harms the irrigated field.
