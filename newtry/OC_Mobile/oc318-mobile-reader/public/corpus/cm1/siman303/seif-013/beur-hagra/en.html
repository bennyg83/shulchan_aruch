It overpowered him, etc. As stated on Bava Metzia 36b. Although that interpretation is needed specifically according to Abaye, whereas Rava does not require it, Ramban explains that Abaye and Rava dispute Rabba's position, and Rabba said, 'What could he have done?' We, however, rule like Rav Pappa, as stated in seif 11, and the guardian should not have allowed it to ascend. See Rosh there.
(Supplement) It overpowered him, etc. See Baer HaGolah and Rosh on the Mishna, citing Raavad, and see siman 291:11. According to Raavad, the Gemara needed this interpretation only for Abaye, because according to Rava the question 'should he have held it, etc.' is sufficient. Tosafot, however, explain that Rava assumes the animal overpowered him when ascending and again when descending. Abaye asks that if so, the latter clause, where he brought it up, should likewise exempt him, because bringing it up to graze was not negligent. The Gemara answers that there the fall occurred without the animal overpowering him. This explains Rif's wording, 'and we established it, etc.,' which is less suitable under Ramban's interpretation. (End of supplement.) See 81.
