(Seif 10) The matter is not known, etc. The Beit Yosef explains that, presumably, he was able to save it, as the Sema writes in seif katan 7, and he limits the ruling specifically to a paid custodian. This is difficult, for in monetary law we do not follow the majority, as the Tur and the Mechaber write in siman 232, seif 21, and as I cited in siman 260, seif 8, in the name of Tosafot. Here too, why should we extract payment from the custodian rather than consider the possibility that no hired rescuers could have been found, or that they would have been unable to save it? Moreover, I do not know any source for this ruling in the Talmud. It appears to me that the reason is that the Torah exempted a custodian only through an oath. One who cannot take the required oath must pay, for as soon as he says that the item was lost he becomes obligated to swear, and here he cannot swear. According to this, there is no distinction between an unpaid custodian and a paid custodian.
