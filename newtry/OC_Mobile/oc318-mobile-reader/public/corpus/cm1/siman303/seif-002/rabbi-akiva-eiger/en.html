[Shulchan Aruch] And some say that even if he guarded properly. Note: There are three approaches. Tosafos, chapter 3 of Bava Metzia, hold that in a complete unavoidable accident, such as where the animal was placed in an embankment or the custodian was overcome by sleep due to illness, even a paid custodian is exempt. The difference between a paid and unpaid custodian is only where he closed the door sufficiently to withstand an ordinary wind but not every wind, or where the owner entered at the time people ordinarily enter, which involves some negligence. In a complete unavoidable accident he is entirely exempt. Nimukei Yosef, and Rabbeinu Yerucham in the name of Ramban, distinguish between leaving the animal in an embankment, where he is liable, and being overcome by illness, where he is exempt because at that time he has no duty to guard at all. Rashba, Rosh, Tur, and Tosafos in chapter HaKones hold that he is liable for every unavoidable circumstance unless the accident affected the object itself, such as armed bandits taking it. But where the unavoidable circumstance affected the custodian’s body, such as illness, he remains liable.
