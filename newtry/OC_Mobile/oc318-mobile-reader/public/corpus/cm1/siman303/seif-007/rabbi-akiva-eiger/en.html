[Shach, letter 8] Nevertheless, even an unpaid guardian. Note: At first glance one could say that from that point onward he becomes a paid guardian because of the perutah of Rav Yosef, since he is occupied with a mitzvah by having preceded, for payment, to save the animal. This accords with the Rashba, who holds that even a single perutah makes him a paid guardian. According to Tosafot, however, an unpaid guardian of a lost object becomes a paid guardian because of spreading and shaking it out, and that rationale does not apply here. According to what the Ritva writes, cited in Shitah Mekubetzet to Bava Metzia 82 and at the end of the volume, page 145c, here he would not be a paid guardian. Yet in the Ritva's novellae at the end of chapter 6 of Shevuot he writes another explanation, according to which one may say that here he is a paid guardian. Examine carefully.
