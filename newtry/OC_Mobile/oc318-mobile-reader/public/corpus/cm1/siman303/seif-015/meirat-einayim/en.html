And it died in the thief's house, he is liable. One might ask why the case specifically says it was stolen from the marsh. Even if stolen from his house, a paid custodian is liable for theft. The case can concern an armed robber, for whose taking he would be exempt if the animal had been in his house. When he left it in the marsh, however, the matter began with negligence even though it ended in unavoidable accident. He becomes liable from the moment the armed robber takes it from the marsh, because had it remained in his house we might say the robber would not have come and taken it. Once liability attached through the theft, the later natural death in the robber's possession does not exempt him. This also explains why the Tur and Mechaber repeat the law here regarding a paid custodian although they already wrote it in siman 291 regarding an unpaid custodian.
