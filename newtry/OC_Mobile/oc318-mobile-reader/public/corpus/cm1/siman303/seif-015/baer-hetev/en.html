The thief. One might ask why the law specifically says that it was stolen from the marsh. Even if it was stolen from his house, a paid custodian should be liable. The answer is that the case includes even theft by an armed robber, for which he would not be liable had the animal been stolen from his house. When it was stolen from the marsh, however, he became liable immediately upon the theft, because we say that had it remained in his house the armed robber would not have come. Once he became liable for the theft, he is not subsequently exempted because the animal died in the thief's hands, since the matter began with negligence and ended in an unavoidable accident. This also explains why the Tur and Shulchan Aruch had to repeat this law here regarding a paid custodian, even though they had already stated it in siman 291 regarding an unpaid custodian. Shach.
