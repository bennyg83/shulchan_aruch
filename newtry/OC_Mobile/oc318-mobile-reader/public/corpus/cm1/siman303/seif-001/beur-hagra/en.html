A.S. Like a curse in the P.A. and C. K.C.:
Reuven, etc. Although at first he did not undertake the task for compensation, once Reuven told him that the surplus would belong to him and Shimon remained silent, Shimon immediately became a paid guardian. This resembles a guardian of a lost article according to Rav Yosef. Even Rabba disagrees only because he does not regard that small benefit as significant, as Tosafot explain. The Mishna in the second chapter asks, 'What shall be done with their proceeds, etc.,' and the Gemara holds him liable even though he never actually used that benefit. Rabbi Akiva disagrees only because he may not use the money. The same principle appears at the end of the third chapter regarding a moneychanger. Shimon's silence constitutes acceptance, as Bava Batra 138a states that where one was silent and only later protested, all agree, etc.
