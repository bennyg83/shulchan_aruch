Up to the value of the animal. The Gemara explains that the owner still prefers the animal to be saved because otherwise he must trouble himself to buy another and because this particular animal is familiar and accustomed to his household. Nevertheless, if rescuers will not save it for an amount equal to the animal's value, the custodian need not add payment for the inconvenience of buying another animal or for its familiarity. Perhaps the owner would not reimburse that extra expense. This also explains the later rule concerning a shepherd who says, "I saved it," and relies on a migo. If, by claiming it was torn apart, he could have retained only the animal's value, for example ten gold coins, while his present claim sought ten plus another gold coin for labor, the migo would fail. We must therefore say that the owner is not obligated to reimburse an additional labor charge beyond the animal's value, and correspondingly the custodian need not spend beyond that value.
