Perhaps non-Jews entered and stole them. See Responsa Chut HaShani, siman 81. He writes that one who examines the Beit Yosef will see that this applies specifically where thieves are commonly present. The Beit Yosef writes: "Perhaps they were stolen by non-Jews who go about stealing utensils from houses and shops before they burn; he is therefore liable to pay, unless those non-Jews who go about are armed robbers." Thus he repeats the expression "non-Jews who go about," meaning that at the time of a fire it is common for non-Jews to circulate in search of things to steal, since people are preoccupied with the fire and do not pay attention to them. Otherwise, however, we do not presume theft and do not impose liability upon a custodian merely because theft is possible, as stated above at the end of siman 298 in the gloss. See there further. At first glance this requires study in light of Terumat HaDeshen, siman 353, cited by the Sema above, siman 291, seif katan 12. Perhaps Chut HaShani follows the view of Maharshal, Chelek 8, siman 89, cited in Responsa Zikhron Yosef, siman 1, letter 12, who disputes the aforementioned Terumat HaDeshen and writes that the party in possession may say, "I maintain" in accordance with the authorities who exempt him. See there. See also Netivot HaMishpat on these words of Rama. He concludes that according to the Shach, seif katan 4, who rules principally like the authorities who exempt a custodian when the theft itself occurred through circumstances beyond his control, the custodian is exempt here as well. This is straightforward.
