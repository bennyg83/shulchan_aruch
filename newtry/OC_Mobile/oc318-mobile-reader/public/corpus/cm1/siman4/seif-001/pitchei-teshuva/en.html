To take a pledge for his debt. Shaar Mishpat writes that it appears that if the debtor denies the debt, the creditor may seize a pledge from him without witnesses, so that he will be believed by virtue of a migo that he could have denied having seized it. The prohibition against taking a pledge on one's own applies where one can obtain the pledge through beit din. Where, however, he cannot collect through beit din, he may seize property himself, as explained below, siman 97, seif 15, concerning a beit din agent entering a debtor's house to take a pledge. He adduces a strong proof from siman 61, seif 6, concerning one who cannot find a judge willing to hear his case. See there further. Ketzot HaChoshen, however, both here and in siman 97, inclines to prohibit this. He concludes that even according to those who permit entry into the house where there is no other way to obtain payment, as stated by the Tur there, that permission is specifically for beit din, whose intent is for the sake of Heaven. They are authorized to do so, but the individual creditor is not. Financial loss does not permit one to transgress Torah law, and Hashem will make good the financial loss incurred through observance of a mitzvah. See there.
