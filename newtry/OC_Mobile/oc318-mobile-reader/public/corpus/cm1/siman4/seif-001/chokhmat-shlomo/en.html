Seif 1. In the gloss: “But mere seizure, where he took it as collateral,” etc. Note: See Ketzot HaChoshen, who writes that even a deposit in one’s possession may not be withheld on account of a claim, based upon the holy Zohar, and he brings support from the Ritva in the chapter HaKotev. In truth, however, the Gemara itself appears to permit such conduct expressly. In Shabbat, chapter HaShoel, 148a, regarding Rava bar Ulla, Rashi explains that after Yom Tov he would take some object from the other person and retain it. Since Rava acted this way, this is a concrete precedent and clearly indicates that such conduct can be permitted. One cannot say that he took the object as an actual purchase and merely withheld its price, for Rashi would then have written that he withheld the money. Rashi instead says that he “retained it,” referring to the object itself. The implication is that he did not acquire it as a purchase, but took an item as a deposit or loan and retained it. Since Rava did so, this stratagem was permissible. It is possible, however, that a genuine deposit is more stringent. Where from the outset he never intended to accept the item as a true deposit but only as a device to secure his claim, one cannot say that he must perform a complete act of restitution, since the arrangement was never intended as an ordinary deposit. But where he initially accepted it as a genuine deposit, without any intention of withholding it, once it entered his possession as a deposit he is obligated to return it fully and may not detain it. In this way the teaching of the holy Zohar can be reconciled with our Gemara, and the ruling of Ketzot HaChoshen can be maintained. See also Bava Kamma, where it is apparent that if a person has a servant or maidservant in his household and fears that the servant will cause him loss, for example because the servant is known as a thief, or where he wishes to expel someone from his house and that person refuses to leave, he may even strike him and may execute judgment for himself. This is apparent from the discussion on 28a concerning a thieving servant. I later saw that this law is explicit below in Shulchan Aruch, siman 421, seif 14, in the Rama. Indeed, the later authorities raised a difficulty from there to our case. This is not the place to elaborate. Perhaps Hashem will grant me the opportunity to return to it elsewhere.
