Proof. The Shach writes that the Mechaber stated this ruling on the basis of his discussion in the Beit Yosef defending the Ramah and rejecting the Tur. Responsa Masat Binyamin likewise argues at length for the Ramah. In the Shach's view, however, the Tur is primary and the holder of the receipt prevails. He rejects all of Masat Binyamin's proofs. Masat Binyamin argued that otherwise two people with the same name could never lend to anyone, since the borrower might persuade the other namesake to give him a receipt. The Shach answers that we do not presume wickedness. Witnesses who write such a receipt would certainly distinguish the parties by adding identifying names, because where two people with the same name are already known in the city the witnesses are certainly aware of it. See Responsa Rashach, part 3, siman 96. The Taz likewise disputes Masat Binyamin and writes that, as he explained in siman 42, seif 8, practical halachah should be fixed according to the Tur, that an undifferentiated receipt remains valid.
