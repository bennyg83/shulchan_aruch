But if they are triply identified in the document. Shach, seif katan 17, follows Tur and rules in favor of the holder of the receipt. In my view the law follows Mechaber. Suppose one borrower borrowed from many creditors, or many borrowers borrowed from many creditors, and witnesses say merely that one creditor holds a paid document but cannot identify which one. Certainly each creditor swears and collects; this is not meaningful testimony against any specific document. Only when witnesses identify a particular creditor and borrower do we treat all that creditor's deeds against that borrower as presumptively paid, as explained in siman 54. Likewise, in siman 31 two separate creditors each collect even though one of their documents must be invalid, because each deed has its own presumption. The rule that an untripled receipt defeats both same-name documents must therefore concern documents that themselves are not triply identified. The Mishnah's advice, 'How shall they do? They should triple their names,' means that proper tripling in the document prevents an untripled receipt from defeating it, in accordance with Mechaber and Rama. This is a rabbinic enactment. Why did the Sages allow an untripled receipt to defeat an untripled deed? Because otherwise, if the borrower's copy of the debt instrument were lost, he could never protect himself through a receipt. An untripled receipt would be useless, while a tripled receipt identifying one creditor could be manipulated if the untripled debt instrument were later transferred to the other namesake. The Sages therefore made an untripled receipt effective against an untripled document and placed the loss on the creditor who failed to follow their advice to distinguish the names in the deed. A properly tripled creditor did not act negligently and should not lose through an untripled receipt. This also explains why the ordinary cases of uncertain deposits do not require payment to everyone where the claimants themselves could have avoided the uncertainty. The practical law is therefore clearly like Mechaber.
