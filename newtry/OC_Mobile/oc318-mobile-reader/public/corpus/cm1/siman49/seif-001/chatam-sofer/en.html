Sema, seif katan 21, "and not concerning his father." Note: see Ramban on Parashat Noach, on the phrase "the brother of Yefet the elder," where he writes that the descriptive title always refers to the person being discussed, as in "Yeshayahu son of Amotz the prophet." See there. This requires study, since Amotz too was a prophet, as explained in the first chapter of Megillah. It also appears contrary to the explicit Gemara in Sanhedrin 69b, where Shem is younger than Yefet without dissent. It would seem that where the matter is well known and there is no room for error, such as "David son of Yishai, king" or "Yehoshua son of Yehotzadak, the High Priest," one should not interrupt the principal name by writing "David the king son of Yishai." Yet that is because a son is identified through his father, and they should not be separated when the matter is known. By contrast, regarding Shem and Yefet, even if we adopt the Gemara's initial assumption that Cham and Yefet were listed in birth order, so that it was already known that Shem was first, Scripture still should not have said "the brother of Yefet the elder," but rather "Shem the elder, brother of Yefet," since one brother is not normally identified through another. Rather, this proves that "the elder" refers to Yefet, and Scripture listed them in order of wisdom. But an honorific such as "may he live long" or "may his light shine" is not a matter of public identity, and the witnesses should separate it from the words "son of" and the father's name if it refers only to one of them. Since this is not done, it must refer to both. See what I wrote in the margin of Shulchan Aruch, Even HaEzer, siman 129, concerning "Yehudah Maccabi," that the initials should be read as "Mattityahu Kohen ben Yochanan."
