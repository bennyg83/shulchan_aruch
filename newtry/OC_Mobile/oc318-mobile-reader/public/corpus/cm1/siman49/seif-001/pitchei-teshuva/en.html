That the borrower's name. See Baer Heitev, who writes that this is unlike the law of a receipt, and concludes that the witnesses must therefore also recognize the borrower by name. Dagul Merevavah explains: We need not fear that this borrower himself owes another person who has the same name as the creditor now appearing before the witnesses. If this borrower later produces the receipt against his other creditor, and that creditor claims that he never instructed anyone to write such a receipt, the witnesses can be questioned. Since they already saw the borrower, they will recognize him and recall that at the time another creditor was with him. By contrast, if the receipt comes into the hands of a borrower whom the witnesses never saw, and they saw neither borrower nor creditor, nothing will cause them to suspect the matter at all, and beit din will act in accordance with the receipt. See Derishah. See also Tumim and Netivot HaMishpat on this point.
