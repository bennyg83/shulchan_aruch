Bnei Shmuel asks that above, according to the view that this principle does not distinguish monetary matters, Rivash appears to reject reliance on "a matter destined to become known." There is no difficulty. First, here the fear is already remote, namely that people will falsely change their names. Baal HaIttur indeed says that ordinarily we need not fear such a scheme, so even slight corroboration was enough for the Sages. Second, Rivash himself writes in responsum 155 that where the matter is known to everyone and will certainly be clarified, we do rely on the principle even in monetary law, as in Persian documents. This case is similar: if the wrong borrower later appears and protests that he never participated, the false identification will become public and obvious. There is therefore no difficulty.
