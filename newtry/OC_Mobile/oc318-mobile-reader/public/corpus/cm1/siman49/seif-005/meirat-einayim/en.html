Since two people with that name have not been established there. This refers only to the first concern, that perhaps a fraudster adopted the same name and admitted liability to this lender. On that concern the rule says that we are not worried because two people of that name have not been established in the city. But if two such people are established in the city, a document cannot be collected from either of them merely by name. The second concern, however, that perhaps a fraudster adopted the name of the creditor, is not entertained even where two people in the city share that name, for we rule that where two Yosef ben Shimon live in one city each may produce a promissory note against another person, as the Tur and Mechaber write nearby. Rambam, in chapter 24 of Hilchot Malveh, after writing like the Mechaber that we rely on the fact that two people with the name have not been established, concludes: 'For there is a presumption that witnesses do not sign a document unless they recognize the persons named in it.' This also explains the first clause, preventing the borrower from saying, 'I do not owe this debt; another person assumed my name.' Since no second person of that name is established, and witnesses do not sign unless they know the borrower and lender, we do not suspect a fraudster who adopted the name. The Tur gives only the reason that witnesses do not sign unless they recognize the parties, which primarily appears to address the opening clause. The latter concern, that the lender is not the true lender, would not seem to require an explanation, since the holder of the document can collect even in a city where two Yosef ben Shimon are established. Still, it is somewhat strained that the Tur would begin with two issues and explain only the first. It therefore appears that the reason that the witnesses recognize the parties also addresses the latter concern. Their recognition means both recognition of the name and visual recognition. A fraudster could not later collect under the other lender's identity because the witnesses would recognize that he was not the person for whom they signed. Perhaps this is also the reason why each of two Yosef ben Shimon in one city may collect a document against a third person: we do not suspect such fraud because the witnesses visually recognized the lender. Therefore the person who actually produces the document is maintained in the presumption that it is his. The Mechaber abbreviated Rambam's wording. Alternatively, Rambam's phrase, 'since two people have not been established,' may apply to the latter clause as well. It teaches that even if the borrower claims with certainty, 'The lender is a fraudster; I saw him standing beside the actual creditor when I acknowledged the debt before the witnesses,' or a similar factual claim, if another person of the same name were known in the city and the borrower asserted that he saw him there, beit din would need to investigate carefully. But where no two people of that name have been established in the city, such a concern is not accepted without stronger proof. Understand this well.
