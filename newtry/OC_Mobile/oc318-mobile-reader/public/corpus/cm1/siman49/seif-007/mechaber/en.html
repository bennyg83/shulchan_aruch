If two residents of one city are each named Yosef son of Shimon, neither can produce against the other a document containing a formal act of acquisition, because the defendant may say, 'This document you produce against me was mine, and I returned it to you when you repaid the debt you owed me.' {Rama: The Mechaber's explanation appears contradictory, because that defense would apply whether or not the document contains an acquisition. Others explain that neither can produce a document because a loan document may be written for a borrower outside the lender's presence, creating concern that the person producing it was himself the borrower. That concern applies only to a document containing an acquisition, as explained at the end of siman 39.} No third person can produce a debt document against them, because each may say it was written against the other, unless the original signatories identify both the document and the borrower. If other witnesses identify him, the debt has only the status of an oral loan. If one namesake was a minor on the document's date, collection is made from the other. To avoid ambiguity, they should include their grandfathers' names; if those too are identical, they should add another identifying feature. {Rama: Some say that if they come from different cities, a debt document may be produced against them because the borrower's or lender's city must be written. Others say cities are not written in documents, and this is the practice. If the city was written, however, it certainly serves as an identifying mark. If one namesake's father is deceased and the document describes the other father as living, that wording is identifying when attributed by the witnesses, but not when merely included in the first-person text, because the other may have inserted it deceptively and the witnesses did not scrutinize every detail.} They may produce documents against a third person, who cannot say, 'I became obligated to your namesake rather than you,' because possession of the document establishes ownership. {Rama: If neither holds the document, then even if the debtor admits owing one of them, neither can collect without mutual authorizations.} If the debtor holds a receipt from either namesake, neither may claim against him, because he may tell each, 'You wrote this receipt.' If they authorize one another, one can collect, since the debtor admits paying only one. If he claims he paid both and asked for one receipt sufficient against either claimant, that defense is valid and mutual authorization does not help. See also simanim 61, 67, 90, and 442.}
