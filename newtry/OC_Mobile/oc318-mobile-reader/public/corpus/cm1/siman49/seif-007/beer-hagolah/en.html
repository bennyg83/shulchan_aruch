p) For if it contains no kinyan, since one does not write a document for the borrower unless the lender is with him, holders of identical names may collect from one another, as the Maggid Mishneh writes there in chapter 24 in the name of the Rashba, cited in the Beit Yosef.
tz) According to the Rashbam's explanation there.
k) In my humble opinion, with a document that contains no kinyan, since it cannot be written for the borrower unless the lender is present, the lender can establish his identity through those witnesses, who can testify that this is the borrower concerning whom we testified. The borrower therefore cannot claim, "This document was mine and I returned it to you." I later saw something close to this in the Sema.
This reason is stated in the Gemara there, 173a. What the Rama writes in the name of some commentators, that the Gemara's conclusion presents no concern specifically in a document containing a kinyan, is not stated in the Gemara itself. Rather, the Maggid Mishneh writes this, as stated above in seif katan p. Sema.
