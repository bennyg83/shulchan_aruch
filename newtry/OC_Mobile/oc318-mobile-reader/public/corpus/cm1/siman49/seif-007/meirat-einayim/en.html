He should say to him: This document that you produce, etc. Rashbam gives this reason in explaining the mishnah, and the Tur likewise records it. The Gemara brings a baraita whose tanna disagrees and holds that two people with identical names may collect from one another. The Gemara explains that the dispute depends on whether a loan document may be written for the borrower without the lender being present. According to our mishnah, it may be written, so there is concern that the borrower asked the witnesses to write a document for a contemplated loan from one of the two people with that name, received it into his own hand, and later produced it against the other person and said, 'You borrowed from me.' According to the baraita, a document is not written for the borrower without the lender present. Since the lender was present and did not object to the ambiguity, we too need not be concerned. Maggid Mishneh cites Rashba that even according to the tanna of our mishnah, if the document contains no kinyan, the two may collect from each other because everyone agrees that such a document is not written for the borrower without the lender present. This explains the Mechaber and Rama. Rama's claim that the Mechaber contradicts himself can be reconciled. The Mechaber may accept Rashbam and the Tur's reason and nevertheless require a kinyan here. With a document lacking kinyan, the borrower is afraid to lie and say it is his, because such a document is not written for the borrower alone unless the lender is present. The witnesses therefore know both parties and will be alert to the matter, and the falsehood is likely to be exposed. With a kinyan, however, it may be written for the borrower alone even if the witnesses do not recognize the lender at all according to the Mechaber's view at the beginning of this siman. There is therefore greater concern for falsehood. Still, a difficulty remains: once the case involves a kinyan there is an even simpler concern, that the person producing the document is himself the borrower and never borrowed on it at all. Rashbam used this reason in order to explain every case, including one without a kinyan. This requires study.
