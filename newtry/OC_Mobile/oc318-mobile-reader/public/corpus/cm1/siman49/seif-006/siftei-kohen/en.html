[" And it still requires delivery to its owner. Meaning, it was lost before delivery. Once it reached him, however, the witnesses may not write another, except in a gift document or a sale without responsibility, as explained at the end of the following siman:"]
[" And some say that the concept of having completed their agency does not apply to documents at all, etc. The Rama’s words here, and likewise in Darkei Moshe, are surprising to me. Darkei Moshe cites the responsum of Rashba brought by Beit Yosef, new material 2: Whenever a person told witnesses, ‘Write me a power of attorney,’ and they discovered that it was not properly drafted, they may return and write it even one hundred times. Witnesses may write ten documents concerning one field in the case of a gift, and even in a sale they may do so with respect to the portion of the sale document that contains no responsibility, as stated in Get Pashut. We apply the rule that witnesses have completed their agency specifically to a get, because the Torah says, ‘and he shall write.’ Likewise, in a sale document, whenever they rewrite the responsibility clause, the seller’s property becomes encumbered anew through that document, and therefore they have already completed their agency. This concludes Rashba. Ran in HaMekabel writes that regarding documents that still require delivery, where the recipient acquires nothing until the document reaches his hand, even after the witnesses wrote and properly signed the document, as long as they have not delivered it to him they have not completed their agency and may write even one hundred times. But with documents that do not require delivery, such as one recording a kinyan, where the obligor becomes bound from the moment of the kinyan, once the document has been properly written and signed they have completed their agency and may not write and deliver another. Beit Yosef adds that even regarding documents requiring delivery, if the principal said, ‘Write and give it to a third party,’ and they wrote and gave it to that third party and it was then lost, there is room to doubt whether they may write another; with a get the Gemara leaves this unresolved. Darkei Moshe concludes that all this follows Ran, who holds that once an ordinary document was properly written the witnesses may have completed their agency, but according to Rashba, who allegedly limits the concern to a get and not other documents, these rules would have no place outside gittin. Maggid Mishneh, chapter 24 of Hilchot Malveh, appears to follow Ran. On the basis of this, the Rama here presents a dispute: one opinion says that even if the witnesses wrote a document, as long as it contains no kinyan, etc., they may rewrite it, while another says that the concept of completing agency does not apply to documents at all. In my view, however, the Rama did not understand Rashba correctly. Rashba plainly does not disagree with Ran. Ran’s rule that ordinary documents may not be rewritten after the agency was completed plainly concerns loan documents or sale documents containing responsibility. When Rashba says that witnesses’ completion of agency is a special concern with a get, he intends only to exclude a gift document or a sale document without responsibility. With a sale document containing responsibility, and likewise with a loan document, Rashba agrees with Ran, as is explicit from his own words. The Gemara in Get Pashut states regarding one who paid part of his debt that beit din may write him a new document, but witnesses may not. The Gemara asks: beit din has power to extract money, but how can witnesses, who already fulfilled their agency, perform that agency again? It then cites Rav’s statement that witnesses may write even ten documents for one field, and answers that Rav Yosef refers to a gift document, while Rabbah refers to a sale document without responsibility. Plainly, a loan document is like a sale document with responsibility, because the debtor’s property becomes encumbered to the creditor by that document. This is also clear from another responsum of Rashba cited by Beit Yosef, new material 7: Since these witnesses remember that they erred, they may write him another document properly, because whenever they erred, even though they already delivered the first document to the creditor, they had not fulfilled their agency. This shows that only because they erred may they rewrite it; otherwise they may not. The same follows from the rule above at the beginning of siman 41 that the witnesses to a document themselves do not write another document, and from Rashba, siman 1073, cited by Beit Yosef above in siman 39, new material 13, and from Rashba, siman 991, cited by Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe below in siman 71, new material 25, which I cited above in siman 41, seif katan 3. It is likewise clear from Hagahot Ashri in the name of Rabbenu Yitzchak and from the Rama below at the beginning of siman 54, that only with the debtor’s consent may witnesses write another document. Without his consent, even when they know the first document was not paid, they may not write a new one, because their agency was completed by writing the first. This is precisely the position of the Rosh, the Tur, and the Mechaber here: only when they made an error may they write another document, because then we do not say they fulfilled their agency; otherwise they did. Thus Ran and Rashba do not disagree at all. This appears to me clearly the primary ruling, contrary to the Rama. Ir Shushan and Sema, who followed the Rama, are likewise difficult:"]
