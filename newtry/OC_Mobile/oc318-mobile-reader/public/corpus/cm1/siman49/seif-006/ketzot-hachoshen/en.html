The same applies to any other error. The Shach writes that this applies specifically to a definite error, but not to a doubtful one, because perhaps the first instrument was valid and the witnesses already completed their agency, as explained in Even HaEzer siman 122. It appears to me, however, that even a doubtful error can mean the witnesses have not completed their agency, provided the doubt itself prevents use of the instrument. Beit Yosef writes in siman 122 that even where a get is invalid only rabbinically, another get may be written, because so long as she cannot remarry on the basis of it the first get is mere pottery. Rambam likewise distinguishes between a get that is void and one merely disqualified, yet in both cases permits another to be written. Rosh's phrase about doubts arising from extra stringencies practiced in gittin refers only to stringencies that have no root in Torah or rabbinic law. A genuine rabbinic defect is itself called Torah for this purpose. Pri Chadash goes further and writes that even where the get is not wholly invalid but one may not marry with it initially, it is still treated as mere pottery because it is not fit for its intended use. Accordingly, in monetary documents, where there are no extra get stringencies and every defect is either a Torah doubt or rabbinic doubt, if the uncertainty prevents collection under the instrument, the witnesses have not completed their mission and may write another. This is why the Shulchan Aruch and Rama did not distinguish here between a definite error and a doubtful one, as they do in the laws of gittin.
