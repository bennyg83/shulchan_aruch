For even if they wrote a valid document. The statement that with a document we do not say that the witnesses have completed their agency so long as they did not perform a kinyan is not precisely worded. It implies that even in a case where, with a get, we would say the witnesses completed their agency, nevertheless with ordinary documents we never say so. That is not correct. The case here concerns a document that was lost from their possession before delivery, as Shach explains in seif katan 8. With a get that is lost before delivery, we certainly do not say that the witnesses completed their agency. Who told them, "Write it and leave it in your pockets"? This is explicit in Gittin, chapter HaMekabel, and in Even HaEzer, siman 122. See Ran in chapter HaMekabel, who is the source of this ruling. He is actually more stringent with ordinary documents. If a get is lost before delivery, the agency certainly has not been completed. But with a document containing a kinyan, even if it is lost before delivery, Ran holds that the witnesses may already have completed their agency, because the obligor subjected himself from the moment of the kinyan. Once the document was properly written and signed, the witnesses completed the part of their agency that creates the lien, and they may not simply write and deliver another document. Ran writes that this was said only by comparison to a get, which is legally nothing until it reaches the recipient. Thus, a document without a kinyan is exactly like a get in this respect. If it was lost before delivery, we do not say the witnesses completed their agency. The wording of Shulchan Aruch must therefore be understood narrowly. The second opinion cited there need not disagree at all with the first opinion. The first opinion does not address a case where the document was lost, and it is entirely possible that it agrees with the second opinion in that case. Ran's words themselves initially seem difficult. He says that because the obligor became bound from the moment of the kinyan, once the document was properly written the witnesses completed their agency. On the contrary, if the obligation already exists from the kinyan and the document is needed only as proof, in what sense did the witnesses complete their agency before delivering the document to the creditor? If the obligor said only, "Write it and give it to me," so that they were appointed only to write while he would deliver it himself, they can certainly write another document and give it to the obligor, because for such writing no special agency is required at all. The witnesses' testimony embodied in the signature is completed only upon delivery, as explained in siman 39, seif 13. Were it not for the concern that the obligor might use the document improperly, witnesses could even write in some cases without express instruction from the obligor. See what I wrote there. It therefore appears that Ran follows the Rosh, cited by Shach in siman 43, seif katan 35, who holds that a kinyan does not by itself create the full priority against encumbered property before a document is written. According to that view, when the obligor says, "Write and give," or says it without qualification so that it means "write and give," there are effectively two components of agency. The agency to write enables collection from encumbered property, while the agency to deliver gives the creditor the evidentiary document. Once they have written it, the writing component has been completed, and they have completed that aspect of their agency. By contrast, with a document lacking a kinyan, and likewise with a get, no legal consequence is completed by the writing alone before delivery. Therefore they have not yet completed any operative portion of the agency and may write again and deliver.
And some say that this concept does not apply to documents at all. In my humble opinion, Rama in the gloss speaks of the period before delivery. He does not disagree with the first opinion except regarding a document of kinyan. Ran, who is the first opinion, holds that with such a document the witnesses completed their agency for the reason explained above. The second opinion follows the majority of authorities cited by Shach in siman 43, seif katan 35, who hold that a kinyan by itself creates priority against encumbered property even before anything is written. On that view, writing and delivery are needed only so that the creditor possess evidence. The instruction "write and give" is then one continuous agency, and it is not completed until delivery, because before delivery no evidentiary benefit has reached the creditor. This can be learned from Rashba, who writes concerning a deed of sale with warranty that when the witnesses write a second document, the seller's property becomes newly subjected through that document. Rashba cannot mean that the underlying lien itself first arises through the writing, because one who sells land before witnesses creates rights against purchasers even without a kinyan. Rather, his intent must be that the evidentiary force of the lien is completed by delivery of the first document, and when the witnesses later deliver a second document they are repeating their completed agency. It follows that before the first document is delivered to the purchaser, no agency has yet been completed and the witnesses may rewrite it. The same is true in a loan according to the opinion that a kinyan alone creates priority against encumbered property. Since the writing is only for evidence, and that evidentiary purpose is not completed before delivery, the witnesses may rewrite it before delivery.
If it is established that the matter was a scribal error, the document is valid. See Tumim, who writes that this depends upon the dispute cited by Shach in seif katan 11. According to the opinion that even where the witnesses to the document themselves say which of two people of the same name was intended, the creditor has only the status of an oral lender, the witnesses evidently cannot add identifying details and create another document, even from a later date. All the more so, says Tumim, where there was a complete mistake. It appears to me that even according to the opinion there that the witnesses can add the identifying details and write even from the first date, nevertheless where there was a complete error, such as writing Reuven instead of Shimon, they cannot write another document from the original date if the first document contained no kinyan. The original writing is merely a shard, and creation of a lien requires a valid document. The fact that the witnesses generated publicity does not suffice, as Tumim proves in this siman from siman 45, seif 15, where filling blank space in a document with signatures of relatives leaves the document invalid even though the witnesses immediately generated public notice. The same principle applies here. See Ketzot HaChoshen, who disagrees with Shach, seif katan 6, and writes that where the document is only doubtfully invalid the witnesses may write another. Responsa Ramban, siman 145, and Get Pashut, siman 122, state explicitly that even in a complete doubt the same law applies. The reason is that a document known with certainty to be rabbinically invalid is at least definitely disqualified. A document that is only doubtfully invalid because the halachic ruling itself is uncertain is different. Such a doubt is a doubt caused by lack of knowledge, which is not treated as a true legal doubt, because if one authority does not know the law another may know it. The same principle applies to doubts in the laws of tereifot.
