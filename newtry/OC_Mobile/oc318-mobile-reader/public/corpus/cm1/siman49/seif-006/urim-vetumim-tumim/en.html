And some say that the rule of having completed their agency does not apply to documents, and so forth. Shach challenges Rama for following Responsa Rashba. He argues that Rashba said this only concerning a power of attorney or similar evidentiary document that creates no property lien. In a loan note containing warranty, Rashba would agree that the witnesses may not rewrite it after they have completed their mission, just as he says concerning a deed of sale containing warranty. Shach cites Bava Batra 171, where the Gemara says that once witnesses have completed their agency, how can they return and perform it again? It is clear to me, however, that Rama's words refer back to the immediately preceding ruling of Ran: if there was no kinyan and the transaction still requires delivery of the document, the witnesses are considered to have completed their mission; the implication is that where a kinyan was made, Ran would not permit rewriting. Rama then cites the dissenting view that with documents we do not say the witnesses have completed their agency. On that reading Shach's objection falls away even apart from Rashba, because as I explained at length above in siman 41, Tosafot and Rosh hold that in ordinary documents the only issue with rewriting is the danger of an antedated instrument. Where a kinyan already fixed the property lien and there is no antedating problem, there is no independent bar of "agency completed." Rashba's responsum itself can also be read this way. He says that in a sale deed, if the witnesses rewrite warranty now, the property becomes bound through this new document and their prior agency has therefore been completed. The same would be true of an ordinary loan note without a kinyan, where the document itself creates the purchaser-facing lien. Such a document resembles a get because it is an operative instrument. But where a prior kinyan already created the lien, even if no document were ever written, the property has been bound from the time of the kinyan. The later document serves only as evidence so that the transaction can be proved if the witnesses die or travel overseas. Rashba agrees that in a purely evidentiary document the doctrine of completed agency does not apply in the same way, because it is not a sefer makneh at all. Rama's wording itself indicates that he speaks of a document based on kinyan. If the first document is void because of an error, and there was no prior kinyan, how could they simply rewrite it with the old date? The new document would itself create the lien only now and would plainly be antedated. It is strained to say that Rama silently means they must use the new date. His unqualified language makes sense only where a kinyan already fixed the lien. Thus Shach's attack has no basis. A separate question remains against Rashba: even a deed of sale with warranty does not itself necessarily create the lien, because we rule that one who sells land before witnesses alone can generate purchaser-facing collection rights. If so, the deed may merely prove the transaction. I discuss this at length below in siman 116.
And there is one who says, and so forth. Knesset HaGedolah writes that if the truth is clarified, no authority appears to disagree with this ruling, and Mechaber sometimes uses the phrase "there is one who says" even where he found no dissent. I do not understand this. In my opinion the ruling is in fact disputed. The witnesses wrote "Reuven," but the truth is that they should have written "Levi." The document itself mentions only Reuven. It therefore fails the requirement that its operative identity be evident from within the document itself. Consider the case of two people named Yosef ben Shimon. If the witnesses omitted the identifying marker and later testify which one was the true borrower, many authorities cited by Maggid Mishneh hold that their oral testimony does not cure the document because its identity is not evident from within. Rambam permits it, but his reason is that the witnesses can write another instrument identifying the parties. Here, however, Rosh's responsum indicates that the document itself may remain valid even where rewriting is no longer possible because the witnesses are unavailable. That must rest on a different approach. Rosh may follow Ramban, cited by Ran in Perek Kol HaGet, that even according to R. Meir one does not always require every fact to be evident from within the document. In addition, Rosh follows the view that delivery witnesses create even ordinary documents, and the signature witnesses can be treated as evidence of the writing while the delivery witnesses identify the parties. But according to our ruling that ordinary documents do not require delivery witnesses and ordinarily rest upon the signatures, the identity must be evident from within according to Tosafot and most authorities. Thus Mechaber properly wrote "there is one who says," because the rule is not undisputed. Indeed, in Rosh's responsum, klal 68, the relevant mistake was itself evident from the document: it stated that Reuven and his wife Rachel owed the money, while it was known that Reuven had no wife named Rachel. That internal inconsistency showed the scribal error. This also explains why the ruling differs from earlier cases, such as mistakenly writing one thousand instead of five hundred, or writing the year 58 instead of 57. There the error is not evident from the document itself. Here it is. Rosh's own responsa support this distinction: in one responsum involving a monetary error he says only that the witnesses can write another document, not that the erroneous instrument itself remains valid; in the following responsum, where the error is internally evident, he says the document itself is valid. This is also the intent of Rama's apparently repetitive gloss at the end of seif 2, where he writes that the same applies to any other scribal error. His point is specifically an error that is evident from the document itself. Such a document is considered internally clear enough and valid according to all views, so Rama states the rule without qualification. Although Mechaber's present wording sounds broader, the truth of the matter points in this direction.
