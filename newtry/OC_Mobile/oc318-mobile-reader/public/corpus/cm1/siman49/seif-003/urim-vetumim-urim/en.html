Without a kinyan, etc. Without a kinyan we do not write a loan document for the borrower in the lender's absence because of the concern that it will become antedated. Perhaps the lender is not actually present, and the borrower will bring before the witnesses another person whose name is the same as the lender's so they will prepare the deed, creating the antedating concern. Therefore both parties must be identified. Sema offers a strained explanation, as Shach notes. I wrote above in Tumim to siman 39 other concerns that can reconcile Tur and Rambam. Shach holds that even with a sale lacking a kinyan, the witnesses may not write until they identify both seller and buyer, because we rule that a sale deed without kinyan is not written for the seller unless the buyer is present. See Tumim for my reconciliation of Mechaber's ruling that in a sale it is unnecessary to identify both parties.
