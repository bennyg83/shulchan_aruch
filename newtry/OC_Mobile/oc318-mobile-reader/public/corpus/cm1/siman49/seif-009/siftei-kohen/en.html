And an authenticated receipt is found with the creditor stating that his document, etc. This is difficult. If the receipt is authenticated, it need not be found among cancelled documents; and if it is found among cancelled documents, it is effective even when not authenticated, as explained below in siman 65, seif 18. What Bach writes here is not correct in light of the Talmudic discussions at the end of the first chapter of Bava Metzia and in Get Pashut, Bava Batra 173a. See there. Even if ‘authenticated’ is interpreted merely to mean that the witnesses’ signatures are recognizable, rather than that a formal authentication was written on the receipt, the difficulty remains, because the Tur and Mechaber below in siman 65 write that when a debt document is found among cancelled documents, the document is invalid even if the receipt bears no witnesses at all. It therefore appears that the word ‘authenticated’ should be deleted here, or else the wording of the Tur and Mechaber, ‘and they were found,’ should read ‘or they were found,’ etc. This is also the wording of Baal HaTerumot, Shaar 33, part 4: the case is where the debt documents were found among cancelled documents, etc. Gidulei Terumah there likewise writes that if he had the power he would emend the Tur to read, ‘or two documents were found,’ etc., in accordance with the wording of his teacher. This is the primary reading, contrary to Sema, seif katan 23. Examine this carefully.
