In the lender's possession there is an authenticated receipt stating that his document, etc. This is the proper reading. The fact that the receipt is authenticated proves that the lender did not merely prepare it to hand to the borrower upon future payment, because if the borrower had not yet paid there would have been no reason for the lender to have the receipt authenticated. Lenders do not ordinarily authenticate receipts in advance. Rather, the borrower presumably had the receipt authenticated after payment and receipt of it, and afterward redeposited it with the lender. The Tur likewise states this law specifically with an authenticated receipt. The Gemara itself does not state that qualification and implies that the additional defect of finding the promissory note among torn documents is alone sufficient to treat the document as paid. See Perishah and Derishah, where I explained why the Tur required the receipt to be authenticated.
