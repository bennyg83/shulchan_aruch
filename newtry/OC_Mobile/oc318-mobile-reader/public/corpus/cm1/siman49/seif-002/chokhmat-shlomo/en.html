Seif 2. In the gloss: similarly regarding other scribal errors. His words require study. He appears to understand the rule “warranty is a scribal error” to mean that the witnesses erred: the lender and borrower or seller and purchaser presumably stipulated warranty, but the witnesses mistakenly failed to write it. This is the approach of Nimukei Yosef cited by Sema in siman 39. But Shach there cites the majority of authorities who hold that even where the parties made no express stipulation at all, omission of warranty is still treated as a scribal error because the seller himself is presumed to have intended the usual warranty. If so, no proof can be brought from the warranty rule regarding mistakes by witnesses. It may be that we never attribute such an error to the witnesses, because witnesses are presumed to record matters carefully and correctly. The reason they omitted warranty may simply be that the parties did not expressly stipulate it, and therefore the witnesses had no basis to write it. The law supplies the warranty because of the seller's presumed intent, not because the witnesses made a mistake. Thus one cannot infer from this that we generally attribute omissions to witness error. This requires study. Examine it carefully.
