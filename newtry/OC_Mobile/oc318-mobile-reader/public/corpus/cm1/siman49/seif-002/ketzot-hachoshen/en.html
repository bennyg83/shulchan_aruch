The lender's name. A document is not written for the borrower in the lender's absence, where no kinyan occurred, because perhaps it will be prepared in Nisan and the loan will not occur until Tishrei, making it early dated. If the scribe does not know the true lender's name, the borrower might provide another name and later hand the document to the real lender. Authorities therefore ask why, according to the Rosh and Tur, who accept the principle that the borrower's witnesses can acquire for the lender, the lender's identity must be known at all. Following the Bach in siman 43, the principle applies where the document is prepared ordinarily for eventual delivery. But if the borrower explicitly asks that the document be given back to him, we do not treat the witnesses as acquiring for the lender. Where he appears with another man and says, "Give the document to this person," though that person is not the real lender, the situation is effectively like saying, "Give it to me." When he later transfers it to the true lender, the original witnessing does not relate back to the earlier date. Therefore the lender's identity must be known.

Knesset HaGedolah, citing Bnei Shmuel, infers that the Tur holds that even in monetary law one witness is believed regarding a matter destined to become known, like Rabbi Duran and unlike Rivash, and he writes that this also appears from Raavad, Rosh, Rashba, and Nimukei Yosef. Rama, however, rules in siman 30 that in monetary law there is no distinction between a matter destined to become known and other matters, following Rivash. Therefore the reason we rely upon this principle here is that the requirement not to write a document unless the parties are recognized is only rabbinic. We rule concerning a get that in a time of danger it may be written even though the parties are not recognized. If the concern were biblical, the Sages could not have disregarded it merely because of danger. It must therefore be rabbinic, and in that rabbinic matter the Sages relied upon a single witness where the truth is destined to become known. This is straightforward.
