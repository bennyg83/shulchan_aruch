That this is the borrower's name. The witnesses need not recognize the lender's name. This differs from a receipt, where both names must be recognized. With a receipt, even if they recognize the lender's name there is concern that this lender may deceptively give the receipt to a different borrower whose lender happens to have the same name as the lender named in the receipt, allowing that borrower to discharge his own debt improperly. We do not extend the concern still further to the possibility that there is another lender and borrower whose names both match those before us. With a loan document, by contrast, even if the witnesses do not recognize the lender, they may write it. We do not fear that there is another person with the lender's name and that this borrower will give the document to that person so that he can extract money from someone else who happens to share the borrower's name. If we entertained that concern, it would remain even if the witnesses recognized both parties. Sema and Beurim:
Without a kinyan. There is concern that the person before us is not the true lender and that the document will not be delivered to the true lender until Tishrei, making the document improperly predated. The same applies to a deed of sale without a kinyan. According to the authorities in siman 238 who hold that a sale deed may not be written for the seller unless the purchaser is present, the witnesses must recognize the purchaser's name. In the reverse case. The Sema explains the Mechaber's statement that a Torah scholar is believed when an error is later discovered as follows. If it is unknown whether the witnesses relied on a woman or relative or independently recognized the parties, and the Torah scholar says that they relied on the woman or relative who misled them, he is believed because it is not his manner to investigate personally. Ordinary people, however, where the document does not say that they relied on such a person, are presumed to have investigated and are not believed later to say that they were misled. This applies where the Torah scholar says the parties never stood before him. If he says they did stand before him but he deliberately did not look carefully and relied on a relative, then regarding a woman he is believed because it is not his practice to stare at women. Regarding a man, he is not, Sema. The Shach disagrees and holds that the Mechaber is discussing only identification involving women. Regarding men, a Torah scholar is treated like anyone else. See Beurim, which follows the Nimukei Yosef cited by the Tumim. An ordinary person who relied on a woman or relative cannot later say, "They deceived me," because the person giving the identification fears lying to him about something he can easily verify. A Torah scholar, however, is believed to say that they lied to him because they are less afraid to mislead him, knowing that he does not ordinarily investigate such matters personally.
