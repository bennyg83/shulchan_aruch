d) From the Mishnah, "provided that he recognizes them," Bava Batra 167a, and as the Gemara explains the reason there. From this we learn the same for all other documents.
e) According to the Maggid Mishneh in chapter 24 of Hilkhot Malveh VeLoveh, who comments on the Rambam's ruling that the witnesses must recognize the lender and purchaser as well: perhaps Rabbeinu said this only regarding documents without a kinyan, etc. But for documents containing a kinyan, since they may be written even when the lender or purchaser is not present, even Rabbeinu agrees that the witnesses need not recognize their names. It must be said that the beginning of the chapter, where he writes that a document may be written for the seller, etc., concerns a case with a kinyan and refers to the kinyan mentioned nearby regarding the borrower. This is explicit in Bava Metzia at the end of 12b, according to Rav Assi's interpretation there, as stated above in siman 39, seif 7. See also siman 231 and what I will write there, with Hashem's help.
f) Tur, and so write the Rosh and Nimukei Yosef concerning the incident of Rabbi bar Chanan, Bava Batra there 167b. In my humble opinion, this is explicit from the conclusion of the Gemara, Yevamot 39b.
g) Baal HaTerumot in the name of the Raavad, from the incident of Rava bar Chanan cited above, from siman 61. The Sema writes that where valid witnesses are required and a Torah scholar signed based upon them, he is not believed to say they misled him, because he wrote it on the basis of valid witnesses and those witnesses themselves cannot retract their testimony. This differs where he relied upon a woman or relative, whose statements are not complete testimony. In that case he is believed against them to say that they misled him.
