I already wrote above in siman 38 that Rashbam regards this as merely a rabbinic stringency because of public suspicion, while the Ran challenges him and writes that the early authorities and Rambam do not understand it so. The Beit Yosef cites the Ran below at the end of siman 134. See also Nedarim 87 and the Rosh and Ran there. Rivash, siman 267, writes that the rule that retraction within toch kedei dibbur is effective except in testimony, kiddushin, and similar matters refers, with respect to kiddushin, to the man who actually betrothed the woman and immediately retracts. But an ordinary witness who testifies about kiddushin can retract his testimony within toch kedei dibbur, just as in other testimony. No one ever doubted this. This also resolves the rule above in siman 47:4 that where a woman says, "I was betrothed," and within toch kedei dibbur says, "I am unmarried," she is believed. Her statement functions like testimony, and just as witnesses may retract within that period, so may she. The Bach's lengthy discussion in his later supplement is unnecessary.
