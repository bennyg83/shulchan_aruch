The borrower. The name of the lender or purchaser need not be known to the witnesses. This differs from a receipt, where both parties' identities must be known. A receipt is prepared by the lender to give to the borrower when payment is made. Even if the witnesses know the lender's identity, there is concern that he may scheme by giving this receipt to the borrower of a different person who has the same name as the lender written in the receipt, allowing that borrower to discharge a different debt improperly. Therefore they must also know the borrower's identity. Once both are known, Chazal did not extend the concern so far as to fear that elsewhere there are another lender and borrower bearing the exact same names. In a loan document, by contrast, the borrower comes to the scribe and witnesses to obligate himself and asks them to write a debt document against him. They may write it even without knowing the lender, because if we feared that there is another person with the same name as this borrower and that he will hand the document to a lender whom he falsely claims to owe, so that the lender can extract money from that other person, the same concern would remain even if both identities were known. Therefore the rule is instead that where two persons have identical names, no third party may use an ambiguous document to extract from either one, as explained in seif 7 concerning two men with identical names. Thus the Sema. Nevertheless, the matter requires some study. Even if both parties to a receipt are identified, perhaps this borrower owes nothing to this particular lender but owes another person with the same name, and could use this receipt to discharge that other debt. The issue can be reconciled only with some difficulty.
Without. The reason is that perhaps the person present is not truly the lender, and the borrower will not deliver the document to the actual lender until much later. The lender could then improperly collect from purchasers based on the earlier document date, as explained in siman 39, seif 13. The same applies to a sale document without a kinyan: according to the poskim at the beginning of siman 238 who hold that a sale document may not be written for the seller without the purchaser present except where the document itself effects the acquisition, the witnesses must also know the purchaser's identity. According to the opinion that the witnesses' signatures acquire the right for the beneficiary, however, there is no need in any case to know the lender or purchaser. The Shach writes that the Sema, Bach, and other later authorities offer explanations and distinctions here that do not appear correct to him; what he has written is primary and accords with the Maggid Mishneh.
