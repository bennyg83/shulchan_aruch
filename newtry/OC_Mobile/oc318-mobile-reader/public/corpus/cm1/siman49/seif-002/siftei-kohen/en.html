The seller's name, etc., and if it is a loan deed, etc. The Mechaber implies that in a sale deed the buyer's name need not be identified even where there was no prior acquisition, since the Maggid Mishneh did not require acquisition for a sale deed. This follows the Rambam alone. Most authorities hold that a seller may not receive a sale deed in the buyer's absence unless the sale was already effected by acquisition, just as in a loan deed. The Maggid Mishneh states this in chapter 24, the Bedek HaBayit cites it at the beginning of siman 238, and the Mechaber himself rules so there. The Mechaber should therefore have equated sale and loan deeds. The matter requires study.
Without an acquisition, etc. The concern is that the person before them may not be the true lender and may withhold delivery until later, thereby seizing purchasers unlawfully, as in siman 39, seif 13. The same applies to a sale deed without prior acquisition. According to the authorities and the Mechaber in siman 238, the buyer's identity must also be known because a deed may not be written for the seller in the buyer's absence except after an acquisition. According to the view that a witness's signature immediately acquires for the beneficiary, however, neither lender nor buyer need be identified. The distinctions proposed here by the Sema, Bach, and later authorities are unfounded. The Maggid Mishneh's position supports what I have written. See siman 39, seif 7, seif katan 18 and 21. Therefore it is astonishing, etc. The Sema attempts to reconcile the matter by saying that the Beit Yosef's proof in siman 29 rests on the Rashbam's statement that a scholar does not normally examine women closely and may rely upon others who recognize them. The Sema argues that this cannot be limited to recognizing a woman. His reasoning is incorrect. The scholar may rely on others precisely because propriety prevented his own close examination of the woman. A man identifying another man cannot say that he relied on others when he could have looked himself. The quoted language is not the Rashbam's exact wording but the Beit Yosef's. The Beit Yosef also proves the point from the Tur in Even HaEzer, siman 110, concerning recognition of a woman. The Mechaber should therefore be understood as speaking only of recognizing a woman. Rabbeinu Yerucham's general language concerns a matter in which a scholar does not normally scrutinize, such as a woman's identity. The Raavad, source of the rule in the Baal HaTerumot, likewise discusses a scholar who signed concerning a woman borrower and then admitted error in recognizing her face. The Gemara, Rashbam, Tur, Mabit, and Baal HaTerumot all support this limitation. A scholar may retract regarding a woman's identity; regarding a man's identity he may not. An ordinary person may not retract even regarding a woman. This is the primary ruling, unlike the Sema.
