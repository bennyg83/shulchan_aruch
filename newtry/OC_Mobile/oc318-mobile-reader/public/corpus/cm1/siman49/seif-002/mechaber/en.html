The witnesses must know that the borrower's name is so-and-so son of so-and-so. In a sale document they must know the seller's name, and in a receipt the borrower and lender's names. If a loan document is made without a formal act of acquisition, they must also know the lender's name. If they do not know the parties, even a woman or relative may identify their names. If reliance was placed on a woman or relative and an error was later found, the retraction of a Torah scholar is accepted because a Torah scholar does not ordinarily scrutinize such matters. Some say this leniency applies specifically to identifying a woman, because a Torah scholar does not scrutinize women, as explained in Even HaEzer, siman 110; in other matters there is no difference between a Torah scholar and another person. The Mechaber writes in Beit Yosef, Choshen Mishpat, siman 29, that this is the primary view. Another person, who ordinarily scrutinizes, is not believed to retract, for he presumably examined the matter and is now lying. {Rama: Likewise, when an evident scribal error occurred, the document remains valid and we attribute it to error, just as omission of a guarantee is treated as a scribal error (Rosh, klal 58, siman 32; see above, beginning of siman 39).}
