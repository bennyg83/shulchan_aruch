(See there: Some say that if they are from two cities, etc.) The Tur cites this in the name of Rabbi Yeshayah regarding two namesakes collecting from one another. The existing text is correct. Rabbi Yeshayah’s plural wording, “they may collect,” refers to the two namesakes; if an outsider were collecting from them, singular language would be appropriate. His inference is from the Mishnah: only in one city may they not collect, but from two cities they may. That is obvious if the cities are written, so the Mishnah incidentally teaches that every document must identify the city, even where no known namesake exists. Otherwise the local borrower could claim that the debt belongs to another person of the same name in another city. The lender’s city must likewise be identified, lest the borrower obtain a receipt from a different lender bearing the same name. The phrase “this proves” is therefore Rabbi Yeshayah’s own inference from the Mishnah, not the Tur’s commentary.
