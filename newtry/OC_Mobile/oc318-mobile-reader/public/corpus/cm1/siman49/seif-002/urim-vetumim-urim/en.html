They must recognize, etc. In a loan document they must identify the borrower, and in a sale document the seller. This is obvious. Otherwise another person could present himself under Reuven's name and obligate himself before witnesses as owing Shimon one hundred, or pretend to sell his land, and then hand the deed to Shimon, who would sue the real Reuven, who knows nothing of the transaction. With a receipt, however, even if the witnesses know that the person giving the receipt to Shimon is indeed named Reuven, perhaps a different Shimon owes a different Reuven and the parties are manipulating names. Therefore both parties must be identified. See Tumim. I conclude that the required identification for a receipt means that the witnesses must know that this Shimon actually owes this Reuven who is issuing the receipt. Then the receipt is written properly. Although one can imagine further schemes, we do not pursue such remote possibilities indefinitely.
