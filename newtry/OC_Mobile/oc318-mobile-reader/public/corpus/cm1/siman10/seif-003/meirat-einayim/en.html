He did not come to the order that the elephant of Hashem had fallen, that he did not turn his days to the teaching of many of them:
One who has reached the level of issuing rulings, etc. The Sages interpret "and many are her slain" from the root meaning "to close one's eyes": one who is fit to rule but closes his eyes and refuses thereby destroys the people of his generation.
This is praiseworthy. The Mechaber writes something similar in siman 8:3. See what I wrote there and in the Perishah.
And anyone who refrains from judgment, etc. The word "anyone" means that even if the generation needs him, he should see whether he can refrain from formal adjudication and instead bring the litigants to compromise, if that is possible.
