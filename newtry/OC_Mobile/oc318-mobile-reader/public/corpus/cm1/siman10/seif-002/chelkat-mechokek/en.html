She is permitted to return to her husband who is of sound mind. This is the wording of Rambam, chapter 11 of Hilchot Gerushin. According to the reason given by the Maggid Mishneh, that since the second marriage has no effect by Torah law, as explained below in siman 44, seif 1, the Sages did not impose a stringency upon the first husband who is of sound mind, the same should apply to a minor orphan girl during her father's lifetime who married another man and was divorced. She should be permitted to return to the first husband, because a rabbinic divorce, as explained below in siman 155, seif 1, should not prohibit her to her first husband. One must say that this is less severe than a divorce granted because of a mere rumor, discussed above in seif 1, because there people may think that it was a full divorce, whereas regarding a deaf-mute and a minor everyone knows that there is no Torah-level kiddushin or divorce. See below siman 155, seif 21, concerning the kiddushin of a minor, which at first glance appears to contradict what is written in siman 44, seif 1, concerning a man of sound mind who married the wife of a deaf-mute. If one distinguishes between a deaf-mute and a minor, there would also be room to question the first ruling concerning an orphan during her father's lifetime, distinguishing a minor from a deaf-mute who married the wife of a man of sound mind. However, another distinction can be made so that siman 155 does not contradict siman 44, and therefore the first ruling concerning an orphan during her father's lifetime remains in place, with the same law as the wife of a man of sound mind who was divorced.
