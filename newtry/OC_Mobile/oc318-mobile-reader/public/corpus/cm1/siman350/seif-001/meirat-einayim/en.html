He stole forbidden fat, etc., until the value of the fat. We do not say that the more severe punishment absorbs the monetary liability. Although eating forbidden fat carries lashes and karet, the monetary liability arose earlier, when he lifted the fat in order to steal it. The lashes and karet arise only later, when he eats it. This differs from one who steals a purse on Shabbat and drags it into the public domain, where the monetary and capital liabilities can arise simultaneously, as the Tur and Mechaber write at the beginning of the next siman. The Beit Yosef writes that according to all opinions he is also liable for double payment, but the Mechaber omits that because he is not discussing penalties that are not adjudicated nowadays. The Tur records several additional rules here. First, although generally there is no agency for transgression, if a thief tells his agent to slaughter or sell, the thief is liable for fourfold or fivefold payment. Slaughter is compared to sale, and a sale necessarily involves another person, the purchaser. Second, although in the laws of Shabbat we do not rule like Rabbi Yochanan HaSandlar regarding food cooked on Shabbat, here for slaughter and sale we treat a slaughter prohibited because of Shabbat as an unfit slaughter and exempt from fourfold or fivefold payment. The reason depends on the dispute of Ravina and Rav Acha, and in such disputes the halachah follows the lenient position. Third, if he slaughtered and the animal was later found tereifah, he is nevertheless liable for fourfold or fivefold payment. At the time of slaughter he did not know of the tereifah and the slaughter was then regarded as fit. This differs from slaughter on Shabbat or explicit invalid slaughter, where the defect is known at the time of the act. See the Derishah and my glosses to the Ir Shushan.
