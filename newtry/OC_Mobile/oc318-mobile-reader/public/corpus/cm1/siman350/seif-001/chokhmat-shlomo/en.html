Seif 1. One who stole another person's forbidden fat and ate it, etc. Note: I was asked by the rabbi of the community of Leshnov, of blessed memory, whether, according to the rule that agency for a transgression does operate with respect to me'ilah and with respect to slaughtering a stolen animal, such agency also operates when the act is performed through a non-Jew. I answered him that, in my view, it is clear that agency does not operate through a non-Jew in these cases. With respect to me'ilah itself, the Rambam writes that this special rule applies only when no additional prohibition is combined with it. This proves that we say agency operates in me'ilah only to the extent expressly revealed by Scripture; what Scripture did not reveal remains outside the exception. Accordingly, if another disqualification is combined with the act, one that independently prevents the law of agency from applying, agency does not operate. This follows all the more strongly here. If even a disqualification that itself arises from the principle that there is no agency for a transgression, although it concerns the same type of prohibition, remains outside the Scriptural exception, then certainly a wholly independent disqualification to agency is likewise excluded, because the exception extends no further than what Scripture revealed. This conclusion is also compelled by the Gemara in Perek 2 of Kiddushin. The rule of agency in me'ilah is derived there from terumah through the Scriptural comparison to terumah. Yet in terumah itself a non-Jew cannot serve as agent, for the exposition of "you, also you" was stated primarily regarding terumah. It follows that the agency recognized in me'ilah, which is derived from terumah, does not apply through a non-Jew. Moreover, if we were to say that agency for slaughtering a stolen animal did apply through a non-Jew, the two Scriptural exceptions would no longer be two verses teaching the same principle. Why, then, would the Gemara say there that they cannot serve as a precedent for other cases? Necessarily, slaughtering as well does not create agency through a non-Jew. This is clear and true. This is what I answered the aforementioned rabbi. Understand this well.
