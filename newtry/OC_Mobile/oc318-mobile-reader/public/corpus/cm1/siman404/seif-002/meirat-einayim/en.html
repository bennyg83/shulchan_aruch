He gives according to the value at the time of standing in judgment. The Gemara says that the tortfeasor can say: "The horn of your ox is still buried in my ox." Meaning, so long as the injured animal remains alive, the effect and pain of the goring are still operating within it, and its continuing decline is attributable to the original injury. Therefore valuation follows the later condition. This differs from the previous siman, where the animal died immediately from the goring. Later depreciation of the carcass is no longer caused by the pain of the injury.

Some say that if the appreciation exceeds the expense, etc. The Tur adds that if the expense exceeds the appreciation, the injured party receives no part of the appreciation but takes the amount due according to the damage at the time of injury. In the Perishah I explained that while he receives no appreciation, he also need not contribute to the excess expense. The tortfeasor bears the amount by which expense exceeds appreciation. Although partners ordinarily share necessary expenses when one partner descends with permission, here the tortfeasor is at most like a partner who descended without permission, and the rules differ.
