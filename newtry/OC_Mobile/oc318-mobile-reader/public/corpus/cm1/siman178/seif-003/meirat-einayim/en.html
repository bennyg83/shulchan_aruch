Epic in Kama, which stands to be harvested, means that there are arches who take part in the praise of the Bastar and the abandonment of its own, namely, when it is about to reap and create even though I will then continue to fulfill Harris or the recipient:
And even with a field not intended for planting. This is an additional novelty. Even if the usual use of the field had been sowing and the partner instead planted it, he is not treated as one who deviated and therefore has the lower hand. Rather, he is like one who descended into the field with authorization and receives improvement like a sharecropper, as though the field were intended for planting. The Tur and Mechaber phrase this imprecisely. Since the immediately preceding discussion concerns a field standing ready for harvest, meaning a sown field, they should have written, "even if it was not intended for sowing." They shortened the language because the Gemara and Rambam mention both cases and they adopted parts of the wording of the opening and closing clauses. See the Perishah.
The partner does not recover his full expenditures, etc. Nimukei Yosef writes that where the improvement exceeds the expenditures, the partner is treated like one who entered with permission. But if the expenditures exceed the improvement, he is not fully treated like one who entered with permission, who would recover all his expenditures even beyond the improvement. This partner recovers expenditures only up to the amount of the improvement and must swear. He is no better than one who spends money improving his wife's property and is later divorced. The authorities therefore say that he is "like" one who entered with permission, using the comparative expression, rather than that he is literally treated as such in every respect. See below siman 375:4 for further discussion.
