The partner does not. He is no better than a husband who spent money on his wife's property. See Rambam, chapter 10 of the Laws of Robbery, halakha 7, who compares the two. See below, siman 375, seif 5: 'A husband,' etc.
If he protested, etc. In that case his status is that of one who entered without permission, as stated in Bava Metzia 101a. Rambam rules accordingly there, and so does the Shulchan Aruch there in seifim 2 and 6, that if the owner of the field or land says, 'Remove,' etc., and take what you built, he is obeyed. Maggid Mishneh explains that this applies specifically to one who entered without permission. All this is proven by the Gemara there: 'He said to him: Go appraise it for him, and his hand is on the lower side. He said: I do not want it. In the end,' etc. Meaning, he says that he does not want the improvement and instead requires the builder to remove what is his. See the gloss there, 'specifically,' etc., and seif 7 concerning courtyards, etc.
