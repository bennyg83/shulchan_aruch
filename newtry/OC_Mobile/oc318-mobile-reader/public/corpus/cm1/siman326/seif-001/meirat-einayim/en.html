To sow sesame, etc. The Tur writes that sesame weakens the land more than wheat and is also worth more than wheat seed.
He has only a grievance against him. Some explain that this means the landowner has a grievance against the sharecropper who deviated from their agreement to plant sesame, because the owner wanted sesame. Although he could buy sesame in the market with the proceeds of the wheat, he prefers his own produce. There is only a grievance, not a monetary loss. In my opinion, however, it is more plausible that the sharecropper has a grievance against the landowner because he labored until the wheat crop became equal in value to the sesame crop and nevertheless must give the owner half, without deducting the advantage the owner gained because the soil was not weakened as much. The Tur records all the substance of this law, though he does not state explicitly who has the grievance. He then cites Rambam's words, which are the same as the Mechaber's, that one party has a grievance against the other.
The sharecropper must pay him according to, etc. This teaches that the sharecropper cannot say, "I intended to benefit you by not weakening the soil." He must nevertheless compensate the owner, because people say: better that the earth be weakened than that its owner be weakened through loss.
If the wheat produced more than it ordinarily should, etc. The Tur writes that the sharecropper cannot say: had I planted sesame, the ground would have been weakened more, and the wheat also would not have succeeded so well had I not exerted myself greatly. Therefore I should deduct from your share the benefit you gained because the soil was not weakened, and I should also receive the entire excess value of the wheat over the sesame because it came from my labor. The owner can answer: was it your labor alone that caused the result, and not the quality of my field? Therefore they divide the increase.
