Or documents of. This is Rambam's language. The Shulchan Aruch omits it because forgiveness does not require a kinyan and such a document serves only as evidence. It appears that Rambam holds that only a debt document and a deed of sale written in their courts are valid, whereas other documents are invalid even if they merely serve as evidence, because the testimony of those courts has no independent evidentiary force. Rambam says so in his Commentary to the Mishnah. Only with loan and sale documents, where such a major transaction was carried out before them, do they avoid compromising their reputation. This explains the Gemara's otherwise superfluous phrase, "If not..." It could simply have said that the document is only evidence and that they do not damage their reputation. Rather, only in such major transactions do they refrain from doing so. Rambam therefore requires the document to state specifically that they witnessed the payment of the money. The same reasoning applies to documents of admission and forgiveness. The Shulchan Aruch's phrase that "the essential acquisition..." and that "so-and-so admitted..." is all the Tur's language and follows the Tur's approach. Rambam does not make this distinction; according to him all deeds of gift and admissions written by such courts are invalid even where the acquisition itself was not performed through them, which is why Rambam stated his rule generally.
