A document written in any language, etc. See Baal HaTerumos, Shaar 87, concerning the laws in this siman, Responsa Mahari ibn Lev, part 3, siman 110, Responsa Rama, siman 52, and Responsa Mabit, part 2, siman 309.
And they know how to read it. This follows the Gemara's resolution in the second chapter of Gittin, 19b, that the witnesses themselves know how to read. If they cannot read the language, they should not sign initially merely because someone else reads it before them, even if they understand the language when spoken. Nevertheless, if they did sign, the document is valid after the fact where two other Jews translated the language for them, even if the signatories themselves did not understand it. I reached this conclusion above in siman 45, seif 2. See Responsa Mahari ibn Chaviv, siman 127, Responsa Maharash Kohen, part 2, siman 102, and part 3, simanim 20 and 111.
And debt documents. The Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe write that we rule like the Rambam on this point because Baal HaItur, Ramban, Rashba, Rosh, and Ran agree. This requires study, because those authorities discuss a different issue. I also examined Baal HaItur and did not find such a statement, while the Ran appears to imply the opposite. See there. Examine the Raavad's glosses and the Maggid Mishneh and you will find support for my explanation: a debt document is different because, where it does not collect from encumbered property and therefore generates no public notice, the debtor is not careful to obtain it back and may still claim payment. See there. See also Responsa Mahari ibn Lev, part 1, siman 62, folio 86.
From unencumbered property, and even with deeds of sale. According to this reading, such a document is inferior even to a sale of land made before witnesses, because the Rambam holds that specifically Jewish witnesses generate public notice. The Maggid Mishneh, however, writes that according to the Rambam, deeds of sale should be valid even to collect from encumbered property because they are no worse than a sale before witnesses. Still, the unqualified wording of the Rambam, Tur, and Mechaber does not imply this distinction. The Bach likewise rules otherwise. See Responsa Maharash Kohen, part 3, siman 111, Responsa Mahari MiBeis Levi, siman 24, and Responsa Maharashdam, siman 350.

Or documents of forgiveness, etc. See Shiltei Giborim in Perek Mi SheMes, folio 234b, and Responsa Maharashdam, siman 350. The Sema writes in Perishah that the case does not concern a document stating that the creditor already forgave the debt in the witnesses' presence. If so, the document would serve merely as evidence, and we have already explained that such evidentiary documents made in non Jewish courts can be effective. Rather, the document itself contains the act of forgiveness, so that the forgiveness is effected by the document and the instrument is analogous to a deed of gift. His words require study, because this is plainly not the Rama's meaning. Otherwise, why would the Rama cite an opinion that disagrees and says that documents of forgiveness are valid? In the Sema's case, where the document is constitutive rather than merely evidentiary, even those dissenting authorities would agree that it is invalid. Indeed, the Rivash disagrees precisely because of the Sema's difficulty, that such a document should be only evidentiary, as is explicit in the Beit Yosef in the name of the Rivash. The Bach writes that the document is still not proof because the party can say, "I said that I forgave only jokingly and in jest." He discusses this at length. His words are strained, and the practical law requires study.
