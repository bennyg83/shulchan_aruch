All documents whose signatories are non Jews. This refers to documents in which the body of the document itself effects the acquisition, for example where it says, "My field is given to you," and the field is acquired through delivery of the document, or in a sale where it says, "My field is sold to you," and the seller intends to transfer ownership through this document. Such a document is invalid, because non Jews do not possess legal agency to effect this Jewish documentary acquisition, and they are neither valid witnesses nor valid participants in such a document:
Except documents of commerce. This means that money was paid and the field was acquired through the money, while the document serves only as evidence. The same applies to a loan document. Even such a document is invalid by Torah law because non Jews are not valid witnesses. The Sages validated it only for the sake of orderly commerce. Specifically a document created in an official non Jewish court is trusted because the officials will not damage their own professional standing by fabricating a false document. But when non Jews merely testify orally, they may risk their standing and lie, and they are not believed. See Beurim:

With an ordinary document executed before witnesses to delivery, all agree that it must be drafted according to the formal requirements of Jewish documents.
