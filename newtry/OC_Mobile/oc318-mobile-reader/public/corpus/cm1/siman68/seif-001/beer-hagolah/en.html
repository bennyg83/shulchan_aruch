a) The wording of the Rambam at the beginning of chapter 27 of Hilkhot Malveh VeLoveh, from Ameimar's statement, Gittin 19b.
b) Mishnah and Gemara there, 10b, according to the latter version, except for bills of divorce. This is also the view of most Geonim. The rule of dina d'malchuta dina is not applied to such documents because that rule concerns civil relations between people where the king has an interest or benefit in the law; here there is no such benefit to the sovereign. Maggid Mishneh.
c) As Rafram said to Ravina there, 11a. The Tur writes that it appears the Rambam explains the case as one where gentile witnesses sign the document and the official court, whose judge is appointed by the king, authenticates their signatures.
d) So the Rambam explains what the Rif writes there, specifically official courts that do not accept bribes.
e) There, according to the textual version that reads in Ameimar's statement "from unencumbered property." The Rambam explains that this is because the document lacks public notice, since purchasers did not know what was done in the gentile court.
f) There in the Gemara, according to the latter version, as stated above.
g) So it appears there in the Rambam.
h) So writes the Maggid Mishneh in the name of Baal HaIttur, the Ramban, and the Rashba, that we rule like Shmuel that dina d'malchuta dina.
i) So too writes the Tur there at the end of the siman.
See Rashba, siman 982, regarding an official court record stating that Reuven purchased a particular field, where there was a line drawn through part of it and Reuven was in possession of the field, etc. Sema in the name of Darkei Moshe.
