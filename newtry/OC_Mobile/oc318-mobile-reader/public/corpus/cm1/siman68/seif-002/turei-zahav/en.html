(Tur: “The Ramah likewise ruled,” etc.) This must mean only that both interpret the Gemara’s term “gentile courts” as referring to the judge who authenticates the signatures. Their substantive requirements nevertheless differ. According to the Rambam, the reliability of both the witnesses and the judge must be established. According to the Ramah, once the judge’s reliability is known, the reliability of the witnesses follows, because such a court accepts only witnesses considered trustworthy by it. The Rosh objects to both approaches, since the reliability of gentile witnesses cannot truly be known. His objection to the Rambam concerns the required knowledge of both groups; his objection to the Ramah is that the court’s reliability cannot establish that of the witnesses, since ordinary gentile tribunals accept bribes. The Gemara therefore refers to established official courts.
