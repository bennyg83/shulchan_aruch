If Jewish judges do not know how to read a document executed in a non Jewish court, they give it to two non Jews separately, each without the other present, and each reads it as one speaking innocently without having been asked to testify. On that basis the creditor may collect from unencumbered property. A document whose witnesses are non Jews, but which the borrower delivered to the lender or the seller delivered to the purchaser in the presence of two Jewish witnesses, may be used to collect from unencumbered property even though it was not executed in an official non Jewish court and does not contain all the formalities listed above, provided that the Jewish witnesses to delivery know how to read it and read it when it was delivered in their presence, as stated by the Rosh, and provided it is prepared like a Jewish document so that it cannot be forged, added to, or diminished. Some say this applies only when the names of the signed witnesses are unmistakably non Jewish names that Jews are not normally called, or in a place where Jews do not ordinarily sign documents at all. Rama: If a document was executed in a non Jewish court and the borrower denies it, the scribe is believed to testify that he wrote it. If a document was executed before non Jews and the borrower himself signed it, he is bound by everything written in it even if he cannot read, because by signing he resolved to obligate himself, and we presume it was read before him and that he trusted the person who read it. See above, siman 45, seif 3.
