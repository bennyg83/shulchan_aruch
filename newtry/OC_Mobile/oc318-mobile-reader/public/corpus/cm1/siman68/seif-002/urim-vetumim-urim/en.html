And he collects with it from unencumbered property. This follows the Rambam, who holds that documents of non Jewish courts do not permit collection from encumbered property. According to the Rosh, whose view the Rama cited above, one may collect even from encumbered property.
He does not collect even from unencumbered property. See Tumim for why this is not considered internally forged, since non Jews are signed upon it. I explained there that according to this view we say so only concerning documents of gift and similar documents that themselves effect an acquisition, but not loan documents that merely serve as evidence.
From unencumbered property. Although in ordinary cases this opinion holds that delivery in the presence of witnesses permits collection from encumbered property, here the signature of non Jewish witnesses makes the case inferior, because they do not generate publicity. The witnesses to delivery also generate no publicity, since observers rely upon the signed witnesses, and there is therefore no public notice. Nevertheless the debtor cannot say, "I paid," because once the instrument has the status of a document he would not leave it with the lender after payment.
And it must conform to Jewish legal requirements, etc. Above, regarding a document issued by a recognized court, this requirement was not stated, because that is the view of the Rambam, Ramah, and Ittur, who hold that documents of recognized courts are valid in every form according to the law and practice of those courts. See Tumim at the beginning of the siman, where I proved that Tosafot also hold this view. Many authorities disagree and hold that even a court document must conform to Jewish documentary law.
The judge is believed, etc. The debtor is thereby established as one who falsely denied his obligation, according to the Sema. Although the Rosh wrote that court officials do not lie, this might mean only in writing, whereas orally they may lie. One must then explain that when the judge testifies that this is his handwriting and that he wrote the document, this itself concerns what was written, since if the underlying matter were shown false, he would have given false written testimony. But as to matters not contained in the writing, his oral statement would not be believed. The Mordechai, however, implies that they are believed even orally because they do not lie. In Tumim I concluded that the Mordechai's view is primary.
