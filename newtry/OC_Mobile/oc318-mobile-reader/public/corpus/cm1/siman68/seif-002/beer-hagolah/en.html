k) There, according to the Gemara's interpretation on 9b, following Rabbi Elazar that witnesses to delivery effect the instrument. As for why the Rambam does not distinguish between clearly gentile names and names that are not clearly gentile, as the Gemara does at the beginning of 11a, the Kesef Mishneh writes that perhaps the concern that a document is intrinsically defective because it bears gentile witnesses applies only to gittin. In monetary documents, although related Jewish witnesses create an intrinsic defect because one may confuse the case and validate the document without witnesses to delivery, we do not have that concern with gentile witnesses, even when their names are not unmistakably gentile.
l) Tur, seif 7, in the name of the Ramah, according to the Gemara's interpretation at the beginning of Gittin 11a that the case concerns unmistakably gentile names. The Ramah holds that since Rabbi Shimon's view was established according to Rabbi Elazar, the halachah follows him. The same applies to other documents: where the names are not unmistakably gentile, we are concerned that people may rely upon them, assuming without further inquiry that they are Jewish witnesses.
