And debt instruments. The Shach rules like Raavad that where a document executed in a gentile court does not collect from encumbered property and lacks ordinary publicity, the borrower may claim payment, because he was not careful to retrieve the instrument after paying. Rivash, responsum 475, distinguishes. According to the Geonim, a handwritten obligation that has no status of a formal document may be left with the creditor after payment because it does not collect from encumbered property. But a full formal document, even one that for a particular reason cannot collect from purchasers, is still something people ordinarily do not leave in a creditor's hands after payment. Rivash brings proof from documents executed in gentile courts and implies that no payment claim is accepted against them. This distinction is persuasive: the key question is not mechanically whether the instrument collects from encumbered property, but whether it has the social and legal character of a formal document that a debtor ordinarily retrieves after payment.
