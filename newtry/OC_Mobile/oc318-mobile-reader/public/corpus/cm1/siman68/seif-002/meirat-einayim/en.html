He may collect with it, etc. This view holds that the case is no worse than one where the transaction or admission took place before Jewish witnesses to delivery without any written instrument at all. Therefore the Mechaber writes that collection may be made only from unencumbered property. Although a document delivered before Jewish witnesses, even without Jewish witnesses signed upon it, ordinarily allows collection even from encumbered property, as explained by the Tur and Mechaber at the ends of simanim 40, 51, and 69, here the body of the instrument and the witnesses signed upon it are non-Jewish, so its status is weaker. Nevertheless, it still has the status of a document to the extent that the borrower cannot claim payment while the document remains in the creditor's possession. See the Perishah and Derishah.

According to Rambam, there is room to question whether these requirements apply. See there.
