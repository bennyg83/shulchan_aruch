And all this, etc. This follows Shevuot 48a, where Rabbi Yehudah's formulation, 'When does this apply,' is understood as explaining the Sages. The mishnah concerns those who swear and collect, and the same applies to a moneychanger and a householder. See Rif and Ran.
(Supplement) He said to the shopkeeper, etc.; the purchaser gave, etc. Rif and Rambam understand Rabbi Yehudah as explaining, not disputing, the Sages, and apply the condition that the produce remains piled up to both clauses. Tosafot in the name of Rabbenu Tam, followed by Rosh and Tur, read the case differently: in the first clause the produce was placed in the householder's vessel but remained in the shopkeeper's domain, while in the second the coin remained in the shop. According to Rif and Rambam, Rabbi Yehudah disputes the latter clause and the householder collects with an oath because the produce is closer to his possession; his words explain both the first tanna and Rabbi Yehudah. Tosafot maintain that Rabbi Yehudah disputes both clauses and allows the householder to collect without an oath, while 'When does this apply' explains the Sages. They therefore differ from Rif in five points: the object left in place, the reading concerning the public domain, whether Rabbi Yehudah requires an oath, whether he disputes the first clause, and whose view 'When does this apply' explains. Raavad explains that the first clause concerns produce already slung behind the householder, where the Sages instituted an oath only because it remains in the shop, while in the second the produce is piled on the ground; Rabbi Yehudah disputes only the first clause. Rabbenu Chananel substantially follows Rif but understands the coin, not the produce, to be lying in the public domain. Rashi understands Rabbi Yehudah to dispute the first clause and treats the oath as a hesset oath, but Tosafot object that hesset had not yet been instituted. It may instead be understood as an oath resembling a Torah oath because the goods remain on his shoulder. See citations 80 and 81.
(Supplement) According to Semag. His view must be aligned with Tosafot: Rabbi Yehudah permits collection without an oath and 'When does this apply' explains the Sages. In summary, Rif has the produce piled up in the latter clause; Rabbenu Chananel has the coin lying there; Tosafot place it in the shop; Rashi places the relevant item in the possessor's hand; Rif reads the phrase as explaining both views, Rabbenu Chananel and Tosafot as explaining the Sages, Rashi as disputing them, and Raavad as described above.
