The purchaser takes an oath in accordance with the enactment of the Sages, etc. The reason is that the shopkeeper admits that he placed these fruits in the public domain for this purchaser, who had bought them, and claims only that the purchaser has not yet paid him. The shopkeeper is therefore treated as one who seeks to extract money from the purchaser. The purchaser accordingly takes an oath and is exempt from paying the dinar, while taking the fruits that the shopkeeper admits he purchased. In the latter case, however, the shopkeeper does not admit that he placed those fruits in the public domain for this purchaser. The purchaser is then the one seeking to remove known property from the shopkeeper's possession. The shopkeeper therefore takes an oath and is exempt from the dinar that the purchaser says he already paid, and the fruits remain with the shopkeeper. The same applies in the corresponding case of a money changer and a customer, as the Mechaber concludes. These rulings follow the approach of the Rif, Rambam, and Ran. According to Rashi, Tosafot, the Rosh, and the Tur, however, the law is not so. I discussed this at length in the Derishah and demonstrated from several proofs that there are three approaches to this sugya. The Mechaber states the matter according to the Rambam because in the Beit Yosef he understands even the Rosh and the Tur as following the Rif and Rambam. For this reason Rama too says nothing here in the name of an alternative view, as is normally his practice, and in Darkei Moshe he cites the Beit Yosef without comment. In my view, however, that understanding is incorrect, as I demonstrated there. Examine this carefully.
