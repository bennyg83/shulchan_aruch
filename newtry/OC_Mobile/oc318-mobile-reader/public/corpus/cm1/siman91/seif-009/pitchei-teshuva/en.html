It is true. Baer Heitev explains that this evidentiary value applies to a ledger that records obligations against its owner, but not to a ledger used to impose obligations upon another person. Teshuvot Noda BiYehudah, second edition, siman 15, rules likewise and writes that no distinction exists between ordinary account books and the principal business ledger carefully maintained by merchants. Such a ledger has no special evidentiary superiority under Jewish law. He explains the Yerushalmi concerning the shopkeeper's ledger in the same manner, and this is also clear from a responsum of the Rosh, klal 86.
