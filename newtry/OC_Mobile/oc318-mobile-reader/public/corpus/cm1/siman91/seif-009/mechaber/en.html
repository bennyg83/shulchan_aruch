If a buyer tells a storekeeper, 'Give me fruit for one dinar,' the storekeeper gives it, and the fruit is lying in the public domain, while the storekeeper demands the dinar and the buyer says, 'I gave it to you, and you threw it into your purse,' the buyer takes the oath instituted by the Sages while holding a sacred object and takes the fruit. If the buyer gave the storekeeper a dinar and comes to take fruit lying in the public domain, while the storekeeper says, 'That dinar was payment for fruit I already gave you and which you brought into your house,' the storekeeper takes an oath while holding a sacred object. The same law applies when one gives a dinar to a money changer in order to take coins piled in the public domain. If the money changer admits that he sold them but says he has not yet received the dinar, the buyer takes an oath while holding a sacred object that he gave the dinar and takes the coins. If the money changer does not admit selling them, then even if he admits that he received a dinar from the buyer now but claims it was payment for coins the buyer had already brought into his house, the money changer takes an oath while holding a sacred object. {Rama: All this applies when the fruit or money lies in the public domain. If the buyer has already brought it into his domain and the storekeeper demands the dinar, the buyer takes a heses oath and is exempt. If the fruit or money is no longer in the storekeeper's domain and the buyer claims it, the storekeeper takes a heses oath and is exempt.}
