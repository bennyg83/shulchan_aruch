Indications. If one merely says, 'I found in my ledger that you owe me,' we do not rely upon his ledger. The Rosh states this in a responsum and the Tur cites it. It implies that in such a case we do not rely upon the ledger at all, so he cannot even require the defendant to take a hesset oath because there are no supporting indications. Otherwise, why mention a ledger? Even if he stated the claim with certainty he would not thereby be entitled to swear and collect. Accordingly, when the Rama says that the same applies where one relies upon his ledger as true, this means that he knows clearly that his ledger is maintained with great precision, which itself constitutes supporting indications. If so, there is no distinction between swearing in order to collect and imposing an oath upon the defendant on the basis of the ledger. The ruling in siman 75, seif 23, that one may impose an oath on the basis of his father's ledger applies even if he does not know that his father's ledger was precise. To collect from heirs in the following seif, however, supporting indications and convincing presumptions are certainly required. See siman 81, seif 23 in the gloss. Shach.
