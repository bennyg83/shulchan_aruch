And she sold it and died, it remains, etc. During her lifetime the husband consumes the fruits and the purchaser receives no fruits. One should not ask, according to the Rosh cited above in the gloss to siman 90:9, that even during her lifetime the husband can remove the body of the land from the purchaser, and therefore perhaps the subsequent beneficiary should acquire rather than the purchaser because the purchaser acquired nothing during her life. Here it is different. Since the donor explicitly excluded the husband and said, "After you, so-and-so shall acquire, but the husband shall not acquire," the husband does not have the status of the first purchaser. The purchaser from the woman acquires the body of the land even according to the Rosh, while the husband merely consumes the fruits during her lifetime. Afterward the purchaser acquires fully, and he precedes the subsequent beneficiary. See Bach, who writes that according to the Rosh one can perhaps say that if the husband did remove the property from the purchaser it remains with him, even though this causes loss to the purchaser. In my humble opinion, the matter is clear as I have written.
