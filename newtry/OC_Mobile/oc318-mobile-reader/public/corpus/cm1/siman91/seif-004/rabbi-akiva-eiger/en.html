[Shulchan Aruch] The matter may be adjudicated on the basis of his ledger. Note: Gedulei Terumah, Shaar 29, part 1, siman 2, discusses at length why the shopkeeper should collect without an oath on the basis of his ledger and supporting circumstances, arguing that the employer should be no better than heirs. See there. With due respect, however, there is no difficulty. Here we adjudicate a claimant’s right to extract money on the basis of the defendant’s own ledger, which is like an admission by the defendant. In the case of the shopkeeper, however, the Rosh rules only that although the shopkeeper does not remember independently, he may still advance a definite claim based on his own ledger together with supporting circumstances. It is obvious that he must nevertheless swear, because his own ledger is no stronger than his own completely definite claim, upon which he would still have to swear lest he be lying. This is exceedingly straightforward.
