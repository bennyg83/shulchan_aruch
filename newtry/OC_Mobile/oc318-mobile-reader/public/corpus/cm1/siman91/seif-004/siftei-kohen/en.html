Since there are grounds supporting the matter. If one merely says, “I found in my ledger that you owe me,” the ledger is not relied upon. The Rosh writes this in a responsum and derives it from the Mishna, as cited by the Tur. This implies that the ledger is not relied upon at all in such a case, so the claimant cannot even impose a hesset oath upon the defendant when there are no supporting circumstances. Otherwise, why discuss a ledger? Even one who makes a definite claim cannot swear and collect merely on that basis. Accordingly, when the Rosh and Rama say that a person may swear regarding any matter on the basis of a ledger upon which he relies as true, they refer to one who knows with certainty that his ledger is maintained with exact care. That itself constitutes grounds supporting the matter. This is also implied by the language of the Rosh's responsum: “It appears that a person who records all his affairs carefully in his ledger so that he will remember them” may swear on that basis. Rama's words, “upon which he relies as true,” likewise mean that he knows the ledger is exact and never mistaken, or that he has other supporting circumstances. Accordingly, there is no distinction between swearing in order to collect and imposing an oath upon the defendant on the basis of a ledger. Nevertheless, above in siman 75:23, one may impose an oath on the basis of his father's ledger even when he does not know that his father's ledger was maintained with precision. To collect from heirs under the following seif, however, there must certainly be supporting circumstances and compelling indications, as in the incident discussed in the Rosh's responsum below. The Bach does not appear at first glance to agree with this, but perhaps his words can also be understood accordingly. See also siman 81:23 in Rama.
