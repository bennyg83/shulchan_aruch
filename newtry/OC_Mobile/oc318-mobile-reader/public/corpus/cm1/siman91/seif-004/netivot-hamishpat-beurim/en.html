And some say. See Shach seif katan 21, who questions this view from siman 121. The view can be explained as follows. Its central reason for imposing only a shevuat heset upon the workers is that the shopkeeper's bari assertion is not considered a true bari claim against the workers because he is financially interested in the outcome. The reason both parties swear and collect in seif 1 is instead that the employer himself makes a bari claim that he cannot owe two payments, only one, and that is considered a sufficient bari claim, like the two-depositor cases in siman 76, as explained above. Although Bava Metzia 3 says that the workers can argue, "What do we have to do with the shopkeeper?" Shitah Mekubetzet explains that this establishes the employer's payment liability because the uncertainty arose through his negligence. It does not remove the need for an oath. In the two-depositor case, where the bailee was negligent and must pay both, each claimant still must swear, as explained in siman 76. Here, when the shopkeeper himself does not collect and the employer owes him nothing, the employer no longer has a direct bari claim against the workers arising from a competing payment. Therefore the workers are not subjected to the severe oath. They may still be required to take a shevuat heset, because the presumption that an agent performs his mission creates sufficient grounds for suspicion, similar to the rule in siman 78 seif 17 in Rama. This also resolves siman 121, as Tumim suggests. There, the agent has a migo that he could have said the money was lost through unavoidable accident. He is therefore not financially interested and his statement can function as a bari claim on behalf of the sender, creating the testimony-like basis that requires the recipient to swear before collecting. Here, however, the case is one where money was given to a moneychanger to exchange and deliver different coins to the worker. The moneychanger has no migo of unavoidable accident regarding the new coins, and he also lacks a migo of return because the instruction "give" can operate like a transfer of entitlement. He is therefore interested when he says that he paid the worker, and his statement does not create a full bari claim. The worker cannot be required to take the severe oath, only a shevuat heset after receiving payment, when the claim becomes bari for the reason explained. The law of ma'amad sheloshtan will be explained, with God's help, in siman 126.
