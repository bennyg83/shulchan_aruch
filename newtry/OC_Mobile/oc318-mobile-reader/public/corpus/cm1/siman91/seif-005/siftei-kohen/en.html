Where there are grounds supporting the matter. For example, Reuven delivered one thousand zuz to Shimon for investment without a document four months before Shimon's death. Shimon died without leaving instructions. After his death a ledger in his handwriting was found, recording the collateral taken for Reuven's one thousand zuz under a profit-sharing arrangement. The collateral itself was found with that ledger, and every note bore Reuven's name. The Rosh therefore returned Reuven's money to him even though Shimon left a minor son, as stated in the Rosh's responsa and cited by the Tur. Responsa Mahari ben Lev, beginning of siman 15, rules likewise. See also Responsa Maharam Alshich 137, Mabit II 74, Maharshal 200, and Maharit 72, and below at the end of siman 250. The Rosh further writes that a person who regularly records every business transaction immediately in his ledger, and does not find a particular transaction recorded there, may swear concerning it, for this is considered a definite assertion because it is absent from his ledger. Mahari ben Lev, book II, siman 50, writes that according to this, if fifty is recorded in his ledger and fifty is not recorded, he is not treated as one who is obligated to swear but cannot do so. He adds, however, that the Rosh speaks only of a person who records his transactions immediately in a ledger kept before him. If he waits in the interim, he may have forgotten and failed to record the matter. See there.
