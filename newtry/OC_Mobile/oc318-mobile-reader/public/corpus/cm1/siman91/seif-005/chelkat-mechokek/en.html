And she married and died. In Choshen Mishpat 103:4, the Mechaber copies Rambam's language: if land was appraised and transferred to a woman and she married, or land was appraised away from her and she married, the husband has the status of a purchaser with respect to his wife's property and the original owner cannot compel him to reverse the appraisal. The Mechaber there does not mention that she died. Maggid Mishneh explains, in Hilchot Malveh VeLoveh chapter 22, that if land was appraised to a woman and she then married, and the former owners wish to repay the money and recover the land, the husband need not return it, because the Sages treated him as a purchaser. If he were merely an heir, he would not yet inherit during his wife's lifetime. Likewise, if land was appraised away from her and she then married, and either she or her husband wishes to return the money, the original holder need not return the land. He need not return it to the husband because the husband is like a purchaser after the appraisal, and not to the woman because we treat her as though she had sold her property. Nevertheless, if the husband dies during her lifetime and she then wishes to repay the money, the land is returned to her; similarly, if the former owners repay her, she returns the land. Rashi and the Rosh, however, explain the rule specifically where the woman died, because only then does the husband become a complete purchaser of the body of the property. During her lifetime, although he is treated as a purchaser regarding fruits, the body remains hers. Why then should she not be able to return the land to the original owners? All the more so where land was appraised away from her, why should she not be able to redeem her own land during her lifetime? Maggid Mishneh's statement, "not to the woman, because we treat her as though she sold her property," is not clear to me, because the land was appraised away from her before marriage. The Gemara's simple rule is that it is not returned to her. One can perhaps say that the principle of what is right and good applies only where a person fully returns to his estate. Here the husband would consume the fruits, and that is not a complete restoration of what is right and good. The Mechaber here rules without qualification like Rashi and the Rosh by writing, "and she married and died," while in Choshen Mishpat he rules without qualification like Rambam. See Lechem Chamudot here; his words are not clear to me.
