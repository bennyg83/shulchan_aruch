[Shach, subparagraph 10] And the case concerns workers who have no heirs. Note: This still requires examination. In such a case, perhaps it is not considered one certain payment obligation. Specifically where the workers have heirs, even though he need not pay them directly, the employer certainly owes one payment, either to the shopkeeper or to the workers’ heirs. His exemption from the workers’ heirs results only from the shopkeeper’s contradictory claim that perhaps the shopkeeper paid the workers. If so, the employer must then pay the shopkeeper, and one can understand why the shopkeeper might collect without an oath, because the employer loses nothing. The shopkeeper’s claim that he paid the workers itself exempts the employer from their heirs. But where the workers have no heirs, even if the shopkeeper never paid them, the employer is free from payment and keeps the money as ownerless property. In such a case, one can say that the shopkeeper must swear. See Shach, subparagraph 1, and consider carefully. See also Knesset HaGedolah, subparagraph 12, and Mahari ben Lev, part 2, siman 50.
