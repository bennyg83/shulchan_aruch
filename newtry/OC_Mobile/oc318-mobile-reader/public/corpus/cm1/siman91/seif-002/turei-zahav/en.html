(Seif 3) “And the workers consented.” Sema, seif katan 9, writes that even silence constitutes consent, because the later clause does not state a greater novelty. I see no proof. The later clause teaches that even if the workers expressly consented, the employer is not discharged, because the transfer did not occur in the presence of all three parties. As to the law itself, it appears to me that silence is ineffective here. The workers may say, “We did not consider it necessary to answer you, because we never accepted the arrangement,” as stated in siman 81.
