When does this apply... The Gemara compares this to two lenders. If there were only one lender he would collect without an oath, as explained above, because no oath is taken on a doubtful claim. This applies specifically where both parties make claims, as above.
And some say specifically... As stated at the end of Bava Kamma 118a.
But if... Then it is like one who says, "I do not know whether I owe you," as stated there. Compare Bava Kamma 114b regarding one who recognizes his articles and must swear, and Perek HaIshah Shenaflu concerning one who spent money on his wife's property. Rabbeinu Chananel derives from there that whenever one party knows and the other does not, the one who knows swears, provided that he expended with permission. The first view distinguishes our case because of the presumption that an agent carries out his mission, a presumption relied upon even in serious prohibitions. Kesubos teaches that although generally a certain claim does not automatically prevail over a doubtful one because of the defendant's presumption of possession, where an additional presumption supports the claimant the result can differ. See Kesubos 75b and Baal HaTerumos citing Rabbi Yosef ibn Plat. This differs from recognizing one's articles or spending on a wife's property, where the amount expended is itself unknown and therefore requires an oath.
