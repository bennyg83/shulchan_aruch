Another payment. The Shach explains that the reason given is not simply lack of a certain claim because the case is one where the parties had already contradicted one another while alive. The Mechaber implies that if they died, the heirs do not collect, even the workers' heirs. See Beurim: according to the Mechaber, if both sides died after having already contradicted one another but before taking their oaths, the money remains deposited until Eliyahu comes. If they had never contradicted one another and neither the shopkeeper nor workers had demanded payment from the employer, so that it is uncertain whether payment had already been made, they divide because there is no dishonest claimant. The Shach rules that if the workers died, payment must be made to their heirs. See Beurim: specifically where they contradicted one another while alive does the dispute arise. Otherwise there is no payment to heirs. But if the shopkeeper died, then where only one payment is required, it is paid to the shopkeeper's heirs. Where two payments would be required, no payment is made to the shopkeeper's heirs. Accordingly, if both died, payment is made to the workers' heirs but not to the shopkeeper's heirs. Where one expended money improving another person's property, if there is actual improvement and he dies without having sworn, his heirs are paid. But where he claims expenses exceeding the improvement and dies without swearing, the heirs do not collect. In the case of two people who deposited as in siman 76, where each ordinarily swears and collects, if they both die without swearing, two payments are made to their heirs. See Beurim.
