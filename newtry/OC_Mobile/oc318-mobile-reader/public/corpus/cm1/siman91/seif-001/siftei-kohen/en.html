Give my workers a sela, etc. The Sema writes that the same applies if he said, give my son a sela. The shopkeeper does not collect from the employer until he swears while holding a sacred object, even though the employer need not pay his son anything. This is explicit in the Mishna and in the Tur, seif 6, in the responsum of the Rosh that he cites. Presumably the Mishna and Tur discuss a case where he owes his son nothing, and the Ran writes this explicitly. Even the Rambam and Mechaber in seif 2 agree here that the shopkeeper must swear while holding a sacred object. Only there, where the employer is certainly obligated to pay one of the two parties, he loses nothing by paying once. Here, by contrast, if the shopkeeper did not give the money, the employer owes no payment at all. Examine carefully. See also below, seif katan 11.
And he admits that he told him so, etc. But if the employer denies the instruction entirely or in part, he is exempt even from a heset oath, because the workers likewise contradict the shopkeeper. This is contrary to the Sema, who writes that the employer takes a heset oath or a Torah oath as below in seif 6. The cases are not comparable, as I explain below. Examine carefully.
Both of them swear, etc. Even if the shopkeeper is a non Jew, if he contradicts either the Jewish worker or the Jewish employer, the Jew must swear. This is clear from the responsum of the Rashba cited by the Beit Yosef in siman 72, new section 18, and below at the end of siman 185. It appears that even according to what I write below in siman 149, seif 14, that the Maggid Mishneh in chapter 14 of Hilchos To'en seems to imply that a heset oath is not administered based on the definite claim of a non Jew, nevertheless here, where the Jew seeks to collect, all may agree that he cannot collect without an oath. Examine carefully.
And they must swear, etc. The Bach writes that this requirement that each swear in the other's presence is only initially, but if one already swore outside the other's presence he need not swear again, as above in siman 87, seif 23. This is not compelling. The present case may be different because one of them is certainly swearing falsely. Moreover, in siman 87 itself the Ritva disagrees, as cited by the Beit Yosef there in new section 26. See there.
The shopkeeper in the presence of the workers, etc. The wording implies that the shopkeeper swears first, and this appears correct. If neither swears, the employer must in any event pay the workers and not the shopkeeper. The workers can therefore say that perhaps the shopkeeper will not swear, whereas the shopkeeper necessarily must swear if he wishes to collect. Baal HaTerumos, Shaar 29, likewise writes in the name of Rabbeinu Ephraim that the witnesses first swear that they were agents, as cited by the Beit Yosef. Here too, the shopkeeper functions as the employer's agent. Although I write below in siman 121, seif katan 45, that we do not follow Rabbeinu Ephraim regarding those witnesses, that is because there we rule that the witnesses take heset to be exempt while the creditor takes an oath with a sacred object to collect. Here, however, both parties swear and collect, so Rabbeinu Ephraim's rule teaches that the shopkeeper should swear first. Examine carefully.
Specifically when both are present. If one is absent, each can say to the employer: bring the opposing party and I will swear before him. I need my money now, so I will swear even in his absence. So writes the Ran. This concerns a case where they had initially contradicted each other before beit din. Otherwise the workers need not swear at all, as below in seif katan 11. Nevertheless, according to what I write below in seif katan 13, that if the opposing party is nearby and is known to be coming, the employer can say, wait a little, or swear and collect immediately, then here, even if he is nearby, the claimant may say, I will swear immediately and will not wait at all. Examine carefully.
The same applies if he said to him, lend me, etc. Meaning, do not think that this enactment applies only to a shopkeeper because he customarily gives goods on credit. It applies even if the employer said to an ordinary person who is not a shopkeeper, lend me money and pay my creditor. Baal HaTerumos, Shaar 29, states this, contrary to the Maggid Mishneh in the name of one authority, as cited by the Beit Yosef.
And pay my creditor, etc. This is because the shopkeeper or other lender need not pay the workers or creditor in the presence of witnesses, except where that particular creditor holds a note against the employer and the employer specifically referred to the note, as above in siman 58, end of seif 1.
