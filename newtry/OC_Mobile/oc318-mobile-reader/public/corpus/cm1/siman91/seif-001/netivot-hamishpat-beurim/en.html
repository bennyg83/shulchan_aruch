They both swear. See Shach, seif katan 3, who writes that the Jew must swear. Tumim asks that a definite assertion made by a non Jew is not treated as a halachically definite claim. In my view there is no difficulty. Even if both parties were Jews, this would not be an ordinary definite claim, because a statement based on the word of an interested party is not considered a definite claim, as explained above in siman 75. The real basis is that in Bava Metzia 3 the Gemara compares the case of the shopkeeper's ledger to two people who deposited money, and in siman 76, Shach seif katan 4, explains that the defendant's position is considered definite because he knows with certainty that he owes only one hundred to one of them, not two hundred to both. The same is true here. He definitely claims that only one payment is owed to one of the claimants, not two full payments to both. Ketzot HaChoshen asks that halachically he is not obligated to pay the non Jew, so the case should resemble seif 2, where the workers need not swear when the employer will make only one payment. This too is no difficulty. Since secular law compels him to pay the non Jew, he is in practical terms making two payments, and the oath structure therefore applies.
