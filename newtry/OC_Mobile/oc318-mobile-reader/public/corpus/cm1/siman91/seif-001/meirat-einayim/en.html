Give my workers one sela.
The same applies if he said, 'Give my son one sela.' The shopkeeper does not collect from the householder without an oath while holding a sacred object, even though the householder owes his son nothing.
This is explicit in the Mishnah and the Tur.And he admits that he told him so, etc. But if the householder denies the instruction entirely or partially, he takes the appropriate oath and is exempt from the shopkeeper, as explained in seif 6.Both swear, etc.
The workers swear that they did not receive payment, and the shopkeeper swears that he paid them, and each collects from the householder according to the enactment.
Although the householder may thereby pay twice, Chazal imposed these oaths because both claims arise from his instruction and neither claimant can otherwise prove the matter.
The details depend on whether the shopkeeper had been advanced money, whether the workers agreed to look solely to him, and whether the householder admits the instruction.
