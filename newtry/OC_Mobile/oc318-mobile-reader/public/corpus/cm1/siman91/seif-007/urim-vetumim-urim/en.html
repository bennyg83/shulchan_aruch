He is believed. See Tumim. The later authorities asked, according to Rama above in seif 2, following Tur, who holds that even where there is no contradiction at all the shopkeeper collects only with an oath, why here does he collect without an oath? They answer in the name of Maharshal that here it is known that the shopkeeper gave something to the workers, and the only uncertainty concerns how much. I wrote that it appears more correct to distinguish on the basis of the shopkeeper's negligence. Ordinarily he should have given in the presence of witnesses, whereas here the employer trusted him and instructed him to give the workers whatever they requested. See there.
Without an oath. Sema holds that hesset is nevertheless required, because he found this difficult in light of the rule regarding one who incurs expenses above in seif 3. Shach already challenged him, since the wording implies that there is no oath at all, and this is correct. As to the substance of the difficulty, see Tumim. It is clear that if the employer admits, according to the shopkeeper's account, that the workers did not receive more than the amount of their wages, this is not comparable to the earlier case. If the employer claims that the shopkeeper gave them more, there is room to doubt whether it is comparable. See Tumim for the reasoning. One point, however, appears clear: if the shopkeeper supplied the workers with food and drink and the workers do not know how much they ate and drank, the shopkeeper may collect only after swearing how much he expended for them. See there.
That even, etc. Shach writes that this is a scribal error, for it implies that even when the workers are not present the shopkeeper collects only with an oath, which is incorrect. Likewise, when Rama writes that he gave the workers more than their stipulated wages, the same oath is required even where he gave only the amount of their wages. Rama states the greater novelty, that even if he gave more, he collects by swearing. See Tumim, seif katan 14. It is possible, however, that Rama's words are exact: if the employer claims that the shopkeeper gave the workers more than the amount stipulated for them, then in every case the shopkeeper must swear. See there.
