Without an oath. The Sema explains that this means without a severe oath, but that a shevuat heset is still required. Otherwise, why should this differ from the end of seif 3 in the gloss, where one who incurred expenses swears and collects? According to this reading, however, the word "oath" is used differently in the two places. There "swears and collects" means a shevuat heset, while here "collects without an oath" means without a severe oath. The Sema acknowledges that this is somewhat strained. The Shach disagrees and writes that Baal HaTerumot proves explicitly that even a shevuat heset is not required here. Also, as explained above, "swears and collects" in seif 3 means a severe oath while holding a sacred object. There is no difficulty because the Tur gives the distinction here: since the employer made payment depend entirely on the workers' direction to give them whatever they needed, it is as though he had fixed the amount in advance. The Taz likewise challenges the Sema.
And some say. The wording is imprecise. It should say, "And if the workers are present," because the Mechaber certainly agrees with this rule and there is no dispute. Baal HaTerumot explicitly presents these as two separate laws. Shach. The Taz agrees.
