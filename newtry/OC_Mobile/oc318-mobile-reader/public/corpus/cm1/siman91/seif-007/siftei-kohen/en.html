The shopkeeper is believed, without an oath. The Sema cites Maharshal that even according to the opinion above in seif 2, which requires a shopkeeper who alone seeks payment after the workers died to take an oath, that is only when the employer does not know whether the shopkeeper paid them anything. Here the employer knows that he paid them, but does not know how much. Since he admits payment occurred, the shopkeeper collects without an oath. The Bach writes likewise, and this is also evident from the Tur, unlike the Levush.
He collects from the employer without an oath. The Sema writes that this must mean without a stringent oath, but that a hesset oath is still required. Otherwise, how is this different from Rama's ruling near the end of siman 75 that one who claims, “I am uncertain whether I owe you,” is exempt but must take a hesset oath? This is not correct. Baal HaTerumot expressly writes that the worker collects without any oath, even hesset, when no shopkeeper contradicts him, and the same applies to the shopkeeper when no worker contradicts him. The reason is the presumption that an agent performs his agency. See what I wrote above.
Even if the workers are present. The wording is imprecise. It should say, “If the workers are present,” as the Levush states. Certainly the Mechaber also agrees that when the workers are present and deny receiving payment, the shopkeeper must take an oath. Rama means only to clarify the case where the workers are not contradicting him. Cited cross-references in the source include: 80, 81.
