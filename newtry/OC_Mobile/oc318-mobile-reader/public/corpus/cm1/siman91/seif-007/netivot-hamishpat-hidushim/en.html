He did not set a fixed amount. The reason is that the employer caused the uncertainty through his own negligence, since he could have fixed an amount. It is also as though the employer authorized reliance on the workers' word, because even if the shopkeeper had paid them before witnesses, the workers could still have claimed whatever amount they wished, since the employer instructed him to give them whatever they requested. This differs from a case where the uncertainty arises because the workers say that they received nothing. There, had payment been made before witnesses, the workers could not have contradicted the shopkeeper. The shopkeeper was negligent by not paying before witnesses. Therefore his oath is required by the strict law, while the requirement to pay is only by enactment, as the Ramban writes:
Without an oath. He need not take even a hesset oath. Shach, unlike the Sema. But if the employer instructed the shopkeeper to provide them food and drink, where it was impossible to set an exact amount, the shopkeeper must swear like any person who seeks reimbursement for expenditures:
That even if. This is a scribal error. It should read: "And if the workers are present," and so forth. Even the Mechaber agrees in this case. Shach.
