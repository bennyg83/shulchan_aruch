The shopkeeper is believed, etc., without an oath. See the Derishah, where I wrote in the name of Maharshal that even according to the opinion cited by the Tur and Rama above in seif 2, that when the workers died and only the shopkeeper comes to collect from the employer he does not collect without an oath, that applies specifically when the employer does not know whether the shopkeeper gave the workers anything. Here, however, the case is one where the employer knows that he gave them something, but does not know how much. Maharshal therefore concedes that the shopkeeper collects without a stringent oath. One must nevertheless say that he still requires an oath of hesset. Otherwise, this would be difficult in light of Rama's ruling at the end of seif 3 that one who incurred expenses and the defendant does not know how much takes an oath and collects. According to this explanation, the two unqualified expressions concerning an oath are not identical. Rama's statement there, 'takes an oath and collects,' means an oath of hesset, while the Mechaber's statement here, 'collects without an oath,' means without a stringent oath, although hesset is still required. This is somewhat strained. In the Perishah I offered another reconciliation of the Tur's wording. Examine it carefully.
Even if the workers are present, etc. Meaning, the workers contradict the shopkeeper and say that he did not give them as much as he claims, and also that the employer did not owe them as much as he says. Nevertheless, the shopkeeper collects upon taking an oath.
