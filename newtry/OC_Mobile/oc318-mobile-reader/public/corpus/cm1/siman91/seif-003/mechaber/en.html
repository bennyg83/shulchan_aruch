When does the preceding rule apply? When a storekeeper extends credit to the employer and pays all his obligations, and the employer later pays the storekeeper. But if the employer first gave money to the storekeeper and said, 'Give the workers one sela,' then if he directed the workers to the storekeeper in the presence of all three and the workers consented, they have no claim against the employer. The storekeeper takes a heses oath and is exempt. If the employer directed them to the storekeeper outside the presence of all three, the storekeeper takes a heses oath to the employer that he fulfilled his agency and is exempt; the workers take an oath resembling a Torah oath to the employer that they received nothing from the storekeeper and collect from the employer. Some say this too is only a heses oath. {Rama: If a person incurred expenses on another's property with the owner's permission and claims those expenses, while the defendant does not know how much they were, the claimant swears and collects. The same applies whenever one party knows and the other does not: the one who knows swears, as stated in Maharik, responsum 10, and Responsa Maimoniyot, Sefer Kinyan, responsum 7; see note 226.}
