One who incurred expenses. Teshuvot Chut HaShani, siman 38, discusses a debt owed by a non-Jew to Shimon but written in Reuven's name. Shimon asked Reuven to litigate with the debtor and agreed to reimburse his expenses. Reuven incurred substantial expenses and succeeded in collecting the debt. Among the expenses he claimed a sum that a municipal official, one of the mediators, owed Reuven personally but refused to pay because of the effort he had expended in the case. Shimon argued that he need not reimburse that personal debt and that Reuven should sue the official. He also produced one witness that the official said he was withholding payment because of favors done for Reuven throughout the year. Reuven insisted that the official withheld it only because of the present debt collection. The responsum rules that Reuven need not antagonize the official and may include the loss among his expenses, as follows from a responsum of the Rosh cited in siman 176. If the favor received from the official was worth less than the claimed expense, the value of the favor would be assessed. Because Reuven definitely claims that the withholding resulted from this case, he should accept a ban upon himself affirming that account; the witness's statement is not decisive, since the official may have misrepresented matters to him. In my view, however, Shimon should reimburse only the actual value of the debt against the official, taking into account the official's assets and power, rather than its face amount. It also requires study whether Reuven should merely accept a ban or should actually have to swear to this point.
