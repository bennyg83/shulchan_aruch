But if the householder advanced money to the shopkeeper and said, 'Give my workers one sela.' It is possible that the denomination of the money he advanced does not circulate as readily as the sela.
A similar distinction is found regarding a money changer who sells a minted dinar for unminted money.And the workers consented.
The workers have no claim against the employer. This is so even according to the Rosh and those who agree with him, whose view the Tur cites in siman 126, that even where the employer directed payment in the presence of all three parties, the person to whom payment was directed may retract. There, the designated payer admits that he still has not given anything to the recipient and merely puts him off from day to day. Here, however, the shopkeeper who was designated to pay says that he already gave the worker, who was the recipient, what the employer instructed him to give. See the Prisha, where I wrote more concerning this.
The word 'consented' is not exact.
It is enough that they remained silent and did not explicitly say that they had no dealings with the householder and would look only to the shopkeeper.
Mere silence does not necessarily release the householder from his underlying obligation unless the circumstances clearly show an agreed substitution of debtor.
The claimant swears and collects. The Rama writes without qualification that he swears and collects. It appears that this depends upon the dispute above in seif 2 concerning a case where the workers died and the shopkeeper alone comes to collect. According to the Mechaber there, he need not take a stringent oath, whereas according to the dissenting view cited by the Rama there, he must take a stringent oath here as well. However, according to what I write below in seif 7 in the name of Maharshal, one must distinguish. Specifically there there is doubt whether the shopkeeper gave them anything at all. Here, however, it is certain that he incurred expenses; we simply do not know how much. Therefore, even according to that view, a heset oath suffices. See there in seif 2.
The rule therefore depends on whether the workers expressly accepted the shopkeeper as the sole person responsible for payment or merely accepted the arrangement as a convenient method of payment.
