When does this apply... but... if... As stated in the first chapter of Gittin, a transfer in the presence of all three parties is effective and cannot be retracted. This applies not only to gifts but also to payment, as shown by the case of the gardeners there. By contrast, where an employer merely directs a shopkeeper to extend credit, the law of ma'amad sheloshtan does not apply unless the third party already owes money to the transferor. This distinction appears in Perek HaMekabel regarding one who directs a worker to collect from a shopkeeper. There the Gemara asks whether the worker may return to the employer, and Rabbah says he may. We rule like Rabbah because the Mishnah supports him. Although the Yerushalmi in Perek Kol HaNishba'in says that if the employer placed the worker together with the shopkeeper the employer is no longer liable, the Rif explains that this follows Rav Sheshes, whereas we rule like Rabbah. Still, we may learn from the Yerushalmi a case where the employer first gave the money to the shopkeeper. Thus the text's distinction follows Rabbah.
And if not... This is the Yerushalmi just cited.
He swears... See Beer HaGolah. The Rif explains that an agent who swears to be exempt takes a heses oath, whereas a lender who swears in order to collect takes an oath modeled on Torah law, as in the Mishnah and Yerushalmi.
And some say... Since the Gemara includes both together, some hold that just as the agent's oath is heses, so is the lender's. See Beer HaGolah. Above in siman 121 seif 9 the Shulchan Aruch rules without qualification like the first view.
One who spent... and the same applies... As stated in Perek HaIshah Shenaflu, as above.
(Collected notes) One who... and the same applies... See Tosafos, Kesubos 80a, citing Rabbeinu Chananel. He distinguishes that there the claimant knows while the other party does not, so the rule of a certain claim against a doubtful claim applies, and from there derives the stated law. The Ran cites this. (End of collected notes.)
