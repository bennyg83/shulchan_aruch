But not the shopkeeper. Even according to the view that if one lends to a minor, the minor must repay, here the householder's obligation to the shopkeeper is based on the fact that the shopkeeper can say, "You trusted him, for you did not tell me, 'Give it before witnesses.'" That logic applies with ordinary adults. But a minor lacks sufficient understanding, so the shopkeeper had no right to rely upon the minor and should have given only before witnesses. In addition, the essential liability here arises because the shopkeeper gave the money at the householder's direction, and a minor cannot serve as a halachic agent.
