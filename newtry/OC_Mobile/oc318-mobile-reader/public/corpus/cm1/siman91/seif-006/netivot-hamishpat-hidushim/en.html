The householder takes a hesset oath. The Shach explains that the workers admit receiving payment, and the dispute is only that the householder says he did not instruct the shopkeeper to give them so much. But if the workers deny receiving it, that would create an oath against an oath, and the householder takes neither hesset nor a Torah oath. See Beurim: if the shopkeeper wishes to swear and has already sworn, he may then obligate the householder to a hesset oath on his own claim. If the householder partially admits, he must take a Torah oath. But one witness who testifies that the householder instructed him to give the workers the money cannot obligate the householder to a Torah oath, even after the shopkeeper has sworn. This appears primary.
