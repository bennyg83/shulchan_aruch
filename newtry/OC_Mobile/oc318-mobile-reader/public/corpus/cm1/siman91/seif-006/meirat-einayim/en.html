The householder takes a hesset oath, etc. This is unlike the Ran, whose view is cited in Darkei Moshe, that the householder need not swear even a hesset oath. The Ran reasons that even if the householder admits that he instructed the shopkeeper to pay, the shopkeeper cannot collect except through an oath. Therefore the householder's denial is not a denial that by itself removes definite money, similar to the rule at the end of siman 87. Other authorities distinguish: there, even when the defendant admits, he himself swears and is exempt, so no definite money rests upon him. Here, by contrast, when the householder admits the instruction, the shopkeeper can swear and collect money from him. His denial therefore does amount to a denial of a monetary claim, and a hesset oath is imposed.
