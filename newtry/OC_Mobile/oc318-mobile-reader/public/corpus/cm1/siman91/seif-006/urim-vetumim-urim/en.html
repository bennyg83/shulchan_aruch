The employer swears hesset. See Tumim. According to the Mechaber, who follows Rambam and holds that a shopkeeper collects by strict law even without an oath, it is readily understandable why the employer is obligated to swear, because if he admitted the claim he would become liable for money. The shopkeeper's oath is required only to ease the employer's mind. According to Rama, however, who follows Tur and Ramah and holds that by strict law the shopkeeper cannot collect even with an oath, this is difficult, for if the employer admitted the claim he would still not become liable for money. See Tumim, where I offered several resolutions. One is that the shopkeeper claims that at the outset the employer trusted him and exempted him from an oath. Another is that witnesses exist, or the workers admit, that the shopkeeper gave them the goods, but the shopkeeper claims that the employer told him, 'Give the workers if they say that they completed their work,' while the employer denies making that statement, and in fact they did not complete their work. In such a case, were the employer to admit the statement, he would become liable for money. See there.
