This is difficult, for communal liability ordinarily follows the present assessment. The Rashba was asked about a debt that the community undertook ten years earlier and must now pay, although the tax assessment had changed in the meantime. Some argued that payment should follow the original assessment, while others argued that it should follow the present one. He answered that the established custom in those lands was always to follow the current assessment, and therefore everything depended upon custom. By strict law, however, one would follow the original assessment, as with any ordinary debt. Once the debt was fixed, even one who had been wealthy and became poor would have to pay from the cloak upon his shoulders. Why, then, should the present case not follow the current assessment, leaving the private lenders no special share even if they never despaired? It appears that when the original individuals remain among those currently assessed, but their respective valuations have changed, the community follows the present assessment in accordance with the custom described by the Rashba. In the Maharik’s case, however, the individuals had lost their assets and were no longer included among the taxpayers or in the assessment at all. The custom does not address such a case, and we revert to strict law. Therefore, if they had not despaired, they would receive according to the amounts they originally advanced. From this I derived a ruling in an actual case. The entire community paid its assessed share, but in order to complete the required collection, those who had already paid were compelled to contribute an additional amount to cover poor members who had paid nothing. The poor later became wealthy and were required to repay what they had failed to contribute. That repayment should be divided among the community according to the current assessment. The same applies to an individual claiming a share of such repayment: he receives according to the present assessment, not according to what he contributed at the earlier time. I have seen one who erred in this matter and ruled that he should receive according to his former assessment. That is incorrect because of the custom recorded by the Rashba. This is distinct from the Maharik’s case, as explained. Nevertheless, the Maharik’s underlying ruling requires study, as stated above.
