And they despaired of the majority. See Taz's gloss and Ketzot HaChoshen, who argues that despair can operate regarding a debt. Tumim correctly concludes that ordinary despair does not cancel a debt. Here the case can involve a ruler who has lawful power under dina d'malchuta to impose or remit fixed and variable taxes. The despair is therefore effective because the ruler can discharge himself from the debt through a lawful tax assessment. Ordinary debt is different. One cannot simply despair away another person's personal obligation, and the personal lien resembles land, against which despair does not work.
