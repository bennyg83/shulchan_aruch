Proximity. The reason is that when the city is open and has no wall, marauders who sometimes come upon it first lay hands on the outer houses. Those houses therefore require more protection than the inner ones. Sema.
Majority. Responsa Rosh does not state the rule in that form. He was asked about a community imposing a cherem and whether, when a wealthy minority objects, the ordinary majority controls. He answered that since the cherem concerns money, one follows the majority of wealth, for it is improper that the majority of persons who pay only a minority of the tax should impose a cherem upon the wealthy according to their own view. It may be that the Rosh meant only that the numerical majority of the community cannot impose a cherem without the wealthy minority's consent, but not that the wealthy minority is itself deemed a majority empowered to impose a cherem on the numerical majority. It is also possible that he regards the two sides as equal, requiring compromise in such matters. This requires study. Ibid.
Equally. The same applies when their wealth is unequal and one has more property than the other. One follows the assessment of their wealth and of their houses. The Tur and Mechaber merely chose a clear-cut example. Ibid.
Are fighting. For when one side prevails, it enters the entire city and plunders its property, but ordinarily leaves the inhabitants alive in order to subject them to labor and taxation under their rulers. Therefore payment is assessed only according to the value of property. When the attack also concerns lives, so that they kill and seize property, payment is assessed according to both life and property. Ibid.
Group. See below, siman 272, seif 15, and Responsa Chacham Tzvi, siman 132.
What appears appropriate. Because accusations against the community are not all alike. Sometimes they concern money and sometimes lives. Sema.
For a woman. Responsa Mahari ben Lev, part 3, siman 100, rules that assessments are not taken from nichsei melog. See there. It appears that this does not disagree with the Rama, for the Rama discusses money from which the husband has no right to fruits, such as money given to her on condition that her husband have no rights in it, or the like. So it seems to me. See Responsa Mabit, part 1, siman 71, and Responsa Maharashdam, siman 401. Shach.
For his sons. See the sefer Imrei Binah, beginning of folio 90; Responsa Raanach, siman 40; Responsa Menachem Azariah, siman 43; and Responsa Maharam Lublin, siman 43. Ibid.
That one does not. The reason is that they may say, "We cannot determine the accuracy of your valuation." This is unlike the ruling in siman 375, seif 8, where if one says, "Let the judges come and assess the expenditure and I will collect without an oath," he is heard. There, the amount of expenditure can be estimated through other workers who performed comparable work. Sema.
To collect. The Sema understands that according to the Rashba two things are required: an assessment and the commencement of collection. See there. The Taz disagrees, cites at length the wording of Responsa Rashba and Responsa Rosh on this matter, and explains them according to his view. He concludes that the "collection" mentioned by the Rama clearly means fixing the basis of collection, namely making the assessment. That alone suffices, because from then on the obligation for the entire year is fixed according to that assessment until a new assessment is made. See there at length.
Inferior. See Be'er Sheva, folio 96, and Responsa Rashach, part 2, siman 162.
From interest. See Responsa Maharashdam, siman 236. See also above, siman 104, seif 15, what the Sema writes there. Shach.
Benefit. Terumat HaDeshen states: Money donated for a mitzvah is exempt. But if one spends only part of the profit, for example, one set aside a large principal in order to maintain a perpetual lamp from its profits, and the investment earns twice the amount spent on the lamp, while he retains the principal on condition that if he or his descendants become poor they may reclaim it for themselves, the money is not exempt. Sema.
They disagree. Responsa Ran, siman 10, implies that the practice follows the dissenting opinion. See there, and Responsa Maharashdam, simanim 362, 374, 403, 404, and 405. Shach.
Real estate. Because real estate does not come here, unlike a deposit. See Darkei Moshe. Sema.
Who erred. The Shach writes that one who examines Rabbeinu Yerucham will see clearly that he is not discussing a case where the person who swore says that he erred; rather, the passage concerns the assessors. Nevertheless, as a matter of law, if a person can prove his error, he should be heard. This is entirely unlike siman 81, seif 31, because here he does not swear that he possesses a specified amount; he swears only that he does not possess more than a certain amount. Even if he produced a written list and swore, presumably he did not swear that the list itself was exact, but only that he did not possess more than the amount listed. So it appears to me.
As merchandise. Regarding real estate, see Darkei Moshe, which cites Mahari Weil, siman 84, that houses held for sale are treated like other merchandise and are taxed. Maharam of Merseburg writes that they are exempt, because mere intention does not create liability. The Darkei Moshe explains that if one purchased a house for the purpose of reselling it at a profit, he must pay tax on it, but not when he merely wishes to sell his own house, or a house he inherited, or the like. See there. Sema.
