Regarding one who falsely swore concerning his acknowledgment of a stated amount and there are valid witnesses, see Responsa Mahari Weil, siman 153. He rules that the offender should first receive lashes before the evening recitation of VeHu Rachum, three times on Monday, Thursday, and Monday in the synagogue. After the lashes he should confess aloud so all can hear, stating in the vernacular that he swore falsely regarding his acknowledgment, sinned, acted perversely and rebelliously, and will not return to that path. He should fast forty consecutive days excluding Shabbat, Rosh Chodesh, and festivals falling within them, and afterward fast every Monday and Thursday for a full year. If after the forty days he cannot bear the fasting, he should give money to charity and accept another form of suffering imposed by the local sages. All his life he should be careful never to utter an oath even regarding truth. But if he holds a paid or false document and merely retains it until he can bring a claim, or if one can recover money from a gentile through claims and objections, he need not contribute anything from that. Chavot Yair siman 343; see there. Sema in the name of Darkei Moshe.
Mahari Weil, siman 124, writes that tax assessors are believed regarding how much each person admitted before them, even after they have left office. They are believed without acceptance of cherem or oath. Mahari Monin ruled likewise, and he received the same from his teacher Rabbi Shalom. He brings proof there. Sema cites this.
Terumat HaDeshen there cites both views and concludes in accordance with prevailing custom and the first view, that one must contribute even from deposits, because the government does not care whether he earned profit from the money or not. Rather, whatever wealth he possesses is assessed, even if it is outside the country and even if it never came into his physical possession. See there, siman 342. See above siman 81, seif 28, that he cannot claim he erred in his oath. Sema struggles to reconcile the texts, but I saw that Mahari Weil himself says he cannot claim error. It therefore appears clear to me that a scribal error entered the books, and the text should read: according to the assessment I can say they erred, whereas according to the oath I cannot.
