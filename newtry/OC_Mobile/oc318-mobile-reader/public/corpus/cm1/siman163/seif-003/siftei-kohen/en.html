The Mordechai, however, implies that townspeople are not believed against an individual concerning the amount he admitted so long as they have a financial interest in the matter. They need not contribute from interest, etc. See Responsa Maharashdam, siman 236, and above siman 104, Sema, seif katan 34.
One who has a deposit, etc. Some say he must contribute from it, etc., and others disagree. Terumat HaDeshen presents both views and writes that, according to those who disagree, if a person has concealed his wealth and placed it with a trusted person he need not pay tax on it, unless he is exceptionally wealthy, in which case the ruler's attention remains upon him regardless. Terumat HaDeshen concludes that practice should follow the first view. This is the Sema's language. Responsa Ran, siman 10, however, implies that the practice was the opposite. See there, and see Responsa Maharashdam, simanim 362, 364, 403, 404, and 405.
In a place where contributions are imposed according to an assessment. See Sema, seif katan 29, where he distinguishes this from a tax collection based on each person's sworn declaration of his assets. In the latter case, since the matter depends upon the person assessed stating the truth under oath, even if he already swore, if he can prove that he erred we listen to him and reduce the assessment accordingly, notwithstanding the Tur in siman 81, seif 31. One who examines Mahari Weil there will see clearly that he does not discuss a person who says that he himself erred. Rather, he speaks of the assessors. Nevertheless, as a matter of law, if the person can prove his mistake we listen to him. This is also entirely unlike siman 81. Here he does not swear affirmatively that he owns a specific amount; he swears only that he owns no more than a stated amount. Even if he submitted a written inventory and swore, ordinarily he did not swear that the inventory was exact, but only that he possessed no more than what was listed. This appears correct.
