Matters for which the residents of a city may compel one another. It contains six seifim:
The residents of a city may compel one another even a minority may compel the majority. Rabbeinu Yerucham, Netiv 31, part 6. to build a wall, gates, and a bar for the city, to build a synagogue, and to purchase a Torah scroll, Prophets, and Writings so that anyone from the community who wishes may read from them. Rama: The same applies to all communal needs. See Orach Chaim siman 55 regarding hiring a chazzan for the city, and also siman 53 there. The residents may compel one another to host guests, distribute charity to them, and contribute to the communal charity fund. Mordechai, first chapter of Bava Batra, and Teshuvot Maimoniyot, Sefer Kinyan, siman 59. See above, simanim 4 and 7, regarding a dispute between a city and an individual, whether the city may judge the case and whether the city is considered to be in possession. Regarding hiring for a minyan, see the end of Orach Chaim siman 55. All who live in the city must share the expenses incurred to eliminate an informer. Rosh, responsa, klal 6, simanim 21 and 26; repeated below in siman 388. When communal needs cannot be settled by agreement, all householders who pay taxes should assemble and undertake that each will express his view for the sake of Heaven, and the majority is followed. If the minority refuses, the majority may compel them, even through non-Jewish courts, and may spend money for this purpose, and the dissenters must pay their share. One who refuses to express his view under the communal ban loses his vote, and the majority is determined among those who do express their views. Responsa Maharam, Sefer Kinyan, siman 27, and Hagahot Maimoniyot, chapter 11 of Hilchot Tefillah. See Yoreh Deah siman 256 regarding when one must contribute to the charity fund.
