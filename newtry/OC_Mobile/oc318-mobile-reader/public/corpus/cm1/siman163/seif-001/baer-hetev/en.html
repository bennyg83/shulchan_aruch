That he read. The Sema writes that nowadays, when books are readily available among us, the custom is not to compel residents to purchase books, except for a Sefer Torah. Nevertheless, to prevent neglect of Torah study, beit din may compel the residents of a city to lend books to those who need them for study, as the Rosh writes in Responsa, klal 93, cited by Rama at the end of siman 292. There is also support for this from the present law. See Responsa Maharam Alshich, simanim 56 and 59, concerning tax laws, and also siman 64; Responsa Mahari Mintz, siman 7; Responsa Maharam Padua, siman 42; Responsa Maharit, beginning of siman 69; Responsa Raanach, siman 98; Responsa Rashach, part 2, siman 45; and Responsa Rabbi Yosef of the House of Levi, siman 23. Where townspeople spent a large sum to obtain governmental permission to rebuild the roof of a synagogue that had burned, Responsa Shaar Ephraim, siman 145, rules that in our communities, where synagogue seats are purchased for money, the seat owners must bear almost the principal portion of that expense. A cantor. Siman 55 does not discuss hiring a cantor. It discusses hiring people to complete a minyan on the High Holy Days, which is the ruling Rama cites immediately below. The laws governing the qualifications of a cantor are explained there in siman 53. See Darkei Moshe there, who records many laws on the subject, and also the beginning of this siman. Sema.
