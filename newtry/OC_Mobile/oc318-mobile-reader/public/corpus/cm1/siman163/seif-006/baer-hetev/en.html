A fixed obligation. Responsa Rosh states: "Or where he had to pay the ruler because of his occupation, while the community spent money to pay taxes, if the community paid the ruler so that he would protect and shelter them, this person too must pay his share." Darkei Moshe cites this and also cites Maharam of Merseburg: If a wealthy man is singled out by the ruler to pay by himself, while the community refuses to permit him to separate, he should place his tax money in a bundle, go to the ruler, and say, "I am not permitted to separate and refrain from paying taxes together with my fellows. I cannot pay you separately and also pay with them." If the ruler says, "I wish to exempt you," he is exempt, provided that he accepts a cherem that neither he nor his agent first approached the ruler to seek the exemption. Sema.
To separate them. Where a king or ruler imposes a charge upon one or two wealthy persons, and another Jew has influence in the royal or governmental court and can attempt to obtain their exemption, while it is certain that if these individuals are exempted the ruler will impose the charge on others, may he act to save them? Responsa Maharibal, part 2, siman 40, rules that if the ruler has already imposed the charge upon identified individuals and expressly singled them out, so that they are already caught in his net, no Jew may attempt to exempt them in any manner that will certainly harm others. But if a decree was issued generally to impose the charge upon two persons without identifying them, a Jew may act so that particular individuals of his choosing are excluded from the decree, even though others will certainly be substituted. He brings a clear proof from the account of Mephibosheth in the chapter HaArel, where David prayed that the Ark not accept him. In other circumstances, however, the Gemara says that improper favoritism would be involved. With respect to money in dealings with a non-Jewish ruler, the matter can be comparable to danger to life, like one trapped in a net. He discusses this at length; see there, for the distinction is sound. Enactments. The Rosh writes in Responsa, klal 6, siman 10, that where an enactment says that anyone possessing one hundred gold coins must pay tax, one who owns a field worth that amount must likewise pay. He further writes there in siman 28 concerning tax collectors who came to place Reuven under cherem for failure to pay his tax. Shimon gave collateral on his behalf, and afterward Shimon wished the community to return his collateral and instead place Reuven under cherem. The community is not required to do so.
