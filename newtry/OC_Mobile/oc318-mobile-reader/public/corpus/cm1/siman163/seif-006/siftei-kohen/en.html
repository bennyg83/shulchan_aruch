Or to separate them, etc. Where a king or official imposes a charge upon one or two wealthy persons, and a Jew has influence in the royal or official court and can seek their exemption, but it is certain that if they are exempted the charge will be imposed upon others, may he save these persons or not? Responsa Mahari ibn Lev, part 2, siman 40, rules that if the king has already imposed the charge upon specifically identified persons and named them, so that they have already been caught in his net, no Jew may seek their exemption in any manner that will certainly harm others. If, however, a general decree was issued to impose the charge upon two unspecified persons, a Jew may seek to have whichever persons he chooses excluded from the decree, even though others will certainly be included instead. He brings a clear proof from Mephibosheth in the chapter HaArel, where David prayed that the Ark not receive him. With respect to gentiles, however, the Gemara says that favoritism is involved; see the Gemara. In monetary matters involving gentiles, the matter is like danger to life, as indicated by the expression, 'as one caught in a net.' He discusses this at length; see there, and examine it carefully, for the ruling is correct. See also Responsa Rabbi Eliyahu ibn Chaim, siman 49; Responsa Maharashdam, siman 304; Responsa Maharan Sasson, siman 48, page 70c; Responsa Mahari ibn Lev, pages 71 and 72; and Sefer Alef, beginning of rule 12.
