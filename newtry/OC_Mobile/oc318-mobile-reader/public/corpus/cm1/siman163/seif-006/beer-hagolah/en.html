The language of Tur, from Rav Ashi's statement, 'an individual resident assists the town,' Bava Batra 55a. Rashbam explains that the king's servants demanded too much from him, and the townspeople appeased them and obtained his exemption; what was reduced from him is collected from the rest.
There, from Rav Ashi's statement that an official writ is heavenly assistance. Rashbam explains it as royal officials, while Tosafot explain it as royal documents. Beit Yosef writes that they interceded with the king, saying the man was poor and unable to work, and the king consented and wrote him an exemption from his tax. Rosh, rule 61, end of siman 10, writes that regarding the king's levy, communities customarily seize anyone who fails to pay, because dina d'malkhuta dina.
Sema writes that the community has the stronger position and may say: we exempted you from one collection, not from the whole year if additional collections occur. Beit Yosef, from seif katan 2, in the name of Ritva, writes that if the public agreed to exempt someone from tax for a fixed period so that he would move among them or because of another benefit he gave them, and he dies meanwhile, his heirs must pay tax from the inherited property.
