A person who is idle and has no business activity in the city is subject to the following rule. If the townspeople interceded for him when the tax collector came to collect the fixed levy and apportioned it among the residents according to his estimate, and they told him that this person was idle and unfit to pay tax, causing the collector to exempt him and impose his omitted share upon the others, the exempted person must reimburse them for the share shifted to them. If the collector exempted him on his own initiative, however, even though his share was imposed on the other residents, he is exempt. Rama: An idle person is liable for city tax only if he resides in the city. A transient visitor owes nothing because he earns nothing there. This applies where he was exempted before the tax was fixed. If the tax was fixed upon the townspeople and only afterward the idle person was exempted, he must pay his share. If a ruler waives an individual's share of taxes at that individual's request, he must pay his share to the community. If the ruler exempts him without solicitation, the benefit belongs to him alone. Some say that before the community has reached a settlement with the ruler, the ruler has no power to exempt one person, lighten one person's burden, increase another's, or separate the taxpayers from one another. After a settlement has been reached, however, if the ruler then waives one person's share, even at his request, the waiver belongs to that person and he need not reimburse the community. A person whom the king or ruler exempts from taxes and levies remains liable for all other municipal needs. An individual who pays a separate levy to the king must nevertheless contribute with the community. The community members are partners with respect to fixed taxes, but not with respect to arbitrary accusations. If the ruler singles out some of them, the others need not contribute, even if the accusation was first directed against all and some escaped or obtained exemption through their own efforts. See above, siman 128. If an accusation is brought against one city and there is concern that it will spread to other communities, all communities exposed to the danger must contribute. With respect to an unfixed tax, the king or ruler may combine or separate the taxpayers as he wishes. No individual or community may induce the king to attach another community to them or separate it if doing so harms others. One who does so has the law of an informer. They may, however, state truthfully that they are few in number or present a similar claim, and the king may then act as he chooses. If the inhabitants of a province were partners in paying taxes and the king later granted part of the province to his son, they are separated if the tax is thereafter paid to the son. If it continues to be paid to the king, the original partnership remains. A person seized by the ruler who was forced to pay a large sum and was thereby exempted from taxes need not contribute with the community. If he serves the ruler and receives exemption in return, he remains liable, as explained. Some say that if expenses are incurred to secure the ruler's assistance in collecting promissory notes, persons who do not transact through notes need not contribute. See above at the end of seif 3 of this siman. If a community exempts a person from taxes, the exemption is effective, and communal enactments require no formal act of acquisition. The community may nevertheless claim that it exempted him for one year only. See below at the end of siman 231 concerning communal enactments. Ancient regulations state that no tallit or book may be removed from a synagogue without the owner's permission. A person who does not share the public burden may compel others to comply with communal obligations, but he may not serve as a collector.
