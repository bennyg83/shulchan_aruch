A person. Bava Basra 25a.
The rule that a person. This follows Rabbeinu Chananel's explanation that the town rescued him. Terumas HaDeshen and Maharam of Rothenburg discuss it.
All this where the ruler exempted him. Once liability to the community already arose, a ruler cannot unlawfully exempt him from the community's claim. Ri proves this from Gittin's wording "the law of the kingdom," rather than simply "the king."
One who waived. Rosh and Mordechai cite the Tosefta, following Rabbi Simcha's explanation at the end of Bava Kama.
Some say that before... but. This follows the plain Tosefta. Before the community settles the amount, an official's special demand upon one member can increase the burden on the others, like the caravan case in Bava Kama 116b. He can waive only his own share, not theirs.
One whom the ruler exempted. Bava Basra 8a shows that even people exempt from ordinary taxes can owe for public protection, except Torah scholars who do not require such protection.
An individual who gives. He remains a partner in the community and cannot unilaterally divide the common obligation, analogous to Bava Metzia 69a-b.
The community are partners. The first chapter's incident of the royal levy was an arbitrary imposition; the instruction to flee showed that those who escaped would thereby avoid it.
Or were exempted. All the more so.
If there came. Bava Metzia 140 concerning exchanged rainwater. Although the loss there is immediate and certain, communal liability can likewise include future but predictable government exactions.
Regarding a tax that is not. Bava Basra 153a.
They have no authority. Because it is not a valid law of the kingdom, as above.
Residents of the province. Bava Kama 116b. In circumstances of shared loss, joint contribution applies, all the more where the king himself separated the groups.
One whom the ruler seized. If the ruler's taking from him substantially reduces the others' taxes, the partnership principle of Bava Kama applies. This differs from one who independently serves the ruler.
Some say that if. Bava Kama 113b distinguishes a resident of the city from one attached to the land. Even if they are partners in some taxes, they are not partners in every obligation.
A community. A waiver does not require a kinyan. As stated above in siman 12.
All communal statements. Bava Basra 8b allows townspeople to make conditions, and Rabbeinu Chananel explains this without a kinyan. The Yerushalmi Megillah likewise teaches that seven representatives of the city can act with communal authority in ordinary matters.
Nevertheless, they may. The text should mean that the community are the possessors, and the burden of proof is on the claimant.
Not to take out. Even where communal property may be used for its intended purpose, one may prohibit removing it, analogous to one borrowing a Torah scroll who may not lend it onward.
One who is not... may. Bava Basra 7b concerning Rabbi Yehudah Nesia's levy shows that even a leader is not automatically exempt from communal authority. Rabbi could compel those who fled to return for an obligation that lawfully applied.
But not. Kiddushin 76b teaches that a Torah scholar exempt from communal labor cannot be appointed as collector over others for that same labor. This includes excavation work where scholars are exempt even if others personally labor.
