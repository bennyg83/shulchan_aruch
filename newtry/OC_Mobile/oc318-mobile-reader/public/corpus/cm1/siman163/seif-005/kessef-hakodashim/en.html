(And all the communities are obligated to contribute to this.) Regarding the rule at the end of siman 163, it requires study whether the community that paid the settlement must have expressly intended also to save the other communities. Maharik does not expressly state such a condition. Perhaps it was unnecessary because it was self evident from the circumstances that they intended to protect all of the communities, especially since they had demanded contributions from the others before making the settlement. If, however, it is genuinely doubtful whether they intended to save the other communities, siman 64, seif 4, appears to imply that the others need not contribute where the paying community would have had to pay the same amount solely to save itself. In a case of doubt money cannot be extracted, for the burden of proof rests upon the claimant, and the other communities can say that they do not know whether they ever became obligated. Still, there may be a presumption that a person does not spend money for nothing. If the paying community could reduce its own loss by intending the payment to protect the others as well, perhaps we presume that this was indeed its intent. The matter requires further study.
