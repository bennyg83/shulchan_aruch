A courtyard in another city. See Shivat Tzion, siman 107, who applies this rule to synagogue seats. Individuals from a small neighboring community had purchased ten seats in the synagogue of a larger community when the seats were originally allocated, and at that time they also shared in synagogue expenses. For several years they later ceased contributing and did not ordinarily come there to pray. The larger community sought either to rescind the sale and return the purchase money or to require continued participation in the synagogue's expenses. He ruled that the purchasers retain their seats and need not contribute to the ongoing expenses of a chazzan, shamash, and lights so long as they do not use the synagogue for prayer. They must, however, contribute proportionately to structural repairs of the synagogue and to governmental taxes assessed on the synagogue property, according to the value represented by their ten seats. The larger community may also compel them to rent those seats to local residents at a fair annual rental, as Rama rules in siman 162:7. All this applies if the original sale contained no condition. If the sale was expressly conditioned on their continuing to contribute to synagogue expenses, their refusal to do so causes them to lose their rights. If the parties dispute whether such a condition existed and the matter cannot be clarified from the communal ledger or other evidence, the larger community is treated as the party in possession, since the synagogue belongs to that community, and the outside purchasers must bring proof.
