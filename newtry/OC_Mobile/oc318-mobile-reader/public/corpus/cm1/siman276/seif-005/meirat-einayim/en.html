But a fetus does not inherit, etc. The Gemara explains that the fetus dies first. Tosafot explain that because its vitality is slight, the effect of the Angel of Death reaches it and cuts its signs before the mother dies. The rule that if one slaughters an animal and finds a living nine month fetus inside, the fetus can survive, concerns a case where the mother was slaughtered or killed by a person, so the mother died first. Likewise, where the mother dies and then gives birth, the case may involve death caused by a person, or a fetus already uprooted and ready to emerge, in which case the fetus does not necessarily die first. One may ask: if in killing, or where the fetus has already been uprooted to emerge, the mother dies first, why does the Mishnah state specifically that a one day old child inherits and transmits? A fetus should sometimes inherit as well. Tosafot answer that the one day old child provides an absolute rule, while a fetus does not. The Rambam likewise rules this way in chapter 1 of Hilchot Nachalot. This is unlike the Ir Shushan, who writes that even if the fetus later emerges alive and dies without becoming viable, it does not inherit. That is incorrect. Once it emerges alive it inherits and transmits, as Tosafot states. If he meant a fetus born at eight months and inherently nonviable, that is also not simple. The Maggid Mishneh questioned the Rambam's rule that even one born before full term inherits unless he is clearly nonviable. In Hilchot Yibbum the Rambam limits this to a fetus whose hair and nails are complete. Thus one cannot simply distinguish fetus from born child as the Ir Shushan does.
