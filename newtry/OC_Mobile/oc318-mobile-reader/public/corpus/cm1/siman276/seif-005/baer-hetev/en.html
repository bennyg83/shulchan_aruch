To bequeath. See Responsa Rashach, volume 2, siman 197.
A fetus. The Gemara in Bava Batra 142 states: what is the reason? Because the fetus dies first. Tosafot explain that, as stated in the first chapter of Arachin, because its vitality is slight, the drop of the Angel of Death enters it and cuts its vital passages. The statement in the chapter HaMekasheh cited by the Tur, Yoreh Deah siman 13, that if one slaughtered an animal and found a living nine-month fetus inside it, refers specifically to slaughter or killing, in which case the mother dies first. The statement in the chapter HaShochet that the mother died and afterward gave birth refers either to death caused by a person, or to a case where the fetus had already been displaced toward birth, in which case it does not die first, as implied in Arachin. One might ask: if so, why does the Mishnah specifically state 'a one-day-old child'? A fetus too can inherit and transmit inheritance. The answer is that the rule for a one-day-old child is categorical, whereas with a fetus it is not categorical that it can inherit and transmit. The Rambam likewise rules this in the first chapter of Hilchot Nachalot, unlike the Levush, who writes that even if this fetus later emerged into the world and died without becoming viable, it does not inherit and transmit. That is incorrect, because once it was born alive it inherits and transmits, as Tosafot write. One cannot say that the Levush means that she miscarried it at eight months and it was not viable, for then why distinguish a fetus? The same would be true if it had been born during the mother's lifetime. Although the Rambam writes there that once it is born, even if its months were incomplete, it nevertheless inherits its mother, the Maggid Mishneh writes that this ruling requires study and that he does not know a clear proof for it. In the first chapter of Hilchot Yibbum he explains that the case is specifically where the child's hair and nails were complete, as Darkei Moshe cites. If so, the same issue arises regarding a fetus, and from where is there a basis to distinguish between them? Sema.
