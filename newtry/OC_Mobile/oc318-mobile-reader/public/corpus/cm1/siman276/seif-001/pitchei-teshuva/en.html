His son inherits him. Chatam Sofer, Even HaEzer II:158, discusses a man who struck his mother. The communal officers penalized him until he declared, "I willingly remove myself from my mother's inheritance when she dies." This was recorded in the communal register and he signed it. After her death he argued that such a renunciation was legally ineffective and sought his share. Chatam Sofer agrees that no disclaimer or acquisition can ordinarily remove a person from the inheritance of his own ancestors, as Ran writes at the beginning of HaKotev and as Beit Shmuel and Beit Meir rule, contrary to an implication in Chavot Yair 50. Here, however, because the undertaking was imposed as a penalty, Chatam Sofer rules that he can be compelled to honor it, especially since he also bound himself by a vow not to enter those assets. Nevertheless, although he himself is not allowed to take the property, if he has sons they inherit the grandmother's estate through their father, because their right derives from their paternal grandmother's line.
