The heirs do not inherit, etc. Likewise, even the owner himself cannot remove the benefit from others when it is in their possession, as stated in Responsa Maharam in Perek Shevuat HaDayanim and Maharik, shoresh 161. The law concerning an heir likewise refers to such a case. If the benefit is already in the heir's possession, it is obvious that the discretionary benefit belongs to him. Maharam writes that just as the owner cannot bequeath it, he cannot give it. The Darkei Moshe cites this. Meaning, if it is in others' possession, he cannot remove it from them and give it to others. If it is in his own possession, however, he may give it to others so that they may distribute it to whomever they choose. Examine this carefully.
