The heirs do not, etc., or a benefit of discretion, etc. See Baer Heitev and Noda BiYehudah, second edition, Choshen Mishpat 51. A wealthy man before his death set aside a fixed fund whose principal was to remain intact and whose profits were to support scholars studying in his beit midrash. The power to select the scholars was assigned to his sons and descendants. The town leaders and representatives of the community undertook to pay the profits to the deceased's descendants. Generations later, unworthy heirs began selecting unsuitable scholars in exchange for money. The question was whether the town leaders could object and select suitable scholars themselves. Noda BiYehudah writes that the heirs possess only a benefit of discretion, and siman 276 expressly rules that heirs do not inherit such a benefit. The Shach adds that even the original holder cannot extract it from another party who presently holds the property. Thus the heirs lack the power to extract control from the communal officers, who may alter the selection and choose appropriate scholars as they see fit. The original written agreement also stated that the leaders accepted all its details under a ban and a Torah oath, but that personal oath-bound obligation applied only to the first generation. Nevertheless, it is improper to be ungrateful and deliberately oppose the deceased's intent. Since the heirs themselves are acting deceitfully for profit, the communal officers may respond firmly. At minimum they may prevent the heirs from choosing anyone except a proper scholar approved by the town's leading authorities, while it is also appropriate that the officers themselves not choose without the heirs' agreement.
