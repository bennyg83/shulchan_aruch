But a son of a maidservant or gentile woman, etc. The reason is that such children are not genealogically attributed to their father. Regarding a maidservant Scripture says, "the woman and her children shall belong to her master." Regarding a gentile woman the law is learned from "for he will turn your son away from Me," using masculine language only in the case of a Jewish daughter married to a gentile, where her child is Jewish and may be turned away. Scripture does not say correspondingly that a gentile woman will turn "your son" away, because a child born to her is not genealogically considered the Jewish father's child. See Even HaEzer siman 4.
Or a right of discretionary benefit, etc. Maharam Padua writes that just as one cannot bequeath a mere right of discretionary benefit, one cannot give it as a gift to others either. Darkei Moshe cites this at the end of the siman. See siman 37:9 and siman 87:35 regarding rights of discretionary benefit.
