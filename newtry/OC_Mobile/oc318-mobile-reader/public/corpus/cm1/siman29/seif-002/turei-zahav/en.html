There: “Since they were silent” The Sema cites a responsum of the Rosh that one who simply says “I do not remember” may not later testify. This is difficult from Ketubot, where Rav Ashi first told Rav Kahana, “I do not know,” and later remembered and testified. The distinction does not depend on the word “now.” The Rosh’s own responsum distinguishes one who says, “I never paid attention to this,” who may not later testify, from one who says, “I do not presently remember,” thereby indicating that the matter may be restored to memory. Even the simple phrase “I do not remember” is uncertain language implying prior knowledge now forgotten. This is the correct explanation.
