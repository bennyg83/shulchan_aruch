After the witness testified in beit din, etc. The Tur and Rambam say, "after their testimony was examined in beit din," because until the examination is complete a witness can retract. Since monetary law and similar cases do not ordinarily require formal examination and interrogation, the Mechaber changed the wording and simply wrote, "after he testified." Sema. See Tumim for what I wrote about whether a witness may retract before formal examination.
And this is after tokh kedei dibbur. The measure of tokh kedei dibbur is the time needed to say three words, "Shalom alecha, rebbi." See what I wrote below in siman 255 in explanation of Rambam.
He cannot retract, etc. The Sema writes that the first witness has until the second witness completes his testimony, plus tokh kedei dibbur after the second testimony, to retract. This is correct, as is explicit in Shevuos 32. It appears that this applies only where the second witness began within tokh kedei dibbur after the first completed his testimony and continued immediately. If the second waited longer than tokh kedei dibbur after the first had completed, the first can no longer retract. This is likewise implied by that Gemara. Even if they did not testify affirmatively but both denied knowledge, saying, "We know no testimony," the first may retract within tokh kedei dibbur of the second witness's denial and say, "I do know," and then testify, provided the second denial began within tokh kedei dibbur of the first. See the Gemara there. The Sema further writes that if the litigant interrupts the witness with words during his testimony, the testimony is not yet complete and the witness may finish his statement, provided he does not contradict his earlier testimony. From the Sema it appears that these are the Ran's words, but in truth this is Beit Yosef's own statement. One who examines the passage will see that it concerns a witness who had already completed his statement and was then interrupted by the litigant. In that situation he may add a condition but not wholly contradict his testimony. If he was interrupted before completing his testimony, it is obvious that he may even say something inconsistent with what had appeared from his incomplete words, because his testimony had not yet been completed. The Sema abbreviated Beit Yosef's point.
He cannot add a condition to his testimony, etc. See what I wrote at length in siman 46, where this subject properly belongs.
But regarding something that proves he erred, etc. See Tumim, where I concluded as a matter of law contrary to Ritva's implication that this is limited to tokh kedei dibbur. Although ordinarily a witness can retract within tokh kedei dibbur in any event, where there is reason to say that he retracted from fear he cannot do so unless there is objective proof of error. Ritva's limitation does not appear correct. See there.
And similarly regarding every error, etc. See what I wrote above in siman 43, seif 8, regarding Rama's gloss.
We should reconcile his words. Even by interpreting them in a somewhat remote sense, as Rivash writes in responsum 476. There one witness says that an oral condition existed that was not written in the document, while other witnesses testify that all conditions were written, although they admit that many oral matters were discussed but not written. Rivash holds that there is no contradiction. Perhaps those witnesses did not regard the particular oral statement as a formal condition, and therefore said that no oral condition remained unwritten. See there, and Sema. See what I wrote below in siman 30 at length concerning how far we reconcile witnesses' statements.
The words of two witnesses, etc. Such as where one says the event occurred on the second day of the month and the other says the third, and we say that one of them erred concerning the declaration of the new month.
And they did not say, "We do not know." The Sema cites Responsa Rosh that if a witness says, "I do not remember now," he may later return and testify, but if he simply says, "I do not know," he cannot later change his testimony. See Tumim, where I wrote that this distinction is unfounded and has no substance. I also noted difficulties in the Rosh's words, and the practical law requires study, especially because Hagahos HaRosh in the second chapter of Kesubos implies that even one who says, "I do not remember," cannot later return and testify. See there.
And even if they said, "We do not know," and gave an explanation, etc. See Tumim, where I wrote that this does not refer to a case in which they were subjected to a cherem having the force of an oath and denied knowledge. Once they did so, they cannot later testify even if they offer an explanation. See there. Responsa Penei Moshe, siman 107, writes that judges likewise cannot revise their statements after final judgment. The sages of his generation agreed with him. This is correct.
