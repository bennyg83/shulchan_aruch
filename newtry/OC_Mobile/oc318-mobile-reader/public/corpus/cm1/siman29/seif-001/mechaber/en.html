Witnesses May Not Retract. Three seifim. After a witness testifies in beit din and the brief period needed to correct speech has passed, he may not retract. If he says, 'I was mistaken,' 'I acted unintentionally and now remember that it was otherwise,' or 'I said it from fear,' he is not heard, even if he gives a reason. Nor may he add a condition to his testimony, though some say that he may add a condition. If his words themselves demonstrate an error, however, his correction is heard. For example, if a person was required to bring witnesses that he was not deceitful, brought them, and the beit din asked, 'Do you know him to be deceitful?' and they answered yes, but when the litigant protested they explained, 'We meant only that you are not deceitful,' their latter words are accepted. A person ordinarily does not bring a witness to testify against him, so they plainly erred. Witnesses are similarly believed to correct any error that witnesses commonly make; this is not considered retracting and testifying again. Likewise, if they do not contradict the substance of their first words, but merely clarify ambiguous words that admit two interpretations, one likely and one remote, their words should be construed, wherever possible, so their testimony is not contradicted, just as apparently conflicting testimony of two witnesses should be reconciled where possible. {Rama: If a ban was proclaimed in the synagogue requiring witnesses to testify before leaving, and after they left witnesses came and said, 'We did not then pay attention to the need to testify, but now we remember,' they may testify because they were silent and did not say that they knew nothing. Even if they said, 'We do not know,' but later gave a credible explanation for saying so, they may testify, since they did not previously state the opposite of their later testimony. See also siman 80.}
