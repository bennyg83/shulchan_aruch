(Collected note) Likewise he cannot add, etc. According to Rashi there, s.v. "Al Korchech, etc.," and Tosafot prove this there, s.v. "Ika DeAmrei, etc." The Ramban likewise demonstrates it from the statement there: "rather we say these words are to uproot, etc." If their handwriting is verified from elsewhere, on the contrary, that itself is the reason that if they uproot the document they are not believed at all. Further, how could a single witness ever be believed when his handwriting is verified from elsewhere? If so, one witness would establish monetary liability, contrary to "one witness shall not rise, etc.," for a single witness establishes only an oath. Even if he merely says it was repaid, one witness is not believed at all, even without contradiction, and certainly not when contradicted. (End of collected note.)
And some say. So rules the Shulchan Aruch in siman 82.
(Collected note) And some say, etc. The Ran likewise writes that if, when their handwriting is verified elsewhere, they are not believed, then we consider their original testimony as though they had explicitly written that there was no condition. If so, even when their handwriting is not verified elsewhere they should not be believed, because they make themselves wicked, like witnesses who say the document was a document of trust. Rather, the Gemara's question applies in every case: if they are not believed, they are never believed; if believed, they are always believed. See his explanation of the sugya. (End of collected note.)

(Collected note) Likewise with every error, etc. Yerushalmi, chapter 10 of Sheviit: antedated promissory notes are invalid, etc. Who informs us? Shimon bar Ba in the name of Rabbi Yochanan: those signed on the document. But did Reish Lakish not say that the words of those signed on a document are treated as testimony already examined in beit din? There they say, "we never signed at all"; here they say, "we signed regarding this and did not sign regarding that." Rav Zerachyah writes that from here we learn the rule for every mistake that witnesses are prone to make. See below, siman 43, seif 8 in the gloss, where the Ramban disagrees and holds that the Yerushalmi concerns a case where their handwriting is not verified elsewhere, so it is not called retracting and retestifying, as explained in chapter 2 of Ketubot. The novelty is that it is not regarded as though they said, "it was a trust document"; see there. (End of collected note.)
