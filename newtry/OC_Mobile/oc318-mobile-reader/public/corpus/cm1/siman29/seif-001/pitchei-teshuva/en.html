He cannot retract. Shaar Mishpat considers two sets of witnesses who contradict one another, such as two saying that a loan occurred or was repaid and two denying it, after which one set retracts its first statement. Does the rule that a witness cannot testify and then retract still apply, leaving the case as two against two, or may the first set retract because two witnesses contradict them? Rivash, siman 267, states explicitly that even where two sets contradict one another, a retraction is ineffective. Shaar Mishpat then limits this. Rivash speaks where the retracting witnesses say that they deliberately lied at first, in which case they are not believed, since a person cannot render himself wicked even for an instant. But if they say, 'We were mistaken,' they may retract when two witnesses contradict them. Since the Shulchan Aruch rules that where it is demonstrable that witnesses erred they may correct their testimony, as where a litigant's own evidence establishes his error, all the more so where two witnesses directly contradict them, which is strong proof that they erred; see there.
