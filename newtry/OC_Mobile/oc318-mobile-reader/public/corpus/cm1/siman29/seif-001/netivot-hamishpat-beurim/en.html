After he testified. See Sema, whose language implies that in matters requiring formal examination and interrogation, a witness can retract before those examinations are completed. Urim VeTumim objects from Tosafot in Pesachim, which appears to show that even in such matters a witness cannot retract. There is no difficulty after Tosafot's own later resolution. Since the Torah specifically requires the seven interrogations and provides that contradiction within them can invalidate testimony, a witness can still nullify his testimony by contradicting himself during that unfinished process. All the more so he may retract the underlying event before the process is complete. It would be absurd if he could invalidate himself by changing the date but not by saying, 'The loan never occurred at all.' He also has a migo: he could have destroyed the testimony by contradicting one of the formal interrogations. Conversely, once the testimony has already been invalidated by contradiction in the interrogations, he certainly cannot later, after the time for immediate retraction has passed, restore the testimony by changing again. Tosafot's earlier language therefore remains consistent.
