After he testified in beit din. The Tur and Rambam, Hilchot Edut chapter 3, write "after his testimony was examined in beit din." The Mechaber deliberately altered their language because in monetary cases, and generally in matters adjudicated nowadays, formal derishah and chakirah are not required, as explained at the beginning of the following siman. The questions and examinations described in the preceding siman, seif 8, suffice. Thus the Tur and Rambam's language is not meant literally. See Perishah. See also the end of siman 46 regarding one witness whose testimony is in a document and who can retract in the situation explained there.
And this is after the time of toch kedei dibbur. Within toch kedei dibbur a witness may retract. Even the first witness may retract within the period of the second witness's testimony where both came to beit din together to testify, because it is treated as one testimony. So writes the Ran. In Derishah I wrote further that if a litigant interrupted the witness and he then immediately says he wishes to complete his testimony, he may do so and is believed, provided he does not entirely contradict his original testimony.

We do not listen to him, for Scripture says, "if he does not tell, he shall bear his sin." This implies that once the witness has failed to state something in his first testimony, he bears responsibility for that omission and cannot later return and change the testimony. Hagahot Mordechai writes that witnesses who testified in beit din and then returned and testified differently are given lashes, while the original testimony remains in force.
He cannot add a condition to his testimony. This too contradicts his earlier words, for his original unqualified testimony implied that the matter was effective in every circumstance. This rule applies where they testified orally before beit din, or by a document whose signatures can be authenticated independently of them. Otherwise, the principle that the mouth which prohibited is the mouth which permits applies. See Perishah. See also the end of siman 46 and the end of siman 82 concerning one witness who says there was a condition and another who says there was no condition. The Tur writes here that according to the Rosh we set one witness against the other and the defendant takes a shevuat heset.
We must reconcile his words. Meaning that even if the reconciliation requires an explanation somewhat distant from the plain implication, we strive to interpret the testimony consistently. Rivash, siman 476, writes similarly concerning one witness who says there was an oral condition between a cantor and the community, while other witnesses say all conditions were written in the document. They nevertheless admit that not everything discussed orally was written. This is not an actual contradiction, because perhaps they did not consider those oral discussions to constitute a formal condition.

Since they remained silent. The same applies if a witness first said, "I do not remember now." He may later say, "I reflected upon the matter and remembered." This differs from one who initially says simply, "I do not remember," who may not later return and testify. So writes the Rosh in Responsa, rule 59, siman 1.
And they did not say, "We do not know." If witnesses state in beit din, "We do not know," they may no longer return and testify differently. Tosafot at the end of chapter HaKones; Beit Yosef. See also siman 28.
Since they did not state the opposite. At the beginning of this siman the Mechaber ruled that a witness may not retract even if he offers an explanation for the change. That case concerns one who actually states the opposite of his first testimony. Here he merely adds or explains a matter that does not contradict the earlier statement.
