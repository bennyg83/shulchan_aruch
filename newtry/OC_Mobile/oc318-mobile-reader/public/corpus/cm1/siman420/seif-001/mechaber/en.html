It is forbidden for a person to strike another. If he does strike him, he violates a Torah prohibition, as it is stated, "lest he add." If the Torah was particular that a wicked person not be struck beyond the measure of his wickedness, all the more so may a righteous person not be struck. One who raises his hand against another intending to strike him is called wicked even if he does not actually strike him. Rama: See below, siman 421, seif 13. Regarding a man who strikes his wife, see Even HaEzer siman 154. Some say that there is an ancient communal ban against one who strikes another person, and that the ban must be released before he may be counted in a minyan of ten. As soon as he accepts upon himself to submit to judgment, the ban may be released even if the person who was struck does not consent. Maharam of Rothenburg and Hagahot Maimoniyot, chapter HaChovel.
