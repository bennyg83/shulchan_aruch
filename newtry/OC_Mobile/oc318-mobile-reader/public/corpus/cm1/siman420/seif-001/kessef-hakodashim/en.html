It is a great sin. Although one who humiliates another verbally is exempt from monetary payment, there is nevertheless punishment. It appears this applies where, according to the listeners' understanding, the words objectively carry the character of verbal oppression or humiliation and the speaker intended to embarrass him. If the speaker genuinely thought the words were not humiliating in the eyes of the listeners, or believed he was properly voicing a legitimate grievance against his fellow, and afterward it became clear that he had been mistaken and had no justified complaint, then if the listeners understand that he acted innocently or under a factual error, he appears exempt from further consequence. Just as an incompetent person who humiliates another is exempt, one whose specific error deprived him of normal understanding in that matter resembles an incompetent person in that respect. Perhaps even spitting would be treated more leniently where the act came from a genuine error rather than knowing and settled intention. Verbal statements are especially likely to arise from the mistaken belief that one is entitled to speak that way. Words that are merely somewhat impudent toward an honored person, without substantial degradation or the normal character of verbal oppression, do not necessarily fall into the severe category. If the townspeople wish to enact a local ordinance to close a breach in conduct according to the needs of their time and place, they may do so for the sake of Heaven through the proper communal authority, as explained at the end of siman 231, but no more than is justified. This requires further study.
