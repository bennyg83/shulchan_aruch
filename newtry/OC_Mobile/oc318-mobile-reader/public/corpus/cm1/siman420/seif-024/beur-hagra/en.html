Likewise, the person who is humiliated, etc. This is stated on 86a. Rambam rules like Rabbi Yehuda, whose law prevails against Rabbi Meir and Rabbi Shimon, as stated in Eruvin chapter 4. Although the Mishna was interpreted according to Rabbi Shimon, that was only because Rabbi Yehuda says that a blind person, etc. On this issue the plain sense of the Mishna better accords with Rabbi Yehuda. Rif therefore omits their dispute, following the Mishna's plain meaning, unlike Rosh, who rules like Rabbi Shimon.
(Supplement) Likewise, etc. This follows Rabbi Yehuda. Rosh rules like Rabbi Shimon because the Gemara establishes the Mishna according to him, but that is not conclusive. Rosh also brings proof from the Mishna on 90a concerning one who uncovered a woman's head, where Rabbi Akiva's response implies that the first tanna likewise evaluates humiliation according to free persons who have lost their wealth. This is a strong proof.
And one who degrades, etc. This follows the discussion in HaNizakin. See Rambam, Laws of the Temple Vessels, chapter 4.
