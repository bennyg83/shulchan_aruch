Even, etc. The Torah imposes liability for a wound. Therefore the rule applies specifically where the injury does not heal, as Shabbat 107b asks, 'From where is liability for a wound derived, etc.?' This explains, 'therefore, etc.,' following Chullin chapter 3 regarding creeping creatures, and the same applies to a person. Nevertheless, 88a implies that it applies specifically to a visible place, such as the face, and Maggid Mishneh objects accordingly. This must be so, because the injured person is evaluated as a slave, while Ketubot 58 and Kiddushin 11 teach that concealed blemishes do not reduce such value. Likewise, freedom of a slave depends on visible, nonregenerating blemishes.
