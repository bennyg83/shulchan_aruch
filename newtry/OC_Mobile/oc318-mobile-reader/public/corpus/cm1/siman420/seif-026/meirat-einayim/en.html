He gives him the value of the whole person. The Tur cites my father Rosh: with respect to bodily damage and loss of work there is no difference whether the assessment is made once for the entire injury or separately for each limb. In either case we determine how much his value was reduced because of each limb until all the losses are combined. Likewise, the loss of work between one injury and the next is plainly paid, because until he becomes deaf he remains capable of work once each injury heals. The only difference concerns pain, medical expenses, and humiliation. Clearly each blow creates its own pain, medical expense, and humiliation, but their combined valuation when each blow is assessed separately is greater than if all injuries are assessed as though inflicted at one time, even if the difference is small. For bodily damage and loss of work there is no such difference at all, because in the end we still assess how much his value was reduced by each individual limb. End of Rosh's words, with some wording adjusted for clarity. This is what Rama means when he says that he gives pain, medical expenses, and humiliation according to a combined estimate, because those categories differ depending on whether there is one assessment or a separate assessment for each injury, unlike bodily damage and loss of work, where there is no difference.
They assessed each injury separately, etc. This is an unresolved question in the Gemara. Rambam rules according to his general approach to unresolved monetary questions: absent seizure, we rule leniently for the defendant and stringently for the claimant, and he pays only the value of the whole person. If the injured party seized the separate valuations for each limb, the seizure is effective. Rama writes that in siman 388 it is explained that some disagree and hold that seizure does not help in a doubt of law. Know that Rosh and Tur follow that latter view in siman 388 and in other laws discussed in this siman, yet regarding this case they nevertheless hold that he gives the separate assessed value of each injury. Their reason is that the practical difference is only slight, concerning medical expenses and humiliation, while bodily damage and loss of work do not differ at all. Therefore, once the court had already assessed each limb separately, they rule that those separate valuations are collected.
If the injured party seized the damage value of each limb and the value of the whole person, it is not taken from him, etc. Rambam likewise writes this in chapter 2 of Hilchot Chovel. Maggid Mishneh explains that if Rambam means by "the value of each limb" all of the separate damage payments, then the first phrase "the value of the whole person" cannot mean the person's value when he was entirely healthy. Rather, the first "value of the whole" means the value after the accumulated injuries as assessed in the unresolved question, while the later "value of the whole" refers to the remaining value of the person after his eye, hand, and foot have already been lost. Certainly, if the injured party seized both all separate limb values and also the entire original value of the healthy person, we remove the excess from him. See Derishah for the explanation of Maggid Mishneh's words and the related discussion.
