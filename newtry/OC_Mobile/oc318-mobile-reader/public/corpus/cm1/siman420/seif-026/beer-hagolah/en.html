yud) Ibid., law 13. The Maggid Mishneh writes that this is Rava's question there, followed by an 'if you say': since they did not first assess him, he gives him the value of the entire injury. The Rambam rules according to the 'if you say,' as is his general practice in the Gemara.
kaf) So appears in the Gemara, Rambam, and Tur. The meaning is that the injuries are treated as though they were all inflicted at one time, because the total appraisal is then less than the sum of separate appraisals, Tosafot.
lamed) The Maggid Mishneh writes likewise there in the name of Tosafot, and that the Rashba agreed. It is possible that this is also the Rambam's view.
mem) Ibid., an unresolved question in the Gemara.
nun) The Maggid Mishneh explains that this refers to the value after his worth was already reduced by the loss of his hand and foot. Certainly, if the injured party seized the full value he had when healthy, it is removed from him. If all the appraisals necessarily produced the same total, there would be no issue, but there is a difference between one combined appraisal and multiple appraisals, as stated above.
