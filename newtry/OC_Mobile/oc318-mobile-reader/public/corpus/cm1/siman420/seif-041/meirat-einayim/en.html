This is the amount, etc. Meaning, medical expenses and loss of work are included in this fixed amount. Even if that blow caused actual monetary expense for medical treatment or loss of work, he need not pay more. The Ir Shushan changes the wording and writes, "They said that he should pay for pain, humiliation, medical expenses, and loss of work," which implies that the original enactment was fixed with medical costs and loss of work in mind. This change is unnecessary, and we do not find that the Sages stated it this way.
Whether he requires medical treatment. At the end of this siman Rama cites those who disagree and hold that these fixed sums are not compensation for medical expenses and loss of work. The Sages fixed set amounts only for pain and humiliation, which exist in every blow a person inflicts upon another. If the victim also incurs medical expenses or loss of work, those are paid in addition according to the particular circumstances, because the Sages could not set a uniform amount for them.
He pays one hundred sela'im, etc. The Tur concludes here that the same applies if he uncovered a woman's head, for which he pays one hundred sela'im. Darkei Moshe cites Nimukei Maharam: one who mocks the words of the Sages receives lashes, and one who calls another man's wife a zonah or a promiscuous woman receives four sets of lashes. The Mishnah teaches that one who uncovers a woman's head pays four hundred zuz, and because fines are not collected in Babylonia, four sets of lashes are imposed instead. See above, siman 2, what Darkei Moshe cites in the name of Mahariv, meaning that each set of lashes corresponds to forty zuz.
