He receives forty lashes. See Tosafos to Kiddushin 28a, who writes that this is measure for measure: he says that his fellow violated a negative commandment, and therefore, etc.; see there. In Magen Avraham, siman 610, seif katan 6, it is explained that this refers to the prohibition, "A mamzer shall not enter," etc.; see there and Machatzis HaShekel ad loc. See Responsa Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat, siman 181, who writes that one should not object that, if so, what can be said where one humiliates an unmarried man. One can answer that, in any event, he seeks to bring him to liability for lashes when he marries a woman. This is also explicit in Yam Shel Shlomo, Bava Kamma, chapter 8, siman 44; see there. Yam Shel Shlomo there further writes that the rule of lashes does not apply only to one who calls another a mamzer, but to every defamatory accusation that entails a negative prohibition. Chatam Sofer there comments that, were it not for his words, he would have said that where one tells another, "You ate forbidden fat," and the like, perhaps he already repented; likewise, the ancestors of a mamzer may have acted under duress or repented. By contrast, the status of mamzer is a continuing blemish, and he thereby suspects him of being destined to violate the prohibition of "shall not enter" whenever he marries a woman. Chatam Sofer there further writes that the lashes are customarily redeemed with money. Mahariv writes that the amount is forty gold coins, and so writes Rama in Shulchan Aruch at the end of siman 2, etc. In Yam Shel Shlomo, Bava Kamma, chapter 8, siman 49, he writes that he found in the name of the great authorities that the lashes are redeemed with five Rhenish gold coins, and that this custom has the force of law even against the will of the person who was humiliated. In truth, this is a scribal error in Yam Shel Shlomo, and it should read eight Rhenish gold coins; so too in Responsa Maharshal, siman 101, it states eight gold coins, etc. See also Responsa Nachalas Shivah, part 2, end of siman 66. I further saw in Yam Shel Shlomo there that he protests the practice of distributing the fine to yeshiva students and the like, and he charges anyone who sees his work to tell the person who was humiliated that the money is his and that he may do with it whatever he wishes. Mahariv, however, at the end of siman 147, rules that he should give forty gold coins to charity, with the person who was humiliated choosing what is to be done with them. It appears to me that there is no dispute. Certainly, where the insult was to the honor of the humiliated person himself, the money belongs to him, and he may even use it to drink beer, as Maharshal rules. But where the insult was to the honor of his ancestors, he should not eat and rejoice from the humiliation of his ancestors. Rather, so that there be atonement for the offender, the money should be distributed to charity for the souls of those who dwell in the dust. Nevertheless, the benefit of directing the distribution belongs to the son, for he is their avenger of blood. Mahariv there is explicitly discussing such a case. These are his words; see there further. See also Atzmos Yosef to Kiddushin there, who cites the responsum of Ri Migash, of blessed memory, that if one called another "a mamzer, son of a mamzer," he is liable twice; but if he said to him, "Your father and you are mamzerim," he is liable only for one set of lashes. The reason is that it is known that when a man is a mamzer, his son and his descendants to the end of all generations are mamzerim. Therefore, once he first called the father a mamzer, he thereby necessarily characterized his offspring as mamzerim. But if he first called the son a mamzer, it is possible that the father is legitimate; when he then added the insult to the father by saying that he is the son of a mamzer, he incurs two sets of lashes. See there.
