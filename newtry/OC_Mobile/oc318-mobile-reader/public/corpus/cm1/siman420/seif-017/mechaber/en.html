How is compensation for lost time assessed? If no limb was permanently lost, but the injured person became ill and bedridden, or his hand swelled and will recover, the tortfeasor pays for each day according to what a worker in that occupation would accept to remain idle. If a limb was lost, such as where a hand was severed, the value of the hand is paid as bodily damage. Lost time is then assessed as though the victim were fit only to guard cucumbers, and the daily wage of such a guard is calculated for all the days of illness. If a leg was severed, he is assessed as a gatekeeper; if an eye was blinded, as one who turns a mill. The same principle applies in comparable cases. Rama: This applies to an ordinary person without a specialized trade. If he is a craftsman who can resume his craft after recovery, lost time is assessed according to the occupation from which he was actually prevented.
