The wording of the Tur, seif 24, and Rambam likewise writes in chapter 3, law 2. The Gemara straightforwardly asks, 'Is a naked person subject to shame?' and 'Is one in a bathhouse subject to shame?' Bava Kamma 86b. The Maggid Mishneh writes that Rambam follows Rashi: one who is not particular about walking naked before people is not subject to shame merely because someone spits on him or strikes him, and in a bathhouse people stand naked, so there is no shame. Tosafot, however, write that it is obvious that one who spits on or strikes a naked person is liable; the Mishnah itself says one who shames a naked person is liable. They explain the Gemara as discussing shame caused by undressing him, for if he is already naked, what can be removed? Rashba explains the bathhouse similarly. Beit Yosef concludes that because this is a question of extracting money, we rule leniently.
