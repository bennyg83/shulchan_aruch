One who humiliates a naked person. This is Rambam's wording. In Perishah I demonstrated that by writing "one who humiliates a naked person," Rambam follows Rashi, who holds that even if one humiliates him in other ways, such as spitting upon him or striking him, he is nevertheless exempt from the humiliation payment, because in a bathhouse, where the person walks naked, he is not considered susceptible to humiliation in the ordinary sense. All the more so if he is walking naked outside a bathhouse. If, however, he is not fully naked, he remains susceptible to humiliation and one is liable if another lifts his clothing somewhat further, all the more so if he humiliates him in another manner. Rabbeinu Yitzchak disagrees and holds that even where a person is naked or in a bathhouse, the exemption applies only to humiliation specifically through removing clothing when he is already fully undressed while bathing. If he is walking to bathe in a river and has lifted his garments somewhat, and another person lifts them further, or a wind lifted them and another lifts them further, the baraita's statement that one who humiliates a naked person or someone in a bathhouse is liable applies. It is surprising that Rama did not cite Rabbeinu Yitzchak's view, which contradicts Rashi's interpretation adopted by Rambam and the Mechaber.
