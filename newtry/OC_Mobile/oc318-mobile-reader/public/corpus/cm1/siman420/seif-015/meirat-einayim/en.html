As though he were a slave sold in the marketplace. Rashi and Nimukei Yosef at the beginning of chapter HaChovel explain this as though he were sold as a Hebrew slave. Rosh, however, writes that he is assessed like a Canaanite slave sold for permanent service, because the loss of a limb is permanent. A Hebrew slave is sold only for six years. If we attempted to assess the value repeatedly in successive six-year terms, the total would exceed the value obtained by assessing him once as a lifelong slave. I already discussed this in siman 1. Even if the injured person is distinguished, bodily damage is assessed by the value of a slave. The injury to his dignity because of his stature is compensated under humiliation, which depends on the standing of the person humiliated, as explained below. For bodily damage, however, everyone is assessed by the same standard.
Because it does not damage him as much. The Tur writes in the name of Rosh that assessing a person according to his occupation is used specifically to increase the valuation. For example, if a gem cutter's hand was severed, the loss is greater because his occupation depends upon his hand. But if his leg was severed and his loss in value as a gem cutter would be less than the loss in value of an ordinary servant available for all forms of work, because the master of a gem cutter does not care as much about the loss of his leg since he remains fit for his original occupation and may even be more fixed in that work, we do not assess him specifically as a gem cutter. It is reasonable that he should not lose because of his profession, since he could choose not to pursue that occupation and instead be like any other person available for general service. Rama intended this in his abbreviated language.
