Only one eighth, etc. The Tur and Rambam write that a provincial sela contains half a dinar of silver, which is one eighth of a Tyrian sela containing four silver dinars, and three and a half dinars of copper. Therefore, one who is obligated to pay one hundred sela'im pays twelve and a half sela'im of pure silver. In Perishah I explained that this does not mean he fulfills his entire obligation merely by paying twelve and a half pure silver sela'im. Since he was obligated in provincial sela'im, each containing seven parts copper and one part silver, payment of the silver component does not discharge the copper component. Rather, the statement means that when he pays in provincial sela'im, he must ensure that they contain no less than the equivalent of twelve and a half sela'im of silver, with the remainder made up of copper. Because places and periods differ, and the silver content of the provincial sela may be reduced while copper is added, the authorities stated the minimum silver content so that it not fall below this amount. This also explains why the Mechaber simply states the ratio and does not instruct him to give twelve and a half silver sela'im. His purpose is only to teach how much silver the coin must contain. Rambam, at the end of chapter 3 of Hilchot Chovel, likewise hints to this by beginning: "All these sela'im are of the coinage of Eretz Yisrael at that time, when each sela contained... half a dinar of silver," etc. His wording shows that he is teaching the composition of the coin in the era when the Sages of the Mishnah fixed these amounts. From that standard we learn for every later time and place according to the local coinage, provided its silver content is not less than this amount. See Perishah for further discussion.
