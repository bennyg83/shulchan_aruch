peh) The Mishnah there, as Rav Pappa explains it on 91a.
tzadi) Ibid., where the scholars of Eretz Yisrael said in the name of Rabbi Yose bar Avin.
kuf) Rambam there, law 5.
reish) Tur, seif 33, in the name of Rav Sherira Gaon, and he writes that his father the Rosh likewise ruled in chapter HaChovel. The Rosh writes that verbal humiliation is logically more severe than humiliation caused by physical injury, for nothing is as serious as lashon hara and a defamatory report that one spreads about another. He further writes in responsum, klal 101, siman 1, that Jewish communities everywhere instituted protective enactments against abusive speech and fined one who humiliates another according to the identity of the offender and the person humiliated. Beit din should act similarly in every case.
shin) Hagahot Mordechai writes that if one calls another an apostate after he has repented, he violates the cherem of Rabbeinu Gershom. If he did not know of the cherem, however, he is not called a transgressor. The Sema cites this, and it is explained at the end of siman 334. Hagahot Mordechai to Kiddushin writes regarding a man who insulted a woman that he should fast Monday, Thursday, Monday, sit barefoot before the synagogue, and request forgiveness publicly. The Sema cites this. See siman 421, seif 13.
tav) In the name of the great authorities of Ashkenaz, with proof from the end of the second chapter of Bava Metzia 33a, where Rav Chisda asked Rav Huna concerning a student whose teacher needs him, and Rav Huna responded that Rav Chisda did not need him.
a) He writes there that the offender need appease only the person whom he insulted, not that person's mother.
bet) In the name of a responsum of Rabbeinu Avigdor Kohen. He further writes in his name that if one called another 'son of a prostitute,' he may explain his words as meaning 'son of an innkeeper.'
gimel) He likewise writes regarding one who called another 'Canaan' that he cannot explain that he meant the verse 'Canaan, in his hand are deceitful scales,' because then he could likewise explain 'slave' as meaning a Hebrew slave or 'the borrower is servant to the lender.' But regarding a woman about whom a rumor had spread that she committed adultery in Tiberias, and one person shouted 'Tiberias, Tiberias' at one of her sons during a quarrel, this is not considered public humiliation. Nevertheless, he should take ten men and respectfully request forgiveness from him in the synagogue courtyard. The Sema cites this in abbreviated form.
dalet) He concludes that if the insulted person has died, the offender's agent should take two people from the city where he is buried and go to his grave to request forgiveness in the offender's name.
