One who spat at his fellow. See Responsa Rabbi Yosef of the House of Levi, siman 29, concerning these laws.
One who speaks ill of those who dwell in the dust, etc. See Responsa Rabbi Eliyahu ibn Chaim, simanim 50, 93, and 111.
But not regarding other forms of humiliation, etc. See above, siman 87, in the Sema, seif katan 75 and 76. The Sema writes there that this means that in that case they impose a cherem after testimony, but even with humiliation of those who dwell in the dust there is no need to take an oath concerning it. End of his words. The Agudah in Perek HaZahav writes: If a person claims, "You humiliated me with words," or with respect to those who dwell in the dust, and the other denies it, it appears that he is not believed to require an oath, because a heset oath was not instituted for this. However, Sefer Maimoniyot and Sefer HaMitzvot do not appear to hold this way. End of his words. I do not know why he says that Sefer Maimoniyot and Sefer HaMitzvot do not appear this way. If it is because at the beginning of Hilchot To'en VeNit'an it is written that where one claims against his fellow, "You humiliated me," and the other says, "It never happened," then in a place where a monetary penalty is collected he takes a heset oath, here too, if the claim were true, they would penalize him. One can answer that there it is different, because if the matter were true they would impose upon him the fixed monetary payment established by Torah law, unlike here. This is proven by the fact that the Tur as well, at the end of siman 87, rules like Rambam and Semag, yet earlier there he writes that if one claims, "You cursed me," or "You spread an evil report about me," we do not administer an oath, but only impose a cherem concerning it, even though if it were established as true they would penalize him. See Maharshal in Perek HaChovel at length concerning these laws. See also Responsa Rabbi Yosef of Trani, part 4, siman 16, who writes that any expression spoken to one's fellow in a figurative or polished manner, such as calling a group "sons of doves," or saying of one who came to reside from elsewhere and became a judge, "the stranger who is among you shall rise above you," even though that verse refers to a non-Jew, is not considered humiliation. As for the woman who said, "and Rav's belly," that woman was at fault, for she should have identified him with another sign. See there for his proof.
One who calls his fellow a slave. See Responsa Rabbi Moshe Alshich, siman 73, and Responsa Mabit, part 8, second collection of questions, siman 77, page 108, concerning one who called another "son of a maidservant" and was placed under nidui. See further there, siman 125, page 174.
