And beit din has, etc. As explained above in siman 27 seif 2.
And some say, etc. This is also stated in siman 1 seif 6; see what I wrote there.
And some say that they administer lashes, etc. Mordechai writes that they lash him because he violated the full prohibition of verbal oppression, as stated at the end of chapter HaZahav in Bava Metzia. Beit Yosef asks that a prohibition without an action does not receive Torah lashes except for the known exceptions. It is possible that Mordechai means rabbinic disciplinary lashes. In any event, formal Torah lashes are not administered nowadays.
And one who spreads an evil report, etc. This is the Rosh's wording there. See what I wrote at the end of seif 41.
One who says to another, etc. As in the fourth chapter of Taanis, "like a widow, but not actually a widow," and elsewhere, where comparison language does not assert the literal status. So too here it can mean that he behaves falsely like a mamzer, without asserting that he is literally a mamzer. Even where the comparative prefix is absent, speech is not always literal, as at the end of the second chapter of Nedarim where "my children will become mamzerim" is not necessarily a literal legal declaration. Certainly this applies here.
And some disagree, etc. From the beginning of the second chapter of Yoma, where the Gemara asks, "perhaps like a one-year-old," etc., and a frightening vision was shown to him, etc.
One who speaks evil, etc. See what I wrote in Orach Chayyim siman 606 seif 3.
And if they are relatives, etc. The Gemara at the end of Yoma, as stated there in seif 2.
One who calls another, etc. As stated in chapter 7 of Sanhedrin, regarding whom we call wicked, etc.
But if he cannot, etc. There is an even broader rule: even if he claims that his accusation is true, if he cannot prove it he is liable, as indicated in the first chapter of Kiddushin concerning one who tells another to swear that he is not his slave.
