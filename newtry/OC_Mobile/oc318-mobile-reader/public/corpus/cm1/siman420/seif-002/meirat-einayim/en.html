His fellow's Canaanite slave. The Beit Yosef questions this phrase and writes that the same law should apply even to one's own Canaanite slave, because the slave is obligated in mitzvot. For this reason the Mechaber omitted the words "his fellow's." In the Derishah I defended the Tur's wording. It was explained that when a blow causes damage worth a perutah, payment is made and lashes are not given. But payment does not apply when a master injures his own slave, because whatever the slave acquires belongs to the master. If we were to impose lashes upon the master for a blow worth less than a perutah, we would create the strange result that he could deliberately strike more severely, causing damage worth a perutah, and thereby escape both lashes and payment. Therefore the Tur and Mechaber later rule generally that one who injures his own Canaanite slave is exempt. The Rambam writes the same. It is strained to limit that later rule only to injuries worth a perutah or more. See the Derishah here and there.
