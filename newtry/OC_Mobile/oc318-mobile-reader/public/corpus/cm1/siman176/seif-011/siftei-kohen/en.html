Reuven went and released debts belonging to Shimon, etc. The Rama cites the Mordechai in the name of Maharam, but his formulation is imprecise. The decisive distinction is this: if Shimon has evidence that the debts are still outstanding, Reuven is not believed to say, 'I released them with your permission,' by a migo that he could have denied releasing them, because that migo opposes the strong presumption that a person does not destroy his own property without cause. If there is no evidence that the debts remain unpaid, Reuven is believed by that migo, since perhaps the debts had already been paid and Shimon therefore authorized the release. This is explicit in the Agudah, Hagahot Maimoniyot, and Maharshal, and is also implied by a responsum of the Rosh. The Rama himself rules below in siman 382 that one who certainly caused damage is not believed to claim authorization merely by virtue of a migo. If Reuven does not know whether the debts were paid, the absence of witnesses that they remain outstanding allows his migo. But if he admits that they are unpaid, he is liable, since his migo then conflicts with the presumption that no one authorizes gratuitous release of his debts. Maharshal adds that Reuven may demand that Shimon swear he never authorized the release, by analogy to a debtor who may demand an oath from a creditor. This is difficult where liability rests on the strong presumption, because such a presumption can permit collection without an oath, as in a claim of payment before the due date. The better interpretation is that Maharshal refers to a case where witnesses establish that the debts remain unpaid but not that Reuven released them, so Reuven has a migo but loses because of the presumption. Even there it remains arguable that Shimon should collect without an oath. Where there are witnesses that Reuven released the debts but no witnesses that the debtors had not already paid, Maharshal's ruling is persuasive: Reuven may require Shimon to swear that the debts were unpaid and that he never authorized their release, because until that oath the existence of actual damage is uncertain. This appears correct.
He must pay according to what the debts were worth if sold. This is the ruling of Maharam in the Mordechai, the Agudah, and Hagahot Maimoniyot, and Maharshal rules likewise. The Rama did not need to state it expressly because he relied on his ruling above, siman 66:32. The same must be understood below in siman 386:2. See Responsa Maharam Mintz, siman 44.
