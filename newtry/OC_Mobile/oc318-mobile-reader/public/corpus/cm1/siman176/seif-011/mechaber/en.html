Therefore, if one gives money to another as partnership funds to buy wheat for trade, and he instead buys barley, or was told to buy barley and buys wheat, any loss is borne by the one who deviated, while any profit is shared. See below, siman 183, seif 5. Likewise, if he used partnership money to join another partnership, he alone bears any loss, but the profit is shared. If he joined another partnership using his own money, both loss and profit are his alone. If they stipulated otherwise, the stipulation governs. Rama: If Reuven and Shimon were partners and had a claim against a nobleman under whose authority Reuven lived, and Reuven created the debt, Shimon cannot compel Reuven to press the claim when Reuven fears the nobleman. Reuven's refusal is justified. See below, seif 47. If Reuven released debts owed to Shimon and says Shimon instructed him to do so, then if this is true Reuven is exempt even if Shimon did not expressly say that he would be exempt. If Shimon denies it and there is proof that the debts belonged to Shimon and that Reuven released them, Reuven must pay and is not believed to say he acted with permission. If Shimon has no proof, Reuven is believed through a migo, since he could have said the debts were not Shimon's or that he never released them. See seif 50 below.
