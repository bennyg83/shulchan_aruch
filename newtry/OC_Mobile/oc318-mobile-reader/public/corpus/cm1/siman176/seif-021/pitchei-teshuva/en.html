Yoreh Deah, siman 177, seif 35, makes clear that even if one partner says, "I will accept responsibility for your half, and if you lose I will reimburse you," the other need not agree, because the joint fortune of two partners is considered preferable. Tzemach Tzedek, responsum 99, was asked about three partners who bought merchandise on credit for a full year and agreed that all three would conduct the business throughout the year. One wished to sell his share to another while promising to remain fully involved in the work and liability as before. The other two objected, fearing that once he no longer had a financial stake he would become lax. Tzemach Tzedek ruled for them. Since the entire economic interest would belong to the purchaser, there is reason to fear reduced diligence, comparable to the law of joint carriers and sailors. Shaar Mishpat agrees. He adds that if a partner sells only half of his interest while retaining responsibility and active involvement with the whole, it remains doubtful whether the others can object on the ground that a larger retained stake induces greater diligence. If the purchaser will himself enter the partnership and take part in management, however, the existing partners may certainly object, because they may say that they do not wish to multiply opinions and decision makers in their business, which can itself cause loss. Pitchei Teshuva notes that this latter point is already included in the Rama's ruling above that a partner may not join others into the partnership without consent.
