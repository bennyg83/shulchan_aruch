Some say that if partnership property was stolen or lost, the partners divide the loss in proportion to their respective capital. Rama: Reuven had a debt and told Shimon to assist him in collecting it in exchange for one-third of the profit. They did so and afterward purchased merchandise. They are like two people who contributed to a common purse, because Shimon has a share in the profit. This applies only if they afterward purchased the merchandise together with the intention of partnership. If Reuven alone purchased it and Shimon merely assisted him, Shimon has no share in the later profit, because Reuven promised him only a share in collecting the debt, not in subsequent gains. Reuven need pay him only the customary fee for such assistance. If Reuven and Shimon joined to lend to a non-Jew, Reuven advanced his share, and the borrower then said he needed no more, and Reuven asked Shimon to reimburse him for Shimon's share but Shimon delayed and then died, Shimon's heirs nevertheless acquire his share of the profit, because he never waived it. If Shimon expressly said he would not contribute his share, he altered the partnership arrangement and forfeited his share of the profit. See seif 41 below.
