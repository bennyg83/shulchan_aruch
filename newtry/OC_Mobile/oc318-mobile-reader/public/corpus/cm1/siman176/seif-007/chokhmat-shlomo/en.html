Seif 41, and see above seif 7 in the Rama's gloss. Note: I was asked about partners in a house where one partner alone paid for fire insurance. The house later burned, and he refused to share the insurance proceeds with the other partner. This is not comparable to a partner who says, “I am saving for myself,” because there the property or debt itself belonged to the partnership, whereas insurance proceeds are an external gain arising from a separate transaction. Nor does the rule stated here automatically apply, because there the parties first agreed to become partners in the relevant enterprise. Here they never agreed to become partners in the insurance. The ruling therefore depends on the law of the kingdom. If a person may validly insure another person's house and collect the fire payment, the second partner has no share in the proceeds, since the payment derives from the policyholder's independent transaction. But if royal law permits insurance recovery only by virtue of an ownership interest in the insured house, then the payment received by the one partner derives from the joint ownership itself. In that case he cannot trade with the other's property for his sole benefit, and the rule of one who enters with permission applies, so the other partner shares in the proceeds. This appears correct in practice.
