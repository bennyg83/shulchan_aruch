If they were stolen. See Shach, seif katan 106, who asks that this is an explicit Tosefta cited by Ran in the first chapter of Gittin. In my view there is no difficulty. The Tosefta does not discuss partners, but merely two people who placed their money in one purse. Here the case concerns an actual partnership, in which losses are ordinarily borne equally. The exception is a loss occurring while the original contributed property remains intact, which is borne according to the amount each contributed, for the reason Sema gives in seif katan 16. Although that rationale does not itself apply to theft or loss, one might have thought that such a loss should be shared equally. Ramah and Rama teach that nevertheless it is more reasonable to allocate it according to capital so long as the original partnership funds have not changed form.
It is like two people who placed money into one purse. Ketzot HaChoshen writes that according to Shach in siman 122, where one promises an agent one third of the debt he will collect, the promise is ineffective because it is a davar shelo ba la'olam. If so, no share in the principal was transferred here, because the debtor could simply pay the promised third from his own property. Ketzot also asks from siman 332, where one who promises a specific object as wages does not transfer that object. These questions are not difficult. If one says to his fellow, "Buy merchandise with the money you owe me and let it be held in partnership," and the fellow purchases with partnership intent, the sender acquires his share, as explained in siman 183. Even if no money had yet been delivered at all, if one buys property on another's behalf with partnership intent, the principal acquires. Therefore, even if the recipient here did not acquire the particular five gold pieces he received, but only became obligated to pay three gold pieces, once he was told to purchase merchandise for the partnership and did so, the merchandise became partnership property.
