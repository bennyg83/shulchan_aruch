Because of the currency, etc. Baer Heitev explains that the result follows the relative capital contributions. Netivot HaMishpat questions the stated reason. He therefore explains that when one partner draws or takes possession of the other's contribution, each acquires an interest in the other's money proportionate to his capital. Thus any rise or fall in property that remains intact follows their respective capital shares. Equal division applies only where the profit resulted from their joint labor and business activity. Rabbi Moshe Rothenburg, Choshen Mishpat, responsum 18, disagrees. Although the authorities generally say that an increase or decrease in property still in existence follows the capital, that is because ordinarily both partners contribute the same type of currency and the change affects all of it. If one partner contributed a distinct currency and it is known that only that currency rose or fell, the gain or loss belongs to that partner alone. He develops this point at length and also discusses Netivot HaMishpat, seif katan 12.
