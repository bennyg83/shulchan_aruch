A partner may ordinarily withdraw like a worker, even in the middle of the agreed period. Shitah Mekubetzet, citing Ran, limits what this means. If no profit has yet arisen, or the withdrawing partner is willing to forgo the profit, he can say, "I no longer wish to work unless we divide now." Even where the parties fixed a term, he is no worse than an employee who may withdraw in the middle of the day. But once profit has already arisen, the profit is subjected to the principal. It must remain available to cover later losses until the agreed period ends. The partner may stop performing future work, but he cannot demand his share of the profit immediately while refusing to continue the venture. His right in the profit is incomplete until he fulfills the agreed service or the term ends. This resolves the question in Kiddushin concerning a woman betrothed on condition that the man show her one hundred. If he shows her partnership capital, the condition is not satisfied even if he already has accrued profit, because that profit is subjected to the principal. Rama rules accordingly in Even HaEzer. Beit Shmuel asked that a recipient of an iska can withdraw during the term. The answer is that he can withdraw from future work, but he cannot take the accrued profit before completing the agreed service. This is exactly Ran's distinction.
