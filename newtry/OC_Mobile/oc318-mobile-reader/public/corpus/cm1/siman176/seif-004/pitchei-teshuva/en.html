And it is acquired only through a full kinyan. Beit David, Choshen Mishpat, responsum 4, discusses the merchants' practice called "abtrett gelt": when many bidders are standing over merchandise and their competition will greatly reduce the price, one who wishes to buy promises the others a sum of money if they refrain from bidding. He rules that if this promise was made only verbally, without a kinyan and without money deposited with a third party, it is ineffective. It cannot be equated with hiring workers, which requires no kinyan, because wages are normally paid for an act or speech performed by the worker, whereas here payment is promised solely for refraining from action. Even if it is customary to pay for such abstention, a kinyan is required. He brings support from Ketubot 81b. Pitchei Teshuva adds that a kinyan is effective only where the other bidders were themselves entitled to bid without violating another's rights. If their proposed competition involved prohibited encroachment, as discussed in siman 237, even a kinyan may not bind, comparable to the rule concerning an unsuitable yavam where even a document and kinyan do not necessarily allow recovery because the woman may say that she merely misled him. Whether depositing the money with a third party changes the matter requires study.
