Or he released him. See Sema, seif katan 69, who asks from the law of a custodian who entrusts to another custodian. His question is this: just as with a deposit the owner can say, "You are trustworthy to me without an oath; the second custodian is not," so too if Reuven released Shimon from an oath and Shimon then released Levi, Reuven should be able to say that he trusted Shimon without an oath but never trusted Levi. Sema's answer, that Reuven agreed to take a share in the profit, means as follows. The present case necessarily concerns one where there was no loss, for if there were an actual loss, seif 10 already states that the loss caused by the unauthorized act is borne by the actor personally. The only issue is whether Levi earned more profit than he reports. Levi was unwilling to enter the arrangement unless Shimon released him from an oath. Shimon, knowing that Levi could earn substantial profit, joined with him for the benefit of the partnership and granted that release. Since Reuven accepted Shimon's action and seeks his share of the profit Levi earned, the release Shimon gave Levi is effective as part of that transaction. See Taz, whose conclusion is that perhaps Shach agrees with this.
Reuven may still require Levi to swear. See Sema, seif katan 69. At first glance this is difficult. Shach in siman 77, seif katan 19, writes that one partner's waiver of money can be effective. If a partner can waive the underlying money, why should he not be able to waive an oath? Even if Levi had earned much more and Shimon waived the excess, Shach there would treat the waiver as effective. It is possible to distinguish. Where a partner's unauthorized act can be financially repaired, we say that the other partner made him an agent both for benefit and for possible detriment, because the second partner suffers no ultimate loss: the acting partner must reimburse him for any improper waiver, as Shach explains in siman 77. But where one partner releases a third party from an oath, the extent of the resulting loss is unknown and cannot later be repaired. In such a situation the other partner cannot be presumed to have accepted the unauthorized detriment. If the third party did not know that there was another partner, Taz holds that the release is effective, like an agent acting in siman 183. Even there, however, there is room for question. Once it becomes clear that the property belongs to another person, the law may resemble a purchaser from a robber, who can recover only what the law permits.
