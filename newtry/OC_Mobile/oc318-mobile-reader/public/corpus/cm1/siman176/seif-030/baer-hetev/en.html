Or. The Sema explains that the text means specifically either Reuven exempted Shimon or Shimon exempted Levi, and not that both occurred. If Reuven exempted Shimon and Shimon then exempted Levi, Reuven may no longer administer an oath to Levi. This is evident, he says, from the actual words of the Itur cited by the Beit Yosef in this siman, section 24. This is not persuasive. What does Reuven's exemption of Shimon have to do with Levi? Reuven can say to Levi, I never exempted you. Although the wording of the Itur as cited in the Beit Yosef appears to support the Sema, it seems to be a scribal error. The Taz also takes this view. See his discussion there. The Itur itself, under the entry Partnership, page 26, end of column 3, states explicitly: It appears that even if Reuven exempted Shimon from an oath, or Shimon exempted Levi, Reuven may still administer an oath to Levi unless Reuven exempted him and those deriving rights through him. This explicitly accords with my view. As for the Sema's additional question from seif 10, that one who enters an unspecified partnership with another may not bring others into it, see Responsa Raanach, siman 65, where the question is resolved by distinguishing whether the third partner contributed money. Shach. The Taz writes that he does not understand the question at all, for here Shimon enters a partnership with Levi with Reuven's consent, and the present issue concerns only exemption from an oath.
