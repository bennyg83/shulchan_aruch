Or Shimon exempted Levi. The Sema explains that it specifically says "or Shimon exempted Levi," and not "and Shimon exempted Levi." For if Reuven exempted Shimon and Shimon then exempted Levi, Reuven cannot make Levi swear. The Sema claims this is evident from the Baal HaItur cited by the Beit Yosef in this siman. This is not satisfactory. What relevance does Reuven's exemption of Shimon have to Levi? Reuven can say to Levi, "I did not exempt you." Although the wording of the Baal HaItur as quoted by the Beit Yosef appears to support the Sema, it seems to be a scribal error. In the Baal HaItur itself, under the entry Partnership, page 26, end of column 3, it explicitly states: even if Reuven exempted Shimon from an oath, or if Shimon exempted Levi, Reuven may still impose an oath upon Levi unless Reuven exempted both Levi and those deriving rights through him. Thus the text explicitly supports my position. See also the Sema's question from seif 10. Responsa Rabbi Eliyahu ibn Chaim, siman 65, resolves it by distinguishing whether the third partner actually gave money or not.
