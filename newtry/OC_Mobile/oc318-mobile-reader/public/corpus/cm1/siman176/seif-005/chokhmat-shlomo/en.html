Seif 16: They entered a partnership without fixing a term. Note: See Shach, seif katan 29, and compare his words below, seif katan 40. The apparent contradiction can be resolved. Where the partnership has a fixed term, such as property rented or purchased for a period, or a debt originally extended on the understanding that the borrower will repay both partners at a certain time, one partner cannot invoke “gud o agud” during that term, because they consented to remain partners until then. But where no term was fixed for the merchandise, even one hour is not necessarily included. A partner may say, “I agreed to remain your partner only until now.” Therefore, if the other wishes to sell to outsiders or to himself, he may compel him: “Sell to me; and if you say the price is too low, then you purchase it at that price.” This explains why the Shach here writes simply that gud o agud is available. There is no need to force his words into the position of the Mechaber.
