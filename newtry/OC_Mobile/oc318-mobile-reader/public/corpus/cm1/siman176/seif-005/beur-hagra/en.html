The partners who contributed. Kesubos 93.
And they lost value. Rif, and so it is written in the Yerushalmi there. This also appears from the Gemara, which establishes "they lost value" as referring to the deterioration of a coin, etc.
And even. In accordance with Rav Hamnuna.
For slaughter. Ran likewise writes that this is not comparable to an animal intended for plowing, even according to Rif. Otherwise the Gemara's challenge to Rabbah, "let it distinguish within that case itself," would also be difficult according to Rav Hamnuna.
For if they had slaughtered it. Rif writes this, and it also appears from the Gemara's wording, "and it stands for slaughter."
When does this apply. There in the Gemara.
The money remains intact. Rif writes this.
And certainly if. The Gemara there: "this one purchased..."
And they are still intact. Ran writes that even according to Rif and Rambam, if they entered partnership in produce, the profit is divided equally if the produce is no longer extant. Otherwise, what is the Gemara's challenge there from "we learned similarly..."? It could establish that case as one where they partnered in produce. See Ran there, and see the Rosh. Their words likewise indicate this when they state, regarding money, that the money remains intact, as above.
And if they stipulated between themselves. The Yerushalmi there derives this from the case of an ox that successively gored four or five oxen, where each party bears loss in proportion to his money. It answers that oxen are regarded as though the parties had stipulated. See Kesubos there.
