I am saving for myself. Baer Heitev records the dispute whether this declaration must be made before three, the Sema requiring three and the Shach disagreeing. Noda BiYehuda, Choshen Mishpat, responsum 30, section 27, rules in his case that according to the Shach the officer was believed to say that he had declared, "I saved for myself." He adds that even according to the Sema the case was different. There the parties were partners in the very same debt, so one could not divide it without his fellow's knowledge and the objection "who divided it for you?" applied. In Noda BiYehuda's case, however, the officer had merely undertaken to work to collect the others' separate debts with the same effort he used for his own. He never became their partner in the debts themselves. Even if he violated his oath by collecting his own debt first, what he did was effective, and no formal beit din division was required. The Shach and Baer Heitev further cite Rashba that where he received the money privately he is believed to say, "I saved for myself," through a migo that he could have made a different claim. This implies that without a migo he would not be believed merely on oath. Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat, responsum 100, explains the reason; his discussion is cited below in siman 181. The Taz at first appears to imply that even without a migo an oath suffices, but this is not conclusive. The Taz's final statement that a private declaration is effective can be understood only as removing the declarant from an oath obligation in the case discussed in siman 181. The contrary attribution in Misgeret therefore requires study.
