The first. Regarding the distinction made by the Sema, the Shach writes that his words require study. See the end of siman 410. The Taz likewise disagrees with the Sema and adds: I was asked about Reuven, who rented a horse from Shimon for one month, and one day later hired Shimon himself together with all his horses for two months. While they were traveling, the horse originally rented was stolen. I ruled that Reuven is exempt. Although the first rental began when the owner was not in his service, the later new hiring while the owner was in his service supersedes the earlier rental. One cannot object from the present ruling that the first renter is liable because the initial rental began when the owner was not in his service. Here there was an entirely new hiring, since the single horse had originally been rented for only one month. This resembles the rule in the chapter HaShoel, cited by the Tur in siman 346, that if one rents a cow from a woman and then marries her, the later borrowing while the owner is in his service supersedes the earlier rental when the owner was not in his service. The same applies here. Taz.
