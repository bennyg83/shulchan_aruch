Only as an unpaid custodian. The Shach writes that he is not even an unpaid custodian. See Beurim: if each partner handles his own half at home, each is a paid custodian, but the arrangement is custody with the owner present, which exempts him from payment while still requiring the custodian's oath. If one partner merely guards the other's portion gratuitously, without accepting responsibility, he is not even an unpaid custodian and need not swear unless he expressly accepted custody.
Begin to mess with me today and keep your husbands against everyone, but if they work each other in their house and then they will be saved to me tomorrow, then keep them in their hands against each other, but if they work each other in their house and then we will be able to keep them new and each of them at their disposal, and they will not keep their owners as a new attraction:
The first is liable. See Beurim: if they first handled the property jointly and each then took half home to work with, and it was stolen while in both their hands, each owes the other one quarter of the entire amount. If they worked jointly in the shared shop, neither owes the other because each was guarding together with his fellow. If after joint work each later handled the property alone, the partner from whose hand it was stolen must pay the other. If it was stolen from the second partner under circumstances where the second's custodial period began while the first was working with him, the second is exempt because it was custody with the owner present. The Taz explains a rotating monthly arrangement in detail: where Reuven alone guarded in Nisan, and at the beginning of Shimon's month in Iyar Shimon hired Reuven to work with him, Shimon is exempt because his custody began while the owner was present. If at the beginning of Reuven's next month Shimon was hired to work with Reuven and the item was then stolen, Reuven must pay Shimon one quarter of the total. The same pattern continues in later months according to whose custodial period began with the other partner present. If initially each guarded alone during his own month, so that neither had custody with the owner present at the beginning, and only afterward one hired the other to help during his month, the partner whose month it was remains liable according to the original custodial undertaking. Similar rules apply where one asks the other to help gratuitously: negligence can create liability, while ordinary theft follows the paid custodian status where applicable. See Beurim for the reasoning.
They divided the partnership. See Beurim: this concerns a case where the original custody did not begin with the owner present, or where there was a new act of transfer creating a fresh custody, such as one partner taking the property home from the shared store. With money, he is a paid custodian because he may use loose funds, like money deposited with a money changer. With an object, the same can apply where he alone is the litigant against the gentile and takes the money for himself because he does not trust the other partner. Alternatively, it concerns property left with the partner who was obligated to guard during that month.
