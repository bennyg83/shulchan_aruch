They are paid bailees. Each partner works with and guards his fellow's share in exchange for the other's reciprocal work and guarding. For this reason the rule applies specifically where they made such an arrangement at the outset, as Rama writes in the gloss, because then each effectively hired the other at the beginning of the partnership.
Even if afterward they worked together, etc. The Torah's exemption of a bailee when the owner is 'with him in his work' depends on the owner being with him at the beginning of the bailment, even if he is not present at the time of theft. If the owner was not with him when the guarding began, then even if he is working with him when the item is stolen or lost through unavoidable circumstances, the bailee is not exempt but bears his normal liability. See the end of siman 291 and the Perishah.
If at first one alone worked with it. This is the correct reading, unlike the Tur's printed wording, 'if at first each one worked with it alone.' If each had separately handled it, both would have equal liability or exemption once they later worked together. See the Perishah, where I reconcile the Tur's wording.
The first is liable, etc. In the Perishah I wrote that if the theft occurred while both were working together, the first partner is liable only for one quarter of the total value. At that time the duty of guarding was divided equally between them. The second is exempt from theft regarding the half entrusted to his guarding because the first owner was then working with him. But the first partner was responsible only for guarding half the property. Half of that half was his own and the other half, one quarter of the total, belonged to his fellow, which he must pay. If, after working together, they separated and the first again guarded alone and it was stolen from him, he certainly must pay his fellow's full half. If it was stolen while the second later guarded alone, he is entirely exempt toward his fellow. See further in the Perishah.
