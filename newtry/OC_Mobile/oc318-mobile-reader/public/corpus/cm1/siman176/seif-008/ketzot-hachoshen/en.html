A partnership agreement may create the law of guarding while the owner is simultaneously "with him." A case came before us involving partners in a business who agreed at the beginning that each would manage the enterprise in alternating months. Partnership money was stolen while in one partner's hands during his assigned month. At the beginning of the partnership the other partner was working with him, but that period of common service had ended before the particular money came into his possession. The question was whether we look to the later moment when the specific funds were physically delivered, in which case the owner was no longer with him, or to the initial assumption of the custodial obligation, when the guarding relationship began with the owner present. Maharit indicates that such a case is guarding with the owner and the custodian is exempt. He explains Raavad's view that a wife who breaks household utensils while performing domestic work can be exempt because the husband is considered hired to her at the moment the household guarding obligation arose, namely at marriage. Even if his immediate service later ended, the original custodial undertaking began while he was with her. The same remains true even for objects that were not yet physically present at the time, because her obligation to guard them arises from the marital framework rather than from each later delivery. So too with partners who from the outset accepted responsibility to manage the partnership in alternating periods. The custodial obligation began with the partnership itself. If at that initial moment the owner was "with him," the later arrival of particular partnership funds does not create a new independent custodial undertaking. The exemption of guarding with the owner therefore applies.
