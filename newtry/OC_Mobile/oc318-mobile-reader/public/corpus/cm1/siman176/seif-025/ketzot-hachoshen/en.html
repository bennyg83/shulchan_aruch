Ritva writes that where one partner is a woman or minor, they can be treated like a partner who is absent from the city for litigation purposes. He nevertheless says a husband litigating concerning his wife's property ordinarily needs no authorization because his hand is like hers and he can sue even against her will. Rashba and Ran, however, appear to treat a wife like any other present partner. They explain Abaye's rule that a husband needs authorization when there are no fruits in the land, while if there are fruits he can litigate because, through his fruit right, he is effectively a partner. Yet even then they limit his power over the land itself to a case where the wife is in the city and does not object. If she is absent, or present and protesting, he cannot litigate the body of the land merely through the fruit right. See what I wrote in siman 122.
