A debt owed by others. It appears that this refers to debts whose due date has already passed. Such debts resemble merchandise with no fixed time for sale, which the partners may divide whenever they wish. But debts whose due date has not yet arrived resemble merchandise whose sale has a known time, as in seif 17, and cannot be divided until maturity. One partner can say, "The labor and fortune of two is better." Since they knowingly extended the debt beyond the original partnership term, it is as though they extended the time for division of that receivable itself. This resembles Taz's rule in this seif. See Sema, seif katan 52. There is no distinction between oral and written debts. Although siman 173, seif 2, in the gloss, holds that division between partners requires a kinyan, and an oral debt cannot ordinarily be transferred by kinyan except through ma'amad sheloshtan, an allocation made through appraisal of beit din does not require a kinyan, as Shach writes in siman 101, seif katan 3.
