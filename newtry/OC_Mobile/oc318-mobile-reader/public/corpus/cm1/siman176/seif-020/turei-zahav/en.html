Seif 28: I am saving for myself. I do not understand the Sema's limitation. Since the partner faces a loss, he may announce that he will rescue for himself whatever he can, whether money or merchandise, while the other partner may exert himself regarding his own share. There is no concern of theft. This differs from seif 18, where assets presently available must be divided and an improper division can constitute theft, so beit din is required. Here only a declaration is needed that from this day onward he no longer acts as a partner. Beit din is unnecessary, and witnesses suffice. Indeed, the Mordechai, cited by the Rama in siman 181, says that one who claims he declared, “I am saving for myself,” but has no witnesses, may swear that he intended to save only for himself and then keeps what he saved. This shows that even witnesses are not indispensable. The declaration is effective even privately, subject only to the need to avoid the oath. A responsum of Mahari Weil likewise states explicitly that such a declaration is effective without beit din.
