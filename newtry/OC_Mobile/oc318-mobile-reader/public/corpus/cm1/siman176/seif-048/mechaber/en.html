If two partners bought silk and, when they came to sell it, its weight was deficient, the deficiency is part of the partnership loss and is divided between them. Shimon must swear that he did not use it for himself and was not negligent in guarding it. If one partner carried partnership merchandise elsewhere to sell, was captured, and the other redeemed him from partnership funds, and the partnership then continued without their keeping strict accounts with one another, upon the redeemed partner's death his heirs first take from the common fund an amount equal to the redemption payment. Rama: A partner is not obligated to redeem his fellow partner even if the capture resulted from partnership business. Any established custom governs. If one received an investment and the ruler seized him together with other Jews, forcing him to use investment funds for his ransom, then if local practice is to assess such payments against business capital, it is treated like other taxes and may be paid from the investment. This concerns partners. When one person sends another on his business and the messenger is captured, some say that if he went without compensation the sender must redeem him, because the sender has the liability of a borrower, who is responsible even for unavoidable accidents. Others disagree. Likewise, if one summoned another and undertook to protect him from every loss, but he was captured on the road, the sender is nevertheless exempt from redeeming him, because this is an uncommon accident.
