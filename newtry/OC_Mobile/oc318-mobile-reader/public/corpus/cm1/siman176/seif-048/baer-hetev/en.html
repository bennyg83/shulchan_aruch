Included. The main novelty in the responsum is where one promised to share profit and loss with his fellow, teaching that this case is included. But it is obvious that if one partner bought silk for the partnership and it lost weight, the loss is his alone, because partners have the status of paid bailees. Taz.
Who was seized. See Responsa R. Aharon Sasson, siman 76, chapters 4 and 5; the Responsa Rashach associated with part 2, siman 8; Responsa Maharashdam, siman 181; and Responsa Rama, siman 46.
They divide. See Maharik there, who distinguishes between an unrelated person and one's son who acts as an agent, whom one is obligated to redeem. See also the end of siman 188, where the ruling is stated without qualification according to the dissenting opinion, and where I explained the basis of the distinction. Thus writes the Sema. The Taz writes that the halachah follows the dissenting opinion. He further records a case of two people who bought a house in partnership and stipulated that whenever one wished to withdraw, he would be paid the value of his share. The house later increased in value. At first glance one might think he need not be paid according to its current value, as stated in Orach Chaim siman 154 regarding a Sefer Torah. In truth, however, the cases are not comparable, and he must be paid the full current value of his share, as I proved in my commentary to Orach Chaim there.
