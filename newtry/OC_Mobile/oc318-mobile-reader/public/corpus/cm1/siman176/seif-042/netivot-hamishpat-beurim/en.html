Because if it had been lost through ones he would have been exempt from Shimon. See above in siman 388, seif 4, where Rama explains the distinction so that the laws do not conflict. See Shach there, seif katan 28, who writes that if one partner had a jointly owned pledge and handed it to the nobleman, that is considered actively delivering the property and he is liable. This must not mean that the pledge was in his possession with the other partner's consent as an ordinary entrusted partnership asset. In that case, if a coercive official took it, the holder would be exempt, as explained in siman 292 regarding a custodian who physically hands over property under coercion. See Sema and Shach there. Rather, the case is one where the partner originally took the pledge into his own house and refused to give it to the other partner. He never became a lawful custodian on behalf of the other partner. When he then hands it to the nobleman, he cannot rely on the ordinary custodian's exemption.
