He can annul the division, etc. The Tur writes: If one partner divided the partnership property without the other's knowledge and then there was profit or loss, everything remains jointly shared as though no division had taken place. Some say that any loss is his alone, because his fellow cannot challenge the division while he himself cannot challenge and annul a division that he made. The Tur rejects this, because he divided only on the assumption that the division was legally valid, and since it was not valid he may annul it. The Beit Yosef agreed with the first view, reasoning that this is like a partner who deviates from the agreed method of business, where if the deviation causes a loss he bears it himself, while any profit is shared. In the Derishah I explained that the cases are not comparable. There the loss arose because of the deviation. Here, even had he not divided, the loss would have occurred, because he did not change the manner in which the merchandise was handled. Indeed, the author of the Shulchan Aruch himself appears to have retracted what he wrote in the Beit Yosef, for here he rules simply that the division has no effect. This implies both for profit and for loss, so any loss remains jointly shared as though no division occurred.
