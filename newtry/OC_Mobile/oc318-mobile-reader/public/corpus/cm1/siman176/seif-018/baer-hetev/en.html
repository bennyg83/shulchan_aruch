Laymen. The Sema explains that even all three may be complete laymen. It does not mean what the Tur and Shulchan Aruch state in siman 3, that among any three there is generally one who is learned and competent, for if so, what novelty would there be here? An ordinary beit din is likewise constituted. The Ran writes similarly. The Shach adds that if there are promissory notes, the beit din divides them by appraisal. This is evident from what the Beit Yosef writes in section 17, after citing the Rashba's responsum, that the Rosh disagrees, while the Rashba concedes in practice. See there. See also Responsa Maharashdam, siman 165.
