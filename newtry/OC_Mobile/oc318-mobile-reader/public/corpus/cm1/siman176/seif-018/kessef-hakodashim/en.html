Seif 22: One who gives money to another. Where people send money to an agent to purchase merchandise for them, to be brought together with merchandise that the agent intends to bring for himself, and the agent becomes ill and cannot travel, they may take back their money even if it appears likely that he will recover soon. This applies even though they fixed a wage for him for bringing the merchandise. It is comparable to a borrower concerning whom it is subsequently reported that he is dissipating his assets: even though he acquired the money, it may be taken from him. If he recovers shortly afterward, then so long as the senders have not sent the money through someone else, they must give it back to the original agent. If there was a particular reason why they did not send it through another person, and absent that reason they would have hurried to send it, it may be said that the original agency was a mistaken arrangement from the outset. Likewise, if his recovery occurred in an unusual manner, one may say that they would not have entrusted him with the money had they known what would happen. On the other hand, since their assessment concerning the money necessarily included the possibility of his recovery, perhaps the arrangement should not be regarded as mistaken.
