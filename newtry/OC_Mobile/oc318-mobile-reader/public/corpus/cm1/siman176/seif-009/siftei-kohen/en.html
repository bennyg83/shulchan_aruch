And with respect to the eldest brother, even property he acquired for himself is not appraised. It appears that even his Shabbat garments are not appraised. Regarding the sons and daughters, the Rosh explains that ordinary garments are not appraised because they are embarrassed to bring them before beit din; therefore Shabbat garments, which they can bring, are appraised. The eldest brother is different. His garments are not appraised because the heirs benefit from his appearing dignified so that his words will be heeded in business dealings. This reason applies equally to fine Shabbat garments. The Rambam, however, implies that Shabbat garments are appraised because owners do not ordinarily intend to transfer ownership of such distinguished clothing, as the Maggid Mishneh explains. According to that reason, even the eldest brother's Shabbat garments should be appraised. The contrary opinion in siman 97:26, that even Shabbat garments are not appraised, rests on the creditor's lack of a lien over garments already acquired for the wife, a reason inapplicable to the eldest brother. The matter therefore requires study. See Responsa Rabbi Betzalel, siman 38.
