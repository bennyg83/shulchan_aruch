Shabbat. Because the garments can be sent to beit din for appraisal. They are like the partners' own garments, which, when still extant, are appraised for purposes of division. Sema.
And the eldest. The Shach writes that it appears that with respect to the eldest brother, even Shabbat garments are not appraised. The reason is that the brothers are pleased that his words should carry authority, and this applies to Shabbat garments too, because wearing fine Shabbat clothing helps him command respect when dealing with merchants. However, from the Rambam in chapter 1 of Hilchot Malveh it appears that Shabbat garments are appraised because owners do not contemplate transferring ownership of such distinguished garments, as the Maggid Mishneh explains. The Shulchan Aruch rules likewise in siman 97, seif 26. Even according to the dissenting view cited by the Rama there that Shabbat garments are not appraised, that is specifically there, for the reason given that once the wife acquired them when purchased, the creditor has no lien upon them. That reason does not apply to the eldest brother. If so, even for the eldest brother they should be appraised. This requires study. See Responsa R. Betzalel, siman 38. See also Responsa Rashach, part 1, siman 139; Responsa Maharam Mintz, siman 99; Responsa Maharashdam, siman 127; Lechem Rav, siman 109; and Responsa R. Aharon Sasson, simanim 134 and 135.
The custom. Whenever the prevailing custom is otherwise, it is as though they stipulated with one another from the outset and relied on that custom. Sema.
