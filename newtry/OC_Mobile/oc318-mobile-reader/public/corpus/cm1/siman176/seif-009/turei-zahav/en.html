There: The first is liable. The Sema writes that if the property was stolen while both partners were occupied together, the first partner is liable for only one quarter. I am astonished that such words were written. Since responsibility for guarding rested equally on both, the first partner's exclusive responsibility during the period when he worked alone ended when the other joined him. He never accepted sole responsibility beyond that period. Thereafter each accepted only half the guarding obligation, and the first should not bear more than half the loss. Consider jointly owned property of an adult and a minor that both guard. If it is lost under circumstances creating ordinary liability, the adult certainly does not reimburse the minor from his own funds merely because the minor is not legally liable for guarding. Likewise, the Gemara teaches that where an item was first rented without its owner present and later rented anew with its owner present, the later rental with the owner terminates the earlier status even without a new act of drawing. So too, once joint guarding begins, the prior sole guarding ends. The straightforward rule is therefore this: if theft occurs while both work together, they bear the loss equally, because neither accepted responsibility for the other's share at that time. If it occurs while the first alone is working, he pays the other partner's entire half. If it occurs while the second alone is working, the first bears no liability. The Beit Yosef cites Maharam in the Mordechai that even without an express agreement, once one partner begins guarding alone he is a paid guardian, because the other can say, “I intended to guard later as well.” The Mordechai, his student, disagrees. The halachah does not follow a student against his teacher. I was asked about Reuven, who rented one horse from Shimon for a month and, after one day, hired Shimon himself together with all his horses for two months. While traveling, the first horse was stolen. I ruled that Reuven was exempt. Although the initial rental was without the owner present, the later arrangement was a new rental with the owner present and displaced the earlier status. This differs from the present partnership case, where the original guarding continues. It resembles the law of a cow rented from a woman with its owner present, where the renter later marries her and the new status displaces the earlier rental.
