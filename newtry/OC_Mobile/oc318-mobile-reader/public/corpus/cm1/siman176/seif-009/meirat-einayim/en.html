Brothers who are partners. Meaning, brothers who jointly own the estate their father left. The same applies if they formed a partnership with their own money, or to other partners, as Rama writes in the gloss.
But if they were completely worn out, etc. Chazal assessed that in such a case the partners waived claims against one another because they neither protested nor made a contrary stipulation at the outset.
They are not appraised. Since wives and children do not ordinarily come to beit din to have the clothes they are wearing appraised, the partners are presumed to waive these garments. Shabbos clothing, however, can be sent to beit din for appraisal and is treated like the partners' own clothing: if still in existence at the time of division, it is appraised and divided.
The eldest of the brothers, etc. Meaning, the senior brother who conducts business with the inherited property. We presume the others are content that he dress respectably so that his words will be heeded while conducting their affairs. Therefore they fully waive the value even of garments still in existence when they divide.
We follow the custom in this. Wherever local custom is otherwise, it is as though the partners expressly stipulated according to that custom from the beginning. Rama's statement means that in matters such as this that depend on assessment of ordinary intent, custom governs, unlike the presentation in Ir Shushan.
