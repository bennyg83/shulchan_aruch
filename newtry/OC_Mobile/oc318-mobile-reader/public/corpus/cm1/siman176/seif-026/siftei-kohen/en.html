When is this said, etc., where one has a partner. A person had a partner overseas. Reuven said to him: Write to your partner that he should purchase a certain item for me and send it together with your merchandise. He did so, but the ship sank. See what Bach writes in the name of a responsum of Rav Hai, also cited in Responsa Rashdam, siman 106. Liability rests upon Reuven only if he expressly said, 'The risk is mine.' If he did not mention this, he is exempt from payment. See there. See also Responsa Rashdam, simanim 153 and 162.
So that the partner did not become liable. Regarding what Rama writes in seif 25, in the gloss, that when they were defendants one does not prejudice the other at all, it appears to me that the case there is different, because they were defendants from the outset. Here, by contrast, the partner was initially the plaintiff, and the defendant then asserted a counterclaim against him in that same proceeding. This distinction requires study, for it appears at first glance that the Mordechai disagrees with the Rosh. Examine this carefully. This too was written in my youth.
