By words alone. Radbaz, I, 326, indicates that a partner may likewise be removed entirely from the partnership by a verbal waiver alone. He was asked about Reuven and Shimon, who entered a binding partnership for a fixed term and stipulated, under oath, that even after the term if one wished to renew the partnership the other could not object. During the term Reuven told Shimon, "Come, let us divide, for I no longer want the partnership." Shimon agreed and they began dividing. When Reuven saw that there was profit he attempted to retract. Radbaz ruled for Shimon. Once Reuven said that he no longer wanted the partnership, both the partnership and the oath were waived, and a waiver requires no kinyan. His argument that he wished to create a new partnership under the renewal clause failed, because the statement "I no longer want the partnership" waived the entire arrangement, old and new. Radbaz elsewhere clarifies that this applies to a commercial partnership in which property has been bound to the partnership. It does not necessarily apply to craftsmen whose partnership consists in their future labor, as discussed above. Maharam Alshich, responsum 80, at first appears otherwise. Two business partners were involved in a purchase made by one of them after a relative urged him to buy and gave money for a share. The relative later withdrew. The purchaser responded mildly that if he would take his share, good, and if not, Heaven would have mercy and he would not summon him to court. When they returned home, the purchaser's partner insisted that the relative accept his share. Maharam Alshich held that even an explicit waiver would not necessarily release the relative from taking his share in merchandise already acquired, because waiver applies to a debt, not to a tangible object belonging to another. Pitchei Teshuva explains that the two responsa need not conflict. Radbaz may agree that where existing merchandise already belongs to the partnership, the word "waiver" cannot divest a partner of title to his portion of that object, just as the Rama rules in siman 241. Radbaz's point is that the partnership itself, prospectively and apart from existing goods, can be terminated verbally. Maharam Alshich can agree to that. Where there is a written partnership instrument still held by the waiving party, however, the issue depends on the dispute in siman 241 whether a waiver is effective while the claimant still holds a document. The Shach treats that as a doubtful question of law. Radbaz's own language appears to assume that even a written debt may be waived orally, but for practical ruling the matter remains doubtful under the Shach. Mayim Chayim discusses a parallel case in which Reuven publicly waived a written partnership while still holding the instrument. One authority argued that waiver is ineffective where the document remains in the claimant's possession. Other leading authorities, however, including the Taz, Sema, and Beit Shmuel, hold that even one who retains the document may effectively waive. At the least, according to the Shach, the matter is a doubt of law rather than a certain invalidity of the waiver. See also above, siman 12, seif 8.
