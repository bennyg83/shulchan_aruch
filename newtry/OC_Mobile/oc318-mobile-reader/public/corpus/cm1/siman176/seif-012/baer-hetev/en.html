To divide. The Shach challenges the Rama and writes that the primary ruling follows the Baal HaIttur, who disagrees and understands the Yerushalmi, from which this rule is derived, as a rhetorical question. This appears to be the correct reading of the Yerushalmi in Bava Batra, chapter Mi SheMet. It also appears to be the Beit Yosef's view, and is stated plainly in Responsa Ramban, siman 21, cited by the Beit Yosef in siman 176, seif 2. Moreover, the Yerushalmi explicitly compares a found object with theft and robbery. Since with respect to a found object we rule that it belongs to the finder himself, as stated in siman 62, the same should apply here. This requires study. I heard from a great authority that, in any event, one should not rule in practice like the Rama here, because the law is fundamentally unreasonable: why should a partner who steals or robs be required to share the proceeds with his partner? Even the dissenting opinion in the Hagahot Mordechai and Hagahot Maimoniyot speaks only where the theft was committed under circumstances involving danger. See Responsa Maharam Alshakar, siman 172, and Responsa Maharashdam, siman 168. Shach. See also what the Taz writes on this matter.
