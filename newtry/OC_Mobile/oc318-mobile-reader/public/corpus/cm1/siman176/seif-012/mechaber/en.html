If one partner, or a person managing the partnership business, traded in non-kosher carcasses, tereifot, or similar prohibited items, the profit is shared, but if he incurred a loss, he bears it alone. Rama: Likewise, if a partner stole or robbed, he must share the gain with his partner. Hagahot Mordechai, chapter Chezkas, and Hagahot Maimoniyot, chapter 5 of Hilchot Shutfim. If a loss follows, he bears it alone, but only if the loss occurred before his partner learned of the act. If he divided the stolen proceeds with the partner and later an accusation or governmental claim arose because of it, or if he purchased stolen property, shared it with his partner, and a claim later arose, the loss is also shared, since the partner accepted the conduct. No kinyan is required for this acceptance, as explained. Binyamin Ze'ev, siman 360. If, however, they divided ordinary profits and an unjust claim later arose against one partner, the other need not share that loss. Rosh, responsa, klal 79, siman 6. See seif 38 of this siman and the end of siman 177. If one hires another to conduct his business on the condition that all found property that comes into the agent's hands belongs to the employer, and the agent collects debts from non-Jews that had already been paid, those collections are included in "found property," because a paid document is worth no more than its paper. Therefore, if the agent returns such a document to the non-Jew, he need not reimburse his employer, and he may even do so initially. Responsa Rashba 1014 and Ritva.
