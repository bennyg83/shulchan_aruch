One who hired his fellow. The wording implies that the worker acquires the found item specifically because the employer stipulated that all finds should belong to him. Taz likewise implies that an express stipulation is needed, and Rashba's responsum says the same. At first glance this is difficult, because an ordinary worker's found object belongs to the worker himself, as explained in siman 270. The answer is that if a person hires a worker for every task he needs performed in a certain field and then directs him to gather found objects in that field, the finds belong to the employer, as explained in siman 270. This is because the employer effectively showed him the particular items to collect. Here, the employer delivered paid-off documents to the worker, plainly in order for him to attempt collection. This resembles directing him toward the found object. Ordinarily the benefit would therefore belong to the employer. But because the employer expressly stipulated that every find arising from this particular enterprise would belong to the worker, and collection on a paid document is itself a kind of unexpected find, it falls within that stipulation and belongs entirely to the worker.
