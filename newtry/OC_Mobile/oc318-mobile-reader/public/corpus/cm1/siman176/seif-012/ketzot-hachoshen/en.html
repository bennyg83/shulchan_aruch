Rashba was asked about partners who stipulated that whatever they earned through their bodies and capital would belong to the shop, except for money coming from another source. He ruled that a found object is not included. The phrase "through their bodies" means through the labor and effort of their bodies, not every benefit that happens to come to them personally. Rashba proves this from family law. A daughter's earnings belong to her father, and a wife's earnings belong to her husband, but their found objects do not belong to them by strict law. Those rights exist only by rabbinic enactment, for family peace and to prevent concealment. Likewise, a gift given by another belongs to the daughter or wife herself, not to the father or husband, though the husband may receive fruits from his wife's melog property. This responsum appears not to have been before Chelkat Mechokek and Beit Shmuel when they questioned whether a father acquires a gift given to his daughter. Rashba states clearly that he does not. I nevertheless cited elsewhere a contrary indication from the Shulchan Aruch and addressed the issue there.
