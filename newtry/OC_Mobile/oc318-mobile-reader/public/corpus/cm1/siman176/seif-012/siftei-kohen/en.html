Likewise, if the partner stole or robbed, etc. This ruling does not appear correct. The Rama understood the Hagahot Maimoniyot and Hagahot Mordechai as ruling that when two people went to market and one stole a purse, they divide it. Careful examination shows, however, that the Mordechai's opening language is the same as that cited in Hagahot Maimoniyot, but the Hagahot Mordechai then cites the Ittur and plainly concludes like the Ittur, who disagrees and reads the Yerushalmi as a rhetorical question: does a partner who finds a lost item, steals, or robs share it? This is the principal reading of the Yerushalmi in Bava Batra. The Beit Yosef, the Ramban in responsum 21, and the law below in siman 177:2 all support it. The Yerushalmi expressly equates finding a lost item with stealing or robbing, and since a found item belongs to the finder, the same applies here. Moreover, a great authority told me that one should not rule practically like the Rama's gloss because the law is inherently implausible. Even the contrary view in the glosses may concern only a theft committed under circumstances involving shared danger. See Responsa Rabbi Aharon Sasson, siman 172, and Responsa Maharashdam, siman 168.
