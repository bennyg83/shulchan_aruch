With. The Taz writes that the text should read: with the non-Jew.
A Jew. The Shach writes that the Rama's words are astonishing. It is not reasonable that one Jew may seize property for another, since the resulting loss will also fall upon the second partner. How can he seize and thereby cause loss to his fellow who owes him nothing? See there at length. He concludes: It therefore appears to me that the Rama speaks where he seized an item that was not known to belong to the partnership, and he says, 'I do not believe you that it belongs to the partnership.' The partners themselves acknowledge to one another that it does belong to the partnership, while he claims that he owes the other nothing. In that case the law is as the Rama writes. See Responsa Maharashdam, siman 167. This requires study.
Must. Because an ordinary non-Jew is presumed to lie, and one cannot impose a shevuat hesset on the basis of a doubtful claim supported only by the non-Jew, since hesset is administered only on a definite claim. See above, siman 75, seif 25, and siman 149, seif 18. Sema.
