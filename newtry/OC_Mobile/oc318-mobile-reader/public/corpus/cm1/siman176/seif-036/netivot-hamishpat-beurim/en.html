The same applies if he was. See Shach, seif katan 48, who questions this. However, according to what I wrote in siman 58, one cannot seize from the house or domain of the partner who is not personally liable, but one may seize from the house of the partner who is liable. See there, where I brought a proof from Bekhorot 48a. Accordingly there is no difficulty, for the case can be one where he seized from the liable partner's house.
Except that he must swear. This is difficult, because the claim is uncertain, and an interested party cannot impose an oath based on an uncertain claim, as explained in siman 75. It appears that according to what I wrote above, the case is one where he seized from the house of the person who owed him. It is therefore as though partnership money certainly came into his possession and he does not know how it left his possession. In such a case he must swear even in response to an uncertain claim, as the Shach writes in siman 121, seif katan 46. However, the statement that when the matter concerns a gentile he need not swear remains difficult, because partners may impose an oath upon one another even on an uncertain claim. It must therefore be speaking where the other partner is not seeking to administer the partnership oath, but only an oath based on a definite claim. Concerning an oath based on a gentile's statement, see Shach, siman 149, seif katan 13.
