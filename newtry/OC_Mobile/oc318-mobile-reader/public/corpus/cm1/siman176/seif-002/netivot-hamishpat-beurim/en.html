Or they rented a place jointly. Ketzot HaChoshen asks that in a jointly owned courtyard partners do not acquire from one another merely by placing an object there, unless one measures and places it into his own receptacle. He answers that acquisition of a half interest differs from acquisition of the whole. This distinction is difficult. There is no reason why buying half should differ from buying all. The correct distinction is between an ordinary jointly owned courtyard, in which each partner has the right to place his own property, and a place rented specifically for partnership property alone. Ran in Nedarim explains that a person prohibited from benefit may nevertheless enter a jointly owned courtyard because of bereirah: whenever he uses a particular spot, that spot is treated as his for the moment. For the same reason, when Reuven places his own object in an ordinary joint courtyard, the particular place occupied by the object is treated as Reuven's own area, not Shimon's courtyard, and therefore cannot acquire the object for Shimon. This resembles the rule that a person's four cubits do not acquire a sale object in an alley once the seller's vessel came first and made the place effectively his own. But if the partners rented a place on the condition that it be used only for partnership assets and not for either partner's private goods, then when one partner improperly places his own separate object there, bereirah does not make that spot exclusively his, because he has no right to use the place for private property at all. The area remains jointly possessed and can therefore acquire the object for both partners by the law that one's courtyard acquires for him. It is analogous to acquisition from a stranger through a shared courtyard precisely because the placing partner had no independent right to put his private item there. The same reasoning explains siman 157 concerning a wall raised above four cubits. Once one partner reveals that he wishes to acquire the wall, Nimukei Yosef says the shared courtyard acquires it for him. Although the wall stands in a joint domain, the other partner had no right to build above four cubits against his fellow's objection. In a place where one partner lacks the right to act independently, the other partner's courtyard right can operate. Likewise, in siman 207 concerning gaming on a board owned by both, the board can acquire the money because it is dedicated to the joint activity. Machaneh Ephraim distinguishes instead between an item already resting in the shared place and one brought from outside and deposited there, because the latter involves a change of domain. The Gemara in Bava Batra 84 and 85 does not support that distinction. The primary explanation is therefore the one stated above.
