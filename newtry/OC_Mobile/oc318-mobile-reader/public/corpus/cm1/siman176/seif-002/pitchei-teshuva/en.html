And some say that they can effect acquisition, etc. Netivot HaMishpat rules that this is the principal view: with a kinyan, even rights in future profits become binding and a partner cannot withdraw; profits already earned are binding even without a kinyan. Radbaz, I, 350, was asked about two craftsmen who became partners in their labor and made a kinyan. Reuven later told Shimon, "I waive the partnership for you," Shimon accepted, but no new kinyan was made; Reuven then regretted it. Shimon argued that waiver requires no kinyan. Radbaz ruled for Reuven. Waiver without kinyan applies to an existing monetary claim, or to a monetary partnership in which capital has been placed into a common fund and the property itself is bound to the partnership. A partnership in future craftsmanship, however, is sustained through the law of hired labor, for a person cannot convey something not yet in existence. Their future earnings are therefore not acquired as property but their persons are bound as workers. Radbaz compared this to a Hebrew servant, whose person is acquired and who is not released merely by a verbal statement. Therefore a verbal waiver alone does not remove the obligation unless a kinyan is made. Pitchei Teshuva notes that Radbaz followed Maharam, cited in Rashba and Mordechai, that a worker's personal obligation is not waived by words alone. Yet the Rama at the end of siman 333 rules otherwise, that even if an employer merely tells the worker to leave, he is released, and the Shach there concludes that this is the principal ruling and that Maharam relied on the contrary position only as an additional consideration. Radbaz himself also rules this way elsewhere. Likewise, Radbaz's statement that a worker may withdraw midway only where no kinyan was made follows the Ritva in the name of his teachers, but the Shach disputes that and rules that even after a kinyan a worker may withdraw. In the present case of craftsmen who became partners, the ordinary doctrine of a worker withdrawing does not apply in any event, because, as the Sema explains, where both are obligated to work neither bears the status of a servant to the other. The practical question whether a mere verbal waiver terminates this partnership therefore remains in need of study. See also below, seif 10, seif katan 12.
