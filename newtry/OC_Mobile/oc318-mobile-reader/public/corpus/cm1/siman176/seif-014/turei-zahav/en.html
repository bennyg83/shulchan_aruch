(Seif 12, in the gloss: if he stole, etc.) This refers back to the preceding rule concerning partners in a general partnership, including found property. If they are partners only in a specific enterprise, however, the rule does not apply. In the Sema's edition of the Shulchan Aruch, the gloss first reads, "and similarly if he stole," etc., through the end of the gloss, and only afterward begins the gloss, "one whom they hired," etc. According to that arrangement, theft would belong to the partnership even without any prior stipulation. The Beit Yosef, in his novellot section 35, cites Baal HaIttur as holding that found property and theft belong to the individual partner, and it is surprising that the Rama did not cite that view. It appears that as a matter of law one certainly cannot extract money in such a case, particularly against Baal HaIttur. See siman 177. There is another reason. In Perek HaShoel the Gemara says that a person is pleased to acquire permitted gain but not forbidden gain. Therefore, even if the partners expressly wrote that found property too would belong to the partnership, theft is not included. Theft from a non-Jew is prohibited, so we presume that a person did not wish to acquire such prohibited profit. Even if he is now pleased with it, we leave him in his prior presumption and say that only now has his conduct deteriorated.
