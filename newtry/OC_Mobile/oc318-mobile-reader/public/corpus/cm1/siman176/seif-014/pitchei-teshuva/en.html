If they stipulated between themselves. See Baer Heitev. Mayim Chayim, Choshen Mishpat, responsum 17, discusses a partner who admitted that a condition had been made at the beginning of the partnership before witnesses, but claimed that afterward the other partner privately canceled it and told him to handle the other's share just as he handled his own. After discussing the laws of a dispute over fulfillment of a condition, he concludes that where the parties agree that the condition originally existed but dispute whether it was later canceled, the party alleging cancellation is not believed without a plausible basis. This is even weaker than a claim that a negative condition was actually violated, regarding which the Shulchan Aruch, Beit Shmuel, and Shach indicate that the claimant is not believed merely by assertion. All the more so, one cannot simply claim that the condition itself was annulled.
