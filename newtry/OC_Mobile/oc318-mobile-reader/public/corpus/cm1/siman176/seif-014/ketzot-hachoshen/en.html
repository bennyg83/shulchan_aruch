Beit Yosef inferred from Rashi that if a partner sells partnership produce before the recognized selling season, he is negligent. I find this difficult. A tortfeasor and custodian ordinarily pay according to the value at the time of damage or negligence. Why should an early seller later owe the higher value reached at the proper selling season? Perhaps Rashi means only that the partner is subject to complaint for selling early, whereas after the proper season no complaint remains. More likely, Rashi follows his own view in the case of porters who broke a wine barrel. If the barrel was broken on a day when it would normally sell for five, they must pay five even if on ordinary days it sells for four, because the owner can say, "Had it remained with me on market day, it would have been worth five." Under that view, a partner who wrongfully sells before the proper season can be charged the later seasonal value. Rosh, however, limits the market day rule to a barrel actually broken on market day. If broken on an ordinary day, the measure is the four it was worth then. According to Rosh, the early selling partner likewise should not pay more than the value at the moment he removed the goods from the partnership. This requires study.
Rama rules in siman 304 like Rosh, that if the barrel was broken on an ordinary day the custodian does not pay the higher future market price but only the value at the time of the break. The same should therefore apply to a partner who wrongfully sells before the proper season. Even if early sale counts as negligence, he should not be liable for more than the value of the goods when he removed them from the partnership. This point remains difficult against the simple inference from Rashi. See what I wrote in siman 304, seif katan 1.
