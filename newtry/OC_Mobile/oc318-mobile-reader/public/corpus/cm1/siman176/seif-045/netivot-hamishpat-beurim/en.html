We do not listen to him. See Sema, who explains the case as one where the partner was traveling there anyway. Taz says it concerns a case where even had he remained at home he would have needed the same food expenses. In my view Taz's explanation is necessary. Otherwise, where the merchandise sold there at a higher price, the other partner would certainly have to contribute to the added travel expense according to the benefit received. One who acts intending to benefit another can recover payment for the benefit even where he himself suffered no corresponding loss, as explained in siman 264 in the gloss. Whenever one performs a service for another's benefit, the recipient cannot say, "You did it for me for free," merely because the actor would have incurred some of the expense anyway. Thus the exemption here must concern expenses that the traveling partner would have incurred even at home and that therefore created no additional cost attributable to the partnership.
