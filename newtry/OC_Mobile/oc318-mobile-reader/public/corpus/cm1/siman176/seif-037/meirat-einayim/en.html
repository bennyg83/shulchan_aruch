That the Jew did not pay the non-Jew, etc. But if Shimon cannot establish this, then even if he proves that he himself had to pay the non-Jew, Reuven need not reimburse him. Shimon became obligated to the non-Jew only on condition that the Jew failed to pay. Unless the non-Jew proves that condition, Shimon owes him nothing under strict law; if the non-Jew nevertheless pressured him, that is an improper exaction, and one person is not held liable merely because another was subjected to an improper claim on his account. See the Derishah here and what I wrote above in siman 93:15 concerning distinctions in this matter.
