The documents. The Rosh's responsum makes clear that the documents were in Reuven's possession and the community seized them from him. The Tur likewise records the wording of that responsum in siman 158, and the Darchei Moshe writes the same here. It also appears from there that the promissory notes were written solely in Reuven's name, although the money belonged to the partnership and half belonged to Shimon. See there. Sema.
To withdraw. This wording implies that they jointly purchased merchandise and one of them now wishes to withdraw from the partnership, without even taking his own share. The end of that responsum likewise implies this. In such a case he cannot do so, because if the merchandise later suffers a loss, the remaining partners would lose more than their proper shares, including the portion attributable to him. But if he wishes to take his own share, he may divide without their consent, as stated in seif 18. The text speaks of many partners to teach that even if one of them agrees to allow him to withdraw, he cannot do so without the consent of all. All the more so where there are only two. It does not appear correct to explain that the rule allowing division without the other partner's consent applies only to two equal partners, but not to one against many because his view is nullified. If that were so, why would the text require the consent of all? Consent of half would suffice. Rather, the explanation above is correct. Shach.
