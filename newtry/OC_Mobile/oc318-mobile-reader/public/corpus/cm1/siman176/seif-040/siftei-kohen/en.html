And afterward they sustained a loss, etc. The case is that they purchased for twelve hundred, the merchandise depreciated to eight hundred, and they had not yet sold it. Shimon and Levi therefore feared that it might decline further to four hundred or less. They agreed to give Reuven an additional one hundred and withdrew from the partnership rather than wait until the sale. The statement, 'If Yehudah also wishes to withdraw,' means as follows. Even without withdrawing from the partnership he must pay the one hundred for the reason I explained. Nevertheless, if Yehudah agrees to wait until the sale, the merchandise may rise again and he may then owe only part of the one hundred, or nothing at all. The ruling therefore speaks where he too wishes to withdraw before the sale. Even if they sold it for eight hundred, it is treated as though they sold it for nine hundred, counting the one hundred they received from Shimon and Levi, and Yehudah must nevertheless pay one hundred. This is explicit in the responsum of the Rosh cited by Beit Yosef in this siman, where the matter is explained more fully. I found this written.
