He values the merchandise according to what it is worth when he transports it. See Sema, seif katan 83, who writes that this applies even where the investor originally delivered the merchandise at a fixed stated value. At first glance this is difficult. If the investor expressly said that he was unwilling to give it as an iska except at that fixed value, why can he not retract when the merchandise falls in price? The reason appears to be that a sale made through a prohibited pricing arrangement remains valid at the lawful price. Siman 208 states that where a sale was made in a prohibited manner, such as adding to the price because payment was deferred, the underlying sale remains effective at the permitted market value and neither party can void it merely because of the prohibited stipulation. Here, the iska does not begin until the recipient actually transports the merchandise. If he wishes to sell it locally before transporting it, he is not permitted to do so, as Rashbam explains in Bava Batra 91 and Beit Yosef in siman 230. Yoreh Deah, siman 177, likewise forbids an investor to value merchandise given in iska above its actual value. Since the iska begins only when the recipient transports it, it is forbidden at that moment to value it above its then-current market price. If the investor had set a higher fixed figure, that resembles a transaction made under a prohibited price condition, and the transaction is sustained at the lawful value, namely the merchandise's value at the time of transport. There remains a difficulty. Siman 208, regarding one who fixes a price before the market rate is established, allows the buyer to retract. If so, here too the investor should be able to retract. The ruling that he cannot therefore requires further study.
