He bears the loss himself. Shevut Yaakov, III, 166, discusses horse merchants who formed a partnership, pooled their money, and planned to send some of them on a particular commercial venture. The partners later sent one member his share through a messenger, claiming that they had determined the venture was impossible. He refused to accept the money because he suspected they were deceiving him, and in fact before the messenger returned they had already departed on the venture. The venture ultimately failed and produced only travel expenses and loss, which they sought to share with him. Shevut Yaakov ruled that the partners who deceived him must bear the loss themselves. A partner who departs from the agreed terms bears the resulting loss, and one who conducts business through prohibited merchandise likewise bears the loss caused by the prohibited act. Here the partners violated their undertaking and their word, even after having confirmed the partnership under a rabbinic curse. It was fitting that they bear the loss themselves rather than shift it to the partner whom they deceived.
