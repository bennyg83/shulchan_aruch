If the non-Jew became entitled to an oath from him, he may accept it. Because of the potential loss of his own money, the Sages were lenient. The prohibition 'it shall not be heard on your mouth' directly warns a Jew against causing such invocation in dealings with another Jew. The prohibition of placing a stumbling block before the blind also does not apply here in the same manner, because the non-Jew himself is not commanded regarding that oath formula. See further in the Perishah at the beginning of siman 182. It is nevertheless a pious practice not to cause even a non-Jew to utter such an oath because of him. See the Perishah at the beginning of siman 122 and Even HaEzer, siman 156.
