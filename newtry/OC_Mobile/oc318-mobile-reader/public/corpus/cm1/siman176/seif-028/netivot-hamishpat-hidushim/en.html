If he said, "I am saving for myself." The Sema says he must make that declaration before beit din. The Shach and Taz disagree and hold that saying it before witnesses is sufficient. If he has a migo, he is believed to say that he declared, even privately, that he was saving for himself, and the declaration is effective.
And the other does not wish to give. See Beurim: the case is where through a bribe only half the property can be saved. If the other partner refuses to contribute to the bribe, one may save the entire recoverable half for himself. If the other partner is also willing to pay the bribe, he cannot say, "I saved for myself," and must divide the recovered property. But if by paying a bribe all the property can be saved, one may say, "I am saving my share for myself. You too should exert yourself and pay the bribe for your share," wherever there is imminent loss. See Beurim further: one may say, "I am saving for myself," only where, according to a reasonable assessment, either everything can be saved through effort or nothing can be saved. If only half can realistically be saved, for example because the debtor is poor and has assets for only half, one may not appropriate the recoverable half solely for himself. Mishneh LaMelech writes that where the debt instrument is written in only one partner's name, that partner cannot say, "I am saving for myself," because the other partner cannot sue on the note at all. This is correct.
