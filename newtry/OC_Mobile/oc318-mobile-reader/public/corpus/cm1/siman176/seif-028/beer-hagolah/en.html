Mordechai in HaMekabel records a question to Maharam and brings proof from Rava's statement concerning two people who entered an iska together and one said, "Come, let us divide," Bava Metzia 105a. Proof can also be brought from the last chapter of HaGozel, Bava Kamma 116b, section 18.
Hagahot Maimoniyot, chapter 4 of Hilkhot Shutafin, records Maharam's ruling.
He explains that in such a case a partner may divide without the other partner's knowledge, as we say regarding a caravan attacked by a raiding force in the last chapter of HaGozel, Bava Kamma 116b. Mordechai writes this rule there.
Hagahot Maimoniyot in that chapter explains that since there is a risk of loss, if he makes this declaration he may separate his share, as cited above.
Responsa Mahariv, siman 100, concerning one who received money from a gentile but by mistake received less than he was entitled to: he cannot say, "I took for myself; why did you not take your own share?" But if before taking the money from the gentile he declared, "I am rescuing my own share," he may say so.
