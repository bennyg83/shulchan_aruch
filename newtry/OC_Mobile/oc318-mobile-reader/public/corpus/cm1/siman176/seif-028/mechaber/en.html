If two partners share a debt owed by a non-Jew, and one collects part and wishes to keep it for himself, telling the other to collect his own share from the debtor, he is not heard. If a powerful official became angry with one partner and told the other, 'I will assist you in collecting your share, but not the other share,' and that partner declared, 'I am rescuing this for myself,' he rescues it for himself. The same applies when two people share a doubtful or difficult debt that requires a bribe to recover, and one refuses to contribute: the other may say, 'I will pay the bribe for my share and rescue it for myself.' Rama: In every case of impending loss, one partner may divide without the other's consent and rescue his own share. Likewise, if partners had a debt against a violent non-Jew and one appeased him into paying that partner's share, then if he first said, 'I am rescuing it for myself,' it belongs to him alone. See below, siman 181, seif 2.
