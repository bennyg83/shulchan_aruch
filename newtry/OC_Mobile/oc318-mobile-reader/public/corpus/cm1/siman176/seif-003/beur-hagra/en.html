Craftsmen... for a person does not. Kiddushin 62a to b, and in several places.
And some say they may. Like the law of a slave, whose entire labor is acquired, and who may be acquired by chalipin, Kiddushin 22b.
And some say that even. Bava Kamma 116b: "donkey drivers may stipulate..." Rabbeinu Tam explains that this applies even without a kinyan, as stated at the end of the seventh chapter of Bava Batra concerning brothers who divided by lottery, "through that benefit..." The same applies to those slaughterers there on 9a, and likewise in the Tosefta, "they may stipulate..." See there and Ramban's novellae to the first chapter of Bava Batra.
And some say, and they say. Like the law of a worker, Bava Metzia 10a: "but if he said to him... when he retracts..." See Rashi, Bava Kamma 116b, "here, with a worker..." This applies specifically when he expressly said so. He is not believed to say that he retracted so long as he did not state it explicitly. Therefore Rabbi Yochanan in Bava Metzia there establishes the case as one where he said, "give it to me," rather than relying on the first answer that he had said so initially, because he is not believed to say that he acquired it previously unless he had made the declaration beforehand, as the Tur writes there.
All this. As above, because of that benefit, etc., and as stated in Bava Metzia 94a: "With what? With words..."
