According to this, the same applies where the partners purchase merchandise and one partner supplies the money while the other cannot repay his required share. The latter is removed from the partnership. Even if he or his heirs later wish to contribute the money, they cannot reenter, because his partnership status has already ended. At first glance, however, by what kinyan did the second partner acquire the merchandise or debt so that the other cannot retract? Mere words do not transfer property. It is possible that this resembles a partner who deviates in a manner causing an irrecoverable loss, whom the others may remove from the partnership as I explained above. Once the parties agree to that removal, it is treated as a division of the partnership, and division between partners does not require a separate kinyan, as Shach writes in siman 101. Nevertheless, where one partner refuses to contribute the money, the other can certainly compel him to pay his share. Since the paying partner advanced funds to the non Jew at his fellow's direction, it is as though he lent that amount to his fellow partner. Only if the paying partner agrees to terminate the other's partnership interest does the latter become exempt from contributing the money and the paying partner acquires the other's share.
