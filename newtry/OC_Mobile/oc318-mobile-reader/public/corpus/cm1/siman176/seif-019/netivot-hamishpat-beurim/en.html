See Beit Yosef immediately after this responsum, citing Ritva concerning three people who jointly accepted an iska from one investor, who stipulated to give them one third of the profit. One of the three later died. Ritva rules that the investor must still give the survivors the full one third, because they can say, "We are now exerting ourselves more." At first glance this contradicts the present rule. The distinction is that there the three are recipients of an iska. We rule that an iska is half loan and half deposit, and the recipient's profit share comes from the loan half. Since all three jointly received the iska, each is guarantor for the others. The loan half belongs to them, and the investor may not take profit from that half because that would be ribbis. Therefore, when one recipient dies, the investor cannot reduce the one-third share allocated to the recipients. Here, however, all three contributed their own money to an ordinary partnership. They may divide profit and loss among themselves however they wish without a ribbis problem, as explained in Yoreh Deah, siman 177, in the gloss. The widow can therefore take the amount the others relinquished from Reuven's share because a partnership of two workers is not equivalent to one of three workers. As for the questioner's rationale in the cited responsum, that the survivors can say, "We work harder," it is possible the actual ruling was not based on the questioner's reason, but on the iska structure just explained.
