He cannot divide until the fixed time arrives. The Tur says the same. This is unlike an ordinary worker, and unlike one who accepts an iska from another for a fixed time, who can retract as explained by the Tur and Mechaber below in seif 23. There, the term 'servant' can apply to the one who alone works with the other's property, and the Torah says, 'The children of Israel are servants to Me.' But where two parties jointly form a partnership and both work in it, neither is the other's servant. All agree that one partner cannot simply retract before the fixed time. This is unlike Beit Yosef, who writes that according to Tosafos and Rosh one partner can retract even from a partnership. See the Derishah.
From the principal, but not from the profit. Meaning, not from the gain. The Gemara explains that profit is subordinated to maintaining the principal. This rationale applies specifically where two people accepted an iska from a third party, because they owe that person a fixed amount of principal and must retain the profits to replenish any deficiency. It does not literally apply to ordinary partners. There is, however, another reason: each can say, 'We agreed not to divide any part of the enterprise, but to keep earning with it until the term ends.' Even if one says, 'I will take my share of principal and profit; you conduct business with your share and keep your profits,' the other can answer, 'The fortune of two together is stronger.' The Gemara ultimately gives this rationale as well. See the Derishah.
