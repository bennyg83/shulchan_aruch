The term. So writes the Tur, and he maintains that this is not comparable to a worker or to one who accepts an investment arrangement for a fixed term, discussed in seif 23. There, the designation of servant applies to him, and Scripture states, "For the Children of Israel are servants to Me." By contrast, where two people entered a partnership together and both conduct the business, neither is called the other's servant. All agree that one cannot withdraw before the term, unlike the Beit Yosef, who wrote that according to Tosafot and the Rosh they may withdraw. Sema.
Of the profit. Meaning, of the gain. The Gemara explains that the profit is pledged to protect the principal. This reason, however, applies specifically where two people accepted an investment from a third party, for then each can say: Since we owe such and such principal to that person, the profit must remain in our hands to replenish any deficiency in the principal. That rationale does not apply to ordinary partners. There is, however, another reason: one partner may say, We did not divide anything; rather, we agreed to continue profiting from it until the end of the term. Even if the other says, I will take my share of principal and profit, and you may continue with your own share and keep its profit, the second may answer that the joint fortune of two is superior. The Gemara ultimately gives this reason as well. Sema.
