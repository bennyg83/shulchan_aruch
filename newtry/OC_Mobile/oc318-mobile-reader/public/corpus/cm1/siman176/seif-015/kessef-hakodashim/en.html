A fixed term. Partners entered a one-year lease partnership in what is called an ordina, and in the middle of the term one seeks to withdraw because he suspects theft by the other partner's wife and says he found supporting indications. Even if he proves the basis of the suspicion, it appears at first similar to the rule at the beginning of siman 171 that where partners voluntarily entered the partnership, one cannot later demand 'buy or sell,' because he should have anticipated the ordinary risks at the outset. We do not ordinarily presume people are thieves, but Chazal nevertheless said, 'Honor him and suspect him,' meaning one should exercise caution without affirmatively labeling him a thief. Thus perhaps he should have anticipated such a concern. Yet if he can actually establish grounds for the suspicion, the case may resemble the rule in siman 71 that where a person was granted special credibility and later his trustworthiness is impaired, the earlier grant of credibility falls away. Perhaps the same applies here. This requires further study. In practice we arranged a compromise.
