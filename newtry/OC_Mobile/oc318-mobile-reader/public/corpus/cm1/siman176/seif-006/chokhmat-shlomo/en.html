Seif 18: If he divided with fewer than three, his act is ineffective. Note: I am uncertain whether three are also required where the partners originally joined specifically to purchase merchandise and divide the merchandise immediately. Perhaps, since they expressly agreed to divide, two suffice, or perhaps an equal division could even be made privately by one partner. What is clear is that the rule that partners who jointly purchase are mutually responsible applies only where they entered a general partnership for shared profit or fixed shares. If they joined only to buy and immediately divide the goods, they do not become mutually responsible for later developments, except as to payment of the purchase price. As to the first question, the extra stipulation that they joined in order to divide seems intended to add something. In an ordinary partnership one may already divide without the other's consent before three judges. Why stipulate division unless the condition permits division without three? The Gemara, however, speaks of witnesses that a division occurred before three, which might imply that three are always required. Yet that case can be explained as one where there were witnesses that they had entered an ordinary partnership. Where there are no such witnesses, a partner who claims that they joined specifically for immediate division has a migo and can also claim that he divided before three. I therefore incline to the view that where the original agreement was specifically to divide the merchandise immediately, three are unnecessary. I so ruled in a case that came before me.
