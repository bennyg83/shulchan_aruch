There is an authority who says that this applies only, etc. This means that if they sold it alive, the loss is divided equally. See Responsa Maharit, siman 128; Responsa Rabbi Shlomo Kohen, book 2, siman 87; Responsa Maharashdam, siman 173; and Responsa Rabbi Aharon Sasson, siman 112.
And the entire asset was lost, etc. See Responsa Mabit, part 1, siman 12, who writes that the loss is borne equally and one partner must pay the other from his own property. His words there indicate that the Ramah disagrees with the Rambam in siman 93. The Bach distinguishes between the present case, where the loss did not arise from commercial activity, and siman 93, where it did. If the partner knew of this loss, he must pay even from his own property. See there.
They take according to the money contributed. This applies even if they sold the animal alive. See Responsa Rabbi Moshe Alshich, siman 136.
