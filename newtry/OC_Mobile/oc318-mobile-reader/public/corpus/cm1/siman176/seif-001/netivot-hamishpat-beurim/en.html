According to the manner of their acquisition their partnership continues. Perishah writes that according to Rambam, if the partnership was not perfected by a valid kinyan, the parties can retract even regarding profits already earned. This is difficult in light of siman 183. If Shimon was told to buy merchandise jointly for both of them, the principal cannot retract after the agent purchased, and the agent likewise cannot retract unless he explicitly withdrew before purchase. Why should joint merchants be worse than an ordinary agency? Likewise, Rama's case where one lender tells the borrower to hold the money as an iska for half the profit should not be worse than saying, "Buy merchandise with the money you owe me for our joint account." It therefore appears that once they actually begin trading, Rambam too agrees that the partnership is effective at least as to what has already been acquired and earned. Tur's language supports this, since he does not present a dispute with Rambam. Taz expressly says that beginning to conduct business is effective even without prior commingling, even prospectively. Sema requires commingling for prospective inability to retract, but expressly concedes that as to profits already earned, beginning the business suffices. The reason is that once one partner has not retracted and purchases with partnership intent, the purchased merchandise is automatically acquired for the other as well, because he acts as his agent. An agent instructed to purchase acquires for the principal even without informing the seller. Rama's case of the borrower concerns money lost before the borrower actually purchased merchandise for the partnership. Until purchase, the lender has not yet acquired an interest in the money itself, so if it is lost the borrower remains liable. But once the borrower purchases merchandise for the partnership, whether pursuant to a specified instruction or a general instruction to buy suitable merchandise, the lender acquires his share. Darkei Moshe's wording that the borrower "engaged in business with it" can mean that he began going out to trade with the money and the money was lost before purchase, or that he purchased with part and the rest was lost. If the lender does not believe him and says perhaps he never bought any merchandise at all, there is no oath beyond a general cherem, because the lender's claim is uncertain. This resembles one who instructs another to pay a third party and the agent says he paid while the third party is not present to contradict him. Likewise, if the lender instructed him to place the money in a particular place and he says he did so, he is believed. There remains a conceptual difficulty. Rambam holds that a kinyan does not take effect on things not yet in existence. If so, how does pulling the merchandise already devoted to the partnership prevent future retraction from continuing to work with it? In my view Rambam nevertheless treats the parties as workers hired to one another. Just as pulling a craftsman's tools can bind the worker not to retract, as Tosafot explain in Bava Metzia, so too the money or merchandise with which the partners conduct business are their instruments of trade. Pulling or taking possession of those business assets therefore binds their personal obligations to continue the partnership. This resembles Sema's explanation in siman 310, in the name of the Rosh, that pulling the rented donkey can bind the lessor's assets to provide a substitute. We thus find that acquisition of an object can generate a personal obligation. The alternative explanation in Sema, that committing one's person to acquire things expected to come into existence is effective, is strained. The better rationale is that taking possession of the partnership assets binds their personal service obligations.
