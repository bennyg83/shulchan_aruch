A document. The Sema writes in the name of the Ir Shushan that if the witnesses wrote in the document that the parties accepted upon themselves a severe ban and a Torah oath, it is effective. I do not understand why the punishment of an oath and ban should be needed to uphold the acquisition, since there is a distinction between them, as the Rama writes in siman 209, seif 4. It therefore appears to me that the matter depends on language of personal obligation: if one party obligated himself to the other to perform the act, that obligation is effective for all purposes, as explained in siman 60. His fellow. Meaning that he pulls the money for purposes of partnership, but not so that the money becomes his own, because that would resemble barter, which is ineffective with currency. So writes the Sema. The Taz writes: I do not understand this. Barter means acquiring another object through the item given in exchange, whereas pulling money in order to acquire the very coins themselves is certainly effective. Rabbi Tzvi Ashkenazi, in his glosses, explains the Sema as follows: he means that each should not pull the other's money so that it becomes his own, because then they have merely exchanged equivalent items and it is as though neither made an effective act of acquisition. What difference is there whether the black maneh belonged to Reuven and the white to Shimon before the pulling, or the reverse after the pulling? In either case neither has a right in the other's property. The word "currency" in the Sema is a scribal error and should read, "as barter, and it is ineffective." This is clear. See Responsa Maharashdam, simanim 170, 178, and 274.
They entered into partnership. The Sema writes that this means they formed the partnership by placing their funds together, mingling them, and beginning their joint business. In that case it is effective even to prevent either from withdrawing prospectively until the time they originally fixed. If they did not place and mingle their money together, but each began purchasing separately with his own funds, that does not prevent either from withdrawing as to future dealings. See what the Taz writes on this. The Shach writes that even without a formal kinyan this is effective. Conversely, if they merely combined their money but did not begin buying and selling, even a formal kinyan is ineffective, because a kinyan made with currency adds nothing, since currency cannot be acquired through barter. What the Sema writes in seif 2 concerning mixing their produce, distinguishing it from mixing money, does not mean that there a kinyan is needed and effective. Rather, mere mixing of produce without beginning commercial activity is ineffective, while acquiring the produce itself is comparable to beginning to buy and sell with money. The Sema therefore asked that just as merely mixing currency is ineffective without beginning business, so too merely mixing produce should be ineffective without a kinyan. He answers that with currency the concept of mixture is not truly applicable in the same way. Shach.
