Seif 8: Partners are paid custodians for one another. Note: If the partners own an object intended for use and allocate fixed periods for each to use it, I am uncertain about liability when it is lost while one partner is using it. At first glance, since partners are paid custodians for each other, the user should be a borrower during his period and liable even for unavoidable accidents. On further analysis, however, the opposite appears correct. The Gemara regarding a pit owned by two partners teaches that, according to Rabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov, when each draws water he is regarded as drawing from his own share, and he does not thereby become custodian of the other's share. Although we generally say there is no retroactive clarification, the Ran explains that the “clarification” in joint use is different: the partners are understood from the outset to have acquired on the basis that each one's period of use is then regarded as his own. The same should apply to an object intended for alternating personal use. While one partner uses it, it is treated as his for that period. He is therefore not a borrower of the other's share. Indeed, he may not even be a paid custodian, but rather resembles one who acquires an animal for a fixed period, who is liable only as an unpaid custodian. The rule that partners are paid custodians for one another applies instead to partnership property held for commerce or investment, where this reasoning does not apply and both remain joint owners while one has custody. For property meant for alternating use, each user's period is treated as his own, like a temporary purchaser.
