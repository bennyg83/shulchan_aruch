A governmental penalty. Concerning the governmental rules about tobacco: because the decree applies equally to all residents of the country, it appears to fall under the rule that the law of the kingdom is law. If partners engage jointly in concealed tobacco trading and the goods are discovered, causing both forfeiture of the tobacco and an additional monetary penalty, it requires study how the loss is allocated. Urim VeTumim at the end of siman 128 cites Knesset HaGedolah as recording many laws of smuggling, though I have not now examined how he treats this case. Siman 176, in discussing theft, implies that a partner who did not know of the unlawful act is not liable, whereas knowledge and consent may place him within the liability. Yet the later language there may imply that actual benefit is required. Darkei Moshe refers to the end of siman 177 and to Beit Yosef in the name of Rashba regarding theft. In the tobacco case, forfeiture of the very goods may be more directly treated as a partnership loss than an additional external fine. From the outset both may have understood that the concealed goods themselves would be lost if discovered, whereas they may not have contemplated a separate penalty imposed upon a person. If, however, the partnership was not fully settled and the partner holding the goods expressly withdrew, stating that he would treat the purchase as his own and repay the money advanced by his fellow, then from the moment he excluded the other from the anticipated profit he should also bear the subsequent loss. A partner who deliberately deviates against his fellow's will bears the loss while any profit remains shared. Although merely declaring that he intends to keep the profit is not a physical act like buying a risky commodity, the excluded partner can say: had you not pushed me away from the profit, I would have hurried to sell and avoided the penalty, especially where the penalty arose only because of delay. The precise scope of this claim, particularly where the goods could perhaps still have been saved, requires study.
