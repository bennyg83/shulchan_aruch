There: But if Shimon has no witnesses. As the Rama's wording stands, the ruling is difficult. Shimon admits that the debt belonged to the partnership, so his claim appears to be a migo against witnesses. In the Mordechai, however, Maharam's wording is: if there are no witnesses who know that the debt remained unpaid, Shimon is believed to say, “You told me to release him,” through the migo that he could have said, “I never released him.” This is not a migo against witnesses. The same responsum in Hagahot Maimoniyot says that perhaps the instruction to release the debtor was given because he had already paid. The Rama must therefore mean that where there are no witnesses both that the debt belonged to the partnership and that it remained unpaid, Shimon may say that Reuven ordered the release because the debt had been paid. This is the essential distinction between the opening and concluding cases. His migo that the event never occurred allows him to say, “You instructed me to release him because he had paid.” The Mordechai further states that if Shimon must reimburse Reuven, he pays only the market value for which the debt could have been sold.
