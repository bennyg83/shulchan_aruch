Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat, responsum 93, argues at length that the Rama's ruling is correct, namely that he does have the status of an unpaid custodian. He notes that the Shach did not have before him the words of the Raah cited in Shitah Mekubetzet to the beginning of the fourth chapter of Bava Kamma. Chatam Sofer presented his analysis to Rabbi Mordechai Banet, who agreed that the practical ruling follows the Rama, and he writes that one should not depart from it even on a claim of kim li. In responsum 97 Chatam Sofer applied this rule to Reuven, Shimon, and Levi, partners who bought pearls. Pearl merchants normally attract additional sellers when they visibly carry pearls. Reuven therefore kept the partnership pearls in his pouch, so that any profit from sale of those pearls would be shared but new pearls offered to him because of them could be bought for himself. Shimon later asked to take the partnership pearls because he wished to sell them, and Reuven handed them over. Shimon did not sell them during the market. On the journey home he cried out that the partnership pearls, together with his own, had been lost from a pouch sewn beneath his garment; the stitching had come loose and the pouch tore away. Reuven and Levi argued that such a loss necessarily reflected negligence. Either the stitching was already damaged and Shimon negligently placed the partnership property in a defective pouch, or he overloaded the pouch until the stitching tore. Chatam Sofer ruled that Shimon had to pay the full market value of the partnership pearls, not merely their original discounted purchase price. Under the Rama he was at least an unpaid custodian. In fact, because possession of the partnership pearls gave him commercial benefit by attracting other pearl sellers, he was a paid custodian, no less than a shopkeeper entrusted with money or a broker who derives benefit from holding goods. A paid custodian is liable for theft and loss, even theft accompanied by unusual circumstances, and this case was in any event close to negligence. Chatam Sofer addresses the Shach's two objections, that no custodial status exists and that, even if it did, the case should be treated as guarding while the owner is employed with him. He rejects both and concludes that the Rama's ruling stands. He adds that the facts themselves strongly indicated negligence. Although there were Rishonim who might support the Shach, once the Rama had ruled otherwise there was no basis to depart from him, and Chatam Sofer believed that had the Shach seen the Raah in Shitah Mekubetzet he would have agreed. Nevertheless, in the actual case Chatam Sofer labored to arrange a compromise between the parties.
