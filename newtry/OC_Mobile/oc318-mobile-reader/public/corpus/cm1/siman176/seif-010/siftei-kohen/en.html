And he shall not engage in another business, etc. The Sema infers from the later phrase, 'But if he joined with another,' that the partner himself may not engage in another business even with his own funds. In my view, since the text says that he 'joined in partnership,' it ordinarily means that he too actively conducts the business, for each partner's activity is like that of both. Certainly, at the outset he may not engage in another business. The later passage does not address permission to do so; it teaches only that if he violated the rule and entered another partnership, the profit does not become jointly owned merely because his outside activity caused him to neglect the original partnership. This is also the Beit Yosef's explanation. The Sema's reading is difficult, because if the partner did no work at all, it would be obvious that profit earned from his separate money belongs to him. One might force the Sema to mean that outside activity is permitted only where another person is his partner, but even then, if he actively conducts that other business, it remains prohibited at the outset. See Responsa Maharashdam, simanim 161, 170, and 190; Responsa Rabbi Aharon Sasson, simanim 190 and 136; Responsa Rabbi Shlomo Kohen, book 2, simanim 86 and 88; Responsa Rabbi Eliyahu ibn Chaim, siman 44; and Responsa Mabit, part 2, siman 324.
