And he should not engage in another business. Since the text later says, 'But if he partnered with another using his own money, etc.,' it follows that partnership with another is permitted, but he himself may not engage even his own funds in a separate business while another's iska is in his hands. See the Tur in Yoreh Deah, siman 176, who says, 'and anything similar.' This excludes a shepherd who already has his own animals and accepts additional animals under an iska, where the new iska requires no additional exertion. This implies that only an analogous case is permitted, while ordinarily one who accepts an iska may not occupy himself with an unrelated business of his own. Therefore Rambam and the Mechaber write simply, 'he should not engage in another business,' meaning one outside the partnership. Beit Yosef explains it this way. See the Derishah.
That is always sold on credit. The word 'always' excludes merchandise that is only sometimes sold on credit. Rama writes in the gloss that if some people sell such goods only for cash, he may not extend credit without his partner's consent.
Afterward he informed him and he agreed to his conduct, etc. This implies explicit agreement. Mere silence is not treated here as admission, because he may have remained silent only because the act had already been done and could not be changed. See the Tur, seif 34, on this law.
These matters do not require a kinyan, etc. Once the partner agrees, it is waiver, and waiver requires no kinyan. Beit Yosef says so. In the Perishah I explained why the text nevertheless needed to state this.
Or he sold on credit, etc. The same applies if he extended the due date of an existing partnership debt. See Responsa Rosh, klal 88, siman 2, which discusses this at length.
The profit is divided equally. The profit arose from partnership money and the deviating partner did not intend through his deviation to acquire it for himself. If there is a loss, however, his fellow can say, 'Had you not deviated, you would not have suffered this loss.'
