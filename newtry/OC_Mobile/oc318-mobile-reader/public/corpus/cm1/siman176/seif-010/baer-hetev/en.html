The Sema writes that from the next seif, which says, "but if he entered into partnership with another using his own money," it appears that he himself may not engage even in another business using his own funds. In my humble opinion, since the text says "entered into partnership," it normally means that he also actively conducts the business, since each partner works like the other. Certainly, initially he may not engage in another commercial venture. The next seif merely teaches that if he violated this rule and entered another partnership, one should not say that because he neglected the present enterprise by engaging in another business, the profits from the second venture belong to the first partnership. The ruling teaches otherwise. The Beit Yosef explains it similarly. See Responsa Maharashdam, simanim 168, 170, and 190; Responsa Maharam Alshakar, simanim 190 and 136; Responsa Rashach, part 2, simanim 86 and 88; Responsa Raanach, siman 44; and Responsa Mabit, part 2, siman 324.
