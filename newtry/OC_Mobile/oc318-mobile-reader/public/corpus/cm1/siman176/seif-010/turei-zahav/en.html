There, in the gloss: They divided the partnership. I have not found a satisfactory basis for this ruling. Its reason is that partners are paid guardians for one another, as Maharik writes explicitly. But if after the division the property remains in only one partner's hands, why is he still a paid guardian? He receives no further benefit from the other. One must say that the present guarding continues from the guarding that began while the partnership existed, when each was a paid guardian for the other. If so, it should also be guarding with the owner present, since the other partner was present at the outset. I can reconcile the ruling only where the partner now retaining the property had begun guarding alone before the other partner joined. In that case the original guarding did not begin with the owner present, and it never ceased. The matter requires study.
