The tortfeasor must trouble himself, etc. As explained, the injured party ordinarily arranges to raise the carcass from the pit after learning of the event and recovers the expense from the tortfeasor. If the tortfeasor refuses either to raise it or to provide the cost, he must pay the corresponding diminution. If the offending ox was innocuous he pays half the loss, and if forewarned the entire loss. Scripture says, "He shall return money to its owner, and the dead animal shall be his," implying that the tortfeasor must restore even the carcass to the injured party's possession and then add money up to the amount of the damage. Although that verse concerns a pit, the same applies to damage by a forewarned ox.
