The damager must smear the wound. See Sema, who writes that, as he explained above in seif katan 2, the injured party must raise the animal from the pit and then collects his expenses of raising it from the damager. Responsa Beit Ephraim, Choshen Mishpat, siman 35, writes that Maharshal in Yam Shel Shlomo, Bava Kamma, siman 31, and Shitah Mekubetzet to Bava Metzia 56 in the name of Ramban do not agree with the Sema's understanding of the Rosh and Tur. Rather, the actual labor of raising the animal is incumbent upon the damager. Responsibility for further loss, however, rests on the injured party in the following sense: as soon as he learns of the matter he must endeavor to demand that the damager raise it. If he demanded this and the damager was negligent, the damager is liable. But if the injured party knew and did not demand that it be raised, his silence shows that he did not object to its remaining there, and from then on the risk is his and the loss falls upon him.
