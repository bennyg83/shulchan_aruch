Seif 3, the tortfeasor must undertake the effort, and so forth. The Sema requires the tortfeasor to bear even the carcass's depreciation after death, valuing it according to what it was worth alive, if he failed to pay the removal expense. This is astonishing. Scripture places upon the injured party the loss occurring after death. Why should failure to pay a relatively small expense make the tortfeasor liable for the entire depreciation? It appears to me that this seif teaches only that the effort rests on the tortfeasor. Two obligations therefore rest upon him: the expense and labor of handling the carcass, and payment for the reduction from the animal's value while alive. The seif does not address depreciation after death. If he has not paid the expense, the court compels him to pay it, but any later depreciation of the carcass belongs to the injured party.
