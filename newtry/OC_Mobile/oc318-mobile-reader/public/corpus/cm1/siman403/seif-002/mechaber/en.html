The depreciation of the carcass is borne by the injured owner, while appreciation of the carcass is divided between the tortfeasor and the injured owner. For example, if an ox worth two hundred was gored and died, and its carcass was worth one hundred at death but fell in value to eighty by the time of judgment, the tortfeasor pays only one hundred. If his ox was tam, he pays fifty from the body of the damaging ox. Nimukei Yosef. This applies specifically when the injured owner learned of the death at the time it occurred. If he did not know, the loss in value remains on the tortfeasor until the injured owner learns of it. Some say the same applies if the market price falls. Tur, seif 8. Others disagree and hold that a fall in market price is borne by the injured owner even before he learns of it. Nimukei Yosef in the cited chapter. If the carcass appreciates and is worth one hundred twenty at the time of judgment, the tortfeasor pays ninety; if his ox was tam, he pays forty-five from its body. This is the meaning of, "They shall also divide the dead animal." Rama: If the carcass appreciates so greatly that half the appreciation exceeds the amount of the damage, we do not say that the tortfeasor takes half the appreciation and thereby profits. Tur.
