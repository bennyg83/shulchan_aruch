Heavier. The Tur cites the Ramah that this applies specifically where the animal was rented for a woman without identifying her. If it was rented for this particular woman, the renter may not substitute another woman. Likewise, if it was rented for this particular load, he may not substitute another load even if it is no heavier. Others permit substitution when the second load is no heavier, and the Rosh agrees. Nimukei Yosef, chapter HaOmanin, cites the commentators similarly, and Maggid Mishneh cites the Ramban and Rashba regarding "this wine" in accordance with the permissive opinion; the Beit Yosef cites them in siman 311, seif 4. The Ir Shushan and Sema also rule so without qualification there, and this is the primary ruling. All this applies where the renter or owner accompanies the animal. Otherwise, the renter may not change even from a heavier load to a lighter one, because a renter may not sublet the animal, as the Tur writes in the name of the Ramah. It appears that one may substitute a member of one's household even when one does not accompany the animal, because anyone who deposits property does so with the understanding that the custodian's wife and children may handle it. Shach.
