He is not liable to pay. This applies even where it is known that the animal died because of the work. The reason appears to be that since the owner ordinarily rents the animal out, we assume that if the renter had said initially that a woman would ride it, the owner would also have rented it to him for an increased fee. Therefore the renter is exempt from the loss itself and must only add the additional rental amount. This is specifically where the renter said that he was renting it for a man. But if the owner himself said, "I am renting it to you for a man," that is an express restriction and the renter is liable if a woman rode it and it died from the work. Likewise, this applies specifically where it is known that the animal died because of the work. If instead a snake bit it, there was no misappropriation. Similarly, the Mechaber's case of mountain travel concerns where the owner himself said that he was renting it also for the mountain route. But if the renter merely said that he intended to take it through the mountains, he is exempt. The dissent cited by Rama holds that even if only the renter stated that a woman would ride it, he is liable if it died from the work. If a gentile rode it, however, everyone agrees he is liable because a gentile does not ordinarily take care with a Jew's animal, and that is full negligence.
