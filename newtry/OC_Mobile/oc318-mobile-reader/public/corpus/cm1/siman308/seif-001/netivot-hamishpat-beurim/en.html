He is not liable to pay, and some disagree. Beit Yosef writes that Ramban and Rosh, whose view Mechaber brings in siman 309, disagree. Ketzot HaChoshen objects that Ramban himself says liability applies when it certainly died because of the changed use, and Rosh also bases liability on strong proof that the animal died from the deviation. In my view Rosh in fact imposes liability even where there is no conclusive proof at the outset. His discussion of proof was needed because in that responsum the animal died only after it had already been returned. One could then claim that the owner caused the later death through food, medication, or some later event. Rosh therefore required strong indication that the illness had begun while the animal was still in the renter's possession and resulted from the changed use. Where the death occurs during the changed work itself, however, Rosh can impose liability whenever there is a reasonable basis to connect the death to the heavier or altered work, even if one cannot prove it with certainty. The deviation is treated as negligence where the altered work could plausibly have caused the loss. Rashba, by contrast, exempts where there is a strong presumption that the owner would have agreed to the altered use had he been asked in advance. On that view the renter is like one who used with permission, even if the animal certainly died because of that work. Accordingly, even moving from mountain work to valley work can be exempt if the owner never expressed insistence on the particular use. Rosh's liability should still be limited to losses connected with the altered work, such as wear or death that can plausibly result from the heavier work or different conditions. Other accidents, such as a snakebite, do not become the renter's responsibility merely because he changed the use. This is no worse than one who takes a donkey that stands for hire, who is not thereby a robber liable for all accidents. The same applies to depreciation. If the depreciation is attributable to the altered work, Rosh can impose it, like the rule that a wrongdoer may have to bear depreciation. Rashba can exempt even such work-related depreciation where the owner would plainly have consented to the heavier work and did not show any contrary preference.
