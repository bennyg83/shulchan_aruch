They disagree. The Darkei Moshe writes that this is the responsum of the Rosh cited below in siman 309, seif 3, and the view of Rivah in the Mordechai. According to this, it is somewhat difficult why the Rama there rules without qualification like the Mechaber. It appears, however, that the cases differ. There the lessor specifically mentioned that the animal was to be returned the next day, so the renter should have considered that the lessor knows the nature of his own animal. Similarly, Rivah in the Mordechai may be speaking where the lessor said explicitly, "I rent it to you for an Israelite man to ride." Here, by contrast, the animal was simply rented for a man without further specification. Indeed, if the lessor explicitly said, "I rent it for a man to ride," the renter would be liable for changing the rider. This distinction appears in the students of the Rashba cited by the Beit Yosef at the end of this siman, and the Beit Yosef agrees with them; it is also cited in the Darkei Moshe and by the Sema below.
