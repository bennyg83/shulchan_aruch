He feels it. Meaning, he should have thrown off the load when he realized that it was too heavy. Since he did not, one might say that he knew of the excess and waived his claim. Nevertheless, the renter is liable. We say that because the laborer did not know that more than the agreed weight had been loaded upon him, even when he felt it was unusually heavy he reasoned: "I regularly carry a load of this size. Why is today different? I must be experiencing temporary weakness, and it will pass immediately." He therefore did not cast off the load, and meanwhile he was injured by it. For this reason the renter is liable. Sema.
By ordinance. Meaning, whenever sudden damage threatens a person's property, there is a beit din ordinance permitting him to take another person's vessel in order to save his own, provided he pays the owner for that vessel. The same applies to similar cases. See simanim 264 and 265 for the details. It is somewhat difficult that the Rama rules here without qualification that he may take it, while in siman 264, seif 5, and at the end of siman 274 he records dissenting opinions, namely the Rif and Rambam cited by the Tur. One must say that in the Rama's view even those authorities disagree only with the Rosh and those who hold that the owner of the wine can be compelled to spill his wine against his will. But if the owner is absent and cannot express that he does not consent, perhaps even the dissenters agree that the owner of the honey may spill the other's wine on the understanding that he will pay for it. For this reason the Rama writes here that he is not deemed a robber. Sema.
