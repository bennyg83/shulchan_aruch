(Seif 7) The porter. The Gemara asks: He is a person of understanding, so if the load was too heavy he should have thrown it down. Abaye answers that the case is where he collapsed immediately. Rava says that the practical difference concerns additional wages, which must be paid to him. Rav Ashi says that he thought the weakness that seized him was temporary. The Tur writes that the law follows Rav Ashi, excluding Rava. This means that we plainly do not rule like Abaye, who imposes liability only if he collapsed immediately. Even Rava disagrees with Abaye, for Rava holds that additional wages must be paid even when no injury occurred. Rav Ashi, however, imposes liability only where injury occurred and the porter nevertheless continued carrying it. It appears to me that this applies specifically where he hired him without specifying a particular amount and the ordinary load is thirty kav. In such a case, if no injury occurred and he carried the additional amount, the employer need not add to his wages, since we see that this load was suitable for him. But if he hired him for a specified weight and added one-thirtieth to the load, he must pay additional wages even if no injury occurred. The argument that he should have cast it down does not apply, for in truth he was physically capable of carrying that amount; he merely did not wish to exert himself beyond the stipulated measure, and he was misled into thinking that no addition had been made. Likewise, wherever there is a known standard load for a person, the employer must pay for the entire addition if it equals one-thirtieth. Why should this differ from the ruling at the end of the relevant chapter that he must pay wages for the addition? Whenever a standard measure is known, it is as though that measure was expressly stipulated. Although the porter can bear it, why should the employer profit from another person’s strength? It therefore appears that he must pay for the entire addition. For this reason the Tur specifically mentions the standard load of a person. This appears clear to me.
