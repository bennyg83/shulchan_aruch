To carry a specified weight, etc., he is liable. Meaning, even though other animals can carry the load that this renter placed upon the animal and even more, nevertheless, since this particular owner fixed with him how much may be placed upon it, the renter is liable if he exceeds that amount. This applies even though the owner did not expressly warn him, "If you place more upon it you will be liable for accidents," because we say that the owner knew his own animal and knew that it could not bear more than the stated amount. The renter therefore had no right to deviate from his instructions. This applies specifically where the owner fixed the amount of the load. But if the renter approached the owner and said, "Rent me your animal and I will carry such-and-such number of se'ah upon it to a particular place," then even if the renter adds one thirtieth or more beyond the amount he himself stated, he remains exempt from accidents so long as ordinary animals can carry the amount he actually loaded. He must merely add rental payment for the additional weight. There was no disclosure by the owner that his animal could not bear the ordinary load of other animals, similar to what was explained at the beginning of this siman concerning one who rented an animal for a man and then placed a woman upon it.
