A purchaser. Since the Mechaber states the rule without qualification, it implies that it applies even while the woman is alive and living with her husband. This is somewhat difficult, because in siman 103, seif 10, the Rama writes that some say it applies specifically after the woman has died. One may answer that here the case concerns nichsei tzon barzel, which are immediately regarded as the husband's property, whereas there the case concerns nichsei melog, which remain called by her name. Sema. In truth, however, this distinction is not precise, because here too the case concerns nichsei melog, as explained by the Shach. Rather, in siman 103 the husband suffers no loss, since he owns only the fruits, and what difference is it to him whether the fruits come from this land or from the debt representing the appraisal that the creditor must return to her? Here, however, the husband does suffer a loss, and he brought it upon himself by failing to write an after-acquired property clause. Therefore all agree that he is treated as a purchaser, just as an oral lender cannot collect from a husband, as stated in Even HaEzer, siman 91, seif 4, and as explained at the end of Bava Batra, Perek Yesh Nochalin. See what I wrote there in my work. The gloss adds: see what I wrote on this in siman 103, seif katan 29. The Shach writes that the underlying ruling requires study, and that at minimum it is a case of legal doubt. Therefore, if the lender seized the property, it is not removed from him. See his lengthy discussion. He further writes that the Mechaber's phrase 'and she then married' is not exact. Baal HaTerumot derived the rule from the case in which she borrowed, consumed the funds, and then married, and therefore used the same expression here. In truth, even if she married first and Reuven subsequently died, according to this opinion the creditor cannot collect from the husband because a husband has the status of a purchaser. This is also evident from the Gemara at the end of Perek Mi SheHayah Nasui and from the Tur, Even HaEzer, beginning of siman 91, regarding the statement that when the condition is made while she is married, what meaning can there be to saying, 'after you, so-and-so shall acquire'? See there; also Responsa Maharit, simanim 9, 33, and 34, and Responsa Maharashdam, siman 189. See also what the Taz writes regarding this rule.
