And she later married. The same applies if she married during her father's lifetime. In either case, the moment property later falls to her by inheritance, her husband acquires rights in it, and he is treated as a purchaser from her because his hand is always like that of a purchaser. See Shach.
A husband is treated as a purchaser of his wife's property. This applies even during her lifetime. Sema asks that above, at the end of siman 103, Rama appears to treat the husband as a purchaser only after her death. Sema therefore attempts to explain this case as involving tzon barzel property, where everyone would treat him as a purchaser. Shach correctly objects that the source in Baal HaTerumos speaks specifically of nichsei melog. See what I wrote at the end of siman 103. The return of an appraisal is exceptional and resolves the apparent contradiction.
He is treated as a purchaser. Although in situations involving loss to others the Sages sometimes treat a husband as an heir rather than a purchaser, here the creditor lacks an after acquired property clause and is therefore comparable to an oral creditor. In such a case we do not apply the special rule treating the husband as an heir. See Tumim for my resolution of Shach's argument. Shach nevertheless concludes that if the creditor seized the property, it is not removed from him. See Tumim. I wrote that this is correct where he seized movable property and there is at least some express lien on movable and landed property. Without that, seizure does not help. See there.
