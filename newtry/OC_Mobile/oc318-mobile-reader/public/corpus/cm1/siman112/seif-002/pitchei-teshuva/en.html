And there are those who disagree. See Baer Heitev in the name of the Shach, and see Responsa Mishkenot Yaakov, siman 72, concerning this. See Responsa Chavat Yair, siman 230, regarding Reuven, who borrowed from Shimon and accepted by kinyan, in full legal form, a lien upon his property. Before the document was written, Reuven found a house offered cheaply and wanted to buy it and resell it for profit. He came to the witnesses to the kinyan and also told the scribe that he was retracting from the future property lien. They informed Shimon. Shimon came to Reuven and said, "If so, return my money." Reuven answered yes, but postponed him from day to day for several weeks. In the meantime Reuven bought the house and then sold it without public announcement, later losing his assets. Shimon sought to seize the house from the purchaser, since it had been bought without announcement. Chavat Yair ruled that Shimon could properly seize it even without relying on the kinyan, as explained at the beginning of siman 39. The borrower's retraction from the future property lien is ineffective, because the Shach in siman 112 already decided in accordance with the Ramban that he cannot retract. Nor does Shimon's agreement to cancel the kinyan entirely constitute an express waiver. When Shimon said, "If so, return my money," he meant that the kinyan would be canceled automatically when the money was returned, not before. All this applies where the document was not yet written, and even if it was written but had not yet reached Shimon. If, however, Shimon later received the document and it omitted the future property lien, his silence constitutes waiver. See there.
