If he did not write d'akni, etc. According to the first view in seif 1, which does not treat omission of d'akni as a scribal error, this is straightforward. According to the second view, which does, the case is where he expressly excluded later-acquired fields from the lien or wrote only partial responsibility without including d'akni.
The lender must bring proof. Because the purchaser presently possesses the property, he is considered the one in possession, and the burden of proof rests on the lender.
The lender must bring witnesses, etc. Where we cannot simply preserve the field in the seller's ancestral possession because witnesses show that he bought it from others, the lender is the one seeking to extract and must prove his case.
But if there are witnesses, etc., and when he acquired it. The witnesses must establish not merely that the seller acquired the property but that he acquired it after the loan. In the first clause, where there were no witnesses establishing the borrower's prior possession, the purchaser's current possession means slight proof that the seller acquired it is enough. But where there are witnesses that the seller had been in possession, we preserve that prior status unless the purchaser supplies clear proof of when the acquisition occurred. For the same reason, in the following seif the heir must prove that the father acquired the field afterward. Levush's omission of the words "and when he acquired it" is therefore incorrect.
