If not... some say concerning... Bava Metzia 110b. Rabbi Chanina initially proposed one rule, and an elder corrected him. Rashi explains the analogous uncertainty concerning orphans.
And some say... but... Bava Basra 31a, where Abaye rejects a migo because the witnesses' testimony concerning ancestral ownership is understood broadly. The case must be one where the witnesses did not expressly testify that the property always belonged to the challenger's ancestors, for otherwise Rabbah could not invoke a migo against witnesses. Yet Abaye treats their ordinary wording as establishing longstanding ancestral ownership. See Ramban there. The same interpretive principle applies here. See Baal HaTerumos.
And it appears to me... The Tur writes similarly, following Tosafos' interpretation there. The Tur and Rama adopt the same approach in siman 146.
