The word must be brought to light and a held person can say that he has bought and does not work for you:
And when did he acquire it? Ir Shushan omits the requirement that if the creditor proves when the debtor acquired it, there is no longer room to say it came by inheritance. Sema and Bach hold that if the creditor merely proves by purchase witnesses that the property once belonged to the debtor, we assume he bought it only later; but if he proves the debtor had established possession, then even if the purchaser proves a later purchase, perhaps the debtor had owned it earlier by inheritance and later reacquired it. Therefore one must establish when the acquisition occurred. In Tumim I wrote differently: even if the purchaser proves a later purchase, perhaps the debtor had originally inherited the property, sold it, and then repurchased it before the purchaser acquired it. Therefore witnesses must establish that the debtor first acquired it before selling it to this purchaser. No further proof is needed.
The first reasoning is primary. Sema limits this to land. Regarding movables he applies the presumption, 'Here they are found, here they were,' and therefore explains the later ruling in siman 250 that with a gift of movables effective from now until death, the heirs must prove the movables were not already in the donor's possession at the time of the gift. The Shach disagrees and says Rama there relies on what he wrote here, and the same dispute applies. This is correct. See Tumim. I wrote that one could even argue the reverse: with movables stronger proof may be required than with land, and I brought support from the Gemara.
