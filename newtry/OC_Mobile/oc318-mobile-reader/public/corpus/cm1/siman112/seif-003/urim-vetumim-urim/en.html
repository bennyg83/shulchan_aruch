That they acquired property afterward. See Tumim. Why is it not enough to prove that these movables and assets were not inherited but were acquired? The reason is that regarding movables we may more readily say, 'Here found, here they were.' Therefore proof of purchase is not enough; one must establish that the acquisition occurred afterward. This siman speaks of movables, as appears later in siman 250. I also discussed Mechaber's apparent inconsistency: here he cites two views without deciding; in siman 250 he rules that the heirs must prove the property was not already owned at the time of the gift, while above in siman 104:5 he treats movables as though acquired afterward and divides among creditors. I suggested, with difficulty, a distinction between a gift recipient and a lien claimant. All this concerns movables. With money, everyone agrees we presume the money entered his hands only later, because people ordinarily spend and replace money rather than preserve the same coins.
