And the later creditor claims, etc. The Tur likewise writes this. The Sema explains that the later creditor does not claim that the debtor acquired the property between the two loans and that it belongs to him alone, as in siman 104, because such a claim is unusual, namely that the property never belonged to the debtor at the earlier time. His meaning is only that people do not commonly make such a claim. If it is made, however, it is legally effective, as the Bach states expressly. My revered teacher wrote that the Talmud and authorities speak only of one who borrowed twice and then acquired property, in which case the creditors divide. Where he borrowed, acquired property, and then borrowed again, the property appears to be encumbered to the first creditor. We therefore need not adopt the Sema's strained explanation. Above in siman 104, where both documents contain after-acquired-property clauses, the same law should apply. Even where neither contains such a clause, at least the creditors should divide. See also above, siman 104, seif katan 14. The Bach asks why the later creditor is not believed through a migo that he could have claimed the entire property. According to my teacher's explanation there is no question. Even otherwise, the question is not difficult. In the familiar case of two people holding a garment, where one says, 'It is all mine,' and the other says, 'Half is mine,' the first is not believed through a migo that he could have claimed all of it under a different formulation. All the answers given there by Tosafot, Nimukei Yosef, and the other commentators apply here. It is also forced to say that the Sema refers to a case where both creditors seek to collect from a purchaser. But the Tur already rules that in such a case neither may remove the property from the purchaser. If so, and if the present passage concerned collection from a purchaser, the property would belong entirely to the later creditor, since the earlier creditor cannot collect while the later creditor can. Examine this carefully.
