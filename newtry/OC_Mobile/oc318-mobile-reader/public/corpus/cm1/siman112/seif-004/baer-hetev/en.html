From both of them. The Tur also writes this. The reason they do not state that the later creditor claims that the debtor acquired the property between the two loans and that it therefore belongs exclusively to him, as the Tur and Shulchan Aruch write in siman 104, is that this is an inferior claim. He would have to assert not only that the property never previously belonged to the debtor, but also that the debtor acquired it specifically during the interval between the two loans. Sema. The Shach explains that the Sema means only that people do not ordinarily make such a claim, but if the later creditor does make it, the claim is valid. This is also explicit in the Bach, seif 5; see there. My revered teacher wrote that we find in the language of the Gemara and poskim only the case of one who borrowed twice and then acquired property, in which case they divide it. But if he borrowed, acquired property, and then borrowed again, it appears that the property is pledged to the first creditor. If so, there is no need for the Sema's strained explanation. See above in the Tur and Shulchan Aruch, siman 104, concerning a case where an after-acquired property clause was written to both. The same rule should apply where it was written to neither, or at minimum they should divide. Thus far my revered teacher. See what I wrote above, siman 104, seif katan 15. The Bach asks why the later creditor should not be believed by virtue of a migo that he could have claimed that the entire property was his. According to my revered teacher's explanation there is no difficulty. Even apart from that there is no difficulty, for this is no better than two people holding a garment where one claims only half, and he is not believed by a migo that he could have claimed all of it, as Tosafot, Nimukei Yosef, and the other commentators explain. All the answers given there apply here as well. See there.
And some say. The Rama does not write here, as he did in the two preceding seifim, that the primary ruling follows the first opinion, even though the Tur treats the cases alike. This must be because in those earlier cases the real property is left in the possession of the purchaser or heir, who already holds it, whereas here neither party is in possession of the real property. The Darkei Moshe explicitly distinguishes the cases for this reason and writes that this appears to be the primary ruling. Sema.
