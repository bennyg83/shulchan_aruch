[Shulchan Aruch] And neither document contains an after-acquired-property clause. Note: The same law applies even if he wrote such a clause for both creditors. Perhaps the Mechaber stated the case this way to teach a greater novelty, lest we say that in such circumstances the second creditor should be believed by a migo that the debtor acquired the property in the interval between the two loans, in accordance with the Bach’s difficulty cited by the Shach. According to the Shach, however, who holds that if the debtor acquired the property in the interval they divide it, this explanation is difficult. One must therefore resort to the Perishah’s explanation, that there is some novelty in teaching that even when he did not write an after-acquired-property clause for either creditor, the cited opinion nevertheless places the property in the presumptive right of the first. This is because, had he written such a clause for both, the first creditor’s claim would be stronger than the second’s from two directions: whether the debtor acquired the property before the second loan or acquired it between the two loans, the first creditor would have priority.
