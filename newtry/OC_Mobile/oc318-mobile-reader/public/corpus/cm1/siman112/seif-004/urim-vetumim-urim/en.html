And the later creditor claims, etc. Why does he not claim that the debtor acquired the land between the first and second loans, in which case it would belong entirely to the second creditor? Sema calls that an inferior claim. This is strained. If the first loan was in year one, the second in year three, and collection occurs in year four, why is acquisition during the two years between loans less likely than acquisition in the single year afterward? The principal explanation is as the Shach writes and as I wrote above in siman 104: according to these authorities, where one borrowed without an after-acquired-property clause, then acquired property, then borrowed again, the first creditor's lien still reaches the acquired property. The later creditor therefore gains only by claiming the property was acquired after both loans.
