And some disagree. See Baer Heitev in the name of the Shach, and Responsa Mishkenot Yaakov, siman 72, which discusses this dispute. See also Responsa Chavot Yair, siman 230, concerning Reuven who borrowed from Shimon and accepted by kinyan all the obligations customarily written in a deed, including a lien on after acquired property. Before the deed was written, Reuven found a house being sold cheaply and wished to buy it for resale at a profit. He told the witnesses to the kinyan and the scribe that he retracted from the prospective lien on after acquired property, and Shimon was informed. Shimon then told Reuven: if so, return my money. Reuven agreed, but delayed for several weeks. In the meantime Reuven bought the house and resold it without public announcement, and later became insolvent. Shimon sought to collect the house from the purchaser. Chavot Yair rules that Shimon may collect from the purchaser even apart from the kinyan, as explained at the end of siman 39. Reuven's attempted retraction from the lien on after acquired property is ineffective, for the Shach in siman 112 rules like the Ramban that he cannot retract. Nor did Shimon's willingness to cancel the kinyan constitute an express waiver. His statement, "then return my money," meant only that the kinyan would be void when the money was actually returned, not beforehand. This applies where the deed had not yet been written, and even if it was written but had not yet reached Shimon. If, however, Shimon later accepted a deed that omitted the after acquired property clause and remained silent, that silence constitutes waiver. See there.
