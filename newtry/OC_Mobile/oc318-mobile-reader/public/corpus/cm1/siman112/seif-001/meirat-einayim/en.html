He may retract so long as he has not yet acquired it. The Maggid Mishneh in chapter 20 of Hilchot Malveh VeLoveh writes this in the name of the Rashba, and Darkei Moshe cites it in siman 104. The Rashba proves it from the case of one who borrowed from two lenders, wrote both a clause for subsequently acquired property, and only afterward acquired property. The property is encumbered to both and they divide it. If he could not retract the first commitment, it would have been encumbered entirely to the first lender, because just as he could not retract the whole commitment he should not be able to retract half of it. But once the property has already come into existence, all agree that he cannot retract, and the first lender acquires the lien from the original undertaking. Therefore, when he borrowed from one and then another and only afterward acquired property, we say that both divide because by writing the same clause to the second lender before acquiring the property, he showed his intention to stand by his undertaking to both, so that when the property is acquired they share. The "some disagree" view is cited by Nimukei Yosef in the name of the Ramban in chapter Chezkat HaBatim in the discussion of the cow and garment. Nimukei Yosef also cites the Rashba's opinion there and in chapter Eizehu Neshech. I have recorded it in siman 60 and again below in siman 209.
