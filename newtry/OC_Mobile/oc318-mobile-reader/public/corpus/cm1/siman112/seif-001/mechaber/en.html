A creditor does not collect from encumbered property that the borrower acquired only after the loan unless it was expressly included in the lien. This siman contains 5 seifim.
A creditor may collect from encumbered property only if the borrower owned the property at the time of the loan and later sold or gave it away. If the borrower acquired the property only after borrowing and then sold it, that property was not automatically encumbered to the creditor and cannot be collected from the purchasers. While such later acquired property remains in the borrower's own possession, however, the creditor may collect from it even though the borrower acquired it after the loan. If the borrower encumbered both property that he then owned and property that he would later acquire, the lien also attaches to what he later acquires. If he subsequently acquires and sells such property, the creditor may collect it from the purchaser. This applies specifically where the later acquired property was expressly included in the lien. If the document did not state that future acquisitions were included, they are not. Rama: Some say that since we rule that omission of standard liability language is treated as a scribal error, anything that ordinarily should be included in the lien is treated as though omitted by scribal error, including a clause covering later acquired property. However, if the document expressly states only some forms of liability and does not mention later acquired property, some say we no longer treat the omission as a scribal error. Since the writer specified some items, what he wrote is included and what he omitted is excluded. Some say that even where the borrower expressly encumbered later acquired property, he may retract that commitment as long as he has not yet acquired the property. Others disagree.
