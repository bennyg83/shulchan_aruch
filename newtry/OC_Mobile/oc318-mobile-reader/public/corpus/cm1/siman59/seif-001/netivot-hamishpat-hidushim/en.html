In it. When one party is certain and the other uncertain, certainty prevails because the ordinary burden of proof rule does not apply in the same manner. This is true even if the document contains a credibility clause, since the lender himself says he does not know. If the lender holds collateral he must return it. If the borrower too is uncertain, the lender collects without an oath. If the borrower dies, the lender likewise does not collect where the lender himself is uncertain, unless the matter was one the lender could not reasonably have known, such as where he appointed an agent and did not give the agent the document and is unsure whether the borrower paid the agent. In that case he may collect from the borrower's heirs. If the borrower is alive and demands return of the document, the lender must return it. If the borrower died and the heirs demand the document, the lender need not return it, but must leave it with beit din lest he later die and someone collect upon it. If the borrower admits that the debt was not paid but claims the lender owes him an offsetting amount and the lender is uncertain, the lender swears that he does not know and collects the document debt. If the lender dies, his heirs take the heirs' oath and collect. But if the heirs admit that payment occurred and do not know whether it was partial or complete, they collect nothing.
And he collects. Even from encumbered property, but specifically from purchasers who bought before he said, "I was paid." From purchasers who bought afterward he cannot collect, because they may say, "We relied on your statement." It is possible that so long as the document remained in the lender's hand, purchasers should have been cautious. See Beurim. Once beit din has already ruled that the document must be taken from him and returned to the borrower, he cannot later say, "I remembered." If the borrower never demanded return of the document, the lender may later say, "I remembered," even if the document is no longer in his possession, provided he proves it was destroyed by accident. Likewise, heirs who deposited the document with beit din may later say they remembered. If someone has an uncertain oral claim and beit din exempted the defendant from an oath, the claimant may later say, "I remembered," and impose the oath. If the lender is unsure whether he may test the borrower by suing in the hope that the borrower will admit, Baal HaTerumot rules that if the document was not found among the torn documents he may test him, but if it was among the torn documents it is forbidden even to test him.
