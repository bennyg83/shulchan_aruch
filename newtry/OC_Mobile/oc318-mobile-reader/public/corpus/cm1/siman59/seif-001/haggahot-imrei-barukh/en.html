(Netivot HaMishpat, siman 59, seif katan 2) And beit din ruled that the document must be returned, etc. Note: At first glance Baal HaTerumot's ruling that when the borrower demands return of his document the lender must return it requires study. Regarding the obligation to return the document, even if the borrower claims certainty and the lender is uncertain, the lender's uncertainty is like "I do not know whether I became obligated," because if he was not paid he has no duty to return the document. Nor can the obligation be based simply on "do not keep wrongdoing in your tent" because of the concern that the document may later fall before orphans who could collect with it. If that were the reason, beit din should require return even without the borrower's demand, as the Shach states below in siman 61, seif katan 16. Why would the court not itself tell the lender not to retain wrongdoing? Perhaps the reason is instead that this is a case of one person benefiting while the other suffers no loss, and we compel against the trait of Sodom. If so, even after beit din has ruled that the document be returned, this is not a final adjudication establishing that the borrower was definitely truthful. In truth his certain claim cannot extract property from the lender who is uncertain. Rather, because in any event the lender cannot collect with this document, he must return it for one of the reasons above. This case therefore cannot be compared to the Maharshal's case where one says, "I do not know whether I paid," and beit din obligates payment. According to those authorities who hold that the paper of the document belongs to the borrower and is held by the lender only as a pledge, these reasons are unnecessary. The case is then exactly like siman 72:14 regarding a pledge where the lender says he does not know, in which case he must return the pledge because one cannot retain another's property on a doubtful claim. This is Responsa Rashba, siman 1041, and in siman 977 the Rashba adds that the same applies to a debt document. The Rashba follows his view in his novellae to Ketubot 85 that the body of the document belongs to the borrower and is merely pledged to the lender. Those novellae, though attributed to the Rashba, may in fact be the Ramban's. According to this author and the Ketzot HaChoshen in siman 66, however, the document in the lender's hands is like a gift on condition that it be returned when the borrower pays. On that approach, if the lender is uncertain whether payment occurred, strict law may not obligate him to return the document. Yet the Ketzot's foundation is that a conveyancing document is required, while the novellae attributed to Rashba or Ramban mention that point and nevertheless say that the document is only a pledge. See what this author writes below in siman 66, seif katan 31. The Ketzot's explanation of the Rashba there is forced, for the Rashba in siman 977 clearly compares the paper of the document to an ordinary pledge.
