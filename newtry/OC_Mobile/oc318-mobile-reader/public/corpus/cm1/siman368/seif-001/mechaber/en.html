The law of one who rescues or purchases from Jewish or non-Jewish robbers. It contains one seif.
One who rescues property from Jewish robbers acquires it because owners ordinarily despair, unless he knows they did not. One who rescues from non-Jewish robbers or tax collectors must return it because owners ordinarily do not despair, unless they actually did. The distinction is that owners know non-Jewish courts recover property from robbers even on weak evidence and inference. Rama: Ordinary theft creates a presumption of despair even when the thief is non-Jewish, so a purchaser would ordinarily acquire through despair and transfer of possession unless it is known that the owner did not despair. Nevertheless, custom is to return all stolen property and not deviate. There is no distinction between theft and robbery: in every case the owner reimburses the purchaser and takes his property.
