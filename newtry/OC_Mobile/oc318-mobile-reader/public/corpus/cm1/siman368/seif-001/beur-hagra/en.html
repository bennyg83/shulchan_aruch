And because of what is called. Same name:
Ordinarily, etc. The authorities rule like Ulla. According to Rabbah, the Sages would never recognize despair in ordinary robbery, yet the sugyot repeatedly assume despair by a robber. Raza and Rosh therefore rule like Ulla. Raza adds that this applies to a Jewish thief, but with a non-Jew even ordinary theft does not create presumed despair. 'Bandit' in these passages means a thief, not necessarily an armed robber. Tosafot, Mordechai, and Rashba agree, and the halakha follows the first Tanna. Tur cites one explanation, then claims that Rosh implies the reverse, and Rama follows him. Many authorities reject Tur's inference as a serious error, because the sugyot clearly distinguish a Jewish thief from a non-Jewish thief. Rambam and Shulchan Aruch likewise distinguish them. A tax collector is different, because even where he is Jewish the owner does not ordinarily despair. See simanim 50, 81, and 369.
