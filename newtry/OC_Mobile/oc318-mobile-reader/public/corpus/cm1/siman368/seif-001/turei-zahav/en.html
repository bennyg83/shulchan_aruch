From the hands of Jewish bandits. This means a robber. I have already discussed this at length in siman 361, seif 2, and I explained there also the opinion cited here by the Tur, not as the Beit Yosef explains it here. See there. I also wrote there that the rescue discussed here is effected by appeasing the robber, and therefore constitutes a change of possession. This is necessarily so, for otherwise how could the rescuer acquire it from the robber? The Tur and the Mechaber explicitly rule at the end of siman 361 that if another person takes the object from the robber against the robber's will, it is as though he took it from the owner's house, and even after the owners have despaired he must return it so long as it remains intact. My father-in-law wrote in siman 369 that the present case concerns one who took it forcibly from the robber, but this is not correct. In that event there is no change of possession, and he must return it to the original owners, since despair alone does not effect acquisition.
