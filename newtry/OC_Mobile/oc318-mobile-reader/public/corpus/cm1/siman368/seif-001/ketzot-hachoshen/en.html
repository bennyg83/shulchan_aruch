An ordinary theft is presumed to involve despair. Maharshal argues that the dispute in the Gemara concerns a Jewish robber, because otherwise one could answer that the Mishnah speaks specifically of a Jew. The Shach rejects the proof. Maharshal's reasoning is persuasive. Even according to Rabbah, who says the dispute remains where actual despair is known, the Sages can still distinguish circumstances in which the owner's despair is genuine from those in which he inwardly hopes to recover through legal pressure. A Jewish robber can therefore present a different despair profile from a gentile or outlaw. The fact that actual despair was expressed does not erase every contextual distinction. Thus the sugya can indeed be read as concerning a Jewish robber.
