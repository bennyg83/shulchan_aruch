The document is not thereby disqualified. Responsa Shevut Yaakov, part 3, siman 182, discusses a son who, after his father's death, produced a debt document against the estate for four hundred Reichsthaler. The brothers argued that the document was more than fifteen years old, that the son had never demanded payment while his father was alive and wealthy although the son himself was extremely poor, and they produced letters in which the son had asked his father for gifts. Had a debt truly been owed, they argued, he would have demanded it rather than request charity. Shevut Yaakov answers that strictly under the law these circumstantial indications do not invalidate the document, as the Tur writes in the name of the Rosh. However, Responsa Rashdam, Choshen Mishpat, siman 367, indicates that in such cases beit din should impose a compromise close to the law. Shevut Yaakov had elsewhere explained that a compromise close to law means reducing by one third or adding one third. He therefore initially gave the holder of the document the choice to accept one third of his claim and release the heirs. If he refused within twenty-four hours and insisted on swearing and collecting the entire debt, the heirs would then have the option to pay two thirds in exchange for return of the document. If neither side accepted the compromise, the holder would take a stringent oath and the heirs would be obligated to pay in full. Even if one of the compromise paths is chosen, beit din should impose a general ban upon anyone who knows that his claim is false. See also Responsa Divrei Rivot, siman 109, where a compulsory compromise was likewise imposed on both parties regarding an old document, and he concludes that this is a judgment of peace. See also the following seif katan.
And likewise every old document. Netivot HaMishpat writes that the custom is not to collect an old document from heirs, and the borrower is believed to claim payment even if he says that he paid before the document became old. Likewise, heirs are exempt even if their father died before the document became old. A document without interest is considered old after three years; one bearing periodic profits or interest is considered old after six years. See Knesset HaGedolah, Hagahot Tur, note 13 and following, and Ateret Tzvi, seif katan 16.
