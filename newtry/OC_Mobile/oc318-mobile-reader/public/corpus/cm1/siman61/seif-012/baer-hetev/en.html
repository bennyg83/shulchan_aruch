His. The reason is that the borrower ordinarily pays the scribe's fee. If the lender paid it instead, that payment is itself treated as a small loan to the borrower, who must reimburse him. Nevertheless, the lender may not retain the debt document merely on account of the unpaid scribe's fee, as the Mechaber writes in siman 57, seif 1. Thus the Sema. The Shach writes that the Sema appears to understand that even if the lender tears the document or writes on it that it has been paid, he still may not retain the paper until the fee is paid. That does appear to follow the Mechaber there. The Shach, however, already disputed the Mechaber and proved that in such circumstances the lender may retain the physical paper until the borrower reimburses the scribe's fee. See there at length.
