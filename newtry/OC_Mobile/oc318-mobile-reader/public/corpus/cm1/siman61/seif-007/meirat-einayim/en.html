That he became obligated to him for the entire amount, etc. In Perishah I explained the apparent facts. Reuven and Shimon were partners in several dealings. Shimon obligated himself to Reuven in a document up to one thousand gold coins. The document stated that whenever their accounting later showed an amount remaining in Shimon's hands for Reuven, and Shimon wrote and signed that amount in his own handwriting, Shimon would be obligated for that amount under the witness-signed document so that Reuven could collect even from purchasers. They wrote the cap of one thousand because they knew the amount could not exceed that, while the actual obligation would be whatever lesser amount the later accounting showed. The question was from what time Reuven could collect: from the first of Nisan, the date of the original document, or only from the later date when Shimon's handwritten acknowledgment was authenticated by beit din, for example the first of Iyar. It was obvious that he could not collect from the date of the handwritten acknowledgment itself before authentication, because an unauthenticated handwritten note cannot create such rights. The answer was that he collects from the date of the original document. From that date purchasers should have refrained from buying Shimon's property because the obligation was publicized then. They cannot say that until the later writing clarified the precise amount there was no definite debt. They should have been concerned that Shimon might immediately give the handwritten accounting to Reuven, since such handwriting creates no public notice by itself. Even beit din authentication of handwriting does not create the kind of publicity upon which purchasers may safely rely. For this reason a creditor does not collect from purchasers merely through the borrower's authenticated handwriting, as explained in siman 69.
