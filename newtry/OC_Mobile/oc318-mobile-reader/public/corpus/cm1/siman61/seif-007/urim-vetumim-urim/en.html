That he became obligated to him, etc. Meaning that Reuven regularly extends merchandise on credit to Shimon in small amounts. Reuven does not wish to continue lending without a document creating a lien on property, but taking a new document each time would be burdensome. He therefore takes a document for one thousand gold coins, estimating that his future extensions of credit will eventually reach that amount, and writes that Shimon is not yet obligated to pay because the money has not yet been lent. Each time Reuven later extends credit, Shimon gives him a written acknowledgment of the amount. Ultimately Reuven totals all those successive written acknowledgments and collects under the original document. This is the explanation given in Perishah, and it is more straightforward than Sema's interpretation that at the time the document was written on the first of Nisan, the lender's money was already in the borrower's possession but the precise account had not yet been determined until later. That reading is strained. The first interpretation is clear and correct. See Tumim for why this obligation may be collected from encumbered property and what difficulty remains because the amount was not fixed.
