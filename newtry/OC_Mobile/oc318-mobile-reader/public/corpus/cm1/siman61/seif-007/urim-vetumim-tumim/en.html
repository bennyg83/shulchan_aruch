For then publicity went forth, etc. The author of Mishneh LaMelech, chapter 11 of Hilchot Malveh, expresses surprise. Since there was no kinyan, for no kinyan is mentioned at all, by what mechanism could the lien take effect from the first of Nisan when the loan itself was not made until the twenty seventh of Nisan? In truth, according to the Rosh, who holds that witnesses create the rights through their signatures, there is no difficulty. What difference does it make that the loan took place later? Even according to the Mechaber, who did not rule like Abaye, there is nevertheless no difficulty. Once the document reached the lender on the first of Nisan, although the loan itself was later, if the borrower says that the lien should take effect from now, the document is valid and is not considered antedated. Rashi writes this explicitly in Ketubot 19b, s.v. "witnesses who are signed," and the Ran writes likewise at length. See above, siman 39, seif 13, where I discussed this at length. I am surprised that Mishneh LaMelech did not notice this point.
When a document was established, etc. According to the view that the problem is that the obligation is not written, the rule is straightforward. Even according to the view that the problem is that the amount is not fixed, the rule also works according to the Rosh in Perek Metziat HaIshah, who holds that where there is an upper limit, even if there is no lower fixed amount, it is still called a fixed obligation. Here the amount is capped at one thousand gold coins. But according to Tosafos in Ketubot 50b, s.v. "what advantage," who write explicitly that even where there is an upper limit, so long as there is no lower fixed amount it is not considered fixed, there would be no lien upon property here, because the amount might be less than one thousand gold coins. This ruling therefore requires study.
