We do not listen to him. See Baer Heitev. See Responsa Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat, siman 5, who distinguishes between the ruling here and that of siman 45, seif 3. Where the person himself signs, everything in the document binds him, even a statement making his field a boundary for another. Here, where only witnesses sign, only the actual matter they testify to binds him, and a boundary description does not cause him loss merely by their testimony. Urim, siman 61, likewise writes this from Responsa Rashba, siman 1256. Examination of that responsum indicates that the rule is especially applicable to a bride on her wedding day, where it is known that she did not read the document and it was not read to her; in an ordinary case the document may retain the force of a regular document even then. See there further concerning a community that claimed its obligation to pay its rabbi twenty gold coins weekly was limited to three years, while the rabbi's handwritten document stated that it continued forever. They claimed they signed without knowing or reading the contents, while the other side said they knew. Chatam Sofer ruled that their claim has no force, and even those who had not personally signed could not successfully object. See there.
