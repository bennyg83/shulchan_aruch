And he did not understand, etc. We do not listen to him. He should have said at the outset, 'I do not understand it; explain it to me so that I will know.' Silence in matters of this type is treated as acceptance of the obligation to observe what scribes ordinarily write in documents and ketubot, including their technical details. See above siman 45, seif 3.
The same applies to other textual details, etc., as explained nearby. Meaning, immediately afterward in seif 15 the Mechaber writes this. One might ask why Rama needed to state it here as well. The answer is that the nearby rule, that the language of the document is read precisely and we do not say, etc., could have been understood only as an instruction to interpret the writing precisely without worrying that the signer was unlearned. Had he expressly claimed, 'I did not understand,' one might have thought that we listen to him. Rama therefore teaches here that just as a husband who says he did not understand his wife's ketubah is not heard, the same applies to other technical formulations in legal documents.
