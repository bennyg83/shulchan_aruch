We do not listen to him. Even if it is known that he is an ignoramus and did not understand the document, since he remained silent and did not ask that its contents be explained, we say that he accepted whatever obligations were written in it. Rashba's responsum implies that this applies specifically to obligations running from the obligor to the beneficiary of the document. If the clause does not concern the beneficiary at all, such as where the obligor made the property a boundary marker for some third person, and he credibly claims that he did not understand the document, his claim can be heard, because the rationale that he accepted every obligation toward the beneficiary does not apply. This reconciles Rashba with Rosh's responsum cited here.
