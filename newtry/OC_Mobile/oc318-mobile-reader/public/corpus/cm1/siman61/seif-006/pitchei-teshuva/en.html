We do not listen to him. See Baer Heitev. See Responsa Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat, siman 5, who writes that there is a legal distinction between the ruling here and that of siman 45, seif 3. There, where the person himself signed the document, everything written in the document is included in his acceptance, even a statement identifying his field as a boundary for another person's property. Here, however, he himself did not sign and only witnesses signed. Therefore, only the substance of the matter to which the witnesses testify binds him, but a statement that his field serves as a boundary for another does not cause him loss merely through their testimony. Urim, siman 61, likewise writes this and derives it from Responsa Rashba, siman 1256. One who examines the responsum itself will see, however, that even this applies specifically to a bride on her wedding day, where it is known and presumed that she did not read the document and it was not read before her. In an ordinary case, perhaps even in such circumstances the document has the full force of any other document. This is the substance of Chatam Sofer's words. See there further concerning a community that claimed they had obligated themselves to pay their rabbi twenty gold coins per week only for three years, whereas a document written by the rabbi in his own handwriting stated that they had obligated themselves forever. They claimed this was written without their knowledge and that they signed without reading its contents, while the opposing party denied this and said they knew what was written. Chatam Sofer ruled that their claim has no substance, and even those community members whose signatures did not appear on the document cannot successfully object. See there.
