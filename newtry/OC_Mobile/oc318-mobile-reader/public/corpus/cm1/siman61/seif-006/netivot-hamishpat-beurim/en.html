See Sema, seif katan 17, through his statement that this applies specifically where there is a prohibition upon Jewish judges to adjudicate according to gentile law. His words are difficult to me. If the creditor himself is permitted to collect this money through gentiles, then necessarily the stipulation is effective, comparable to one who stipulates that the Sabbatical year shall not cancel his debt. If so, the judges too should be able to adjudicate accordingly. How can it be that the creditor is permitted to collect, while the judges are forbidden to rule that he may do so? See also Shach, seif katan 11, through his statement that it is obvious even according to Maharu"kh that this is so even where there was no established custom. This too is difficult to me, for how can a custom permit transgression of Torah law? The words of the Sema indicate that the essential point depends upon the debtor having accepted this condition upon himself. Since the debtor accepted it, there is no Torah prohibition in enforcing it. Rather, where there is no established custom, we interpret his undertaking as referring only to a case in which there will be no Jewish judge available. Where there is an established custom, we interpret his words in their ordinary and unrestricted sense, even where a Jewish judge is available. See the Sema there.
