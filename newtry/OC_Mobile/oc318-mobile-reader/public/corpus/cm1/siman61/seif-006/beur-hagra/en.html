A document. Bava Metzia 94a states that anyone who stipulates contrary to what is written in the Torah has made a void condition. Although the Gemara says that Rabbi Yehudah validates conditions in monetary matters, that means one may obligate himself where the Torah exempted him, but not stipulate to violate Torah law. This is like a condition excluding the law of overreaching. See Tosafot, Makkot 3b, s.v. Al Menat, citing Rashbam, and Bava Metzia 51b.
(Supplement) A document. The Sema writes that local custom is followed, or that an express clause is effective. His words are correct only where no Torah prohibition is violated. A condition cannot authorize violation of Torah law. One may obligate himself where the Torah exempted him, or exempt himself from a monetary duty, and custom may alter a monetary rule, but neither condition nor custom can permit what the Torah forbids. See siman 281, seif 4, in the Rama. The Taz already challenged the contrary view in siman 26, seif 2. (End of supplement.)
