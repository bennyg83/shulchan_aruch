See Responsa Chavos Yair, siman 194, who writes that the clauses our scribes customarily include in all documents, stating that the undertaking was made with a formal handshake, actual execution, and a severe ban, are effective to obligate the person under human law, but not to obligate him under Heavenly law. See what I write in siman 72, seif 17, seif katan 4. He further writes that the practice of some scribes to write that every receipt is invalid unless written upon the document itself, without informing the borrower of this clause, is contrary to Torah law. If such a clause was written and the borrower produces a receipt in the lender's handwriting and claims that he did not know of the condition, his claim is valid because such a stipulation is not ordinary. Moreover, even without this, the receipt should be effective, because the clause resembles 'Do not repay me except before so and so.' If the borrower repaid before others, then unless the lender can claim that the payment was applied to another debt, the payment is effective. See siman 70 and see there.
