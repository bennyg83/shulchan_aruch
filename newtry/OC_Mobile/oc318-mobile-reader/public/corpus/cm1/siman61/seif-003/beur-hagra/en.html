Who became obligated to swear. A woman is also made to swear, as stated in siman 96, derived from Kesubos, "She shall swear at the beginning and at the end." Even where they did not hear that he died, as there 107a, certainly this applies with respect to other claimants.
According to the enactment of the Geonim. They based it upon the oath that the item is not in his possession, as stated in Mordechai at the beginning of the third chapter of Bava Metzia.
(Collected note) If there is... This implies that even if he wishes to pay with land, she must swear under the enactment of the Geonim. So states Darkei Moshe at the beginning of siman 99. The Sema rejects this and says it means that he has no land either, but his words are difficult. The Rosh, responsa klal 39, seif 5, and the Tur conclude that otherwise the Sages accomplished nothing by their enactment, since everyone could say, "I do not wish to give movables or swear; collect land for the creditor." The Rashba, responsum 1026, likewise rules so, as cited by Darkei Moshe and Beit Yosef in siman 101, seif 7. Although the Sema rejected that proof by saying that the case involved a person known to possess assets, the Shach in siman 101, seif katan 5, already rejected that distinction. Sefer HaTerumos, sha'ar 4, part 1, however, writes that he is not made to swear, as do Baal HaItur and Mordechai at the beginning of HaMafkid according to their approach. Their view is that under Talmudic law one swears upon the assertion that he has nothing, based upon the law that one swears that the deposit is not in his possession, because where it initially was in his possession and liability attached, uncertainty does not discharge him. This differs from a case where perhaps he had no available funds when payment became due. A proof is brought from Pesachim 31a, "If he is wise..." Why could they not demand movables specifically? Evidently he claims he has none. This proof requires study: would the Sages teach a person to make a false claim in order to deprive orphans? Moreover, what is the Gemara's question in Kesubos 110a, "And further, let him collect...?" One could establish the Mishnah where it is known that he has movables. Must the Mishnah be restricted to one who is not presumed wealthy and makes a false claim? The earlier difficulty is answered by Tosafos there 92a, s.v. i. Mordechai in HaKosev and Hagahos Maimoniyos, siman 53, further cite the incident at 86a concerning one who concealed his assets and was not made to swear. This is the basis of siman 101, seif 1: "If he has no... even..." and likewise seif 10. The proof is not decisive, as Tosafos there, s.v. hahu, indicates. In any event, because he was discovered before the oath to be lying, they did not believe him, as stated there and in the Shulchan Aruch. Even according to Mordechai and Hagahos Maimoniyos, the Shach writes that their rule concerns the Talmudic oath, but there is no proof regarding the enactment of the Geonim. Thus the rulings of the Shulchan Aruch appear internally inconsistent. (End of note.)
