Seif 9, gloss. An old document. Regarding an old promissory note, the time measure stated in Misgeret HaShulchan is three or six years where the debtor was not in flight. If he had fled but there were also several years in which he was safely accessible, perhaps the ordinary three-year measure does not apply. A person who had once fallen financially is not ordinarily pressed immediately upon showing some improvement, and creditors often wait until it becomes publicly clear that he has recovered substantially. If an old note contains a clause of creditor credibility and the creditor gives a reasonable explanation for the delay, that credibility may remain effective even if the explanation is not fully proved and is somewhat remote. Without any explanation, it may be no better than siman 61:9, where even a stipulation that a particular defense will not be raised may not overcome the effect of extreme staleness. Perhaps unusually expansive language granting credibility "forever, even after payment" could indicate that age of the document is not to weaken it, though even then some minimal explanation for the delay should be required. An enactment concerning old debts may also operate differently against heirs. Even where they have a guardian, there is generally less urgency to sue them, particularly because their property is often not immediately available. Although it is proper not to delay unnecessarily, especially lest the case become one where the borrower died during the lender's lifetime, the mere passage of time against heirs does not create the same evidentiary force. If beit din has no strong circumstantial assessment, the burden of proof remains on the claimant.
