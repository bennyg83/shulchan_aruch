Likewise, a document that was not authorized to be written. This means that the witnesses wrote it on their own, without the debtor's knowledge. It is invalid under the rule, 'from their mouths, and not from their writing,' and no judgment may be rendered on its basis, as I explained at length above in siman 39, seif 3, seif katan 8. This is also implied by the Rosh, Bava Batra, chapter Get Pashut, siman 7. Regarding the draft of a document retained by the scribe, mentioned by the Mechaber, it appears to me that there is no dispute at all. The Beit Yosef, Darkei Moshe, and Bach write that the Rosh disagrees with the Rivash, as the Rama states here, but in my humble opinion they do not disagree. The Rivash discusses a case in which the debtor obligated himself before two witnesses for one hundred zuz by means of a document, but did not obligate himself that the scribe would write the document. The witnesses were merely to prepare a complete document. Therefore, the draft entered in the scribe's register has no status as a document, since it was not written for delivery to the creditor and was left with the scribe. The Rosh, by contrast, discusses one who obligated himself that the witnesses would record that he must execute, as of that day, a document containing every strengthening clause selected by the scribe, and he accepted this by kinyan before witnesses. If he then fails to do so, the witnesses' writing stands in place of the scribe's document, because they delivered their writing to the creditor. Study the Rivash and the responsum of the Rosh, and you will see that their words accord with this explanation. Nevertheless, both the Rosh and Rivash concern a case in which the debtor instructed them to write a document, so the document was authorized to be written. Where they wrote it without the debtor's knowledge, it is invalid under the rule, 'from their mouths, and not from their writing.' No judgment may be rendered upon it, and it is not even treated as oral testimony. Consider this carefully.
