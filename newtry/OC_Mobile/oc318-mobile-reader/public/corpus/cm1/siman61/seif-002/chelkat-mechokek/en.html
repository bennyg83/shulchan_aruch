Rather, with respect to all matters she is still like an arusah. This implies that he does not inherit her, does not acquire her found objects or earnings, and does not have rights to the fruits of her property, the four rights of a husband explained below in siman 69. Although above, siman 55:3, once the appointed time for marriage arrives the husband becomes obligated to support her and thereby acquires her earnings and found objects, and siman 56 states that once she is delivered to the husband's agents he inherits her even if she dies on the road before chuppah, Rambam may hold that all of this applies only where she is fit for chuppah and relations. If she is a niddah, delivery to the husband's agents is no stronger than the chuppah itself. Indeed, above in siman 55:3, where she became a niddah at the appointed time, he does not owe support. Still, Rambam's position is difficult. Ketubot 56a asks: if she entered chuppah but relations did not occur, does the affection of chuppah acquire the ketubah addition or only the affection of relations? Rav Ashi then asks: if she entered chuppah and became a niddah, assuming the affection of chuppah is sufficient, does this apply only to a chuppah fit for relations, or even to one unfit for relations? The Beit Yosef notes the method of the Geonim and Rambam that a question posed on an "if you say" premise treats the prior question as resolved in favor of that premise. But Rabin's first question cannot concern inheritance. When the father delivers her to the husband's agents, although she has not had relations, the husband already inherits her. Why then should Rav Ashi's second question be extended to all marital rights, including inheritance? The two questions were asked as a chain only regarding the ketubah addition. Nor can one say that delivery to the husband's agents is stronger than chuppah without relations. Ketubot 48b indicates the opposite: entering chuppah without relations is more clearly treated as nisuin than mere delivery to the husband's agents. Therefore, since he already inherits upon delivery to his agents, all the more so he should inherit after chuppah even without relations. I saw in Responsa Rosh, rule 54, siman 2, cited in the following gloss, that regarding a woman dangerously ill who was brought under chuppah, the Rosh ruled that the husband does not inherit her, comparing it to chuppah of a niddah. This suggests he then understood the matter like Rambam, and likewise interpreted delivery to the husband's agents as applying only when she was fit for relations. But in his rulings he later retracted and held that chuppah of a niddah is effective for other marital matters, and that the Gemara's doubt concerns only the ketubah addition. Responsa Rosh, rule 37, siman 1, likewise rules that from the moment she was secluded in his home he inherits her, although she was embarrassed to immerse and was not fit for relations. Darkei Moshe therefore rules like the later authorities that chuppah of a niddah is a valid chuppah, unlike Rambam. Accordingly, in practice, a widow who entered chuppah before Shabbat may immerse on Shabbat. He already acquired marital rights in her, including inheritance, earnings, and found objects, when she entered his domain. Her immersion does not create a new marital acquisition on her side. The concern is only from the husband's side, and the marital acquisition already took effect. Masat Binyamin, siman 91, raised this question. Especially according to Ri, where the groom is informed that she is not pure, as stated nearby in seif 2, the chuppah effects all marital rights. There is therefore no new forbidden acquisition created on Shabbat by her immersion.
