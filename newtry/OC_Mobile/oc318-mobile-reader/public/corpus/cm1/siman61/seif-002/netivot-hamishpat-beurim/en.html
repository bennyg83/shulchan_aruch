From their writing. Ketzot HaChoshen questions a responsum holding that a writing saying, 'So-and-so betrothed so-and-so,' signed by witnesses, is ordinary written testimony and therefore invalid. He argues from Rif and Nimukei Yosef concerning a kiddushin document that cannot safely be handed to the parties because of possible erasure. In my view the responsum is correct. The Sages deliberately did not create an ordinary evidentiary deed for kiddushin. The same reason they did not institute a date in kiddushin applies. To whom would the evidentiary instrument be delivered without creating risk of erasure, concealment, or misuse? If such a deed were established as ordinary proof of kiddushin, the same system would need reliable dating as well, because later divorce and subsequent kiddushin can make timing decisive. The absence of such an enactment shows that ordinary proof of the marriage act was not entrusted to a freestanding written testimonial document. The constitutive kiddushin deed is different. A document saying, 'You are hereby betrothed to me,' by which the man actually effects kiddushin, is itself a Torah instrument. Its witnesses do not testify that the kiddushin already happened. They attest that the instrument was prepared under the man's authority, and the actual kiddushin occurs through later delivery, ordinarily before delivery witnesses. In that sense it is like a get. The signed document can therefore have evidentiary force concerning its own valid preparation even though no separate documentary system was enacted to prove a past act of kiddushin. A writing produced later merely saying, 'So-and-so betrothed so-and-so,' is wholly different. Its entire function is to narrate a completed past event. That is ordinary testimony from writing and is not one of the constitutive documents recognized by Torah law. Kiddushin and divorce are not merely private monetary matters between two people. They create personal-status consequences against the world. Therefore ordinary self-authored or informally signed written proof cannot substitute for the required witness structure. This also explains why one should not reason from a get or kiddushin deed to every written statement. The Torah recognized the constitutive instrument itself. It did not thereby authorize a separate evidentiary certificate of a past marriage act.
