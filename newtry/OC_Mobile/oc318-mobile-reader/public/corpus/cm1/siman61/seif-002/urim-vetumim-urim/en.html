The witnesses erred. Meaning that the borrower never instructed them to write and deliver the deed. The witnesses mistakenly wrote it on their own and handed it to the lender. According to this view, such a writing lacks the status of a legal instrument. It is merely ordinary testimony put into writing, which is invalid under the requirement that testimony come "from their mouths" rather than from their writing. A true deed is different. Either Scripture itself gives formal deeds special validity, or the Sages validated them to facilitate lending. See what I wrote at length above in simanim 28 and 39.
However, some disagree. The Shach in seif katan 2 says there is no real disagreement. He explains that Rosh, the source of this view, speaks where the debtor obligated himself before witnesses, ordered them to write that obligation into a deed, and told them to deliver it to the lender. In such a case everyone agrees it is a fully valid deed. This explanation is strained. Responsa Rosh explicitly says that it is called a "memorandum document" because the witnesses write the matters delivered to them merely as a reminder for themselves. That is unlike the Shach's case, where the borrower expressly commanded delivery to the lender. Moreover, on the Shach's reading, what purpose would there be in speaking of two different writings? The correct conclusion is therefore as Mechaber presents it: Rosh and Rivash genuinely disagree. See what I wrote above in siman 39 concerning whether such a writing, made without the borrower's instruction, can nevertheless function to prove an oral loan.
