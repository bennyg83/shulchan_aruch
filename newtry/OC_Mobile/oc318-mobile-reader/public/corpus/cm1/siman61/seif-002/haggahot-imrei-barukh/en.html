(Netivot HaMishpat, seif katan 5) And there even an oral undertaking is discussed. Note: This requires study. An oral obligation is effective where a person received money and orally obligated himself to the lender and to anyone deriving rights through him. In such a case the obligation takes effect through the money received, on condition that he be obligated to the owner of the money and to anyone coming through him. The rule here, however, concerns a document in which a person obligates himself to whoever produces it. Such a case can exist even where he received no money from anyone and voluntarily created a new obligation solely through the document. Since that obligation is created only through the document, we must say that the first person who acquires the document acts as agent for whoever later produces it. The Rosh's responsum explicitly says that although the witnesses who acquired the document on behalf of the eventual bearer did not know him, the acquisition nevertheless works, as in Gittin, and it is considered as though the obligor said, "I obligate myself to you and to anyone who comes through you." This directly contradicts the Mechaber's explanation here. The Rosh's words further imply that even where the borrower received money and the lender refused to lend unless he obligated himself to any bearer of the document, the later bearer would have no right without the rule that one may act beneficially for a person in his absence.
