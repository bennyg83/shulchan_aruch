Not as an asmachta, etc. See siman 42:10. Such language is written in order to strengthen the document holder, so that the document is construed in a manner favorable to him and not as a mere boilerplate form. See siman 113, where the Tur explains 'not as forms of documents' to mean that it was written for immediate collection, not merely because documents are commonly drafted that way.
It applies to all the general clauses at the beginning of the transaction. Beit Yosef cites this responsum of Rav Hai at the end of seif 3. It appears to mean that a clause accepting 'all rights' applies only to general rights and matters that are reasonably understood to have been within the parties' contemplation, without which the transaction would not be complete. The phrase 'at the beginning of the transaction' means that when a person undertakes a debt document he ordinarily has those essential matters in mind from the outset. In siman 71:22 Beit Yosef cites the same material with a slightly different wording, but the meaning is the same. There he also cites Ritva in similar terms.
Up to double. Accurate editions of the Tur read 'up to double.' It appears that only where he accepted so extreme a liability is it treated as an asmachta, similar to 'if I do not perform I will pay one thousand zuz.' But an ordinary undertaking to reimburse actual expenses is not an asmachta. It falls under the rule that monetary conditions are binding. The Rosh's responsum implies this, and the Tur cites it in siman 71:22; Rama also cites it there. Darkei Moshe writes that an undertaking to pay expenses is not an asmachta and wonders at the Rosh's contrary formulation. According to my reading there is no difficulty. The Rosh calls it asmachta only where the person accepted liability up to double the amount. Even if he actually incurred that much expense, one can say the obligor never contemplated such an unusually large sum and wrote it only in the manner of asmachta. See Hagahot Darkei Moshe. Also see the end of siman 14, where the Tur and Mechaber say that one need reimburse expenses only from the time of refusal onward when no express undertaking existed. If he expressly undertook the expenses, the stipulation governs.
See below, siman 207, etc. There the Tur and Mechaber expressly rule in seif 18 that writing 'not as an asmachta' does not help where the instrument is in fact plainly an asmachta.
