At the end of the siman the Tur writes: and for this purpose it constitutes proper testimony, etc. This is difficult, because the wording implies that there is at least some difference between them concerning the accounting that he now wishes to make. If so, why should he not be believed by a migo that he could have said, "I was only jesting with you," which would be effective if he made that claim, as stated immediately before? One may answer that since witnesses testify that they already conducted an accounting, we regard it as established that they left no further account unresolved at that time. His migo is therefore a migo against witnesses. When the Rosh writes that he is not precise in the accounting, one could otherwise ask that even a complete denial should not help because witnesses testify that an accounting was conducted. What could he gain by claiming that he was jesting, when the Tur discusses that claim only afterward and he has not actually made it? It therefore appears that the meaning is this: had he denied the admission, we would advance on his behalf the claim that he spoke merely to avoid appearing wealthy, as explained in siman 32, seif 7. Since he does not deny it, we do not advance that claim for him, and he himself has not made it.
