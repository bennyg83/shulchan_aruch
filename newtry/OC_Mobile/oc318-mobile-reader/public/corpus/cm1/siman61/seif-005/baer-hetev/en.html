As an asmachta. See above, siman 42, seif 10, where this wording is used so that the holder of the document has the stronger position. The Tur in siman 113 explains that the phrase "not like standard forms of documents" means that the instrument was written for immediate collection rather than merely because such language commonly appears in standard forms. Thus the Sema.
General clauses. The Sema writes that this appears to mean only general provisions and matters reasonably presumed to have been intended, without which the transaction recorded in the document could not be completed. The Shach disagrees. He explains that the phrase refers to all general warranty clauses mentioned at the beginning of the instrument. Likewise, if a document states, "I accepted the credibility provisions of this document upon myself like all such documents," it refers to all standard credibility clauses, not to warranty clauses. Shach.
That are specified. Meaning that even if local practice is to include a credibility clause in every document, perhaps this borrower did not intend to include it. This applies where it is not known that the borrower was aware of the custom. If he knew the custom, however, the clause is treated as written even if omitted. See the end of siman 42. Shach.
The double amount. The Sema writes that only where the obligor accepted an unusually severe penalty of this sort is the undertaking considered an asmachta, comparable to "if I leave the field fallow and do not work it," etc. A simple undertaking to reimburse expenses is not an asmachta; rather, ordinary monetary conditions are binding. See the end of siman 14. The Shach adds that if the claimant had merchandise ready to purchase and argues that he would have earned profit from it, then even if the document states that the obligor must pay "all losses" he causes, the lost profit is not recoverable.
