See Responsa Chavos Yair, siman 194, who writes that the clauses our scribes customarily include in all documents, stating that the undertaking was made with a formal handshake, actual execution, and a severe ban, are effective to obligate the person under human law, but not to obligate him under Heavenly law. See what I write in siman 72, seif 17, seif katan 4. He further writes that where scribes write that every receipt is invalid unless written upon the document itself, without informing the borrower, this is contrary to Torah law. If the borrower later produces a receipt in the lender's handwriting and claims that he did not know of the condition, his claim is valid, since such a clause is unusual. Even otherwise the receipt is effective, because it resembles a condition that repayment be made only before specified witnesses. If he repays before others, the payment is effective unless the lender can claim that it was applied to another debt. See siman 70 and see there.
