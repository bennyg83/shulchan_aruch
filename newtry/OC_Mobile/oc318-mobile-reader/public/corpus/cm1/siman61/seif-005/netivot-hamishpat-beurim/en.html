For example, credibility. See Shach. The case is where a document comes before us and contains no credibility clause. We do not say that the clause was omitted by scribal error. Scribes customarily write credibility expressly and do not rely on an oral understanding. The doctrine of scribal error is presumed for responsibility clauses, not for every omitted advantage. But where it is independently known that the obligor made an unqualified kinyan authorizing the writing of a document, his intention ordinarily includes the standard credibility clause that scribes customarily insert. Urim VeTumim states this as well. The distinction is between reconstructing a missing clause from the face of an already completed document and determining the scope of a known general authorization to draft the customary instrument.
