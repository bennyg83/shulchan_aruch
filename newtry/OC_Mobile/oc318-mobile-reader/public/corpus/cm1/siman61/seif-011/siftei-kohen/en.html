For violating a prohibition. Namely, the verse, ‘Do not let injustice dwell in your tent.’ It appears that this applies even where the borrower remains silent and does not demand the document, or where the lender wishes to return it but continually postpones doing so from day to day. The ruling teaches that the lender is placed under a ban until he returns it immediately. For if the case were one in which the borrower demands the document and the lender refuses altogether to return it, there would be no need to invoke the prohibition derived from the Writings. The lender would in any event be obligated to return the borrower’s document so that he could not collect from him a second time. Necessarily, the case is one in which the lender does not provide a receipt, for if he provides a receipt there is no violation of ‘Do not let injustice dwell in your tent,’ as I proved above in siman 57. Alternatively, perhaps even where he provides a receipt there remains a violation here. Although I proved in siman 57 that there is no violation, that was where the receipt is written on the document itself, or where part of the debt remains outstanding so that the lender must necessarily retain the document. Here, however, where he has no need at all to retain the document, retaining it is called injustice, since the receipt may be lost. Why should he keep the document for no purpose?
