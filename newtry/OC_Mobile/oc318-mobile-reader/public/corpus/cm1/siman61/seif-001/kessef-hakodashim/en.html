Not as an asmachta. If a document states that it was executed in every manner most effective under the laws of Chazal, and as though it contained every clause customarily effective in documents, perhaps that does not remove a concern of asmachta. The Shach writes that customary boilerplate whose particulars were unknown to the obligor may not be included in his consent. The Sema also leaves unresolved whether merely agreeing that an undertaking should not be treated as an asmachta is enough. Nowadays civil law may require the litigant himself to sign the document in addition to the witnesses to the kinyan sudar. This has an additional advantage under the Shach in siman 42. An unlearned person who does not know the technical clauses of documents is not ordinarily presumed to accept every enhancement contained in standard forms. But where he personally signs after a lengthy document has been written out, even the Shach would agree that he considered and accepted everything written. With today's lengthy documents, even the most unlearned person knows that many legal-strengthening clauses are included, and by accepting a kinyan sudar to execute the customary long document, he accepts them. If one scribe then omitted certain clauses concerning credibility, it appears that the creditor does not lose those rights where the witnesses testify that the kinyan was made to execute the customary full document.
