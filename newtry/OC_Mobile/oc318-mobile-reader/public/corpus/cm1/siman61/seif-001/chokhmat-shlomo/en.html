Seif 4. A document stating that whoever produces it may collect. The Sema writes that we do not say that because such a stipulation is ineffective for a non-Jew due to a prohibition, it is ineffective in general. It is not immediately obvious what prohibition he means. In my opinion he refers to lo techanem, the prohibition against granting a gratuitous benefit to a non-Jew. If this is a gratuitous obligation, the point is plain: by binding himself for nothing to a non-Jew, he violates that prohibition. Even if the document represents an actual loan, the non-Jew did not lend the money. If we say he comes only through the rights of the original Jewish lender, then he should require an authorization from that lender. A clause allowing any bearer to collect instead creates a direct obligation to the bearer himself, independent of agency from the original creditor. Consequently, when that bearer is a non-Jew, the debtor has gratuitously subjected himself to him, and the prohibition of lo techanem applies. This, in my opinion, is the Sema’s meaning, rather than the strained explanation offered by Netivot HaMishpat.
