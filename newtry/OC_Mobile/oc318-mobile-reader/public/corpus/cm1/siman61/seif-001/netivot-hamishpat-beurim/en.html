The intent is only concerning the document. See Shach, seif katan 1, through his words, "the intent is only concerning the entire document". The word "entire" is a scribal error and should read "and so forth." One must understand what Reuven is claiming. If he admits his signature, what novelty is there? Even in a document signed by witnesses, an admission of this kind is certainly effective, because even without any document his own admission would bind him. If he denies his signature, the document certainly requires authentication, and once it is authenticated he can no longer deny it. What then is the novelty? If he claims payment, trust, or some other defense, in any event he is believed against his own handwriting, as explained in siman 69. It therefore appears that the novelty is that he cannot say, "I intended only to jest, because I knew that this document was ineffective," treating it like an oral admission made without witnesses. The ruling teaches that this is a valid document. In an analogous document signed by witnesses, however, even if he admits making it, he can say that he intended only to jest if he made the admission before disqualified witnesses and told them to write, for such a writing has no legal effect.
