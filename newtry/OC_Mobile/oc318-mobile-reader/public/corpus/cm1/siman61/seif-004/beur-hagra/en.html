(Collected note) A document... See Beer HaGolah, seif katan 7, and the Sema. Their words are correct, for any monetary condition is valid, contrary to the Shach. Their proof from siman 103, seif 7, is difficult, because there the issue is a stipulation to contravene Torah law; even there, the language can be interpreted to mean, "if no judge can be found." See further in this siman, seif 6, in the Sema there, and siman 26, seif katan 11. But the matter does not fundamentally depend upon whether authorization was expressly waived. The Shulchan Aruch itself writes explicitly "without authorization." Even if the document says simply, "whoever produces it," any monetary condition is valid. In the responsum cited there, the reason was that such wording was routinely inserted merely for reinforcement, and clauses of credibility were likewise commonly inserted without the borrower's instruction, as discussed in siman 71, seif 14. Therefore the local custom was not to collect without authorization unless the document expressly stated "even without authorization," because that wording was not customarily inserted on the scribe's own initiative. Thus when the Shulchan Aruch writes, "if there is no...," the basis is necessarily the local custom of writing such clauses without the borrower's instruction. But if he instructed the witnesses to write it, the condition remains valid despite custom, as Tosafos to Bava Metzia 83a, s.v. hasocher, and the Gemara there 83b establish, as explained at the beginning of siman 331. Although in siman 71 it says, "if the witnesses...," that qualification is unnecessary here because the custom itself is established. (End of note.)
