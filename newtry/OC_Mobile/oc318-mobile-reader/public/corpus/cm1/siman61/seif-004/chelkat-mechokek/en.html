A sick woman who is dangerously ill. In a responsum the Rosh ruled that such a chuppah is ineffective for two reasons. First, it resembles chuppah of a niddah. According to the later authorities who rule that chuppah of a niddah is valid, the same should apply to a sick woman, and indeed her case is stronger, since even a sick woman may be physically capable of the minimal act of relations, unlike a niddah, whom her husband may not even touch, as explained in Yoreh Deah 195:17. Although one could distinguish that a niddah is expected to become permitted, while this woman is in danger of death, we nevertheless rule that most sick people recover. Moreover, Yevamot 27b teaches that chuppah can have legal effect even with a woman forbidden to the man, such as a widow to a Kohen Gadol, whom he brought under chuppah without prior kiddushin or relations; the chuppah disqualifies her from her father's terumah although she can never become fit for him. The same should therefore apply to a dangerously ill woman. The Rosh's second reason was based on an assessment of intent. The case involved Reuven, who left land to his daughter with a provision that if she died before marriage the land would go to charity. When she became deathly ill, her relatives brought her bed under the chuppah so that her husband would inherit and the land would not pass to charity. The Rosh ruled that we follow Reuven's clear intent to benefit his soul through charity rather than allow his heirs to circumvent it. That reasoning itself implies that without the charitable provision her husband would inherit her. It is therefore difficult that the Rama rules here that chuppah of a dangerously ill woman is not a valid chuppah while Darkei Moshe accepted the later authorities that chuppah of a niddah is valid. See the Bach at the end of siman 56, who distinguishes the sickness discussed by Mordechai, where the husband inherits, from the dangerous illness discussed by the Rosh, where he does not. He did not take into account that according to our accepted rule that chuppah of a niddah is effective for inheritance, the Rosh's case should also result in inheritance, as explained above.
