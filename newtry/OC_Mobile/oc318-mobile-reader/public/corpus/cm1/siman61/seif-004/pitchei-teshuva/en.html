Whether a Jew or a non-Jew. The Sema explains that we do not say that because the condition cannot take effect for a non-Jew due to a prohibition, it should also fail for a Jew. Dagul Merevavah comments that he does not know what prohibition the Sema has in mind. Rather, the reason the condition does not take effect for a non-Jew is that the witnesses cannot acquire on the non-Jew's behalf. Acquisition for another is based on agency, and there is no agency for a non-Jew. This is explicit in Responsa Rosh, rule 68, siman 7, and in the Beit Yosef here. See Tumim and Netivot HaMishpat.
