(Shach, seif katan 13) And an even greater point appears. Note: This is explicit in Responsa Rivash, siman 210, cited in seif 2 regarding a mere memorandum. Where the writing itself explains the matter, the statement of what he admitted is only a memorandum. Yet from the Mechaber here and from the Rosh's responsum it is clear that the writing is valid testimony against a claim that the event never occurred. The Shach's explanation of the Rosh's conclusion, "and for this purpose it is valid testimony," is therefore very surprising, as later authorities have already noted. In my humble opinion the matter is simple in light of the Ran's novellae to chapter Zeh Borer. Witnesses may write an admission concerning land even though the litigant did not say "write," and this is not considered testimony from writing, because we assess that the admitter is pleased for the matter to be written since he suffers no loss from the writing. It is therefore as though he ordered them to write. So too here, because the witnesses know that he admitted the matter, the writing is treated as authorized for the limited purpose of defeating a claim that no admission ever occurred. But for collection from encumbered property he certainly was not presumed to desire the writing, and for that purpose a direct instruction to write would be required. This precisely explains the Mechaber's words, taken from the Rosh's responsum, that the witnesses wrote only "we remembered." Such a writing was acceptable to the litigant at the time of the admission and is therefore like an instruction to write. The Rosh's formulation is exact: there is no objection to this writing; they intended it only as a memorandum; and for this purpose it is valid testimony establishing what he admitted before them. The Rosh's foundation is that because the admitter had no reason to object to this limited writing, the witnesses acted properly even without an express instruction, just as witnesses may write an admission concerning land and it remains effective even if he later denies the admission.
