A writing that states, 'Explanation of what Reuven admitted before us: that he holds such-and-such from Yaakov's estate,' does not have the status of a document permitting collection from encumbered property. It contains no formal act of acquisition and is merely an admission. Its wording shows it was intended as a memorandum, not a legal instrument: it says, 'Explanation of what he admitted before us,' rather than the customary document language, 'He caused himself to be witnessed,' or the customary admission language, 'He admitted before us.' If a document omits the lender's name but the borrower obligates himself to whoever produces it, any holder may collect, even if known not to be the original lender, provided the holder was alive when it was written. {Rama: See above, siman 50.}
