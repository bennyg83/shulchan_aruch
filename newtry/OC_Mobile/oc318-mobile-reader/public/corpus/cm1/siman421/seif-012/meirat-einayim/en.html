He is liable for her damages. The Tur writes that he is liable for four categories. The Mechaber's phrase "for her damages" likewise means all categories of damage except humiliation, which is not included because he did not intend to humiliate her. For the other categories, although he has permission to have marital relations with her, he was nevertheless obligated to take care not to injure her. The law governing the money paid for those four categories, what is done with it and its legal status, will be explained in siman 423 and also in Even HaEzer siman 83.
"Cut off my hand," etc. See Derishah, where I wrote that according to this view, even if he initially said explicitly, "on condition that you be exempt," he is nevertheless liable specifically in these cases because they concern primary limbs whose loss is serious and known. A person does not ordinarily wish to lose such limbs, and he assumes that his fellow will not actually cut off his hand or blind him even if he says, "on condition that you be exempt." But if he says, "Strike me or wound me on condition that you be exempt," and the other wounds him without depriving him of a primary limb, the other is exempt, like one who says, "Break my jug," or other property, as explained at the beginning of siman 380.
For example, where he said, "Cut off my hand," etc. This continues through the statement that the words are understood rhetorically. Where he did not explicitly say "on condition that you be exempt," but the words can be read as a rhetorical question, we interpret them rhetorically and impose liability even where no primary limb was lost. The examples of cutting off a hand, etc., are stated only to teach that if he did expressly say "on condition that you be exempt," he is exempt even for such injuries. Only with property damage do we exempt even where the language can be understood rhetorically, as the Tur and Mechaber write at the beginning of siman 380, but not with bodily pain. This is unlike the Ir Shushan, who wrote that even these authorities impose liability without an explicit exemption only for injury to primary limbs. That is incorrect. See Perishah and Derishah here and above in siman 380.
