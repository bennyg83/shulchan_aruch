heh) Tur, seif 19, from the Mishnah in the third chapter on 33a.
vav) Ibid., in the name of his father the Rosh in his rulings. The meaning is after the parties had separated from one another. See above in siman 420.
zayin) Maharam of Rothenburg writes: Reuven claimed that Shimon called him a mamzer. Shimon responded that Reuven had first whispered in his ear that Shimon was a mamzer, and therefore Shimon became enraged and called him that. This is a compelling circumstantial inference, because if Shimon suddenly called him a mamzer, it is evident that his account is true. He therefore need not swear concerning this, as written above in siman 420, seif 38, Sema in the name of Darkei Moshe.
chet) Nimukei Maharam writes that a woman's status regarding humiliation is the same as a man's. If a husband does not wish his wife to be humiliated, he must redeem her through monetary payment in the amount assessed by beit din. See above in siman 424, seif 8, Sema in the name of Darkei Moshe.
tet) Ibid., in the same source.
yud) Ibid., in the same source.
kaf) The Mordechai in chapter HaMeniach writes that when two people are fighting, one should not shove the other, but should disengage gently. If he shoves him, he is liable for the resulting injury. In my humble opinion, this was learned from the law of an ox that mounted another ox, explained in siman 383, seif 2.
