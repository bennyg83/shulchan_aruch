With the excess. See above, siman 402, regarding two oxen that damaged one another and payment of full damages from the excess; from there one can learn the law of one person injuring another. See Responsa Mabit, part II, siman 311.
Immediately. See the Sema, who writes at length that the word "immediately" is a scribal error.
Himself. Even others are obligated to strike the aggressor in order to save their fellow, as stated in siman 425; all the more so may the victim himself do so. Sema.
And humiliations. See Responsa Ra'anach, simanim 50, 93, and 111, and Maharshal, chapter 8 of Bava Kamma, siman 42.
From a prohibition. The Tur includes an additional clause before this: if one sees another striking his father, son, or brother, and strikes the aggressor in order to save his relative, he is exempt. The Tur did not group brother and son with the final case of one who sees another Jew being struck, and gave a separate rationale: in the middle case, to save his relative; in the final case, to separate the aggressor from a prohibition. I infer a practical difference. If the rescuer is not a person ordinarily concerned to stop wrongdoing, and often sees one person strike another without intervening, then if he strikes this aggressor we may say he acted from hatred and it is forbidden. By contrast, if he sees someone striking his father, son, or brother, he may save his relative by striking the aggressor even though he does not ordinarily save others. The Mechaber does not distinguish, perhaps because he did not notice this point, but the distinction is sound. Sema.
