If one person called his fellow a thief, and the other said to him, etc. See Yam Shel Shlomo, chapter HaManiach, siman 26, and chapter HaChovel, siman 42. He writes that if one person reviled another with a defamatory name and the latter responded by striking him, although the striker violated a prohibition and requires atonement, he is not treated as a wicked person. We say that his heart was inflamed by anger and that a person cannot always restrain himself in the face of anguish, as Scripture says, “There is one who speaks rashly,” and the like. He therefore pays only for damage, pain, medical expenses, and loss of labor. As for the person who spread the defamatory accusation, although he is exempt from payment for humiliation because he already received his punishment through the blow, he must nevertheless confess publicly that what he said was false and seek forgiveness. Responsa Shvut Yaakov, part I, siman 179, cites this as halachah. See also there, part II, siman 149, concerning this matter. Responsa Chavot Yair, siman 65, sets forth many rules governing these matters.
