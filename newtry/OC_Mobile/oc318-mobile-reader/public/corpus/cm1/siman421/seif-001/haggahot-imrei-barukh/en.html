(Mechaber, seif 13) And he wounded him severely, he is liable. Note: Yam Shel Shlomo, Bava Kamma, chapter 3, siman 27, explains that one who was attacked and then struck back is not liable for excessive wounds inflicted while freeing and saving himself from the attacker. While his anger is still inflamed, this resembles compulsion. Even during the moment when striking was necessary for self-defense, he is not liable merely because he failed to calibrate the blows precisely and could technically have saved himself with a lesser blow. A person is not fully master of his spirit in such a moment so as to measure with exact precision. See there at length for his carefully reasoned explanation.
