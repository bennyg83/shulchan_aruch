And the householder injured him, etc. The householder does not anticipate that someone will enter his domain without permission. The Tur adds another case: if the entrant was injured on his own, the householder is exempt. The Beit Yosef calls that phrase a scribal error and the Mechaber omits it. In the Perishah I explained that it need not be deleted. In the first clause the householder is exempt only when he did not know the person had entered. The second clause teaches that if the entrant is injured on his own, the householder can be exempt even if he knew the person had entered, because the householder did not directly injure him.
