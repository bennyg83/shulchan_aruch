He may remove him. See Responsa Chut HaShani, end of siman 61, who writes that the reason we do not say that the employer should instead require the servant to swear that he will not steal from him, which would be preferable to voiding the employment agreement, and even to striking him, is that the law does not authorize one to impose an oath at all upon a person whom he merely suspects may harm him when he does not yet have a claim against him. The other can say to him, "Guard yourself so that I do not harm you." Since this servant lives with him constantly in the same house and it is impossible for him to protect himself from him, he may therefore dismiss him; see there. According to this, if the servant voluntarily chose to swear to this, the employer could not dismiss him. The plain implication of Rama's words, however, does not appear so, and this requires further study.
