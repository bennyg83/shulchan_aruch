He called his fellow a thief, and the other says to him, etc. See Yam Shel Shlomo, chapter HaManiach, siman 26, and chapter HaChovel, siman 42, who writes as follows: If one person insulted his fellow with a defamatory epithet and the latter responded by striking him, although the latter transgressed a prohibition and requires atonement, he is nevertheless not subject to the status of a wicked person at all. Rather, we say that his heart became inflamed, and a person cannot be expected to endure his distress, for there are those who speak rashly, etc. He pays only for damage, pain, medical expenses, and loss of work. As for the one who spread the defamatory report, although he is exempt from payment for humiliation because he already received his punishment, he must nevertheless confess and state publicly that he spoke falsely, and he must request forgiveness. End of his words. Responsa Shevut Yaakov, part 1, siman 179, cites this as practical law. See there further in part 2, siman 149, concerning this matter. See also Responsa Chavos Yair, siman 65, who writes many principles concerning these matters.
