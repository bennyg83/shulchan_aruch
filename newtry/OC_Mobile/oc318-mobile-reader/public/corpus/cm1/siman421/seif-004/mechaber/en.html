The rule that a sleeping person is liable applies when both people were already sleeping together and one turned over onto the other and injured him or tore his clothing. But if one person was sleeping and another later came and lay beside him, the one who came later is liable if he injures the first, while the first is exempt if he injures the later arrival. The same applies to any comparable case of a great unavoidable accident, in which the person causing the damage is exempt. Rabbeinu Yerucham, Netiv 31, part 2. Likewise, if someone places his utensils beside a sleeping person and the sleeper rolls onto them and breaks them, the sleeper is exempt, because the person who placed the utensils there was negligent in placing them beside him.
