Because they benefit if the poor become wealthier, etc. This requires explanation. The two witnesses themselves undertake to give the fixed amount they had previously been assessed. The remaining townspeople, however, will contribute less because the disputed maneh now belongs to the poor fund. If so, how do the poor become wealthier through these two witnesses? The witnesses are merely paying what they already owed. We must say that if the witnesses did not undertake their fixed share, they would plainly be interested. We would suspect that they are not generous and wish to save their own charitable assessment. But even after they undertake that amount, another benefit remains. The townspeople may be charitable people who would voluntarily give to the local poor even beyond strict legal obligation. Local poor take precedence over poor elsewhere. If the poor fund receives this maneh, the poor of the city will need less from future donations. The witnesses thereby reduce the pressure and expectation upon themselves as local donors. This is somewhat strained. A better explanation is that the "wealth" of the poor means independence. When the poor receive this maneh, they need less support from the townspeople and are less dependent upon them. They are, in that sense, wealthier because they are content with what they have and do not need to seek as much charity from the residents. That reduction in dependence itself benefits the townspeople. Rashbam explains the Gemara's question, "let two give what is fixed upon them," and the answer, "once there is relief, there is relief," as follows. Even if the two witnesses pay their present assessment, in a later year the poor will have more resources and the witnesses will be assessed less. Beit Yosef explains Rashbam similarly: even if they pay now, they benefit later. Tosafot ask why the witnesses cannot accept upon themselves to pay the same amount forever. This is not a real difficulty. The Gemara's proposal was that they pay what they would have owed anyway even if their testimony were rejected. That costs them nothing extra now and therefore can remove present interest. But if they undertake to pay the same amount forever even after the poor no longer need charity, that is a new voluntary gift. They do not want to assume such a burden. If they continue giving it as charity, once the poor have sufficient resources they may no longer be permitted to receive it as charity. If the witnesses instead give it as an unconditional gift, they may still remain obligated in the separate mitzvah of tzedakah toward other needy persons. They would then lose twice: the permanent gift plus their ordinary charitable obligation. Therefore the Gemara correctly says the interest cannot be removed by such an undertaking.
