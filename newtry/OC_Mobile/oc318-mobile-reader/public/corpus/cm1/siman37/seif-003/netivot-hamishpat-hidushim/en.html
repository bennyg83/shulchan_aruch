The Shach writes that if the plaintiff has two separate groups of witnesses, the defendant may testify that one group is disqualified, because he is not personally interested in invalidating them when the plaintiff still has another group. But if the plaintiff has produced only one group and merely says that he has another group elsewhere, the defendant may not testify to disqualify the present witnesses, because he is interested, since he may hope that the other group will never appear. If the group he seeks to disqualify says that they observed the event together with the qualified witnesses, he cannot disqualify them, because he is interested in the matter.
