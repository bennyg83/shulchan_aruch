They may testify for him. I explained in Perishah from the Rosh that a lender, guarantor, and first or second purchaser ordinarily do not know whether the borrower or seller has other earlier debts. Therefore, once enough property remains in the borrower's or seller's hands to cover their own exposure, they need not suspect additional earlier creditors. The seller himself differs because he knows how much he owes. Therefore, he cannot testify for the purchaser merely because property worth the purchase price remains with him, as the Tur and Mechaber explain later in seif 15.
And an unconditional guarantor, etc. The Tur explains that because the creditor may legally collect first from such a guarantor, the guarantor has a strong interest in having the lender collect easily from the borrower instead. This remains true even if the disputed field is superior or inferior land while the remaining property is intermediate. The creditor may prefer a slightly larger amount of inferior land, or the borrower may prefer to satisfy him with a smaller amount of superior land. Therefore the guarantor remains interested in the testimony.
