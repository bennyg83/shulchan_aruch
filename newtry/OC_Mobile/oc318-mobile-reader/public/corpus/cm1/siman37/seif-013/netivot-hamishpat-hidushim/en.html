The lender cannot testify. If the land leaves the borrower's possession, the lender may have no property from which to collect and will suffer loss. The same applies to a guarantor, because if the creditor cannot collect from the borrower he may return against the guarantor.

They may testify. The same applies if the lender has a reliable guarantor. We do not say that perhaps he fears the borrower owes many other debts and this land will not suffice. We do not suspect unknown debts, for if so, why did he lend to him originally? The statement elsewhere that one wishes to keep property before his creditor concerns the debtor himself, who knows his hidden financial affairs. Other people are not suspected of giving false testimony because of debts unknown to them. Sema. See Netivot, seif katan 11: movable property is not considered a sufficient remaining asset for this purpose. Nevertheless, he may testify concerning movables even if the borrower has no other property, because movables are not subject to the lender's lien in the same way. Likewise he may testify concerning land not legally encumbered to him, such as land in an oral loan or after acquired land where the lien is not effective. Only property truly subject to his lien and not freely saleable is treated as though it were his own for purposes of interested testimony.

Even if the field about which he testifies is superior or inferior land and the borrower retains intermediate land, he may still have an interest because perhaps he wishes to receive a slightly larger amount of inferior land or a slightly smaller amount of superior land. Even if he can ultimately recover from the borrower, he may prefer not to enter litigation. If the borrower retains superior, intermediate, and inferior land, everyone agrees he may testify. Sema. The Shach writes that because the authorities disagree, the burden of proof rests upon the claimant. It may be possible to distinguish: if the witness testifies positively that this field belongs to the borrower through three years of possession, perhaps his testimony is valid because the claimant bears the burden of proof. But if he comes to disqualify the challenger's witnesses, a doubt cannot remove their definite presumption of qualification. Urim VeTumim.
