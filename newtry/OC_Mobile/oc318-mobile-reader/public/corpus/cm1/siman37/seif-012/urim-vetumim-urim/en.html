Sema cites Rabbeinu Yerucham that a litigant cannot testify that the witnesses coming to obligate him are disqualified, even if he claims no personal interest because the opposing party says he has another pair. Shach correctly limits this. If another independent pair is actually available, the litigant is no longer interested and may disqualify the first pair. The Gemara's case is where the claimant merely says he has two pairs but has so far produced only one. The defendant remains interested in disqualifying them because the claimant may never bring the second pair and is not obligated to do so. If, however, the first and second groups say they all observed the same event together, the defendant remains interested in disqualifying the first pair, because finding one member of a joint group disqualified can invalidate all the testimony. That is obvious.
