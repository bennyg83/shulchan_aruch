Any testimony from which a person derives benefit is disqualified. Ir Shushan writes that this is not because we suspect him of lying, but because a person is considered related to himself. I do not know his source. In my view the simple reason is precisely suspicion arising from the benefit he stands to receive. The Tur and Mechaber say so at the end of this siman, and nearby, in seif 9, one who sold land without responsibility may not testify for the buyer when an adverse claimant appears, because we fear he will testify falsely in order to keep the land available for his own creditor. Likewise here the witness must accept upon himself that if a creditor later comes he will remove him with money, specifically in order to eliminate that interest. The phrase "a person is related to himself" is used where testimony would make himself wicked, not in ordinary monetary interest. Nimukei Yosef likewise writes that an interested witness can testify adversely to his own interest, unlike one disqualified by kinship.

Even concerning the partner's share his testimony does not help, etc. The reason is that whatever remains in his partner's hands will still partly belong to him so long as they have not fully divided beforehand. But if they already divided completely, he may testify for his former partner. He can no longer say the prior division was mistaken merely because his own share later left his possession through a theft claim. This resembles the laws of brothers who divided and a third brother later arrived from overseas. Here, since he himself attacks the adverse claimant's witnesses but is not believed regarding his own share, we tell him that his own bad fortune caused his field to be lost.
His testimony does not help. Ir Shushan again explains that since the testimony touches his own share and he cannot testify for himself, part of the testimony is void and therefore all is void. This follows his opening theory in this siman, but it is not precise. Rashbam and Nimukei Yosef explicitly explain the reason as concern that the partners will later divide again, as I wrote immediately above.
Unless he removes himself from it, etc. The Gemara says he removes himself and writes to his partner, "I have no claim or dealings in your share," and makes a kinyan. Most authorities hold that the Gemara is not literal and that this wording alone is insufficient, because he may have removed only claims and dealings and not ownership of the land itself. The kinyan must expressly state that he removes himself from the actual share of land he owns in the partnership. The Tur's language indicates this clearly: unless he removes himself and they make a kinyan that no right at all remains to him. Darkei Moshe likewise supports this.
