Seif 1. A benefit from it. I have not presently found a clear ruling whether a financial interest worth less than a perutah disqualifies a witness. Although even very remote interests can disqualify, an amount below a perutah, even where people would object to its taking initially and it may be prohibited as a half measure, is not legally treated as money and is normally waivable after the fact. There is therefore room to say that it does not create disqualifying interest. A slight indication may be drawn from Kiddushin 43, where the Gemara separately discusses agents for kiddushin, gittin, and monetary matters. Had even sub-perutah value created witness-interest, agents in kiddushin or divorce could already have a possible interest in the document or coin involved, and the separate monetary case would be less necessary. The Gemara elsewhere contemplates valuing a document to see whether it is worth a perutah, implying that ordinary documents often have value near that threshold. This is not a complete proof, but it leans toward saying that less than a perutah is not disqualifying interest. The matter requires further study. By contrast, a broker can be disqualified where his testimony will cause a transaction to be redone and he is likely to earn a new brokerage fee. In a community where most wagon hires are arranged only through brokers, even an indirect prospect that the brokers have a shared arrangement or standing understanding can create financial interest. The laws of paid craftsmen and custodians illustrate that even remote benefit may matter where an actual monetary prospect exists.
