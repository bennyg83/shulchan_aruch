(Seif 1: even concerning the partner’s share.) The Sema implies that after partners divide, if a claimant seeks only the share of one partner on the ground that it was stolen, the other partner may not testify, lest the dispossessed partner later claim that the division was mistaken. Since that partner does not disqualify the claimant’s witnesses, he could say, “They lawfully took the property from me, and the division was void retroactively.” But if so, why did Rashbam explain the Gemara’s objection, “He is an interested witness,” specifically where they had not divided? Even after division, a partner could not testify when the claim concerned only the other’s share. The decisors likewise should have stated this rule instead of discussing only the ordinary case of undivided partners, where the claimant seeks to take the other share as well. Below, in siman 171, seif 1, we will prove clearly that a division is rescinded only among brothers, because they stand as heirs, not because the original division was mistaken. Rashbam’s words therefore retain their plain meaning: once partners divide, each may testify for the other whether the claimant attacks the whole field or only one share, because neither bears responsibility for the other. Only before division is the witness interested, for, as Rashbam and Nimukei Yosef state explicitly, whatever the claimant takes causes both partners a loss. They never suggest that if the claimant takes from one, that partner may later recover from the other.
