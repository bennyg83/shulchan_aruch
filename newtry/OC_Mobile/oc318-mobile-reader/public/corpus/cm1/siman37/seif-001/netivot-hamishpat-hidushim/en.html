If he signed a document before becoming interested in the matter and only later acquired an interest, his testimony is disqualified at the time he becomes interested. If he testifies in a way that harms his own interest, however, he is believed because he has removed his own benefit. This applies only when he testifies about property that is not actually his but he has some indirect benefit from the testimony. His testimony will not help. Since the partners have not yet divided, whatever remains for the other partner will later be shared with him. If they already divided, however, his testimony can help the second partner. Even if the property remains in the second partner's possession, the first cannot later say, "Our division was mistaken," because once they divided the property left his possession. This is so even though they admit that the opposing witnesses testified falsely and that his portion was taken through theft. The loss is treated as having fallen upon his own field. See Beurim, seif katan 3, that after division his testimony is effective only if he says, "The field that was in my possession was not stolen, and I also testify that the field in my partner's possession was not stolen." If instead he testifies that the challenger's witnesses are disqualified, he remains interested even after division. See further in Beurim that only where the alleged victim of theft actually removed the property can one potentially claim that the division was mistaken when he does not admit that the opposing testimony was false. But if a creditor collected the property, he cannot claim mistaken division.
