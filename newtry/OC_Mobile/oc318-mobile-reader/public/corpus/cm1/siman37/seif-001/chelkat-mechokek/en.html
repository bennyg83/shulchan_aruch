And likewise he is entitled to her found objects, etc. If others gave her a gift, there is room to doubt whether it resembles the money of kiddushin, which belongs to the father, or whether kiddushin are different because the father could marry her to an undesirable man, while a gift conferred upon her by others belongs to her and the father has no right to it. A gift is not necessarily comparable to a found object. A married woman likewise owns the principal of a gift herself, while her husband merely has the right to its fruits, as the Rosh writes in the first chapter of Kiddushin in the name of the Yerushalmi, even though her found objects belong to her husband.
