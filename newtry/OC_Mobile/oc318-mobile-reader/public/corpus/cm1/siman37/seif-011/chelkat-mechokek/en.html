He says to her, "Behold, you are..." This means where the father is not present at the kiddushin, for otherwise how could he say to the daughter, "your daughter"? But where the father is present, he should initially say to him, "Your daughter is betrothed to me." Nevertheless, if he says to the daughter, "Behold, you are betrothed to me," there is no objection. So writes Terumat HaDeshen, siman 213. See Rivash, siman 479, who writes that the formulation, "Behold, your daughter," is effective according to everyone even when the money is given through the daughter herself. This appears to mean that he says to the father, "Behold, your daughter is betrothed to me with the money that I will give her," and he then gives her the money with the father's consent. But to say to the daughter herself, "Behold, your daughter is betrothed," is not meaningful language at all.
