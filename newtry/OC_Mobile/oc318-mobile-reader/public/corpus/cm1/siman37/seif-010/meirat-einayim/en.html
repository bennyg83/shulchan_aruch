Whenever presently, etc. In chapter Chazakat HaBatim, Bava Batra 44a, Shmuel says that one who sells a field to another without warranty may not testify on the buyer's behalf concerning ownership, because he has an interest in leaving the field available for his creditor. The Gemara asks: if the seller has another field, the creditor can collect from that one; if he has no other field, what difference does it make to him? Tosafot ask that it matters if he later becomes wealthy, for then he will be exempt if this field remains with the buyer. They answer that perhaps this future possibility does not disqualify him because presently he gains nothing. This accords with the Mechaber here. Yet the Gemara answers the question differently: even if he has no other field, he prefers not to be called a wicked borrower who does not repay. The Tur and Mechaber themselves write this below in seif 15. Why then is he not disqualified for that reason here? One must say that the present case is one in which this reasoning does not apply, such as a person whom we know does not care if people call him a wicked borrower.
