(Seif 9: it appears he rules like Rav Pappa. This is the correct text.) The Beit Yosef wonders, since in chapter 19 of Hilchot Mekhirah the Rambam rules like Rav Zevid. He therefore explains here and in Kesef Mishneh that the Rambam also follows Rav Zevid here, but even Rav Zevid requires that the purchaser know the property was not the seller’s. Otherwise, since an ordinary sale carries implied responsibility, the seller would remain liable. The Kesef Mishneh then distinguishes Rav Pappa as requiring such knowledge where responsibility was expressly excluded, whereas Rav Zevid would not. These words are difficult. The case of the cow and cloak itself concerns an express sale without responsibility, as it follows the case of land sold without responsibility. Moreover, the Rambam says that if Shimon did not admit, Reuven may not testify to destroy Yehudah’s right, because if the property leaves Shimon’s possession he will sue Reuven for the price, saying, “You sold me something that was not yours.” If ordinary responsibility applied, repayment would follow even if the property truly belonged to Reuven, so the Rambam’s reason would be unnecessary. The case must therefore involve express exclusion of responsibility, and the Rambam omitted repetition because he had just stated it regarding the land. To reconcile the Rambam’s rulings according to the Tur, chapter 19 must concern an express condition that the seller bear no responsibility whatsoever, even for theft. The Rambam rules that if it is later established that the land was stolen, the seller owes nothing because monetary conditions are valid. The Tur’s objection is that even then the buyer should recover because no sale occurred at all, since one cannot sell what is not his. The Rambam therefore teaches that the condition validates the transaction, consistent with Rav Pappa.
