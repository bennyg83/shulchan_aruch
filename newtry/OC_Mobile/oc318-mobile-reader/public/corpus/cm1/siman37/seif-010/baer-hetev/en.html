If he becomes wealthy. In the chapter Chezkas HaBatim, regarding the rule that one who sold a field without responsibility may not testify for the buyer because he thereby leaves it available to his creditor, the Gemara asks: if the seller owns other land, the creditor can collect from that; and if he owns no other land, what does he gain? Tosafot ask that there is a practical difference because if he later becomes wealthy he will be relieved of the debt, and answer that perhaps this future benefit is insufficient to disqualify him because he presently gains nothing. This is the rule stated here by the Mechaber. The Sema asks that the Gemara itself answers that the seller has no other land but does not want to be called a wicked borrower who fails to pay. If so, he should be disqualified for that reason. One must therefore say that the case here is one in which that reason does not apply, such as where we know he does not care if people call him a wicked borrower. The Shach is hesitant about the Sema's explanation, since this depends upon a person's inner feelings and is difficult to determine. Rather, one practical case is where it is known that the seller has no creditor at all, but only liability for damages. According to Maharshal and other authorities, damages may be collected from encumbered property, yet the seller may testify because the concern of being called a wicked borrower does not apply, since he never borrowed anything. Other cases can likewise be found. Examine carefully.
