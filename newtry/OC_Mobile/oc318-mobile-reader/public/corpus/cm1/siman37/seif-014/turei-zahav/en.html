(See there: if he labored, he may not testify, etc.) Why does the Gemara not distinguish two cases where he has no produce in the land, one in which he labored and one in which he did not? The Gemara seeks to reject Rabbi Yonah’s view that once he purchased from him he can never testify. The same must be said according to Rabbenu Yitzhak concerning why the Gemara does not distinguish two cases where he has produce in the land, one where he stipulated for more: it teaches that when he has no produce in the land he may always testify, even after laboring, contrary to Rashbam.
