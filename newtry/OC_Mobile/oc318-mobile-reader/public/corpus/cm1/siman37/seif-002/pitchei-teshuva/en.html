See Baer Heitev citing Meisharim, and Shach's qualification that this applies in the stated circumstance, but if the other group is before us a litigant may testify that one group is disqualified. Tumim writes that this is so only if the group he seeks to disqualify does not say that it witnessed the event together with the first group. If they say that they all saw it together, then by disqualifying these witnesses the entire testimony would be void under the rule that if one witness is found to be a relative or otherwise disqualified all the testimony is void. The litigant is therefore interested in the result and cannot testify. Netivot HaMishpat expresses the point differently, saying that if the group he wishes to disqualify says that they saw the event together with the qualified witnesses, he cannot disqualify them because then even the qualified witnesses would be invalidated. I do not understand why he changed Tumim's wording. Netivot seems to mean that the first group, the one he wishes to disqualify, says it saw together with the second, but the second group does not say so. If the first group is disqualified, it would also not be believed to say that they all saw together, and the second group would remain qualified. Tumim's formulation is therefore correct: if the second group says that they all saw together, then the litigant is interested. This requires study. Rabbi Moshe, author of Tiferet LeMoshe, notes in his gloss to Shach that Rashi, Sanhedrin 23b, appears to show that even if the second group is before us, the litigant is not believed to disqualify the first group, lest some other group later be found disqualified and he thereby lose the case because the first group had already been disqualified.
