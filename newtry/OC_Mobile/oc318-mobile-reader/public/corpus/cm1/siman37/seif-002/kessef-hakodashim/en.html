Although we rule that a financial interest does not require that the witness be qualified from beginning to end, nevertheless, where it is known that he once had an interest, perhaps the present possessor may claim that the interest has not yet been removed. Ordinarily, one who alleges bias against a witness or judge must prove it, because most people are not interested parties. But where the interest was once certain, that presumption is weaker. This requires further study. Urim VeTumim writes that regarding a relative of an interested party, the possessor may claim 'kim li' that the witness must have been qualified from beginning to end. If the witness himself was both related and interested, perhaps he too requires qualification from beginning to end, and the same may apply to his relative. Yet there is room to distinguish according to the reasons. If an interested person is not considered a witness at all but rather a litigant, then even if he was also related, he did not bear the status of a disqualified witness at the outset. Once the interest is removed, the prior relationship may not impair his later testimony. If, however, the reason financial bias does not require qualification from beginning to end is merely that such a disqualification is lighter, then where he was also a relative, the disqualification as a relative may still require that he have been qualified from the outset. This requires further study.
