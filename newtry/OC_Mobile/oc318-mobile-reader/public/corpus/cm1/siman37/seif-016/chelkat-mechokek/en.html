If he appointed another agent. See the Bach, who writes that this law also applies where a husband or wife appointed a second agent. He further writes that if the second agent acted first, she requires a get only from the second party. Either the second appointment canceled the first, in which case the first agent's kiddushin are void, or the first was not canceled and the principal merely feared that the first might not succeed. Since the second agent in fact acted first, the first had not succeeded, and only the second kiddushin are effective. These words are not compelling. It is possible that the principal did not cancel the first at all, but merely thought he might never succeed; if he ultimately did succeed, even later, his agency may still have remained in force.
