If Reuven sold a cow or garment to Shimon without a guarantee and Yehudah seeks to remove it from Shimon by claiming that he purchased it from a robber, Reuven may testify for Shimon. Reuven gains nothing from its remaining with Shimon, because even if Reuven's creditor appears, a creditor cannot seize sold movable property, even if the debtor designated it as an apotiki, meaning that payment would come exclusively from it. Nowadays, even if movable property was encumbered together with land, a creditor does not seize movable property that was sold. {Rama: If Yehudah claims that Reuven himself stole it, Reuven cannot testify where Yehudah would have to reimburse Shimon under the enactment protecting ordinary commerce, because Yehudah could then recover that reimbursement from Reuven (Tur).}
