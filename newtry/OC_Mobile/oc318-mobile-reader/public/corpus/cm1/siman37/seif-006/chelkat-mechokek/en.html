And signs of maturity were found, it is doubtful and she requires a get from both. Here too, 'from both' is not precise. The same applies if only her father betrothed her or only she betrothed herself. It always remains doubtful kiddushin, and her own definite claim does not help against others unless witnesses testify that she was still a na'arah after the kiddushin or that she had already become a bogeret before the kiddushin. The phrase 'signs were found' means signs of bogeret status. Since she is within the six-month period, there is a doubt whether the signs of bogeret status were present at the time of kiddushin or whether she matured only now, and therefore the kiddushin are doubtful. If, after the six months, her father betrothed her and signs of bogeret status were found much later, she was certainly already a bogeret at the time of kiddushin, and we do not consider her father's kiddushin even though the examination took place long afterward. It is possible that after six months, even without signs of bogeret status, we rely upon Shmuel's statement that there is no interval between na'arah and bogeret status and she is certainly a bogeret. The signs of bogeret status would then be relevant only to make her a bogeret within the six months. See Raavad's gloss to chapter 1 of Hilchot Ishut. All this follows those authorities who hold that signs of na'arah status and signs of bogeret status are separate, and that if she develops signs of bogeret status within six months she is a bogeret. Rambam, however, does not mention any distinct signs for a bogeret, implying that in his view she never becomes a bogeret before six months have passed. His statement that the daughter was doubtfully a bogeret therefore means there was a doubt whether six months had elapsed since she became a na'arah. The Gemara's language, 'if it was within six months, she matured only now,' is difficult according to Rambam, as the Maggid Mishneh writes. Signs of bogeret status are explained in Niddah 47b, where the Tannaim dispute them. Rashi explains that even the Mishnah there concerns signs of bogeret status, while Tosafot reject his explanation. The Gemara rules like all of the stated signs as a stringency. Rif writes that if she developed any one of these signs and then betrothed herself during her father's lifetime, her kiddushin are effective and she requires a get, and her father may no longer annul her vows. If her father betrothed her without her consent, she requires a get. It is surprising that this law is not mentioned here and that the signs of bogeret status are not listed here at all.
From the Responsa of Rashbatz cited by the Beit Yosef it appears that if signs of na'arah status were observed three or four months after she turned twelve, we presume that the hairs had been present from the time she became twelve. Once six months have elapsed after her twelfth birthday, she is therefore certainly a bogeret and her father has no authority over her, even though only two months have passed since the hairs were actually observed. See Choshen Mishpat siman 35, which rules similarly regarding testimony.
