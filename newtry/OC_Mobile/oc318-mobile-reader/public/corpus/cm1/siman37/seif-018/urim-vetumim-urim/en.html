It is proper. This applies after the witness already signed the formal document. If he signed only in a scribe's memorandum book and, before the actual deed was signed, the creditor sold the claim to that witness, the witness is interested and should not sign the deed. Sema cites Rivash and Darkei Moshe. I do not understand Sema's phrase “initially,” implying that the signature would help after the fact. If one follows the opinion that a debt can be assigned incidentally without formal written transfer, Rivash implies that a deed signed afterward by the purchasing witness is invalid even after the fact, leaving the document with at most one valid witness. If one follows the authorities who say the debt cannot yet be sold before the deed exists or without proper written transfer, then the memorandum entry is not itself a transferable document and no acquisition occurred; the witness is not interested at all and may sign. Rivash's responsum supports this analysis. Sema therefore requires study.
