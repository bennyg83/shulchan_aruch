Until he withdraws his interest, etc. Rabbenu Baruch, Maharam, and Hagahot Maimoniyot to chapter 15 of Hilchot Edut hold that withdrawal is effective only for judges, not for witnesses, because testimony must begin and end in a state of eligibility. If the witness was disqualified when he observed the event, later withdrawal does not help. Maharam's responsum is printed as no. 995. Tosafot in Niddah 50a, the Rosh in responsa, klal 58, in the name of Maharam and Rabbi Yitzchak of Paris, and Terumat HaDeshen no. 341 rule likewise; see also Maharit no. 26. Although the Beit Yosef at the end of siman 33 rejected Maharam because partners may testify for one another after withdrawal, there is no difficulty, for that case may concern testimony observed only after the partner withdrew. Tosafot and other authorities imply this by raising the requirement of eligibility at the beginning and end only regarding the later case of two townsmen withdrawing, not regarding partners generally. Mahara ibn Chayim no. 103 therefore rules that, although the Beit Yosef follows the majority, a possessor may plead kim li where Maharam, Tosafot in Niddah, and Rabbenu Baruch support him.
