Even though he bears no warranty, etc. The Tur adds that although Shimon, the purchaser, knows that this field always belonged to Reuven and his ancestors, Reuven may still not testify for him. The Sema explains that if Reuven remains silent, Shimon has neither a monetary claim against him, because the sale was without warranty, nor even a grievance, because he knows that the challenger is relying on false testimony. The Ir Shushan writes similarly. They evidently had the Beit Yosef's reading in the Tur, namely that Reuven sold without warranty and Shimon recognizes the field. That reading is not primary. The Tur later rules in accordance with Rav Zevid that Reuven may testify even where the purchaser does not recognize the article, showing that mere concern for the purchaser's grievance does not disqualify him. The accurate editions read that the field was sold either without warranty or with warranty, and that Shimon recognizes it. The Bach also adopts this reading. This accords with Tosafot's first answer on Bava Batra 43a, that a person will not give false testimony merely to avoid a grievance. Tosafot's alternative answer was stated only to make the case easier, but as a matter of law such a grievance does not disqualify a witness. Otherwise, when the Gemara at the end of 44a asks from Rav Pappa, it should already have objected that the purchaser has a grievance, before arriving at the answer that he recognizes it as the offspring of his donkey. Hagahot Ashri in the name of Or Zarua rules this way, and so do the authorities cited below at the end of seif katan 23 concerning a cow and garment. They permit testimony even where the purchaser does not recognize the item, proving that we do not suspect false testimony merely because of a possible grievance. This is the correct ruling. 40100217
