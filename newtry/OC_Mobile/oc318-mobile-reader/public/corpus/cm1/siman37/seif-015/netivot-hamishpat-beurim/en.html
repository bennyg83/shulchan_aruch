In movable property, however, the purchaser generally can recover what he paid from the person from whom the stolen goods are taken through the marketplace enactment, so he suffers no true financial loss and therefore has less basis for resentment. In land, by contrast, if the field is taken as stolen property, he can lose his money and thus has a meaningful grievance against the seller. That distinction resolves the difficulty.
