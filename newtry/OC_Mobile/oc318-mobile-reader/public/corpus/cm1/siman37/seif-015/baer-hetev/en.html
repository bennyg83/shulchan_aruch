To challenge. The Tur writes: if a challenger asserts that Reuven stole the field from him, it is obvious that Shimon may not testify for Reuven because Shimon prefers not to have Reuven called a thief. Even if the challenger says only that Reuven purchased it from a thief, Shimon still may not testify for him. Sema.
Warranty. Even if Shimon knows that the field always belonged to Reuven and his ancestors, so that even if Reuven remained silent Shimon would have no claim or grievance against him because he knows Reuven is truthful and the challenger is relying on false testimony, Shimon nevertheless may not testify for him. So writes the Sema in the name of the Tur. See the Shach.
And he will seize it. This concern exists even if the seller owns other land, because perhaps the witness knows that the seller has many creditors, or perhaps this particular field was designated as an express apotiki for a creditor. Even if the seller bought the field and immediately sold it without borrowing anything in the interim, we are concerned that he may have borrowed before acquiring it and obligated property he would later acquire. So writes the Tur.
To testify. In this case Reuven's creditor can take the field from the challenger just as he could have taken it from Reuven or Shimon. Ibid., Sema.
