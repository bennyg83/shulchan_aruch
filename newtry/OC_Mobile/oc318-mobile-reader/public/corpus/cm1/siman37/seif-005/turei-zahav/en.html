(Seif 16: with the claim that he purchased it from a robber.) The Sema explains that with movable property there is no concern that the purchaser does not wish to be called a robber. This distinction is astonishing. In the first chapter of Bava Metzia, the Gemara labors to identify a practical difference between the opinion concerned that he not be called a robber and the opinion concerned that he preserve his trustworthiness. Why does it not answer that movable property differs, since according to the “robber” rationale there is no concern, while according to the “trustworthiness” rationale there is? Likewise, in Eizehu Neshekh Tosafot explain that mistakenly forgiven produce is considered forgiven because a person wishes to preserve his trustworthiness. It follows that even according to the concern that he not be called a robber, movable property remains relevant. Therefore the Mechaber and the Tur write at the beginning of seif 9 that the claim is that Reuven purchased the property from a robber, meaning Reuven purchased it from the robber, excluding a case where Reuven himself is accused of stealing it, which would raise the concern of being called a robber. The Rama’s nearby case, where the claimant must reimburse Shimon’s purchase price, might appear interested even without that factor because Shimon would not wish to be called a robber. The answer is that the purchaser knew the property belonged to Reuven, the seller.
