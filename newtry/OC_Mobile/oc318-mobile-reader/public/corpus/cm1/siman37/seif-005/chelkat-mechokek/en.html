But if she does not contradict him, she requires a get from both. In Kiddushin 79a, Rav and Shmuel dispute a case where her father betrothed her while traveling and she betrothed herself in the city, and she is found to be a bogeret when examined that day. Rav says that she is presently a bogeret and her own kiddushin take effect, not her father's. Shmuel says we are concerned for the kiddushin of both, since perhaps she was still a na'arah when her father betrothed her and his kiddushin preceded hers. It is possible that the phrase 'from both' is not precise, for the same law would apply if only her father betrothed her: whenever she does not contradict him, Rav too would agree that we are concerned for his kiddushin and do not leave her with her prior presumption of being unmarried. Tosafot there, s.v. 'he betrothed her on the road,' write that Shmuel agrees that if only her father betrothed her, she is not betrothed, since we retain her prior presumption of being unmarried and she is presently a bogeret, so her father's kiddushin do not take effect. Tosafot refer to a case where she contradicts him. All the more so, if she expressly admits the matter, she is certainly believed to prohibit herself, provided she did not betroth herself to another after her father's kiddushin.
