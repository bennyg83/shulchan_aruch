If Reuven stole a garment or field from Shimon and Yehudah challenges Reuven, claiming that the garment or field is his, Shimon cannot testify that it is not Yehudah's. Shimon wants the property to remain with Reuven because it may be easier to recover it from Reuven than from Yehudah. Likewise, if Reuven sold the stolen field or bequeathed it to Levi and Yehudah challenges Levi, Shimon cannot testify that it is not Yehudah's. If Reuven sold the stolen garment to Levi, Shimon despaired of recovering it, and Yehudah then challenges Levi, Shimon may testify that it is not Yehudah's if Reuven has died. The garment can never return to Shimon because the purchaser acquired it through despair and a change of possession; Reuven the robber is dead, so Shimon has no one from whom to collect its value. If Reuven is alive, Shimon may not testify even about the garment, because he benefits from its not remaining with Yehudah: he can prove that Reuven stole it and collect its value. Likewise, if the garment is held by Reuven's heirs, Shimon cannot testify because, if it remains with an heir, it will ultimately return to him. The same applies in similar cases. Even when Reuven died, this applies only if he left no real property subject to collection. If he left such property, his estate is liable and Shimon remains interested. Nowadays, since the Sages enacted that a creditor may collect even from the movable property of orphans, Shimon cannot testify even if the robber died without leaving real property, because he still has an interest. {Rama: All this applies when Shimon testifies only that the field or garment does not belong to Yehudah. If he testifies affirmatively that it belongs to Reuven, he may testify in every case. Once he admits that it belongs to Reuven, he can no longer claim it for himself and therefore has no interest in the testimony (Tur). See also siman 40.}
