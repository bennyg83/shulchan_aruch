Reuven stole a cloak or field from Shimon, etc. The Tur cites a dispute. Rabbeinu Yonah explains that even if we do not know with certainty that Reuven stole it from Shimon, but merely heard Shimon complain beforehand that Reuven stole it, Shimon is nevertheless considered interested in the testimony. Rabbeinu Yitzchak, however, explains that he is considered interested only when we know that Shimon has evidence with which he could legally recover it from Reuven.
Because it may be easier for him to recover it from Reuven, etc. In the Perishah I explained that this convenience includes the possibility that if it reaches Yehudah, even if Shimon has witnesses that the item was his and Reuven stole it, he may still be unable to recover from Yehudah if Yehudah also has witnesses that it was always his and never Shimon's. The evidence would then be two against two, and the land would remain in Yehudah's possession because he is then the possessor.
Likewise, if Reuven sold the field, etc. Here the wording specifically says 'the field,' because land is not subject to despair and cannot be acquired through despair followed by transfer of possession, unlike a cloak. In the opening clause, where the item is still in Reuven's hands and there has been no transfer of possession, the law of field and cloak is the same.
If he sold the cloak, etc., and Shimon despaired. The Mechaber follows Rambam's wording in chapter 5 of Hilchot Geneivah and chapter 2 of Hilchot Gezeilah, which does not require the owner's despair to precede the transfer of possession. Tosafot, the Rosh, and the Tur disagree, as explained below in simanim 353 and 361.
Because if it remains with the heir it can return to him. An heir's possession is not considered a change of possession. If it reaches Yehudah, however, and the contestant says it never belonged to Shimon, it will not return to Shimon even if Shimon also brings witnesses, for the reason explained above.
He is liable, etc. Meaning, the robber's monetary liability remains against Reuven's estate even after his death. Therefore Shimon is considered interested in this testimony.
