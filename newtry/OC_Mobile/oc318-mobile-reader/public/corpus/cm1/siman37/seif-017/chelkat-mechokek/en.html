Even if her father had arranged the shidduchin beforehand. The Bach asks: since her father arranged the match, he revealed that he desired this man to betroth her, and it is explained below that in such a case the kiddushin are effective. Because of this difficulty he emends the text to read, 'even if he arranged the shidduchin through her father,' in accordance with Rashi, meaning that the man sent a proposal to her father to betroth her to him. There is no need for this emendation. Although the father agreed to the shidduchin, he did not necessarily consent to the kiddushin taking place now, for perhaps he consented only on condition that the kiddushin money be given specifically into his hand. The Torah granted the father authority over his daughter, and he does not wish her to receive kiddushin without his permission unless he expressly reveals that he is satisfied with her acceptance.
