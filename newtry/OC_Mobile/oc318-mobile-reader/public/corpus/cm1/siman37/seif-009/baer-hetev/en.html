Money. According to the view that the right to designate a recipient has monetary value, their testimony is wholly ineffective if they delivered all the testimony at one time. Even if they testified one after another, their testimony is effective only to establish that the property is consecrated, not to permit the treasurer to distribute it to whomever he wishes. See below, beginning of siman 203, where the Rama expressly rules that such discretionary benefit is not considered money. The Mechaber likewise rules in siman 87, seif 35, and the Tur in Yoreh Deah, siman 331. Thus writes the Sema. The Shach challenges the Rama and concludes that even according to the view that discretionary benefit is not money, this is only where the funds will be distributed to other poor people who are not the witness's relatives. If they may be given to his relatives, it is obvious that he is personally interested. This is not merely discretionary benefit but actual monetary benefit to his relatives. In my view, the Rama's words require analysis.
