(Seif 8: or we know he owns land sufficient for the debt.) How can this ever be known, since perhaps he owes another, larger creditor? Tosafot explicitly state in Hezkat HaBatim, folio 46, that even if he owns other land he may not testify, lest another large creditor exist. The Gemara’s earlier statement that if he has other land the creditor will resort to it does not mean that we know the total extent of his liabilities, which is impossible, but that if the facts ultimately show enough land for all debts, that is where collection will occur. It is therefore surprising that the Tur writes, “we know he has sufficient land.” It is also difficult why the Tur needs concern for an explicitly designated apotiki when concern for being regarded as a wicked borrower already exists. The case must be that witnesses remained with him continuously and know he incurred no other debts beyond those matched by his available lands, but they do not know the form in which one debt was created. The Tur teaches that there is still concern it was made as an explicit apotiki, even though there is no concern that he will be called a wicked borrower.
