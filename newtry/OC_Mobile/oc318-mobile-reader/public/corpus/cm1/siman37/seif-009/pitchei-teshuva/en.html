Even though he bears no warranty, etc. See Shach, seif katan 21, who notes that our editions of the Tur read: "even though he sold it to him without warranty, and Shimon recognizes," and Bach writes that this is the correct reading. Rabbi Moshe, author of Tiferet LeMoshe, objects: If so, why may Shimon not testify for him? If the land remains with the purchaser he would be regarded as wicked toward the purchaser. Shach, seif katan 25, similarly cites Lechem Mishneh's difficulty with Beit Yosef's explanation of Rambam. It is strained to say that the case involves a seller who accepted ordinary warranty but not warranty for a defect arising from his own title. Perisha raised this same objection and therefore deleted the words "without warranty." Bach preserves the reading and indeed answers that the seller accepted ordinary warranty but not warranty for a defect arising from his own title. Tumim discusses the matter at length and concludes that there is no practical difference: whether the purchaser recognizes that the property truly belongs to the seller or does not, and whether the sale was with warranty or without warranty, the seller may not testify.
