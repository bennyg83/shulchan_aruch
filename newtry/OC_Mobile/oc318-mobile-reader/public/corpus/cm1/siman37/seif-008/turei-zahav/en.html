(In the Tur, seif 4: the claimant must pay him for his labor.) This appears difficult in light of siman 336: where one hires a worker to work on his own property but directs him to another’s property, the employer pays the agreed wage and then recovers from the owner only the benefit conferred. Here, however, the Tur says the claimant pays the cultivator’s labor costs. The distinction is that there the work is complete and we can compare the expenditure with the actual increase in value; the owner pays only the lesser benefit. Here a sharecropper is not entitled to his compensation until he completes the work and takes his share of the produce, but the claimant removes him before the produce comes. The employer therefore owes only compensation for the labor already performed, which is the field’s increase in value to date. That amount itself is measured by what one would have to spend to produce such an improvement, and the claimant must pay the same labor value. Thus there is no difference whether the sharecropper receives it from the claimant or the person who hired him. Although completion might have yielded him more because he was promised above the customary share, he will not testify falsely merely for that possible future gain, since he suffers no present loss and can hire himself elsewhere at a premium if he is a skilled sharecropper. I later saw that the Sema in siman 336 proposes emending the text here, but that is not correct in my opinion.
