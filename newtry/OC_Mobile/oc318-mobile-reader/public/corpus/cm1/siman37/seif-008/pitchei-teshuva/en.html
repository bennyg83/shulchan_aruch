If he took the rent into his hand. Sha'ar Mishpat writes that this is the precise language of Rambam, Hilchot Edut, chapter 15, and the Tur here. At first glance one might have thought that even if the tenant did not actually bring the rent before beit din, but merely said, "I have not yet paid, and I will pay the rent to whichever claimant is established as owner of the land," he would no longer be interested. This also seems to follow from Tur and Shulchan Aruch, siman 140, seif 9. Sha'ar Mishpat nevertheless concludes that the essential law requires that the money actually be brought before beit din. Rambam and Tur's phrase "if he took the rent into his hand" is deliberate. Otherwise the tenant remains interested because one claimant may be easier for him than the other, as the Mechaber explains in the preceding seif. The language of Tur and Shulchan Aruch in siman 140 is imprecise on this point. This is the correct rule. See the following seif katan for a consequence.
