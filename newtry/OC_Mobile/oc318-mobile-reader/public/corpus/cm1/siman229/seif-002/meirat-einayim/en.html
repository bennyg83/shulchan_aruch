And that wines and oils be clear, etc. Meaning that even one who buys wine or oil without specification is protected by local custom where the practice is not to mix in even the lees from the same barrel. In a place with no such custom, mixing the same barrel's lees is permitted according to the opinion Rama cites at the end of the preceding siman, and according to the Beit Yosef even Rambam agrees, as I wrote there in seif 15. Even according to my interpretation that Rambam by law forbids mixing at the time of measurement, he nevertheless holds that the buyer ordinarily must accept a certain amount of lees afterward. Here the ruling is that in a place where the custom is to sell wine and oil clear, no lees are sold to the buyer at all.
Therefore, one who removes a pebble, etc. This is the correct reading, and it is Rambam's language in chapter 18. The Tur does not have the word 'therefore' but states the law independently, as the Gemara itself does. The Mechaber's wording can be explained to mean: therefore the Sages also said that one who removes a pebble from grain must compensate the seller. Since the seller was entitled to sell the grain with the pebble included and the pebble counted within the measure, the person who removed it caused him a loss. Thus the law of removing a pebble is not learned from local custom. On the contrary, the larger point is that we follow custom, as can be seen from the fact that the Sages themselves recognized ordinary mixtures as part of a sale and required compensation when one removed what the seller was entitled to include.
He gives him horns of horns, and he is made from the island of Dina Dakarim, and he is damaged by his hands and is done immediately to the IDF:
If you say, let him return it, they have already said, etc. The Ir Shushan connects this to the preceding siman, where he writes that it is forbidden to mix even a small amount into refined oil. In my view the cases are not comparable. Even according to the opinion that permits mixing the same barrel's lees into wine or oil at the time of measurement, those lees were originally part of the same wine or oil. They naturally separated and settled to the bottom only afterward. Therefore they may be remixed so long as they were never removed from the barrel. A pebble is not of the same species as the wheat and was never part of the grain. The Sages merely recognized that grain ordinarily contains pebbles and dust and that buyers knowingly waive the ordinary amount while it remains mixed in. Once a pebble has been removed, there is no basis to injure the purchaser actively by putting it back and charging for stone as though it were wheat. Examine this carefully.
