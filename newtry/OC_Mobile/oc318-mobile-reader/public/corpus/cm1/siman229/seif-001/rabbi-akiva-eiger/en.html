[Rama] And some say specifically. Note: Since ordinarily so much dirt is not present, we suspect that he mixed it in deliberately. Wherever it is known that he mixed it in, all the more so the purchaser need not accept any of it. These are the words of the Tur. His words "wherever it is known" mean that even if the admixture is one quarter or less than one quarter, if it is known that he deliberately mixed in any amount, the purchaser need not accept any of it, as the Rosh explicitly writes. If it exceeds one quarter, then even without proof we suspect deliberate mixture. One might ask why above, at the beginning of siman 52, we do not penalize the permitted portion because of the prohibited portion, and similarly here the purchaser should need to return only the excess over one quarter, or only what is known to have been mixed. There, however, there is certainly a permitted portion, whereas here who can say that the entire mixture was not deliberately added? This is the answer given in our sugya. Shitah Mekubetzet cites Aliyot DeRabbeinu Yonah that according to the rationale of a penalty, we do not collect it in Babylonia. Nevertheless, if the purchaser seized property from him, even in the presence of witnesses, we do not remove it from him. See above, siman 1, seif 5 in the gloss, and Yoreh Deah, siman 267, end of the chapter.
