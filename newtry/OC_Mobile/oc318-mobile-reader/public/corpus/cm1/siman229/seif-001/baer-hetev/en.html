Legumes. Rashbam explains that legumes commonly grow among wheat, weeds among barley, and dirt is commonly found among lentils and legumes because they are uprooted together with clods of earth rather than harvested like other grain. Regarding dirt in wheat and barley, the Gemara presents differing possibilities. According to one view, with wheat and barley the buyer need not accept dirt even below one quarter because dirt is uncommon in them, and he bought them on the assumption that they were clean. According to another view, he must accept dirt there too. Thus far the Sema. The Shach writes that he does not understand this, because in any event less than one quarter must be accepted, as stated in the Gemara concerning one who selects a pebble from another's granary. See the Gemara. The Beit Yosef states this explicitly.
For all. The Sema writes that the correct text is 'one for ten' and that the word 'for all' should be deleted. The novelty is that one might otherwise think that a purchaser of only ten, which is a small quantity, is particular that all of them be good.
Anything. See above, siman 218, seif 8, and siman 227, seif 2.
Everything. This opinion holds that the quarter-per-seah tolerance was fixed because that amount ordinarily occurs. If more is present, we suspect that the seller mixed it in. But where it is known that he did not mix it in, what difference is there between one quarter and more than one quarter, since the seller never promised high-quality produce without qualification? This is as the ruling concludes. Sema.
