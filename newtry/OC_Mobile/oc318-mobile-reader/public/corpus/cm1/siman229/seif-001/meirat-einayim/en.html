A quarter kav of legumes, etc. The baraita in HaMokher Peirot states this. Rashbam explains that legumes commonly grow among wheat, while straw chaff is common among barley. Dust is common among lentils and legumes because they are uprooted with clods of earth rather than cut like other grain. The Ir Shushan's statement that legumes commonly become mixed with wheat on the threshing floor can be understood similarly: legumes initially grow among the stalks of wheat, and afterward when both are threshed their contents become mixed. Regarding dust in wheat and barley, the Gemara records two views. Some say the purchaser need not accept even less than a quarter kav of dust because dust is uncommon there and the buyer purchases on the assumption that the grain is clean. Others say that even there he accepts the ordinary measure of dust. The baraita mentions dust specifically in legumes as a greater novelty: even in legumes and lentils, where dust is common, he accepts only one quarter and no more. But legumes in barley and chaff in legumes and lentils appear from the Gemara to be accepted even in more than a quarter, because those mixtures are not considered as significant as defects in wheat.
