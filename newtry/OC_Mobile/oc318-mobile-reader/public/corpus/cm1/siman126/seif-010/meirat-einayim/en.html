And the borrower was found to be poor, etc., because he misled him. The Tur does not say 'because he misled him,' which would imply that the transferor knew from the outset that the borrower was poor. Rather, the Tur says: If he was poor at that time and Levi did not know it, the arrangement was a mistaken transaction and Levi may return against Reuven. This applies even if the transferor himself did not know that the borrower was poor at the time. The Mechaber follows his own approach, for in the Beit Yosef he questions why the Tur presents this as a separate opinion when the same law appears in the Yerushalmi cited immediately before. According to what I have explained there is no difficulty. The Yerushalmi speaks of deliberate deception, where the transferor knew from the outset that the debtor was poor. In that case, even an express release is ineffective. Examine this carefully.
