He said to him, etc. This follows the Jerusalem Talmud and Rif's approach, even without an express release. Raavad explains that if express release were required, then even without fraud the creditor could argue that his waiver was mistaken because he did not know the new debtor was poor. The Jerusalem Talmud's term means that he later became impoverished. Its distinction concerns a case where the original debtor knew of the poverty and the creditor did not, which is why the ruling says that he deceived him. If the creditor knew, he cannot retract, and the same applies when neither party knew because there was no fraud.
