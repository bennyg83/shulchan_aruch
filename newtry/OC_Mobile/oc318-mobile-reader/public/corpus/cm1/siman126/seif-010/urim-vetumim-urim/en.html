And the borrower was found poor, etc. Sema holds that if he was wealthy at the time of the transfer and later became poor, then even according to Rosh the claimant cannot return to the transferor. The transferor can say, 'Your bad fortune caused his later poverty.' Sema cites Rosh's responsum as proof. The Shach disagrees. See Tumim. I agreed with the Shach and reconciled Rosh differently. I also showed that Sema's proof from the Jerusalem Talmud is not decisive because the Jerusalem Talmud follows its own approach. Sema further says that if the claimant knew the transferee was poor, he cannot later return because he knowingly accepted the risk. The Shach objects that we are not dealing with fools; the claimant merely accepted the burden of collection, not complete loss. The Shach is correct.
