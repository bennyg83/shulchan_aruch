(In the Tur, seif 13: and Baal HaIttur likewise wrote, etc.) Apparently he interprets the Yerushalmi as dealing specifically with a case where the assignor expressly released himself from liability. Only in that situation do we say that, absent collusion, the assignor is exempt. Alternatively, the case may concern a recipient who delayed suing until the designated payer became poor. In that situation everyone agrees that the assignor is exempt. According to this second answer, however, the Yerushalmi's statement, "but if they acted collusively, he is liable," does not fit well. Nor does it fully reconcile the Rosh's responsum cited by our master earlier, where he says, "and the one who owed him became poor," implying that had he been wealthy at the time of the three-party assignment, the assignor would have been exempt. One must therefore explain the Rosh and Baal HaIttur along the first approach. The Sema writes that the Rosh too agrees where the designated payer became poor afterward, and disputes only a case where the designated payer keeps delaying him. I do not know how he then understands our master's words in the Rosh's name, "or he has no property," which imply that because he became poor the recipient may return to the assignor. One cannot say he was poor from the outset, because immediately afterward our master says, "and so I say in every three-party assignment." If he had been poor from the outset, everyone would agree that this was a mistaken transaction.
