And I will pay him. There is no distinction here between a shopkeeper or money changer and other people. So writes the Beit Yosef in the name of the majority of authorities, excluding the Nimukei Yosef in chapter HaMekabel. See Sema.
To retract. Responsa Mahara Sasson, siman 111, writes that even according to those who disagree with the Rambam in siman 60 and hold that a person can obligate himself by kinyan in an obligation of no fixed amount, they agree that by ma'amad sheloshtan one cannot obligate himself in an obligation of no fixed amount. See Shach. Above, siman 60 in Tumim, I clarified that there is in fact no dispute between the Rambam and the other authorities.
Even if he waived, etc., he may retract. The Sema holds that even if Shimon does not retract, Levi nevertheless has the right to retract. The Shach disagrees and writes that if Shimon does not retract, Levi too cannot retract, because the waiver was not made in error. He supports this from Tosafot, the Rosh, and Nimukei Yosef in chapter HaMekabel. See Shach, seif katan 15. See Tumim, where I wrote that the Rosh's words provide no decisive proof for either view, and the Nimukei Yosef likewise appears inconclusive. Nevertheless, as a matter of law, the Shach's view is correct. See Tumim, seif katan 4, where I explained the reason.
By kinyan. A kinyan is effective, because he certainly intended to release him completely even if Shimon does not pay. If his intent had been merely to release him on condition that Shimon pay, no kinyan would have been necessary, since he was already released upon payment. So write Tosafot and the Rosh; see Shach. Responsa Mahara Sasson, siman 103, attempts to interpret Tosafot differently, but his words are difficult, for the Rosh's words teach us how Tosafot should be understood. See Tumim, where I discussed this somewhat at length.
And likewise, if the recipient, etc., completely. The Sema understands this as referring to a case with a kinyan. The Shach agrees with the Bach that the same applies even without a kinyan, provided the creditor expressly released the first debtor completely. This is also implied by Tosafot in chapter HaMekabel. See Tumim. My own view inclines to say that if he expressly released the first debtor whether the second pays or not, then certainly the second becomes obligated under the law of guarantorship, as explained above in siman 129. If, however, he merely gave an absolute release without expressly saying that he would never return against him, the matter depends on the Rosh's interpretation. In Tumim, seif katan 5, I brought the difficulty and answer of the Pilpula Charifta, whose intent accords with the Sema. But as I explained there at length and resolved the Pilpula Charifta's difficulty, with the help of Heaven, in three ways, the Sema's ruling remains doubtful. According to two of the explanations of the Rosh, the law is in accordance with the Sema. See there at length. This requires study.
