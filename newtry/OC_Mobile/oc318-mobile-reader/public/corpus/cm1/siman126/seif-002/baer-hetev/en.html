And I will pay him. There is no distinction here between a shopkeeper, money changer, or any other person. Beit Yosef in the name of most authorities, excluding Nimukei Yosef in HaMekabel. Sema.
To retract. Responsa Mahara Sasson 111 writes that even one who holds, in siman 60, seif 2, that a person may obligate himself by kinyan in an unlimited amount agrees that ma'amad sheloshtan cannot create an unlimited obligation. Shach.
To the borrower. The wording might imply that this applies even when Shimon does not retract, and Rabbeinu Yeruham is cited by the Sema as saying so explicitly. The Shach writes that there is no reason for that. Why should a waiver not work in such a case? The Rosh at the end of HaMekabel, Tosafot, and Nimukei Yosef there clearly imply that waiver without a kinyan does work where Shimon remains bound. Only when Shimon retracts does the waiver fail, because it was a waiver made in error. We do not assume that Reuven intended foolishly to release the original debtor while the substitute debtor would refuse to pay. Rabbeinu Yeruham too must be understood this way. The Bach appears to have understood him accordingly, and Responsa Mahari Weil 33 explicitly says that only when Shimon retracts is the waiver ineffective. This is the primary ruling. Shach.
Acquired. This means where the creditor says, "I waive your obligation and will rely upon so-and-so." But if he releases the borrower completely, the release plainly works. The Bach writes this correctly, since siman 241, seif 2, rules that a waiver requires no kinyan. Tosafot in HaMekabel likewise state that if he says, "I release you completely even if the other person does not pay," he cannot retract. This appears to be the Rama's intent in the later phrase, "and similarly, if the recipient released the giver completely." There is no need for the Sema's explanation that a kinyan is involved. Shach.
With a kinyan. A kinyan does help because it shows that he intended to release the borrower completely even if Shimon never pays. If the release depended only upon Shimon paying, no kinyan would be necessary, since the borrower was already discharged upon payment. So writes the Rosh from Tosafot.
Part. The Sema explains that this teaches that because the recipient began accepting payment and the new debtor began paying, they are not thereby compelled to complete the entire arrangement.
Gave. Meaning before Reuven retracted. Shach.
To give them. The Shach explains that the substitute debtor must pay even if nothing belonging to Reuven had actually been in his possession. The same rule applies even without ma'amad sheloshtan, because the recipient released the original debtor based upon his undertaking, as stated in the gloss to siman 129, seif 3. The Ir Shushan unnecessarily added that the release here occurred through ma'amad sheloshtan. See the Sema.
