And even if the creditor waived it. See Shach, seif katan 15, who writes that in such a case waiver is effective without a kinyan. In my view, Sema is correct. Shach's approach creates a difficulty in the words of the Rosh, cited by Shach in seif katan 17: 'A kinyan is effective, because he certainly intended to release him entirely even if Shimon would not pay, for if he intended to release him only when the other pays, no kinyan was needed, since he has already been paid.' According to Shach, the Rosh's words 'if he intended to release him when he pays' must mean when the money changer is willing to pay and has not retracted. If so, why does the Rosh conclude that no kinyan was required because he had already been paid? According to Shach, he has not yet been paid. Shach indeed had the reading 'he has already been released,' but all the texts read 'he has already been paid.' Moreover, according to Shach, 'he has already been released' would have to mean that the release should have been effective even without this kinyan. Yet according to Rabbah he may certainly retract even while the money changer is willing to pay, so perhaps the kinyan was needed only to prevent such retraction when payment is offered. Further, Shach holds that with a kinyan the transferor is released even if the money changer retracts. But if, as the Rosh writes, waiver is ineffective when the money is not paid because it was a mistaken waiver, a kinyan should likewise be ineffective, for a mistaken kinyan is no better than a mistaken waiver. Rather, Sema is correct. When the Rosh says that he intended to release him entirely even if the other does not pay, he means where the money changer merely delays and does not pay, but has not retracted. When he says that if he intended to release him when payment is made no kinyan was needed, he means after payment had actually been made, for then he has already been paid. It follows that without a kinyan, waiver is ineffective even when the money changer has not retracted. Shach's objection, that this is not a mistaken waiver, is no objection, because the Rosh's reason is that this is not considered a waiver at all when he did not forgive the debt absolutely. With a kinyan it is effective so long as the money changer has not retracted, but if the money changer retracts, even a kinyan is ineffective because it was made in error, contrary to Sema and Shach. The Rosh proves release by kinyan only because otherwise there would have been no need for a kinyan. Once the kinyan serves to prevent retraction when payment is made, that proof disappears as to a case in which no payment is made. If, however, the transferor fully released the debtor whether the other pays or not, at the recipient's request, the money changer is certainly liable. This is the meaning of Rama. There is also proof for Sema from the Rosh's discussion of workers and the Yerushalmi. The Rosh first writes that if the money changer does not pay, all agree that the worker may return to the employer, yet afterward he explains the Yerushalmi according to R. Shimon, who holds that the worker may not return, because the employer became immediately liable to pay the workers and the arrangement is like a guarantor at the time the money is given. The distinction is that the employer's immediate obligation includes the prohibition of delaying wages. When he assigns the worker to the money changer and the worker cannot return so long as the money changer has not retracted, the employer is thereby released from that prohibition. This is comparable to releasing someone at another's request and creates guarantor liability, but only once the day has passed. If the money changer retracted before the day passed, the employer was never released from the prohibition and there is no guarantor liability. Tumim also writes this. Thus the Yerushalmi can follow R. Shimon where the day already passed, while our Gemara discusses a case before the day passed, and therefore the worker may return if he was not paid. This proves Sema's position. If Shach were correct that a waiver of the employer already prevents the worker from returning so long as the money changer has not retracted, the money changer would become liable as a guarantor even according to Rabbah, and the Gemara would not need to establish the case according to Rav Sheshet.
