If the original creditor waived the debt, the Shach says the waiver is effective so long as the transferee has not yet repudiated the arrangement. He reads Tosafot, Rosh, and Nimukei Yosef as saying that only once the transferee himself withdraws does the creditor's waiver become an obvious mistaken waiver. In my view, so long as Shimon has not actually paid the transferee, the original creditor's release of Reuven should also be revocable. His waiver was plainly made on the understanding that Shimon would make the payment. If Shimon has not yet paid, the purpose of the waiver has not been completed. Just as Shimon can still withdraw where the law allows it, the creditor likewise can say that his release was conditioned on receiving payment from Shimon. The early authorities' statement that it would be foolish to release Reuven if Shimon does not pay was intended to reject the claim that an ordinary waiver is automatically final without a kinyan. It does not prove that the creditor becomes trapped the moment Shimon says he does not intend to withdraw. The creditor could otherwise release Reuven, allow Reuven to become impoverished or disappear, and then later have Shimon withdraw, leaving the creditor with nothing. That too is plainly not the intent. Therefore, until Shimon actually pays, the creditor can still return to Reuven unless he expressly discharged Reuven regardless of whether Shimon pays, or made a kinyan that has the force of such an express discharge. The authorities themselves say that a kinyan is equivalent to an explicit release, because otherwise no kinyan would have been needed. This is the better rule.
