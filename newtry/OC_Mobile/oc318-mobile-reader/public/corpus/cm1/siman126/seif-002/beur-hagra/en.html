And each one, etc. Meaning, once the transfer has taken effect, none of the three may retract. Proof is from Rav Sheshet, who says that one may not retract. Although we rule like Rabbah, that is because Rabbah holds that the rule applies specifically when the funds were actually in the third party's possession, while Rav Sheshet holds that there is no distinction. The same follows from the Yerushalmi concerning the shopkeeper and employer cited by the Rif there, and from the Yerushalmi in Kiddushin that I cite below.
If the creditor waived, etc. See Shach. Tosafot there, s.v. Chozer, likewise write that where he said to him, etc., and the present case concerns precisely such circumstances.
So long as, etc. Tosafot there continue: "And now it is proven that waiver," etc., even if he did not explicitly release him completely, etc.
Nevertheless, if, etc. This follows from the Mishnah at the beginning of the chapter Kol HaNishba'in concerning, etc., "He said to the shopkeeper," etc. Even according to the Rif, who writes that we do not follow the Yerushalmi there, halachah 5, which states, "This applies when he did not place him with the shopkeeper, but if he placed him with him, the employer owes nothing," although that Yerushalmi concerns a case where he did place him with the shopkeeper, nevertheless we follow Rabbah as above.
