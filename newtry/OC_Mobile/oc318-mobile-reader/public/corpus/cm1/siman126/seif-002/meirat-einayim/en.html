Even if the lender forgave the borrower, etc. This means that even if Levi, the creditor to whom Reuven owed money, forgave Reuven because he agreed to collect instead from Shimon, Levi may nevertheless return against Reuven if collection from Shimon fails.
So long as he did not acquire it. Meaning that Levi did not release Reuven through a formal kinyan. Rabbeinu Yerucham writes explicitly that this is only where the creditor forgave Reuven without a kinyan. Although ordinary forgiveness is effective without a kinyan, if he released him through a kinyan the borrower is exempt even if the third party ultimately does not pay. This is also the meaning of Rama's following gloss, 'Likewise, if the recipient completely released the giver,' meaning through a kinyan. He further writes there that this applies even if Shimon did not retract, once it turns out that Reuven had nothing in Shimon's hands.

This person who has the money in his hands must, etc. This is difficult, because the case here concerns one in whose hands the transferring party, Reuven, had no money. It appears that Rama refers back to the Mechaber's preceding statement that if the third party nevertheless gave the money, he may recover it from Reuven. Rama then adds that if the recipient completely released the giver, meaning Levi released Reuven in a manner that fully discharged him, such as by a kinyan upon the release, then even if the money is still in the hands of the third party and has not yet been delivered to the recipient, and that third party now wishes to retract because Reuven had nothing in his hands, he must nevertheless pay it. This is no worse than an ordinary guarantor, who becomes obligated even without a kinyan at the time money is advanced or when the creditor, relying upon his guarantee, fully releases the borrower. Rama states this very rule in siman 129:3. Thus Rama abbreviated by saying, 'this person who has the money in his hands,' although more precisely he means the person in whose hands the money was supposed to be at the time the giver was released. The Mordechai there concerns a ruling official and a Jew, not specifically a release made by kinyan. Therefore Rama states the principle broadly: whenever the recipient released the giver in a manner that prevents him from returning against the giver, the third party becomes liable. This differs from the preceding case of ordinary forgiveness, where the recipient could still return against the giver.
