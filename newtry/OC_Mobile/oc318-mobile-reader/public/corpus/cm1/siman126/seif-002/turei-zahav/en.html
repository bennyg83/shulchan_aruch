(Seif 15) The transferor must swear. The transferor need not pay the recipient because the recipient released him, even according to the Rosh, since the recipient caused his own loss by accepting the payer's admission. The recipient cannot say, “I released you only because I believed that he owed you,” because the transferor may answer, “I do not believe your assertion that you now know he owed me nothing.” One might ask why the designated payer is not liable to the recipient for causation, since on the strength of his admission the recipient released the transferor's debt, and he should have examined the matter before admitting. How is this different from seif 14, where a depositary may recover from the transferor because the transferor caused him to accept liability, even in a gift case? Here the recipient himself knew the payer's dealings and therefore the payer was no more negligent in failing to investigate than the recipient, who accepted him as payment of a definite debt. Even according to the Rosh, who allows later recourse against the payer, the recipient initially relies on him and ceases pressing the transferor because he believes payment has been received. He should have considered that the payer might later prove he owed nothing and refuse to admit liability. The recipient was therefore more negligent, because he abandoned a certain debtor for a doubtful one. It is more appropriate that he bear the loss than the payer, whose duty to investigate was less. In the earlier case, by contrast, the transferor and the designated debtor or depositary were equally mistaken, so the party who caused the loss should bear it.
