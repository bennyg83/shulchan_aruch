But if he did not have one hundred in his possession, etc. Nimukei Yosef cites a Gaon who limits this rule to an ordinary person. A shopkeeper or moneychanger, whose occupation it is and who benefits when people rely on his word, fully obligates himself and cannot retract even though he presently holds none of the assignor’s money. The Beit Yosef writes that the Rif clearly disagrees; otherwise the Gemara’s difficulty would not arise, and it would have been unnecessary to interpret the Yerushalmi according to Rav Sheshet, because even according to Rav the worker could return to the employer under the Gaon’s view. He adds that the Rosh also follows the Rif and rejects the Gaon’s responsum. In my humble opinion, this reasoning is difficult. The Rif and Rosh were troubled by an apparent contradiction between the laws of a worker, as may be seen there, and Nimukei Yosef implies that the Rif could still agree with the Gaon on this point. Nevertheless, Tosafot and the Rosh explicitly state the opposite of the Gaon, and the other authorities also appear to disagree. The Darkei Moshe and Sema, seif katan 7, rule accordingly.

And even if the creditor forgave, etc. At first glance this appears to apply even where Shimon does not retract, and the Sema, seif katan 8, expressly says so in the name of Rabbeinu Yerucham. In truth, there is no reason why forgiveness should not be effective in such a case. Anyone who studies the Rosh at the end of the chapter HaMekabel, Tosafot, and Nimukei Yosef there will see that they clearly hold that forgiveness without an acquisition is effective when Shimon does not retract. It fails only when Shimon retracts, because then it was forgiveness made in error. Are we dealing with fools, that Shimon should refuse to pay while the creditor nevertheless releases Reuven? Rabbeinu Yerucham’s words must also be understood this way. The Bach, seif 5, likewise understood him as referring specifically to a case where Shimon retracts, and Responsa Mahari Weil, siman 33, states this explicitly. This is the correct ruling.
The creditor forgave the debtor, etc. This means that he said, “I forgive you and will rely on that other person.” But if he released the debtor completely, the release is unquestionably effective. The Bach writes this, and correctly so, because below in siman 241, seif 2, it is established that forgiveness requires no formal act of acquisition. Tosafot at the beginning of the chapter HaMekabel, Bava Metzia 112a, likewise write that where the creditor says, “I release you completely even if the other person does not pay,” he plainly cannot return to the debtor. This appears to be the Rama’s intent in his later statement, “Likewise, if the recipient completely released the giver.” There is no need for the Sema’s explanation in seif katan 9 that the case involves a formal act of acquisition.
