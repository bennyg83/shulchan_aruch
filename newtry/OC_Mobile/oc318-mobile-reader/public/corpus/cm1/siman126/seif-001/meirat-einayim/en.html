Whether a loan or a deposit. We do not limit ma'amad sheloshtan to a deposit that physically exists and exclude a loan that has been spent.
Whether he gave it as a gift, etc. The novelty is that the enactment is not limited to repayment of a debt for purposes of commercial convenience, where a purchaser of goods without cash can direct the seller to his own debtor. It also applies to a pure gift, even though gifts are less common.
He acquires, and there is no, etc. Ma'amad sheloshtan is described in the Gemara as a binding law without an ordinary transactional rationale. Tosafos and the Tur explain that Chazal created this otherwise unusual acquisition for market convenience, so a purchaser who lacks cash can direct the seller to collect from his debtor without waiting to perform another kinyan.
Even while they are still engaged in the same matter. The Tur says the rule permitting retraction while the parties are still discussing the transaction applies specifically to chalipin, where the kinyan does not occur in the body of the object being acquired. With acquisition by pulling, delivery, lifting, and other acts performed upon the object itself, neither party can retract. Ma'amad sheloshtan is likewise already completed: the original creditor has withdrawn and the new creditor has entered his place.
The giver likewise cannot retract and waive it. Although one who sells a promissory note may later waive the debt to the borrower, because the seller transferred only the lien on the borrower's property while the borrower's personal obligation remained attached to the original lender, ma'amad sheloshtan is different. In that standing, the borrower's personal obligation itself leaves Reuven and becomes bound to Levi. Reuven therefore no longer has any right left to waive.
Even if Levi was born only later, etc. This rejects the theory that ma'amad sheloshtan works because when the borrower originally became obligated he implicitly became obligated to anyone then capable of standing in the lender's place. If that were the reason, the transferee would have to have been alive at the time of the original loan. Rather, the acquisition works because of the market enactment, and therefore even one born later can acquire.
