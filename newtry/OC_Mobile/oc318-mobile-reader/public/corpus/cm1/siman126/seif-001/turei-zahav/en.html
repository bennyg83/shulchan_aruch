(Seif 6) He is not believed to say that he paid him. The Mechaber's words are astonishing and I cannot reconcile them. Why is the sender not believed to say, “You informed me that the designated payer refused to pay you, I believed you, and I paid you myself”? By law that payer was not obligated, since the transfer was ineffective, so payment from the sender would be entirely plausible. Moreover, this is the Rosh's ruling cited by the Tur, and according to the Rosh even where the transfer was effective the sender may say that he later paid after being told that the payer refused, because the recipient could then return against him, as the second opinion in seif 9 holds. Sema, seif katan 13, limits the present case to one where the recipient never informed him that the payer was not liable. But if no demand was made, he obviously did not pay, and there is no issue of credibility. If a demand was made, he necessarily learned that the payer refused, so why is he not believed to say that he then paid from his own pocket? In the Tur's source, the Rosh speaks of one who claims a different payment after initially saying that he had paid through the three-party transfer. He is like one who says, “I never borrowed,” which includes an admission that he never repaid. That source is clear. The Mechaber's formulation, however, cannot be sustained. Nor may it be forced to mean merely that after admitting payment by transfer he cannot later claim another payment, for that is not a question of ordinary credibility.
