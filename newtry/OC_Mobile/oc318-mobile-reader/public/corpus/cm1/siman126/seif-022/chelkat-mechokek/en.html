In the first Adar, if he wrote simply "Adar," it is valid. Bach objects that Rambam, Hilchot Nedarim 10, rules like Rabbi Meir that an unspecified "Adar" means the second Adar. If so, writing simply "Adar" in the first Adar produces a postdated get, which some authorities invalidate. See below, siman 127:9. Even if Rambam indeed ruled like Rabbi Meir, there would be no difficulty, because most authorities rule like Rabbi Yehudah. Tur in Choshen Mishpat 43 states regarding two documents written in a leap year that the second month is called "second Adar." So too Sefer HaTerumot, Agudah in chapter Konam in the name of Ri, Rosh, Ran, Rabbeinu Yerucham, Mordechai, and Raavad in his glosses all rule like Rabbi Yehudah. Therefore, even if Rambam disagreed, the halachah would follow the majority. In truth, however, one who studies Rambam will see that he too rules like Rabbi Yehudah. The editor, son of the author, adds the following explanation. The mishnah in Nedarim 63a teaches: if one vows not to taste wine until Rosh Chodesh Adar, the reference is to the first Adar; if he says until the end of Adar, according to the Rambam's text the reference is to the end of the second Adar. The Gemara asks: this seems to show that an unspecified Adar means the first Adar, so perhaps the mishnah follows Rabbi Yehudah, for a baraita records that Rabbi Meir says that in the first Adar one writes "first Adar" and in the second simply "Adar," while Rabbi Yehudah says the first is simply "Adar" and the second "second Adar." Abaye answers that the mishnah can even follow Rabbi Meir. The distinction is whether the person knew that the year was intercalated. If he knew, an unspecified Adar means the second Adar according to Rabbi Meir. If he did not know, his intent was merely the Adar adjacent to Shevat, namely the first Adar. Another baraita is then cited supporting the distinction. According to Rambam's version of the mishnah, the Gemara's initial question already assumes a distinction between how a person writes the name of the month in a document and what he intends in vows. Even though according to Rabbi Yehudah one writes the first month simply as "Adar," saying "until the end of Adar" can still mean the end of the second Adar because the two Adars are viewed together as one extended month for purposes of the vow. The supporting baraita can therefore accord with Rabbi Yehudah as well. Thus there is no contradiction between Rambam's ruling in vows and Rabbi Yehudah's rule for documents. The Rama's ruling is therefore correct. See Darkei Moshe concerning what he found written in an old get manual.
