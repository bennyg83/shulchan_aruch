There, seif 30, in the name of his father the Rosh at the end of the first chapter of Gittin.
He explains there that the depositary is only a paid bailee to the same extent that he was a bailee for the first owner, and is exempt from unavoidable loss. Even if the gentile compelled him until he brought the deposit to his house, he is exempt. This is not considered saving himself with another's property, because the coercion was directed specifically at this deposit. He brings proof from the last chapter of HaGozel, Bava Kamma 117b, from the incident in which a silver cup was deposited with a certain man, and from Rav Asi's ruling there.
See there. The case was that they seized collateral belonging to the depositary. Ri ruled that the depositary may retain the depositor's collateral against his own seized collateral, based on the incident in the last chapter of HaGozel cited above.
