He is believed to say, I erred, by a migo that the matter never occurred. See what I wrote above in siman 81. According to the Rosh, who allows a person to retract in that circumstance, this does not seem to be a valid migo, because it is a migo used to contradict a witness. If he says, "It never happened," the assignee can receive payment from the assignor according to law and afterward testify against the alleged debtor in beit din, forcing him to swear against the witness. We must therefore say that the present passage does not address that complication and concerns a case where the assignee is a relative, disqualified witness, or the like. The matter requires study.
And even if he gave him a handwritten writing, he is believed to say, I erred. This requires study. The Rama disagrees with Maharik without presenting a proof, and the Shach cites Mahari ibn Lev that he cannot claim error. It appears that even according to the Rama the case may concern only his signature at the bottom with the body written by someone else. He can then say, "I forgot the context of my signature and others wrote the text above it," and all the more so can he claim an accounting error. But if the entire instrument is in his own handwriting, perhaps even the Rama agrees that this is a stronger act than an oral admission before witnesses. How could he fail to reflect before writing the entire statement? Whether he can claim error in such a case therefore requires study. It seems that even the Rama would agree that he cannot.
