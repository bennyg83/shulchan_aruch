Before witnesses, etc. Even if a payment date was fixed and the debt is still within its term, so that he has no migo at all, he is exempt once he proves the error by witnesses. See above siman 81, seif 20, Shach, and what I wrote there in Tumim.
His debt. This excludes a case where the money was given to him as a gift, for then the giver is exempt, since had he known of the error he would not have given it. See Sema.
I was joking, etc. Certainly he cannot say that he confessed merely so that people would not think him wealthy, as explained above in simanim 32 and 81, because the claim that one confessed to avoid appearing wealthy applies only when he confessed voluntarily without any demand. See Sema. The Shach writes that even if the transfer was not made before witnesses and no payment date was fixed, so that he could have said, 'It never happened,' or, 'I paid,' nevertheless he cannot say, 'I was joking with you,' because the reason for the law still applies. See there, and see what I wrote in Urim VeTumim in the cited siman.
Through a migo that it never happened. See what I wrote above in siman 81. In Tumim I wrote that the Shulchan Aruch's case here is where the transferee is a relative, disqualified witness, or the like. See there for the reasoning.
Through a migo that I paid you. The Sema understands from the Mechaber's statement that this is like a migo against witnesses, implying that even where there were no witnesses to the transfer he cannot say, 'I erred.' The Shach disagrees and explains that the Mechaber means that if the matter had not been clear to him, he would not have admitted it before witnesses.
Even if he gave him a handwritten document, etc. Mahari Ben Lev ruled that one is not believed to claim error against his own handwriting, as cited by the Sema and Shach. See Tumim, where I left the Rama's disagreement with Maharik without proof requiring study. I suggested distinguishing between a document merely signed by him and one written entirely in his own hand. If it is wholly in his handwriting, the Rama too may agree that he is not believed to say, 'I erred.'
