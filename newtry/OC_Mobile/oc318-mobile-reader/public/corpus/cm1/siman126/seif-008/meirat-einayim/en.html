Whether a small gift. This teaches that even a small gift requires the recipient to be present for ma'amad sheloshtan. The case does not concern a poor recipient, for a promise to a poor person may be independently binding because a statement for charity has the force of a vow.
From the person holding the money. The Tur cites Ramah that a kinyan must be made with the person who actually holds the funds. A kinyan with the original giver does not help with a loan because money lent is no longer in his possession and a person cannot transfer through chalipin something not in his possession. Even with a deposit of coins physically intact, ordinary chalipin does not acquire currency. With a loan, the borrower's assets are instead subject to the lender for the debt, but those assets are not in the giver's possession so as to transfer them through chalipin. Authorization is different because it is fundamentally agency, not complete transfer.
If he wrote it as an acknowledgment. "Acknowledgment" is not exclusive. The point is to exclude language of future gift. Something not presently deliverable that is transferred through the language "I will give" is merely a kinyan on words and does not work. Likewise, existing deposited currency cannot be transferred by chalipin merely through ordinary gift language because currency is not acquired through chalipin. The Tur's formulation in Ramah's name must be understood accordingly.
