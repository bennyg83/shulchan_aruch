He did not acquire, whether the amount was small or large. The Sema writes that this does not concern a poor recipient, because a promise to the poor must be fulfilled even if made privately, either because speech to a sacred purpose is equivalent to delivery or because it is a vow. This wording may mislead. Here, even if the recipient is poor, the giver may retract. The case differs from siman 125, end of seif 5, where the giver handed money to his agent. There the money remained under the giver's legal control and the poor acquired it. Here, when he merely says, “I have a maneh in your hands,” the debt is not in the giver's possession, and the poor do not acquire unless the poor person or the charity collector was present with the debtor in the presence of all three, as the authorities state and as codified in Yoreh Deah 258:8. With a deposit, the charity collector need not be present because a deposit remains legally in its owner's possession wherever it is, as explained there in seif 7. Even regarding a deposit, this appears limited to movable property, as the Tur and Mechaber expressly state. If the deposit consists of unsealed money that the custodian may use, as explained below in siman 292:7, the poor do not acquire, because such money cannot be regarded as remaining in the owner's possession. The Sema must therefore be speaking of movable property or sealed money held as a deposit.
