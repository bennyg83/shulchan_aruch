(Seif 13: and some say that certainly if he became poor, etc.) The Beit Yosef asks that this is simply the Rif's opinion. The Sema answers that there is a distinction depending on whether the recipient knew or did not know. I do not see a reason for such a distinction. Whenever a person was misled, the transaction is mistaken. Compare the Rama in siman 232, seif 18, that even one who was himself deceived may not deceive others. The gloss objects that this comparison is unclear, because there the deceiver's knowledge is irrelevant, whereas here the issue is whether the deceived party knew and knowingly accepted the risk. In any event, the principal reason for citing the "some say" is apparently the latter clause, which contains a novelty: where the document grants credibility, Levi collects without an oath. One might otherwise have thought that such credibility is entirely ineffective in this setting. At first glance this is indeed difficult, because why should it differ from siman 71, seif 3, where credibility does not overcome an admission that payment was made or similar matters, because the credibility clause addresses only repayment?
