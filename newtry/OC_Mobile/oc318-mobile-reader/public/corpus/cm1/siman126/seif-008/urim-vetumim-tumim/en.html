There is no distinction whether it is a small gift. The Shach in seif katan 29 challenges the Sema, who writes that the case does not concern a poor recipient, because even with a poor person the rule applies to a loan claim that cannot be acquired by the poor person except through maamad sheloshtan. The Shach's substantive point is correct. Nevertheless, the language of the Shulchan Aruch troubled the Sema because it says that the giver "can retract." If the recipient is poor, the commitment is certainly a vow and beit din compels fulfillment under the verse "that which has gone out of your lips," which includes charity. So long as the money has not reached a charity treasurer or comparable representative, the donor may seek annulment of the vow, but without annulment he certainly cannot simply retract. How then can the Shulchan Aruch say without qualification that he may retract? This is why the Sema explained that the case does not concern a poor person. The Shach further writes that regarding a deposit, if the recipient is poor, the giver certainly cannot retract because the depositary acquires on behalf of the poor person, unless the money was given as loose money that the depositary may use, in which case it is treated like a loan. This follows the Shach's general approach. I already wrote in siman 74 that his position can be understood even if we do not accept the late Chacham Tzvi's view. In any event, such money remains sufficiently within the depositor's legal ownership that he can consecrate it or give it as a gift, because it is considered immediately payable.
