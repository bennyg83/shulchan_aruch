Small. The Sema writes that the case here cannot involve a poor recipient. If one verbally pledges a small gift to a poor person, he must fulfill his words even if he made the statement privately, because a statement to sacred or charitable purposes has binding force or because of a vow, as the Tur and Shulchan Aruch state at the end of siman 125, seif 5. The Shach warns that this formulation may mislead. Here, even if the intended recipient is poor, the transferor may retract. This differs from siman 125 because there the transferor actually gives the money to his own agent, so the money remains legally within his control and the poor can acquire it. Here he merely says, "I have one maneh in your possession," and because the debt itself is not legally within the transferor's possession, the poor do not acquire it unless the poor person or charity administrator is present at the ma'amad sheloshtan, as stated in Yoreh Deah 258, seif 8. Only with an actual deposit need the charity administrator not be present, because a deposit wherever it is located remains in the owner's legal possession, as stated there in seif 7. Even with a deposit, however, this appears to apply specifically to movable objects. Where money was deposited unsealed and the bailee may use it, as stated in siman 292, seif 7, the poor do not acquire because such money is not regarded as continuously in the depositor's possession. Thus the Sema must be speaking of deposited movable objects or sealed money. Shach.
In his possession. A kinyan from the person presently holding the money can help, but a kinyan from the original transferor cannot transfer a loan, because money lent is given for expenditure. Even regarding a deposit of money, coin cannot be acquired by barter, as stated in siman 203. If, however, the deposit consists of movable objects and the depositor transfers them by kinyan, then a kinyan from the transferor is effective. Shach.
Admission. The same applies where he creates an express personal obligation to give the amount. Shach.
To retract. Although an admission may ordinarily be legally effective even without a kinyan, as stated in siman 60, seif 6, and in the gloss to siman 245, seif 1, here a kinyan is necessary so that he cannot claim that he was joking or that he admitted merely so as not to appear wealthy, as stated in siman 81, seif 17. In an admission made through ma'amad sheloshtan those defenses are unavailable even without a kinyan, as stated there and below in seif 13. Here, however, the case is outside ma'amad sheloshtan. Shach.
