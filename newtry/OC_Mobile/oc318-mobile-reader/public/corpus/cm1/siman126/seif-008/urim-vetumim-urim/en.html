Whether a small gift or a large gift. Sema says this does not refer to a poor recipient. If the recipient is poor, the donor must keep his word even if no third person is involved, either because a statement to charity is like delivery to the Temple treasury or because of a vow. The Shach disagrees and says the seif applies even to a poor person. Nevertheless, the debt held by another person is not acquired without maamad sheloshtan. See Tumim. The practical law clearly follows the Shach, although I wrote that the Shulchan Aruch's phrasing made Sema's interpretation understandable. The Shach adds that with a deposit the donor certainly cannot retract when the recipient is poor, because the custodian can acquire on his behalf, except where the deposited money is permitted for the custodian's use, in which case it resembles a loan. I already disagreed with that point above in siman 47. See also what I wrote in Tumim to siman 74.
In his possession. A kinyan from the giver does not help for a loan, because a loan is given for expenditure, nor generally for deposited coins, because currency is not acquired through exchange, as explained in siman 203. But if the deposited property is movable goods rather than currency, a kinyan from the giver does help. Shach.
An admission. The same applies if he obligates himself by creating a new personal obligation to pay. Shach.
To retract. Although an admission ordinarily works even without a kinyan, here the kinyan is needed so that he cannot later claim that the admission was merely joking or made to avoid appearing wealthy, as explained in siman 81. An admission made through maamad sheloshtan itself is not subject to those pleas even without a kinyan, but the present case is without maamad sheloshtan. Shach. See above in simanim 81 and 245.
