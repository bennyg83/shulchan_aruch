See there: And my father the Rosh wrote likewise in the chapter HaMekabel. Beit Yosef asks that there the money was not in the moneychanger's hands for the employer, and moreover even an express release was ineffective. One point answers the other. There, even an express release is no stronger when the moneychanger holds nothing than an ordinary acceptance here where he does hold funds. Just as there the recipient may return against the employer because he can say, “My express release was mistaken,” so here he may say, “I mistakenly agreed to collect from this person.” This is precise in the Rosh's language: if the moneychanger refuses to pay, the recipient certainly returns against the employer, even if he said in the three-party setting, “I rely on the moneychanger,” because the moneychanger can retract when he holds nothing. Thus, specifically where the moneychanger holds nothing may the recipient return even after expressly releasing the employer. If the payer holds funds, an express release prevents return. It follows that where he holds funds the recipient may still return so long as he did not expressly release the transferor. According to the Rosh, therefore, Rama's rule in seif 2 that a complete release is effective must be understood accordingly. The Tur identifies the chapter where the Rosh wrote this so that the reader can see that the implications are exact although not stated expressly.
