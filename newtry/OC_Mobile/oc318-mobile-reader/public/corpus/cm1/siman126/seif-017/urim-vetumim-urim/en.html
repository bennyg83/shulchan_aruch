And he became obligated, etc. Meaning through a kinyan, a document, or another legally effective obligation to pay an actual debt. This is unlike the next siman, which concerns compensation for services. The text mentions a particular time for greater novelty; all the more so if the obligation is immediate. Shach.
With consent, etc. Tur cites Rosh: a merchant told a broker to receive money on his behalf from a money changer, and the money changer retained it for a debt owed by the broker. If the broker truly owes him, he lawfully retained it. Beit Yosef struggled with the case. Perishah and Bach correctly explain that the merchant made the broker only an ordinary agent, but when standing with the money changer he said, 'Give it to him,' without mentioning that he was merely an agent. The Shach gives a remote interpretation that the broker held the merchant's own money and retained it for brokerage fees. See Tumim. I showed that the Shach's reading is strained and inconsistent with Rosh and Tur. The correct explanation is Bach and Perishah without hesitation.
