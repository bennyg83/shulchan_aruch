Since at the time he said to give, etc. The Tur cites the Rosh: Since Reuven had become obligated to the community because the tax farming agreement had already been finalized in a manner from which no party could retract without the consent of all, Shimon acquired the money through the three party transfer and Reuven must give it to him. Although the underlying tax farming agreement was later canceled, Shimon does not lose because Reuven voluntarily agreed to cancel it. However, if Reuven is a violent person who does not obey beit din, or has no property, the community must pay Shimon, because the community was not discharged merely by the recipient's silence unless he expressly released them. This follows the second opinion stated by the Mechaber in seif 9. The Mechaber may have omitted the end of the responsum because he was not addressing that dispute here and relied upon his earlier ruling. The Tur later cites another responsum of the Rosh concerning Reuven who claimed a maneh from Shimon and Shimon replied that Levi had paid him on Shimon's behalf. Its main point, that once Shimon had directed him through a three party transfer and the transferor believed he had thereby been paid, even though nothing was actually in the third party's hands, we presume he did not pay a second time, is already stated by the Mechaber in seif 6. The Tur also cites a responsum involving a merchant who instructed a broker, which the Mechaber omitted because in the Beit Yosef he disagreed with that ruling. Rama, who defended it in Darkei Moshe, might have cited it, but perhaps omitted it because according to his explanation it adds little. In the Perishah I explained that responsum in a manner that avoids the Beit Yosef's objections.
