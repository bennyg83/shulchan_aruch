Maamad sheloshtan through an agent, etc. It is ineffective. The Rosh proves this from the incident of Isur the convert. Why did Isur not simply send an agent to Rava to effect the transfer by maamad sheloshtan? What did Rava gain by saying, "If it is maamad sheloshtan, I will not go"? According to those commentators who hold that maamad sheloshtan cannot be imposed against the holder's will, one could answer differently. Rava said only, "I will not go," rather than, "I refuse to consent," because he would have been embarrassed to tell Isur directly that he would not agree. If Isur sent an agent, however, Rava could simply tell the agent, "I do not consent," and the transfer would fail. Therefore the Ramban, who holds that maamad sheloshtan requires consent, does not derive the rule from Isur's incident but states it only from logical reasoning. This is precise.
Some say that it is effective. See the Shach, who notes that although we do not find an express dissenting authority, the Mechaber nevertheless states the ruling as "some say." In my view the Mechaber considered Rabbenu Ephraim to disagree. The Baal HaTerumot, Gate 28, end of part 4, cites Rabbenu Ephraim objecting to the Rif's explanation in chapter 4 and 5 that Rabbi Yehudah's statement "the hand of the poor is our hand" operates through maamad sheloshtan. Rabbenu Ephraim argues: how can the poor acquire? If Reuven says to Shimon in Levi's presence, "The maneh of mine in your hand, give it to Levi so that he should acquire it for Yehudah," Yehudah acquires nothing until the money actually reaches Levi's hand, because the original transferor did not directly transfer anything to Yehudah. All the more so where the statement was merely "give it to the poor." Rabbi Yehudah cannot acquire on behalf of all the poor, even if he represents the great beit din. If Rabbenu Ephraim held that an agent of the recipient can receive a maamad sheloshtan transfer for him, what is his question? Rabbi Yehudah was effectively the poor people's receiving agent. A charity treasurer is even stronger than an ordinary agent, and beit din certainly so. Terumat HaDeshen writes concerning an apotropos acquiring for orphans that such a representative is stronger than an ordinary agent. When the donor said "give it to the poor," Rabbi Yehudah represented the poor and his hand was like theirs. There is no need for all the poor to stand there personally. The Gidulei Terumah himself writes similarly, and the Shach likewise notes it in seif katan 26. It is therefore clear that Rabbenu Ephraim and the Baal HaTerumot hold that the special halachah of maamad sheloshtan does not operate through a receiving agent. A charity treasurer is no stronger than a full receiving agent for this purpose. Their question against the Rif is then straightforward. This is why the Mechaber uses the formulation "some say." In practical law we follow the Rif and those who agree with him rather than Rabbenu Ephraim and Baal HaTerumot, but there is indeed a dissenting view. This issue may also depend on differing textual versions of the incident in Bava Batra. According to the version that says Rav Mari bar Rachel was in the study hall, it is necessarily proved that maamad sheloshtan can be effected through an agent. Rava said, "If it is maamad sheloshtan, I will not go," even though Rav Mari himself was not present. The transfer would therefore have had to operate through someone standing for him. But the Ramban in his chiddushim notes that there are variant readings and not all versions say Rav Mari was elsewhere. According to those versions one can maintain that maamad sheloshtan does not work through an agent. The Sages instituted only the precise mechanism they enacted. Nevertheless, the Rosh's logic from the incident in the chapter Shor Shenagach remains persuasive. This is precise.
He can retract. The Sema in seif katan 56 explains that the transferor can retract before the money reaches Levi, but once it reaches Levi, Levi acquires it for Yehudah. The Shach in seif katan 87 challenges him, but the Shach did not speak correctly. The Baal HaTerumot expressly states this in the passage cited above in the name of Rabbenu Ephraim: "If Reuven says to Shimon in Levi's presence, 'Give the maneh in your hand to Levi so that he acquire it for Yehudah,' so long as it has not reached Levi's hand, Yehudah has acquired nothing and the other did not transfer anything to him." This plainly implies that once it reaches Levi's hand, Yehudah does acquire. The reason is simple. By saying, "Give it to Levi so that he acquire it for Yehudah," the transferor has effectively told Levi, "Acquire it for Yehudah." Once the money actually reaches Levi, he acquires it for Yehudah through ordinary agency for acquisition. This is simple and explicit.
