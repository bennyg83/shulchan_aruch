Like an agent. As below in siman 227. However, another person may transfer property to orphans in the presence of their guardian, as Rama rules in siman 290 seif 11. Even according to the view reflected by the Mechaber below that an assignee's own agent may not suffice for three-party assignment, all agree that a guardian's hand is superior for acquiring on behalf of orphans. Rosh, citing Rabbeinu Chananel, states this principle, and Tur and Shulchan Aruch at the beginning of siman 105 also indicate it. Thus Rama in siman 290 allows acquisition by three-party assignment in the presence of an orphan's guardian, while here regarding an ordinary agent he cites only an opinion that it works. Shach.

This differs from the law in Yoreh Deah siman 258 seif 8, where one says that a debt owed to him by a particular person shall be charity, and if he says this in the presence of the debtor and the charity collector, the collector acquires by three-party assignment. There the collector's hand is legally the hand of the poor, as though the poor themselves were present. This is unlike Baal HaTerumot, who compares the cases and objects to Rif's ruling that the poor acquire by three-party assignment. In Shach's view Rif is correct, as Gedolei Terumah also writes.
