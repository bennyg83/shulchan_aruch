He is like an agent. This is as explained below in siman 227:30.
He cannot transfer. Another person may nevertheless transfer property to orphans in the presence of their guardian. Terumat HaDeshen states this, and Rama rules likewise below in siman 290:11. Even if some dispute whether the recipient's appointed agent can participate in an assignment before all three, all agree regarding an orphans' guardian. The Rosh writes in the name of Rabbeinu Chananel that, for acquiring on behalf of orphans, a guardian's legal hand is stronger than that of an ordinary agent. This is also evident from the Tur and Mechaber at the beginning of siman 105. Therefore Rama states without qualification in siman 290 that a transfer may be made in the presence of an orphans' guardian, whereas here, concerning an ordinary agent, he follows the Mechaber's formulation, “There is one who says,” implying possible disagreement.
There is one who says. I do not know why the Mechaber uses this formulation, because no authority appears to dispute the rule. Perhaps he inferred a dispute from the Tur's words, “It appears that even according to my father.” He may have understood Ramban's general statement that assignment in the presence of all three is ineffective through an agent to include the recipient's agent, with the Rosh disagreeing. In my view, that is not the Tur's meaning. Ramban did not address the recipient's agent. The Tur means that even according to the Rosh, who expressly permits participation by the recipient's appointed agent, the result differs if the recipient never appointed the person and the assignor merely said that the person should acquire for him. Rabbeinu Yerucham states explicitly that an assignment through the assignor's agent is ineffective, but if the recipient appointed an agent it is effective, and attributes this distinction to Ramban. Perhaps the Mechaber wrote “There is one who says” merely because he did not find the rule stated expressly by many other authorities. For practical law, assignment through the recipient's agent is effective according to all. Ramban's contrary novelty concerns only the assignor's agent, and even that rule is not found among most other authorities. Their silence would ordinarily indicate that the general principle, “A person's agent is like himself,” applies.
It is effective. For removing property from the assignor through his own agent, we say that the exceptional enactment should not be extended beyond its stated terms. For acquiring on behalf of the recipient, however, the ordinary rule applies that the recipient's agent is legally equivalent to him. See Responsa Maharshal, siman 359.
If the recipient did not appoint him as an agent, but said, “Give it to Levi so that he acquire it.” This implies that if the recipient did appoint Levi as his agent, then even where Reuven says to Shimon in Levi's presence, “Give it to Levi so that he acquire it for Yehudah, the recipient,” Yehudah acquires. Since Levi is the recipient's agent and all three are together, Reuven's direction is understood not as a separate act of acquisition by an unauthorized third party, but as an instruction that Levi acquire immediately in Yehudah's name pursuant to his appointment, through the enactment of the presence of all three. Gidulei Terumah states likewise.
Give it to Levi so that he may acquire it for Yehudah, etc. The Bach writes that only with this wording did Yehudah fail to acquire it. If, however, at the time of the assignment he merely said, ‘Give it to Levi,’ then even though he had initially arranged with Levi that Levi should acquire it for Yehudah, Levi already acquired it for Yehudah through the law of an assignment in the presence of all three parties, and neither party may retract. The Bach proves this from the incident involving the merchant and the broker. In my humble opinion, this is incorrect. Since Levi is not to acquire the money for himself but only for Yehudah, and Levi cannot acquire it for Yehudah before anything reaches his possession, the mere wording ‘Give it to Levi’ does not help. Nor is there proof from the merchant and broker. There the money changer can say that, because the instruction was simply to give the money to the broker, he assumed that it belonged to the broker himself. Here, however, it is known that the money does not belong to Levi, so by what means should Yehudah acquire it?
To Levi so that he may acquire it, etc. This is not comparable to the ruling that one who says, in the presence of his debtor and the charity collector or another distinguished townsman, that a debt owed to him shall belong to charity, causes the collector or distinguished person to acquire it through an assignment in the presence of all three parties. There, the collector’s hand is regarded as the hand of the poor themselves, as though the poor were standing there and acquiring it directly. This is not an acquisition performed on their behalf. It resembles an administrator for orphans, whose standing is stronger than that of an ordinary agent because it is as though the orphans themselves were present. Therefore, the Rif’s ruling that the poor acquire in this manner is correct, unlike the comparison made by the Baal HaTerumot. See also the Gedolei Terumah and Yam Shel Shlomo.
He may retract. The Tur explains that Levi cannot acquire the maneh for Yehudah until it comes into Levi’s possession, and therefore Yehudah has acquired nothing. The Sema understands this to mean only that Yehudah did not acquire through the assignment while the money was still with Shimon, but that once Shimon gives the maneh to Levi at Reuven’s direction, Yehudah certainly acquires it. In my humble opinion, even after Shimon gives it to Levi, Yehudah has not acquired it by virtue of the prior assignment, because the three-party standing has already ended. Only where Reuven initially used language of delivery or acquisition, such as ‘Give the maneh to Yehudah,’ ‘Bring it to him,’ or ‘Acquire it for him,’ does Yehudah acquire when the money later reaches Levi, under the ordinary laws of acquisition discussed in siman 125.
