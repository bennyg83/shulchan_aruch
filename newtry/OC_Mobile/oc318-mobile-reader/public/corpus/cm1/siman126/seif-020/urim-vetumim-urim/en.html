It is not effective. The Tur gives the reason that ma'amad sheloshtan is a law without an articulated rationale, and therefore we should not extend it beyond its stated scope. See Sema. See Tumim, where I noted that the Rosh brings a proof from the case of Isur Giyora, but in my opinion that case is not such a strong proof. See there for the reasoning.
Like an agent, etc. As explained below in siman 227. But another person may transfer property to orphans in the presence of their guardians, as the Rama writes in siman 290. Even according to the Mechaber's view below that if the recipient sent his own agent there is an opinion that the transfer does not work, everyone agrees in the case of guardians, because the hand of a guardian to acquire for orphans is stronger than that of an ordinary agent, as the Rosh writes in the first chapter of Bava Metzia. This explains why the Rama here rules without qualification in accordance with the Mechaber. See Shach.
There is an opinion, etc. That it is effective. The Shach questions why the Mechaber uses the expression 'there is an opinion,' since no one appears to disagree. See what I wrote on this. In Tumim I suggested that the Mechaber had in mind Rabbeinu Ephraim, cited by the Baal HaTerumot, who disagrees with the Rif's interpretation in Bava Kamma that Rabbi Yose's statement, 'We are the hand of the poor,' is based on ma'amad sheloshtan. A charity administrator is certainly stronger than an ordinary agent. See there at length. I also concluded that the matter may depend on variant readings in the case of Isur Giyora. As practical law, the Rosh's view appears correct, and for that reason the Mechaber used the expression 'there is an opinion.'
He may retract. The Sema writes that this means before the money reaches Levi, but afterward Levi acquires it for Yehudah. The Shach disagrees. See Tumim, where I agreed with the Sema.
