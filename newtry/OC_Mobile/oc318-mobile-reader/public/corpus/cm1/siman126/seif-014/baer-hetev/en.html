And collects. The Sema writes that because the transferor admits that nothing was actually owed to him, and because his act caused the substitute debtor to pay the recipient, the substitute debtor may recover from the transferor. The same applies in the first case where the substitute debtor paid a debt but the recipient is violent or otherwise judgment proof. The Tur states this explicitly. The Shach questions why the transferor should be liable. He can say, "Had I known there was a mistake, I would not have told you to pay." The fact that the substitute debtor paid because of his instruction is only indirect causation, for which siman 386 ordinarily exempts. Further, the transferor can say, "You caused the problem yourself by failing to remember and making the mistake. Just as you erred, I erred too. What did I do to you?" See the Shach for his proposed resolution.
