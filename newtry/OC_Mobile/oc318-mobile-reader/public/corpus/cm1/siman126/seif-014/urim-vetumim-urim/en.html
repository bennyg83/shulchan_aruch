And he then collects again, etc. Sema explains that the transferor admits he did not actually owe the transferee, and through his act he caused the payer to be compelled to pay the recipient. The same applies earlier if he paid because of the assigned debt and the recipient is a powerful person or otherwise uncollectible: he can recover from the transferor. Tur states this explicitly. The Shach objects that this appears to be only indirect causation. See Tumim. I wrote that it is actionable causation because maamad sheloshtan can be imposed without the payer's consent. I also explained that the matter may depend on the dispute whether such causation is treated as strict law or as a penalty. See there. I further concluded that the transferor must stand by his statement that he erred. See there for the reasoning.
