He may return and collect it. See Shach, seif katan 67, who asks that this is only indirect causation. It may be answered as follows. If Reuven's object was deposited with Shimon, and Reuven mistakenly thought that it belonged to Shimon and acknowledged to Shimon that it was his, after which Shimon gave the object as a gift to a non Jew, Shimon must pay. A person must exercise greater care not to damage another than to avoid being damaged himself, as Tosafot write in Bava Kamma and in several places. Since one who causes direct damage is liable even when acting inadvertently, it makes no difference whether the mistake arose through another person or through the owner of the object himself. The same principle applies here.
