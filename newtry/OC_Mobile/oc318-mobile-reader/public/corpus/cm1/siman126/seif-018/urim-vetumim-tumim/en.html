Reuven rented an object, etc. This is because rental payment is due only at the end. The Shach in seif katan 76 questions this. Even though payment is due only at the end, we rule that rental liability accrues continuously from beginning to end. Therefore, the portion already earned at the time of the transfer is an existing debt, merely not yet due. Why should it not be transferable by maamad sheloshtan? His substantive point is correct. The Rosh's actual case, however, was different. A person rented an implement, for example a tool used to bore a precious stone, and agreed that for every day the boring process lasted, whether many days or few, he would pay a dinar daily for use of the tool. Initially they expected the work to take a long time, so that the total rental would reach one hundred dinar, and the owner transferred that anticipated rental by maamad sheloshtan. In fact the work was completed quickly, so the earned rental amounted only to fifty. This is clear from the Rosh's own account. Accordingly, until the boring was actually completed, the rental obligation remained contingent. If an unavoidable accident occurred before completion, for example a fire or armed robbers destroyed the tool and the precious stone, the renter might owe no rental at all even for the earlier days, under the rule of "this ship and this wine," where if the ship breaks and payment was not prepaid, the owner does not collect. Likewise, even if the stone itself remained intact but no substitute tool could be found to complete the boring, he might be exempt from the prior rental, as the Tur and Shulchan Aruch rule below in siman 310. Thus before the work was completed, it was not merely that the time for payment had not arrived. It was uncertain whether any enforceable payment obligation would ever mature. Tosafot and most poskim explain that a ketubah cannot be transferred by maamad sheloshtan because not only is its payment date not yet reached, but it is also uncertain whether it will ever become collectible, since the wife may die during the husband's lifetime. This rental claim is similar. Therefore the Rosh and Tur correctly rule that maamad sheloshtan does not acquire it. Their statement that "rental is paid only at the end" means that the obligation is not finally fixed for payment until the agreed rental use or task is complete. If completion never occurs because of the relevant accident, the earlier rental may be void retroactively.
Now I saw in the abbreviated work of the Gaon Maharg that he attempts to answer the Shach from Tosafot in Ketubot, who write that a daughter's one-tenth inheritance portion can be collected from house rent even for the half-year already elapsed during the father's lifetime. I do not understand the relevance. Maharg himself cites the Ran that such rent is treated like land, analogous to produce attached to the ground and still needing the ground. Here the rental of an object is a movable claim. Why should maamad sheloshtan not apply to it merely because house rent has a land-like quality for another law? Perhaps the Gaon's reading of the Ran is that house rent is considered attached and still needing the land because if the house reaches a dangerous condition for dwelling, even without collapsing entirely, the landlord may be obligated to repair it, as stated in siman 312 in the name of the Rosh, and the cost can be taken from the rent. The rent therefore still depends upon preservation of the land, and if the house fails in the relevant manner the rent may disappear. In that sense it resembles produce that still needs the ground. If so, one could analogize this case too to what I wrote above, namely that the rental claim remains contingent until completion. This is possible, though it seems unlikely that this was the Gaon's intent. According to the poskim below in siman 312 who reject the Rosh's repair rule, the simpler explanation of the one-tenth inheritance case is that the reason daughters do not ordinarily collect that portion from movable property is lack of reliance. Where contemporary practice creates sufficient reliance, they do collect. House rent, even though it accrues from beginning to end and has not yet reached its payment date, is treated like land for this limited purpose because the obligation arises from land and is sufficiently secure that the daughters rely upon it. This is a rule about the daughter's reliance, not about whether the claim is an existing movable debt transferable through maamad sheloshtan. It therefore has no bearing on the Shach's case. What I wrote above resolves the Rosh without difficulty.
