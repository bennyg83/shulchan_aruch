Rent is payable only at the end. This ruling is difficult, although it comes from the Tur in the name of the Rosh. Even though rent is not collectible until completion, the employer becomes indebted as the work proceeds. The obligation is comparable to a loan not yet due. Ritva writes that all agree that wages accrue from beginning to end. If only part of the work is performed, the worker is paid proportionately, and if the object is lost through unavoidable circumstances the owners owe the accrued amount, although payment is not due until the work is completed. Tosafot, Ran, and the Rosh likewise state that wages accrue throughout the work, and the Gemara treats the accrued sum as a loan. This is the accepted law. Yoreh Deah 143 accordingly rules that wages paid for constructing idolatry are permitted where the final hammer blow has no value, because the wages accrued throughout the work before the prohibited object was completed. In the laws of kiddushin, all authorities and the Tur rule that where a woman owes the man wages, kiddushin effected with that debt is invalid even after payment became due, because the wages had already accrued as a loan and one cannot betroth with a loan. One might answer that earned wages are treated as prospective property that never physically entered the worker's hands, and creditors and a ketubah do not collect from such property. The Sages may therefore not have enacted assignment in the presence of all three for wages, because the employer cannot truthfully say, “I have a maneh of yours in my hands.” Yet this does not fit the Rosh, Tur, and Mechaber. If that were their reason, it would apply even if wages were payable from the beginning. They instead rely specifically upon the rule that rent is payable only at the end. Their language therefore indicates that assignment before all three can apply even to an obligation that never physically entered the creditor's hands, as I wrote above. Their ruling here remains difficult.
