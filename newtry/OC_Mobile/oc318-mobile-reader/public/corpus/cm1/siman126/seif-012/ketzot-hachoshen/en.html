If Reuven says in a three party arrangement, "The debt instrument that I have against you, give it to Levi," the Shach in siman 66 leaves the law unresolved because Ramban holds that a debt instrument is not transferred by a three party arrangement. The Tur and Shulchan Aruch rule straightforwardly that it is. I likewise found Tosafot in the first chapter of Gittin and in Perek Mi SheMet supporting the view that three party transfer does not apply to debt instruments. Ran, however, holds that it does. Ran asks why, according to the view that a deathly ill person's instruction "give one hundred to so and so" is established as a three party transfer, the same reasoning does not work in the opening clause concerning a bill of emancipation. He answers that three party transfer operates where the recipient could have acquired the object himself, under the principle, "Since he could acquire for himself, he can acquire for another." A bill of emancipation cannot be acquired by the messenger for himself, so the mechanism is unavailable. His argument presupposes that three party transfer can apply even to a document in the appropriate setting. The dispute therefore remains substantial, and one should not disregard Ramban and Tosafot where money is being extracted.
