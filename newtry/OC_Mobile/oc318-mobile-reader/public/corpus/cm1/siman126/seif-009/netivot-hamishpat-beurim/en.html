But if the recipient asked the transferor to make the assignment through ma'amad sheloshtan, and because of that assignment the transferor can no longer claim against the third party, it is as though the transferor released the third party at the recipient's request. If the third party later became impoverished, the transferor was released at the recipient's request and the recipient is liable as a guarantor. The Rosh already holds that even release from the prohibition of delaying wages can generate guarantor liability, all the more so here. Accordingly, in seif katan 42 as well, where Shach disputes Sema, Sema is correct in such a case. Because of the assignment, the third party is believed to say that he paid the recipient and is thereby discharged. The recipient caused this result by requesting the assignment, so it is as though the third party was released at the recipient's request, and the recipient has the law of a guarantor. See Shach, seif katan 39, where there is a scribal error. It should read: 'Even though he later became impoverished, he may return against the transferor,' and this is the correct ruling.
