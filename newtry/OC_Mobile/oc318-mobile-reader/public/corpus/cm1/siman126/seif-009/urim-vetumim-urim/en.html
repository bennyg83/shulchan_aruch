He cannot. From the moment he transferred him to Shimon through ma'amad sheloshtan, he was discharged from the original debtor. See Shach.
And some say that he can, etc. The Tur cites this in the name of his father, the Rosh, in chapter HaMekabel. The Beit Yosef asks that the Rosh there discusses a shopkeeper who has nothing of the debtor's in his possession. The Perishah, and similarly the Bach, understand that since the Rosh holds that even an express release can be a waiver made in error, the Tur understood that ma'amad sheloshtan cannot be stronger than an express release. See Tumim, where I wrote that their explanation is strained to the point of being virtually unintelligible, and offered a broad and satisfactory resolution.
Initially, etc. Meaning, this is the basic legal order, for the second party is only in the position of a guarantor. But it is obvious that if collection from Shimon is not readily possible, the creditor may collect from Reuven. This is the intent of the Shach's lengthy discussion.
In accordance with the latter opinion, etc. The Shach enumerates many authorities who hold that he cannot retract. He cites the Ritva in Kiddushin as holding that he can retract, based on a difficulty, and writes that the Ritva's words are not understood. The Shach therefore rules without doubt that the burden of proof is on the claimant. See Tumim, where I explained that the Ritva's words are correct and the Shach's objection does not apply. Nevertheless, I concluded that the Ritva's difficulty provides no proof that retraction is permitted. See there at length.
And if he was negligent, etc. He is exempt. See Tumim, where I explained why this differs from the rule below in siman 131, that if a guarantor tells the creditor to sue the borrower and the creditor negligently delays until the borrower loses his property, the guarantor nevertheless remains liable. The cases can be distinguished.
And if the custodian says that he gave it. This discussion is misplaced here. Its proper place is above in siman 91, where I discussed at length the Sema's and Shach's remarks written here.
