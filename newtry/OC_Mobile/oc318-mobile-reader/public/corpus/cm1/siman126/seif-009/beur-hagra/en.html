If Reuven said... This follows from the Yerushalmi cited above: "This was said where they did not..." Even though according to the worker's version the shopkeeper does not wish to pay him, and although we do not rule in accordance with that Yerushalmi for another reason, the dispute of Rabbah and Rav Sheshet as to whether he may retract concerns where nothing was owed to him. It implies that if the third party did owe him, he certainly cannot retract. Likewise, Yerushalmi Kiddushin 3:4, cited by the Rif in HaMekabel, states: Reuven owed Shimon, and transferred the claim to Levi; if Levi became impoverished, Reuven is no longer liable to Shimon, as stated above in seif 10.
Some say that he may... The Baal HaItur explains the Yerushalmi as speaking where he expressly released the original debtor, for then everyone agrees he cannot return to him, since a waiver requires no kinyan, as Tosafos and the Rosh write. They prove this from Gittin 78, "Throw my debt," Bava Metzia 71, "he placed it on the ground," and Kiddushin 16. The Rosh establishes the dispute of Rabbah and Rav Sheshet even where the money changer himself cannot retract and the creditor can collect from him, because in such a case ma'amad sheloshtan prevents retraction. Even if... As stated in that Yerushalmi: "This was said where they did not act by collusion, but if they acted by collusion he is liable." According to this view, the Yerushalmi is speaking where he expressly released him.
Accordingly, we do not... Meaning, where he did not expressly release him, as stated in the gloss above, and so rules the Shach.
If he was negligent... The Gaon explains the Yerushalmi in Kiddushin as a case where he was negligent, interpreting "he became impoverished" to mean that afterward he became poor. The Yerushalmi's phrase "where they did not act by collusion" means that he had been solvent, and because of the creditor's delay he lost his opportunity.
