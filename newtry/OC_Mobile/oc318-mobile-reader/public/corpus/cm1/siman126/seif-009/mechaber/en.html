If Reuven says to Shimon in the presence of all three, 'Give Levi the one hundred of mine that you hold,' and Shimon puts Levi off, Levi cannot return and sue Reuven. Some say he may proceed against Reuven as long as he did not expressly release him. Even if he expressly released Reuven, some say he may proceed against the giver if the giver deceived him in the manner explained below in seif 10, as stated in Mahariv 33. Even according to this view, however, he may not initially sue Reuven while he can still collect from Shimon. {Rama: It appears proper to rule according to the latter view. We therefore do not need the distinctions written below in se'ifim 10 and 11, which follow the first view. If the recipient was negligent and did not demand the money until the custodian became impoverished, so that he can no longer collect from him, all agree that he cannot proceed against the giver. It is as though the giver showed him money from which to collect his debt and he failed to take it, in which case the giver is exempt. If the custodian claims he paid, see above, siman 91, seif 3, regarding whether the giver is exempt.}
