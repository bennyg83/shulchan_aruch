It is documented, and Reuven must bring proof. The reason given is that with a document he lacks a migo that he could have claimed repayment. I do not fully understand. If there are witnesses to the maamad sheloshtan transfer, then he already has witnesses to what amounts to payment, and the document has been weakened. The only dispute is whether the transfer was valid because the assignee was wealthy at the time or was already impoverished. Why should the burden not lie on the claimant under the ordinary rule that the one seeking to extract must prove his case? The strength of a document is ordinarily that the debtor cannot say, "I repaid you," because the creditor still holds the document. Here, however, witnesses establish the transfer, so there is no longer a "why is your document in my hand" argument. I initially thought the case concerned no witnesses to the transfer. Then with an oral loan the debtor would be believed regarding everything through a migo, while with a document he would not. But Rambam and Baal HaTerumot do not sound this way. We must therefore say that the claim, "he was wealthy and afterward became poor," is an inherently weak claim. We ordinarily preserve the prior chazakah and assume a person's financial status continued as it had been. With an oral loan, the debtor's migo permits even such a weak claim. With a document, where there is no migo, we do not accept this weak assertion and instead assume that the person was already poor at the time of transfer.
