By document. Meaning an authenticated document, as stated at the beginning of siman 82. Gidulei Terumah notes that the law does not present the case where Levi claims, "I did not know he was poor," while Reuven says he told him privately and Levi nevertheless accepted. This might imply that Levi cannot make such a claim, because we regard it as self-evident that a rational person would not voluntarily lose his money for nothing, and even an oral debt would make the opposing migo ineffective against this certainty. The Shach disagrees. With a documented debt Reuven may require Levi to take a shevuat heset that he did not know the debtor was poor, because Reuven makes a definite claim that he informed him and Levi accepted. This is no weaker than claiming, "You waived the documented debt," where siman 82 implies that after payment he can require a shevuat heset according to all views. With an oral debt, Levi may claim, "You waived it," through a migo that he could have denied the debt entirely or claimed payment, as stated in siman 75, seif 7. We do not say that rational people never make such agreements, because perhaps Reuven persuaded him and he agreed to waive. The same applies here. Therefore, if the debt is oral and Reuven claims definitely that Levi knew the other debtor was poor and nevertheless accepted, Levi is believed through a migo of denial or payment. The case is not mentioned because ordinarily Levi would not know of the poverty and accept, and uncommon cases are not discussed. Another explanation is that the law here wishes to distinguish between a document containing ne'emanut and one without it. Regarding a claim of waiver there are two views: some say even with ne'emanut the debtor may require the lender to swear that he did not waive; others treat the claim like a claim that the document was a trust document, so even without ne'emanut the debtor cannot require such an oath. Thus waiver does not provide a useful distinction between documents with and without ne'emanut. The claim that the substitute debtor was wealthy and later became poor does, because it resembles a claim of payment. Shach.
Shall swear. He swears while holding a sacred object if Reuven says, "Swear to me," as stated in siman 82, seif 2. Shach.
