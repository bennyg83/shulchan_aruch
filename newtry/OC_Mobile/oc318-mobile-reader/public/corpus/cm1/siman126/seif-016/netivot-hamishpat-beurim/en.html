Certainly, if from the outset Shimon knew that he was mistaken and that he owed Reuven nothing, yet on Reuven's instruction he gave Levi a dinar, he cannot afterward recover it from Levi. Therefore, when he paid and witnesses later established that he had been mistaken, we say that before making payment he carefully examined the matter and discovered the mistake. The general rule is that before making a payment a person carefully verifies the facts, and this is even stronger than the presumption applied to an admission. Consequently, what he paid was paid as Reuven's agent, and he can no longer claim it from Reuven. Although witnesses testify that he had been mistaken, they cannot testify that he still did not know of the mistake before payment. God alone examines the heart. Since there are no witnesses on that point, the presumption that a person carefully verifies before paying remains in force. We therefore say that he knew of the mistake, paid as Reuven's agent, and Levi acquired what he received.
