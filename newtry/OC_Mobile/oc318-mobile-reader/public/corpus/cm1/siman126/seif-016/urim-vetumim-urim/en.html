To extract from him. We say that what he already gave was given in order to uphold his own words, even if it is now known that he was not legally obligated. A person may prefer to preserve his credibility. He may therefore recover only the excess beyond what he actually paid. Tur. Sema holds this applies where the original admission was not before witnesses. If it was before witnesses, he is not believed even regarding the excess to say, 'I erred,' because one ordinarily checks his accounts carefully before admitting before witnesses. Perishah says the same. The Shach disputes this and says Rosh's responsum can be read even where the admission was before witnesses. See Tumim. I wrote that the plain reading of Rosh is closer to Perishah, although the two answers in Rosh may depend on the dispute whether maamad sheloshtan works when he did not say, 'the maneh I have in your hands.' Rosh follows his own view. Bach further holds that even if witnesses establish actual error, what was already paid cannot be recovered. The Shach leaves that doubtful. In Tumim I agreed with Bach.
