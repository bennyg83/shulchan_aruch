For presumably it was on account of the one hundred, etc. This implies that if he explicitly said, “Lend me one hundred and give it to him,” then even though he already owed the recipient one hundred, the recipient does not acquire according to any opinion. And some say that he did not acquire. It appears to me that this dissent applies only where the assignor owed the third party exactly one hundred. If he owed him more and said, “Give one hundred to Levi,” Levi acquires through an assembly of three. The reason is straightforward. This also resolves all the proofs cited by the Baal HaTerumot, gate 28, beginning of part 3, in the name of a manuscript of the Raavan, against this dissenting view. However, the reasoning and wording of Responsa Rosh, as cited by the Tur, appear at first glance to dispute even this. The Rosh writes that an assembly of three is a law without an intrinsic rationale, and we have only what the Sages instituted, namely: “You have one hundred of mine in your possession; give it to so-and-so.” Nevertheless, as a matter of law, I would decide as I have written. I later saw that the Mechaber writes in Bedek HaBayit: “As a matter of halakhah, since the Ramban’s proofs are numerous, we follow his view.” In my humble opinion, the correct ruling is as I wrote, and it appears that the Mechaber decided only the case where the third party owed more than one hundred, on the strength of the Ramban’s proofs. This explains why the Shulchan Aruch cites both opinions here without deciding. It is strained to say that by presenting the Ramban’s position anonymously and the Rosh’s as “some say,” he implicitly decided for the first view. When the Mechaber reaches a conclusion in the Beit Yosef or Bedek HaBayit, his normal practice is to state the adopted view without citing the dissent in the Shulchan Aruch. He therefore must have brought the dissent here only for the case where the third party owed exactly one hundred. Examine this carefully. See also Responsa Maharam ibn Sasson, siman 111.
He did not acquire. Since acquisition by an assembly of three is an unexplained enactment, we have only the precise form instituted by the Sages: “You have one hundred of mine; give it to so-and-so.” If he did not mention the one hundred in the third party’s possession, the statement appears no more than a guarantee undertaken after the money was advanced. The Aruch HaShulchan and Sema so explain. Their words clearly imply that he is not liable even under the law of guaranty, although he holds Reuven’s money. Responsa Rosh, rule 89, decision 6, likewise indicates this. See what I write below in siman 129, seif 3, in the gloss.
