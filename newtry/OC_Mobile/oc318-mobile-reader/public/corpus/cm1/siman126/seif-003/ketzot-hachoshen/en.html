Some say he did not acquire. See Rosh, who explains that because ma'amad sheloshtan is a halacha without an articulated reason, we limit it to its precise form, namely, 'the maneh I have in your hand.' The first opinion, that he acquires even without that exact wording, is Ramban's view, cited by Baal HaTerumot, gate 28. Ramban's principal proof is Gittin 12a: if one said, 'Give one hundred to so-and-so,' and died, it is given after death. Rav Zevid establishes the case according to Rav, requiring the money to be gathered and deposited, under the rule of ma'amad sheloshtan. Yet the Mishnah does not say, 'the maneh I have in your hand,' but simply, 'give one hundred.' Gidulei Terumah objects that according to Rav Pappa, who establishes the case as a dying person's transfer and is concerned for a buried maneh, the case must necessarily involve the words 'this maneh'; otherwise one could suspect the specified money was buried. Since the Mishnah omits those words there too, perhaps Rav Zevid can likewise understand it as though the precise words were implicit. But according to what I wrote in siman 202 resolving Maharsha's difficulty, that Mishnah does not concern a specifically identified maneh. Ramban's proof therefore stands.
