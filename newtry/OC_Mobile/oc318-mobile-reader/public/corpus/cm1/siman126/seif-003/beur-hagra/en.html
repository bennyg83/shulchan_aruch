Reuven said, etc. An unqualified instruction to give means from the speaker's own property, as in the case of Geniva, 'Give four hundred,' and in the instruction to give four hundred zuz for a ketubah. The same language appears throughout the laws of deathbed gifts. Tosafot in Gittin explains that even an unspecified maneh is given. The mishna's case of 'Give a maneh' is established as a transfer in the presence of all three, necessarily without saying 'this maneh' or 'from my property.' According to our law, the money need not even be gathered together. See citations 37 and 100.
And some say, etc. According to this view, the mishna concerns one who said, 'from my property,' following the Gemara's reading, 'this maneh from my property.' The proof is that the Gemara requires him to say, 'I have a maneh in your possession.' It cannot be merely to teach the distinction stated above between a deposit and a loan, for then it should have framed the case explicitly as a deposit according to the view that the enactment applies only to deposits. Rather, Rav addresses the precise wording that must be used toward the third party. See citation 95.
