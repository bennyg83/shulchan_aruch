And some say. This is the view of Rabbi Meir HaLevi Abulafia and Rosh in responsum. Their reason is that maamad sheloshtan is a law without an ordinary rationale, and therefore one should not extend it beyond its precise enactment. They hold that it applies only when the transferor mentions, 'the maneh of mine that is in your hands.' Tur cites this. See Tumim, where I analyzed the Gemara in Gittin and the difficulty of Gidulei Terumah, and offered a strong answer supporting these authorities.
He did not acquire. The Shach writes that he is not liable even under ordinary guaranty law, despite holding Reuven's money. Responsa Rosh likewise implies this. See below in siman 129:2 in Rama. He further rules that if Shimon owed Reuven more than the amount transferred to Levi, Levi acquires under maamad sheloshtan. See also Responsa Rabbi Aharon Sasson 111. In Tumim I wrote that this resolves Gidulei Terumah's question on Rosh in chapter HaMekabel: why does Rosh say the shopkeeper is not obligated to pay the worker because he holds nothing for the employer? Even if he did hold something, if the transfer was unqualified perhaps it would still fail. According to the Shach this is answered. Nevertheless, the rationale for his distinction remains unclear to me, and his wording is too brief. I therefore resolved Gidulei Terumah's question differently, and concluded that regarding a shopkeeper or money changer Rosh agrees as practical law with Ramban. See there.
