It is Rabbi Yitzhak's view in Mordechai's later glosses, but Rabbi Meir of Pontoise disagrees and holds that a person may be seized for another only in matters of taxes. The Mechaber already rules that way in siman 128, seif 2, and it is the settled law among the authorities. Why, then, does Rama cite Rabbi Yitzhak here? I later saw an accurately corrected Mordechai in which Rabbi Meir of Pontoise's paragraph belongs earlier and refers to the preceding case concerning the washerman's levy.
