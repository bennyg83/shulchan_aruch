If he said to him, "One hundred..." it does not... For ma'amad sheloshtan is effective even against the transferee's will, as stated above in seif 7. Tosafos likewise asks that according to the view limiting it to a deposit, why was ma'amad sheloshtan needed, since Bava Basra 85a teaches that deposited property in the other person's domain is acquired there. Even according to Rabbeinu Tam, who holds that regarding a gift "give" is not equivalent to "acquire," or according to the view that this wording must be used specifically at the time of delivery, as stated above in siman 125, seifim 6 and 7, he could still have said "acquire it." Moreover, it could be acquired through an admission, which is effective even when the deposit is not presently in the custodian's possession, as stated in Bava Basra 149a. Further, from the statement there, "If it were by ma'amad sheloshtan I would not go," it appears that had he gone there, the acquisition would have taken effect even against his will.
Others disagree. They infer this from Gittin, from the expression "in consideration of that benefit." They reject the foregoing proofs because not everyone knows the laws of acquisition by "acquire" or by admission, and Rava's point was that if he had actually gone there he would not have been able to brazenly deny it. See Tosafos there. In seif 7 the ruling is stated anonymously in accordance with the first view. See Shach, seif katan 26.
