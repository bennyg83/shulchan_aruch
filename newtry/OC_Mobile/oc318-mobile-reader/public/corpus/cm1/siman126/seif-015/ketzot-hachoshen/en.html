He must swear to the recipient, because the recipient can say, 'The accounting was correct, and your admission does not give you power to impose liability upon me.' Even if the transferor says, 'I made a mistake in the accounting,' he is exempt, because it is analogous to one who says, 'You have a maneh of mine,' and the other answers, 'I do not know,' where the possessor is exempt. Here the debtor says in effect, 'I certainly paid you. You are the one claiming that a mistake was later found, and I do not know of that mistake.' Nevertheless he must swear that he does not know and is then exempt. These are Rashba's words in his novellae. See what I wrote in siman 75, seif katan 6.
