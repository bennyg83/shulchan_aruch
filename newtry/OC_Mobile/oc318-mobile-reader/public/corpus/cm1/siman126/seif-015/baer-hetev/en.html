To swear. The Shach cites the Rashba's chiddushim: The substitute debtor can say, "My accounting was correct. Your later admission to the recipient cannot impose liability upon me." Even if he says, "I forgot the accounting," he is exempt, because the case resembles one who is told, "You owe me one maneh," and answers, "I do not know," who is exempt. It is unlike one who says, "You lent me one maneh, and I do not know whether I repaid you," because here the substitute debtor says, "I definitely paid you. You are the one claiming that an error has been found, and I do not know of any such error." He nevertheless swears that he does not know and is exempt. See the Taz.
An oath. Even if the original giver admits that the substitute debtor owes him nothing, seif 13 already explains that the giver remains exempt because the gift was made in error. Sema.
