And the giver must swear, etc. See Sema seif katan 45, who writes that even according to the Rosh the recipient cannot return to the first debtor here, because the giver can say, "Who instructed you to make a false admission?" The Bach writes similarly, and the Beit Yosef gives this as one of his answers. This disagrees with the Ran. From here there is open proof that according to the Rosh, the first debtor remains only like an ordinary guarantor, and so long as the creditor can collect from the second obligor he cannot proceed against the first. If so, the first can properly argue: "You had a means of collecting from the second, but you ruined it through your own conduct. What claim do you have against me?" This supports the Sema above in siman 91. If, by contrast, we followed the Shach's position that the creditor may immediately choose to collect from the first because he remains a direct debtor, then why should the creditor lose that right merely because his own statement prevented collection from the second? His statement caused no loss to the first. If the creditor collects from the first, the first can in turn collect from the second, exactly as the Ran expressly writes. Why should the recipient's right be weakened by his admission? Had he made no admission, he could simply say, "I choose to collect from the first." Why should the admission change that? He can say to the first: "What concern is my admission to you? I proceed against you under my original law. You may proceed against the second under yours. My statement neither adds nor subtracts from your position." This is simple. We must therefore conclude, contrary to the Shach, that according to the Rosh the first debtor is only like a guarantor. The Maharit's argument cited by the Shach in seif katan 58 is then correct: if the second obligor claims that he already paid, the creditor cannot simply return to the first debtor, because the first has only guarantor liability. The Sema's ruling above in siman 91 is correct and the Shach's contrary view is not.
