One who became liable, etc. See Tumim. According to Mechaber, even if judgment had not yet been entered, the heirs must pay. The phrase 'became liable in judgment' is stated for greater novelty, because with a penalty even a judgment may not obligate the heirs. As to movables, since by Talmudic law they were not encumbered, even Mechaber may require that judgment have been entered. Rama below, siman 388:2, holds that even with an informer, if no judgment was entered the heirs are not liable.
But if he was liable for a penalty. Shach limits the exemption to the portion beyond actual damage, whereas Rama below appears to hold that if judgment was entered the heirs must pay the amount of actual damage. In Tumim I wrote that no such distinction should be made. In every genuine penalty, even after judgment, the heirs do not pay.
