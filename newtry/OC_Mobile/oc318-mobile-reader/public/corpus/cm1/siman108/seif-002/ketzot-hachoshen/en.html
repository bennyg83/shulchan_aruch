The Shulchan Aruch cites an opinion that where a person was ordered by beit din to pay money because of informing against another and then died, the award can be collected from his heirs. The Shach asks why this is cited merely as "there is one who says," since no one appears to disagree. Although a mere informer can be subject to a penalty that does not necessarily pass to his children, once he stood before beit din and was adjudged liable, the obligation became an ordinary fixed monetary debt. The case stated here expressly says that beit din imposed monetary liability, and therefore collection from the heirs follows.
