There is one who says, etc. I do not know why the Mechaber records this. The Mordechai merely relates that Rabbeinu Gershom ruled in an actual case that even according to the view that an oral loan is not collected from heirs, here, because the debtor had stood in judgment, the matter became public. The Gemara says the same at the end of Bava Batra. According to our ruling that an oral loan is collected from heirs, collection would be possible even without prior adjudication, provided the case falls within one of the three previously stated categories. Otherwise, we would plead payment for the heirs. Likewise, whenever we say that a judicial ruling becomes public, this applies only where it is known that the debt was not later paid, as explained in siman 39:9, seif katan 28, siman 51, seif katan 1, and siman 79. The practical difference concerns purchasers: once liability was imposed by beit din, the creditor may collect from encumbered property because the ruling became public. This is obvious from siman 39 and many other places, and it is neither necessary nor especially relevant to state it here regarding heirs. Nor should it have been presented as 'there is one who says,' since, once the case necessarily falls within one of those three categories, no authority disputes it. Perhaps the formulation was chosen because of the concluding case of a fine. This requires study. The comments of the Ir Shushan here are not clear. See also siman 388:2.
