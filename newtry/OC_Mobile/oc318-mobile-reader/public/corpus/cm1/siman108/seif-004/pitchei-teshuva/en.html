And we plead on their behalf. See Baer Heitev. See Responsa Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat, end of siman 136, concerning an iska document presented against orphans. Should we plead for them that their father earned no profit and therefore they should pay only the principal and not the stipulated profit? In his view there is no doubt that we do plead this for them, because one should not extract from orphans. Indeed, even a claim of unavoidable loss would be pleaded for them to defeat the creditor's principal, were it not that such loss is exceedingly uncommon. Failure to earn profit, however, is a common minority occurrence. He concludes that in his view there is no basis for requiring the orphans to pay the profit. See there. See Responsa Bigdei Yesha, siman 47, which cites Responsa Tur HaEven, siman 64, as ruling that where a lender produces a debt document against the borrower's heirs providing for a fixed annual profit, and the borrower died before the payment date, the heirs must pay the profits accrued until their father's death. This is because the enactment of Maharam of Krakow established that so long as the borrower has not sworn, "I earned no profit," he must pay according to the agreed rate, and the oath takes the place of payment. Thus the custom of all batei din is to collect profits from orphans and not to plead for them that perhaps their father earned no profit and would have sworn accordingly. So long as he had not sworn, he remained liable for the profits, and if he died during the term the heirs must pay until the payment date by virtue of their father's agreement. Bigdei Yesha discusses this at length and challenges it on several grounds, concluding that the ruling of Tur HaEven is entirely incorrect and that he had never heard expert batei din rule so. The correct law is that the orphans are exempt from all stipulated profits, even if their father died during the term. See there.
