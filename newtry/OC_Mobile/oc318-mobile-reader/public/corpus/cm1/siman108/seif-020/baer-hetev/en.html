From their father. This rule that seizure is effective applies specifically where both estates are mutually indebted to one another. Where they are not mutually indebted, and one heir seized the debtor's intermediate-quality land for his own claim, the seizure is ineffective. Beit Yosef in the name of Baal HaTerumot. Shach.
Retains. This applies specifically to land, upon which an ordinary lender relies. Since during their fathers' lifetimes the land became subject to the debt that the debtor owed his own creditor, and the property is now in the heirs' possession and under their control, the seizure is effective even if they never heard during his lifetime that he intended to retain it. This differs from the other lifetime seizures discussed above in simanim 64 and 104, where the creditor happened to receive promissory notes or movable property of the debtor as a deposit. An ordinary lender does not generally rely upon movable property, and there it must therefore be known that the debtor demanded the property and the creditor refused to return it because he was retaining it for his debt. Sema.
