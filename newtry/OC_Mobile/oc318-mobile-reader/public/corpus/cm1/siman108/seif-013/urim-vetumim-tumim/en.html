See the Shach, who understands the Mechaber to mean that the heirs need not swear that their father did not instruct them regarding payment, because the father himself would not have been required to swear at all. Accordingly, no oath rests on the heirs. The Shach ultimately favors the authorities who hold that even the father would have had to swear, but that his inability to swear would not trigger the rule that one who must swear and cannot do so must pay. His conclusion is persuasive, because the Rivash's main foundation is the case of land specifically designated on the boundary, where no oath is required. The Rosh already distinguishes that ketubah case from ordinary creditors on the ground that the ketubah was still within its term. Once that distinction is made, the Rivash's general proof falls away. In any event, the dispute concerns uncertainty whether the mortgage debt itself was repaid. If the parties disagree instead about the original amount of the loan or the duration of the mortgage, the creditor plainly must swear. Even with a movable pledge, the Geonim require an oath regarding the amount claimed up to the value of the collateral. The same is certainly true of mortgaged land. This is accepted by all authorities. The Shach's proof from a responsum of the Rashba therefore does not establish his broader point, because that responsum concerns a dispute over the amount of the loan or a similar term, where everyone requires an oath. There is also a difficulty in the Mechaber's ruling in light of his position in Yoreh Deah that a land mortgage is permitted only in the form of a Sura mortgage. If the agreed years have not yet ended, the debt is still within its term and all agree that no oath is required. If the years have ended, there is no remaining debt at all, and the land returns without payment. The present ruling has practical form only according to the Tur, who permits a mortgage with deduction of produce. For one who prohibits that form, the case exists primarily with a pledge of movables. This should be kept in mind.
