The entire law, etc. See the Sema, seif katan 30, p. 108b, through his discussion of one who seeks to collect when the property had already been awarded to him, and his statement that after the creditor dies a person cannot transmit an oath to his children. It appears to me that the responsum also concerns a case where the debtor died during the creditor's lifetime, leaving land to his heirs, and the creditor died afterward, as the Tur and Shulchan Aruch state in seifim 31 and 32. The Sema cites this in seif katan 40. The difficulty is how the creditor sought to collect from encumbered property when the heir possessed unencumbered property. This can be resolved. Examine the Sema, seif katan 45, and you will see that my explanation is correct and that his objection is also resolved. Responsa Rabbi Aharon Sasson, siman 82, cites the Rivash, siman 483, that even where the creditor admits partial payment or one witness testifies, we apply the rule that one cannot transmit an oath to his children. This resolves the Sema's questions, though the entire matter still requires study. Regarding the Sema's distinction between a case already adjudicated and one not adjudicated, see Tosafot at the end of Shevuot and the Yerushalmi in HaKotev, which imply such a distinction; the Gemara can be reconciled accordingly.

The Ritva writes in HaKotev, on the Mishna that heirs collecting from heirs may collect only with an oath, that the Baal HaIttur limits this to where the other heirs claim, 'Our father told us that he paid.' Otherwise, we do not plead payment for them, because one heir is not afforded a plea that operates to the detriment of another heir.
