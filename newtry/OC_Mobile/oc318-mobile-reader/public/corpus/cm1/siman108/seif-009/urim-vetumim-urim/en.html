So too against the borrower's heirs. Even if they do not demand, 'Swear to us,' beit din makes that claim for them. Although the lender's children are also orphans, the law always favors the heirs of the defendant as possessors. Therefore the lender's heirs, when seeking to extract, must also swear that their father gave them no contrary instruction.
They had not been disturbed by their father, and they could not claim to you that I had done so for the orphans of everything that their father had commanded to claim:
Themselves. The Shach cites Ritva in the name of Baal HaIttur that only if the heirs say, 'Our father told us he paid,' do we make the claim; otherwise we do not, because we do not make one heir's damaging claim against another heir. I do not understand why the Shach cites this as practical law. Tur already cites Baal HaIttur for that view, Raavad disagrees, Tur agrees with Raavad, and Mechaber and Rama omit Baal HaIttur's position. The Shach's citation is therefore puzzling.
