Without. The Sema explains that even had the borrower died during the lender's lifetime, the lender would have collected from the borrower's heirs without an oath, because the collateral was already in the lender's possession and we do not suspect that the borrower had left him other security. Therefore, even after the lender dies, his heirs collect from the borrower's heirs after taking the oath that their father did not instruct them that the debt had been paid. The Shach objects that if their father himself could collect without an oath, why should the heirs swear? The Gemara expressly says that if the father could collect without an oath, his heirs should not require one. The following seif, which seems to require an oath even where the father did not, must therefore distinguish between an express credibility clause and collateral. See Responsa Rabbi Moshe Alshich, siman 83, and above simanim 66 and 72. The Shach concludes that where the collateral is movable, all agree, and where the collateral exists without a written note and the borrower died first, the heirs must swear that their father did not instruct them otherwise. In his view an oath is required, but the rule that a person cannot transmit an oath-bound claim to his children does not apply here.
