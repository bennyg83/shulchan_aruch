He collects without an oath. If the collateral was held without a document and the debtor died during the creditor's lifetime, the heirs must also swear that their father did not instruct them that he had been paid. See siman 72:17, the Sema, seif katan 49, and the Beit Yosef, new section 5. In my view, the primary rule is that an oath is required, although we do not apply here the rule that a person cannot transmit an oath to his children. The Rashba rules this in responsum 939, cited by the Beit Yosef here and in siman 140. The Ritva likewise requires an oath, as cited at the end of siman 110, and writes that this is the view of the leading authorities, his teachers, and the Baal HaIttur. The Ittur, in the laws of specific lien, p. 41a, cites the Ri Migash and Rabbi Yehudah, who require an oath, and agrees. His reasoning is that if the pledged land were swept away by a river the creditor could collect from other assets; therefore, although he holds the land, he may not collect without an oath, since land remains in its owner's possession and the debtor may say, 'Take your pledge and pay me what is mine,' unless the agreement expressly provided that payment would come only from this property. This is correct. The Rivash, siman 78, argues that collection is without an oath based on the case in Ketubot where particular land was designated for a ketubah. That is no proof. The Baal HaTerumot explains that a ketubah differs because it is not collectible during the husband's lifetime and there is no ordinary concern that security was transferred in advance. The Rosh, in the name of the Raavad and Rabbeinu Yonah, similarly limits the exemption from oath to a ketubah, whereas an ordinary creditor remains subject to concern for payment. The Rosh appears to agree, as do his abridged rulings. The Rivash's attribution to the Rosh is not compelling; perhaps the Rivash meant only that the rule against transmitting an oath does not apply, as the Rosh writes in responsa, rule 86, siman 4. Indeed, the Rosh in Chezkat HaBatim, cited by the Tur at the end of siman 150, rules that we plead for heirs every defense their father could have raised and require an oath from the creditor even on an uncertain claim regarding collateral. Thus, if the father could have demanded, 'Swear that I did not pay you,' we plead the same for his heirs. Nevertheless, the Ri Migash's view that the oath cannot be transmitted appears incorrect against the other authorities and the Mechaber. The correct reason is that possession of collateral prevents application of that rule, which applies only where money is being extracted. The Rashba and Rivash themselves make this distinction. The Sema's statement that the heirs swear the oath of 'our father did not instruct us' is surprising: if their father would have collected without an oath, why should they swear? The Gemara says explicitly, 'If their father would collect without an oath, should they require one?' See Responsa Maharam Alshich, siman 83.
