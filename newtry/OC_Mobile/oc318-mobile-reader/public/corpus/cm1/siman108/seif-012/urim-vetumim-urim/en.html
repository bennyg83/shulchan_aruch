The heirs also collect. The Shach explains Mechaber to mean without an oath, and this is correct as an interpretation. On the substantive law, however, the Shach disagrees and rules that an oath is required, and the heirs must swear that their father gave them no contrary instruction. The special rule that one cannot bequeath an oath to one's children does not apply. His reasoning is persuasive. All this is where the only doubt is whether payment occurred. If there is concern that the debt itself was smaller, or that a mortgage was subject to a larger deduction, or similar matters, then everyone agrees an oath is required, like the Geonic rule for one who collects on a pledge.
