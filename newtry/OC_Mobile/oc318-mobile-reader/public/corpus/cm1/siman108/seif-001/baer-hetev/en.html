And instructed. The Sema explains that in such a case we do not say that the dying person made the admission merely so that his children would not appear wealthy. The Shach questions this, because even an admission during illness may sometimes be explained as made so as not to enrich his children, as stated in siman 81, seif 14. Rather, the case must be one in which that defense is unavailable, as explained there. See Responsa Mabit II, 345, and Responsa Maharashdam 306.
Claimed. Meaning they claimed definitely. In that case, even with heirs we apply the rule that one who says, "I never borrowed," is treated as having admitted that he never repaid. But if the heirs did not make such a definite claim, the Tur already explains in siman 72, seif 29, that heirs and guardians may advance whatever claim is appropriate and may change from one claim to another. Sema.
Seif 9. See seifim 2 and 9 there in the Sema and Shach. See also what is written there in seif katan 10.
During his lifetime. In such a case a person cannot bequeath to his children money that he himself could collect only by taking an oath, as the Tur and Shulchan Aruch state in seif 11. But where one of these circumstances applies and the father would not have needed to swear, he may bequeath the claim and his children collect without an oath as well. Sema.
Because we. Although the claimants too are heirs, and one authority holds that we do not advance a claim for the debtor's heirs if doing so harms the lender's heirs, we do not rule that way. Rather, we follow the view that we do advance such a claim in order to leave the money where it is presently held. Sema.
Are paid. Meaning even though the deceased did not expressly instruct that they be paid, and even though they are his relatives. The statement that his wife is not believed means that she is not believed to say that they had not already been paid. Sema.
