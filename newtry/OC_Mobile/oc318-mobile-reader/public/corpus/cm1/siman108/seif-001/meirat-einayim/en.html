It is not collected from the heirs, etc. This applies even to adult heirs, because it is an oral loan and their father would have been believed with a hesset oath to say that he repaid it. Whatever their father could have claimed, we claim on behalf of the heirs. They are exempt even without taking a hesset oath that they do not know whether their father remained indebted. Heirs do not take either the severe oath that their father did not instruct them or a hesset oath merely to exempt themselves. Such oaths apply when heirs come to collect on a document and the borrower claims it was paid and the document contains no credibility clause for the heirs, as explained below in seif 5. To exempt themselves they need not swear, as the Rosh, Tur, and Mechaber rule in siman 69 and at the end of siman 71.
He instructed during his illness, etc. In such a case we do not say that he admitted the debt merely so that his children should not appear wealthy.
The due date had not yet arrived. There is a presumption that a person does not repay before the due date, and one may collect on the strength of this presumption even for an oral loan and even from minor heirs, as stated below in seif 3 and above in siman 78.
But if witnesses came, etc. Perhaps he repaid, because one who borrowed before witnesses need not repay before witnesses.
However, if the heirs claim, etc. The Tur and Mechaber state this where the lender holds a document, and it concerns heirs who make the claim with certainty. An adult heir who makes a definite claim is treated as though he made it concerning his own affairs. Therefore, under the rule that one who says "I never borrowed" is as though he says "I did not repay," once witnesses establish the loan the heirs cannot later claim payment. This rule applies even to heirs. But where they do not make a definite claim, the Tur in siman 72 explains that heirs and guardians may advance alternative claims and may change from one claim to another.
See above siman 69. There it is explained that their father would have been believed with a hesset oath to say that a handwritten obligation was paid if it lacked a credibility clause, and we make the same claim for the heirs. I discussed there why the Tur says that if the handwriting is not authenticated and cannot be authenticated, one does not collect even if one of the stated circumstances applies, although an oral loan could be collected in one of those circumstances.
Even though the borrower died during the lender's lifetime. Ordinarily one cannot bequeath to his heirs money that he himself could collect only upon taking an oath, as explained below in seif 11. But where one of the circumstances above existed so that their father would not have been required to swear, he can bequeath the claim and his heirs collect without an oath.
For we claim for them, etc. Although here the claimants too are heirs, and the Tur cites an opinion that we do not advance a claim for the borrower's heirs to the detriment of the lender's heirs, we follow the other view that we do advance such a claim in order to leave the money where it is.
