Under what circumstances a lender collects from a borrower's heirs, and which oath a person may transmit to his children. This siman contains 21 seifim.
An oral loan is collected from heirs only in one of three circumstances: where the debtor admitted the debt and, during his illness, instructed that so and so still had an outstanding debt against him; where the loan was made for a fixed term and the term had not yet expired; or where the debtor was placed under nidui until he paid and died while still under nidui. In all these cases the creditor collects from the heirs without an oath. But if witnesses merely testify that their father owed the claimant one hundred, the creditor collects nothing from the heirs, because perhaps the father paid. Rama: If the orphans claim, "Our father never borrowed," that is equivalent to saying that the debt was not repaid, and since witnesses establish that he did borrow, they must pay. This is as will be explained below in seif 15 of this siman. Likewise, if the creditor produces a writing in the father's own hand acknowledging that he owes him, he collects nothing from the heirs, because perhaps the father paid. See above, siman 69, seif 5. If the lender died and his heirs seek to collect from the borrower's heirs, then if the case falls within one of these three circumstances, the lender's heirs collect without an oath even if the borrower died during the lender's lifetime. If it does not fall within one of these three circumstances, they cannot collect even where the borrower's heirs themselves make no claim, because beit din advances for them the claim that perhaps their father paid. Rama: Expenses incurred for medical care during the borrower's illness have the same law as any other debt. If it is known how much was spent and that it was not repaid during his lifetime, the amount is collected from the orphans. Otherwise it is not collected, and the borrower's wife is not believed to establish it.
