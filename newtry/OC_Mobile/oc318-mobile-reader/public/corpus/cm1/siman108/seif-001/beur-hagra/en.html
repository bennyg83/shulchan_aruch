An oral loan... See Beer HaGolah. Although according to Rav Pappa the distinction applies specifically to minor orphans, as explained in Perek HaKosev, according to Rav Huna, whose reason is that beit din pleads on behalf of heirs, the same applies to adult heirs. Perek HaNizakin explicitly includes both adults and minors in certain protections. The Gemara there does not distinguish adult heirs because that point itself is disputed. We rule according to Abaye the Elder and the stated halachic conclusion.
Or if it was... See Beer HaGolah. The Gemara does not distinguish this case because Rav Pappa and Rav Huna do not accept Reish Lakish's approach there.
But if... likewise... As stated there from the challenge involving a guarantor. The same applies to witnesses. See Beer HaGolah.
However... Kesubos 88a.
And if he died... As stated in Shevuos concerning the rule that one cannot transmit an oath-bound claim to one's heirs, which does not apply in this case.
Even the heirs of... The Mishnah must be discussing a case where the defendant did not expressly demand an oath, as shown by its comparison to one who impairs a document and by the Gemara's challenge concerning the fathers. Even if one could argue that the defendant did make such a demand, the wording "likewise the heirs" means that just as a creditor collecting from heirs must swear, so heirs collecting from heirs must swear. Since beit din pleads for heirs, the oath applies even without their express demand.
And his wife... Unless she herself could have delivered the relevant property or evidence, as stated at the end of the third chapter of Sanhedrin 31a.
