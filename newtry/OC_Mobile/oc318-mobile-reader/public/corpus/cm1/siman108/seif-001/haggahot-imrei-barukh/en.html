(Shach, seif katan 8) And so, in my humble opinion, this is the principal ruling; otherwise, etc. Note: This question was also raised by the Ramban in Milchamot Hashem. At first glance, however, it is difficult to understand. Since the bailee is ultimately believed to say "I returned it," perhaps he in fact returned it and did not insist on taking back the deposit document because he knew he would be believed by a migo that he could claim unavoidable accident. Why, then, should we not make this claim on behalf of the heirs? I later saw that the Tumim raises this point in rejecting the Shach, but he did not note that the Ramban himself writes like the Shach. It appears possible to defend the Ramban as follows. He may have been troubled by the Sema's question here, seif katan 17: why is the bailee believed by a migo of unavoidable accident when accidents are uncommon, and an uncommon migo is generally not accepted? The Sema answers that because a claim of return involves brazenness, the bailee prefers to claim unavoidable accident, which involves no brazenness. But according to the Rashba in his novellae to Shevuot 45, in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah, and Responsa Toldot Adam of the Rashba, siman 273, every denial of another person's money involves brazenness even where the other party cannot know that the claim is false. This is why a migo applies in the case of "this field belonged to your father," because both alternatives involve comparable brazenness. We therefore must say that returning a deposit without taking back the deposit document is itself an uncommon and weak claim. Even though the bailee, if he claims return, must take an oath and would have to swear in any event, he is believed because he has a migo to claim unavoidable accident. Since the return claim is itself uncommon, the alternative of unavoidable accident is not a worse claim, and a migo from one uncommon claim to another can be accepted. But if so, because return without taking back the document is itself uncommon, just as we do not make the uncommon claim of unavoidable accident for heirs, so too we should not claim for them that their father returned the deposit.
