They collect without an oath. The Tur explains that the only oath that could have rested upon the heirs was the oath that their father had not instructed them that the debt was paid. The credibility clause granted by the borrower to the lender and his heirs exempts them from that oath, because no other oath was applicable to them.
Among the torn documents, etc. Because the debt instrument itself has a defect indicating possible payment. Perhaps the lender was paid but did not have the note available to return to the borrower, so he wrote a receipt instead. Although the receipt is found in the lender's possession rather than the borrower's, perhaps the borrower trusted the lender and left the receipt with him until the lender could search for the original note, intending to take the receipt if the note could not be found. If the note is not found among the torn documents, however, the document retains its presumption of validity. We then say that the lender prepared the receipt in advance in case the borrower suddenly came to pay when the lender did not have the note available to return and would lack time to write a receipt. Even if the note itself was found among the torn papers, if no receipt was found in the lender's possession the heirs would still collect on the note, because we would say that it happened to be placed there and had not yet been paid. All this is explained in siman 65:18.

But they do not swear that our father did not instruct us. Nor must they swear that their father did not instruct them through another person. Since, because of their minority, they were not fit to receive such instructions directly from him, it is not his ordinary practice to give them a temporary message through another person. Rather, he would write the matter down as a record or entrust it to guardians. See the Beit Yosef.
