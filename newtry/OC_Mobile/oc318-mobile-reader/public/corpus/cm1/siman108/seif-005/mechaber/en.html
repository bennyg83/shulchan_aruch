If the lender died and his heirs come to collect the document from the borrower, while the borrower says, 'I paid their father,' and they say, 'We do not know,' he is told, 'Go and pay.' If he says, 'Swear to me,' then if the document grants credibility to the lender and his heirs, they collect without an oath. If it does not grant them credibility, they swear while holding a sacred object that their father did not instruct them through another person, did not tell them orally, and that they found no receipt among his documents stating that this note was paid. Had they found such a receipt, it would be presumed paid, provided the note was found among torn documents, as explained in siman 65. Their oath also includes that their father did not tell them that he had placed a paid note among his documents. Even if an heir was a minor lying in a cradle when the ancestor died, he swears and collects upon reaching adulthood. Such an heir need swear only, 'I did not find a receipt.' He need not swear, 'Our father did not instruct us,' because he was not capable of receiving instructions.
