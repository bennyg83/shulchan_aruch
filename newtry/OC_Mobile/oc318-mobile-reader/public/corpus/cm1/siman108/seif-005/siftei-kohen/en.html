["They shall swear, etc. See the Rosh at the beginning of the first chapter of Bava Metzia, who writes that if they have one witness who heard the father say that this document has not been paid, they collect without an oath; see there."]
["While holding a sacred object. This refers to the immediately preceding case, where the borrower says to them, 'Swear to me.' Otherwise they need not swear, as is evident from the Gemara in Kol HaNishba'in and in HaKotev, and from the Tur and all the authorities. However, if they impair the document by admitting partial payment, or one witness testifies that it was paid, or they come to collect from encumbered property, they must swear in every case. So writes Rabbeinu Yerucham, Netiv 9, part 2; see there carefully. The Beit Yosef cites him at the end of seif 8. Examine Rabbeinu Yerucham himself and you will see that his intent is as I have written. See also what I wrote above at the beginning of siman 84."]
["Provided that it was found, etc. Meaning, the reason the document is presumed paid is that it was found among torn documents. Therefore the heirs must swear even without a specific demand, for perhaps there had been a receipt and for that reason the document was placed among the torn documents. This is also implied by the wording of the Maggid Mishneh at the beginning of chapter 17 of Hilchot Malveh, unlike Lechem Mishneh at the end of chapter 16 of Hilchot Malveh, who appears to understand that the heirs need swear this oath only if they knew that the document had been placed among the torn documents. He therefore wrote that the Rambam stated the matter too briefly and should have explained that it was found among torn documents, as he explained in chapter 16 of Hilchot Malveh. According to what I have written, however, there was no need for him to explain anything here. Lechem Mishneh further wrote there that in chapter 24 of Hilchot Malveh as well, where the Rambam writes that if among a person's documents a receipt is found stating that the document of Yosef ben Shimon was paid, the Rambam should have specified that it was found among torn documents, as the Gemara establishes at the end of the first chapter of Bava Metzia. He was not precise in this, for in chapter 24 the Rambam expressly discusses a receipt found with the borrower, while the two persons named Yosef ben Shimon are the lenders, as the Rambam explicitly writes that both of their documents are paid. This follows Abaye's explanation in Bava Batra 173a, as the Rif and Rosh write in Gittin Peshutin. The passage at the end of the first chapter of Bava Metzia follows the explanation of Rabbi Yirmeyah, as Tosafos write in Gittin Peshutin there. This is clear."]
