He writes that many authorities regard theft and loss as common enough that such a plea is made for heirs according to all views. This, however, creates a difficulty in the Gemara's challenge to Rav Chisda from the opinion that the creditor collects the entire amount of a business investment document. Perhaps that authority holds only that the bailee lacks a migo from unavoidable accident, because a business custodian is a paid bailee and an unavoidable accident is uncommon, so an uncommon migo is not recognized. Rav Chisda, by contrast, stated his rule regarding an ordinary deposit. An unpaid bailee can plead theft or loss, which are common claims, and therefore has a normal migo that all would recognize. If so, the Gemara would have no question. It therefore appears that no distinction should be made. This is also implied by the unqualified language of all the poskim. All the more so according to the Rambam, who holds that with an iska the half treated as a deposit imposes only the responsibility of an unpaid bailee, yet even there we do not plead for the heirs theft or loss, but only repayment.
