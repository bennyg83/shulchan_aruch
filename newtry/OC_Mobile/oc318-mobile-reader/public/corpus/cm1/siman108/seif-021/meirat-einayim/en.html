If the injured party comes to collect his damages, etc. It appears that the case is where their father caused the damage, died, and left land. For minor heirs the Sages enacted that collection be made from inferior land, but from adult heirs the injured party takes according to his ordinary law. This differs from a creditor collecting the father's debt from heirs, who collects inferior land even from adults. A creditor's Torah right is itself only to inferior land, and collection from intermediate land was enacted merely to prevent people from refusing to lend. Therefore restoring collection from orphans to inferior land does not create such concern, since a lender does not ordinarily anticipate that the borrower will die. So rules the Rosh. It is somewhat difficult that the Mechaber here states the Rosh's view without qualification, while at the end of siman 419 he writes without qualification that damages are collected from inferior land because anyone collecting from orphans collects only from inferior land. That wording implies that damages and ordinary debt are equal and that in both cases collection is only from inferior land even where the heirs are adults. The Maggid Mishneh likewise writes in chapter 8 of Hilchot Nizkei Mamon that the Rambam holds that even from adult heirs only inferior land is collected. There is no difficulty in the Tur. Here he quotes the Rosh. In siman 419 he writes that damages are collected from inferior land, but there he is quoting the language of the Mishnah at the beginning of HaNizakin as explained by Rav Nachman, while the Rosh understands the Mishnah specifically to concern minors for the reason just stated. The Tur's statement there that the injured party's claim becomes like a debt against the heirs is intended to explain why collection cannot be made from their movable property. See my further discussion in the Sema to siman 419.
