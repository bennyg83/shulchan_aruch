And if they are adults... We do not generally find a distinction between adult heirs and other adults, as Rivash writes. Even regarding neighboring rights, adults are treated ordinarily, as explained above in siman 175 seif 47. The special treatment of heirs regarding oaths is because they do not know their father's affairs, so beit din pleads for them like purchasers. The rule that creditors collect inferior land from heirs, even adults, is different: since lenders do not ordinarily anticipate that the borrower will die and leave heirs, the Rabbis left the Torah rule in place because there is no concern that lending would thereby be restricted. Damages differ. The Gemara expressly gives this rationale. See Shach here and below at the end of siman 419.
