There is an opinion that says, etc. The Tur writes that the holder is not believed merely by virtue of a migo that he could have burned the document, except in the case of a true third party with whom both sides deposited it. The reason is that even such a third party is not believed initially because he presently has a migo to burn or conceal it. Rather, he is believed because both parties trusted him from the outset and did not suspect him of burning or hiding it. Since they initially entrusted him, he is believed in all he says concerning it. This reasoning does not apply to ordinary partners or brothers where a debt instrument belonging to the estate comes out from under the hand of only one of them. In the Perishah and at the beginning of siman 56 I explained the language of the Gemara proving this. The ruling in siman 93:14, where a note in Shimon's name against Levi belongs to the partnership of Shimon and Reuven and Shimon says Levi paid, so that Levi is discharged by Shimon's admission, is different. There the document itself was written in Shimon's name. Even though it expressly states that the money belongs to the partnership, since it is in Shimon's hands and bears his name, he is believed to discharge Levi, while Shimon must then pay Reuven his half. Here the document was written in the father's name and was publicly established as the father's document. It was not in this particular heir's power to return or cancel it. Examine this carefully.
