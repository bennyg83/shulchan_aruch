We do not tear the document. See the Shach, who favors the Ramah's view that where the father never demanded payment during his lifetime, a receipt found afterward is not thereby discredited. He further writes that even according to Rabbi Yosef ibn Migash, who treats the father's failure to demand the document as a defect in the receipt, that concern applies only before the debt's due date. Once the debt is due and the father simply did not demand the document, even Rabbi Yosef ibn Migash would agree that the receipt is not discredited. This is correct. Rabbi Yosef's reasoning is that even if the debt was paid, the debtor should have demanded return of his note. The Rosh asks why he needed to do so, since the creditor may simply have said that the note was lost and we hold that a receipt may be written in place of returning the note. The Baal HaTerumot makes the same point. The concern therefore must be focused on payment before maturity. If the debtor chose to pay early, what compelled him to do so while leaving an outstanding instrument that might later be found? Knowing that the creditor still had the document, he should have insisted on its return or on stronger protection. That makes the receipt suspicious. After maturity, however, a creditor may legitimately be unable to produce a lost document, and the debtor cannot compel physical return of something that is lost. Since we allow a receipt, the failure to obtain the note itself carries little weight. It also appears that even according to Rabbi Yosef ibn Migash, if the receipt covers only part of the debt, the receipt is not discredited at all. How could the debtor have compelled surrender of the original note while a substantial balance remained unpaid? Nor could he necessarily compel the creditor to write the partial payment on the face of the document. The creditor may prefer a separate receipt so that the remaining debt continues to pressure payment, as the Gemara recognizes, and the debtor himself may prefer a separate receipt because a partially paid document is subject to the creditor's oath. There is therefore no suspicious failure to demand the original document when the receipt reflects only partial payment.
