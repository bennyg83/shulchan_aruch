Even if he stipulated, etc. This applies even if the stipulation was written in the document. It appears to me from the Rosh that, where such a stipulation exists, the creditor collects superior land from adult heirs. The statement that the condition is ineffective concerns minor heirs. Tosafot explain that although liens are biblical and monetary conditions are generally effective, the Sages made no distinction among orphans. The Rosh then cites Rabbeinu Yonah that the Sages limited a tort claimant, whose Torah entitlement is superior land, only against minors and not adult heirs. The same should apply where a creditor's stipulation gives him a Torah-level right to superior land: the Sages displaced it only for minors. The Tur, however, appears to disagree, perhaps because regarding ordinary creditors the Sages made no distinctions at all, lest one confuse cases with and without a stipulation. In my view, the Rosh's position is as stated. Rabbeinu Yerucham adds that even an unspecified apotiki does not permit collection from superior land unless the debtor wrote, 'You shall have payment only from this property.'
