Conditioned. Even if such a condition was written in the note, the Tur appears to hold that a creditor does not collect superior land. The Shach, however, understands the Rosh differently. Where an effective condition was made, the creditor collects superior land from adult heirs. The rule that the condition does not help applies only to minors. Although a creditor's lien is Biblical and monetary conditions are ordinarily valid, Chazal did not distinguish among cases involving minor orphans. This follows Tosafot. The Rosh later cites Rabbeinu Yonah that Chazal did not enact such a limitation in damages, where Torah law itself requires superior land, except as to minors. So too, where the creditor's condition gives him a Torah-level right to superior land, Chazal displaced it only for minors, not adults. The Tur does not appear to read the Rosh this way and apparently holds that Chazal made no distinctions regarding creditors so that one would not confuse a conditioned case with an unconditioned one. The Shach concludes that the Rosh's view is as he explained.
