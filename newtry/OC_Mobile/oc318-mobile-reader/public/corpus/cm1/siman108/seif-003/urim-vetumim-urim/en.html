And it was within its term, etc. If the debtor died under excommunication, that is a fact known to beit din that imposed it. Likewise, the testament mentioned above is a clarified fact and may be accepted, as stated below in siman 110. But the fact that a debt was within its term must ordinarily have been established while the debtor was alive. It may also be established through his handwritten obligation containing a due date, which can be authenticated even against minor heirs. See below in siman 110.
Similar to a Torah oath. This follows the rule that one who comes to collect from heirs may collect only with an oath. Sema explains that if the debtor were alive and demanded, 'Swear to me,' the creditor would have to swear, so upon the debtor's death we make that claim for the heirs. Tosafos in Shevuos 41a, however, implies that this is not the reason, because even where the creditor was generally granted credibility, he nevertheless must swear to heirs. Rather, the oath is an independent rabbinic enactment.
His law is as explained in siman 82. If he refuses to swear, we do not seize property from him immediately. Rather, he is excommunicated. See above, siman 82, and my discussion in Tumim concerning the distinction between a Torah scholar and other people.
