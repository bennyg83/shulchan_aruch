["But a hired worker, etc. Rather, the employer swears a heset oath against the heirs of the worker and is exempt. The Bach cites this in the name of Maharshal. The Bach challenges it, because the Ran and Ramah imply that the employer swears while holding a sacred object. I say that the Baal HaMaor wrote in accordance with Maharshal, and this also appears to be the view of the Rambam, the Tur and Shulchan Aruch, and the other authorities, as I wrote above in siman 89, seif katan 3; see there."]
["And one who was robbed, etc. Regarding a shopkeeper and his ledger and regarding workers, in the case of workers, since the debt to them is certain and the only doubt is whether they were paid, according to all opinions their heirs swear the oath of heirs and collect. The Ran writes this explicitly in HaIsh Mekadesh, and the Maggid Mishneh writes likewise in chapter 16 of Hilchot Malveh in the name of the Ramban. This is the principal ruling, as I wrote above in siman 91, seif katan 10. As to the shopkeeper, it appears that this depends on the dispute between the Ramban and the Baal HaMaor that I cited above in siman 92, seif 9. I already wrote there that the principal ruling follows the Baal HaMaor, that a shopkeeper has the same law as workers; see there."]
["The heirs do not, etc. In the case of a heset oath that was reversed onto the claimant, the defendant swears heset and is exempt. See the Bach, and this is straightforward."]
