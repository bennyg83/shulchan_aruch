A statement of Rav Pappa there. See above, end of siman 84, where the opposite was written. There I cited Bach's resolution. In my humble opinion, the better distinction is that there the lender had already stood in judgment and beit din had obligated him to swear on the basis of the single witness, but he died before swearing. Rabbenu Yerucham, Netiv 26, part 3, page 75, end of column 3, writes similarly, as Sema notes. In my view the Mechaber hinted to this by changing his wording. There he writes, 'one witness testifies concerning the document that it has been paid, and the lender died before swearing,' which implies that the witness testified in the lender's presence, that beit din thereby obligated him to swear, and that he delayed taking the oath and died before swearing. Property of this kind is not transmitted to heirs, because it is possible that even had he lived he would not have sworn. If he intended to swear, he should have done so immediately. The same applies to Responsa Rosh, rule 68, siman 19, cited by Beit Yosef there at the end of siman 84, where a witness testified that in his presence the lender promised the borrower to return a document for a certain sum when he found it. Since the lender admitted before the witness that he would return it to the borrower, the presumption arising from the document's presence among the orphans is weakened, and we do not say, 'if it was paid, why is the document still in his hand?' By contrast, where the witness merely testifies that the document was paid, we say that since the lender did not return the document to the borrower, it is probable that he would have sworn had he lived. Likewise, in rule 86, siman 3, concerning collection from purchasers, where Rosh ruled that he does not collect, the reason is that there was another weakness in the claim: after Levi swore, he did not remove the land from Shimon, and the matter remained unresolved. We therefore say that Shimon surely paid Levi afterward. These distinctions resolve many of Sema's difficulties.
