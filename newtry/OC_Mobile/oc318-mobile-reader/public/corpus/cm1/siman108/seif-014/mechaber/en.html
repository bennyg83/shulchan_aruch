The rule stated in this law, that a person does not transmit an oath to his children as an inheritance, is not extended to every analogous case. Thus, if one impaired his document by admitting partial payment and died, then although he could collect only upon taking an oath, his sons swear that their father did not instruct them through another person, did not tell them orally, and that they found nothing among their father's documents indicating that the entire note was paid. They then collect the balance of the note from the borrower or his heirs, provided the borrower did not die before the lender. Similarly, when one comes to collect in the opposing party's absence or from purchasers, or when one witness testifies that the note was paid, and the lender died before swearing while the borrower was alive, the lender's heirs take the heirs' oath and collect. If two witnesses testify that part was paid, the heirs swear that their father did not instruct them that more was paid than the witnesses attest. Only in cases of this kind, where they hold a document, do they swear and collect. But the heirs of a hired worker or a victim of robbery, who hold no document, do not swear and collect. The same applies when a heses oath was reversed to the claimant and the claimant died.
