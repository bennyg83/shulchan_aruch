And one who was robbed. The Sema explains that this includes claims against a hirer, a robber, and the defendant himself. Chazal did not enact that heirs may extract money merely by taking the oath that their father did not instruct them otherwise, which is not an oath based on certain knowledge, except where they hold a promissory note whose obligor admits that the debt remains outstanding. The Shach adds that an employer facing the heirs of a worker takes only a hesset oath and is exempt, citing Maharshal. The Bach disagrees and understands the Ran and Ramah to require an oath while holding a sacred object. The Shach responds that Maharshal's position is supported by Baal HaMaor, Rambam, Tur, and other poskim, as explained in siman 89, seif 2.

The Shach writes that in such a case the defendant takes a hesset oath and is exempt. As for a shopkeeper relying on his ledger and workers, workers certainly have a definite claim while payment is uncertain, so according to all views their heirs swear that their father did not instruct them otherwise and collect. The Ran states this expressly, and the Maggid Mishneh cites the Ramban accordingly. This is the primary ruling, as explained in siman 91. The law of a shopkeeper appears to depend on the dispute between the Ramban and Baal HaMaor. The Shach ruled there in accordance with Baal HaMaor, that a shopkeeper has the same law as the workers.
