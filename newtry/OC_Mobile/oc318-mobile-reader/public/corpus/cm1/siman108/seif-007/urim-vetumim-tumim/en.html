If the minor has an administrator, etc. This ruling requires study. What is the basis for requiring this oath? It is explicit above in siman 37, seif 1, and below in siman 290, seif 12, that an administrator is qualified to testify and is treated like any other witness. If so, the administrator is a single witness who supports the claimant, and we hold that a supporting single witness exempts from an oath. This should be especially true with an oath imposed on one who collects, where the strict law would allow collection without the oath, as explained above in siman 87. The source of this ruling is the Raavad in a responsum, as cited by the Baal HaTerumot, shaar 14, and the Tur. According to the Maggid Mishneh in Hilkhot Mekhirah, chapter 20, the Raavad does not hold that a supporting single witness exempts from an oath. But that does not resolve the ruling for the Tur and the Mechaber, who do hold that a supporting witness exempts. More fundamentally, even according to the Raavad, an administrator is still in the category of a witness. If there were two administrators they would be believed by everyone. What then can be achieved by having a single administrator swear? Either he is believed as a witness without an oath, or his oath does not transform him into competent testimony. Indeed, a witness who himself takes an oath is not thereby strengthened as a witness, as we hold in the chapter HaMekadesh. We would have to say that in this setting he is not treated as a witness at all, but as a litigant, so an oath is both required and effective. But that directly conflicts with simanim 37 and 290, where an administrator is treated as a witness. Below in siman 290, seif 2, the Rama cites Mordechai explicitly that if a debtor says he paid money belonging to orphans to their administrator and the administrator denies it, where it is known that the money belonged to the orphans the administrator is believed without an oath. If we believe him that he was not paid, all the more should we believe him concerning whether the father instructed the heirs about payment. The difficulty is substantial. One can answer, though somewhat narrowly, that an administrator is treated as a witness only after the orphan reaches majority and brings his own claim. At that stage the administrator is no longer a party to the litigation and may testify like any other witness. While the orphan is still a minor and the administrator himself prosecutes the claim, the administrator functions as the litigating representative and is treated like the orphan for procedural purposes. He then cannot simultaneously serve as an independent witness, and must take the same oath the orphan would have been required to take. Even this requires study, because the source in the Raavad is itself questionable. He derives the rule from the case where a guardian and the guardian's wife swear and collect, but there no debt instrument exists at all. Strict law would not allow collection merely on their statements. The Sages created a special remedy analogous to the enactment for one who was robbed, and it is therefore understandable that they permit collection only with an oath. Here, by contrast, there is a valid debt instrument and the oath is merely a rabbinic safeguard to satisfy the defendant. Why should that safeguard disqualify an administrator from acting as a supporting witness? All the more so where there are two administrators. If the theory is that they are litigants, both would be litigants, but surely two independent qualified administrators should not be weaker than two witnesses. If two exempt from an oath, one supporting witness should likewise exempt where the law recognizes a supporting witness. The better limitation may be that the ruling applies only where the administrator has a migo because he could have destroyed the document. If he has no such migo, by what authority can an administrator weaken the minor's document? We do not live by his admission, and he has no right to harm the minor. Perhaps even the Raavad meant only that when the administrator himself comes to collect while knowing that the claim is not valid, he may not do so without the required oath. The document itself should not be torn, however, but left until the orphans reach majority. At most the administrator is then a single witness testifying that the document was paid.
