When do the lender's heirs swear and collect from the borrower's heirs? Where the lender died first and the borrower died afterwards. If the borrower died during the lender's lifetime and the lender then died, the lender's heirs, whether sons, brothers, or other heirs, collect nothing even with an oath. The lender had already become obligated to swear to the borrower's heirs, as is required of one who collects from the property of orphans. Rama: Even if the lender already swore, that does not help where there remains a concern that he was paid after the oath. A person cannot bequeath to his children money that he himself could collect only by taking an oath. If a judge nevertheless required the lender's heirs to swear and they collected the debt, the money is not taken back from them. Likewise, if the lender's heirs seized property first, it is not removed from their possession. Therefore, a debt document held by orphans who seek to collect from other orphans, where the borrower's father died first, is neither torn up nor used to collect. Rama: The rule that the document is not torn applies specifically to a guardian. A beit din before which the case is actually litigated, and into whose possession the document comes, certainly tears it once the court has ruled that it cannot be used to collect. Nevertheless, if they did not tear it and a later beit din collected on it, what that later beit din did stands. Even if there was a guarantor, where the borrower died first the lender's heirs may not collect from the guarantor if the borrower left property. Otherwise the guarantor, after paying, would return and collect from the borrower's heirs because their father left them property. There is no distinction here between an ordinary guarantor and an arev kablan. Some say that if the guarantor himself is the heir, the lender's heirs may collect from him.
