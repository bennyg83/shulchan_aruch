[Shach, letter 7] He is not believed against a presumption. Note: See Mordechai, first chapter of Bava Batra, who asks why Reish Lakish should not be believed to say, “I paid within the fixed time,” by virtue of a migo that he could have claimed that the debt was forgiven. He proves from this that a claim of forgiveness is a weak claim and is believed only by virtue of a migo. This is cited by the Rama at the beginning of siman 70. It is clear that the Mordechai holds that even a full migo against a presumption depends upon this unresolved question. Nimukei Yosef, first chapter of Bava Metzia, page 12a, in the sugya of one who forcibly seized an item before us, likewise writes: “Alternatively, a migo against a presumption was left unresolved in the first chapter of Bava Batra; therefore, the rule is lenient for the claimant and stringent for the defendant.” It is thus also clear that Nimukei Yosef disagrees with the Shach.
