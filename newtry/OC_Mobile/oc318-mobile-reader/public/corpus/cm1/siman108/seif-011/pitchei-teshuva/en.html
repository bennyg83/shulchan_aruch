Shaar Mishpat writes that even if the debtor traveled overseas before the debt became due and later died there, the creditor may collect from the heirs only after taking an oath. We are concerned that after the due date the debtor may have sent payment to the creditor through an agent. He proves this from Baal HaTerumot, sha'ar 15, and the Tur above, siman 106, regarding collection in the debtor's absence. This, he says, challenges Responsa Noda BiYehudah, siman 18, which ruled that when a husband left home and died on the road, his wife may collect her ketubah without an oath according to the authorities who do not suspect prior transfer of funds when a person dies suddenly and the obligation was still within its term. Noda BiYehudah reasoned that the only concern would be that the husband had transferred property to her before death, and since he was away from home there was no such concern. Shaar Mishpat says that Baal HaTerumot and the Tur were overlooked there, and that although She'erit Yosef, siman 67, also rules like Noda BiYehudah, his own analysis is primary. Pitchei Teshuva adds that it may nevertheless be possible to distinguish between an ordinary debt and a woman's ketubah.
