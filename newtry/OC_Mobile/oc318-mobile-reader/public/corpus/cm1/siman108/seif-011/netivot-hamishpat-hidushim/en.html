If the borrower left assets. According to our practice after the enactment of the Geonim, there is no distinction between a guarantor and a purchaser. If the borrower left even movable property, collection is first directed against the heirs. If he left nothing, collection may be made from the guarantor or purchaser. See Beurim: where there is an earlier creditor and a later creditor, and the borrower died during the earlier creditor's lifetime, the later creditor may collect from the heirs, and the earlier creditor can no longer remove it from him. But heirs who come by virtue of their mother's ketubah against a later creditor may say, "We waive our mother's oath," and they take priority over the later creditor.
