Assets. This means both land and movables, because even under strict Talmudic law the heirs are commanded to use inherited movables to pay their father's debt, as the Tur writes at the beginning of siman 107. If the father left them nothing at all, then although the borrower's heirs may later reimburse the guarantor out of gratitude for what he did for their father, the lender's heirs may nevertheless collect from the guarantor, since as a matter of law the borrower's heirs owe the guarantor nothing. The Tur writes this in this siman, seifim 31 and 32. He further distinguishes purchasers: even where the borrower died during the lender's lifetime, the lender's heirs may collect from purchasers if the borrower left no land, even if he left movables, because purchasers cannot ordinarily recover against the borrower's heirs. But if the borrower left land, the lender likewise may not collect from a purchaser in this case, since the purchaser has recourse against the heirs and it is as though the lender were first seeking collection from them. The Sema wonders why the Mechaber omitted this rule, perhaps because he regarded it as obvious from the law of a guarantor. The Shach answers that the Mechaber's position, evident from Bedek HaBayit and the Beit Yosef, is that purchasers may recover from inherited movables under the Geonic enactment, and that is why he omitted the Tur's distinction. The Shach adds that nowadays movables are treated like land for these purposes, citing siman 419 and Rabbeinu Yerucham. See also siman 129, seif 15.
