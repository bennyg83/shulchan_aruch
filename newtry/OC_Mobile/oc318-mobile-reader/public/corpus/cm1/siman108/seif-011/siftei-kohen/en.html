If the debtor left property. The Sema, seif katan 40, wonders why the Mechaber omitted this rule. There is no difficulty. He did not see the Mechaber's words in Bedek HaBayit at the beginning of siman 101 and here, and the Beit Yosef likewise states the point briefly. The Mechaber holds that purchasers may collect from movable property under the enactment of the Geonim. For this reason he also omitted the Tur's qualification at the beginning of siman 101. This also follows from the Rambam at the end of chapter 13 of the laws of lender and borrower and even from the Bach's own words at the beginning of siman 101. Although the Bach here argues from Rabbeinu Ephraim, cited by the Rosh in the first chapter of Bava Kamma, that a damaged party does not collect from movable property, this is incorrect. Most authorities agree that today movable property is treated as land for all legal purposes. The Rosh, Tur, and Shulchan Aruch at the end of siman 419 state that because present-day reliance is primarily upon movable property, it has the legal status of land even without an enactment. Rabbeinu Yerucham says likewise, and the Mechaber rules accordingly in siman 419:3, unlike Rabbeinu Ephraim. Moreover, Rabbeinu Ephraim may have disagreed only regarding tort claims, because they are uncommon, not regarding purchasers. This is the primary ruling.
