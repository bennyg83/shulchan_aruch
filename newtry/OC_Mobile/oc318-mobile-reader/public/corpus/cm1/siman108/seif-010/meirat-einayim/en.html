Some say that they too, etc. This view holds that the reason a person cannot bequeath an oath to his children is that their father was required to swear with certainty, "I was not paid," and the heirs cannot take that oath. Here, by contrast, both the first heirs and the heirs of those heirs take only an oath based on lack of knowledge, namely that their predecessor did not tell them the document was paid. Baal HaTerumot in Shaar 14, ruling 3, records both reasons behind the two opinions and says that the practical difference between them is precisely this case of heirs of heirs. Although in this case of heirs of heirs the borrower did not die during the lender's lifetime, it is not automatically included in the restriction "do not add," because the lender's grandchildren are seeking to extract money from the borrower's heirs that their own father could not have collected from those heirs except with an oath. Rav and Shmuel formulated their case as "the borrower died during the lender's lifetime" only because they were discussing the lender's first generation of heirs, where the oath issue necessarily arises in that fashion. Examine this carefully.
