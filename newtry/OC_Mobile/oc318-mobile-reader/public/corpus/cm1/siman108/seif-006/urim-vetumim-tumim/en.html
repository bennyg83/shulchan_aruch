Or exchanged property. This ruling requires study. The Rif, Rosh, and Rambam mention only merchandise that came directly from the original investment, meaning that the deceased received money and purchased the identified goods with those very funds. Such goods are unquestionably treated as the investor's property and may be collected without an oath. If the original merchandise was later exchanged, however, perhaps the deceased first returned the investor's property and then acquired the present goods with his own funds. Maharam says explicitly in the responsa printed with Mishneh Torah, Sefer Kinyan, din 11, that if the merchandise changed form and was exchanged for other goods, we plead for the heirs that the original property may have been returned, based on the migo that an unavoidable accident could have occurred. The Tur in Yoreh Deah siman 176 nevertheless includes exchanged property, and from there the Mechaber took the present ruling. The issue therefore rests on an actual dispute among authorities and requires study.
