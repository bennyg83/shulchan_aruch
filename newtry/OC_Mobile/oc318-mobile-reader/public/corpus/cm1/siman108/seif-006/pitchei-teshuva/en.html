He must swear. Tumim asks that an appointed guardian is generally qualified to testify, as stated above in siman 37, seif 8, and below in siman 290, seif 12. If so, why is an oath required here? The guardian should be like any other single witness who supports the claimant and exempts him from an oath, especially where the claimant is one who by strict law would take without an oath and the oath is only an added requirement. Tumim gives an extended resolution, and Netivot HaMishpat offers a different answer. Responsa Chut HaShani, simanim 6 and 7, also discusses the issue at length. Shevut Yaakov, volume 2, siman 171, writes simply that the present case must concern a guardian who is disqualified from testimony, such as a relative. Therefore the claimant must swear. If the guardian is qualified as a witness, no oath is required, just as with any other witness. On that basis he ruled in a case where a father-appointed guardian for orphans sued Reuven after finding in the deceased Shimon's ledger that Reuven owed one hundred gold coins on specified pledges. Reuven claimed that he had paid fifty during Shimon's lifetime and owed only fifty. Shevut Yaakov concluded that Reuven must pay according to the ledger entry and has no oath to impose either upon the orphans or upon the guardian, because the guardian claims that the deceased gave him no contrary instruction. See also above, siman 72, seif 35, seif katan 29.
