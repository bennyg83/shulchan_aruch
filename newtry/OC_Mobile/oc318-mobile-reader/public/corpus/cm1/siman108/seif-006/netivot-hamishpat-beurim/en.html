If the minor has a guardian. Tumim asks what relevance the guardian's oath has, since a guardian is believed as a witness, as explained in siman 290, seif 12, in the gloss. There is no difficulty. There, the case certainly concerns a guardian who does not conduct transactions with the orphans' property, but was appointed only to litigate concerning their property, as explained in Agudat Ezov. If a guardian who actually conducts transactions with the orphans' property is alleged by the borrower to have received repayment and the guardian denies it, there could be no more interested witness than he, because if he admitted the borrower's claim he would have to reimburse the orphans. The cited ruling must therefore concern a guardian appointed only to litigate. Here, by contrast, the case concerns a guardian who conducts transactions with the orphans' property and holds their money. Even toward the orphans he would ordinarily need to swear, lest he have taken some of their money for himself, but the Sages exempted him because they feared people would refrain from accepting guardianship. Here, when he swears against the borrower, that concern does not apply, as the Sema explains. He is then treated like an actual litigant who must swear when coming to collect. We suspect that even if the orphans' father told him the debt was paid, he might nevertheless sue on the note for his own benefit and falsely seek collection because of some old loan he believes is owed to him. He must therefore swear. This is clear.
