They collect without an oath, etc. The reason is that the borrower's heirs themselves assert with certainty in their father's name that no loan ever occurred. Anyone who says "I did not borrow" is as though he says "I did not repay," and the document establishes that a loan existed. Although the lender had given them credibility, that credibility was only to say that the debt had been repaid, whereas they themselves admit that it was not repaid. This applies specifically where the heirs make a definite claim that their father told them he never borrowed, as explained in siman 75, seif 16, where such a definite inherited claim is treated like one made concerning their own affairs.
Even if the lender granted credibility to the borrower. At the end of siman 71 the Mechaber rules that credibility granted to the borrower extends to his heirs as well. Therefore there was no need to state the greater novelty that he also granted credibility expressly to the heirs. Even if the borrower or his heirs now wish to swear and be exempt, we do not listen; the lender's heirs swear and collect.
