The creditor trusted the borrower. See Sema, seif katan 3, who writes that even if the borrower or his heirs wish to swear and thereby be exempt, the creditor's heirs swear and collect. This is very difficult. The Mechaber explicitly says that they collect without an oath. Moreover, what oath would the borrower or heirs take when their original position is that no loan ever existed, and what novelty would this teach? There is plainly a textual error in Sema. The correct reading should be: even if the borrower or his heirs wish to swear that payment was later made and thereby be exempt. Tur in siman 79 rules that when the original denial occurred outside beit din, the debtor can later say that he paid afterward. One might therefore think that heirs are likewise believed to say that their father later paid, because his earlier statement, "I never borrowed," was made outside beit din, and the father himself would have been believed to say that he subsequently paid. The heirs might say that their father later told them he paid, or that they themselves later paid, particularly where the creditor had granted them credibility. The ruling teaches that the granted credibility is nevertheless lost once the borrower became established as a false denier, even where the denial occurred outside beit din, for the reason Sema gives in siman 71. Because the borrower's special credibility was already nullified during the creditor's lifetime, the creditor or his heirs may collect upon the required oath. The doctrine that an oath cannot be inherited does not apply, because this oath is required only if the borrower's heirs make a definite claim of later payment. It is unlike ordinary oaths against heirs, where the claimant must swear even absent a definite defense.
