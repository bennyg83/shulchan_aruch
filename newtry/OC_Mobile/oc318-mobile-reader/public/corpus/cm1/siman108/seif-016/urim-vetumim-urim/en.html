Rabbi Yosef ibn Migash holds that even if the debt had not matured and the father never sued in beit din, we can still say the borrower should have demanded return of his document. The Shach follows Rabbi Meir HaLevi Abulafia that only if the lender sued during life and the borrower failed to produce the receipt do we see a defect. Otherwise there is no reason to weaken the receipt. Even according to Rabbi Yosef ibn Migash, the rule applies only where the due date had not yet arrived and therefore there is also the presumption that a person does not pay within the term. Once the term arrived, that additional presumption is absent. I wrote the same in Tumim for another reason. The defect also applies specifically to a receipt that cancels the entire document. A receipt for only part of the debt does not weaken the document in the same way. Once the remainder is paid, the document is torn.
