The rule says that even witnesses who remember the payment cannot testify after the creditor has died, because testimony is not accepted outside the presence of the litigant. Beit Hillel asked that we validate documents even outside a litigant's presence. Rashba's own explanation of document validation resolves this. Validation witnesses do not testify about the debt itself. They testify only that the signatures are authentic. That limited testimony may be received without the opposing litigant present. A receipt found only after the creditor's death is different. Simply identifying the signatures does not help, because the suspicious timing allows concern that the receipt was fabricated, and even the signatories' recognition can be explained as imitation. The only effective testimony would be direct testimony that payment actually occurred or that the receipt was genuinely delivered. That is testimony concerning the debt itself and cannot be received outside the litigant's presence. Even if the signatories say with certainty that they themselves signed and delivered this receipt, they are effectively testifying that payment or waiver occurred. That is substantive testimony concerning the money in the document, not mere signature validation. Nevertheless, because Tosafot explains the rule permitting validation outside a party's presence differently, namely that validation is only rabbinic, we cannot remove the original debt instrument from the heirs merely on the strength of Rashba's stricter approach. In a case of doubt, possession of the instrument remains with the heirs.
