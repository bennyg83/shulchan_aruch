A minor heir, etc. This is the language of the Rambam, chapter 6 of the laws of lender and borrower, law 18. The reference to the Tur, seif 34, in the name of the Ri Migash and the Rosh is incorrect, as I explain below.
A receipt produced after his father's death, etc. The Tur cites Rabbi Yosef: even if the note had not yet matured during the father's lifetime, the receipt is not accepted, because if it were genuine it would have been produced during the father's lifetime. The Sema presents this without qualification, although the Ramah disagrees and requires that the creditor actually sued during his lifetime and the receipt was not then produced. Perhaps the Sema relied on the Rosh, who cites Rabbi Yosef without qualification, or on the Beit Yosef's view that the Rambam agrees, following the Baal HaTerumot. Yet the Baal HaTerumot himself ultimately rules like the Ramah, as do the Rashba, Maggid Mishneh, Nimukei Yosef, and apparently Rabbeinu Yerucham. The Rambam's language is not decisive. This latter view appears primary. It further seems that Rabbi Yosef refers to a receipt whose signatures are not recognized and which the heir now seeks to validate. Because of the suspicious circumstances, it is not validated. The Ramah disagrees that this alone is suspicious, but where the receipt was not produced when suit was brought during the father's lifetime, even a formally validated receipt is rejected, because we suspect that the heir practiced until he forged the signatures accurately. If the receipt is already validated, however, Rabbi Yosef may agree that mere failure to display it earlier is insufficient. This is unlike the Sema's apparent understanding. Moreover, one view holds that a receipt is always validated and the note torn. It also appears that Rabbi Yosef agrees that if the note had matured and the creditor had sued during the father's lifetime without the receipt being produced, it is rejected. His wording is precise. Otherwise there would be no sufficient reason to impair it merely because it was not shown. Before maturity, however, he combines the omission with the presumption that a person does not pay before the due date.

Bedek HaBayit notes that this is difficult because a document may ordinarily be validated outside the presence of the opposing party, but answers that this case differs because the circumstances themselves create suspicion. This is correct, unlike the Gidulei Terumah, who leaves the matter unresolved. The Bach misunderstood the Beit Yosef as referring only to witnesses who recall the payment, and therefore ruled that validation is accepted. He also overlooked the Beit Yosef's citation of the Maggid Mishneh in the name of the Rashba and the Shulchan Aruch's ruling here. The Ramban in the first chapter of Bava Batra likewise supports Bedek HaBayit's distinction: although Ravina ordinarily permits validation outside the opposing party's presence, where the receipt was not produced earlier it is treated as defective and we do not trouble to validate it until the minors attain majority.
