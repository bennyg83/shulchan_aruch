Some explain that the Tur and Mechaber deliberately say "like an oral loan" with the comparative prefix, teaching that although this obligation arises from robbery and thus is a liability written in the Torah, since every robber must pay for what he caused to be lost, nevertheless it is collected only from unencumbered property. The amount of the loss and produce consumed does not become publicly known. But this explanation is incorrect. If that were the point, the same wording should have been used regarding stolen movable property. Rather, the comparison teaches that although robbery of land itself is a public matter, the loss of its produce is not publicized and is therefore like an oral loan.
