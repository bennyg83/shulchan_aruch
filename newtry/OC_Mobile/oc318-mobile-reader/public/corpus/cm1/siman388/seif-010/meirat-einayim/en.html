It is permitted to kill, etc. Rivash, siman 236, writes that if a person is arrested for his own person in order to judge him because he is an informer, or because he made statements rendering him liable to death, and he offers sufficient guarantors to ensure he will not flee, the guaranty is not accepted. What can be done to the guarantors if he escapes? Darkei Moshe cites this.
With his body or with his property. An informer is worse than an ordinary tortfeasor even though he does not physically damage another's property with his own hands. The Gemara derives this from the verse, "Your children fainted, they lie at the head of every street like a wild ox in a net." Just as once an animal falls into a hunter's net no mercy is shown to it, so too Jewish property that falls into gentile hands is subjected to repeated false charges and exactions, often leading even to danger to life. Therefore an informer has the law of a pursuer. Nevertheless, he is no worse than an ordinary pursuer. After the offense has already been completed, one does not kill him without prior warning and a proper beit din judgment, unless he has acquired an established status as a habitual informer, as the Mechaber explains later in seif 11.
