It is permitted to kill an informer wherever he is found, even in the present era, and he may be killed before he actually informs. Once he says, 'I will inform against so-and-so's person or property,' even concerning property of slight value, he has exposed himself to death. He is warned, 'Do not inform.' If he defiantly says, 'I will nevertheless inform,' it is a mitzvah to kill him, and whoever acts first acquires the merit. Rama: If there is no time to warn him, warning is unnecessary. Some say that an informer may not be killed unless it is impossible to stop him by injuring one of his limbs. If he can be stopped by cutting out his tongue, blinding him, or another lesser measure, it is forbidden to kill him, because he is no worse than any other pursuer.
