The Maggid Mishneh writes that the ruling follows Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi in the Yerushalmi, in the latter chapter HaGozel and the last chapter of Ketubot, that one person is held responsible for another only regarding the royal land tax and poll tax. The term land tax refers to the royal levy mentioned in the Bavli, interpreted as provisions, lodging, and related impositions. Since these are obligations imposed by the king, if one person paid them for another, the other must reimburse him, even if the king collected from the payer unlawfully. Certainly, if the king lawfully collected from him because of the other's obligation, the other must reimburse him, as in the cases in the latter chapter HaGozel, Bava Kamma 113b. 'Poll tax' means the annual head tax, as Rashi and the Raavad explain there.
a) The Kesef Mishneh writes that it appears the Rambam alludes to the Gemara in HaGozel: this applies to a granary tax and the poll tax of that year, but once the year has passed and the king has already been appeased, the obligation has passed.
