Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat, siman 154, writes that the position of Rabbeinu Baruch in Mordechai, the dissenting view cited here, is sound in both reasoning and intent. His argument is a valid a fortiori inference: if the person's own body is not recoverable, all the more so his money. The only difficulty is the verse, "and a righteous person shall wear it," but the verse does not specify which righteous person. The person who presently withholds the money is himself the first righteous person. Why should it be removed from him merely to give it to another righteous person, even if the latter already has a stronger legal claim? This answers Shach's assertion that Rif and his followers disagree and also shows that no proof can be drawn from the responsum attributed to Rashi. Chatam Sofer concludes that, in any event, no authority clearly disputes Rabbeinu Baruch, his reasoning is sound, and Rama established this view in Shulchan Aruch.
