And likewise if he admitted. See Baer Heitev, who concludes that the law is not as implied by Maharshal. Ketzot HaChoshen writes that Shitah Mekubetzet to Bava Kamma 91 implies like Maharshal, that even with a rabbinic penalty, one who admits is exempt. Responsa Shivat Tzion, siman 109, writes that Raavan, siman 92, states explicitly like Maharshal that admission exempts even from a rabbinic penalty. He also seeks to prove that the Rambam, chapter 7 of Hilchot Chovel U'Mazik, halachah 4, agrees, and Responsa Meil Tzedakah, end of siman 61, assumes this as obvious. Shivat Tzion applies this to a community whose enactment provided that anyone who violated one of its regulations must pay a penalty of eighteen gold coins to the communal fund. A householder was suspected of violating a regulation. When the communal leaders investigated, he brazenly challenged them and boasted that he had violated the regulation many times. The leaders wished to penalize him on the basis of his own admission and asked whether the principle that one who admits to a penalty is exempt applies where there are no witnesses. Shivat Tzion notes that this communal penalty is analogous to a rabbinic penalty, and the Shach in siman 388 ruled that the exemption for self-admission does not apply to rabbinic penalties, against Maharshal. He nevertheless concludes against the Shach. Since Rambam, Raavan, Maharshal, and Meil Tzedakah all hold that self-admission exempts even from a rabbinic penalty, that is the proper halachah, or at minimum one cannot extract money from the violator, who may say that he follows those authorities. Nor may the community argue that irrespective of the general law he is liable by force of the communal enactment and accepted custom, as in Yoreh Deah siman 214. The enactment did not explicitly say that one becomes liable through his own admission. Its ordinary meaning is that the penalty is imposed when the violation is established in accordance with law, namely by witnesses. Even if the enactment is ambiguous, money cannot be extracted on the basis of doubt. Accordingly, the community cannot demand the fixed penalty recorded in its ledger. The communal leaders may, however, impose a separate disciplinary fine for his brazenness toward them and his boastful contempt for communal regulations, but they should act deliberately and with restraint.
