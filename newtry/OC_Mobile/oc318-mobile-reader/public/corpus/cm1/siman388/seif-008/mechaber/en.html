An informer who voluntarily revealed another person's property is not administered either a severe oath or a shevuat heset, because he is wicked and there is no greater disqualification than this. Even if he has not yet informed but publicly says, "I will go and inform," he becomes disqualified as a witness, and we do not apply the principle that people sometimes make threats they do not carry out. Mordechai, chapter HaGozel Batra. Others disagree unless he is known to act this way habitually. Mordechai, chapter HaNishba'in. Even if it is not known whether he is habitually an informer, the person threatened may protect himself through non-Jewish authorities even if this causes the informer a loss. Maharam of Rothenburg and Responsa Rashba, siman 181; Rosh, klal 16. But an informer who was compelled to identify or deliver property and actually handled and delivered it, although he is liable to pay, is not deemed wicked for this purpose. He is merely financially liable and may be required to swear like any other qualified person. Rama: Likewise, if he admits that he informed and there are no witnesses, although a person is not disqualified as a witness by his own admission, he is nevertheless liable to pay. Mordechai, chapter HaGozel Batra.
