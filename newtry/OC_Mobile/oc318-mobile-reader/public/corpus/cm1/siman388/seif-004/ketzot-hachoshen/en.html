This is not called physically handing it over. The Shach writes that if the parties jointly held a pledge for the debt and one partner handed the pledge to the government officer, this counts as physically handing over property. He relies on Maharam and on the distinction in the judicial-error cases between a mere ruling and actually transferring a pledge. In my view even handing over a pledge is ordinarily only damage to a lien, not direct destruction of the creditor's property. The body of the pledge still belongs to the debtor and can be redeemed with money. Therefore, even where a creditor has significant rights in collateral, the wrong is conceptually damage to his lien and ordinarily falls under *dina de-garmi*. Responsa Maimoniyot supports this. Where a horse was pledged jointly to Reuven and Shimon and Shimon caused it to be lent to Levi, who then lost it, Rabbeinu Tam treated Shimon as one who damaged Reuven's lien. The analysis was not ordinary direct taking of Reuven's property. Accordingly, if the pledge was handed over under compulsion, the partner can be exempt under authorities who exempt accidental *garmi*. The judicial-error case involving physical transfer of a pledge has a special structure that I discussed in siman 25 and does not prove that every interference with collateral is direct physical damage.
It is not called physically handing it over. Even according to Tosafot, who can treat a mistaken judicial ruling that immediately establishes the law as direct damage, the present case is different. Speech is treated like an act only where the words themselves legally change the status, such as a judge's ruling that causes the losing party's property right to terminate. Here, one partner's coerced waiver does not actually erase the other partner's share of the debt. A partner cannot waive his fellow's portion. In addition, a waiver extracted by coercion is itself defective. If the debtor refuses to pay because of the partner's words, those words merely caused the later refusal. They did not themselves extinguish the debt. The case is therefore at most consequential damage.
