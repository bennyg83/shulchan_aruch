[Seif 3] The king compelled him. The Rambam writes: “The king compelled him to hand over this person until he showed him stores of wine or oil, or until he showed him another person's money.” Maharshal infers from the Rambam's failure to say “the wine or oil of so-and-so” that liability applies only where the coercer demanded money and he showed another person's money, because he should have shown his own money and instead saved himself with another's property. But if he was compelled to show stores of wine or oil, he is exempt even if he showed another person's stores, and need not appease the coercer with his own money, since the coercion was not directed toward money as such. This implies that even when money is demanded, if he has no cash of his own he is exempt when he shows another person's money, despite having property worth money, because he need not satisfy the coercer with substitute property. The Sema similarly writes that if the coercer seeks the person of his fellow in order to seize him, and so forth. It appears to me, however, that no permission exists to point out another person's body, even if that person could redeem himself with money. Since the coercer intends to beat him until he pays, we say: why is your blood redder than your fellow's? The only permission found in the law of coercion is to show another person's property. If the coercer expressly demands the body of one's fellow, he may not identify him, even if the fellow could pay, because the coerced person can likewise save himself with his own money.
