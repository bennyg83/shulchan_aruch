If the person informed upon seized, etc. The Gemara leaves unresolved whether the Sages enacted that a person harmed by an informer should be believed by oath to collect, as they enacted for one who was robbed. The Rambam holds that seizure is effective in such an unresolved case. Ri and the Rosh hold that because it is a doubt in law, seizure does not help. See the Derishah at the beginning of this siman.

One who says to another, "You informed upon me." Rashba writes in responsum 576 that an informer who admits part of the claim pays what he admits but not the remainder. Even if he says, "I know about fifty and do not know about fifty," we do not apply the rule that one who is obligated to swear and cannot swear must pay, because he was not expected to know the exact amount of damage caused. Darkei Moshe cites this.
Even if he merely informed, etc. Meaning, if the ruler himself saw the matter or gentiles saw it and would certainly tell the ruler what Reuven had done, such as where Reuven struck Shimon in public, then even if Levi afterward went and reported Reuven to the ruler, Levi is exempt. Without Levi's report the ruler would certainly have learned of it from the gentile witnesses.
