If he seized, etc., and some say that in a doubt, etc. This follows his general approach, as stated in Yoreh Deah siman 315 and above.
And if they knew, etc. In such a case no special enactment for a robbed claimant is required. It is the ordinary rule of the Mishna in Shevuos that the opposing party swears and the claimant collects. See Sema.
And there is one who says, etc. The Rambam, representing the first opinion, disagrees. As in seif 1 concerning one who hands property to an official, he does not distinguish between voluntary and coerced physical delivery. Since he treats garmi as true damage, where the informer denies the act but says he does not know the amount, the rules of an uncertain monetary oath apply. Tosafos and the Raavad take a different approach to matters one was not expected to know. See my comments in simanim 90 and 87. According to Riva, if dina d'garmi is rabbinic, the special oath consequences would apply only to one who physically took and handed over the property, while the Rif and Rambam reject Riva's premise.
(Collected note) And there is one who says, etc. Rabbeinu Yitzchak disagrees and writes that the Gemara's unresolved question applies even in this circumstance. The Rashba cites the Raavad similarly, and Nimukei Yosef derives it from the sugya on 98b. (End of note.)
And all this concerns an informer, etc., and likewise if he caused, etc. The Sages disputed Rabbi Meir regarding dina d'garmi only in monetary damage, not bodily injury. The Torah obligates a person who injures another for loss of work and medical expenses. Locking a person in a room is therefore treated differently from merely causing loss to an animal. Bodily injury is considered akin to direct damage. The question concerning an informer is only within the law of garmi, not ordinary direct damage.
And he must pay, etc. Meaning even where the informer did not hand over the victim's property at all, but the victim had to spend money to redeem himself, comparable to medical expenses in personal injury.
One who says to another, etc. The same evidentiary principle applies even in actual theft, as stated in siman 75 seif 8. This is why seif 7 speaks of witnesses that he informed, paralleling the Mishna in Shevuos concerning one who was robbed. See Sema in the name of the Rashba.
And some say he must, etc. As in Shevuos, where the oath is taken in the presence of the opposing party, and the Yerushalmi explains that this is because of embarrassment. See siman 87 seif 23.
And he need not do, etc. Meaning where the person whose property was delivered does not know of the proceeding. See siman 176 seif 36. Even when the claimant himself brings the claim, however, he must swear in the official's presence where required. See Shach.
Two, etc. Like five who sat on a bench together, as stated in simanim 381 and 383 seif 3. The Mordechai's additional rule that each pays only half even if the other cannot pay is omitted because it depends on the dispute in siman 410 seif 37.
And if one after another, etc. This parallels the bundle and bench cases. Once the first person's act already made the loss certain, the second may have caused nothing additional. The Gemara's language concerning an informer treats the first effective disclosure as destruction of the property.
And even if he did not hand it over, etc. See Beer HaGolah and the preceding explanation.
Some say, etc. Maharam MiRotenburg seeks support from Rav Huna's severe sanction. This proof is not conclusive because such measures are limited to Jewish judicial authority or to cases where no other rescue is possible, as stated there and in the gloss to seif 9. See Shach.
