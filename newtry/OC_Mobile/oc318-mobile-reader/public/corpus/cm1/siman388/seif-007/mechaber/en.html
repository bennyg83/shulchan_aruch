If witnesses testify that a person informed against another's property, such as by pointing it out voluntarily or by physically carrying and handing it over under coercion, but they do not know the amount of the resulting loss, and the victim claims a specific amount while the informer denies it, then if the victim seized property it is not removed from him. He must swear while holding a sacred object and keeps what he seized. Some say that seizure does not help in a case of unresolved law. If he did not seize, nothing is collected from the informer without clear proof. If the facts are known but the victim had to compromise with the ruler for a certain amount, the victim swears and collects. Some say that if the informer admits he does not know how much loss he caused, the victim swears and collects. Rama: These rules concern informing against property. If one delivered another person's body to violent authorities, the victim swears and collects; the same applies where he caused the victim to be imprisoned, because this is treated like direct physical damage and he must pay the full loss. If one person accuses another of informing and the accused denies it, the accused takes a rabbinic oath. Some require him to swear before the ruler that he did not inform. Such a public clearing of suspicion is required only where one Jewish witness testifies against him; non-Jews are not believed for this purpose. If two people informed together, each pays half. If they informed one after the other, the second is exempt so long as the loss from the first report had not yet ended, because the damage is attributed to the first cause. This applies even where the first did not directly report but caused a violent person or non-Jew to report to the ruler. Some say that a person who was beaten by another may complain before non-Jewish authorities even though this may cause the assailant a substantial loss.
