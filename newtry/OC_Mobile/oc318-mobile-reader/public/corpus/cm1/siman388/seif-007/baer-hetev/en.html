Witnesses. If there are no witnesses against him, he takes a shevuat heset that he did not inform. So write Hagahot Ashri and Darkei Moshe. See Responsa Rashakh, part III, simanim 87 and 88, at length regarding informer law.
Was compelled. The Rambam indeed writes this, and so does the Ir Shushan, treating it as an unresolved question. But it is difficult: where one was compelled and physically took and handed over the property, the Gemara's unresolved question about an informer liable through garmi should not apply. Taking and handing over is direct damage, yet because he was compelled he is not disqualified and can swear that he was compelled and be exempt. The Rambam, Mechaber, and Ir Shushan require great study. Shach.
Denies. The Maggid Mishneh, cited by the Beit Yosef, understands the Rambam as following Rabbeinu Tam, the later "some say" cited by the Mechaber; Maharshal follows this too. The Shach disagrees and explains that the Rambam follows his own view that one who partially admits and says "I do not know" regarding the balance must pay when unable to swear, even where he was not expected to know. Therefore the Rambam needed the informer to deny the remainder. If he said he did not know the amount, he would be one obligated to swear who cannot swear, and the claimant would collect without an oath. Ibid.
Seized. Even with witnesses. Maharshal writes that if the informer brought the government directly upon another's money, this is like handing it over physically, and likewise if he informed on the person's body, the victim swears and collects. The Shach questions why bringing the government upon the property is considered direct delivery; if he did not himself hand it over, it is still garmi. Ibid.
Clear. Not merely based on estimation. See the Bach.
Such and such. Perhaps the victim exaggerated or could have settled for less. See Responsa Maharam Mintz, siman 44.
And collects. See the Shach's lengthy explanation of this dispute. He further cites Responsa Rashba, siman 571, that where one informed on another and the amount of the loss is unknown, if the victim seized movable property without witnesses, the informer can impose only a general cherem upon whoever took his property unlawfully. The Shach explains that this remains correct even according to those who generally reject seizure in a legal doubt, because here the uncertainty concerns whether the claimant is believed by oath. Since he seized without witnesses, he is believed through migo. If he chooses to retain the seized property rather than redeem it and collect his claim, he need not swear and only a general cherem remains.
His person. The Shach strongly rejects this ruling and concludes that whether one informs on another's property or person, if he did no physical act, such as personally bringing him to imprisonment, and acted only by speech, liability is solely through dina d'garmi. It therefore falls within the Gemara's unresolved question whether the enactment allowing an injured party to swear and collect applies to an informer according to one who holds garmi liable. He explains that he elaborated because Maharil ruled otherwise and Maharshal, Rama, Maharam of Lublin, and other later authorities followed him, but in the Shach's view this was an error.
You informed on me. Responsa Rashba, siman 571, writes that an informer who admits part pays what he admits but does not pay the rest. Even if he says, "I know fifty and do not know fifty," we do not say that because he cannot swear he must pay, since he was not expected to know the amount of damage. The Sema cites this. The Rashba compares it to the Yerushalmi's case of a sealed sack, following the Rosh's understanding. According to the Mechaber's rulings in siman 90, seif 10, and siman 298, seif 1, that in the sealed-sack case he must pay because he cannot swear, even though he was not expected to know, the same should apply here. The Bach likewise rejects the Rashba's responsum, and I concluded in siman 72, seif 12, that in every case one who cannot swear must pay unless the claimant admits that the defendant does not know. Shach.
One. Darkei Moshe writes that Responsa Maharam implies this, but careful examination shows no such implication. Hagahot Ashri at the end of chapter HaKones explicitly implies that even without a single witness he must swear before the official. It appears that the Rama meant only that non-Jewish officials are not themselves believed to establish the matter for this purpose. If the victim makes a definite claim that he informed, the alleged informer must clear himself before the official even with no witness. In Rabbeinu Tam's case the victim made a definite claim. The Rama's phrase "through one witness" refers to a case where the victim himself is uncertain. Responsa Maharil, siman 90, likewise supports this. Ibid.
Half. Responsa Maharam concludes that if local practice exempts an informer who was beaten because a person is not held accountable for what he says under severe pain, then the second informer is liable for the entire loss if there are witnesses that both informed together, following Rabbi Natan's principle that where collection cannot be made from one wrongdoer it is made from the other. Ibid.
Was exempted. If there is doubt whether he was already exempt from the first act of informing, money is not extracted due to doubt and the later informer is exempt. So rules Responsa Maharam there.
