His property. It is forbidden to destroy his property directly by hand, but it may be caused indirectly through grama or garmi, as the Tur writes in the name of Baal HaIttur and Mahari Weil, siman 160. Maharshal rules that it is forbidden to deliver his property to the authorities. It appears, however, that if a person is established as a habitual informer and his influence is heard only because of his wealth, so that if he becomes poor his words will no longer be heeded, it is permissible to destroy his wealth or deliver it to the authorities, because by doing so one saves others. Tosafot in Avodah Zarah 26b is uncertain whether one must return an informer's lost property. They write that an informer is worse than an apostate to idolatry, and is regularly listed together with a heretic, while the obligation of returning lost property was not extended to him. Perhaps, however, the view that one may not directly destroy an informer's property also requires returning his lost property, based on the principle that the wicked prepares and the righteous wears. Hagahot Ashri at the end of Elu Metziot writes that an informer is like an apostate to idolatry out of spite, whose lost property need not be returned. See there.
That it is permitted. The Rif and those who follow him necessarily disagree. Maharshal likewise rules that one may not take the informer's property for oneself, and this is the primary view. This is also explicit in Rashi's responsum cited by the Mordechai in the first chapter of Kiddushin and brought by Beit Yosef in siman 283, new matter 2. Shach.
