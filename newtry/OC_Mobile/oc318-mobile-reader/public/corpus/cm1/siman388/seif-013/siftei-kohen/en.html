It is forbidden to destroy an informer’s property directly. It may, however, be destroyed through indirect causation or dina de-garmi, as ruled by the Tur in the name of Baal HaIttur and Mahari Weil. It is forbidden to hand his property to gentiles, as Maharshal writes. Accordingly, one who subsequently informs against the informer’s property must pay the loss. It appears, however, that if a person is established as an informer and his influence rests only on his wealth, so that impoverishing him will prevent future harm, it is permitted to destroy his property or hand it to gentiles, because this saves others. Tosafot question whether one must return an informer’s lost object, noting that an informer is worse than an apostate and is generally grouped with sectarians. Hagahot Ashri rules that an informer is like an apostate acting defiantly, whose lost object need not be returned.
