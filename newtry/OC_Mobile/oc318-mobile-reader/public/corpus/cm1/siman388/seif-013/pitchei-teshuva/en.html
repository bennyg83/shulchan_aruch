That one may take it. See Baer Heitev, who writes that Rif and those who follow him necessarily disagree with this view. See Responsa Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat, siman 154, who writes that the words of Rabbeinu Baruch cited by Mordechai, which constitute this second opinion, are correct in their reasoning. His argument is a sound a fortiori: if one's body may be taken, all the more so one's property. The only reason to transfer it to another is the verse, "and the righteous shall wear it," but the verse does not identify which righteous person. The one presently withholding the property is the first righteous person, so why remove it from him to give it to another, even if the latter already has an established and superior claim? This answers the Shach's contention that Rif and his followers disagree, and there is likewise no proof from Rashi's responsum. Chatam Sofer concludes that, however the matter is framed, there is no one who clearly disputes Rabbeinu Baruch, his reasoning is sound, and the Rama fixed his opinion in the Shulchan Aruch. Who, then, can extract the property from the debtor or depositary against this position? See there, and what I wrote above in siman 283, seif 2, seif katan 1.
