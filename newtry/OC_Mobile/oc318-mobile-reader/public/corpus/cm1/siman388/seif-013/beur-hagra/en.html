It is forbidden, etc. See Be'er HaGolah. Proof comes from the unresolved question at the end of chapter 6 whether the Sages enacted an oath for the victim. If it were permitted simply to destroy the informer's property, certainly the victim could swear and collect. Rif writes so.
And some say, etc. Rif's proof does not clearly establish the matter. Mordechai says the passage may concern one who already carried out his plot and is no longer an established informer, whom all agree may not be killed, or one who repented. The wording 'to destroy it' supports his view and resolves Tosafot's question in Avodah Zarah. The first opinion follows the broader permission stated in Pesachim, especially according to Rif's explanation there regarding one pursuing a forbidden relation. See Hagahot Ashri.
