They divide. Responsa Maharam of Lublin, siman 26, rules according to the first opinion. This is not convincing as against Maharam of Rothenburg, whose stature in his generation was unparalleled. Moreover, the first view is essentially the individual opinion of Rabbi Eliezer of Toul in the Mordechai, while the Mordechai in HaManiach implies that other great authorities disagreed. The Agudah likewise says that the leading authorities ruled otherwise, and Darkei Moshe concludes that way. Maharshal writes that Rabbi Eliezer's exemption from monetary liability cannot be accepted, lest it lead to grave desecration of God's name. See also Responsa Maharam of Lublin, siman 120; Rama, siman 86; and Maharit, end of siman 45. Shach.
