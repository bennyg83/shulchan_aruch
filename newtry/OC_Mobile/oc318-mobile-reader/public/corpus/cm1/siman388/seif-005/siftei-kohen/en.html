It is not proper, however, to decide against Maharam of Rothenburg, who had no equal in his generation, as stated in the rulings of Mahari, siman 142. Moreover, the first view is merely the individual opinion of Rabbi Eliezer of Toul in the Mordechai at the end of HaGozel Batra and in Hagahot Maimoniyot at the end of Sefer Nezikin. The Mordechai in HaManiach implies that the great authorities disagreed. It records an incident in Cologne in which one person deposited an item with another, who later denied it. The depositor recovered his own property through gentiles, and as a result the custodian was accused and suffered a substantial loss. The authority ruled that if the depositor could have recovered his property another way, he must compensate the custodian, like one who could have slipped away from a pursuer and failed to do so. Rabbeinu Yakar ruled similarly. The Agudah writes there that the great authorities ruled this way, and the Darkei Moshe likewise concludes according to them. Maharshal in HaGozel Batra, siman 49, writes that Rabbi Eliezer of Toul’s exemption from monetary liability does not appear correct and that it is forbidden to rule that way, because it would lead to substantial desecration of God’s name. See there. It is close to certain that even Rabbi Eliezer of Toul spoke only where the owner could not recover his property in another manner. Even then, he writes that where the person lacks a migo, he is not believed. The reason is that because he is obligated to return what he seized, he acted unlawfully and cannot say that he intended only to rescue his own property. This explains why the Mordechai and Agudah, although they record the great authorities’ ruling in HaManiach, also cite Rabbi Eliezer of Toul without qualification at the end of HaGozel Batra. See there and note carefully. See also Responsa Maharam of Lublin, siman 120; Responsa of the Rama, siman 86, page 172; and Responsa Maharit at the end of siman 45.
