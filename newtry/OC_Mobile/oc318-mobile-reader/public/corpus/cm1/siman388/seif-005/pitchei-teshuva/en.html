And likewise if he admitted. See Baer Heitev, who rejects the implication of Maharshal. Ketzot HaChoshen writes that Shitah Mekubetzet to Bava Kamma 91 appears to support Maharshal, that even with a rabbinic penalty one who admits is exempt. Responsa Shivat Tzion, siman 109, writes that Raavan, siman 92, states explicitly like Maharshal that admission exempts even from a rabbinic penalty. He also argues that Rambam, Hilchot Chovel Umazik, chapter 7, law 4, appears to agree, and Responsa Meil Tzedakah, end of siman 61, assumes this plainly. On that basis Shivat Tzion discusses a community whose ordinance imposed a fixed penalty of eighteen gold coins payable to the communal fund upon anyone who violated one of its regulations. A householder was suspected of violation, and when communal leaders questioned him he brazenly boasted that he had violated the regulation many times. The leaders wished to impose the fixed penalty based solely on his own admission, with no witnesses. Shivat Tzion writes that this communal penalty resembles a rabbinic penalty. Although Shach in siman 388 rules that admission does not exempt from a rabbinic penalty, contrary to Maharshal, Shivat Tzion concludes against Shach. Since Rambam, Raavan, and Maharshal hold that admission exempts from a rabbinic penalty, and Meil Tzedakah treats this as obvious, this is the practical law. At minimum, money cannot be extracted from the violator because he may plead that he relies on those authorities. One cannot answer that, even if strict penalty law would exempt him, the communal enactment and accepted custom independently obligate him, as in Yoreh Deah, siman 214. The enactment did not expressly say that the penalty would be imposed on the basis of self admission. Its ordinary meaning is that the penalty applies when the violation is established according to law, meaning by witnesses. Even if the enactment's wording is doubtful, money cannot be extracted from doubt. Thus the community cannot collect the fixed penalty entered in its register. Nevertheless, communal leaders may impose a separate discretionary fine for the insolence he displayed toward the communal officers, boasting about repeated violations and treating the regulations lightly, but they should proceed deliberately and proportionately in judgment.
