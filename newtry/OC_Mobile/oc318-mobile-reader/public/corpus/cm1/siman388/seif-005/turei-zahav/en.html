(He is exempt.) The Sema infers that initially he should not identify the property. I am astonished. Rambam immediately explains that if he does not identify it, the coercer will strike or kill him. It is obvious that he may identify it even initially in order to save himself from danger to life. If one argues that he must give his own money instead in order to save himself, what then did Rambam's reason accomplish? Since the coercer never demanded the informer's own money, he has no monetary obligation to sacrifice it. Moreover, in Perek Lo Yachpor 22, regarding causative damage where the defendant is exempt, Rav Tuvyah bar Matnah says, "This teaches that causative damage is forbidden." We see that without any coercion one might have thought such conduct permitted until Rav Matnah expressly taught that it is nevertheless forbidden initially. Here, where the informer acts under coercion, everyone certainly agrees that the act is permitted even initially. This appears to me correct.
