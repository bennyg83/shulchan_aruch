tzadi) Ibid., chapter 8 of Hilkhot Chovel uMazik, law 5, from the incident concerning the irrigation ditch and Rava's statement to Abaye.
kuf) The Rivash writes in responsum 80 regarding one who insulted a Torah scholar and the scholar handed the punishment over to the city official, that this approaches the prohibition of informing. The Sema cites this in the name of Darkei Moshe and explains that the reason is that the scholar's legal right had not yet been established under Jewish law.
reish) See there, where the incident involved one who used gentile authorities to seize property for himself. The case is compared to the dispute concerning Rabbi Abba's bar of metal, where the defendant says, 'Yes, I seized it, but I seized my own,' explained in siman 364, seif 4. He explains that if witnesses saw the seizure through gentile authorities, the defendant cannot simply assert that right, but if there are no witnesses he is believed by migo, citing Avi HaEzri. He further writes in chapter HaMeniach that if one person's deposit was denied by the custodian and the depositor recovered his own property through gentile authorities, but this caused the custodian to be falsely accused and suffer a substantial loss, then if the depositor could have recovered his property by another means he is liable, just as one who could have gently removed an animal but instead shoved it is liable, as explained in siman 383, seif 2.
shin) Mahari Weil, siman 111, writes likewise. He cites the Mordechai at the end of Bava Kamma and Teshuvot Maimoniyot regarding one who tried to compel another through gentile law to submit to Jewish law. When officials attempted to seize the defendant and he resisted, they beat him until he died. Maharam imposed upon the claimant all the laws applicable to a murderer, even though his intent was only to compel the defendant to submit to Jewish law. The Sema cites this in the name of Darkei Moshe.
