If the agent was established as one who obeys. The Shach disputes the rule that a known compliant agent can create agency for transgression. I also find the rationale difficult. The common explanation for "there is no agency for transgression" is that the principal should have assumed the agent would obey Hashem rather than him. But Rashi's treatment of interest through an agent shows that the rule cannot rest entirely on that psychology, since in some settings the principal is permitted to act through the agent. Ritva therefore writes that the central basis is a scriptural decree, not the factual assumption that the agent will refuse. Accordingly, even if this particular agent is known to obey the principal, the Torah rule of no agency for transgression remains.
