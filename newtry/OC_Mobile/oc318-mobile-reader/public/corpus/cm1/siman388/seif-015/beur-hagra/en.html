One who, etc. As above from the second chapter of Avodah Zarah.
Three times, etc. At that point he has an established pattern. See Sanhedrin 81b regarding repeated conduct punishable by kareis.
Through indirect causation, etc. Rabbeinu Chananel explains that "lowering" refers specifically to an indirect method, but the Rambam does not agree, as shown at the end of siman 425 and explicitly here. The Rashba likewise rules this way. See Shach seif katan 56.
One who speaks, etc. As in seif 12 concerning one who terrorizes the community, and also under the court's authority to strengthen communal discipline, as stated in siman 2.
One who sent, etc. See Rashi Bava Kamma 51a concerning the rule that there is no agency for transgression. Here the agent is publicly known not to obey Torah authority, so the ordinary assumption that an agent might refuse does not apply. Bava Metzia 10b and Tosafos Bava Kamma 79a discuss the distinction.
Likewise if he delivered, etc. This parallels the gloss to siman 28 seif 3. The Rashba disagrees, as cited by Beit Yosef in siman 386, following Rabbeinu Yitzchak that where liability does not arise immediately the act is only grama. I do not find this convincing, for pointing out another's property is the classic act of an informer. Even testimony does not create liability until judgment is rendered, and according to Riva the liability may in any event rest upon rabbinic penalty like other informer cases.
