One who is established as having informed on Jews or their property to non-Jewish authorities three times may be dealt with through advice and stratagem to remove the danger he poses. Rama: This may be done through indirect causation, although it is forbidden to kill him directly. If a person makes informing statements before a congregation and, as a result, they reach a government official and cause damage, then even if he does not have the formal status of an informer, beit din may punish him as the judges see fit. Rivash, siman 239, and Responsa Ramban, siman 240. If one sends an agent to inform, and the agent is known to carry out such acts, the sender is liable and cannot invoke the rule that there is no agency for a transgression, because this agent is known to act that way. Likewise, if one gives a paid debt document to a non-Jew knowing that he will deliver it to an official who will force the Jew to pay a second time, he is liable for the resulting loss. Beit Yosef in the name of Tashbetz and Maharam of Rothenburg.
