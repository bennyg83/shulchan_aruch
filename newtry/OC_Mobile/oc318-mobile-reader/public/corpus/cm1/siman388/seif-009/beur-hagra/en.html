It is forbidden, etc., even, etc. Gittin 7a, even regarding a wicked person, etc.
Whether, etc. As in the Gemara's comparison to a trapped animal, etc.
Specifically, etc., for, etc. As stated in seifim 10 and 11. Although property belonging to an informer may not ordinarily be destroyed, as stated in seif 13, that is where destroying it does not rescue the person being informed upon. To save oneself, it is certainly permitted because the informer has the status of a pursuer. See Bava Kamma 117b and Tosafos Sanhedrin 74a.
Or if, etc. The word "or" is a scribal error according to the Shach, and the Mordechai likewise reads the passage accordingly. Where one can stop the danger through a lesser means, killing is forbidden, as stated in seif 10 and in the derivation from "she shall cut off her hand" concerning a pursuer. See Rambam, chapter 1 of Hilchos Rotzeach.
But if, etc. As in chapter 3 regarding two people who injured one another, and certainly where there is no realistic fear of informing and therefore no need for rescue. See siman 421 seif 13.
And every informer, etc. The first chapter of Rosh Hashana.
