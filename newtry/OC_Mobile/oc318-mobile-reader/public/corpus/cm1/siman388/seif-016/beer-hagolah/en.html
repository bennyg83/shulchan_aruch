The laws of informing are discussed in Responsa attributed to Ramban, siman 240, and Responsa Rivash, siman 235. It is also explained there that beit din need not release an informer from imprisonment on substantial guarantees or penalties if the court sees substance in the claimant's allegation and, if established, the offender would be liable to death or another bodily punishment. See also the cited responsum siman 473 and Responsa Rosh, rule 17, that if people sought to seize someone unlawfully and he saved himself at the cost of the pursuer's life, he acted properly. Beit Yosef also cites at length a letter of Rashba to the sages of France regarding an informer whom Rabbi Yonah, a relative of Rabbenu Yonah the Pious, and Rashba ruled was liable to death; the king then ordered his execution. See there. Another responsum of Rabbi Meir of Rothenburg discusses two people who informed on Jewish money, one poor and one wealthy, and whether the wealthy must pay the entire loss. See Hagahot Maimoniyot, chapter 12 of Hilkhot Nizkei Mamon, and Mordechai in HaParah and HaChovel for many laws of informing, including the law of a married woman who informed.
