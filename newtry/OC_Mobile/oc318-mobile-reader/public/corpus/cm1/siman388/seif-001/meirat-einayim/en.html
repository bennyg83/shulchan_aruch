But if people do not ordinarily place such property in this type of container, etc. The Gemara leaves this as an unresolved question: Do people place money in such a vessel or not? The Rambam, Tur, and Mechaber explain that even where it is certain that people do not ordinarily place money there, the question is whether we follow ordinary human practice and exempt the tortfeasor. Thus the Gemara's phrase is rhetorical: Do people place money in such a vessel? Since they do not, and the basket was covered so that the tortfeasor could not see the money, perhaps he is exempt because it never occurred to him that money was inside. Or perhaps the injured party is believed by oath that he had placed money there, and the tortfeasor is liable because what right did he have to throw the vessel into the water at all? The Rambam rules that the burden of proof is upon the claimant, but if the claimant seized, we do not remove it from him, as the Mechaber writes. The Rosh holds that because this is a doubt in law, seizure does not help. That is the dissenting view Rama records nearby in the law of an informer.
