a) The language of the Rambam, chapter 7 of Hilkhot Chovel uMazik, law 17. The Maggid Mishneh writes that this is explicit at the end of chapter HaKones, from Shmuel's statement on Bava Kamma 62a.
b) Ibid., law 18, from the incident there of the man who kicked a chest.
gimel) Ibid.: he swears and collects.
dalet) Straightforward from the Gemara there, from the Mishnah where the Sages concede to Rabbi Yehudah regarding one who burns a building. The Maggid Mishneh writes that the Rambam understands the unresolved question in the preceding incident as concerning an item that is known not ordinarily to be placed there, while the injured party claims that he did place it there. Since the question remains unresolved, the Rambam rules according to his general approach that if the claimant seized payment, it is not removed from him.
heh) The Maggid Mishneh writes that the wording of the Gemara appears to support the other authorities, since it asks whether people ordinarily place such an item there, as Tosafot explain. Nevertheless, if witnesses testify that the claimed item was there, even if such an item is not ordinarily placed there, the damages are collected from the one who caused the loss and the injured party is paid without an oath. The Rashba, however, ultimately rules differently from Tosafot on this point.
vav) Ibid., law 19. The Maggid Mishneh writes that the reason for this law, and the disagreement between the Mechaber and the Raavad, is explained at length in chapter 5 of Hilkhot She'eilah uPikadon. See there, for the margin is too narrow to contain it.
