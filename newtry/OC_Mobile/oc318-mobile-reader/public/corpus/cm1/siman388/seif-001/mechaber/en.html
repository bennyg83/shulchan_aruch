The law that the injured party swears and collects. It contains sixteen seifim.
If a person damages another's property but does not know what he damaged, the injured party swears and collects. For example, if he took another person's purse and threw it into water or fire, or delivered it to a violent person and it was lost, and the owner says that it was filled with gold coins while the damager says, 'I do not know what was in it; perhaps it contained earth or straw,' the owner takes an oath while holding a sacred object and collects, provided he claims articles that a person of his means could reasonably possess or be entrusted with and that are ordinarily placed in such a purse. An unusual container may nevertheless be regarded as customary on Shabbos or a festival, according to the circumstances. If the claimed articles are not ordinarily placed in that type of container, the owner is considered to have been negligent and the damager is exempt. Thus, if one snatched a covered leather bottle or basket and threw it into the sea or burned it, and the owner claims that it contained pearls, he is not believed. Some say that even if witnesses testify that pearls were inside, the damager is exempt because the owner acted negligently, as explained elsewhere. If the owner seized property, it is not removed from him; others disagree. According to the first opinion, he swears that pearls were inside and collects from what he seized. The same principles apply in similar cases. If the damager knows that the purse contained gold coins but does not know how many, and the owner claims one thousand, the owner collects one thousand without an oath, provided that he is a person reasonably able to possess that amount, because the damager is obligated to swear but cannot do so. Some require the owner to swear before collecting, and this is the primary ruling.
