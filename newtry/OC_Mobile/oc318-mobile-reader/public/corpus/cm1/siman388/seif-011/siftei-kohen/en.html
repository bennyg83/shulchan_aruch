Once the informer carried out what he intended, etc. Mordechai cites Maharam bar Baruch, who reports that Rabbeinu Simchah proved that Rif disagrees with Rambam. Rif proves that one may not directly destroy an informer’s property from the Gemara’s unresolved question whether the enactment for one who was robbed applies to an informer. If Rif accepted Rambam’s view that after the informer accomplished his purpose one may not kill him, and therefore may not destroy his property, the Gemara’s case could simply concern an informer who had already accomplished his purpose. Rif’s proof would then fail. This shows that Rif does not accept Rambam’s distinction. Maharshal responds, but in my work I refuted his answer and proved from the Gemara that Rabbeinu Simchah is primary: even after one act a person is called an informer and may be killed. Tosafot and Piskei Tosafot also imply this, though many authorities followed Rambam.
Unless he has become established as an informer. Rambam writes that such a person may be killed lest he inform against others, and that communities in the West regularly killed confirmed informers or handed them to gentile authorities for punishment according to their wickedness. His language implies direct killing. Semag, Piskei Tosafot, Maharam, and Rashba likewise rule that a confirmed informer may be killed directly. The baraita’s phrase ‘they are lowered and not raised’ does not mean that direct killing is forbidden. It includes direct killing, and describes lowering into a pit when direct killing is not possible. This is also the implication of Rambam in Hilchot Rotzeach and of the Gemara’s statement that the informer’s property should not be more protected than his body. Maharshal attempts to read this as permission only for indirect killing, but the language and a fortiori inference do not support him. Rif’s omission of the incident in which Rav Kahana killed an informer does not imply that Rav Kahana acted improperly. Rif relied on the general ruling that the informer’s property is not more protected than his body. Therefore direct killing of a confirmed informer is the primary conclusion.
Established as an informer, etc. Rashba writes that ‘established’ means publicly known as such, not necessarily established before a beit din of twenty-three with testimony received in his presence, for otherwise no one could be established today when we lack ordained judges. One who shows misplaced mercy to such people becomes cruel to his generation. Maharam, however, writes that established means three occurrences, under either Rabbi or Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, with no sign of repentance. Thereafter whoever kills him first has acted meritoriously, for legal presumptions are powerful enough even for capital consequences. The Rosh and the Mechaber below likewise refer to three acts. See Yevamot 64b and Sanhedrin 81b concerning one who repeats an offense a third time.
For he will be killed, etc. Rivash, siman 296, writes that where one is arrested for an offense punishable by death, guarantors are not accepted for him, because there would be no recourse against them if he fled.
