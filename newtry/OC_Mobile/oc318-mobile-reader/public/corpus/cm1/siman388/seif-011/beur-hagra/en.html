After he acted, etc. His potential killing before the act is justified only to save the intended victim, like a pursuer, not as punishment for murder. Mere indirect causation of death does not incur capital punishment, as Sanhedrin 76b shows concerning one who incites an animal, and even false witnesses are not executed once their plan has already resulted in execution. This is all the more true nowadays. See Rivash siman 238.
(Collected note) After the informer acted, etc. Mordechai derives from the Rif that his body is no longer subject to the same rule after the act, pointing out that the Rif proves an informer's property may not be destroyed from the Gemara's question about the robbed claimant's enactment. If the informer himself remained liable to be killed after the act, why would his property require special protection? The proof is not decisive, since that case may concern one who repented. (End of note.)
Afterward he became established, etc. As stated in the second chapter of Avodah Zarah concerning sectarians and informers. See the end of siman 425.
