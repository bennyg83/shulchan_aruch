The husband consumes the produce throughout her lifetime. It requires study why this differs from a wife who physically injures another person. The Tur and Rama later, at the end of siman 424, and the Mechaber regarding injury to her husband, write that she must sell the favorable value of her future property rights and use the proceeds to pay the damages. Perhaps in the case of informing, where she did not directly damage by hand, the Sages were lenient because liability there is itself penal. For the same reason, if the informer dies before judgment, the heirs are not liable, as stated shortly below. But in an obligation that is not penal, such as a loan, borrowing, theft, or direct injury, the language of siman 424 implies that if she has melog property, iron flock property, or additional ketubah rights beyond the basic Torah ketubah, she must sell their actuarial value and pay. I wrote this at the end of siman 346 and discussed it at length in the Derishah there.
