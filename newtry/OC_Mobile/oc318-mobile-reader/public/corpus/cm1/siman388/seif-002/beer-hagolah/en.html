zayin) The language of the Rambam at the beginning of chapter 8 of Hilkhot Chovel uMazik, explained in the first chapter of Bava Kamma 5a and in the tenth chapter 117a.
chet) The Maggid Mishneh writes that this refers to land. Regarding movable property, any item may be considered best quality, as explained above in siman 348 and below in siman 419. If an informer did not pay from best-quality land, the Gemara could not have asked why informers were not counted among those who pay from best-quality land, all the more so than one who renders terumah tamei or mixes forbidden produce, whose damage is not physically recognizable yet who still pays from best-quality land.
tet) Explained in the Gemara there in the tenth chapter, in Rabba's statement. The meaning is that he spoke to the coercive official and showed him where another's property was located.
yud) The Maggid Mishneh explains that this is not the type of unrecognizable damage for which the Sages exempted heirs, as written above in siman 385, seif 1. The Gemara in the latter chapter HaGozel indicates that this is actual law regarding a tax collector and not a penalty. Rabbeinu Hai, the Ramban, and the Rashba likewise write so.
kaf) Ibid., law 2, from the incident involving Rav Huna bar Yehudah, Rava's statement to him, and the baraita in Bava Kamma 117a.
lamed) The alternate interpretation of the Gemara regarding the Mishnah there on 117b.
mem) Ibid., laws 3 and 4. The Maggid Mishneh writes that all these clauses up to the discussion of litigants are explained in the Rif in the latter chapter HaGozel. The commentators disagree on some of them, but the Rif's proofs are the strongest, and the Rashba likewise concludes so. See also siman 108, seif 2.
nun) This is implied there from the incident of the man with whom a silver cup had been deposited and robbers came upon him, Bava Kamma 117b.
