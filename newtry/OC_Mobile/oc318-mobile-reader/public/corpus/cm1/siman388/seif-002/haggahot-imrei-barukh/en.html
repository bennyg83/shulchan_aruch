(Mechaber, seif 7) If he did not seize, we do not extract from the informer except, etc. Note: See above, end of siman 35. Yam Shel Shlomo, Bava Kamma, chapter 1, siman 41, writes that clear testimony is not required against an informer and that a strong circumstantial assessment is sufficient, following Rabbeinu Tam. He later cites Responsa Rabbi Menachem Mintz that witnesses may be believed concerning an informer even on the basis of circumstantial evidence, although they did not hear the actual informing, if they saw him speaking with the officer and the officer immediately thereafter ordered action against the victim, or where there are comparable indications. See there, including the beginning of the siman. See also Responsa Maharil, siman 90, and Shach below, seif katan 39.
