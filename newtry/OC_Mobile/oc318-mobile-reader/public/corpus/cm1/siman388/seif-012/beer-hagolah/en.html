In my humble opinion Rambam's words here are difficult, for he himself writes there that if a gentile deposited or lent money to a Jew and the gentile died, and the Jew withholds the deposit or repayment and does not return it to the heirs when they demand it, he bears sin, as written at the beginning of siman 329. If so, this has nothing to do with a gentile's lost property, which the Torah permits one to keep. Even with a lost object, where returning it creates a sanctification of the Divine Name, returning it is praiseworthy, as explained in siman 266. The established enactment and communal practice is that community leaders carefully prevent falsehood and wrongdoing toward the nations, and publicly authorize disclosure of those who buy on credit or borrow with no intention of paying, all with the consent of the leaders.
