We accept testimony against him in his absence. This is the language of a responsum of the Rosh cited by the Tur at the end of this siman. Even outside the actual moment of informing, when beit din considers whether to cause his death through gentile authorities, testimony need not be accepted in his presence. It is well known that gentile officials draw a habitual informer close to themselves because of the benefit they obtain from him. If beit din had to receive the testimony in his presence and investigate the case with him there, judgment could never be carried out, because he would use gentile protection to escape. This is true when he is not yet personally in danger, all the more so once he sees his own life threatened.
