But if all the tenant's own fields and this rented field were blighted, we attribute the loss to the tenant's bad fortune, even if most of the townspeople's fields were also blighted. The tenant cannot say that the rented field too was struck by a regional calamity, because if the loss were not attributable to his own fortune, some portion of his own fields should have remained. Even if he were excused from paying rent, he would otherwise retain nothing at all from that year's produce. If his argument were correct, Heaven would have left him at least some produce from his own fields to hold. Sema.
