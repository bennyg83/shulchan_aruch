(Seif 2: Specifically in sharecropping) Although I am unworthy to decide between the authorities, I cannot remain silent. The Rama and the Sema have misunderstood the Nimukei Yosef. The Mishnah and Gemara underlying this siman concern a fixed-rent tenant, not a sharecropper. Reish Lakish states that when the field was sown and failed to sprout, the tenant may deduct only if the loss is regional; if he did not sow at all, the landlord may require him to continue sowing throughout the normal season. The Rambam and Tur explain that the duration follows local agricultural practice. How, then, can the Rama say this applies specifically to sharecropping? Moreover, a sharecropper who sowed once and obtained no crop cannot be compelled to sow repeatedly. His undertaking was to plow and sow, and he has fulfilled it; the common misfortune of both parties does not bind him to incur fresh expenses again and again. The Mishnah requiring continued tending where enough crop remains to make a stack concerns a field that has produced something and where the sharecropper's abandonment would directly deprive the owner. If nothing grew, he has completed his obligation. The same is true of any investor who receives funds to conduct a particular venture, conducts it once, and loses; the investor cannot compel repeated ventures. Nor may the owner ordinarily compel a sharecropper to cultivate merely so that the owner's share will consist specifically of produce from that field. The rule “I want wheat from my land” applies once produce already exists and the worker seeks to substitute market grain. If from the outset he elects not to work and is liable to pay the best expected yield under his undertaking, the deed itself shows that monetary compensation is the remedy, not specific performance. The Nimukei Yosef says that a fixed-rent tenant cannot be compelled to continue working solely so the landlord will receive this field's wheat, and proves it from the Mishnah distinguishing a sharecropper, whose abandonment causes the owner actual loss of his share, from a fixed-rent tenant, who must pay rent regardless. He did not mean that a sharecropper may be compelled solely to produce this field's grain. Therefore, if the field was sown and failed, a sharecropper is not required to sow again; the common misfortune affects both. Where the issue is deduction because of a regional calamity, there is no distinction between fixed rent and sharecropping so long as sowing remains possible during the season. The owner may demand his contractual share or rent, and payment may be made even from market produce. The Rama, Levush, and Sema did not analyze the Nimukei Yosef accurately.
