And he brought it there without, etc. As stated in Bava Kamma 48a: “If with permission, it is, etc.” See Rashi there.
The seller said to him, etc. From Bava Batra there: “Furthermore, does meshichah acquire in the public domain? What does ‘until, etc.’ mean?” Although the Gemara establishes the case where he said, “Go pull and acquire,” the Ran writes that this is unlike the case there, 86a, “where he said that he should acquire,” because there, as the Rashbam explains, etc. This is also why Kiddushin there asks, regarding an elephant according to Rabbi Shimon, “By what can it be acquired?” and does not answer that the seller explicitly stipulated. The Ran writes that this differs from the rule, “If he expressly stipulated, his stipulation is effective,” because money legally acquires there, but the parties do not rely on it until the document is written. If he stipulates, their minds are settled. This conflicts with the Rama’s gloss in seif 5, “and likewise if, etc.” See Shach there.
