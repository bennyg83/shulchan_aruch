The seller will delay. With kinyan sudar and situmta, where the seller has not received the purchase money, he will certainly exert himself to rescue the merchandise from a fire so that he will not have to become involved in litigation. One might ask: now that the Sages ruled that money does not acquire, why should the seller not say, "Your money burned in the attic"? The answer is that money can be rescued without significant effort, and the seller will certainly exert himself to save it. Sema. See the Beurim. This answer does not apply to an equal exchange transaction. With kinyan sudar and situmta, even if money was given the acquisition is complete because the Sages did not distinguish. By contrast, with money alone, even if only partial payment was given, the buyer does not acquire and is subject only to the curse of "He Who exacted punishment," although the concern that "your wheat burned in the attic" may not apply in the same way:

And likewise the renter. The Sema asks why the word "likewise" is used, since the two cases rest on different reasons. In the first, Torah law is restored because money ordinarily acquires and the purchaser is near his house. In the second, the renter acquires because the property is in his courtyard. The Shach and Taz answer that "likewise" refers only to the practical result, that both acquire without meshichah, though the reasons differ:

Above in siman 190, where money can acquire in a place where a document is ordinarily written, a stipulation helps because money is fundamentally an acquisition under Torah law and only lack of confidence without a document prevents completion. Where the parties expressly rely on the money, it works. Here, however, the Sages enacted that money does not acquire and did not distinguish. Furthermore, this is a stipulation against a rabbinic enactment, and the Sages strengthened their enactments. The rule that one may stipulate concerning monetary rights applies where waiver is conceptually possible. One cannot stipulate that an act that is not a kinyan shall become a kinyan, just as one cannot stipulate that a person shall inherit where Torah law does not make him an heir. The Bach answers that here the Sages did not uproot the monetary acquisition except for the purchaser's benefit, so he can say, "I do not want this rabbinic enactment made for my benefit." According to the view that the stipulation is effective, even if the seller states it and the purchaser remains silent at the beginning of the sale, it works. See also the Mechaber in siman 207. Sema:
