By Torah law, money acquires. Tosafot writes that even according to the view that money is the Torah mode for a sale, meshichah must certainly acquire where there is no money, such as a found object or gift. Otherwise there would be no Torah mode of acquiring those movables. Shitah Mekubetzet cites Ramban as saying that a gift may instead be acquired by barter, courtyard, or similar means. I find this difficult. Courtyard acquisition itself derives either from one's hand or from agency. If one's actual hand would not acquire movables under the relevant theory, why should a courtyard be stronger? Likewise, where the hand model does not apply, ordinary agency through the courtyard should not produce a mode unavailable to the principal himself. This requires study.
