By Torah law. Rashi explains this from the verse concerning consecrated property, "he shall give the money and it shall be established for him." Ran writes that ordinary transactions cannot be derived from consecrated property. Rather, the rule follows reason: most acquisitions are normally made through money. Sema.
But the Sages instituted otherwise. See Beurim: where there is no monetary deficiency, for example where movables are transferred in satisfaction of an existing debt, meshichah effects a Torah acquisition. If the movables are held by a third party and the seller tells the custodian, "Give them to so and so," while the buyer pays, the command "give" is treated like "acquire for him." Since the seller is barred from retracting because of the "Who exacted payment" curse, the transaction resembles an existing debt where "give" is treated as "acquire." The seller cannot retract, as explained in siman 295:23. If the buyer was present, the buyer likewise cannot retract. This follows Rav Hai Gaon and Machaneh Ephraim.
