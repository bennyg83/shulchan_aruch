But the Sages instituted. See Baer Heitev, who writes that nevertheless, if the seller betrothed a woman with the money, and so forth. See what I wrote concerning this in Pitchei Teshuva to Even HaEzer, siman 28, seif katan 1, and see the novellae of Rabbi Akiva Eiger to Bava Metzia 48a, s.v. "it stands under 'but'," concerning this matter. See also Degul Merevavah, who writes that according to the Sema, who maintains that the Sages did not uproot Torah law where doing so would produce a stringency, it would seem that if one acquired an item by meshichah alone and then used it to betroth a woman, and another man subsequently betrothed her, she should require a get from both men. For the Sages would not have uprooted, by their enactment, the Torah rule that is stringent here, namely that meshichah does not effect acquisition by Torah law. See Even HaEzer, siman 28, and Beit Shmuel, seif katan 3 and seif katan 6. He concludes, however, that in a responsum he explained this matter at length and demonstrated that this is not so. If one acquired the item by meshichah, he may use it to betroth a woman without concern. See also what I wrote below, siman 235, seif 7, seif katan 4.
Or by meshichah, an item whose normal manner of acquisition is not lifting. Shaar Mishpat writes that the unqualified language of the poskim implies that even if the purchaser has already paid the entire price, meshichah does not acquire an item whose normal manner of acquisition is lifting. This appears even more clearly from Rambam, Hilchot Mechirah, chapter 3, halachah 4. See there.
