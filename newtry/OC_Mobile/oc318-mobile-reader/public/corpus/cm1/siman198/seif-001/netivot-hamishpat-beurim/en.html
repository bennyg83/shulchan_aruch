See Sema, seif katan 3, who writes that if the seller used the money to betroth a woman, she is betrothed because, at least stringently, the Sages did not uproot the seller's money acquisition. This reason is needed only according to the view that the seller may not use the money. According to the view that the seller may use it because the Sages did not actually uproot his money acquisition, as Tosafot in Bava Metzia 43 explain, the kiddushin are valid without any additional stringency. After the seller has formally accepted mi shepara and the transaction is rescinded, however, he certainly can no longer use that money for kiddushin.
