(Sema, seif katan 9) Since they are still in his possession. Note: This rationale does not apply where the produce is in another person's domain. The Ramban in his novellae to Gittin 30b, passage beginning "a kor of tithe," makes clear that money does not acquire even when the produce is elsewhere, and even when it is in the buyer's domain. Since he comments on Rashi's words that the Levite acquires by receipt of the money, he appears to follow the view that by Torah law money acquires and only the rabbinic decree concerning the danger that the seller might say, "your wheat burned in the attic," prevents acquisition. If so, it requires study why money should not acquire where the produce is in the buyer's courtyard that is not guarded. Since he need not rely upon acquisition through the courtyard, money alone should acquire where the concern that the seller will say "your wheat burned" does not apply. See Tosafot Ri in Gilyon HaShas to Shevuot 48a. Two rules emerge from his words. First, money does not acquire even where the buyer has already drawn the movables into a public domain and remains next to them there. Second, as long as the seller has not returned the money, he has no power to retract. He may retract only after returning what belongs to the buyer and accepting the curse of "He who punished." It follows that where the parties dispute whether the buyer already paid, this is a dispute over the very acquisition. According to the buyer, who says he paid, the movables are acquired because the seller is not returning the money and indeed denies receiving it. In such a case the original money acquisition remains effective, because the Sages abolished acquisition by money only where the seller returns the money.
