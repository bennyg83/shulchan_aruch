Because rental of movable property, and so forth. See Chiddushei Rabbi Akiva Eiger at the end of Bava Metzia, who expresses great difficulty with this ruling and leaves it requiring very great study. At the end he writes that Nimukei Yosef in the chapter HaShoel, in the discussion of the enactment of meshichah for custodians, cites Rashba as holding that with rental of movable property as well, money does not acquire, and concludes, "and this is the view of the later authorities." See there. Beit Yosef cites this at the end of this siman. See also Marot HaTzovot at the end of his collected notes, who likewise questions the words of Nimukei Yosef in HaShoel and adds that this position also appears explicit from Rashi and Nimukei Yosef in the chapter Merubah, Bava Kamma 79a. If so, at the very least this should be treated as an unresolved doubt of law. He writes that he found no later authority who addressed the point and that in his humble opinion the difficulty remains. He presented the matter before great rabbis, who agreed with him. See also Shitah Mekubetzet to Bava Metzia 99b, s.v. Sechirut, who writes that from Rashi there it appears that rental is not acquired by money. He expresses surprise according to Rabbi Yochanan, who says that by Torah law money acquires, what reason would there be for rental to differ? He suggests that once the Sages enacted meshichah for sales, they did not distinguish rental, because rental is also a sale for a day. He then rejects this answer. See there, and see Responsa Beit Ephraim, Choshen Mishpat, siman 21. Shaar Mishpat likewise discusses this at length and questions the ruling of Shulchan Aruch here. He concludes that it is surprising that the Mechaber rules in accordance with Nimukei Yosef in the chapter HaZahav, even to extract property from the lessor, where Rashi, Tosafos, Rashba, the later authorities cited by Nimukei Yosef in HaShoel, and Baal HaItur in the entry Sechirut hold the opposite, namely that rental of movable property is not acquired by money and requires meshichah. To defend the Mechaber, he suggests, based on Maggid Mishneh in chapter 4 of Hilchot Gezelah, that since by Torah law money acquires, and it was the Sages who instituted that meshichah should be required, where there is a dispute among the authorities whether that rabbinic enactment was extended to rental of movable property, we leave the matter on its Torah law, that money acquires. A doubtful enactment cannot displace a certain Torah rule. See there further. Responsa Neot Desheh, siman 31, however, writes as obvious that this principle is not applied to enactments of the Sages of the Talmud, because their enactments are treated as law itself, and a doubt in their enactment is like any other doubt in law, so that the burden of proof rests upon the claimant.
