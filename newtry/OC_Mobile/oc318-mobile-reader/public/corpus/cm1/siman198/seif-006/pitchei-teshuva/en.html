See the novellae of Rabbi Akiva Eiger at the end of Bava Metzia, where he expresses great difficulty with this ruling and leaves the matter requiring extensive study. At the end he writes that the Nimukei Yosef, in Perek HaShoel, in the discussion of the rule that just as the Sages instituted meshichah regarding custodians, cites the Rashba that with respect to rental of movable property as well, money does not effect acquisition, and concludes that this is also the opinion of the later authorities. See there. These words are cited in the Beit Yosef at the end of the relevant siman, apparently siman 307. Marot HaTzovot, at the end of its collected notes, likewise expresses surprise at the statement of the Nimukei Yosef in Perek HaShoel. He adds that this position is also explicit in Rashi and the Nimukei Yosef in Perek Merubah, Bava Kamma 79b. If so, at the very least the matter should be treated as an unresolved halachic doubt. He writes that he had not seen any of the later authorities raise this point, that in his view the difficulty is substantial, and that he presented it before distinguished rabbis who agreed with him. See also Shitah Mekubetzet to Bava Metzia 99b, s.v. "rental," who writes that Rashi there appears to hold that rental is not acquired through money. He expresses surprise according to Rabbi Yochanan, who maintains that by Torah law money effects acquisition, asking what basis there is for distinguishing rental. One might answer that once the Sages required meshichah for a sale, they did not distinguish rental, since rental too is a sale for a day. The author of that passage, however, writes that this answer is not satisfactory. See there, and see Responsa Beit Ephraim, Choshen Mishpat, siman 21. Shaar Mishpat also discusses this at length and questions the ruling of the Shulchan Aruch here. He writes that it is difficult that the Mechaber ruled unqualifiedly in accordance with the Nimukei Yosef in Perek HaZahav, even to extract money from the lessor, when Rashi, Tosafot, the Rashba, and the consensus of the later authorities cited by the Nimukei Yosef in Perek HaShoel hold the opposite. Baal HaIttur, under the entry for rental, likewise holds that rental of movable property is not acquired through money and specifically requires meshichah. Shaar Mishpat proposes to justify the ruling of the Mechaber based on the Maggid Mishneh in chapter 4 of Hilchot Gezeilah. By Torah law money effects acquisition, while the Sages subsequently required meshichah. Here there is a dispute among the great authorities whether that rabbinic enactment was also extended to rental of movable property. We therefore leave the matter on its Torah footing, under which money effects acquisition, and one may extract the property or money from the lessor. A doubtful rabbinic enactment cannot displace a definite Torah rule. See there at length. See, however, Responsa Neot Deshe, siman 31, part of which I cited above in siman 18, seif 1, seif katan 8. He writes as a simple principle that with respect to enactments of the Sages of the Talmud we do not apply such reasoning. The enactments of the Sages of the Talmud are themselves treated as Torah law for purposes of adjudication, and any doubt concerning the scope of their enactment is therefore treated like an ordinary doubt in law, so that the rule is that the burden of proof rests upon the claimant. He writes that Responsa Perach Mateh Aharon, part 1, siman 11, states the same. Neot Deshe, however, does not mention the words of the Maggid Mishneh cited above. The matter therefore requires study.
