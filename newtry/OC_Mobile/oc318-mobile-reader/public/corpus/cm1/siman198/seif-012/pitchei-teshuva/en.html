He has already disclosed his intent. See Responsa Mishkenot Yaakov, end of siman 51, who wonders about the Rama. Here the Rama rules without qualification, whereas in siman 268, seif 1, concerning acquisition of a found object, he cites the dissenting opinion that even when one disclosed that he preferred to acquire it by falling upon it rather than by his four cubits, he nevertheless acquires through the four cubits. Mishkenot Yaakov writes that this is unlike the Rashba and leaves the matter requiring great study. It is surprising that he did not mention the Mishneh LaMelech, Laws of Acquisition and Gift 2:9, who addresses this well. There is a distinction: there, when the object was within his four cubits, he intended at that very time to acquire it. Although he did not intend to acquire through the legal mechanism of four cubits, he did intend to acquire at that moment. Here, however, when the object was delivered into his hand, he did not intend to acquire it then, but only later by meshichah, and that act had not yet occurred, since the object still had to be moved from the public domain into a side alley. See there, including his reference to the Maggid Mishneh, Laws of Sale 3:3, in the name of the Rashba. See also what I write below, siman 268, seif 1, seif katan 1.
