For there is no concern that a fire will occur. In siman 198 there is a dispute whether, in a case where the reason of “your wheat may burn in the loft” does not apply, payment itself acquires. Consider the sale of a milking cow where the milk belongs to the seller until the cow is taken to the buyer’s home. The seller then seemingly has the status of a borrower of the cow until delivery, because all the benefit is his, like one who borrows a milking cow. Although he must feed it, the Sages say that where an article requires guarding, most of the benefit is still his, and he is a borrower, as in the beginning of the chapter HaShoel. The same would appear true of a plowing cow that the seller continues to plow with all day until delivery. In such cases the concern that the seller will say, “Your wheat burned in the loft,” does not apply, because he is liable even for unavoidable accidents. There is therefore room to say that according to the view mentioned there, payment acquires in such a case. On the other hand, a borrower may still have the defense that the animal died because of the work for which it was borrowed. More generally, everyone agrees in many areas that the Sages did not distinguish between cases. This requires further study.
