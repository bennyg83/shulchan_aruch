Seif 9, in the gloss: Without the courtyard owner's permission. The Maggid Mishneh explains that if the goods were placed there with permission, they are like a deposit in the courtyard owner's hands, and a buyer does not acquire goods in the possession of the one with whom they are deposited. This rule is cited in siman 200. His meaning is that when the courtyard owner accepted the goods from the first owner, he presumably also accepts custody for the second. There is therefore a remedy without physical delivery: the courtyard owner may verbally lend the buyer the place. No special transfer of the ship is then required. If the goods were placed there without permission, the area is like the public domain and only delivery can help. It also appears that where the goods were placed with permission, meshichah does not help, because siman 200 rules that meshichah does not acquire in the premises of the depositary. I do not understand the Sema's statement that granting permission makes the premises the buyer's or jointly owned, where meshichah would work. In this case it does not.
