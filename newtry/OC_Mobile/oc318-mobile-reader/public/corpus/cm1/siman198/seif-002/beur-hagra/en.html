Some say, etc.; and some say. See Tosafot, Bava Kamma 29b; Eruvin 79b; Kiddushin 26a, s.v. alternatively; and Ran, who writes that lifting one handbreadth suffices. He proves this from the passages in HaManiach and Chalon, and from the Yerushalmi in the third chapter of Demai, which infers from Rabbi Elazar's statement that movable property is acquired by overturning it, although one handbreadth is required for an eruv. See Tosafot, Ketubot 31a, s.v. let us say.
(Supplement) Lifting, some say, etc. See Rashi, Ketubot 31a, and Tosafot there, who reject his view and note that when the object is in one's hand three handbreadths are certainly unnecessary. See Tosafot, Bava Kamma 29b, and Ketubot there. (End of supplement.)
(Supplement) Some say that it suffices, etc. This also appears from the Mishna, Bava Batra 84b: until he moves it, etc. The word 'moves' indicates that mere movement suffices. (End of supplement.)
