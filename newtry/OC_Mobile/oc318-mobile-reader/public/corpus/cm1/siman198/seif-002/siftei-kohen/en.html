Some say that he must lift it, etc. Tosafot in the first chapter of Bava Metzia, 9b, and the Hagahot Mordechai there imply that an act is considered lifting when the object rises because of him in an upward direction, even though he never grasps it in his hand. This is also the Rosh's view there and is written by Rabbeinu Yerucham, Netiv 1, part 1. Tosafot in Perek HaGozel Etzim, Bava Kamma 98a, however, imply that the matter is doubtful, and the Hagahot Ashri there state likewise: Rabbeinu Yitzchak was uncertain whether only pushing it downward is ineffective, while if it rose somewhat he acquired because it is as though he took it in his hand, or whether there is no distinction. Regarding living creatures, however, Tosafot in HaGozel also write that it is considered lifting when birds rise because he beats upon the nest. If they descend as a result of his beating upon the nest, this is not lifting; only their upward movement qualifies. See there. The Maadaney Yom Tov in Bava Kamma writes that according to the Rosh it is obvious that one does not acquire merely because the object rose, and that Tosafot in Bava Kamma and Bava Metzia conclude likewise. Anyone who examines Tosafot and the Rosh will see that this is not so, but rather as I have written. See Yam Shel Shlomo at the end of Chullin; his words require study.
