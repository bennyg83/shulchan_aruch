Bet) This is the language of the Rambam there, chapter 7, halachah 3. The Maggid Mishneh writes that these laws were not stated explicitly in the Gemara, as he discusses there in the Rif on HaZahav. Nevertheless, he judges that the words of the Geonim agree with the Rambam. They ruled that when the seller retracts, accepts upon himself the curse of Mi Shepara, and tells the buyer, "Come and take your money," the seller is no longer responsible for the money. If so, when the buyer retracts and the seller tells him, "Come and take your money," this is certainly so, because acceptance of Mi Shepara is not automatic if the seller does not wish to seek such a ruling from beit din. Here the seller is not delaying return of the money for the purpose of having Mi Shepara imposed. See there at length.
Gimel) So writes the Maggid Mishneh according to the Rif, because the money is then regarded in his possession as a complete loan. This is also the view of the Ramban. The Tur likewise writes in siman 17 that the Rif stated this in the name of Rav Hai Gaon.
Dalet) The Tur, seif 18, cites those who distinguish between the cases. The Maggid Mishneh likewise records this as the first view in the name of some commentators.
Heh) There in the name of the Ramban. The Maggid Mishneh likewise cites this in the name of an authority and states that it is the view of the Ramban.
