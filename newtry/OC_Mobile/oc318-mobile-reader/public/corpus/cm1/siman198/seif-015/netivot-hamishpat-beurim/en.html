The reason for the dispute is explained by Beit Yosef: the authorities disagree whether delivery against the recipient's will is legally considered delivery, and whether saying "take what is yours" constitutes delivery. See Sema in siman 120. It appears clear that if the borrower says, "Take what is yours," and the lender responds, "Let it remain with you as a deposit," the borrower is exempt. Since "take what is yours" constitutes delivery, and the lender then willingly agrees that the item remain, there has been consensual delivery, which all agree is valid. This is not comparable to siman 120, where a lender says, "let it remain with you as a deposit," and the debtor is not discharged. There the debtor had not first said "take it," or the circumstances were such that he could not yet tender it, such as before the due date, so there was never a delivery initiated by the debtor. Where the debtor validly says "take it," thereby effecting delivery, and the creditor agrees that it remain, everyone agrees that consensual delivery is valid. The dispute concerns only forced delivery.
