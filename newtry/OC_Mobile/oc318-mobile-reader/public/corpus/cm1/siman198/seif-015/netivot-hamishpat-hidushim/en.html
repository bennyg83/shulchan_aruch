Even for unavoidable accidents. See siman 120 and the Beurim regarding when liability for unavoidable accidents and negligence ends. See what I wrote there. If the seller says, "Take what is yours," and the purchaser answers, "Keep it with you as a deposit," then according to everyone the seller is fully discharged from the sale obligation and thereafter has only the law of an unpaid custodian. The Beit Yosef writes at the end of this siman that after a complete acquisition, when neither party can retract, the seller is not even an unpaid custodian of the produce unless he expressly accepts custodial responsibility. It is as though he said, "The house is before you." It nevertheless appears to me that if the purchaser still owes money, then in an average sale where the seller also benefits, as explained in Nedarim 31, the seller is a paid custodian by virtue of the benefit that he retains the goods as security for the unpaid money.
