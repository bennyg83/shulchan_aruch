A boundary path that the public has taken possession of. Rashbam explains that public acquisition of a path involves actual preparation or improvement of the route for travel. Even where the public has thereby acquired a right of passage through an entrance courtyard or passageway, there is no chazakah for unrestricted passage at night if doors there are normally locked at night. Even if the owner often opened for the public at night, that does not establish a right to night access, because the physical improvement of the path proves only the ordinary right of passage, not every possible time of entry. Since the owner retains a door under his control, his occasional choice to leave it open cannot create a chazakah beyond the times when people ordinarily pass. The same applies at the beginning of the night or shortly before dawn where householders commonly close their premises. The improvement of the path establishes only the customary daytime use.
