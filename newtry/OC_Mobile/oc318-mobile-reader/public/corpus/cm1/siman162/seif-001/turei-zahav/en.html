The ruling originates in a responsum of the Rashba, cited by Beit Yosef in siman 161. The Rashba writes that residents of an alley cannot prevent one another from opening a new entrance from a courtyard into the alley in the manner that courtyard residents can object because of increased traffic, for increased traffic is not a relevant claim in an alley. Nevertheless, if one whose entrance is nearer the outer end seeks to open another entrance beyond his existing entrance toward the inner end, the inner residents may prevent him. This is not because of increased traffic, but because every outer resident can be told, “You have no right to use the alley beyond your entrance.” The inner residents may close off the alley opposite their entrances, as though that inner section were entirely theirs. If the outer resident opens an entrance beyond his present entrance, he thereby takes property from the inner residents. This is the law of five courtyards opening into one alley. These are the Rashba's words. It is thus clear that he is not discussing the Mechaber's case of increased passage around a platform, where the inner residents must make a detour around it. The Rashba has no involvement with that issue. Who says that there is a dispute on this point? According to the Rashba's words, the principal objection among five courtyards opening into an alley concerns opening an additional entrance beyond one's existing entrance because it constitutes taking from the inner residents. Wherever no such taking occurs, one may open the entrance. Nevertheless, who tells us that the detour around a platform is irrelevant? The Sema's words in seif katan 18 are not intelligible to me, for regarding a platform, who mentioned the section inward from the entrance at all?
