And a beam. Meaning, or a beam. They are used to render the alleyway fit for carrying on Shabbat, and either one suffices, as stated in Orach Chaim siman 372. From this we learn that in a through alleyway, which requires a tzurat hapetach on one side, the residents must contribute toward the tzurat hapetach. Thus far the Sema. See Responsa Masat Binyamin, siman 4.
The entrances. Meaning, the lintel over the door prevents me from entering with my bundle on my shoulder, and the door itself prevents me from entering until I must unload the bundle from my shoulder or hand in order to open the door. This causes me delay and trouble. Sema.
May prevent. There is nevertheless a distinction. The residents of the public domain cannot prevent it in every circumstance, as the Rama writes in his gloss, whereas the residents of the alleyway can prevent it in every circumstance. The reason, "I wish to enter," applies even to small alleyways opening into an alleyway that opens into the public domain, and even if it has doorposts. This is unlike the reason that at times the public becomes crowded there. See the Tur. Ibid.
And they announced. The responsum there states that even if they explicitly announced that anyone with a claim concerning locking the entrance should present it, his right is not lost. It concludes with the reason: "His entrance to the public domain itself proclaims his right, and he protests that they are not permitted to do so." Ibid.
Because at times. Meaning, on market day, when there are many people and animals in the public domain from other towns and there is no room to stand there, they press into the alleyways. The Tur concludes that this is not limited to an opening directly into a public domain, but applies likewise to a karmelit or a simta. See there. This is the meaning of the Rama's gloss, "however, small alleyways that open into those..." or into a simta, implying that if it opens directly into the simta itself it is not permitted. Ibid.
Doorposts. The Tur writes in the name of the Ramban: This applies specifically to an alleyway that opens across its full width into the public domain, with no doorway in its fourth side, but is entirely open through. If, however, it has a doorway in the fourth wall that is narrower than the width of the alleyway, with doorposts and a lintel that prevent the public from freely passing through it, doors may be installed. Ibid.
To remove. The responsum there explains the reason: They may say, "We have already acquired a right to use the entire alleyway whenever we wish." It is therefore like a path in which the public has established use, which may not be damaged, as stated in siman 377. Ibid.
