Matters concerning which the residents of an alleyway may compel one another. This siman contains seven seifim.
The residents of an alleyway may compel one another to install a sidepost and crossbeam for the alleyway. If, however, they wish to install doors, even one resident may object and say, 'I wish to carry my bundle all the way to my doorway.' Even if all the residents agree to install doors, the public who use the public domain may prevent them. Rama: Even if the residents sold the alleyway for the purpose of installing doors and publicly announced that anyone who failed to object would forfeit his right, and the public did not object, the public may nevertheless later prevent them. This applies even if they wish to place the doors well inside the alleyway, because at times the public domain becomes congested and people press into the alleyway. Rama: Small alleyways that open into larger alleyways which themselves open into the public domain or into a side street may have doors installed by their residents. Some say that even an alleyway opening directly into the public domain may have doors installed if its entrance is visibly formed like a doorway with doorposts. In one case, an alleyway contained a neighborhood courtyard with a synagogue and a mikvah. The courtyard was closed by doors, while the alleyway opened into a non-Jewish public domain. Jews living elsewhere had a right of passage through the alleyway to reach that courtyard for immersion or prayer. If the alleyway residents wished to install doors to protect themselves from non-Jews entering, the other Jews could prevent them, even though the residents proposed removing the door from the courtyard and placing it at the head of the alleyway. This is the strict law. If, however, the king granted them permission to install doors in their alleyway, the law of the kingdom is law, because the markets and streets belong to the sovereign and he may regulate them as he wishes.
