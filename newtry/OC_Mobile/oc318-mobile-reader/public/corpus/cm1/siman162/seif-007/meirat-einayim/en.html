For they must go around the platform. Meaning that when they wish to walk from their doorway through the alley to the public domain, they will have to detour around the platform. The Mechaber concludes that the inner residents may even object to opening an additional doorway into the courtyard, because that doorway too will force them to alter their route rather than pass directly in front of it, since people customarily place and leave objects at and immediately before an entrance. Unless the inner resident had the power to close the alley there. Meaning at the place where the second resident built the platform beside his doorway, opposite his courtyard. If the inner resident could have closed the alley at that point, leaving the second resident only the width necessary opposite his doorway to enter and leave toward the alley and public domain, then he can also prevent construction of the platform. Since he could, if he wished, close the alley entirely at that point, prevention of the platform is included within that greater right. This is similar to what is written at the end of this siman.
Therefore he may also object to one who comes to close, etc. The Tur explains the case more fully. Reuven has an opening along the northern side of the alley and Shimon's courtyard is opposite it to the south. Reuven's opening is farther inward, toward the closed end of the alley, than Shimon's opening. The Tur concludes that Reuven cannot build from the wall of his own doorway across to the opposite wall and close off the entire inward section. Today or tomorrow Shimon may wish to open another doorway farther inward than his present one, toward the closed end of the alley. See the Perishah.
