There. The Maggid Mishneh writes that this too is compelled by the Gemara and by the baraita. Bach writes that the baraita concerning five courtyards opening into an alley implies this, because it teaches that each uses the area opposite itself, including opening a new entrance either inward or outward.
Kesef Mishneh explains that since the second courtyard may use the area from its entrance outward, it may open into the outer courtyard, because it and the inner courtyard share rights of use there. But inward from its own entrance it has no right of use, so the inner courtyard may prevent it. Rambam carefully writes that the inner courtyard may prevent him, teaching that even if the third courtyard consents, the fourth, which lies deeper inside, may object. Bach explains that the new opening brings traffic closer to the innermost courtyard than the old opening did, and at times crowds may press inward before it.
Meaning, Reuven has an entrance along the northern side of the alley and Shimon's courtyard is opposite on the south. Reuven's entrance lies farther inward toward the closed end than Shimon's. Reuven may not close the alley by building a wall from his entrance to the opposite wall, because Shimon may later wish to open entrances farther inward. Tur.
