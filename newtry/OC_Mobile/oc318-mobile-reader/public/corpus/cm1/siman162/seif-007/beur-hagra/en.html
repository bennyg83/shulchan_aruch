The inner one... because he has no... Beyond the claim of increased traffic, the inner resident has a separate use-right in the area nearest him. The phrase "the inner one" therefore means more than the ordinary traffic claim available to an outer resident.
Who explains that she uses the area opposite her courtyard for herself, and even farther inward wherever there is no courtyard beyond her. Therefore... Here the earlier "some say" fits. Rambam follows his view that an alley also supports a claim of increased traffic, whereas the other commentators disagree. According to them, Rav Huna's question concerns only use of an area where one has no right and could be excluded by a closure. According to Rashba, no resident can object merely for increased traffic, while according to Rambam all may. Their dispute thus turns on three matters: rights opposite one's courtyard, the meaning of closing opposite one's entrance, and whether increased traffic is a claim in an alley. This is why the gloss says that some hold each resident controls the area opposite his courtyard, while others deny that and focus instead on the power to close the way.
