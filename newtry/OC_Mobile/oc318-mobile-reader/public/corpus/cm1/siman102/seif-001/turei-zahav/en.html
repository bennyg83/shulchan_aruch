In place of the very best land. I do not understand the basis of this ruling. According to Sema, seif katan 13, it rests on an assessment of his intention. But we hold that unexpressed intentions are legally ineffective, and at the very least some outward indication is required, as Tosafot write in Ketubot 96, s.v. zavin, and as the Tur writes in siman 204. Consider a wealthy man who obligates himself to give a gift and later loses part of his wealth. Can he revoke the gift? Siman 293 teaches that only where a specifically identified item was lost is the loss borne by the recipient, because one can say, “It was your item that was lost.” That reasoning does not apply here. The Gemara from which this ruling is derived presents no difficulty. In Bava Metzia 66, a man accepted liability and stipulated, “If it is seized from me, you will collect from my choicest of the choicest land.” The other replied, “I will not give you my very best, because I wish to retain it, but I will give you from the ordinary best land that I own.” Ultimately, the very best land was swept away by water, and the Gemara held that he could say, “The ordinary best land now stands in place of the very best.” There, however, he had expressly stated at the outset that he wished to retain the superior field. It therefore remained so afterward as well. Otherwise, the Gemara would have had no reason to recount the entire prior exchange concerning the very best land. The Gemara is not a merchant displaying unnecessary wares. The express declaration is essential. It is therefore difficult that the Mechaber states the rule in abbreviated form without requiring any prior revelation of intent. I later saw that Maharshal, Bava Kamma, chapter 1, section 13, questions the Tur: why may the creditor not collect from the best land that remains in place of the lost very best land, since we have established that even when the undertaking merely says “best land,” he may collect from the very best? In two manuscripts Maharshal found the wording, “he stipulated with him to pay from the very best land,” and he was all the more puzzled why the creditor should collect only from intermediate land. Maharshal therefore concludes against the Tur that when the document provides for best land, the creditor collects whatever best land remains. It appears to me that the manuscript cited by Maharshal is partly correct but omits a crucial phrase. The text should read: “He stipulated with him not to pay from the very best land, but from the ordinary best land, and that land was lost.” This accords exactly with the Gemara and resolves the entire matter.
