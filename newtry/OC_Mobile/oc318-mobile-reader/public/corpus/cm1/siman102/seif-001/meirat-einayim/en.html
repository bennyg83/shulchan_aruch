They collect for him from intermediate land. Ulla says in Bava Kamma 8a that by Torah law a creditor is entitled only to inferior land, from the verse, "You shall stand outside, and the man who owes you shall bring the pledge out to you." A person ordinarily brings out the poorest of his utensils, and similarly, when paying with land, Torah law would permit him to give the poorest field. Chazal enacted that a creditor receives intermediate land so that people would not refuse to lend. We rule like Ulla.
It is appraised according to his own property. The Tur explains that if the debtor owns superior, intermediate, and inferior land, even if his intermediate field is as good as other people's superior land, the creditor collects from that intermediate field. This is a stringency against the debtor, and certainly we likewise follow his own grading where it is lenient, such as when his superior land is only like ordinary intermediate land and his intermediate land like ordinary inferior land. The rule is learned from damages, where the verse says, "the best of his field and the best of his vineyard he shall pay," referring to the field of the person who pays, namely the tortfeasor; the same framework applies to a borrower.
