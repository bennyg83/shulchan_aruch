He collects from intermediate quality land. By Torah law a creditor collects from inferior land, as Scripture says, 'The man whom you have a claim against shall bring out to you,' and a person naturally brings out the least valuable of his utensils. The Sages, however, enacted that a creditor collect from intermediate land so as not to close the door before borrowers, who otherwise would fear that a person could borrow today and tomorrow claim that he has only inferior property. This is stated in the Gemara and by Rambam in chapter 19 of Hilchos Malveh VeLoveh.

We assess according to the debtor's property. If the debtor owns intermediate and inferior land, even if his intermediate property is equal to ordinary intermediate property in the market, since he owns nothing superior to it, that intermediate property is considered his superior category, and the creditor collects only from his inferior property. If the debtor's intermediate property equals ordinary superior land and his inferior property equals ordinary intermediate land, the creditor nevertheless collects from what is intermediate relative to this debtor, because all categories follow the debtor's own holdings. An exception is where he has four levels, exceptionally superior, superior, intermediate, and inferior. Then the creditor takes the intermediate category.
