Even according to the future higher value. See Tumim. If the borrower never indicated any willingness to give superior land at the future higher valuation, everyone agrees the creditor cannot say, "Give me slightly less superior land even at its future higher value," because he has no right to superior land. But if the borrower reveals willingness to do so, the matter depends on the dispute between Mechaber and Rama in seif 2. According to Mechaber the creditor still cannot compel him. According to Rama, following Rosh, once the borrower has revealed such willingness the creditor can compel him to give the land at the future higher valuation. The same principle applies to a woman's ketubah when she demands intermediate land at a reduced quantity.
And to purchase intermediate land. Just as one who owns only inferior land is not required to buy intermediate land and give it to the creditor, so too one who owns superior land is not required to sell it and buy intermediate land. Sema. This refers to exchange. If he sold the superior land for money, it is obvious that the creditor takes the money, because when cash exists the creditor takes cash. The question is only where he seeks to exchange the superior land for intermediate land, and even then he may not be compelled.
