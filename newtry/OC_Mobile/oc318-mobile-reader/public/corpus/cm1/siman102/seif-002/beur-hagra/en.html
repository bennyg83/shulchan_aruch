(Likkut) If he wishes, etc., and some say, etc. Their proof is that even regarding medium-quality land upon which the lien rests, he can defer him to other property and the creditor cannot say, Give me this particular field, as above. See Tosafos there, s.v. 'he can,' etc. The first view, however, holds that although with two parcels of medium-quality land the creditor cannot insist in this manner, with medium-quality and inferior land he can. The proof is from what is stated on daf 8b: R' said, Reuven who sold, etc.; and this was said only, etc., because he can say, It was for this reason that I was exacting, etc. Rashba asks in his novellae there: What is the case? If Shimon retained no medium-quality land, the inferior land in Levi's possession is subject to the lien; and if Shimon did retain medium-quality land, why is it necessary to say, It was for this reason, etc.? Even if he sold medium-quality land while retaining medium-quality land, the creditor cannot collect from the buyer. He answers that two parcels of medium-quality land are different, because the buyer can defer him to the medium-quality land remaining with Shimon. This is unlike medium-quality and inferior land, where he cannot defer him to the medium-quality land, and therefore the Gemara must say, It was for this reason that I was exacting, etc. See Tosafos there, s.v. 'but,' etc., and below, siman 119, seif 1. End.
(Likkut) And some say that he cannot, etc. This follows the foregoing rule. Rosh is cited. The first opinion reasons that although a creditor ordinarily cannot demand a specific intermediate-quality field, he may do so when the alternative is inferior land in another's hands. Rashba distinguishes between equal-quality fields and intermediate versus inferior land. The second view relies on Bava Kamma 8b, but that proof is not conclusive. See citation 119.
