From whichever he wishes. The Torah gives the debtor the right to pay with whichever movable article he chooses, as Scripture says that he shall bring it out to the creditor, and even bran is considered acceptable payment because all movable property is regarded as the best form of payment. The same applies to land where all the fields are of equal intermediate quality. This is simple, as Sema writes.
Whichever he wishes. See Tumim. If all the debtor's fields are inferior, he cannot force the creditor from one field to another. The creditor may say, 'I want this field,' because once the debtor must give inferior land, this particular field is as desirable to the creditor as intermediate land. If the debtor revealed his position and said, 'If you want this intermediate field, pay me according to its expected future higher value,' see Tumim. I wrote that this depends upon the dispute between Rosh and Tosafos reflected in the positions of the Mechaber and Rama. According to Rama, the creditor must accept the future value condition, whereas according to the Mechaber he need not. If the desired intermediate field borders the creditor's own property, see Tumim. I wrote that according to the Mechaber below in siman 174, where we compel division along a common boundary to avoid conduct characteristic of Sodom, the debtor can be compelled to give that field. The same applies where the creditor requests slightly more inferior land. Even Rama would agree there that no prior declaration is required that the creditor must accept it at its future value.
And some say that we do not compel him, etc. Rosh writes that even if the debtor has intermediate land, the creditor cannot compel him to give one particular intermediate field if the debtor wishes to give another. Why, then, should he be compelled to give a particular inferior field? The case must therefore be where the debtor revealed that he is willing to give that field only at its expected future value. Refusing in such a case would close the door before lenders. Maharshal rules like the Mechaber, and Shach cites him. See Tumim, where I answered Rosh's difficulty. Nevertheless, I concluded that the possessor may rely upon Rosh. See there at length.
