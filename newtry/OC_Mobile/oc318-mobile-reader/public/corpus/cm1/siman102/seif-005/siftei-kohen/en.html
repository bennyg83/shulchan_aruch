And he stipulated with him, etc. The Tur implies that if the debtor's superior-quality land was not lost, he gives the creditor superior land, but the creditor does not collect from the very finest tract. Maharshal argues that, just as a victim of damages collects from the finest of the finest, one whose contract simply provides for collection from superior land should also collect from the very finest; if that tract was lost, he should collect from the remaining superior land. His words are astonishing. Even in damages the creditor cannot demand the finest of the finest, for the debtor may direct him to whichever superior tract he chooses, as the Rosh and Tur rule below in siman 419. Moreover, here the right arises from a contractual stipulation, and the holder of a document bears the disadvantage of ambiguity. The Tur's ruling is explicit in the Gemara in Bava Metzia and was adopted by the Rosh. It appears that this passage escaped Maharshal's attention.
