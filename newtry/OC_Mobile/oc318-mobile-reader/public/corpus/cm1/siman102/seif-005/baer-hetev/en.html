In place of. We estimate his intent and say that he agreed to pay from superior land only because he assumed that he would still retain exceptionally superior land. Thus writes the Sema. The Shach writes: This implies that if the exceptionally superior land was not lost, he gives ordinary superior land and the creditor does not collect from the exceptionally superior land. Maharshal challenges the Tur, arguing that since a victim of damages collects from the debtor's best land, the same should apply where the debtor wrote simply that collection would be from superior land. Accordingly, if the exceptionally superior land was lost, the creditor should collect from the remaining superior land. These words are difficult, for even in damages the creditor does not necessarily collect from the very best parcel, as stated in siman 419, where the debtor may direct him to whichever superior parcel he chooses. Maharshal indeed disputes that ruling as well, as cited there in his name. Moreover, this case is different because the right arises from a stipulation, and an ambiguous stipulation is construed against the holder of the document. The Tur's ruling is an explicit Gemara in Bava Metzia 66b. It appears that this passage escaped Maharshal's attention, since he does not mention it at all. It is difficult to say that the Gemara speaks only where the debtor explicitly told the creditor, "I will not give you my exceptionally superior land," for the text does not imply this.
