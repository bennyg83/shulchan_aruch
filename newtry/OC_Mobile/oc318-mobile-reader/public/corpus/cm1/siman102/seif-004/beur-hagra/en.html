(Collected notes) If he has inferior and intermediate land... This follows the Ran at the end of Kesubos that appraisal is made relative to the debtor's own holdings, together with Tosafos' explanation and the Raavad cited by Rashba in Bava Kamma. Ravina frames the dispute in terms of Ulla's enactment and whether creditors by default collect inferior or intermediate land. According to the view that creditors collect inferior land, both Torah law and the enactment point to inferior land. According to the view that they collect intermediate land, the debtor's available property is measured differently. We rule like Ulla because Rava, the later authority, follows him, although the precise application of the sugya is disputed by Rashi, Nimukei Yosef, and the Ramah. (End of collected notes.)
