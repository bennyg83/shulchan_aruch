There, seif 7, from the baraita in Bava Kamma 7b.
According to the Gemara's explanation there at the beginning of 8a, and so said Rav Chisda, etc. Rambam in chapter 19, law 4, writes generally that damages and a creditor are collected from intermediate-quality land, implying that he holds that valuation is relative to the general market. This appears to contradict what was cited in his name in seif 1. Maggid Mishneh discusses this at length there in chapter 24. Bach writes that Rambam also accepts the Gemara's explanation above, but in chapter 19 he merely records the baraita in its original form, as is his practice, and it likewise concerns one who possessed superior-quality land at the time the loan was made, etc.
