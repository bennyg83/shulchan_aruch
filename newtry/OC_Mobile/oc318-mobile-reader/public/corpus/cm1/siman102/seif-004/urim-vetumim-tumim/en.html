And he sold it, etc. Maharshal holds that if the superior land was sold before the debt became due, the creditor collects only inferior land. The Shach says that the Gemara and authorities imply no such distinction. There may in fact be a proof against Maharshal. Bava Kamma 8 resolves conflicting baraitot by saying that in both cases the debtor had no superior land at the time of collection, but in one his intermediate land equaled the world's superior land and in the other it did not. Tosafot ask that if he never had superior land, how can the field be called intermediate at all, and therefore explain one baraita as a case where superior land still exists. According to Maharshal, the Gemara could have simply said that the debtor once had superior land and sold it before the payment date. The name "intermediate" would have attached when the superior land existed, yet because the sale preceded maturity the creditor would collect only inferior land. That would have reconciled the baraitot without Tosafot's strained distinction. The Gemara's failure to do so indicates that even a sale before maturity leaves the creditor entitled to intermediate land. Likewise. The Gemara's statement that if superior and inferior land were sold, a creditor cannot seize inferior land from a later purchaser because that purchaser bought land unsuitable for him implies the same. If selling superior land before maturity converted the retained intermediate land into the debtor's superior land under appraisal according to his own holdings, the creditor's grade would become inferior and the sold inferior parcel would be suitable for him. Yet the Gemara speaks without distinction as to when the superior land was sold. It follows that once superior land existed when the debt arose, the creditor's intermediate grade remains even if that superior land is later sold. Even Tosafot in Gittin, who elsewhere indicate that Rava assesses according to the world, do not help Maharshal, because the codifiers who follow appraisal according to one's own property nevertheless cite this rule without distinction. Rashi in Gittin 50 is still clearer. He writes that even where a condition called for superior land, if that superior land was later swept away by a river or taken by robbers, the creditor collects intermediate land under Ulla's ordinary rule. Rashi does not say that the loss must have occurred after maturity. The loss of superior land is treated like its sale, yet the creditor still takes intermediate. Thus the Shach is correct that Maharshal's distinction is difficult, and even a sale before maturity does not reduce the creditor to inferior land.
