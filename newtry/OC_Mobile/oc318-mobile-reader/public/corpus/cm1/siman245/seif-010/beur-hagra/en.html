The recipient, etc. See the Maggid Mishneh and Kesef Mishneh, who struggled with the explanation. It appears that Rambam had the reading cited by Tosafos in Gittin 32a, s.v. "mevutelet": "however, according to the books that read, etc." The Gemara's challenge there follows its initial assumption that his words remain effective and the gift returns to its owner. Abaye answers according to his own reasoning. Rashi there, s.v. "betelah," was therefore forced to distinguish between land and movable property. Thus, "I do not want it" corresponds to "I do not wish it"; "I do not accept it" corresponds to "it is not a gift," for this necessarily means "I do not accept it." The same applies to "it is void," namely the "void" mentioned there, since otherwise the Gemara would not have challenged as it did. This is also the explicit reading there.
