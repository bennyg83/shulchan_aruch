If a recipient acquired a gift and, after it came into his possession while he remained silent, he later says, "I do not want it," "I do not accept it," "It is void," or, "I find this defect in it," his statement does not return it to the donor. Rather, the property becomes ownerless and whoever first takes it acquires it. Rama: A creditor of the recipient may collect from it. If the recipient protested from the outset, he never acquired and the property returns to the original owner. Rama: These rules apply when his words mean only that he does not want the gift. If he uses language asserting that no gift ever occurred, then although he was initially silent, he is believed by his own admission, even to the detriment of creditors, because the property was not previously established as his. If he says, "I do not want it," regarding movable property, it becomes ownerless. Regarding land, after his initial silence his later words have no effect.
