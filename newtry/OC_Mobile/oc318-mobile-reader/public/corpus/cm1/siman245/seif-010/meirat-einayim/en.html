The recipient accepted the gift and acquired it, and after it came into his hand he was silent, etc. The Tur writes that with a gift of a shechiv mera, if the recipient was silent initially when the giver said, "Give it to him," this is treated like silence when the gift actually comes into his hand, because the words of a shechiv mera are as though written and delivered. Rif and the Rosh likewise write this. The Mechaber copied Rambam, who does not state this. It is possible that Rambam agrees as a matter of law but did not address gifts of a shechiv mera in chapter 4 of Hilchot Zechiyah. See the Derishah.
Or, "I now perceive this defect in it." The Kesef Mishneh writes on Rambam: "I do not know his source, and there is no apparent reason. One can say that initially he was silent because he had not seen the defect, and now that he discovered it he protests, for he never intended to accept it with the defect. Perhaps the case concerns a defect visible to everyone, which he certainly knew from the outset. Even though he says, 'I now perceive this defect,' meaning that he only saw it now, since the defect is obvious we are witnesses that he is lying." End of his words.
He has said nothing, etc., rather it is ownerless. The Mechaber follows his own explanation in the Beit Yosef of Rambam's formulation in chapter 4 of Hilchot Zechiyah, which he copied here. In the Derishah I proved that Rambam's view is that the property becomes ownerless only when he says, "I do not want it," which is the same as the Gemara's phrase "I have no desire for it." As for the other expressions, Rambam holds that the property remains with the recipient. See there.
If he has a creditor, the creditor collects from it. It appears that Rama speaks of a creditor holding a document witnessed by witnesses, so that all of the recipient's property is encumbered to him. The recipient cannot render the property ownerless and thereby remove it from the lien once he has acquired it. Rama therefore concludes that all this applies to one of these expressions, whose meaning is that the recipient already acquired the property and now no longer desires it. These expressions are no stronger than an explicit sale or gift to another person, where a lender holding a promissory note may seize it from the transferee. Rama should not be understood as referring to the Nimukei Yosef in chapter Yesh Nochalin, cited in Darkei Moshe, which says that even if the property is ownerless and whoever first takes it acquires it, if a creditor was first to seize it, the recipient is discharged from the debt and cannot say, "I acquired it from ownerless property and my debt remains." If that were Rama's intent, he would not have written in this formulation that "the creditor collects from it."
If he said, "I have no desire for it," etc. Rama's wording implies that he understands "I do not want it" not to be equivalent to "I have no desire for it," which the Mechaber mentioned immediately above. In the Derishah I proved that according to Rambam they are equivalent.
