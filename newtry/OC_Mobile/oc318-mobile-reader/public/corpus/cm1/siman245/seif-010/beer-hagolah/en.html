Reish) This is the language of the Rambam at the beginning of chapter 4, based on the statements of Rav Yehudah in the name of Shmuel and Rabbi Yochanan, and Rabbi Abba bar Mamel's explanation that they do not disagree, at the end of the eighth chapter of Bava Batra 137b.
The Kesef Mishneh writes that Rambam omitted the case where the recipient says, "It is void" or "It is not a gift," where his words are effective because they plainly refer to the past, because Rambam relied on what he writes at the end of the chapter, halachah 13, concerning a recipient who claims, etc. The Mechaber brings that rule in seif 7. This is not the place to elaborate.
The Kesef Mishneh writes that perhaps the case concerns a visible defect that the recipient certainly recognized from the beginning. Even though he says, "This defect now appears to me," because it is obvious to all, it is evident that he is lying.
Shin) The Maggid Mishneh writes that this is explicit from Rambam's phrase, "he has said nothing."
Tav) A statement of Reish Lakish in Keritot 24a, as explained there at the beginning of 24b.
Alef) Where the creditor holds a deed and there are witnesses, the debtor cannot remove the lien by declaring the property ownerless. This is no better than selling or gifting it, in which case the creditor still collects from it. The same applies if he declares it ownerless and another acquires it. So writes the Sema.
Bet) This is explained in Bava Batra at the beginning of 138a.
Gimel) Tosafot likewise write there in the name of Rabbenu Yitzchak.
The Maggid Mishneh writes that if they died, the property passes to their heirs.
Dalet) This follows Rashi's view. According to Rambam, there is no distinction between saying "I do not want it" and "I am unwilling to accept it." The Tur likewise writes this there.
