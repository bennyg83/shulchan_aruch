This is unlike ordinary monetary doubts, where once one party has seized, the other cannot take it back, as the Mechaber also writes at the beginning of siman 139 concerning the disputed ship. Here the original ownership is known. Therefore, if the original owners later retake the property from the person who first acquired it, no one can remove it from them. Even if that first acquirer then takes it again, we do not leave it in his possession. Thus far the Sema. The Taz disagrees and writes that these words are astonishing, resolving the question from the beginning of siman 139 differently. See there.
And some say. See the Shach, who disagrees and holds there is no dispute at all, and that the Mechaber overlooked the Rambam's words in chapter 9 of Hilchot Zechiyah. He is not satisfied with the Sema's reconciliation. The Taz writes that this 'some say' belongs in seif 10, after the phrase 'rather, it is ownerless and whoever first acquires it gains it,' and that the 'some say' refers to that ruling.
