If the donor caused another person to acquire the gift for the recipient, and when the recipient heard he remained silent but later protested, saying, "I do not accept it," there is doubt whether his silence showed consent followed by retraction, or whether he remained silent because nothing had yet reached his hand and his later protest revealed his original intent. Therefore, if a third person first takes the property for himself, it is not removed from him, for perhaps the recipient acquired and then abandoned it. If the original owners then seize it from that third person, it is not removed from them, for perhaps the recipient never acquired and the property remained theirs. Some say that with a deathly ill person's gift, the recipient acquires once he remains silent upon hearing. Rama: I have not seen anyone distinguish here between a deathly ill and a healthy donor, nor does such a distinction appear correct. Since another acquired the gift for him, a healthy person's gift is acquired in the same manner. The Rosh likewise holds that the recipient acquires in every case.
