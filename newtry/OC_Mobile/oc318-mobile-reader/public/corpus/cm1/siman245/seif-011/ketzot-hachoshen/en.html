Rosh says that where a recipient initially remained silent when a deathly ill donor made the gift, his later protest is too late because the words of a deathly ill person are treated as though written and delivered. This creates a difficulty: a deathbed gift ordinarily takes effect only at death. Before then both donor and, one would think, recipient should be able to retract, just as parties can retract from a transaction stated to take effect after thirty days. Rosh's own theory resolves it. He says that when a deathbed gift ultimately takes effect, it is clarified retroactively that the recipient acquired from the time of the original statement. This is why, if the recipient dies before the donor, the recipient's heirs can sometimes receive. Once the donor dies and the gift becomes effective, the recipient's earlier silence is therefore treated as consent to an acquisition already rooted at the time of the gift, and his attempted retraction does not undo it. Rashba, Ramah, and Ran understand deathbed gifts differently. They hold that the gift truly takes effect only after death; where a recipient died earlier, special rules such as "go" being equivalent to "acquire" may preserve the transfer. According to that approach, a recipient who expressly accepted during the donor's life should still be able to retract before the donor dies, because no acquisition yet exists. This explains why the Shulchan Aruch presents Rosh's rule as an additional opinion rather than an undisputed result.
