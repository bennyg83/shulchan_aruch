(Seif 11: if the original owners returned.) I am greatly astonished at the Sema's words in seif katan 21. According to his view that we place the property in the presumptive ownership of the original owners, no later person's seizure should have any effect. Why, then, does he write that if the original owners return and seize it their position is stronger because they were the first owners? Their original presumption already had the legal force of possession, even when the property was not physically in their hands, under the rule of prior ownership discussed in chapter HaShoel concerning one who exchanges a cow for a donkey. Property in a marsh is treated as though it were in the prior owner's hands. The initial difficulty does not arise. Only in the case of the disputed ship is the rule “whoever is stronger prevails” a rabbinic arrangement. The Sages did not rule that each litigant swear and take half, as Tosafot and the Rosh write at the beginning of Bava Metzia. Their reason is that the Sages assumed only the rightful party would seize, because the other thinks, “why should I exert myself to seize today when tomorrow he may bring witnesses and remove it from me?” Therefore, after the first party seized, a later seizure by the second is ineffective, for there is reason to presume that the first seized lawfully, having exerted himself more at the outset. Here, however, the uncertainty is a doubt of law. The Rambam holds that the Sages made no special arrangement. The rule that each swears and takes half is inapplicable, and “whoever is stronger prevails” is also inapplicable because its rationale does not exist here. The matter therefore remains under the ordinary rule that whoever is in possession prevails. Accordingly, whoever first obtains possession prevails, whether the original owners or another person. The same applies, according to the Rambam, in all doubts of law, as appears in many places. The Rambam needed to say that another person may also seize only to teach that the effectiveness of possession does not apply to the intended recipient. The recipient has no right under either side of the doubt. It is only between the original owners and another possessor that whoever obtains possession prevails.
