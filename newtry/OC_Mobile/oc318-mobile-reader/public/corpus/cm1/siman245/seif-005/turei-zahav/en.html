(Seif 10, in the gloss: a creditor collects from him.) See the Sema in seif katan 18, who explains that the case concerns a written loan. In my humble opinion, the same applies to an oral loan. Since the debtor has nothing else from which to pay, he has no power to declare this property ownerless and directly harm his creditor. This is proven from the rule that a person is not believed to say that property in his possession belongs to others, as stated in siman 99. If he could render it ownerless, he should be believed through a migo that he could now abandon it and allow those others to acquire it. Necessarily, he lacks the power to abandon it when doing so would injure the creditor.
