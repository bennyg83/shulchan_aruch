(Seif 7: he takes a rabbinic oath.) The Tur asks what relevance an oath has here. The Beit Yosef and Maggid Mishneh answer that it matters for the donor's creditor. A further difficulty may be raised: why did the Tur cite the latter clause of the Rambam, concerning the donor's claim, which the Mechaber states in seif 5, when that rule is obvious? He cited it to reject the answer that the issue concerns a creditor. The latter clause must concern a recipient who held the property for the years required for prescriptive possession, as the Maggid Mishneh writes, for otherwise he would not be believed. If so, the first clause must also concern one who held it for those years. In such a case it is obvious that the recipient's creditor may collect from the property, since he is in possession as owner and the donor has already admitted giving it to him. The oath would add nothing. Necessarily, the Rambam is not discussing a creditor at all, and the Tur's question is therefore sound. The Darkei Moshe writes that the recipient is not believed even with an oath where his claim harms a creditor, although perhaps in this particular case he is believed with an oath, since we know that the property was not originally his, as the Tur writes according to Rashi and as discussed by the Sema.
