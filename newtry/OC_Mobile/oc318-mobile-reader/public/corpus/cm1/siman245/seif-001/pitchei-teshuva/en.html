Some say that he acquires. Responsa Maharam Moshe Rothenburg, Choshen Mishpat siman 8, addresses an apparent inconsistency in Ramah. Here Ramah records, as “some say,” that the recipient acquires. In siman 60 seif 6, however, regarding a document stating “because I gave,” where the maker claims the property was not in his possession, Ramah writes that although this follows Rif, many authorities disagree and hold that saying “I gave such and such to so-and-so” constitutes a litigant's admission, equivalent to one hundred witnesses. In siman 191 seif 3, the Mechaber rules that our documents are instruments of conveyance; Sema explains that even though they say “I sold” and “I gave” in the past tense, they effect the transfer now, as Scripture likewise says, “I have given the money for the field.” Ramah offers no gloss there, although according to the dissenters the language would ordinarily be an admission and the document merely evidentiary unless we know that he presently intends to convey. Maharam Moshe Rothenburg explains that even those who disagree with Ran regarding the word “I gave” do not deny that it can express an admission. Rather, they hold that the phrase can bear either sense, admission or present conveyance, because Scripture uses it for a present transaction. Ordinarily the rule is that the burden of proof rests on the claimant. This applies to oral assertions. Where a written instrument is before us, however, although an ambiguous expression is likewise construed against the claimant, if one construction would nullify the instrument altogether we construe it so that the holder of the instrument prevails, as in Shulchan Aruch siman 42 seif 9. Ramah's rulings are therefore consistent. In siman 60, if “I gave” were not treated as an admission, the document would be wholly void because the maker claims the property was not his; therefore it is construed as an admission. Here in siman 245 the statement is merely oral, so there is no reason to preserve a document, and Ramah presents Ran's view only as “some say.” In siman 191, by contrast, if “I gave” were treated only as an admission the instrument would be void, because we know he had not conveyed earlier and now intends to convey; therefore the holder's hand is superior and the documents operate as instruments of conveyance.
