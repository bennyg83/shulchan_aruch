The animal may eat, etc. The verse says, "You shall not muzzle an ox while it threshes." I already explained at the beginning of siman 337 that we derive the worker's right to eat detached produce from the law of a working animal at threshing, and conversely derive the animal's right to eat attached produce from the worker. Thus, if an animal works with attached produce or the like, it too may eat, similar to the worker concerning whom the verse says, "When you come into your fellow's standing grain," which is attached. The work must, however, involve produce that grows from the ground, analogous to threshing grain and a vineyard, both of which are products of the ground.
And it may eat from the load on its back. This too is learned from a worker, concerning whom the verse says, "When you come," indicating that in the course of any labor he may eat, even from a load on his shoulder, as I wrote at the beginning of siman 337. The same applies to an animal. This excludes the view that the animal must be working specifically with its hands and feet in a manner resembling threshing. See siman 337.
He need not take food in his hand, etc. The Torah prohibited only muzzling: "You shall not muzzle." It prohibits an act that prevents the animal from eating, but does not obligate him to feed it with his own hands. The Tur and Mechaber write this regarding a load carried on the animal's back, because in other cases the animal naturally eats by itself.
