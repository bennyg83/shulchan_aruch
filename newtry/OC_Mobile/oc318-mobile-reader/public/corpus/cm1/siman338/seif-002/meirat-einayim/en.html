He receives lashes, as it says, "You shall not muzzle, etc." In this respect an animal differs from a human worker. One who prevents a worker from eating does not receive lashes, as the Tur and Mechaber write at the beginning of siman 337, because the worker is excluded by the verse's word "according to your soul," as explained there.
The same applies to every species of animal, etc. We derive this through the verbal analogy of "ox" from Shabbos, where the verse says, "and all your animals."
"Ox while threshing" is stated only because that is the usual case. Meaning, an ox commonly works in threshing and eats from the produce. So too the Gemara and Rambam explain. Although, as already cited in simanim 337 and 338, the word "threshing" is also needed to teach that one may eat detached produce until the final stage that creates liability for tithes or challah, and "ox" is needed to compare the muzzler to the one muzzled, namely the animal to the human worker so that laws can be derived from one to the other, nevertheless, since the verse specified an ox rather than another animal and threshing rather than another form of detached produce, we say that Scripture spoke of the usual case. Although this is not a true difficulty, because the Torah had to mention some representative case in any event, the principle remains that the verse speaks in ordinary terms.
