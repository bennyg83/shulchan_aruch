And he does not receive lashes. The Beit Yosef writes that although lashes are not administered today, the Tur merely follows the Rambam's language. In my additions to Siftei Kohen on Yoreh Deah, siman 27, I explained that there can still be practical consequences where lashes and payment would otherwise coincide. The Bach suggests that the Tur means the offender pays four kav for a cow and three for a donkey, whereas if lashes applied he could not both be lashed and pay, since one is punished for only one offense. This is difficult, because in the law of muzzling an animal one both receives lashes and pays, as stated by the Gemara and all the authorities, since the monetary obligation arose from the earlier act of drawing the animal into one's service. Moreover, even today the classification of the obligation affects the applicable rules of seizure and payment. The Tur's language therefore has substantive legal significance.
