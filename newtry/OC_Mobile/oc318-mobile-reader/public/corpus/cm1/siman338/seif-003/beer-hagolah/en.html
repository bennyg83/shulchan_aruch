The dispute of Rabbi Yochanan and Reish Lakish there at the end of 90a, and the ruling follows Rabbi Yochanan, as it does in the Halakhot. The Maggid Mishneh writes that the commentators already challenged this from Shevuot chapter 3, 21a, which states that one does not receive lashes for a prohibition lacking an action, except for one who swears, substitutes a consecrated animal, or curses his fellow using the Divine Name. This shows that movement of the lips is not considered an action, and Rambam rules accordingly in chapter 19 of Hilkhot Sanhedrin. The primary explanation is what Rambam writes here, that movement of the lips in muzzling is considered an action because his speech produces an action, for through his words the animal is muzzled. Tosafot there write likewise.
