They rendered them false witnesses. Contradiction and hazamah do not follow the same rules. With contradiction, both groups are disqualified for this testimony, although for other testimony each group may appear independently and testify. With hazamah, however, the first witnesses are disqualified while the latter witnesses remain fully qualified. The reason is that the latter witnesses testify about the bodies of the first witnesses by saying, "You were with us," much as witnesses testify that two men desecrated Shabbat or killed a person; those accused are not believed to respond, "We did not do it." Thus writes the Sema.
To pay. Nevertheless, the claimant may say, "I have no claim except against the defendant," and the defendant may then recover from the witnesses. See the Sema there. See also the Shach, who discusses this law at length.
A practical difference. The Shach writes that there are additional practical differences. See there. Several laws of testimony copied from the work Chukei Mishpat. Some persons are qualified to judge but disqualified to testify. A relative who was accepted as a judge is disqualified from testifying, even in that very case. Rashach, siman 83. Household servants are qualified to testify. Radbaz, siman 96, and Pleitat Beit Yosef, siman 18. Radbaz himself, however, writes in siman 24 that they are personally interested witnesses. Tenants and laborers, and likewise one who receives a stipend from Reuven to teach students without charge, are qualified to judge and testify, and there is not even a concern of pious conduct in doing so. Mahari ben Lev, siman 98. Rashdam, however, writes that a person should be stringent in this out of pious conduct. Even in a matter of prohibition, an enemy is qualified to testify. R. Shlomo HaLevi, siman 18. A yavam may testify for his yevamah. Rashba, manuscript responsum; see also the Bach. In the chapter Zeh Borer, page 28, the Gemara asks whether a man may testify for his stepson's wife, and Rashi explains the issue there. From this it appears that if a man's daughter's husband lost his wife and she left children, those children are disqualified from testifying for the grandfather and he for them, and therefore he is disqualified from testifying for his son-in-law even after his daughter has died, because the children are fit to inherit their father. This is from a manuscript of the gaon R. Yaakov, av beit din of Shterim. My father-in-law wrote in the margin: I find this difficult in light of Mordechai, who rules that one is disqualified from testifying for the wife of his father-in-law's brother because whatever a wife acquires is acquired by her husband, yet he is qualified to testify for the husband of his father-in-law's sister because what the husband acquires is not acquired by the wife. Mordechai does not disqualify him because he is disqualified from testifying for the son of his father-in-law's sister, who is a second-degree relative to a second-degree relative through one marriage and is fit to inherit the husband of his father-in-law's sister. See Mordechai there. End of my father-in-law's comment. Thus far.
