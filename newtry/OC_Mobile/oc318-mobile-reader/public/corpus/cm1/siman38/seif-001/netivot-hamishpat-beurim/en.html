The reason is that hazamah is believed over the first witnesses because it is testimony about the persons of the witnesses themselves: 'You were with us elsewhere.' But if the second group merely contradicts the underlying transaction while also saying they were present with the witnesses, their testimony is no stronger than ordinary contradiction. This does not conflict with the rule that one can testify, 'So-and-so violated me under compulsion,' together with another witness. There the contradiction involved in disproving the witness is itself directed to the witness's own presence and therefore has the character of hazamah. Likewise, where the disproof and contradiction occur in distinct statements after the hazamah has already taken effect, the later contradiction does not undo the earlier hazamah.
There is a practical difference, etc. Shach distinguishes between hazamah outside the witnesses' presence and testimony that the witnesses are robbers. At first glance both concern the persons of the witnesses. His point is that ordinary hazamah is itself a scriptural novelty. Without the special Torah rule, saying 'you were with us elsewhere' would not automatically be stronger than saying 'the borrower was with us elsewhere.' Testimony that the witnesses themselves committed robbery, however, is ordinary direct testimony about their own conduct and is not dependent on that novelty. Urim VeTumim connects this to the dispute whether hazamah received outside the witnesses' presence can disqualify them. In my view even those who invalidate reception of ordinary testimony outside a litigant's presence can accept hazamah or disqualification when the principal proceeding is properly before the relevant litigant and the effect on the witnesses is only a consequence of testimony necessary to the case. Thus where a borrower must defend against witnesses, his evidence disqualifying them can be heard even though the witnesses themselves are not the primary litigants. The same applies to witnesses to divorce and similar cases according to the procedural context.
