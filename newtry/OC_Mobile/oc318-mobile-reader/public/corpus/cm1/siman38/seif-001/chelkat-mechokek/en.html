And if not, she is not betrothed. This applies even if he failed to fulfill the condition because of circumstances beyond his control. She is nevertheless not betrothed, because a compelled failure to perform is still a failure to perform, in accordance with Rabbi Yochanan in the Yerushalmi, cited by the Ran in Kiddushin, chapter HaOmer. The Ran explains the distinction between divorce and kiddushin. A claim of unavoidable circumstances applies where a person undertakes an obligation upon himself and can say, "I never obligated myself with such an event in mind." But here the matter depends upon the will of the other party. If the man is prevented from fulfilling the condition, the woman can say, "What do your circumstances have to do with me? I agreed to become your wife only if the condition was fulfilled." So long as the condition remains unfulfilled, even through compulsion, she did not consent to the kiddushin. The same applies in reverse where the woman was prevented from fulfilling her condition.
