Unless the name of the stolen object changed. Torat Chaim argues that Rambam and the Shulchan Aruch do not require a change of name in addition to change of act. Tur and Rama do require it, and Tosafot and Maggid Mishneh appear to support that position. The requirement cannot mean a full independent change of name, because then change of act would add nothing. Rather, an act can acquire when it produces at least some altered designation of the object. A major change of name by itself can acquire even without physical transformation; where acquisition is based on physical change, a lesser accompanying change of designation is enough. A difficulty remains from chametz kept over the festival. If nonapparent damage were treated as visible damage and therefore as physical change, the object still remains bread before and after. If no altered designation occurred, the robber should still be able to say, "Here is yours before you." Yet the Gemara seems to treat the question differently. This requires study.
