And he dyed it. Even if he dyed it with ordinary dye and not specifically with indigo. So writes Rabbenu Yerucham, and this is implied by Rambam and Shulchan Aruch. The Bach questions Rabbenu Yerucham, but the Gemara and Tosafos support him. The Tur, however, speaks of a case in which the change cannot be reversed, as stated in seif 7 in the Rama, because he rules that a reversible change does not acquire.
Or he beat it and whitened it. This is the correct text, and so appears in Rambam. According to the alternative textual tradition in the statement of Rabbi Chiya bar Abba and Rava, beating is regarded as the greater change. According to our text, mere cleaning alone does not acquire, as Tosafos and the Rosh explain.
And it is not called, etc. See Tosafos there. This is not an absolute rule. Tosafos in Sukkah discuss cases where cutting, pounding, or working wood changes the object without necessarily changing its name. The Gemara itself first asks why he should not acquire through change of form and only afterward through change of name, showing that change of form need not depend on a new name. The same is true where a lamb becomes a ram, even though its basic name remains.
