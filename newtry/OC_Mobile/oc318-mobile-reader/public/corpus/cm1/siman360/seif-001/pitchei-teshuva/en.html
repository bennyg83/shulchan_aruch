The enactment for those who repent in cases of land. See Baer Heitev's discussion of Sema's wording. Levush gives another reason why the enactment for repentant wrongdoers was not made for land. The enactment was intended to prevent a robber from concealing what he stole and refusing to repent. Land cannot be concealed, so that concern does not apply. Responsa Mabit, volume 3, siman 143, writes that if an encroachment did not occur knowingly but builders accidentally placed half an amah of a structure on a neighbor's land, perhaps even the Geonim would agree that the enactment applies and the builder may pay the value of the half amah rather than demolish the structure. Their stringency was because the robber violated the prohibition against moving a neighbor's boundary, and here he did not transgress knowingly. Mishneh LaMelech, Hilchot Geneivah 6:11, cites this briefly and says that Mabit's words do not appear correct at all. Responsa Rishmei She'eilah, end of siman 56, cites Mishneh LaMelech in connection with its own case and leaves the matter requiring analysis. Shaar Mishpat writes that this question appears to depend on the dispute cited by Rama in siman 274 regarding bees that settled on another person's tree and whether the owner may cut the branch to recover them, and similarly the case of two travelers, one with a barrel of wine and one with a jar of honey, where the honey jar breaks and the wine owner may be required to pour out his wine to save the other's honey upon reimbursement because of the mitzvah of returning lost property, as discussed in siman 264.
