Fine. The Rashba holds that this applies specifically when he dried them in the sun, but not when he fired them in a kiln, because then they cannot be crushed back into earth as fine as before. The Tur's language implies that even if he fired them in a kiln the law is the same. The reason is that earth has little value, so one is not particular even if it is not fully restored to its former finely crushed state. This differs from felt that is to be made into wool, where we are particular because wool is valuable and intended to be restored for its original use. Ibid.
A coin. The Sema writes that if he made a silver utensil from it there is a dispute: Tosafot hold that he acquires it, while the Rashba disagrees. The Shach writes that there was no need to cite Tosafot for this, because Tosafot themselves compare it to boards that one made into a chest according to the opinion that a reversible change counts as a change. Since we rule that a reversible change is not a change, as the authorities and the Tur rule regarding boards and similar cases, the same follows here: making a utensil from the coin is not a change. This is also stated by Raavan, page 20b, and Maharshal rules so at the beginning of HaGozel. This is the primary ruling. Shach.
