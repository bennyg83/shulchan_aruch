If the debtor, etc. The Sema in seif katan 2 cites Rabbenu Yerucham, netiv 22, concerning Reuven who sent an agent to take wine from a non Jew in satisfaction of Reuven's debt. The agent did so, drew the wine, placed it on a wagon, and brought it into town. He then gave it to Shimon, to whom the non Jew also owed money. Rabbenu Yerucham rules that Shimon acquired it, and gives reasons for the ruling. In Rabbenu Yerucham the ruling is quoted in the name of Maharam with three reasons: there is no agency for a non Jew; the agent is one who seizes for a creditor where other creditors are harmed; and perhaps the halakhah does not follow Rabbi Shimon that a Jew acquires from a non Jew with money, such that drawing the property from the non Jew would not acquire it. These three reasons must be taken together, and the word "further" indicates their cumulative force. If drawing acquires from a non Jew, then the fact that there is no agency for a non Jew is irrelevant. The Jew appointed this agent to buy property from the non Jew for him. Nor is the agent merely seizing for a creditor where others are harmed if the non Jew voluntarily transferred the wine. Otherwise, whenever a Jew tells an agent to buy wheat from a non Jew, the property would become ownerless merely because the agent's acquisition indirectly disadvantages someone else. It makes no difference whether the agent paid cash in the market or received the goods in settlement of a debt. Remove the prior debt from the case and he still acquired the goods as the Jew's agent by drawing them from the non Jew. No later creditor can nullify that acquisition. The three reasons therefore operate as one. On Maharam's premise, no kinyan occurred because drawing does not acquire from a non Jew. The non Jew's instruction that the wine be delivered to Reuven also cannot itself transfer ownership through agency, because there is no agency for a non Jew. The only remaining basis for Reuven's claim would be that his agent seized the wine for him, and as to that Maharam properly says that the agent is one who seizes for a creditor where others are thereby harmed. This is clear. Accordingly, since the Mechaber rules plainly below in siman 198 that a Jew acquires from a non Jew by drawing the property, Maharam's entire ruling no longer applies. There is a further difficulty. We hold that property of a non Jew can become ownerless in the interval between the non Jew's abandonment and the Jew's acquisition, as where a non Jew sells land, receives the money, and thereby removes himself from the property before the Jewish purchaser acquires by deed. See below in siman 194, Rama seif 3, that with movables the non Jew is removed upon receiving the money, as the Shach explains. Here too, once the non Jew delivered the wine in payment of his debt, he removed himself from the wine. Even if, as Maharam assumes, Reuven the sender did not acquire it directly, the wine would then be ownerless. The agent could acquire it for Reuven because we hold that one who lifts ownerless property for another acquires it for him. Relative to Reuven, the wine is no worse than ownerless property, since the non Jew has already relinquished it. Perhaps in the actual case Reuven had to return the non Jew's promissory note, and because the note had not yet been returned the non Jew had not fully relinquished the wine. The wine would then remain in the non Jew's ownership. This requires study. In any event, the Mechaber acted correctly in omitting this ruling from the Shulchan Aruch, even though he cited it in the Beit Yosef, because according to our ruling that drawing acquires from a non Jew, its legal premise does not stand.
