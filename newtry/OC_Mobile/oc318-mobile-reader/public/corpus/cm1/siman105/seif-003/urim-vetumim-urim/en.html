If the debtor, etc. Where the debtor willingly grants the property for the other person's benefit, the rule against seizing for another creditor does not apply. Sema cites a case: Reuven sent an agent to take wine from a gentile toward a debt the gentile owed Reuven. The agent drew the wine, transported it by wagon into the city, and then gave it to Shimon, whom the gentile also owed money. Sema, following Rabbeinu Yerucham, says Shimon acquires the wine and Reuven has no claim. See Tumim. I wrote that Mechaber correctly omitted this ruling because we do not accept it as practical law. Reuven acquired the wine immediately, and he may remove it from Shimon without hesitation. See there for the reasoning.
For so-and-so. It can be any person, whether the debtor's creditor or someone else, even as a gift. Therefore the text says either "give" or "acquire for him." According to the authority below in siman 225 who holds that, for a gift, the word "give" has the same force as "acquire," "give" suffices. According to the authority who disagrees, he must expressly say, "Acquire for him.
