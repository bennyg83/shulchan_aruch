Specifically where the creditor holds a document that can now be validated in beit din. The Tur adds that even if the debtor can require the creditor to take an oath, we do not remove the property from the person who seized it. This means that if the creditor holds a validatable document, then even where the debtor claims it has been paid and the creditor must swear that it has not, the other person's seizure remains effective.
The other party cannot say: Set me a time. The Tur concludes, "Rather, they tell him to swear, and the seizer takes immediately." The Beit Yosef asks whether, if the creditor is not in the city to swear immediately but is within one day's journey, they send for him or not. In the Perishah I proved that the correct reading in the Tur is: "Rather, they tell him to swear, and the seizer takes immediately," not that the creditor must "swear immediately." Otherwise, why say that the seizer takes, when the property is already in his hands? There are other proofs as well. Accordingly, it is obvious that they give the creditor time to be notified, come, and swear, provided he is not far from the city. See there.
