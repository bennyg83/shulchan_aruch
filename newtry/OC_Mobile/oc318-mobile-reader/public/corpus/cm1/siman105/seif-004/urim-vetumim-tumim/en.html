The Sema in seif katan 6 notes that the Tur, citing the Rif, gives as the example a debtor who is close to death, for after his death the property would become movables of orphans. The Mechaber omitted this detail, because nowadays movables of orphans are subject to creditors. Yet this requires explanation. The Rosh writes in a responsum, klal 106, that according to the Rif even nowadays seizure after death is effective by virtue of the enactment of the Geonim. If so, why did the Rif need to say that the debtor is close to death? More fundamentally, what total loss is there if the creditor does not seize, since movables of orphans are now collectible? The answer must be as the Bach writes, that the orphans may sell or dissipate the movables, leaving no one from whom the creditor can collect. Accordingly, the Rif may be understood consistently with his own view. He holds that the right to seize where loss is anticipated is itself only an enactment, as he states explicitly. Therefore, while the debtor is alive and the movables are subject to collection by strict law, a creditor may seize them under that enactment. After death, however, even where loss is anticipated, he may not seize from the orphans, because the very rule that their movables are subject to collection is itself only an enactment of the Geonim. We do not layer one enactment upon another. The Rif therefore required seizure while the debtor was still alive. This also resolves the Rosh's objection to the Rif. The Rosh maintains that seizure in a case of anticipated loss is authorized by the underlying law, not merely by an enactment. The Maadanei Melekh to Bava Metzia, the Giddulei Terumah, and the Bach ask what practical difference this makes, since the Rosh himself writes in the first chapter of Gittin that Maharam's answer is correct, namely that seizure is allowed only where loss is anticipated, which appears to be the same rule as the Rif's. According to what I have written, the difference is substantial. For the Rif, seizure from movables of orphans is unavailable even where loss is anticipated because it would be an enactment built upon another enactment. For the Rosh, because the right to seize in a case of anticipated loss is part of the law itself, it applies even to movables of orphans. This is the practical novelty.
