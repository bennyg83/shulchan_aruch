Even if he appointed him as an agent. See Baer Heitev concerning the rule derived by the Shach that if a creditor hires someone to seize property for him, the seizure may be effective. Korban Netanel, Gittin chapter 1, letter 69, questions the Shach. He nevertheless cites his teacher, Rabbi Avraham Broda, who agreed with the practical ruling but for a different reason. Since the person was hired to seize, he has his own monetary interest in the seizure: his compensation depends upon successfully taking the property, presumably at a fixed amount for each maneh seized. He therefore has a personal right in what he seizes, and since he acquires an interest for himself, he may also acquire for his employer. See also Responsa Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat siman 100. Be'er HaGolah records another ruling of Maharam in which Reuven sent an agent to seize property, and Maharam ruled that when the agent is a non-Jew the seizure is ineffective because there is no agency for a non-Jew, together with an additional reason. This first reason appears difficult, since the non-Jew is merely acquiring on behalf of the Jew. Tumim, seif katan 4, raises the same objection. Responsa Rabbi Akiva Eiger, siman 129, answers that Maharam follows the position of Tosafot in Bava Metzia 71b that even a transfer through another may be treated as agency, and he reads Tosafot in Gittin 13b similarly. Ritva, cited in Shitah Mekubetzet to Bava Metzia there, disagrees with Tosafot, and Rabbi Akiva Eiger therefore leaves the practical law requiring study.
