For example, if he is a person, etc. Nowadays, when the movable property of orphans is subject to their father's debts, a creditor cannot seize property from a person who is dangerously ill merely to prevent it from becoming movable property in the hands of the heirs, because the property will remain encumbered in any event. If there is a concern that the heirs will destroy, sell, or conceal the movable property, I wrote in Tumim that the issue depends upon the dispute between Rif and Rosh. According to Rif, who understands the right of seizure as a special enactment, one cannot extend it to property of heirs because that would be one enactment built upon another. According to Rosh, who considers the right of seizure to be strict law, one may seize from property destined for the heirs.
