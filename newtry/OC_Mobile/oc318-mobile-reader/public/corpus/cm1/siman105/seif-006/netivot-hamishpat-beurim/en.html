The text should read: There it is different, because at the time the sender gave the money to the agent he knew, and so forth, whereas here the bailee does not know, and so forth. This is also the reading in Urim VeTumim. In my opinion there is no difficulty. Tumim writes that a borrower who pays a debt owed by his creditor in an amount equal to his own loan is nevertheless not discharged from his debt. He must pay his creditor, and then he may demand from the creditor what he paid on his behalf. The rule is that each collects from the other. The Shach in siman 391, seif 2, cites the Rosh in the first chapter of HaGozel regarding Ravina's interpretation of a monkey dyeing wool with dyes. The Rosh writes that only where a person's body benefits through another, or his property benefits from another through an act of his own property, such as an animal eating from the side of the public square or falling into a garden, does he pay for the benefit. But where his property benefits through the act of another, he is exempt. This is the first answer of Tosafot there on 100a. It follows that if a third party stuffs another person's food into his animal's mouth, the owner is exempt from paying for the benefit. Maharshal likewise writes that one who stuffs food into another's animal's mouth is not liable for the benefit. The Shach nevertheless concludes that this refers to food belonging to a third person, so the animal's owner is exempt to the food's owner. But if the person who stuffed the food used his own food, the animal's owner must pay him, for this is no worse than one who enters another's property and improves it without authorization, where he is compensated because he intended to improve the other's property. According to this, even for an authority who holds that one who pays another's debt must be reimbursed, or in a case where he held the debtor's property and therefore all agree that reimbursement is due, the basis is necessarily that he removed a lien from his fellow's property and thereby benefited that property. It follows that if Reuven owes Shimon and Levi pays Reuven's debt to Shimon using Yehudah's money, Reuven is not obligated to reimburse Yehudah, because Yehudah did not intend to improve Reuven's property, just as where one stuffs another person's food into another's animal. The distinction here is therefore clear. In our siman 105, the bailee intended to pay the depositor's debt using his own property, since he himself was obligated to pay, and therefore the one whose liability he discharged must reimburse him. In siman 128, however, Reuven lent the money to Levi and intended to pay his own debt and benefit his own property, while Shimon took Reuven's money that was held by him as a deposit and used it to pay Levi's debt. Reuven therefore cannot demand from Levi the value by which Shimon benefited Levi's property using Reuven's money. This appears correct.
