He is responsible for it. Sema writes that if the creditor later sues his own debtor, the debtor cannot dismiss him by saying, "That person already seized property for you." The creditor can answer, "What do I have to do with him? Who authorized him to act for me? I am suing you, my debtor. You may afterward sue the person who seized." Bach disagrees. Since the case concerns potential loss to the creditor and the right of seizure exists only by special enactment, Bach says the enactment should also release the debtor through the seizure. The language of Baal HaIttur somewhat supports Bach, but as practical law Sema is primary. Where do we find such an enactment in the Gemara, and how can we create one ourselves?
And some say he is not responsible, etc. This is derived from Rosh, who writes that the person from whom property was seized may tell the seizer, "Either expressly accept responsibility or return what you took." This implies that absent an express acceptance, he is not automatically responsible. Otherwise, what need would there be to demand an express undertaking? See Tumim. I wrote that this second opinion appears primary, and that Rambam and Baal HaTerumot also agree, unlike Bach, who ruled primarily like Baal HaIttur.
Express responsibility. This is Mechaber's view in Bedek HaBayit, as the Shach explains. According to Mechaber, Rosh agrees with Baal HaIttur that the seizer becomes responsible, but the person from whom the property was taken may nevertheless demand that he expressly accept responsibility. That is why Mechaber did not introduce the second opinion with "some say," because in his view it is not truly a separate opinion. Rama disagrees and understands Rosh as disputing Baal HaIttur.
