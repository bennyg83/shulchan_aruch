It nevertheless appears that when the creditor arrives, he may sue the debtor for payment, because the creditor has no claim against the seizer unless the seizure was made as the creditor's agent. The debtor then recovers from the seizer whatever was taken from him and for which he was held responsible. The Ir Shushan rules likewise. See the Perishah.
Some say that he is not liable for it. The Tur infers this from the Rosh's wording: if the debtor says to the seizer, "Accept responsibility for it, or return what you seized from me," we listen to him. This implies that absent such a demand the seizer does not automatically bear responsibility. This also shows that the word "if" in the Mechaber's following sentence should be connected with this dissenting view. The text had an evident omission, and Rama supplied it by inserting "and some say, etc.
