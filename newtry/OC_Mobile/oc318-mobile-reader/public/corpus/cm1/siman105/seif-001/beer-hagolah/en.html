Tur. Rosh writes this at the beginning of Bava Metzia, and the Maggid Mishneh likewise writes there that they proved it from the incident beginning with Yemar bar Chashu, contrary to Rashi. Hagahot Maimoniyot writes likewise there in the name of Tosafot, as do Ran at the beginning of Gittin and Rabbenu Yerucham, Netiv 7, part 7. On this basis Maharam ruled concerning Reuven, who sent an agent to acquire wine for him from a gentile in payment of a debt. The agent did so, performed meshichah, placed the wine on a wagon, and when he brought it to the city gave it to Shimon, who was likewise owed money. Reuven claimed the wine from Shimon. Maharam ruled that there is no agency for a gentile; additionally, the agent was one who seized for a creditor where others would thereby suffer loss; and furthermore, perhaps we rule like Rabbi Shimon that a Jew acquires from a gentile not by meshichah but only by money. Shimon is also not deemed wicked under the rule of a poor person pursuing a cake, since the gentile owed him money as well. We also rule that where a later creditor precedes and collects movable property, what he collected remains his. Rabbenu Yerucham, Netiv 22; Sema.
Tur. Rosh likewise writes there in the name of Rabbenu Chananel.
