Even if he appointed him as an agent, etc. The authorities reject Rashi's apparent view that an agent may seize for a creditor where the seizure harms others. The Bach's attempt to distinguish an agent holding formal authorization is unconvincing, because authorization merely defeats the debtor's objection that the agent is not his opposing party. Here the debtor admittedly owes the creditor, so that objection is irrelevant. Nor does appointment before witnesses change the rule. Rashi can, however, be understood differently. A mere verbal instruction does not make the person the creditor's hand for seizure. Rashi means one hired specifically to seize. The Gemara distinguishes a hired worker because his hand is treated as the employer's hand. Although an ordinary worker is not owned like a slave, one hired to gather found objects acts as the employer's designated hand. Thus a person hired specifically to seize from a debtor acquires for the creditor even where other creditors are harmed. The episode of Yemar bar Chashu involved only an ordinary agent, not one hired for that purpose. The other authorities likely agree with this practical distinction, and it is clearly implied by the Gemara's challenge from the law of a worker.
