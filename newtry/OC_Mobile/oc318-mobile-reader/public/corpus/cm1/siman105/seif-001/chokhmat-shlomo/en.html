Seif 1: Even if he appointed him as an agent. Note: Rashi holds that where a creditor appoints another person as his agent, the agent may seize property on his behalf. See, however, the sugya in Bava Kamma concerning the rule that we do not write an authorization to litigate over movable property, where Rashi appears to explain that even an appointed agent remains one who seizes for a creditor and his seizure is ineffective. Rashi’s comments therefore seem contradictory. It is difficult to distinguish by saying that Rashi allows seizure only where the principal expressly appointed the agent to seize, while if he merely appointed him to collect and did not specifically authorize seizure, Rashi agrees that seizure is ineffective. That distinction is strained. See my fifth-edition responsa to Yoreh Deah, Hilchot Bekhor, siman 77, in a responsum to the community of Skala, where with Hashem’s help I reconciled Rashi’s position properly. Regarding the general law of seizing for a creditor, see also my seventh-edition responsa to Even HaEzer, siman 59, where I discussed matters relevant here incidentally. The ruling stated here, “even if he appointed him as an agent,” follows Rashi’s view. See again my fifth-edition work on Yoreh Deah for my resolution of his position.
