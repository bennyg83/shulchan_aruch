Even if he appointed him as agent. Rabbi Aharon Sasson holds in responsum that the possessor can say he is certain like Rashi that appointment as agent makes the seizure effective. The Shach distinguishes whether the agent was paid. In Tumim I concluded that there is no such distinction. The seizure never transfers title, and even Rashi's suggestion appears only in the preliminary discussion, not in the final conclusion. I further concluded from the unqualified language of the authorities that even if the agent is already employed by the creditor and seizes property from the debtor, the seizure does not help for the creditor's debt, because the agent himself has no claim against the debtor. Authorization. Bach holds that seizure under a formal authorization is effective. The Shach disagrees. In Tumim I wrote that Bach's view appears correct where the authorization is legally effective by strict law, as explained below in siman 122. But where the authorization itself has no strict legal force and is recognized only because of a rabbinic enactment, it has the same law as ordinary agency and does not improve the seizure.
