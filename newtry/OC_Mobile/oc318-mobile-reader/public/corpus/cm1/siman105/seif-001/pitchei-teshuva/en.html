And he does not have enough to pay them all. Responsa Mishkenot Yaakov, siman 39, discusses money that beit din attached in the hands of a third party for one creditor and then delivered to a court-appointed stakeholder. Other creditors subsequently appeared and demanded a share, while the first creditor argued that the court agent had already acquired the money for him and that the others must litigate separately and seek other assets. Mishkenot Yaakov rules that all have equal rights in the attached money. First, the court agent never intended to acquire it for the first creditor absolutely; he held it only so that, if the creditor prevailed, it would be paid to him by order of beit din. Second, an agent who seizes for one creditor where this prejudices other creditors cannot acquire for him. The fact that the others might ultimately recover through litigation and effort does not remove the prejudice. The Tur and Shulchan Aruch's phrase that the debtor "does not have enough to pay all the debts" does not necessarily mean that he has literally no other assets. It includes a situation where collection from the other assets is difficult or requires significant effort. If the others could collect easily elsewhere, they would have no reason to demand this fund. But where collection elsewhere entails meaningful difficulty, the seizure is considered prejudicial to them. The attached money therefore belongs proportionately to all the creditors.
