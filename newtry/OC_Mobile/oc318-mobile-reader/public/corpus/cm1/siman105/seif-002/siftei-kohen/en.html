Through a migo that, had he wished, he could have acquired for himself. The Sema, Bach, and Masat Binyamin disagree with the Beit Yosef. I formerly agreed with them, but careful study of the opening chapter of Bava Metzia indicates that the Beit Yosef is correct. Once the person has a genuine right of acquisition through this act of lifting, he may acquire for another even more than he could acquire for himself. This does not depend upon an ordinary migo. Even authorities who reject the formula that one who could acquire for himself may acquire for another agree that when he actually acquires some portion for himself, the same act may acquire the remaining portion for his companion. This follows from the cases of two people jointly lifting a garment. Each can personally acquire only one half, yet each act of lifting also acquires the other half for the companion. Masat Binyamin's proof from seizure of an animal's lead is not persuasive, because there the person acquires only through physical possession of what he holds, not through an act of lifting that can operate for another. The Mordechai likewise proves the opposite of what was claimed, for it teaches that one may sometimes seize for another even without seeking any share for himself.
