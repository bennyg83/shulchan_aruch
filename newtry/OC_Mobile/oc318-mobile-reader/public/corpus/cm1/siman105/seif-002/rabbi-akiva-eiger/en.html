[Shulchan Aruch] Even if he appointed him as an agent and wrote. Note: It follows that if the person who seized the property places it in the creditor’s courtyard, the creditor does not thereby acquire it. Since a courtyard acquires by virtue of agency, this is merely comparable to an agent who transferred the property to another agent, except where the creditor stands beside his field, in which case the courtyard functions as his hand, as explained in the first chapter of Bava Metzia. According to Nimukei Yosef there, however, a guarded courtyard, with respect to something that is beneficial to its owner, functions as an extended hand. See Shach below, siman 243, seif katan 10. Accordingly, if the person who seized the property places it in the creditor’s guarded courtyard, the creditor acquires it. But if he places it in an unguarded courtyard and the creditor is not standing beside his field, then even according to the opinion cited below in siman 200, acquisition is only by virtue of agency and is ineffective here. Examine this carefully. The Gaon, author of Beit Meir, replied to me that one may say the underlying rule that one who seizes for a creditor where others are harmed does not acquire is analogous to the rule that there is no agency for a transgression, because he harms others. That rationale does not apply to a courtyard.
