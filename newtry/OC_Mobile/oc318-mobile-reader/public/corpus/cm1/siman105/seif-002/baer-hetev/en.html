For the one who seizes. The Sema writes that this applies only where he seized an amount equal to his own debt. For example, if each creditor is owed one hundred gold coins and he seized property worth one hundred, then not only where he seized fifty for himself and fifty for his fellow do we apply the migo that he could have seized for himself, but even if he seized the entire one hundred solely for his fellow, the migo applies because he himself was entitled to seize that entire amount. If, however, he seized one hundred fifty and seeks to acquire the excess for his fellow, it is ineffective, since he could not have acquired the entire amount for himself. The Sema proves this from the law of one who lifts a found object for another, whose acquisition is effective because he could have acquired the entire object for himself. This is unlike the Beit Yosef and Levush, who hold that any possibility of acquisition for oneself is enough. The Shach notes that Responsa Masat Binyamin also challenges the Beit Yosef and Levush, but the Shach rejects those objections and agrees with the Beit Yosef and Levush. See also Responsa Maharashdam, siman 381.
