Nimukei Yosef explains that although the person acquiring did not intend to acquire, and we rule that one who hoes the property of a convert thinking it is his own does not acquire it, here the case differs because another person's intent is transferring it to him. Ketzot HaChoshen uses this to answer Tosafot's question in Bava Batra 41 on Rav Nachman, who rules that the land returns, although Rav Nachman elsewhere holds that a monetary waiver is effective. Ketzot cites Nimukei Yosef in chapter Eizehu Neshech that such waiver works like despair and ownerless abandonment, so there is no transferring mind. The recipient therefore does not acquire because he did not know of the abandonment. This does not appear correct. If the property is truly ownerless and a third person who acquires first would obtain it, then at least once the intended recipient learns of the abandonment, if he performs an act of acquisition before the former owner reacquires it, he should certainly acquire. Why then did Rav Nachman say categorically that the land returns? He should have said that whoever first acquires it wins. His statement that the land returns implies that even if the person in possession performs an acquisition before the waiving party retakes possession, it does not help. Evidently, mistaken waiver is not a waiver or abandonment at all. Tosafot therefore correctly ask from Rav Nachman's other rulings that waiver and abandonment are effective. Moreover, can it really be suggested that if one knowingly encroaches on another's boundary while the person assisting him is unaware of his own ownership, the encroacher acquires because of mistaken waiver? The primary rule is as the Rosh writes. Where the owner knows that he is giving up something that is his but errs only in the law, such as a seller of something not yet in existence, so that the other party initially received it permissibly, despair can be effective. Here, however, the assisting party does not know at all that he has lost anything. This is like unknowing despair, and because the property came to the recipient while still prohibited to him, he does not acquire.
