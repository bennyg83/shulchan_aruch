And according to Rambam. Rambam follows his teacher Ri Migash, who explains Rabbi Shimon's teaching in Bava Batra as concerning damage that occurs automatically, for which no presumption is created, as explained in siman 154:8. Rosh records additional resolutions.
Once he knows. This is his general rule regarding possessory use. His proof is Bava Batra 6b: if the neighbor joined the wall with mortar, possession is established immediately. The statement on 41a that conduct in the owner's presence establishes immediate possession concerns a case where the owner did not initially know, yet Rabbi Yishmael son of Rabbi Yose still treats it as immediate. Rabbi Chiya's incident likewise supports this. The Jerusalem Talmud says that even if the owner stood there, he might claim that he was occupied with his studies and did not notice the opening. Although it later says that helping does not create possession, we do not follow that position because the Babylonian Talmud says, 'he waived by coming and assisting.' One cannot explain the Jerusalem Talmud as holding that visual intrusion never creates possession, because the final mishna concerning an Egyptian window proves otherwise. The first opinion therefore understands that window as serving another purpose, not observation. See citation 80.
