The claimant lifted a basket of produce from this field onto his shoulder. The Gemara says, 'he lifted for him baskets of produce.' Rashbam explains that the claimant lifted a basket for the possessor onto his shoulder. Rambam, chapter 11 of Hilchot To'en, and the Tur write instead that the claimant helped him gather the produce one item at a time, lifted it from the ground, and placed it into a vessel. It appears they do not disagree; each states one illustration, and both depend on the same reasoning. The Mechaber chose one, but the same law applies to the other. See the Derishah.
To bring them to his house. This is Rashbam's wording. The Tur writes, 'and he brought them,' with the conjunction. In the Derishah I wrote that they do not disagree, but each follows his own explanation above. According to Rashbam, when the claimant raises the basket onto the possessor's shoulder, it must be apparent that he is helping the possessor carry the produce to the possessor's house. Otherwise there is no indication of chazakah, for perhaps the claimant intended that the possessor carry it to the claimant's own house. According to the Tur, where the claimant himself gathered the produce for the possessor, it need only be apparent at the time of gathering that the produce is for the possessor, and no additional declaration concerning transport to his house is required. According to all opinions, once the possessor carries the basket in the direction of his own home, that is sufficient.
From the produce that he is eating. This is the correct reading, unlike editions that read 'that he ate.' The phrase is absent from the Tur.
He would not purchase it from Shimon for one year. Mordechai concludes there: although a person may purchase his own legal peace, that applies when by doing so he ends all disputes and will not have to complain again. A person does not buy only the ear of the camel, so to speak. Meaning, he would not buy the field for one year only to have to litigate and compromise again afterward. This is why Rama carefully writes here that he would not purchase it for a year. What he concludes generally, 'the same applies if he rented it from him,' and likewise writes below at the end of siman 147:2, is different. Even if he rented it yearly for a gold coin for as long as the other wished, he did not purchase complete peace concerning title. He would still have to litigate. Therefore the Sages said a person may purchase his legal peace, not rent his legal peace.
If the claimant says, 'I placed him there to take the produce.' This is stated in the Gemara. The same rule applies according to the opinion in the gloss that the possessor sent produce to the claimant. If the claimant says, 'I placed him there for the produce,' he is believed. The Tur states this explicitly.
But if he raises that claim after three years, etc., we do not listen to him. Meaning, even where there is no weakness arising from his having lifted the produce for him. See the Perishah.
