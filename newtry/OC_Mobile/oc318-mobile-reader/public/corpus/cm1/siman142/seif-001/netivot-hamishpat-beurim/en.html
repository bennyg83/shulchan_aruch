But if the challenger himself admits that he assisted the possessor and nevertheless claims that he never sold him the property, the challenger is certainly believed if he gives a satisfactory explanation. Although where witnesses testify that he assisted, he is not believed by a migo that he could say, "You descended only for the produce," because that would be a migo against witnesses, as Tosafot write in Bava Batra 38, here the matter is stronger because his own mouth is the source of the admission. The principle of peh she'asar is stronger than an ordinary migo, as Tosafot write in Bava Kamma 72, s.v. ein lecha bo. Nevertheless, he must state before beit din a recognizable reason explaining why he assisted, such as that he acted under compulsion or the like. His assistance itself constitutes an admission and therefore requires an explanation just as an express admission does under siman 79, seif 5. This also explains the difference between the two seifim. In seif 2 it is effective when the assisting party himself admits that he knew Reuven was taking possession, whereas seif 1 speaks specifically of witnesses. In the first case, absent witnesses, he can give an explanation for his assistance and be believed through his own admission. In seif 2, where the relevant fact cannot be established by witnesses in the same manner, the Mechaber teaches that if he admits it and supplies no explanation, the assistance is treated as an admission. The further novelty is that even if the purchaser did not intend to acquire through the act of chazakah that he performed, and the case resembles one who digs in the property of a convert thinking it is his own, who does not acquire, nevertheless where the transferor knew and another person's intent is effecting the acquisition for him, the acquisition can take effect, as the Ramban and Nimukei Yosef explain.
