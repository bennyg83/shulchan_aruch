If the challenger claims, I placed him there only for the produce. It appears that if the challenger dies, beit din makes this claim for his heirs. We make for an heir every claim his father could have made, and this is a common occurrence. Tosafos, Bava Batra 35b, asks that the challenger should be believed by a migo that he could have said, "I placed him there for the produce." Such a migo would not be available for an uncommon claim, showing that this claim itself is considered common. Logic likewise supports that conclusion. Therefore beit din makes this claim for heirs. This is straightforward.
