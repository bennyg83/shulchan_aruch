Seif 2. (And it constitutes a prescriptive right.) If partners own land jointly and one of them builds upon one half in the presence of the other, even though they had a written agreement that neither would build there, the other's silence may sometimes create a presumption that he waived objection. Nevertheless, the entire remaining half belongs to the other partner, whether it is superior or inferior to the portion built upon. One cannot say that the silent partner waived half while the other half somehow remains jointly owned. The builder also does not thereby acquire a right to lean a later structure upon the wall he constructed if doing so would diminish the other partner's portion. If the other partner says that the written agreement was not in his possession at the time of construction and that he learned of his right to object only when the document later came into his hands, his silence need not prove waiver. Matters written in a document are often not fixed in a person's active memory, because he relies upon the writing to remind him later. He may therefore say that he was uncertain what the document provided and did not wish to quarrel with his neighbor while the neighbor was actively building, especially because the builder's conduct suggested confidence that he was entitled to build. The same can be true if the document remained among movable papers and did not come to hand from the time construction began until later. Even without the writing, one partner cannot simply choose an arbitrary half where the portions differ in quality, because division must be made fairly. But he may have been willing to overlook that question while not waiving an express written prohibition against either party building at all. If the parties later entered a compromise by kinyan sudar concerning construction, but did so in ignorance of the document, the compromise may be a mistaken compromise insofar as it dealt with division of the land rather than the separate contractual prohibition. If they expressly compromised even in the event that such a document would later be found, the compromise remains effective despite the prior uncertainty.
