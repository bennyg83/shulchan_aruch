Sema, citing Darkei Moshe, explains: although we say that a person may pay money to settle his dispute, meaning to avoid having to contest it further, a person does not buy only the ears of a camel. In other words, he would not purchase peace for only one year and then, after that year, have to return to litigation and compromise again. The same applies to renting the disputed right: when a person pays to settle his dispute, he seeks a permanent resolution, not merely to rent the settlement and return later to litigation.
