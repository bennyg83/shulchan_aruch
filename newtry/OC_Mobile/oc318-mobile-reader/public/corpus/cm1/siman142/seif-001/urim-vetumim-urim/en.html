He lifted. Rashbam explains the case this way, while the Rambam and Tur write that the owner helped him gather the produce one by one, lifted it from the ground, and placed it into a vessel. It appears that they do not truly disagree. Each describes one valid form of assistance, and the Mechaber simply mentioned one of them. See Sema.
He would not have purchased, etc. The Mordechai concludes that although a person will sometimes pay to settle litigation and avoid further legal dispute, this applies only where he thereby disposes of the entire dispute so that he will not need to litigate again. A person does not ordinarily purchase only temporary peace and remain exposed to renewed litigation. This is the meaning of Maharam's precise wording here that he would not have purchased it 'for one year.' See Darchei Moshe and Sema; this is straightforward.
The same applies if he rented it, etc. The Mechaber writes this unqualifiedly here and below in siman 147, seif 2. Renting is different from buying. Even if he rented the property from the protester every year for, say, one gold coin as long as the protester wished, he did not thereby settle all legal claims by making the property permanently his. He would still need to litigate again in the future. Therefore the principle that a person may pay to settle litigation does not mean that he will continue renting merely to settle litigation. See Sema. See Taz for his comments.
I placed him there for the produce, etc. See Tumim, where I concluded as practical law that if the owner died, beit din advances for his heirs the claim that he had placed the possessor there merely to take the produce. See there for the reasoning.
We do not listen, etc. Meaning, even where there is no adverse circumstance that the owner helped him lift the produce. See Sema. The Shach writes that this resolves the Tur's apparently repetitive wording, 'until after three years,' followed by, 'this applies within three years.' See there; this is correct.
