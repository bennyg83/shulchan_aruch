He lifted a basket of fruit. It must be apparent that the challenger did so for the possessor, so that the fruit would be taken to the possessor's house. The same applies if the challenger helped gather the fruit into the possessor's vessel. Sema. See Beurim: this helps specifically where there are witnesses that the challenger assisted. If the challenger himself admits that he helped, he remains believed to say that he nevertheless never sold the field, provided he gives a recognizable explanation to beit din why he assisted. Since the disqualifying fact comes only from his own admission, he is believed to explain it. But where witnesses establish the assistance, he is not believed through a migo that he entered merely to take the produce, because that would be a migo against witnesses.
He would not have purchased it from Shimon. Although a person sometimes buys his way out of litigation, as explained below in siman 146:18, that applies where he acquires his entire peace from the dispute. Here he bought only one year's right, after which he would again have to litigate. A person does not ordinarily rent his way out of litigation merely for a temporary period. Sema, unlike the Taz.
