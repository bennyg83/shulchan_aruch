Benefit. The buyer causes the seller no loss by eating the item or feeding it to dogs, because the seller himself is forbidden to benefit from it. Therefore the seller may not deduct from the purchase money the value of what the buyer consumed. Even if the buyer sold it to a non-Jew and received money, if the prohibited item's value transfers to the proceeds, those proceeds are themselves forbidden in the buyer's hands. Even where the value does not transfer to the proceeds, such as chametz on Pesach, orlah, and kilay ha-kerem, concerning which the Mishnah teaches that if one sold them and betrothed a woman with the proceeds the betrothal is valid, that leniency applies to others. The seller himself, who is a party to the transaction, is forbidden to sell the item, and if he did so he was penalized and may not benefit from the proceeds. This is the Sema's ruling. Rashi in the chapter Rabbi Yishmael and Tosafot at the beginning of the final chapter of Avodah Zarah write similarly, as do Rashi and Tosafot in Chullin concerning the chametz of habitual transgressors. This is the primary ruling, unlike Maadanei Melech in Bechorot. See Orach Chaim, siman 450, and the Bach there. Shach.
