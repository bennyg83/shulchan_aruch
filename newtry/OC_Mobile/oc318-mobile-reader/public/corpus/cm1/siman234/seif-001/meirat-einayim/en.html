It shall be buried and he shall return, etc. In the Perishah I explained that the purchaser himself must bury the prohibited item and may not return it to the seller to bury, because the seller is not trusted. He might sell it to a gentile and derive benefit from it, although it is prohibited in benefit. This differs from the next seif concerning tereifah meat, which may be sold to a gentile. There the remaining meat is returned to the seller, and we do not suspect that he will eat it. A person suspected of the lesser prohibition of placing a stumbling block before another is not thereby suspected of the more severe prohibition of eating forbidden meat. Nor need we fear that he will resell it to a Jew for profit, because a butcher from whose hand such a serious failure occurred is removed and publicly announced, so that people will not buy from him, as explained in Yoreh Deah.
