And he would be held liable by it. This does not mean that beit din already entered judgment against him. It means that the legal consequence of his first plea would be liability in beit din. Sema and Shach.
He cannot make another plea. This means after the brief interval allowed for retracting one's words. Within that interval he may retract in every case. Shach, and this is clear.
Even if there are witnesses, etc. This is like the earlier case where he said, "I never borrowed," witnesses then testified that he borrowed and repaid, and he cannot afterward rely on their statement of repayment. The Sema asks that above the reason was that he was established as a false denier. There is no difficulty. As to his later claim of payment, we do not need that additional reason. His own admission is stronger than witnesses, and one who says, "I never borrowed," has thereby said, "I did not repay." This is Raavad's objection to the Rambam. See what I wrote above in siman 79, Tumim seif katan 2.
But if he left beit din. The Shach holds that this restriction applies only if he had already been formally held liable by beit din. If judgment had not yet been entered, even though his first plea was legally sufficient to create liability, the concern that someone taught him a false new plea does not apply. In Tumim I proved from several sources that this is incorrect. Once he has left beit din, he cannot change his plea in either circumstance. See there.
We do not listen to him. See Tumim. Even if his later explanation gives the plain and natural meaning of his original words, rather than a strained or remote construction, he still may not alter his plea after leaving beit din. This is unlike Rivash.
From exemption to exemption. He may alter one exempting plea to another exempting plea, but not a plea that creates liability into one that creates exemption. If his first plea required him to swear and the second would exempt him from an oath, he cannot use the second and remains obligated to swear. Likewise, if under the first plea the opposing party would swear and collect, while under the second plea he himself would swear and be exempt, the law follows the first plea and the opposing party swears and collects. So concludes the Shach, seif katan 5, correctly.
But within, etc. The relevant interval is measured from his own statement, not from the opposing litigant's later statement. Although in testimony the second witness's words can be considered within the permissible interval of the first witness because the two pieces of testimony form one unit, the words of one litigant and those of his opponent are not one unit. Therefore only the interval following his own words counts. Rashba.
Unlike those who disagree. See Tumim. I wrote that the view of those who disagree appears primary. Their ruling concerns the period before the litigants leave beit din, whereas Mahari Weil concerns a case after they left. The authorities therefore may not disagree at all. See there at length, where I brought proof from the Gemara.
