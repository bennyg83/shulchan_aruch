When does this apply... As in the incident there and as stated above in siman 79, seif 9. See there. At the end of the first chapter of Bava Metzia the Gemara asks, "What are documents of clarification?" and answers, "documents recording the claims." Nimukei Yosef there holds that the claims were written so the parties could not retract them. Before they were written, a litigant could change from one exempting claim to another, for example, from "I never borrowed" to "I borrowed and repaid," etc.

One cannot say that the purpose is to teach that the halachah follows Abaye, for that is already clear from the Gemara's statement, "Abaye said to him," as Rashbam explains there. See also 33b. And in all this there is no distinction... This follows from Kesubos 94a: "Here he says, had..." and "we said this only..." This shows that a litigant cannot formulate a new claim after another person came and made his claim, because his silence is like an admission. Certainly this is so before beit din.
Unless he gives a reason. A plausible explanation is believed even after he first made a different claim, as above.
Contrary to those who disagree... Their proof is from the rule that one may change one claim to another, and they argue that all the more so in this case. This is incorrect. It applies specifically when he gives a plausible explanation, analogous to the rule of changing one's claim, as above, "unless...".
