He cannot advance another claim. This means after the time of immediate retraction has passed. Within that time he may retract in every case. Shach:
Even if there are witnesses. This is like the case above where he said, "I never borrowed," and witnesses then came and testified that he borrowed and repaid. He cannot then say, "I repaid," and he must pay. The Sema asks that above the reason given was that he became established as a denier, and he struggles to resolve this. There is no difficulty. As to the later claim, "I repaid," we no longer need the reason that he is established as a denier. His own admission is stronger than witness testimony, and one who says, "I never borrowed," is regarded as having said, "I did not repay":
But he left beit din. The Shach holds that this applies specifically if he had already been found liable in beit din. If judgment had not yet been rendered, even though his claim was one that would have caused liability in beit din, the rationale that someone taught him a false claim does not apply:
From exemption to exemption. But not from liability to exemption. If under his first claim he would have been liable to an oath and under the second he would be exempt from an oath, he cannot change his claim and remains liable to the oath. Likewise, if under his first claim the opposing party would swear and collect, whereas under the second he himself would swear and be exempt, the law follows the first claim and the opposing party swears and collects. So the Shach concludes in seif katan 5, and this is correct:
But within the time of immediate retraction. The retraction must be within the interval following his own words, not the words of the other party. Although with testimony, the interval following the second witness is considered immediate with respect to the first, all witness testimony is treated as one unit. The words of this litigant and those of the opposing litigant are not one unit. Therefore, the interval must follow his own words specifically. Rashba:
