Under what circumstances may a person make one claim and then make another? It contains 2 se'ifim.
If a person made one claim in beit din and became liable through it, he may not make another claim that contradicts the first. {Rama: Even witnesses supporting the second claim do not help, as explained at the beginning of siman 79.} But if he comes to clarify the first claim and says, 'This is what I intended,' and its language can bear that clarification, he is heard. When does this apply? When he has not left beit din. If he left beit din, he is not heard, lest others have taught him to make a false claim. Anything he claimed outside beit din, however, he may retract and replace, even by contradicting the first claim, because a person reveals his claims only to beit din. The rule that he may not contradict his first claim applies when he became liable under that claim. If he could prevail even under the first claim, he may retract it and prevail under the other claim even without explaining the first. This is stated at the end of chapter 7 of the Laws of Claimants. Even if he left beit din and returned, he may replace any exculpatory claim with another exculpatory claim until witnesses arrive; see above, siman 79, seif 9. Once witnesses arrive and contradict the first claim upon which he relied, he cannot divert it to another claim unless he gives an explanation for the original claim whose meaning can support the new claim, and only if he has not left beit din. {Rama: All this applies after the short interval called toch kedei dibbur. Within that interval, he may retract and contradict his first claim. See Mahariv 15. In all these cases there is no distinction between one who first made a claim and later seeks to make another, and one who initially remained silent and later seeks to make a claim, unless he explains why he was silent at first. See Responsa of the Rosh, rule 64, responsum 3, and Mahariv 77. This rejects the view that one who remained silent may always return and make a claim.}
