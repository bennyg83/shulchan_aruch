The wording of the Rambam at the end of chapter 3 of Hilkhot To'en VeNit'an.
As the Rashba explains his words in a responsum, cited by the Kesef Mishneh there and by the Beit Yosef: these rules were not said regarding a claim in which the claimant admits something to the defendant or obligates himself in beit din through that claim. One cannot first make a claim in beit din that creates liability and later change it into a claim of exemption. Rather, one may change only from one exempting claim to another exempting claim, etc. He discusses this at length. See there.
So appears in the Tur, but in the Rambam the text reads "the latter claim." In my humble opinion, the Rambam means the most recent claim after he has already raised several successive exempting claims. He writes that so long as his most recent claim has not been contradicted, he may still raise another exempting claim. The Tur, however, merely states that he may raise another claim and does not mention changing from exemption to exemption, and therefore had to write "the first claim."
See Responsa Rashba, siman 1060, that speaking immediately after another person's statement is not considered as part of that person's speech. The Sema writes that this differs from above at the beginning of siman 29, where testimony given immediately after another witness's words is effective, because there both witnesses are regarded as participating in one statement.
