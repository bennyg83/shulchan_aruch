The brothers... All three of these seifim are the language of Rambam, according to the explanation of his teacher, the Ri Migash; see there.
They divide according to. See the Maggid Mishneh.
And if he said... and they compel. As stated there regarding two properties, one by a canal, etc.
And some say. According to Rabbeinu Tam's explanation, s.v. "we raise its value."
And some say. Meaning that perhaps we rule like Rashi, who explains this specifically with a rain-fed field, but without any such claim they compel. See there and Hagahos Maimoniyos at the end of the laws of acquisition.
But if... As stated there in the case of land that one purchased along the boundary, etc. The Ri Migash explains that one side was superior and the other side inferior. Therefore they may say, "we raise its value for him..." If the superior side adjoined his property and he wishes to take one third, they may say that one third of the superior land is better than two thirds of the inferior land. Likewise, if the inferior side adjoined him and he wishes to take two thirds, they may say that two thirds of the inferior land are preferable to one third of the superior land.
Or near... There, "one side by a path..." Here "nigra" means a road, as in "a public path," and as stated in seif 3.
He said to him: Give me. There, as above, for whenever the other party suffers no loss, they compel against the attribute of Sodom. As stated there on 13b, "You take the required measure..." Even Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel disagrees only because the other says that it is a gift. Here, however, it is not a gift, because adjoining portions are more advantageous to me than the superior portion I am giving you, and the respective advantages offset one another.
There were. There, the case of two fields, etc., according to Tosafos' explanation there.
And specifically. Since the Gemara says, "In a case such as this, they compel..." it implies that by strict law they are to divide each field. Accordingly, where there is no issue of the attribute of Sodom, they divide each field. Rosh there.
