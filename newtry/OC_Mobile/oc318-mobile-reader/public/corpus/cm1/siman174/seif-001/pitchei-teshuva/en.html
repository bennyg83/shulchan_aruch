And some say that it remains a doubt. See Baer Heitev, who writes that initially they should cast lots, and so forth. See Responsa Shav Yaakov, part 2, siman 167, concerning two partners who owned a cellar. One wanted his share of the cellar to be assigned adjacent to his house, while the other wanted to cast lots. He cites the words of the Shach here, discusses them somewhat, and concludes: In our case they should cast lots. If the lot falls to the second party, he still does not acquire the disputed advantage absolutely, and the rule would technically remain that whoever is stronger prevails. Nevertheless, my heart tells me that in these times, in exile, there are many concerns with ruling that whoever is stronger prevails. The reason given for that rule, that the person who truly owns the property will endanger himself more readily for it, also does not apply in our generations, for violent people have become numerous and will endanger themselves even for money that is not theirs. Therefore, where division is otherwise unavoidable, as in our case, and the dispute among the authorities concerns only the manner in which the division should be made, they should divide according to the lottery.
