The language of Rambam at the beginning of chapter 12 of Hilkhot Shekhenim. Maggid Mishneh cites the case in HaShutafin, Bava Batra 12b, where both portions lie on one irrigation channel and Rav Yosef says that in such a case one compels against the trait of Sodom. Maggid Mishneh writes that it appears even with two separate fields the same principle applies.
Maggid Mishneh writes that this too follows Rav Yosef there. Know that the commentators have other approaches. Some explain that even where there is one field and all portions are objectively equal, one party may raise the valuation and say, "The portion adjacent to your boundary is worth more to me," etc. Rambam follows his teacher's approach. Kesef Mishneh discusses the challenge from the incident of one who purchased land, where the Gemara concludes that Rav Yosef's view is accepted because the others may say, "We value it more." In Yesh Nochalin the halachah is also ruled like Rav Yosef concerning a field of one and a half units, and Rashi identifies the field there with this case. One may answer that the incident involved two fields on two irrigation channels, or that Rambam did not have the textual reading "the halachah follows Rav Yosef." Kesef Mishneh discusses this at length.
Maggid Mishneh writes that Rambam's teacher ruled so and the later authorities agreed. Kesef Mishneh explains that from Rabbah's words there we learn that whenever one cannot say, "Some prefer this and others prefer that," beit din compels against the trait of Sodom.
Meaning, without a separate appraisal; this is Rambam's wording.
