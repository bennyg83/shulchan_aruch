As one. The Tur states: If there are two brothers and one is the firstborn, they divide their father's estate into three portions. The middle portion necessarily belongs to the firstborn in any event. They then cast lots over the two outer portions, and whichever one falls to the firstborn's lot he takes together with the adjacent middle portion, so that his two shares are contiguous. This is derived from the verse "a double portion," implying that his two portions are to be as one portion and one allotment. The Rambam maintains that even if the middle portion is better than the two outer portions, the firstborn nevertheless takes it. Otherwise, why would the verse be necessary?
