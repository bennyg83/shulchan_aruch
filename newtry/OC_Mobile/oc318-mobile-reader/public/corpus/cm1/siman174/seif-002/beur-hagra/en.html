Some say specifically. The Rosh writes this regarding two fields, consistently with his view that even an ordinary heir, where the portions are unequal, divides each field, and a firstborn's two portions are no better than an ordinary heir's single portion. The Tur inferred that the same applies to one field where one portion is superior. All this follows their approach. Ri Migash, however, writes that even if one third of superior land stands in the middle, the firstborn takes it in any event. The case cannot concern portions of equal quality, for why distinguish a firstborn and an ordinary heir? The same would apply to two ordinary heirs where one owns two portions, since the law follows Rav Yosef that we compel, etc., as stated above. Rava's statement regarding a yavam, 'You are like a firstborn,' therefore concerns this type of case. Where the portions are equal, however, even a yavam takes each portion adjoining the other, as stated above, because we compel. See Baer HaGolah, seif katan 4.
