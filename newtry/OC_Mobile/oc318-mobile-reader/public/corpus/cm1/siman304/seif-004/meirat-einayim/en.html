From here, you have learned from MKs before that in the U.S., when he has been married for years, he pays but crosses that he is easy for the other, and is not a complete criminal who is carrying a complete criminal and must pay the whole:
Although it is heavy for one person. Rama's brevity leaves room for error. The Tur disagrees and writes that this applies specifically where a heavy burden is divided between the two ends of a pole, half tied to each end, with the pole bent in the middle and that center resting on the carrier's shoulder. In such a configuration people commonly strain themselves to carry somewhat more than an ordinary one person load, so long as it does not reach the burden of two people. Therefore he pays only half. But if the entire burden is carried at one point on a pole, the Tur explicitly writes that he must pay all. Nor should Rama's words, "but some disagree," simply be read as Rashi's view cited by the Maggid Mishneh. Rashi's own explanation concerns loads attached at two separate places on the carrying implement. The Maggid Mishneh infers from Rashi that if the burden exceeds one person's normal load but is less than what requires two people, the carrier pays only half. But that inference still applies specifically where the load is distributed in the manner people sometimes carry alone. With one concentrated load, the Rosh requires full payment. Rama's unqualified formulation therefore requires study.
