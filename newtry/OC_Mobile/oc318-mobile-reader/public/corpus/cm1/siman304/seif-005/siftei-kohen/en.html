"The suffering that broke a barrel of wine," he said, "but in the return of the barrels of wine, he died in the dark," and he said, "And if he had not been able to sell him, then he would not be able to give him any more than he could."
But most commentators disagree, etc. In my view, the Rif, Rambam, Maggid Mishneh, and Mechaber refer specifically to a barrel broken on an ordinary day rather than on the market day. On that reading there is no practical dispute. Even if the Rosh disagrees with this interpretation, he would stand alone, and it is possible that he agrees with the law but read the Gemara differently. The Rama may mean only that most commentators disagree with the implication that there is never a distinction between the market day and other days. If it broke on the market day, they rule differently; if on another day, the Mechaber’s rule applies. This is the correct practical ruling.
