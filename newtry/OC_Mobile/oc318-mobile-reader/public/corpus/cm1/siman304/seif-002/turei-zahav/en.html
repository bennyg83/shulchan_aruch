(Seif 5) The porter, etc. The Rambam maintains that we follow the time of restitution. Thus, in every case the porter may return either a barrel of wine or its monetary value, but the amount of money differs. If he returns it on market day, he must return four, because it can then be sold for four. However, if the owner already had wine on that market day and could not have sold this additional barrel, the day is treated like any ordinary day and the porter need return only three. Most of the commentators disagree on two points, and this is the view recorded by the Rama. First, we do not determine whether he must return four or three by the time of restitution, but by the value at the time the barrel was broken. Since it was then worth four, even though the owner could not have sold it because he already had wine of his own, it was nevertheless worth four and could have been sold on another market day. Second, they disagree concerning when he may discharge his liability by returning a barrel of wine. If he broke it on an ordinary day, he may return either a barrel of wine or three. If he broke it on market day and returns it on a market day, even a later market day, he may return either a barrel of wine or four. But if he broke it on market day and returns it on an ordinary day, he must give four specifically, because the owner may say: At the time you broke it I could have sold it for four, and you are not discharged merely by returning a barrel. The same applies if, on the market day when he seeks to return it, the owner already has wine. That market day is then treated like an ordinary day, and the porter must give four rather than wine, since he broke it when its price was high and the owner already has wine available for sale.
