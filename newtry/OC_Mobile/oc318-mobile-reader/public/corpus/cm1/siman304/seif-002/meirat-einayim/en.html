Since the load, etc. See the Derishah, where I proved from the Maggid Mishneh's comments on Rambam that Rambam's reason and intent are as follows. Since this load cannot be carried by one person because of its weight, there is a status of negligence when one person attempts to carry it. Even though another person joined him and assisted in carrying it, since people do not ordinarily combine in this manner, it is treated as though one person carried it. Nevertheless, because the load is light enough for two, he is liable only for half. This appears correct. It is unlike the Ir Shushan, who explains Rambam to mean that because the load is heavy for each of them, each may have relied on the other to relieve his burden and thereby acted negligently. That explanation is incorrect, because the Maggid Mishneh concludes that the rationale he gave is evident from Rambam's second clause, which the Mechaber records below in seif 4. According to my explanation this fits well, because Rambam writes there that one who carries a load beyond his capacity is called negligent. The Ir Shushan's explanation, however, is not mentioned at all in Rambam, neither in the first clause nor in the second. Examine this carefully.
If there are witnesses that they were not negligent. This is explicit in the Gemara and the authorities. We rule like Isi ben Yehudah, who expounds the verse, "There is no witness; the oath of God shall be between them," to imply that where there are witnesses, the bailee does not become exempt through an oath but must bring witnesses. This was already explained by the Tur and from the words of the Mechaber above, simanim 294 and 296. See there.
