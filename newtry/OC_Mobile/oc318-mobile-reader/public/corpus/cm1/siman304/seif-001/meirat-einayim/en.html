For pay, and it broke, by Torah law he must pay, etc. The Mechaber copies the Rambam from chapter 3 of Hilchot Sechirut and does not distinguish between stumbling on a slope and on level ground. The Tur does distinguish. If one stumbles on level ground, he is negligent and is liable by strict law even as an unpaid custodian. He cannot be exempt by swearing that he was not negligent, because stumbling on level ground itself indicates negligence. Nevertheless, where it is not known that he acted deliberately, it is a mitzvah for the employer to act beyond the strict law, as it says, "that you may walk in the way of the good." If the workers are poor and have nothing to eat, there is also a mitzvah to pay their wages, as the continuation says, "and keep the paths of the righteous." Only when he stumbled on a slope is he not treated as negligent, and the rule is as the Mechaber writes. The Gemara at the end of HaOmanin appears to distinguish these cases. The Rif and Rosh, however, also appear not to distinguish, like the Rambam and Mechaber, because they omit that implication. In the Derishah I explained their reasoning.
