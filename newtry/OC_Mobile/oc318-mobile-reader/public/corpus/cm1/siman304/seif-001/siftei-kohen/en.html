For payment, and it broke. The Sema says the Gemara implies a distinction between a sloping place and level ground. In truth, the Gemara more strongly supports the Rambam and Mechaber. Rabbi Elazar questioned his teachers because, in his own reasoning, stumbling on level ground appeared negligent. His teachers and the anonymous first opinion of the Mishna, however, stated the exemption without qualification, showing that they did not regard even such stumbling as negligence. The Tosafot Yom Tov similarly explains that according to the Rif and Rosh, any accidental stumbling is not negligence, even on level ground. This is contrary to the Tur’s ruling that one who stumbles on level ground must pay. The unqualified exemption is the primary law.
