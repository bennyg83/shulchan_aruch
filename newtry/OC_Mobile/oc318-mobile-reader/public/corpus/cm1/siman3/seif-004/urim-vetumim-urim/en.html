Until he knows for what purpose he sits. Mahari Weil advised Rabbi Shimon not to sit with lay communal judges, because lay rulings and Torah rulings can be opposites. Sema.
One who is not an expert, etc. Sema explains "expert" here as one who has learned the law but lacks developed reasoning. Shach disagrees. See siman 25, where this subject belongs.
However, if the community accepted him, etc. The point is that one should not say such acceptance is meaningless merely because it was compelled by the need to satisfy a royal appointment. Bach holds that communal acceptance has effect all the more when there is no royal compulsion.
The king's authorization helps. Meaning, according to one view, that royal authorization can protect the judge from personal liability for an error in the same way as authorization from the Nasi or Exilarch. Bach reads the Tur differently, while Shach considers Rivash's reasoning correct. See Tumim, where I questioned Rivash's proof and found Bach's position more plausible. If the king commanded that no one else may adjudicate, Tashbetz and Knesset HaGedolah question whether another judge may lawfully sit. See Tumim, where I left the matter doubtful.
He causes distress to the public. This language comes from Rivash, and I am surprised the Rama did not also cite that Rabbeinu Tam, Rashbam, Raavan, and one hundred and fifty rabbis placed a severe ban upon one who takes communal authority through a secular ruler without being appointed by the majority of the community for his Torah stature. Maharam records the decree in very severe terms. See how great the punishment is for one who seizes rabbinic authority over a community through governmental power. Unfortunately this destructive affliction has spread in our time, causing communities to be ruined, batei midrash to decline, and Torah study to be weakened. The rabbis of the generation are obligated to create safeguards so that the breach should not exceed what remains standing. May Hashem grant me strength to help establish a needed correction and raise the banner of Torah.
