One who is not. Sanhedrin 6a.
(Anthology) One who is not, etc. See the source citations and the statement at the end of chapter 4 that this is like consecrating an object improperly. The Rambam further writes in chapter 3 that one who appoints an unfit judge is as though he erected a sacred pillar. His reason is that Scripture places the prohibitions against erecting a pillar and offering a blemished sacrifice next to the section concerning judges, just as the Sages expound at the beginning of Sanhedrin from the verse, 'You shall not plant for yourself an asherah.' A proof appears in the Midrash at the beginning of Shoftim, cited by Yalkut Shimoni, that on Solomon's throne a herald proclaimed these six prohibitions, including 'You shall not show favoritism' and 'You shall not sacrifice.' This must be understood as above. I found the same in Maharik, siman 117, who explains at length that 'You shall not plant' refers to a wicked judge, 'You shall not erect' to an ignorant judge, who resembles a silent stone, and 'You shall not sacrifice' to an appointment by the Nasi and a grant of authority that cannot validate an unfit judge, in accordance with the Rambam.

(Anthology) However, etc.; and some say, etc. See the Shach.
(Anthology) Authority, etc.; however, etc. A practical difference is stated in siman 8:1 in the gloss, concerning the community's acceptance of judges. The same applies to any community, as the Beit Yosef writes.
(Anthology) Some say that if, etc. This means that authority exempts him from liability if he errs. The Rivash writes this and the Beit Yosef cites him. Nowadays, however, there is no practical difference in that respect, as stated in seif 2 in the gloss. There remains a practical difference in the case stated in seif 1 in the gloss, beginning 'It appears to me, specifically.' This is also the meaning of the earlier statement concerning authority granted by another. The distinction is relevant here as well. This view holds that even one who has studied and understands the law does not benefit from such authority. The words 'some say' dispute this, because one who has not studied cannot benefit even from authority granted by the Nasi, as the Shulchan Aruch writes. The Rama derived authority for this purpose from authority that exempts a judge from liability, and therefore concludes his gloss on the Shulchan Aruch's ruling in this manner.
(Anthology) Authority, etc. A practical difference concerns three lay judges who erred, as stated above in siman 25:2 in the gloss. The words 'however, if' mean that in such a case they are certainly exempt, since they are treated like an expert whom the litigants accepted. The statement 'some say that if' likewise follows the Rivash, who says that the practical difference is exemption from liability, and nowadays applies to three judges, as explained.
