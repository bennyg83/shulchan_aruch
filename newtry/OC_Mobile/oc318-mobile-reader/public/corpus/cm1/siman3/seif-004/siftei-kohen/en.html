The king's authorization is effective for him. It is effective even to exempt him from payment if he erred, like authorization from the Exilarch discussed below in siman 25, seif 2. Rivash writes this. The Bach argues that the Tur implies that even for one who is learned and analytically competent, royal authorization is ineffective, contrary to Rivash and to a writing attributed to Maharshal that royal authorization exempts a publicly recognized expert from payment for an error. The Bach says this is incorrect and that the judge is clearly liable. He cites the Rambam's commentary to the Mishnah, chapter Zeh Borer, that if a king appointed them and they were experts, their judgments are valid and accepted provided they did not err, which implies that if they erred they must pay. His proof is not compelling. The Tur does not discuss payment concerning one who is learned and analytically competent, and the Rambam there does not discuss payment at all. He means only that if they erred in law the judgment is reversed, as is clear to one who studies the passage. Even if the Tur disagreed, Rivash's reasoning is correct. Authorization from the Exilarch, who himself was appointed by the king, is effective. All the more so authorization from the king or minister himself, since the law of the kingdom is law and it is among the laws of kings and ministers to appoint judges in their lands. Such authorization cannot be inferior to that of the Exilarch, whose power derives from them. See Responsa Rama, siman 123.
