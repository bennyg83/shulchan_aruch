And they may change their claims. Knesset HaGedolah writes that even according to Rambam, who holds that an admission before a single expert is ineffective, a litigant can nevertheless become established as a false denier before that judge. He derives this from Rambam's language in chapter 5 of Hilchot Sanhedrin, which says that an admission before a single expert is ineffective, whereas an admission before three lay judges is effective and one can become established as a false denier. Because Rambam does not explicitly say in the first clause that one cannot become a false denier before a single expert, Knesset HaGedolah infers that one can. He also attributes this to Beit Yosef and Kesef Mishneh. I do not understand his intent, because the Shulchan Aruch expressly rules here that one does not become established as a false denier before a single expert. This also resolves a difficulty in Ran's discussion in Sanhedrin 8. Rabbi Meir and the Sages dispute whether a case of defamatory accusation is adjudicated by three or twenty three. Ulla explains that they dispute concern for reputational consequences. Rashi explains that the husband presently seeks only to defeat the ketubah through a claim of an open entrance, without witnesses, but the Sages fear that witnesses of adultery may later appear and the matter will turn into a capital case. Ran asks: according to Rabbi Meir, who does not fear this, why are three experts needed? Ordinary ketubah, inheritance, and gift disputes are adjudicated even today by lay judges. Ran answers that the concern is that if witnesses later appear and a capital panel must be added, people may say the first judges were unfit. Three experts can at least remain on the later panel, whereas lay judges would have to leave. This is elegant, but one might still ask why three experts are needed, since one expert ordinarily suffices. The answer is that the husband's open entrance claim does not defeat the ketubah where she responds with a certain claim that she was a virgin. She could instead say that she was raped after betrothal, in which case she is believed according to Rabban Gamliel. Before a single expert, her initial statement would not bind her as a formal judicial admission, and she could later change her claim. Therefore one expert cannot finally defeat her ketubah. Before three, she cannot change from one legal claim to another, and the case can be concluded.
