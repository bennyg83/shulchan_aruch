The Sages ordained that he should seat others with him. See Urim, where it is explained from the Yerushalmi that if the litigants come to him on their own to be judged, it is considered as though they accepted him upon themselves, and he may judge alone. On this basis I explained the intent of Tosafos in the first chapter of Megillah regarding Devorah, of whom it says that she sat beneath a palm tree. Tosafos explains that this was because of the prohibition of seclusion when the Jewish people came to her for judgment. At first sight this seems to add nothing beyond Rashi. It appears, however, that Tosafos held as follows. Since we rule that one should not judge alone, Devorah, although she was the principal judge, presumably joined other judges to herself so that she would not sit in judgment alone. If so, why was there any concern of seclusion, and why did she need to sit beneath a palm tree? Tosafos therefore explains that the case was when people came to her of their own accord to be judged. In such a case they are considered to have accepted her upon themselves, so she could judge alone and did not need to join other judges. There was therefore a concern of seclusion, and for that reason she sat beneath the palm tree. This is clear.
