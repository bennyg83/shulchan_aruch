And they are not publicly recognized experts. See Sema and Shach, who were troubled because according to Rambam even an expert's admission before him is not treated as an admission before beit din, while Tur presents the issue as a dispute with Mechaber. Their answers are strained. In my view Rambam's position must be understood differently. If an admission before a public expert were wholly ineffective and could always be replaced by another claim, then all the more he could not receive testimony, for testimony requires beit din. Yet how could he adjudicate at all if witnesses may retract and litigants may endlessly change their claims whenever the ruling turns against them? It is unreasonable to suppose the Sages authorized one expert to judge only after the claims and testimony had first been received by three judges, for no source says so. Rather, whenever a person is legally authorized to judge, he may receive the claims and testimony needed for his own adjudication, and neither witnesses nor litigants can retract in that proceeding. Rashba in Bava Kamma implies this: a public expert cannot judge on the basis of his own sight, which shows that he may certainly judge after hearing two witnesses. Rambam's statement that an admission before a single public expert is not an admission 'before beit din' means only that it does not attain the status of a formal judicial admission for all external purposes, such as being equivalent to a deed, or binding another court through a written record, or establishing him as one who denied in beit din and thereby affecting a later payment claim. The expert himself may nevertheless adjudicate on the basis of it. Likewise, a record written by only that expert is not a formal court record for another court because he lacks the full status of beit din, and testimony received before him cannot simply be transmitted as a judicial record. Rosh, whom Tur cites as disagreeing, holds that the single expert has full beit din status for all these purposes. Therefore Rama correctly qualifies Mechaber's phrase about changing claims by saying that the judges are not publicly recognized experts. Sema's statement that the public accepted them likewise needs no separate proof: a community has authority to establish procedural rules for itself.
