But if there are permanently appointed judges, etc. Responsa Shav Yaakov, Choshen Mishpat, siman 1, writes that according to the rule stated by the Rama below, siman 13, seif 2, that wherever a litigant cannot retract he likewise cannot demand that judges be added, the same applies here with permanently appointed judges. Even before the litigants have presented their claims, since the Rama rules here that one cannot say, “I will not litigate before them, but only through zeh borer,” one likewise cannot demand additional judges. Rabbi Baruch Kahana, av beit din of Furth, whose responsum is printed there at the beginning, writes similarly, but distinguishes between a defendant and a plaintiff. In his view, the Rama equates adding judges with retracting only regarding the defendant, whereas a plaintiff may demand additional judges even where there are permanently appointed judges and even after the pleadings have begun. The author of Shav Yaakov disagrees and maintains that there is no such distinction. Urim VeTumim, siman 13, seif katan 2, however, concludes that even where there is a permanent beit din in the city, so that a litigant cannot say, “I will not litigate before them,” if he asks to add judges his request is heard. He concludes that this is contrary to Shav Yaakov's ruling that judges cannot be added to a permanent beit din, and states that this ruling is incorrect. Netivot HaMishpat there, seif katan 6, likewise records this conclusion.
