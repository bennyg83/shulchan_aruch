(Shach, seif katan 5) And as to five, we do not rule like him, is very forced. Note: These words do not appear in Tosafot. Tosafot's difficulty was that even if those authorities followed Shmuel, Rav Nachman also follows Shmuel, yet nevertheless holds that authentication of documents requires three. Tosafot therefore had to explain that the Gemara's phrase, "perhaps they hold like Shmuel," means that those judges did not distinguish between ordinary adjudication and authentication. In truth, however, Shmuel himself may concede that although two who adjudicated have rendered a valid judgment, authentication of documents requires three even after the fact, as implied by Rav Nachman. Tosafot in Bava Batra, chapter Chezkas HaBatim, explain the distinction: authentication before two resembles two witnesses who heard testimony from other witnesses, which appears as testimony from hearsay and is invalid. Hagahot Maimoniyot, Hilchot Edut chapter 6, cites this explanation and attributes the same view to the Rashba. The Shitah Mekubetzet on Ketubot 22 likewise cites an early authority who writes that although a matter requiring no deliberation may be handled by two, document authentication has a separate reason: it should not appear as testimony from hearsay. Hagahot Maimoniyot also uses language of comparison, "like testimony from hearsay." The intent is that rabbinic enactment requires three for authentication even according to the opinion that two judges' ruling is valid, because with three it is visibly a beit din. For the same reason authentication cannot be performed by a single expert judge. The Ritva, in the name of the Ra'ah, cited by the Shach below in siman 46, seif katan 8, likewise explains that the requirement of three is a rabbinic decree so that authentication not resemble testimony from hearsay. See also Shitah Mekubetzet to Bava Batra, chapter Chezkas HaBatim, which explains that even according to Shmuel, ordinary litigation is recognizable as judicial activity because it involves argument and deliberation, whereas authentication is merely testimony and with fewer than three does not look like a court. The following passage there, in the name of the Aliyot of Rabbeinu Yonah, states that the halachah follows Shmuel, as Rabbeinu Tam also held. Rabbeinu Tam therefore deleted the reading "perhaps they hold like Shmuel," whereas Rabbeinu Yonah retained and explained it. Thus both agree that the halachah follows Shmuel.
