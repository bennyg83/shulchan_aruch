But if there is not even one among them. See Responsa Shevut Yaakov, part I, siman 137, who writes: If all three are entirely unlearned, they are disqualified from judging, as the Rama rules at the beginning of siman 3, and they may not adjudicate unless the litigants accepted them. This means that the litigants must expressly accept them, as in the law of accepting a relative or other disqualified person, as explained in siman 22. Knesset HaGedolah, entry 15, writes that here too it is sufficient that the litigants merely came before them for judgment. This is not precise. His proof is from the Yerushalmi concerning Rabbi Abbahu, who judged alone, where it was said that since they came before him it is as though they accepted him. That applies specifically to an individual expert, who is permitted to judge alone, although the Sages instructed him to seat others with him. Therefore, whenever they come before him, this is deemed acceptance. This is not so with two judges or one judge who are not experts, or with three who are wholly unlearned, who are disqualified by law from judging. They require express acceptance, as where one accepts a relative or other disqualified person. This is also the implication of the wording of the authorities. End of quote.
