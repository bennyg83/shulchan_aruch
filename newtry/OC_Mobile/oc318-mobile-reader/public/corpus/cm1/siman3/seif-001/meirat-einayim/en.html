Beit din may not consist of fewer than three. This is rabbinic. By Torah law, in monetary matters one judge suffices. Rambam states this explicitly in chapter 2 of Hilchot Sanhedrin, ruling like Rav Acha. The Rosh likewise implies this at the end of the first chapter of Sanhedrin, unlike Ir Shushan.
It is impossible that there is not among them one who knows legal reasoning, etc. The Tur says that it is impossible that none among them is learned, meaning he has heard or read in books and knows legal reasoning. This is not the 'learned and reasoning' expert mentioned in the Gemara and poskim, who is called an expert and may judge alone. See the Perishah.
Who does not wish to litigate with the claimant. Siman 13 explains that specifically the defendant cannot avoid litigating in his own city, whereas the claimant can compel him to go to a central court. To the great beit din, even the defendant may in some cases demand transfer. See there.
