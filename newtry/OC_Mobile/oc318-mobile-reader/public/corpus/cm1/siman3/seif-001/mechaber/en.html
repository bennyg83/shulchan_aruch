How Many Judges Are Required. Four seifim. A beit din consists of no fewer than three judges, and any three are called a beit din, even if they are laymen. {Rama: This is because it is impossible that none of them knows the reasoning of the laws. If not even one of them knows the law, they are disqualified from judging (Tur in the name of his father, the Rosh, at the beginning of Sanhedrin). Nevertheless, they may receive the litigants' claims and send them before a halachic authority (Maharam Padua, siman 43).} They may judge a person against his will if the defendant refuses to submit to judgment or refuses to litigate with the claimant in his own city. If he is willing to litigate there but does not want the three judges selected by the claimant, each litigant selects one judge. {Rama: This is as stated below in siman 13. It appears to me that this applies only where there are no permanently appointed judges. If the city has permanent judges, he may not say, 'I will not litigate before them, but only through the procedure in which each party selects a judge.' This is the practice in our city. See below, siman 22, end of seif 1, and siman 40.}
