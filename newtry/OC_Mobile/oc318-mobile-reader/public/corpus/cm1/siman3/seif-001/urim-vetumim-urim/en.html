A beit din consists of no fewer than three. According to Sema, this requirement is rabbinic; by Torah law even one judge can suffice. Rambam writes this in the second chapter of the Laws of Sanhedrin, and Rosh also appears to imply it. Shach agrees with Levush that since we rule that if two judged, their judgment is invalid, the Torah itself requires three, and he leaves Rambam difficult. See Tumim, where I reconcile Rambam and explain Sema's intention.
It is impossible that among them there is not one who knows the reasoning of the laws. This is not the Talmudic category of one who is both learned and analytically capable, which describes a person broad-minded enough to understand and decide and therefore an expert. Here it means that at least one has sound judgment in monetary law. Sema and Shach explain it this way. Still, the judges certainly must be learned in the laws themselves. Maharam of Padua writes so in responsum, and Shach agrees. This is correct.
