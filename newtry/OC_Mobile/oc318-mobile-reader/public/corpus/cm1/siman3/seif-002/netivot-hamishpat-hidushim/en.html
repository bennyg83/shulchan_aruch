Experts for the public. The Sema explains that this means judges whom the public accepted over themselves. Mere expertise, however, does not cause an admission made before them to be regarded as a judicial admission. The Shach also disputes the Sema's statement that public acceptance suffices, holding that even then an admission before them is ineffective. See Urim VeTumim, seif katan 3, who explains that "accepted by the public" means that the community enacted that this individual is to have the legal status of a beit din. See further in Beurim there. It appears that all agree that where he is an expert and his rulings are legally effective, an admission before him is a valid admission, he may receive testimony, and he may adjudicate on the basis of that testimony. The parties cannot retract. Nevertheless, an admission before such judges does not have the full status of an admission in beit din so as to be equivalent to a written admission. Likewise, another beit din may not adjudicate on the basis of testimony or claims that they received, because with respect to transmission they have the status of witnesses, not of a beit din, and reliance on them would be testimony from one witness through another. They themselves may adjudicate on the basis of the claims presented to them, but another beit din may not adjudicate from their written ruling. Rather, they must state orally the ruling they issued:
He is not established as one who has denied falsely. If he said, "You are my witnesses," or made a fully formal admission, then according to the Rambam below in siman 81 he is established as one who has denied falsely. This status applies only to a claim that he had already paid before the admission. For future payment, he remains believed to say that he paid.
