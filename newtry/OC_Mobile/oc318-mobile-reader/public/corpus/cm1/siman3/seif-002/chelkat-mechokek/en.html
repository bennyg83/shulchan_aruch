And this is the present practice everywhere. Although the Rama decided in favor of the Ramach, he did not adopt all of his words. In a place where the practice today includes even rabbinic terumah, such as Eretz Yisrael, as explained in Shulchan Aruch, Yoreh Deah 331, there is reason for concern that if one treats him as a kohen for purposes of being called first to the Torah, one may later elevate him to matters of rabbinic terumah. But in a place where terumah is not practiced at all, he may be called to the Torah, for no mishap will result. This accords with the explanation of the Ran and Maggid Mishneh for our practice, cited by the Beit Yosef here. They conclude, nevertheless, that it is not proper to act this way, meaning that on the strength of his own statement alone he should not be called to the Torah as a kohen even where terumah is not practiced at all.
