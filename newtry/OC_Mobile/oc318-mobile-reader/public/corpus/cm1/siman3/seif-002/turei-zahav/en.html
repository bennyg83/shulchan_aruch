There. In seif 2, in the gloss, “and they are not experts recognized by the public.” The Sema found difficulty with this, as he writes in seif katan 2. In my humble opinion, there is no difficulty. The Rama rules that an admission made before a single expert recognized by the public is effective, but an admission before an ordinary expert is not. He then says that formal ordination does not help, meaning with respect to ordinary experts. Admittedly, the Rambam's own words cannot be explained in this manner, for he writes that although the judge is permitted to adjudicate alone, an admission before him is nevertheless ineffective. This must refer to an expert recognized by the public, since otherwise he would not be permitted to adjudicate alone. Here, however, the Mechaber's quotation does not mention adjudicating alone. The Rama therefore correctly glossed the text in accordance with the view of the Rosh, and this is the primary halacha. The Ramah writes that the judges must be learned in the law even though they are not formally ordained. This is the correct reading. The reading “who are not ordained” is untenable, since formal ordination does not apply today, and the phrase “even though” would have no meaning.
