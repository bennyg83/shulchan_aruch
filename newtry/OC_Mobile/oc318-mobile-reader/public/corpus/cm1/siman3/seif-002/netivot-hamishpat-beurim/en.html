Their judgment is not a judgment. See Shach, who asks why authentication and formal admission should require three more than ordinary adjudication if one assumes that by Torah law Shmuel permits one judge. There are textual errors in the Shach; the proper reading is that Tosafot follows Shmuel only with respect to adjudication, while regarding authentication and admission we do not rule so. Although for us there is a distinction between admission and ordinary judgment regarding a public expert, that distinction exists because the expert's authority in our setting rests on rabbinic enactment. If one assumes that by Torah law Shmuel generally validates one judge, there would be no reason to distinguish authentication and admission from judgment. Yet there remains a difficulty, because Rav Nachman ruled like Shmuel and nevertheless Tosafot say he required three for authentication and admission. See Shach; another textual correction there is that the phrase should read, 'if he is not an expert,' and the word 'but' is erroneous.
