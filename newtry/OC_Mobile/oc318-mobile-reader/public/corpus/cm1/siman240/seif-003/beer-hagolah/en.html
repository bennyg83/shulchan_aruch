There, law 6. The Maggid Mishneh writes that this law is explained in chapter 10 of Ketubot, 94b, in the incident involving the mother of Rami bar Chama. The Rif explains it as a case without a kinyan. Rashi likewise explains that it was not known to whom she first delivered the deed of gift. Had it been known whom she first delivered it to on that day, that recipient would acquire the field, as explained in the next seif. This is unlike the Mechaber's wording in the manuscript version.
The Maggid Mishneh writes that even if we know which document was written first, since we do not know the time of delivery, the priority remains uncertain.
The Maggid Mishneh writes that Rambam rules like Shmuel there, since the halachah follows Shmuel in monetary law, as the Gemara explains his reasoning there: he holds like Rabbi Elazar that witnesses of delivery effect the document, etc. Although it is possible that both documents were delivered simultaneously and the rights of both recipients took effect at once, that situation is uncommon, whereas judicial discretion is more common. So the commentators explain.
