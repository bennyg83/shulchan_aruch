To the judges. The Rif rules here like Shmuel, while elsewhere he rules like Rabbi Meir regarding documents, who holds that signing witnesses effect the transaction. These positions appear contradictory, as explained in the Gemara in chapter Mi SheHayah Nasui. See Baal HaTerumot, Shaar 13, who discusses this at length; the Ran at the end of chapter HaMegarash; and Rashba's novellae. Shach.
Leans. This is Rashi's explanation of shuda d'dayanei, and the Tur writes likewise. It requires study why the Tur did not explain like Rabbeinu Tam, who makes it depend on the judge's preference, since the Rosh in chapter Chezkas HaBatim appears to follow Rabbeinu Tam, as also appears in chapter HaKotev. The Darkei Moshe writes that the Ran and Nimukei Yosef hold that a judge may accept a gift in shuda d'dayanei and that they follow Rabbeinu Tam. In my opinion this is an error, because Tosafot explicitly state that even according to Rabbeinu Tam accepting a bribe is prohibited. Rather, the Ran and Nimukei Yosef certainly mean that it resembles a bribe, and their language is hyperbolic. Ramban's novellae state this explicitly. Ibid.
