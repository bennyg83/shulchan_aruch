It is entrusted to the judges, etc. The poskim rule in accordance with Shmuel, who holds that this case is resolved by judicial discretion. The difficulty concerning the Rif, who here rules like Shmuel but elsewhere rules like Rabbi Meir concerning documents, holding that the signing witnesses effect the legal act, although these positions appear contradictory in the Gemara in the chapter Mi SheHayah Nasui, is discussed at length in Sefer HaTerumot, gate 13, in the Ran at the end of the chapter HaMegareish, and in the novellae of the Rashba. See there.
Whichever person they believe, etc. This follows Rashi's explanation of judicial discretion, and the Tur writes likewise. The Tur requires study, because he did not explain it according to Rabbeinu Tam, under whose view the matter depends upon the judge's choice, even though the Rosh in the chapter Chezkas HaBatim appears to follow Rabbeinu Tam, as also appears from his words in the chapter HaKotev. See Darkei Moshe, who writes that the Ran and Nimukei Yosef hold that it is permitted to accept a gift in a case of judicial discretion and that they follow Rabbeinu Tam. In my humble opinion this is an error, because Tosafot state explicitly that even according to Rabbeinu Tam it is forbidden to accept a bribe. Rather, the Ran and Nimukei Yosef mean only that such conduct resembles bribery, using exaggerated language. The Ramban states this explicitly in his novellae. See there.
