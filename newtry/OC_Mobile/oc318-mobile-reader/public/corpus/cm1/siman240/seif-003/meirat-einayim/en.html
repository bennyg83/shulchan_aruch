There are two notes that have been shot. By the time the Airy Dahan takes away or receives one and two of them on his name and as a result of this book, and henceforth the fires of the denotes are written by two people and anyone who has not in his hand and wants to win a field in his diameter:
Whichever way their judgment inclines. Meaning that if the judges know that the donor or seller was more favorably disposed toward one of the two claimants, they award the property to that person, because it is likely that he was the intended first recipient. This is what the Gemara calls shuda dedayanei according to Rashi, the Rambam, and the Tur. Rama therefore cites an opinion that in our time we do not apply shuda dedayanei. Rabbeinu Tam, the Rosh in Chezkat HaBatim, and the Ran interpret shuda dedayanei differently, namely that the matter is left to the judge's discretion to award to whichever claimant he chooses even without an evidentiary reason. Rabbeinu Tam brings proofs and challenges Rashi's explanation. The Maggid Mishneh cites this view and its proofs, but writes that they are insufficient to reject the interpretation of Rashi and the Rambam.
