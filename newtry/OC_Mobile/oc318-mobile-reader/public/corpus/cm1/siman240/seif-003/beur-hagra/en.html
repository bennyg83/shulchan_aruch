(Collected note) Whichever their judgment inclines toward, etc. Rambam. Rashi and Rashbam in Bava Batra 35a, s.v. “shuda,” explain likewise. Rabbeinu Tam, however, holds that the judge has discretion to award it to whichever party he chooses. He proves this from the end of Gittin chapter 1, where the agent is told to do as he wishes and the Gemara calls this shuda, and from Kiddushin 74a, “let us return and judge it by shuda.” According to Rashi it would mean merely reassessing the parties’ likely intent. Kesubos likewise says, “I am a judge, etc.” If shuda depended only on objective inference, there should be no difference between one formally appointed as judge and another. Rabbeinu Tam therefore understands the authority as a form of judicial disposal conferred upon the judge. The Yerushalmi states explicitly that with shuda d'dayanei, beit din assigns it to whichever party they choose. See Tosafos and the Rosh there and in Bava Batra. (End of note.)
