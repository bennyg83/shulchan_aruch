The addition. Why do we not apply the same reasoning to two ketubot issued to one woman by her husband, as discussed at the end of Even HaEzer, siman 100? There it is different, because when people add to a ketubah they ordinarily write, "and he added for her from his own property," and similar language. Since he did not write that, it is evident that he did not intend an addition. In a sale, however, people do not ordinarily write such language. Therefore, even if the second document does not expressly say that it is an addition, we say that it was written as an addition, and the principal rights are collected from the date of the first document. That he consumed. This is also the Rambam's wording. The Tur, however, writes: "The seller or donor takes the produce until the date of the second deed." There is a practical difference. According to the Tur, only produce still in existence is taken by the seller or donor, but produce already consumed by the buyer or recipient need not be repaid. According to the Rambam, it must be repaid. This is the Sema's explanation. The Bach does not explain it this way, but his words are difficult, for the Rosh and his abridgment explicitly indicate the view recorded by the Tur. See the Bach's discussion in the name of Tosafot.
