(Seif 4: into his hand first.) This implies that if we knew to whom the deed was delivered first, it would be effective even if it was also delivered to the second person on that same day. This is contrary to the Beit Yosef's statement that where both deliveries occurred on one day, we do not distinguish according to whom it was delivered first. The proof he cites from the case of Rami bar Chama, brought by the Beit Yosef below, is not decisive. There, witnesses testified only to the writing, that the deed was written for one person first, but not to the delivery. Since deeds ordinarily do not record hours, the purchaser does not acquire until the deed is first delivered to him. If the deed contained an act of acquisition, however, the one for whom it was first signed would acquire immediately. We must therefore say that the deed in the case of Rami bar Chama contained no act of acquisition.
