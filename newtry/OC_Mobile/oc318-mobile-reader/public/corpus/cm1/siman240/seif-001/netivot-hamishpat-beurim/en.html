If he shows both of them. See Sema, seif katan 6. If he shows both deeds, he cannot collect from purchasers, although he may still collect from free assets. The reason appears to be that production of both deeds indicates that the gift deed reflects the true transaction and the sale deed was not written to create genuine liability but to manipulate rights against purchasers. This resembles the concern Sema discusses in siman 65, seif katan 39. The words "in the meantime" printed in Sema appear to be a textual error. Sema asks why Chazal gave the buyer advice not to show both documents to beit din. In my view the answer is simple. If the recipient knows that the gift deed is the genuine document and the sale deed was written only as a device against a neighboring owner, it is certainly forbidden to use the sale deed dishonestly, even if he possesses only that document. Likewise, if he knows that the sale deed is genuine and the gift deed was written only to collude against creditors, it is forbidden to use the gift deed. The case is therefore where he knows which transaction is truly primary, but different legal questions arise later. If the gift is genuine and the issue is bar metzra, he should not show both deeds because beit din may mistakenly think the sale deed is the real one. Conversely, if the sale is genuine and the issue concerns creditors, he should not show the gift deed because beit din may mistakenly treat the gift as primary. The advice is not to deceive beit din, but to avoid presenting a secondary document that will cause them to misunderstand the true transaction.
