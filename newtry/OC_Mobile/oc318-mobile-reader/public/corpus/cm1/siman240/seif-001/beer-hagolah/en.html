Alef) This is the language of the Rambam, chapter 5 of Hilkhot Zekhiyah U'Matanah, halachah 8, from Rav Nachman's statement in the fourth chapter of Ketubot 44b, as Rav Pappa explains it there.
Bet) As explained above at the beginning of siman 39.
Gimel) As explained in siman 175, seif 24.
Dalet) For example, regarding a right of way: even according to the view that a seller sells restrictively, a giver gives generously. Tosafot write this there.
Heh) Tosafot likewise write that if both the sale document and gift document are produced, the law of a neighboring landowner applies, because even a gift deed containing warranty can be subject to that law. The Maggid Mishneh cites this in the name of one explanation, while others disagree. The Rashba writes that perhaps this case is different because the seller wrote an absolute gift deed, intending that if the neighboring owner later challenges him, the sale should not be treated as a sale and the recipient should acquire through the gift instead. He leaves the matter unresolved. The Beit Yosef cites the Ran in the name of Rav Natrunai Gaon and notes the objection that this view is not clear, and he also cites the Rashba.
Vav) The Gemara explains that it is as though the seller added another layer of protection, because the second document was required for warranty. The buyer may collect under either document. The Tur agrees to this explanation, and the Beit Yosef writes that it is correct. In my humble opinion, the first position recorded by the Mechaber also appears correct, following the "some explain" opinion cited by the Maggid Mishneh and the view of Rav Natrunai Gaon cited by the Ran. For that reason the Mechaber did not distinguish between the cases.
