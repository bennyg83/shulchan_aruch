Or for other matters, etc. The Tur writes that even where the law of bar metzra gives no special advantage, such as where the buyer or seller himself is an adjoining owner, we examine whether the second document provides any other advantage associated with a gift rather than a sale. Tosafot gives the example of a right of way, according to the view that a seller transfers narrowly while a donor gives generously. According to our ruling like Rabbi Akiva, however, even a seller is presumed to transfer generously regarding a necessary path, as explained in siman 214. Thus that precise example has no practical consequence for us. Nevertheless, there are other contexts where a gift gives broader rights than a sale, as explained in siman 215. In my view this is why Rama writes generally, "or for other matters in which a gift is stronger than a sale," and does not repeat the Tur's path example. The Tur himself says only "for example," because he was quoting Tosafot's formulation under the opinion that uses that distinction. The Ir Shushan, however, expressly writes that we rule that a seller transfers narrowly and the purchaser has no path. He repeated this path distinction several times, and this is inaccurate.
