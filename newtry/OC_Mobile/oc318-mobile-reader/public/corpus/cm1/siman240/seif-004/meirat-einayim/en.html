Which of the two received it first. If we knew through delivery witnesses or other qualified observers which claimant first received his deed, we would give the field to the one who received it first that day, even if the second deed was delivered later on that same day. I proved this with several arguments in the Derishah. This also appears from the Mechaber's wording at the end of the siman, unlike the Beit Yosef's statement that when two deeds are delivered on the same day we do not follow priority of delivery.
But if each deed contains a kinyan, etc. The Tur writes that if the witnesses to the kinyanim are not before us, and the witnesses signed on one deed are not the same witnesses signed on the other, so that we cannot determine which kinyan occurred first, we apply shuda dedayanei. But if the witnesses are present, or the same witnesses signed both deeds, we decide according to their testimony. The property is given to the claimant for whom they say the seller first made the kinyan.
