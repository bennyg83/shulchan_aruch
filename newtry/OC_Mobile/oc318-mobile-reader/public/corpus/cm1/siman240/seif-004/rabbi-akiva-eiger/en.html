[Shulchan Aruch] The matter is entrusted to the judges. Note: This applies specifically where the recipient of the gift has no greater right to one date than to the other. But where Reuven transferred all his property to Shimon, and on that same day Shimon transferred all his property to Reuven, and we do not know which transfer was later, we maintain each of them in possession of his own property, meaning his original possession. This applies specifically to land. With movable property, if it is presently in the possession of one of them, we leave it in his possession. See Ketzot HaChoshen, Kuntres HaTefisot, rule 2, siman 3. If Reuven wrote all his property to Shimon, and on that same day Shimon wrote all his property to Levi, and the doubt is whether Reuven first wrote to Shimon, in which case Levi also acquired Reuven's property that Shimon had first acquired, or whether Shimon first wrote to Levi, in which case Reuven's property was not included among Shimon's assets, it appears to me that since Levi cannot acquire Reuven's property unless Shimon first had rights in it for some period of time, Shimon is thereby the original owner of record with respect to Reuven's property, and we leave it in that presumptive status. With movable property, if Levi seized it, the matter depends upon the law of seizure in a case of doubt against the presumption of the original owner.
