The foregoing applies to competing deeds that contain no kinyan and merely state, "Acquire this field by this deed," when we do not know which of the two deeds reached its recipient first. If each deed records a kinyan, however, whoever's kinyan occurred first acquires, and the witnesses are asked which was first. Likewise, if witnesses testify that one recipient's gift deed reached him first, the first recipient acquires. Rama: If one gave a specified gift to another, such as a particular parcel of land or a particular seat in a synagogue, identifying its boundaries, and later gave another person an unspecified synagogue seat or unspecified parcel without boundaries, some say we may assume that he gave the second person a different seat or parcel, and the first gift remains valid. Ritva. Others say that he presumably meant the seat or parcel known to belong to him, and if we do not know that he owned another, the second recipient acquires. This latter view applies specifically to a deathbed gift, which the giver may revoke. With a gift made by a healthy person, the first recipient acquires in any event.
