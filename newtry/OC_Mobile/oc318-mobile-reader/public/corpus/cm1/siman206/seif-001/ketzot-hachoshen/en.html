Sold to you from now. Rivash writes that if the owner obligates himself in writing to sell to the first buyer for a fixed price and not sell to another even for more, the first buyer can acquire. Mishneh LaMelech objected that this helps only if the instrument says the property is sold "from now." He suggested perhaps Rivash speaks in language of personal obligation, "I obligate myself to sell it to you." In my view a true personal obligation of that kind could in principle create a document backed by a lien on the obligor's assets. If he afterward sells to another, the first party might recover from the encumbered assets. Nevertheless, the Shulchan Aruch's requirement of "from now" is more fundamental. A mere promise to sell a particular object later is not necessarily a valid personal obligation at all unless the person accepts present personal responsibility, so that if the object is lost he must satisfy the obligation from elsewhere. Maharit states that an obligation attaches to the person only when responsibility is accepted from now. If the commitment is merely to hand over that specific object, with no broader responsibility, it is a kinyan devarim rather than a true personal debt. Here, the seller does not undertake to compensate from other property if the specific field cannot be delivered. There is therefore no genuine personal obligation. The law must rest on a present transfer, and for that the language "from now" is necessary. The Shulchan Aruch's formulation is therefore primary.
