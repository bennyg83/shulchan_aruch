The law when one says, "When I sell, I will sell to you for one hundred," and later sells to another for more, or says, "I will sell it to you for whatever beit din appraises." It contains four seifim:
If one says to another, "When I sell this field, it is sold to you from now for one hundred," and makes a kinyan to that effect, and later sells it to another for one hundred, the first person acquires it. If he did not state an amount and merely said, "When I sell it I will sell it to you," the first person does not acquire. Tur. Likewise if... If he later sells it for more than one hundred, the second purchaser acquires it, because the first agreement applied only to a voluntary sale under the original circumstances. Here the seller did not wish to sell at the original price and sold only because the second purchaser offered more, so it is comparable to a sale made under compulsion. Rama: This applies when he said, "When I sell, I will sell to you." If he instead said, "Do not sell except to me," or expressly stipulated that even if someone offers more than the fixed price he will nevertheless sell to this person, the condition is binding. Maharik, shoresh 20; Hagahot Mordechai, chapter Eizehu Neshech, and Maggid Mishneh, chapter 8 of Hilchot Mechirah.
