From now for one hundred, etc. Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe write that the phrase "from now" is required because otherwise the matter would be an asmachta or merely a kinyan on words. Rama writes likewise in the gloss at the end of this siman. In the Derishah I wrote that this is not a complete asmachta nor a complete kinyan on words. Rather, the weakness that he revealed an intention that the acquisition not begin immediately combines with the other defect, in accordance with what was explained above at the end of siman 195. See the Derishah.
He did not specify an amount, etc. In that case neither party relies fully upon the agreement. The seller fears the purchaser will agree to buy only for less than the property's value, and the purchaser fears the seller will agree to give it only for more than its value, as explained above in siman 200.
He said only, "When I sell," meaning that he will sell it willingly. In the first printing of Maimoniyot the word translated "as stated" does not appear, and it is likewise absent in an old edition of Shulchan Aruch without glosses. In my opinion, the meaning is that he did not say merely "when I sell." That phrase means that the original sale itself must be willingly made. "Originally" means that the essential sale is to arise from his own decision and desire, not a sale compelled by the addition of money, where the money itself forced him to sell. I later saw a handwritten correction of Rama in Shulchan Aruch. It appears that the printed text mistakenly separated two letters that Rama intended as part of one word, meaning "as originally," namely, as his intention had been at the outset. This is clear.
His condition is valid. Every monetary condition is valid.
