All this, etc. The Gemara says that the money compelled him. Rambam and Shulchan Aruch explain that he had promised only if he later sold, meaning voluntarily, because the expression 'when I sell' implies when I choose to sell. Here he is not selling voluntarily.
(Supplement) All this applies, etc. It is not limited to where the seller himself said, 'when I sell.' The same rule applies if the other party said, 'when you sell,' as Maharik explains. The essential distinction is whether the obligation was made dependent upon the seller's future willingness. If he said, 'Do not sell, etc.,' the rule differs, as stated below. This also appears from Hagahot Mordekhai and Nimukei Yosef. (End of supplement.)
Or he stipulated, etc. Ramban explains that a person does not accept liability for coercion unless he expressly undertakes it. See below, siman 225, seif 2 and 3.
(Supplement) Or he stipulated, etc. Bava Metzia 65b discusses a mortgaged field at a fixed value. Hagahot Mordekhai asks from the rule here that if it was sold for more, etc., and answers that there the party expressly accepted the risk. Nimukei Yosef gives another answer. (End of supplement.)
