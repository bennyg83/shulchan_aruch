He sold it for more than one hundred. Responsa Panim Meirot II, siman 58, writes that the same rule applies if he exchanged the property for another field or house, even where the substitute is not objectively worth more than one hundred. The recipient acquires, because this resembles a sale for more than one hundred: we say the added value induced his consent. An exchange is not governed by ordinary overreaching rules, since a person may prefer a needle to armor. This is certainly true of houses, where one may prefer a smaller house to a larger one, or a house in a location more convenient to him. It can therefore be said that absent the second house he would not have sold his original house for any amount of money. For the same reason, exchange of courtyard for courtyard is not governed by the law of a neighboring purchaser, as stated in siman 175:24.
