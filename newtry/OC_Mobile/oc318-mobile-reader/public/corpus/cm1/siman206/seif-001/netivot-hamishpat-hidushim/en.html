It is hereby sold to you. If he instead says, "I will sell it to you," the matter depends upon the dispute in siman 245:3 whether "from now" can make effective language of "I will give." As to why "from now" is needed even when he says, "It is hereby sold to you," the Sema writes that although this is not a complete asmachta and not merely a kinyan on words, there is an additional defect because he revealed that the acquisition should not begin immediately, while the sudar returns to its owner, as in siman 195. If the acquisition was made with money, that reason does not apply and "from now" is unnecessary. See Beurim for another rationale. If the obligation was written as a present personal liability, or the kinyan was repeated at the end of the document, it is effective without "from now."

Do not sell it except to me. See Beurim: with this wording he certainly does not acquire, because acquisition language must say, "It is hereby sold to you." If he says, "When I sell it, I will sell it only to you," the second party does acquire, because the phrase "when I sell" means when I voluntarily decide to sell, as the Mechaber writes. The present case concerns partners who stipulated at the time of forming the partnership that neither would sell to anyone except the other partner. Partnership conditions can be effective through mere speech, even with the wording, "Do not sell except to me." Since he did not say "when I sell," the partner acquires. The same applies where Reuven sold a house to Shimon and stipulated that Shimon not resell it except to Reuven. Reuven acquires under that condition.
