A beit din of three. The Tur does not say "beit din," but writes: If he said, "according to what three shall appraise it," etc. The Tur holds that there is a difference between using the language of appraisal, where the majority determines the result, and saying, "according to what the three shall say," where all three must agree. The Mechaber follows the Rambam, who requires him to say, "a beit din of three." The Beit Yosef implies that the Rambam's text before him read, "as three shall appraise it, as four shall appraise it," and that the expression "three" means as though he had expressly said, "a beit din of three." According to this view there is no distinction between the language of appraisal and the language of saying. The distinction is whether he specified a beit din of three or did not specify beit din. The Maggid Mishneh likewise explains the Rambam in chapter 8 of Hilchot Mekhirah. The Rambam, Tur, and Mechaber state this law in connection with one who says, "When I sell it, I will sell it to you," as explained below. But the same law applies to one who sells immediately and says, "It is sold to you according to the appraisal of three or four." See the Perishah for why the authorities stated it in the former context.
