He did not sell from now. Beit Yosef and Rama explain that without "from now" the arrangement is only a kinyan devarim and also resembles asmachta. Bach instead says that without a present transfer, sudar kinyan cannot support an acquisition thirty days later because the sudar has already returned to its owner. In my view the same practical result applies even where the transaction is made by money or document rather than sudar. If he does not transfer from now, the language is equivalent to, "It shall be sold after thirty days." During those thirty days the seller can retract. Selling the property to a second buyer is itself a retraction from the first arrangement. Therefore the first party needs a present acquisition structure, not merely a promise of a future sale.
