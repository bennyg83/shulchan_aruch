It is therefore forbidden to leave the document outstanding, because doing so creates an injustice, since the creditor might later collect from encumbered property. The Shach disagrees and holds that it is permitted to retain the document in such a case. It is no worse than a document for one hundred where fifty was repaid, which may be retained. A "document that was waived" here means that no payment was made and the creditor simply waived the debt. The novelty is that even then the document may not be retained:
The scribe's fee. If the creditor wishes to write on the document itself that it was paid, this is permitted, Shach. See Beurim, seif katan 1, that the prohibition applies specifically where the creditor expressly accepted the payment toward the debt in the document. If he received the money without specification, he may say, "What I received was for the scribe's fee, and you still owe me the document debt," in which case he may retain the document. Where retention is forbidden, it remains forbidden even with the borrower's consent, unlike the Tumim:
And in a place. But if the borrower paid the scribe's fee, the document belongs to him and must be returned, Sema:
Provided he writes a receipt. This implies that when the creditor retains the document solely because of the scribe's fee, even giving a receipt does not permit retention. The Shach strongly objects and holds that a receipt always permits retention. Where part of the document debt itself remains unpaid, retention is permitted even without a receipt, as is evident from the Ran, who compares it to a conditional document, and a conditional document may be retained without a receipt. See Beurim, seif katan 2, where I distinguish as follows. After the due date of the entire debt, if part has already been paid, the creditor may compel payment of the remainder and may retain the document without a receipt in order to secure payment, even against the borrower's wishes. Before any payment has been made, however, the creditor cannot compel the borrower to make a partial payment without providing a receipt. As to the scribe's fee, retention is forbidden even if the fee itself is already due. The final clause permitting retention concerns a situation where the remaining portion of the debt has not yet reached its due date, so the creditor cannot compel immediate payment and must instead provide a receipt.
