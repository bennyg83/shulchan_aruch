Whose lien was waived. Although every paid document loses its lien, there are cases in which the lien is waived even though the debt itself remains unpaid. For example, the creditor may waive the lien on the debtor's land and permit him to sell it without allowing collection from purchasers. Thus the Sema. The Shach writes that this explanation is not compelling. Since the creditor can still collect from the debtor's unencumbered property using the document, retaining it does not violate the prohibition against keeping a wrongful document. Rather, the novelty may be that where the lien was waived even though the debt was not paid, and the creditor voluntarily relinquished the document's enforceability, it is forbidden to retain the document. See below, siman 61, seifim 11 and 12.
The scribe's fee. Tosafot and other poskim state explicitly that the creditor may retain the document until he is reimbursed for the scribe's fee. The Shach therefore questions why the Mechaber rules without qualification. In any event, the Shach holds that if the creditor is willing either to tear the document or write on it that it has been paid, everyone agrees he may retain the physical paper until the scribe's fee is paid, contrary to what appears from the Mechaber.
And in a place. If the borrower actually paid the scribe's fee, then even in a locale where borrowers ordinarily do not pay that fee the document belongs to him and must be returned to him. Sema.
A receipt. The Shach disagrees with the Mechaber. Even without a receipt, where the document still relates to a remaining fifty owed to the creditor, everyone agrees that retaining it does not violate the prohibition against keeping a wrongful document. More than that, even if the entire debt documented in it has been paid, the creditor may retain the document as security for another debt, contrary to the Mechaber. See the Shach's discussion.
