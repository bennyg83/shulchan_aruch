He may not retain the document for the scribe's small fee. At first glance this is difficult. A creditor who is owed one hundred may retain the instrument when fifty remains unpaid, and according to Shach can sometimes retain it even without a receipt. Why should a small scribal fee differ? In siman 83 a creditor can apply an unspecified payment to whichever debt he chooses. All the more so he should be able first to apply the payment to the scribe's fee and leave part of the principal unpaid, allowing him to retain the document. The case must therefore be one where he already expressly accepted the payment against the principal debt and only the scribe's fee remains. Then he may not hold the instrument merely for that fee. According to Shach, who permits retaining the instrument for scribal fees, one must distinguish those fees from an unrelated debt. Retaining the document for a separate debt is impermissible once the document's original lien has been extinguished. The scribe's fee, however, arose directly from this very instrument and can be treated as part of the debt associated with it, permitting retention.
