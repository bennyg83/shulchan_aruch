Sema concludes by citing Nimukei Yosef that the legal status of a Torah scholar does not apply nowadays, and Darchei Moshe cites this and appears to conclude accordingly. It is therefore surprising that Rama did not state so here. Birkei Yosef above, siman 15, paragraph 3, cites a handwritten commentary of Rabbi Uri Ashkenazi, who rules that even nowadays a Torah scholar retains this status where otherwise he would suffer monetary loss. He questions Sema's statement that Darchei Moshe accepted Nimukei Yosef. The Darchei Moshe's reference to Yoreh Deah, siman 343, whether the law of a Torah scholar applies nowadays, does not necessarily mean agreement with Nimukei Yosef, but may instead refer to the distinction drawn there regarding taxes. Birkei Yosef writes that although the wording of Darchei Moshe tends toward Sema's reading, Rama's silence here permits the explanation of Rabbi Uri Ashkenazi, distinguishing between the case of a litra of gold and taxation, based on Terumat HaDeshen, siman 341. That responsum supports the view that where the issue is extracting money from a Torah scholar, we preserve his legal status.
