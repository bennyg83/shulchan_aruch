All the increase belongs to the common estate. Meaning, even if the one who improved it spent from his own funds. This appears to me to be Rambam's view, because he distinguishes below only where the person says, "See what I am doing." It follows that without such a declaration, the increase is shared in every case. I discussed this at length in the Derishah.
Even the wages for their labor they do not receive. Although ordinary partners receive compensation for their effort like a sharecropper, brothers waive such claims toward one another. The Rosh writes that this is not limited to adult brothers waiving for minors. The same applies among adults. The Gemara mentions minors only because minors do not ordinarily improve property, whereas when all are adults it is common for one to work today and another tomorrow. The Tur cites the Rosh this way. It is also implicit in Rama's next ruling, from the Tur, that if all the brothers are adults he need not make the declaration before beit din, showing that the preceding rules also concern adult brothers.
If he said, etc., the increase belongs to the one who improved it, etc. Meaning, even the increase produced in the shares of the other brothers.
She is an heir together with her sisters, etc. For example, Reuven married the daughter of his brother Shimon. Shimon had only daughters, and Reuven had no other heir. Shimon died, then Reuven died, so Reuven's estate fell to the daughters of his brother Shimon, and Reuven's own wife inherited him together with her sisters. The Tur likewise explains it according to his approach. See the Perishah.
She improved the estate through expenditures, etc. Meaning, from her own funds, like the earlier case where the adult brother said "See what father left us," provided the increase resulted from expenditures made by the one improving. On this point too the other authorities disagree with Rambam. According to them, once she first said "See, etc.," even increase produced from the existing estate belongs to her, as the Mechaber concludes.
Meaning, they hired workers, etc. Although they exerted themselves in hiring workers or conducting business, since they suffered no out-of-pocket loss and did not physically perform the improvement themselves, but merely incurred additional managerial effort through hired workers, the brothers waive such effort toward one another. So writes the Tur.
If they had informed the minors, etc. In the Perishah I wrote that Nimukei Yosef says even if these minors are infants in cradles and cannot themselves guard the property, since the guarding could be performed by other minors it is not considered physical labor, and the brothers waive it. This also appears from the Gemara's language.
Even in the brothers' shares, etc., and even if the increase came from the estate itself. The Tur writes that although an ordinary partner receives only a sharecropper's portion of the increase even if he says "I am working for myself," brothers are different. They did not originally enter partnership for commercial profit, and when they remain silent after he says, "I am working for myself," they certainly waive their claim, so he takes all the increase. Hagahot Maimoniyot, Sefer Mishpatim, siman 208, writes that if an elder brother suffers an unavoidable loss of property in his hands, even theft or loss, he is exempt because it is considered guarding while the owners are present. Darkei Moshe and Beit Yosef cite this in siman 177.
Only before witnesses. In the Derishah I proved from the language of the Mishnah, Gemara, and reason that even if he made the statement before witnesses outside his brothers' presence, that suffices, because a friend has a friend and the information will become known, like a protest. Rambam and the Mechaber likewise imply this by saying at both beginning and end, "If they said, 'See what father left us,'" rather than "our father," suggesting that the statement is addressed to witnesses, even outside the brothers' presence.
He must say it before beit din. Beit din is the father of minors and may then act for them, perhaps appointing a sharecropper or guardian to improve their portions.
"Give me what my sons earned," etc. Since he placed his sons into the estate without any qualification, he presumably placed them there in his own stead. Whatever they improved is treated as though he himself improved it, and he waived compensation.
