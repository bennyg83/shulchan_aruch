Although Rav Ami said, "they did not finish stating the case before me, etc.," he was concerned only for the final reason there, that the other heir was a minor, because he did not know he had a brother, as stated in seif 3, "and if they were, etc., since, etc.," and "likewise a brother, etc." Certainly where there are minors, even in the incident there he does not receive like a sharecropper, as above. One, etc. Tosefta, chapter 10 of Bava Batra: Brothers whose father left them property, and one of them had sons, and those sons improved the property and brought the increase into the common estate, the father may not say, "give me what my sons improved," and the brothers may not say to him, "give us what your sons consumed." Rather, what they consumed they consumed from the common estate, and what they improved they improved for the common estate.
One of, etc. This follows Bava Kamma 11a, "that which is upon their sons, etc." The reason is that since the brothers saw and remained silent, they waived their rights. One cannot say they remained silent because they intended to give it to them at the time of division, for the father himself has no ownership interest in those items, as stated in Erchin and chapter 9 of Bava Kamma: one who consecrates his property has no rights in the clothing of his wife and children, etc., because he gave it to them as a gift. Nimukei Yosef writes that from this we learn the law where one of the brothers gave a gift, etc.
