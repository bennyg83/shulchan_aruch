And the property increased in value because of expenditure. See Sema, seif katan 5, who writes that even where the increase came from the property itself there is a dispute between Rambam and the other authorities. According to them, even an increase coming from the property itself belongs to the widow once she said, "see what I am doing." In my humble opinion, Rambam understands "an increase of property from the property" to mean, for example, a palm tree that grew and thickened, as many commentators explain and as cited in Shitah Mekubetzet. In such a case all agree that the increase belongs to the heirs.
They hired workers. In my humble opinion, the essential distinction is that digging and planting require separate labor for each individual share, whereas hiring workers together is one undertaking, requiring only somewhat greater effort. People say that when one kneads for one ox, one can knead for several, and in such a case brothers waive claims against one another. A partner, however, even where he hired workers, still receives compensation like an aris if he improved the joint property. It appears that this applies specifically when he improved it through a business or activity that was not the subject of their partnership, such as planting a white field belonging to the partnership or rebuilding a ruin. But if partners formed a partnership specifically to conduct a business, and one worked more than the other or one alone conducted it, he cannot demand wages. Had he informed his fellow, the fellow could also have worked and conducted the business. He therefore certainly waived the claim, assuming that his fellow would work on a later day. For this reason Nimukei Yosef writes that the Mishnah did not state a case of adults with adults, because in such a case they certainly waive claims for this reason. This is especially so where each partner is obligated to do whatever the business requires. One cannot charge his fellow for work that he himself was obligated to perform. At most he could claim for time that his fellow failed to work, and he should have demanded that immediately. This appears obvious to me. See what I wrote above in siman 176.
The increase belongs to themselves. If money was held in the undivided estate from which workers could have been hired, but the adult heirs instead spent their own money to hire workers, it appears that there is an intermediate rule: with respect to the minors' share, they receive compensation like an aris. We cannot say they receive nothing. The entire reason that where property itself produced the increase the adult brothers receive nothing, leaving them worse off than ordinary partners, is that brothers waive claims against one another, as Sema, seif katan 2, writes. Here we see that they did not waive, because they expended their own efforts, and in such a case they do not waive. But we also cannot say that they receive the entire increase. Tosafot in Bava Batra 144, s.v. lo shanu, explain that the increase ordinarily goes to the common estate because had the adults said, "see what we are doing," the minors' friends or relatives could have hired workers for them using their father's property. Here too, since money was available in the common estate, beit din could have hired workers. One cannot say that the adults' use of their own money is equivalent to saying "see what we are doing," because everyone knows they will not personally labor for their siblings, as in seif 2 concerning a Torah scholar. That comparison fails, since with a Torah scholar even work that the minors themselves could perform belongs entirely to him, as is clear from Bava Batra 144 and Rashbam there. The same is true where one expressly said "see what I am doing." Where the adults personally labored, the increase remains common with respect to work the minors could have done. Rather, the difference is that in the present case it was not publicly known whether the adults used their own money or common estate money, so there was no equivalent of a declaration made without witnesses. With Rav Safra, by contrast, his status as a Torah scholar was publicly known.
Brothers who are supported together. Sema, seif katan 15, writes that this applies even where all are adults, explaining that adult brothers who live together and separate their personal food accounts, and so forth, nevertheless waive claims. I do not understand his meaning. Why should living together make them worse than ordinary partners, where one partner who improves the property has the status of one who entered with authorization? Since when they are not supported together they do not waive claims against one another and are treated like ordinary partners, in my humble opinion the present rule applies specifically to a mixture of minors and adults. The reason the minors are otherwise treated as having an unauthorized person descend into their property is that one does not appoint a close relative over a minor's assets, as indicated in Bava Metzia 39. Tosafot in Bava Batra 144, s.v. hishbichu le'atzman, explain that the case there is one where they are not supported together, but where they are supported together there is no concern that the relative will establish a claim of ownership, and so he is not treated as unauthorized. Tosafot needed this according to their own view in Bava Metzia 39 that even as a guardian a close relative is not appointed over a minor's assets. According to Rashba, cited by Rama in seif 7, that the restriction applies only to appointing a relative to take fruits for himself but not to appointing him as guardian, the difficulty disappears. Here the adults did not descend to consume the fruits, only to improve the property, and Rashba's restriction does not apply. According to that view, even if they are not supported together, since they do not consume the fruits and therefore cannot establish possession, they are not treated as unauthorized. Even according to Nimukei Yosef's explanation that the concern is that people who see the relative consuming fruits will fail to inform the minor, the concern does not arise where he does not consume fruits. I am therefore astonished that Rama did not state that according to Rashba, even where they are not supported together, the adult relative is not treated as one who descended without authorization.
