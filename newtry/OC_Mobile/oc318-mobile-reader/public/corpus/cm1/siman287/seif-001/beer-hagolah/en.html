Alef) This is the language of the Tur. Rambam likewise rules in chapter 9 of Hilkhot Nachalot, halachah 2, from the Mishnah in the ninth chapter of Bava Batra 143b.
Bet) The Kesef Mishneh writes that Rambam's text apparently placed Rava's statement, "they taught this only," on the latter clause of the Mishnah concerning where the heirs said, "See what our father left us."
Gimel) The Tur writes, following the Rosh's rulings, that brothers ordinarily waive small claims against one another. The Maggid Mishneh writes that the Ramban and Rashba likewise rule this way, and Rambam agrees in this respect.
Dalet) This is the language of the Rambam there, halachah 2, and the Mishnah there.
Heh) As the Kesef Mishneh explains, Rambam's version apparently read: they taught this only where the property appreciated on its own; but where it appreciated through the use of estate property, the appreciation belongs to the common estate. This statement refers to the latter clause of the Mishnah.
Vav) This explains "through themselves," meaning that the heirs spent their own money or labored personally.
Zayin) This explains "through the estate," meaning that they hired workers using funds of the undivided estate.
Chet) There in the Mishnah, as Rabbi Yirmiyah explains on 144a. The Maggid Mishneh writes that he has already discussed the variant textual readings concerning heirs, and the same applies to the wife. In my humble opinion the version cited by the Kesef Mishneh is correct.
Tet) This follows the reading in our texts and refers to the first clause, where the adult heirs improved the property, etc.
Yod) As the Gemara concludes at the beginning of 144a that a royal service is different, following Rashbam's explanation.
Kaf) This is the correct reading. The Rosh writes it in his rulings. The Maggid Mishneh, chapter 9 of Hilkhot Nachalot, halachah 2, cites some who explain this way and writes that Rambam's wording appears to indicate that all of the profit belongs to him. Yet the Gemara says that he takes only like an ordinary sharecropper, etc.
