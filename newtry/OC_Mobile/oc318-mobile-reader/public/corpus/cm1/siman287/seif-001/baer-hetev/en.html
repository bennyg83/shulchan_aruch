For the common estate. Meaning, even if they improved it with their own funds. This must be so according to the Rambam, who distinguishes below where they said, "See." It follows that where they did not say, "See," the improvement belongs to the common estate in every case. So writes the Sema. See Responsa Maharam of Lublin, siman 109.
The heiress. Meaning, for example, Reuven married the daughter of his brother Shimon. Shimon had no sons, only daughters, and Reuven had no heir. Shimon died, and afterward Reuven died, so Reuven's estate devolved upon the daughters of his brother Shimon, with Reuven's wife inheriting his property together with her sisters. Sema.
Workers. Even though they exerted themselves in hiring workers or in commerce, since there was no outlay from their own pocket and they did not personally labor in improving the property, but only incurred additional effort by hiring others, they waive claims against one another. Tur. Ibid.
To guard. The Nimukei Yosef writes that even if these minors are lying in a cradle and cannot guard, nevertheless, since such guarding can be performed by other minors, it is not considered bodily labor, and they waive claims against one another. This is also implied by the language of the Gemara. Ibid.
Property. The Tur writes: Although an ordinary partner receives from the improvement only as an ordinary sharecropper, even if he says, "I am working for myself," here it is different. Since they are brothers and did not originally join together for the purpose of earning profit, and they remained silent when he said, "I am working for myself," they certainly waived their rights, and he takes all the improvement. Responsa Maimoniyot, Sefer Mishpatim, siman 208, writes that where an elder brother suffered an unavoidable loss of an item in his possession, even if it was stolen or lost, he is exempt, because this is considered guarding while the owner is with him. Darkei Moshe. Ibid.
Witnesses. Even if this declaration was made outside the brothers' presence, that suffices, for a friend has a friend, as in the law of a protest. Ibid.
Beit din. For they are the father of minors, and perhaps they will have compassion and appoint a sharecropper and guardian to improve the minors' share. Ibid.
His sons. Since he placed them in his position without qualification, he presumably placed them there in his stead; whatever they improved is as though he himself improved it, and he waived any claim. Ibid.
