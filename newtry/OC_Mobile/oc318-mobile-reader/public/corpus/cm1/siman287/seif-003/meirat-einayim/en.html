If they were adults, etc., he is appraised like a sharecropper. The Tur and Rama wrote in seif 1 that if the brothers are not supported together and one descends to the property and improves it, his hand is on the lower side. That applies where they live together but separated their food arrangements, so his descent is without authorization. Here the brother was not supported with them only incidentally because he was outside the region and did not even know he had a brother. He entered with authority. One might ask why the Gemara, Tur, and Mechaber say he is appraised like a sharecropper, while regarding property of one reported dead, if a relative descended and the captive later returned, the Rosh holds that the relative receives no share of the appreciation because once he heard the report of death he relied upon it and expected not to lose. The same logic might seem stronger here, since he did not know another brother existed at all. The answer is that there he knew there was at least some possibility the report was false and entered with an awareness that the owner might return. He therefore implicitly accepted that possibility. Here he had no knowledge of the other brother at all and entered lawfully. See the Perishah.
