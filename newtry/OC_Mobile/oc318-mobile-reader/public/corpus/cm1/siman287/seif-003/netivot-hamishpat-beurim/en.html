If they are minors. See Sema, seif katan 14, through his statement that what the adult already consumed is not recovered from him. It appears that Sema said this only regarding fruits produced by the improvement that the adult made without the minor's knowledge. There the Sema is correct. The reason appears to be that if what the adult planted or built were destroyed, such as by fire, causing loss to the orphans' principal, the orphans would certainly have to return the money expended, because they could otherwise say, "remove your trees and go." Thus the orphans do not acquire the trees and building until the improvement becomes known to them and their courtyard acquires it for them. Before they know, a courtyard does not acquire something that does not ordinarily come into it, as explained in siman 268, seif 3. Since they did not acquire before knowledge and the improvements remained at the improver's risk, they are exempt from paying for the fruits already consumed. But certainly, if the adult consumed fruits that belonged directly to the orphans' land and another brother later appears, he must return the fruits he consumed. The mere fact that he ate property not his own before the others knew of the claimant cannot exempt him.
If they were adults. See Sema and what I wrote above concerning this. As to Sema's question from the case where they heard that the absent owner died, in my humble opinion there is no difficulty. In that case, since the relative consumes all the fruits, he has the law of a husband who expends money on his wife's property, where what he expended is expended and what he consumed he consumed. Here, however, he does not consume the fruits at all, as I wrote above. He descended only with intent to improve the property. Why should he not receive compensation like an aris? On what basis should we presume that he waived his labor without payment?
