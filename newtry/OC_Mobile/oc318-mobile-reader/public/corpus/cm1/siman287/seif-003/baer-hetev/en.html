Like a sharecropper. The Rama writes in seif 1 that if the brothers are not supported together and one of them descends into the property and improves it, he has the inferior hand. That ruling applies only where they live together and have separated their food expenses, so that the brother who entered the property is considered one who descended without permission. It does not apply here, where he happened not to be supported with them because he was outside the country. One might ask why this differs from property of a person reported dead, where a relative entered, and afterward the captive returned. The Rosh, as cited by the Rama in siman 285, seif 2, holds that the relative receives nothing for the improvement, because after hearing that the owner died he relied upon that report and would not have undertaken the work if he expected to lose. Why not say the same here, since he did not know at all that he had a brother? The answer is that there, although he heard that the owner had died, he did not know with certainty that he was dead. He entered the property with the possibility in mind that the captive might return and take his own property, so his conduct is somewhat like one who knew of the other party and waived his claim. Here, by contrast, he had no knowledge of the brother at all, and he also entered with authorization. So writes the Sema. See sefer A.A., 102b.
