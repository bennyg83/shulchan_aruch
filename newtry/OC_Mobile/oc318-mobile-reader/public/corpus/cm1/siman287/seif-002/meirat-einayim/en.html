And he conducted business with it, if he was a great Torah scholar, etc. This is not limited to commerce. The same applies if he personally labored to improve the property. Since he is a Torah scholar, we say he surely did not undertake such labor for the common estate but for himself. The Gemara and Tur likewise apply this rule to improvement of property.
Who does not leave his Torah study even for one hour. Meaning, unless a business opportunity for gain is before him, for Torah study is proper together with a livelihood. But for the sake of others, one so devoted to Torah certainly would not abandon his learning. This is what the Mechaber means by saying that such a person does not leave his Torah and occupy himself for his brothers, and the Tur says "for others." Nimukei Yosef writes that this legal status of Torah scholar does not apply today. The Beit Yosef cites him and says his words do not appear correct. Darkei Moshe cites him and concludes like Nimukei Yosef. It is surprising that Rama did not state this in the Shulchan Aruch. See what I wrote at the end of siman 262.
