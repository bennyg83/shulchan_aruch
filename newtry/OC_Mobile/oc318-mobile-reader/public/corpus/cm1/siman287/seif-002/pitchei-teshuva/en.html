He leaves his Torah study. Baer Heitev cites Sema, who notes that Nimukei Yosef wrote that the special rule for a Torah scholar does not apply in our time, and Darkei Moshe appears to cite that view. Birkei Yosef, above siman 15, reports in the name of R. Uri Ashkenazi that the law does apply even in our time so that a Torah scholar should not suffer the loss. He challenges Sema's reading of Darkei Moshe, explaining that Darkei Moshe may merely be referring to the distinction drawn in Yoreh Deah regarding taxes, not adopting Nimukei Yosef wholesale. Birkei Yosef concedes that the language of Darkei Moshe tends toward Sema's reading, but since Rama's gloss here is silent one can understand it as R. Uri did: for purposes of extracting money from a Torah scholar, we preserve the original law. See also above siman 15, seif 1, seif katan 1.
