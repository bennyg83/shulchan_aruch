For a witness does not examine this precisely, etc. The Tur concludes: Therefore, if one witness says wheat and the other says legumes, their testimony does not combine, because people do not make such a mistake. The Sema quotes this. The Mechaber omitted it because in Bedek HaBayit he writes that one may reject the distinction. Since the detail was not one upon which people focus, wheat and legumes are no different from wheat and barley. Tosafot, the Rosh, and Rabbeinu Yerucham, Netiv 11, part 2, rule like the Tur, and their reasoning appears correct. This cannot be dismissed as a detail people do not notice, for the witnesses contradict each other. One of them must be mistaken, and a person does not confuse wheat with legumes. It is therefore a complete contradiction and their testimony is void. See above, siman 30, seif 2. The Bach writes that Rashbam implies that people confuse wheat and barley because they do not distinguish well between wheat flour and barley flour. If so, he argues, they likewise may not distinguish whether the flour they eat is wheat or legume flour. But the Bach confused the word for standing crop with the word for flour. Rashbam writes that people do not distinguish between a standing crop of wheat and a standing crop of barley, meaning that their appearance in the field is similar to an uncareful observer. This proves the opposite, namely, that even without close examination one does not confuse wheat with legumes, since their growth and appearance differ greatly.
