Because the witness does not pay close attention to this. See Shach, seif katan 1, who writes that witnesses do not confuse wheat with legumes and therefore such testimony is contradictory. Tosafot, the Rosh, and the Tur all write this explicitly, and there is no reason to disagree merely on logical grounds. I do, however, have one doubt. Certainly, if two witnesses each testify to possession for all three years, one can properly say that they may confuse wheat and barley. But if one set testifies only concerning a single year, which we rule may combine with other witnesses testifying to the other two years, then if one witness says wheat and the other barley, perhaps the testimony is contradictory. Tosafot on 56b explain that witnesses do not pay close attention because whether wheat or barley was eaten makes no practical difference to the monetary result, since either way the three-year possession creates a chazakah. But witnesses who saw only one year of consumption would obligate the possessor to pay the produce of that year to the landowner. How could they know that there would later be other testimony about subsequent years that would establish ownership of the land? Since according to their testimony the possessor must pay for that year's produce, they should have been careful whether it was wheat or barley, because the difference affects the amount owed substantially. This does not create the same difficulty where one set testifies to all three years. Although at the first year they did not yet know that he would possess two more years and ultimately become exempt from paying for the produce, one can say that they did in fact remember accurately at the time. Later, once they saw that the possession continued for three years and the distinction between wheat and barley no longer affected the result, they stopped keeping the detail in mind and subsequently confused it. But where they testify only to one year, the doubt remains. It is true that Tosafot themselves compare this to the case of one witness saying a black maneh and another a white maneh. Rav Yehudah is the one who says here that one says wheat and one barley, and Rava holds according to Rav Yehudah in Sanhedrin that if the witnesses contradict each other regarding a black or white maneh, the testimony is invalid. Tosafot therefore had to distinguish between a fact the witnesses must notice because it affects how much is paid, and a fact they need not notice. But according to our ruling like the Nehardeans, even when the witnesses contradict each other as to a black or white maneh their testimony can still combine. If so, Tosafot's distinction would seem unnecessary, because there too the difference affects payment and yet the witnesses do not necessarily observe it carefully. The same should be true regarding wheat and barley. Nevertheless, the glosses to the Rosh cite Tosafot's distinction between a matter affecting payment and one that does not, indicating that the distinction is relevant even according to our halachah. It must be because, as the Tur writes above in siman 30, even in a black and white maneh case the claimant must have demanded both, so that his own claim does not contradict the testimony. This implies that the witnesses themselves are expected to notice such a distinction. The matter therefore requires study.
