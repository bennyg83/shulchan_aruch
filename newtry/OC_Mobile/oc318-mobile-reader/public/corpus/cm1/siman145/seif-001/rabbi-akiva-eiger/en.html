[Shulchan Aruch] One witness testified. Note: The Tur writes that if one witness says the years were one, two, and three, and not four, five, and six, while the other says four, five, and six, and not one, two, and three, the testimony is contradictory and the chazakah is void. See Derishah, who explains that it is not necessary that both explicitly contradict one another. Even if one simply says one, two, and three, and the second says, "No, rather four, five, and six," it is enough. This requires examination for me, because perhaps the second witness should be believed by virtue of a migo that he could have omitted the words "No," and simply said four, five, and six. See Ran, chapter 2 of Kesubos, in the discussion where both testify that she was married, that a single witness may be believed through a migo like the litigant himself, and see Ritva there. See also Penei Yehoshua, Kesubos 19b, in the case where one witness says there was a condition, who explains the Ran to mean that one witness is believed by migo only where it operates as the principle that the mouth that prohibited is the mouth that permits. Here too, seemingly, that is the case, for why did he need to say "and not one, two, and three"? He has a migo that he could have remained silent on that point.
