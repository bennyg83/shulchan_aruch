Because the witness does not pay attention to this. Tur adds that if one witness says wheat and the other says legumes, they do not combine, because people do not make that kind of mistake. The Shach says Mechaber omitted this because Beit Yosef held that where the point is incidental, witnesses may err even between wheat and barley. Tosafot, Rosh, Rabbeinu Yerucham, and Tur disagree. The Shach rules against Beit Yosef: once the witnesses contradict each other, we can preserve the testimony only by saying one erred, and between wheat and legumes such error is implausible. See Tumim. The Shach is clearly correct. I added a serious doubt even concerning wheat and barley where there are two full sets of witnesses. If the two witnesses testifying about the same year contradict one another about the crop, perhaps the testimony is fully contradicted because in that setting the produce itself can bear on payment.
