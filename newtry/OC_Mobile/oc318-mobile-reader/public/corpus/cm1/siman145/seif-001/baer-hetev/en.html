He is precise. The Mechaber omits the Tur's ruling concerning one witness who says wheat and another who says legumes. He writes in Bedek HaBayit that one may say that, since this is a matter to which a person does not pay close attention, wheat and legumes are no different from wheat and barley. Tosafot, the Rosh, and Rabbeinu Yerucham, however, rule like the Tur. Their reasoning appears correct, because the principle of a matter to which one does not pay attention does not apply where the witnesses contradict one another. Rather, one must say that one of them is mistaken, and a person does not mistake wheat for legumes. It is therefore a complete contradiction and their testimony is void. See above, siman 30, seif 2. The Bach writes, based on the Rashbam, that people confuse wheat and barley because they do not distinguish well between wheat flour and barley flour, and if so the same would be true of wheat and legumes. But he confused standing grain with flour. The Rashbam writes that people do not distinguish between standing wheat and standing barley, meaning that their appearance in the field can look alike to one who is not careful. This in fact supports Tosafot and the Rosh, for wheat and legumes are visibly very different even without careful examination. Shach.
