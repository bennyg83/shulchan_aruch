(Shach, seif katan 2) According to this, if he claims that he ate only two years, etc. Note: If he claims that he possessed the land for three years, there is no migo, because he desires the land itself and therefore needs an explanation for why he no longer has his purchase document. He claims three years precisely so that he can say that after establishing a chazakah he no longer guarded the document. But when he himself says that he possessed only two years, his statement does not support his claim to the land, since it provides no explanation for loss of the deed. His words concerning two years are therefore unnecessary to his land claim, and he can be believed regarding the fruits by a migo that he could have denied eating them. This must be the view of Baal HaMaor, whose ruling here accords with the Shulchan Aruch. Later, siman 146:38, he explains that the migo there fails because witnesses contradict the claimant. This implies that where there are no contradicting witnesses, the migo can help regarding the fruits even though the land itself returns. The Sema in siman 146 adopts the Rambam's language, and the Shach there requires study.
