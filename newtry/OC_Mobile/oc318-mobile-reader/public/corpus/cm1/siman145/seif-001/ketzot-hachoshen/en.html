The Tur rules that if one witness says the possessor ate wheat and another says barley, the testimony combines because people do not carefully distinguish between the two and the discrepancy is only in secondary detail. Taz asks why the Tur did not require the possessor himself to claim in accordance with both testimonies, as he does in siman 30 regarding one witness to black coins and one to white coins. He therefore suggests the possessor must either say he does not know or frame his claim consistently with both. The Tur's unqualified wording does not support that restriction. The better explanation follows what I wrote in siman 82: where the witnesses disagree only about a detail people commonly confuse, their testimony can combine without requiring the litigant to reconstruct his own claim around both versions. The discrepancy does not undermine the central fact of three years' use.
