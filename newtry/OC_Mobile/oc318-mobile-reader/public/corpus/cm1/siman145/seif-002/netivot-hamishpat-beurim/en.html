The first, third, and fifth years. See Sema, seif katan 2, through his statement that even six witnesses can combine. At first glance this is difficult. Why should there not be chazakah where one witness testifies concerning the first, third, and fifth years, and another concerning the second, fourth, and sixth? If there were six witnesses, each testifying to a single year, their testimony would combine. Why should the fact that the witness to the first year also happens to testify to the third year invalidate what he says about the first? He should be no worse than a separate witness who testified only concerning the third year. This is comparable to a case where one witness says that a maneh was owed in Nisan, Sivan, and Av, while another says that a maneh was owed in Iyar, Tammuz, and Elul, where monetary liability can be established through combination. The reason appears to be that two witnesses whose testimony is separate are believed only where denying the claim would necessarily require us to say that two witnesses are lying. For example, if one says a maneh was owed in Nisan and another says a maneh was owed in Iyar, while the claimant demands both, then if we say that nothing at all is owed we must say both witnesses are false. We do not assume that, and the denial is therefore contradicted by two witnesses to at least one maneh. By contrast, where one witness says the distinguishing sign was on her back and another says it was on her abdomen, the Gemara treats each as only half testimony, because by suspecting only one witness of falsehood she could still be a minor under the competing version. This explains the present case. If one witness testifies to the first, third, and fifth years and another to the second, fourth, and sixth, possession in alternating years does not constitute chazakah. Even if we suspect only one witness of falsehood, no valid continuous chazakah follows. The challenger is therefore not contradicted by two witnesses. This differs from one witness who testifies to years one, two, and three and another to years four, five, and six. According to each witness individually, the possessor established a full three year chazakah, so the challenger's assertion that there was no chazakah is contradicted by two separate witnesses. Likewise, where six witnesses each testify to one year, we cannot deny that there were three continuous years without saying that at least two witnesses are false. We do not do so, and the challenger is contradicted by two witnesses. One should not ask that according to this reasoning, if six witnesses each testify that a separate maneh was owed at the same time, the borrower should have to pay five maneh. That does not follow. Every two witnesses are treated as one testimonial combination. Once the first two testify to two separate maneh, their combined testimony establishes only one maneh, because we preserve as much of the testimony as possible without treating both witnesses in that pair as false. The next two form a new combination and establish a second maneh, and the final two a third. Each pair constitutes its own body of testimony. The same structure applies here. We pair one witness with another so as to preserve the maximum testimony possible, and if the resulting combinations establish three continuous years, the chazakah is sustained, as reflected in siman 30, seif 3.
In the year to which he testifies. Tumim writes that if two witnesses say the possessor consumed the produce in the first, third, and fifth years and left the field fallow in the second, fourth, and sixth, while two others say the reverse, in a place where fields are normally left fallow in alternating years, this establishes chazakah. His reason is that even if only one pair is telling the truth, their account itself describes a valid chazakah. I am not persuaded. If the possessor claims that he consumed produce all six years, he himself admits that he did not establish the required pattern of chazakah, because in a place where fields are normally left fallow he used the field continuously. Moreover, he contradicts and thereby disqualifies both pairs, since each says that he left the field fallow in the years the other pair attributes to consumption, and a litigant's admission has the force of one hundred witnesses against himself. If instead the possessor adopts the account of the first pair and says that he consumed only in the first, third, and fifth years, then he has contradicted the witnesses who say he consumed in the second, fourth, and sixth, while the witnesses supporting the first, third, and fifth years are themselves contradicted by that second pair. His own admission can be used against him to invalidate testimony favorable to him. Even with discrepancies in subsidiary details, such as one hundred versus two hundred, siman 30 requires that the claimant demand both versions. Perhaps Tumim is discussing a case where the possessor died and the heirs themselves do not know the facts. See the Biurim regarding his answer to the question from contradictory testimony in essential investigative details.
The land and the produce return. See Sema, seif katan 3, who writes that the possessor must pay for all six years of produce. Tumim questions this, arguing that since each witness testifies concerning a different three years, he should pay only for three years, just as where one witness says a maneh was owed in Nisan and another says a maneh was owed in Iyar only one maneh is collected. His words are difficult. If the possessor claims only in accordance with one witness, namely that he consumed in the first, third, and fifth years and left the field fallow in the second, fourth, and sixth, in a place where alternating fallow is the norm, then if there were two witnesses supporting him concerning the first, third, and fifth years and they did not know what happened in the intervening years, while one witness said that he also consumed in the second, fourth, and sixth and therefore failed to establish chazakah, the possessor would swear to contradict that single witness. Once he swore, that witness would be treated as absent. Only the two witnesses to the first, third, and fifth years would remain, and their testimony would establish chazakah because we do not suspect that he departed from the local fallow custom. The same logic applies here. If there is only one witness supporting the possessor's own version concerning the first, third, and fifth years, that witness alone would not obligate him to pay for those fruits, as this seif itself states that where a single witness says he consumed for the years of chazakah, he need not pay for the produce. The possessor can then swear against the other witness who says he consumed in the second, fourth, and sixth. Once that witness is neutralized, there is only a single witness saying that he consumed in the first, third, and fifth years, and there should be no payment for the fruits at all. If, however, the possessor himself says that he consumed produce during all six years, then he admits that he did not establish chazakah because he used the field continuously in a place where alternating fallow is customary. Were there no witnesses, he would still not acquire the land, but he would not have to pay for the produce because his assertion that he purchased the field would be believed concerning the fruits by a migo that he could have denied consuming them. Here, however, one witness testifies concerning each set of three years. His own admission together with the single witness creates the rule of one who cannot swear and therefore must pay. The Sema is therefore correct that he must pay for the produce of all six years. If the case concerns heirs, however, we certainly plead for them that the decedent consumed only in the first, third, and fifth years, and they are entirely exempt from payment.
