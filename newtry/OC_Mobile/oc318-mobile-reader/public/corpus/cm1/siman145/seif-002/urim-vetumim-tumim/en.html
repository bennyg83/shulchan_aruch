They do not combine. See what I wrote above in siman 141. If in a town some people leave fields fallow and some do not, then according to everyone, if this possessor did not leave the field fallow, his uninterrupted cultivation can establish chazakah. He can say, "I follow those who do not leave fields fallow." The Tur's concern that he did not consume the field in the ordinary manner does not apply. This creates a question about Rambam's unqualified wording. He does not say that the witnesses contradicted one another. If the town has both practices, why should testimony that he consumed in years one, three, and five not help? According to both sides it is possible that he never left the field fallow and that both descriptions are true. It therefore appears that in a town where some leave fields fallow and some do not, a witness who testifies that the possessor ate produce in years one, three, and five is insufficient unless he also says that the field was actually left fallow in years two and four. Otherwise the challenger can say: this possessor was among those who do not leave fields fallow. He planted in years two and four as well, but did not consume the produce. I therefore did not protest because he had no three continuous years of productive possession. The witness must therefore testify that he knows the field was left fallow in the alternate years. Once that is required, one cannot combine a witness who says one, three, five with another who says two, four, six. If the first testifies that the field was fallow in years two and four while the second says produce was eaten in those years, their testimony is directly contradictory. Thus the Tur's statement that in a place where the entire town follows a fallow cycle one can treat alternate years as chazakah means precisely where that practice is universal. Then a witness to years one, three, and five need not separately testify that the intervening years were fallow, because the town's ordinary practice supplies that assumption. The challenger must prove that the possessor departed from the local custom. The Tur then disagrees because, according to those witnesses themselves, the possessor did not consume the field in the ordinary continuous manner required for chazakah. This resolves the Tur and Rambam and shows that the Bach's explanation through a town with mixed practice is not correct.
That in the year about which he testifies. Suppose one pair says that he consumed in years one, three, and five and left the field fallow in two and four, in a place where fallowing is customary, while a second pair says he left it fallow in one, three, and five and consumed in two, four, and six. Although the pairs contradict one another, he still has a chazakah, because at least one pair must be telling the truth, and according to either pair he held the field in the locally accepted manner. But in a place where fields are not left fallow, if one pair says he consumed in one, three, and five and the other says, "No, only in two, four, and six," he plainly has no chazakah. According to everyone his possession was interrupted. If the witnesses do not contradict one another, and one witness testifies to years one, three, and five while another testifies to two, four, and six, there is a difference between individual witnesses and full pairs. With individual witnesses there is still no chazakah. Even if both are telling the truth and he actually ate every year, only one witness supports each particular year. They cannot be joined like successive loans, because according to each witness separately the possessor held the field only intermittently in a place where intermittent possession is insufficient. By contrast, if one full pair testifies to one, three, and five and another pair to two, four, and six, and they do not contradict each other, there is a chazakah. One pair proves the first year, the other proves the second, and the first proves the third. Three separate pairs could likewise prove three separate years and combine when they do not contradict one another. This explains Rashbam's reading of the case where one witness says one, three, and five and the other says two, four, and six in a place where fields are not left fallow. Rashbam emphasizes that "there is only one witness for each year, which is half testimony." At first sight, if the years are intermittent, why should the problem be specifically that each year has only one witness? Even a full pair would seem insufficient. The answer is that Rashbam's case does not involve explicit contradiction. Therefore two full pairs would establish continuous possession by combining their testimony, while two individual witnesses do not. Maharsha says Rashbam leaves room to question the result with two pairs. According to what I have written, the result is clear.
And the land and the produce return. See the Sema, seif katan 3, who discusses this at length. As I wrote above in siman 141, the case does not concern witnesses who say "not so." Rambam does not mention such contradiction. It concerns a place where alternate fallowing is customary, so under either witness's account there is no legally sufficient chazakah. We do not treat the witnesses as contradicting one another; it is entirely possible that both are truthful, yet no chazakah has been established. The possessor must therefore return both the land and the produce. This is straightforward and requires no lengthy explanation. Still, he need pay for only three years of produce, even though each witness describes a different set of three years. This follows the rule above in siman 30: where one witness says one hundred was lent in Nisan and another says one hundred in Tishrei, and the claimant demands both amounts, the defendant nevertheless need pay only one hundred. The witnesses combine only to one amount because there are not two witnesses for both separate claims. The same applies here. He does not pay six years of produce merely because the two witnesses collectively mention six years.
