One witness testified that he ate in the first, third, and fifth years, etc., while in the year to which one testifies the other does not testify. Rambam writes this in chapter 15 of Hilchot To'en, law 2. Maggid Mishneh explains that the Gemara discusses a place where fields are ordinarily left fallow every other year, as explained in chapter 2, where nonconsecutive years can establish chazakah. Otherwise, why would the contradiction matter? Even if both testified that he ate in years one, three, and five, there would be no chazakah. This is clear. At first glance this is difficult, because he proves a matter that itself needs proof. Perhaps Rambam actually speaks where the witnesses do not contradict one another, but each testifies only to what he saw: one saw years one, three, and five, and the other years two, four, and six, in a place where fields are not normally left fallow. The novelty would then be that although each witness testifies to three years and monetary testimony can combine, as the Tur writes in siman 140:12, where one witness sees years one through three and another sees years four through six, and even six witnesses each testifying to one successive year can combine, nevertheless here they would not combine because according to each witness's own testimony there was an interruption in the possessor's use, whereas in a place that does not leave fields fallow we require testimony of continuous eating. The Tur explicitly writes here in seif 3 that if they do not contradict one another, but one testifies to years one, three, and five and the other to years two, four, and six, there is chazakah in a place where fields are left fallow because together they show continuous use according to local practice, but not in a place where fields are not left fallow, because neither witness testifies to three continuous years. One can answer that had this been Rambam's case, he should not have concluded with the reason that 'in the year to which this one testifies, the other does not testify.' In a place where fields are not left fallow that is not a defect in testimony, because even six witnesses each testifying to a different consecutive year can combine. He should instead have said that neither witness testifies to three continuous years, as the Tur explains. Therefore Rambam must be speaking of a place where fields are left fallow, where witnesses must testify to three years of use separated by fallow years. He therefore needed to explain that in each year to which one testifies, the other does not testify. Had both testified to years one, three, and five, the chazakah would be effective. Maggid Mishneh's wording likewise indicates this. When he says that otherwise even if both testified to years one, three, and five there would be no chazakah, he means precisely that the stated rationale would then be unnecessary. He loosely calls the two testimonies contradictory because each testifies to different years, although they do not expressly contradict one another.
And the land and the produce must be returned. The Tur and the Gemara in chapter Chazakat HaBatim do not mention repayment of produce. Rambam and the Mechaber, who do, must be speaking of a place where fields are left fallow and the witnesses do not expressly contradict one another. Therefore he must repay the produce. Since he ate continuously according to the agricultural cycle in a place that normally leaves fields fallow and the claimant did not protest, the witnesses establish his consumption of produce over all six years. Although Maggid Mishneh says the witnesses 'contradict,' I have explained that this is not literal; they merely appear contradictory because one does not testify to the year to which the other testifies. If the witnesses expressly contradicted one another, the possessor would not have to repay the produce under either analysis. If their testimonies are treated as nullified, there is no testimony concerning the produce, and even if the possessor admits eating continuously in such a place, he is no worse than one who says, 'I bought the field from you and ate its produce for two years,' regarding whom Rif rules that we do not extract the produce, because his own mouth created the liability and he says he ate by virtue of his purchase. If, alternatively, the testimonies remain individually valid but do not combine, the case is no worse than one witness testifying to three continuous years in a place where fields are not left fallow, where the Tur and Mechaber below rule that we do not extract the produce on that basis. The same applies to one witness testifying to years one, three, and five in a fallow region. One can also explain the phrase 'the land and the produce return' to mean produce still physically present and not yet consumed. Such produce certainly returns with the land when there are no witnesses establishing chazakah.
