One witness testified that he consumed it. This applies even if the witnesses do not directly contradict one another, but one saw only years one, three, and five, and the other only years two, four, and six. There is no chazakah. If this is a place where fields are normally left fallow every other year, then according to the combined testimony the possessor used the field continuously and thereby departed from the local pattern, so he did not establish proper chazakah. If it is a place where fields are not normally left fallow, then each witness saw only intermittent use, which likewise does not establish chazakah. Sema and Beurim.
The land and the produce are returned. See Beurim for the detailed applications. In a place where fields are normally left fallow, if the possessor says like one witness that he ate in years one, three, and five, he need not repay produce. He swears against the witness who testified to years two, four, and six. If he says he ate in all six years, he admits that he did not follow the local fallow pattern and therefore did not establish chazakah. Without witnesses he would nevertheless be exempt from repayment of produce through the migo that he could have denied eating. Here, because one witness supports each set of years, he is one obligated to swear and unable to swear and therefore must pay. If the possessor died, we advance for his heirs the claim that he ate only years one, three, and five, and they are exempt from repayment. In a place where fields are not left fallow, if one witness says years one, three, and five and one says two, four, and six, and the possessor says like one of them, he must pay for the three years supported by the other witness and swears against that witness. If he says like both witnesses and admits all six years, he need not pay because the witnesses together establish his use. If the witnesses directly contradict one another, one saying one, three, and five and not two, four, and six, and the other the reverse, they cancel one another and he need not repay produce. Sema. If there are two groups, one saying one, three, and five and not the others, and one saying the reverse, then against orphans the chazakah is established because one of the groups is certainly telling the truth. If the possessor is alive and says he ate all six years, he personally disqualifies both groups by contradicting both. Even if he contradicts only one group, that group is contradicted by him and the other is contradicted by the opposing witnesses, so no testimony remains and there is no chazakah. Tumim adds that in a place where fields are normally left fallow, if one group says he ate in years one, three, and five they need not expressly testify that it was left fallow in the other years, because we presume he followed the local custom. But where some farmers leave fallow and others do not, they must testify specifically that the field was fit to be left fallow in the alternate years. Otherwise perhaps he planted it but simply did not consume the produce. The law there requires study.
