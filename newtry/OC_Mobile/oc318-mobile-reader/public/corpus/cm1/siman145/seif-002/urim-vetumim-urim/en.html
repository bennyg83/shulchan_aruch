They do not combine, etc. See Tumim. According to what I wrote above in siman 141, if local practice is mixed, some fields being left fallow and some not, then where this possessor did not leave it fallow he can say he belongs to those who cultivate continuously. This creates difficulty with Rambam's unqualified ruling here. I nevertheless concluded that Tur, Rambam, and Mechaber mean that in a place of mixed practice, the possession still does not establish title in this configuration. This is unlike Bach's reading. See Sema's extended discussion as well.
In the year about which he testifies, etc. See Tumim. If one set says he cultivated years one, three, and five and left years two and four fallow, while another set says the opposite, then in a locality where fallowing is customary there can still be sufficient possession despite the contradiction. In a locality where no one leaves fields fallow, the contradiction defeats possession. But if the two sets are not actually contradicting each other, so one says he cultivated years one, three, five and the other says years two, four, six, even where no one leaves fields fallow the testimony can combine: one set covers the first year, the other the second, and the first again the third. This is no worse than three separate sets testifying to three years. By contrast, if only one witness says years one, three, five and one witness says two, four, six, no year has two witnesses. I used this to explain Rashbam and resolve Maharsha's uncertainty.
The land and produce return. Tur and the Gemara mention returning the land but not fruit. Rambam and Mechaber add fruit. In my view the case is a locality where fallowing is customary and the witnesses do not truly contradict one another. Since the possessor used the field in consecutive productive seasons contrary to local pattern without the owner protesting, he must repay the produce established by testimony. Maggid Mishneh's reference to contradiction should be understood loosely: the accounts appear contradictory because each set describes years the other does not. If they truly contradicted each other, fruit payment would not follow from mutually cancelling testimony. Sema says the clause may refer to fruit still physically present. See Tumim. I agree with the core distinction, though I do not accept Sema's suggestion that all six years of fruit must be paid. See there and above in siman 30.
