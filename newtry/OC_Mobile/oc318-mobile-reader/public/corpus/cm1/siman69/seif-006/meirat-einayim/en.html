They are exempt even without an oath. In Perishah I proved that even if it is known that their father never swore, they are nevertheless exempt, because their father was not certainly obligated in money. We can say that had he lived, he would have taken the oath. This obligation to swear created by a judgment is no stronger than a handwritten obligation of their father produced against them. Even though their father would at least have had to take a hesset oath that he paid, the heirs are nevertheless exempt without an oath, as explained above. The Tur concludes this siman: if he wrote an obligation in his own handwriting and delivered it before witnesses, it is a complete promissory note and may be collected from encumbered property from the time of delivery, because we rule that witnesses to delivery effect the document. It appears he means that the debtor wrote in the ordinary form of an undersigned obligation that he owes money and delivered it before witnesses, since the entire siman concerns handwriting of that kind. The Mechaber and Rama omitted it here, relying on what they wrote at the end of siman 40 and the end of siman 51. Although those cases are not precisely identical, they hold that there is no distinction: witnesses to delivery generate publicity. At the end of siman 40 the Tur and Mechaber cite Geonim who require the debtor to say to the witnesses before whom he delivered it, 'Sign and testify that it was delivered before you.'
