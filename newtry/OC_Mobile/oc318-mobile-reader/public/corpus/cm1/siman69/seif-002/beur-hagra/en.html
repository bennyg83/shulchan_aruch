Not from. As stated in seif 5.
But if. The rule in the chapter HaDayanim speaks only of one who produces a formal document, not merely handwriting. It cannot refer to handwriting, for then what would the Gemara mean when it asks, "What difference is there between this...?" The case of partial repayment necessarily concerns a full document, as shown by HaKosev, and furthermore the rule that no oath is taken, etc.
And if he admits. As stated in siman 88, seif 29. See there.
And if he denies. Bava Metzia 17a, the incident involving Shabbesai, etc.
(Collected note) And he said it is not his handwriting. See siman 79, seifim 1 and 7, and the rule that if one produces, etc., and the defendant says, "There was never such a debt," this..., which implies specifically where he also denies the underlying debt. This is the language of the Rif and other poskim. See siman 82, seif 1 in the gloss. So writes the Shach. (End of note.)
Some say. From the warning that a person should not sign, etc. The first opinion answers that perhaps he will later deny the entire transaction. According to the second opinion, Tosafos' difficulty in Shevuos, s.v. v'chi, can be resolved by saying the Mishnah concerns handwriting.
(Collected note) Some say he cannot say... They challenge the reasoning of Ramban and other poskim that because this is not a complete document collectible from encumbered property, the debtor is not careful to retrieve it. A deposit document likewise cannot be collected from encumbered property, yet one cannot claim repayment except through a migo of unavoidable loss, as in Bava Batra 70. See also Bava Metzia 13 and the dispute of Rabbi Yochanan, etc. See the Shach. (End of note.)
(Collected note) Some say he cannot say... This is derived from Kesubos chapter 2, "specifically on a shard..." See the Rosh there. Ra'ah answers similarly, and alternatively that one might write his handwriting together with a credibility clause or while the debt is still within its term. (End of note.)
(Collected note) Some say... The first opinion reasons that because the document creates no lien upon property, the argument "Why is your document in my possession?" does not apply. It is like a loan witnessed orally, which need not be repaid before witnesses, since the debtor is not expected to be careful about such proof. But Bava Batra 70 says, "Why is your document in my possession?" regarding a deposit even though there is no lien upon land, and the custodian is believed only because of a migo. Likewise the first chapter of Bava Metzia regarding a found debt document asks whether the borrower owes, and discusses collection from free property. See Toras HaBayis, siman 63. (End of note.)
And if he claims. Why then did the Sages say not to sign? Because perhaps he will not make this claim, as above.
(Collected note) And if he claims... One might ask: if so, why say that he should not sign? Perhaps he will forget. This is the meaning of the gloss, "and it does not help..." As Sefer HaTerumos writes, uncertainty cannot displace certainty. (End of note.)
And it does not help... See the Shach; Sefer HaTerumos also writes so.
Or if he claims. Kesubos 19a: "Actually, where the borrower says..." This shows that he is believed to claim it was a document of trust through a migo that the document is forged. The same applies through a migo of repayment.
He is not believed. Even with an oral loan he is not believed, as stated in siman 71, seif 1.
And if not. From the preceding Kesubos passage. Even with a validated document, the claim that it was a trust document has the same status as a repayment claim against a document containing a credibility clause, as stated in siman 82, seif 10.
