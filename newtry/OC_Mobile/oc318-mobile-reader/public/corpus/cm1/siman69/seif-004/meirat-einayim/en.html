A ledger signed by the parties, etc. In the middle of the lengthy responsum it is written: had he paid, he would not have left the ledger in the other's possession. It has the status of a document because both parties signed it and they also had one witness sign with them as evidence and clarification of their dealings. Indeed it is stronger than an ordinary document: a document records the general framework created at the beginning of the transaction, whereas the ledger contains detailed entries clarifying the ongoing business day by day. This differs from Eir Shushan, who explains that he cannot say 'I paid' because the witness is like a third-party custodian between them and the document is therefore like one emerging from the custodian's hand. That rationale does not appear in the responsum. The reason is as I have explained.
