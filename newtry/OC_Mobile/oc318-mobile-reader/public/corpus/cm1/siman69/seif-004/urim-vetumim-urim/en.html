A ledger. Because the ledger records all of their business dealings in detail, a debtor who paid should not leave the unpaid entry in the other party's possession. The Shach questions whether Rosh's reasoning depends on his general explanation for handwritten notes, namely that a debtor may simply forget that he left one behind. In a ledger, forgetting is less plausible. But according to Ramban's explanation that handwritten obligations are weaker because they do not collect from encumbered property, why should a ledger be different? This is a strong question. Nevertheless Rama already says the judge should decide according to the circumstances. Rosh's practical conclusion is persuasive and may be followed. Rosh's responsum further states that even if the ledger notes that the debt was represented by a separate deed and that deed is found torn, because the debt itself remains recorded in the ledger and the debtor never obtained cancellation of that entry, we may infer that the torn deed came into his hands through some maneuver rather than actual repayment. The debtor remains liable. Chiddushei Gershuni likewise rules this way.
