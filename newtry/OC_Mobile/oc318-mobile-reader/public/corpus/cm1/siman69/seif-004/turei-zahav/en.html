(There.) "If his signature was authenticated." Rav Sherira Gaon holds that here authentication cannot be accomplished by comparing it to signatures on two fields, as above in siman 46, seif 26. The Gemara states that even in that setting there is a concern that someone may have seen the signature and forged it, except that with witnesses we are not concerned that a forger can imitate the signature so precisely, as explained there. That leniency applies specifically to witnesses signed on a document, whose signatures require no authentication by Torah law and only rabbinic authentication. Therefore, such comparison suffices. With a handwritten obligation, however, which requires authentication by Torah law, this method does not suffice. The Tur does not distinguish between authentication of handwriting and other authentication. This is the proper explanation of the Tur, unlike the Beit Yosef's interpretation. In Yoreh Deah, siman 228, seif 38, it appears that even in a Torah law matter recognition of the signature is effective, since there it suffices for granting permission, as stated there from a responsum of Ramban. The cited gloss mentions this in seif katan 46.
