In the underlying responsum, the Rosh writes that if he had paid, he would not have left the ledger in the other party's possession, for it is like a document since both signed it and they also had one witness sign with them to clarify their dealings. Indeed, it is stronger than an ordinary document. This is unlike Ir Shushan, which explains that the witness is like a third-party depositary and the document is regarded as emerging from the depositary's possession. That rationale does not appear in the responsum. The Taz also agrees with the Sema and writes that the Rosh mentioned the additional witness only for greater force. The Shach writes that this law requires study. According to what he wrote in seif 2, whenever an item is not a complete legal document one is not concerned to leave it in another's possession, and the same should apply here. He concludes that everything depends upon the dayanim's assessment after careful inquiry. If the matter cannot be determined, there is no power to extract money. The Bach gives another rationale for the Rambam, that a single witness in a document renders the defendant obligated in an oath and unable to swear, and therefore liable to pay. But the Bach concludes that the Rosh did not give this reason. The Shach writes that the Rosh was correct, because all the authorities disagree with that rule, as explained in siman 51. Even the Rambam said it only regarding a true document signed by one witness after being instructed to write and sign, unlike a ledger where he was not instructed to write and sign it as a legal document, and testimony must be from their mouths.
