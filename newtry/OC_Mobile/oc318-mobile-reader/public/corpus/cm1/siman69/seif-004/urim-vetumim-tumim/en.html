And they caused one witness to sign with them, and so forth. Shach asks: leave the ledger aside. According to Mechaber above in siman 51, even a formal note with one witness can prevent the borrower from saying "I paid," because the borrower becomes obligated to swear against the witness and, if unable to do so, pays under the rule of one who is obligated to swear but cannot swear. The original responsum of Rosh is not difficult, because Rosh disputes Rambam on that point and allows the payment defense against a one-witness note. But Mechaber follows Rambam, so why is the ledger rule necessary? Shach answers that perhaps the witness signed without the debtor's instruction, so the signature never attained formal note status. The wording, however, says that they "caused one witness to sign with them," which sounds more deliberate. There are better applications for the ledger rule. If the ledger is in the creditor's possession together with other writings and the one-witness entry is merely an account notation, the Torah oath structure may not arise in the normal way. The notation then has evidentiary value only as part of the ledger. Also, if the debtor died and the creditor proceeds against heirs, the one witness may not create the same "unable to swear, therefore pay" result. The heirs do not know the facts and are protected by the verse limiting the oath between the original parties. The original debtor, if alive, might be forced to swear against the witness and could suffer the consequences of refusal. The heirs themselves, who make no definite denial, are not necessarily placed in that same structure. Thus the ledger authentication can remain necessary against heirs even if a one-witness formal note would have stronger effect against the debtor himself. The first explanation, that the signature lacked proper documentary authorization, is still the simplest and certainly valid. Rosh's responsum itself also involved disputed records concerning gentile-related transactions, which may further explain why the ledger framework mattered.
