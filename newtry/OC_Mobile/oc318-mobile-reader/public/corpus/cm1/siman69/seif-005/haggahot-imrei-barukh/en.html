(Netivot HaMishpat, seif katan 6) And the wording, 'because he wrote.' Note: See Tosafos, Ketubot 21a, s.v. 'he produced,' who write that one cannot argue, 'why is your document in my possession?' except regarding a document signed by witnesses, through which one can collect from encumbered property. Know that Tumim's difficulty from Bava Metzia is not understood. There is no proof from there that with a witnessed note lacking a property lien the debtor cannot claim that he paid. Even though he is believed to say 'I paid,' when he does not admit the debt and instead claims forgery, the fact that the document was lost impairs it and we do not rely upon its authentication, so it may not be returned. Otherwise the holder might later sue once the loss is forgotten, the defendant might truthfully say, 'It never happened and the document is forged,' and he would not be believed. Although he could exempt himself by claiming payment, if he tells the truth he would lose. This is also why a person should not sign on blank paper, lest someone write above his signature a handwritten obligation and, when he truthfully claims it never happened, he would be obligated to pay. See Tosafos there in Bava Metzia, s.v. 'this.' The law concerning the lender himself, however, is not conclusively established from there; see Penei Yehoshua. In any event, there is no difficulty from that passage against Rif.
