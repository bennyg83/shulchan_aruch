(Collected note) Even though it is within the term. Sefer HaTerumos and the Tur write that if one of these circumstances exists, collection is not made because perhaps the handwriting is forged. Beit Yose questions them: in these circumstances why require validation of handwriting, when even an oral loan could be collected, as explained at the beginning of siman 108? Darkei Moshe answers that perhaps the father made the admission merely so as not to appear wealthy, as in siman 81, seif 14, unlike a validated handwritten admission. Nevertheless, if he died while under excommunication, there is certainly no distinction whether the handwriting was validated, and this is the meaning of the gloss, "however..." which disagrees with the Shulchan Aruch in the Rama's usual manner. But all this is unnecessary. The case may be one where the father admitted in writing that he owed a sum, while the heirs claim that this handwriting did not state this amount and that the present writing is forged; the same applies where he died under excommunication. So explain the Shach and Taz. (End of note.)
