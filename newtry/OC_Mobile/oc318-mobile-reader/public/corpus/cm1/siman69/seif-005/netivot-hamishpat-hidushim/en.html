The Shach asks: if the handwriting itself was not formally validated and the heirs merely admit that it is their father's handwriting, then had their father been alive he would have been believed to say, "I paid," through a migo that he could have claimed forgery. Why should we not advance that same claim for the heirs? He answers that payment within the due date is uncommon, and we do not advance an uncommon claim for heirs. This depends upon the dispute in siman 108 whether we advance for heirs the claim of unavoidable loss, which likewise is an uncommon matter. See Beurim. Here, however, everyone agrees that we do not advance the claim for them.

The language implies that formal validation is needed only where the debt is still within its term, but for an acknowledgment validation would not be needed, because he admitted that he owes the money. The Sema writes that even after an acknowledgment the heirs are exempt if the handwriting was not validated, because perhaps the father admitted merely so as not to appear wealthy. The Shach objects that the case must concern the form of acknowledgment described in siman 81, where that defense does not apply. Otherwise, even if the heirs admit that it is their father's handwriting, we would advance for them the claim that perhaps their father already paid and his later acknowledgment was only to avoid appearing wealthy. See Beurim. Specifically, if he acknowledged the debt without seeing the handwriting, he may later say, "I admitted only so as not to appear wealthy, and I had forgotten that this handwriting was in the creditor's possession." But if he saw the handwriting when he admitted the debt, he cannot later claim that defense. In a case where he was placed under ban and died while still under ban, the Sema explains the need for validation by saying that perhaps this handwriting is forged and the creditor will later collect again on the genuine writing. The Shach asks that a receipt could prevent that. He therefore explains that the case is one where during his illness the debtor admitted that the handwriting held by the creditor was genuine but did not state the amount. Likewise, a case of one who died while under ban is understood in this way. Therefore formal validation is required.
