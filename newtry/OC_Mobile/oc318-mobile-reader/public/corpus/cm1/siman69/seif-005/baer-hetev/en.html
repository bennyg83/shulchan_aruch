And they admit. The Shach writes that this law requires study, although the Tur and Baal HaTerumot state it. What does it help that the heirs admit the handwriting? Had their father been alive and admitted that it was his handwriting, even within the term he would have been believed to claim payment through a migo that he could have denied the handwriting. If so, we should plead for the heirs that perhaps their father paid. Even though the father himself would have been believed only through a migo, we do advance such a plea for heirs. Where he admitted the debt during his illness or died while under excommunication, there is no difficulty, because had he been alive he himself could not have claimed that he had already paid before the admission or excommunication. He could only claim that he paid afterward, and that is not applicable where he gave instructions during his final illness or died under excommunication. But where there is merely handwriting and the debt is still within its term, and he never admitted that it remained unpaid, the law is difficult. The Tur follows his own view in siman 108, seif 7, that we do not plead for heirs that an item was lost through unavoidable circumstances even though their father could have made that claim, because such an event is uncommon. This makes the Mechaber difficult, for there he cites two views whereas here he rules without qualification. Perhaps he holds that even the dissenting authority there concedes that we do not plead that the father paid within the term because there is a presumption that a person does not pay before the due date. In truth, however, this is not so, for the authority who disagrees there also disagrees here. This appears to me the primary ruling. The Baer Heitev adds that according to the Rama, who rules above that one cannot claim payment against handwriting, there is another difficulty. Since we rule that we do not advance for an heir a claim that the father himself could not have made, why is it necessary that the debt be within its term or that the father died under excommunication? This requires study. See below siman 71, seif 17, where I wrote a resolution.
