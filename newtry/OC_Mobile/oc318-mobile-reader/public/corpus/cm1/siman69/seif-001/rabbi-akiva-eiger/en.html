[Rama] It has only the status of other handwriting. Note: So too writes Responsa Maharam Padua, siman 79. If a deed is signed by witnesses and the borrower also signed it, then even if there is no validation of the witnesses’ signatures, it is valid, and the borrower is not believed to say, “I paid,” because through his own signature he admits that the witnesses’ signatures are authentic, and this itself constitutes validation. So writes Maharach Shashon in Responsa Torat Chaim, part 1, siman 93, in the name of the Rashba at the end of chapter Get Pashut, and he writes that Sefer Chukot HaDayanim states likewise. See there.
