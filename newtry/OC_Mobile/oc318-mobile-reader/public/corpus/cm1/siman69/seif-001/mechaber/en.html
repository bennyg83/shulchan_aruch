One Who Produces Another's Authenticated or Unauthenticated Handwriting, and the Defenses of Payment and Partial Admission. Six seifim. If a person produces the debtor's handwriting acknowledging a debt, he may collect from unencumbered property. This applies whether it says, 'I, the undersigned, admit that I owe so-and-so one hundred zuz,' followed by the debtor's signature; was written by someone else and signed below; or was unsigned below but entirely handwritten, 'I, so-and-so son of so-and-so, owe you one hundred zuz.' Where he signs below, the upper text may be written by another. Where his name appears only above, all must be in his handwriting, lest he have signed a blank sheet and another person later wrote beneath it. If all is his handwriting, falsification is not suspected even if he omitted his name and merely wrote, 'I owe so-and-so such-and-such.' Therefore, slips that partners produce against one another are enforceable from unencumbered property when handwritten, even without a signature, if they say, 'I received such-and-such on a specified day.' The writer cannot claim the matter never occurred. {Rama: Even if he writes, 'My signature below shall testify against me like one hundred witnesses,' it still has only the law of ordinary handwriting (Piskei Maharai, siman 201). See also siman 40.}
