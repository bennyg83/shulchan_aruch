And he signed below. Shach cites Hagahot Rosh concerning the warning that a person should not sign his name at the top of a blank sheet. Ribam asks: even if he signs at the beginning of the page, someone could write beside the signature, "I borrowed one hundred from so-and-so." Ribam therefore concludes that if the writing and signature are on the same line, such a claim is not collectible. Shach argues against Ribam from the rule that a witness should not sign in the middle of a line because someone might cut away the earlier document and write before the signature that the witness owes money. The authorities answer that since the risk affects only the witness himself and not an innocent borrower, we do not protect him from his own carelessness. Shach infers that a same-line handwritten obligation can therefore be valid. The two cases can be distinguished. Ribam's inference is from the Gemara's precise advice not to sign at the top of a sheet. If any same-line writing beside the signature could automatically create a valid obligation, Chazal would have warned equally against signing at the beginning of a line. The omission suggests that where everything is written at the very top with no normal document space above it, such a same-line construction does not automatically collect. A person sometimes signs at the top of a sheet merely to provide a signature sample. It is practically impossible to ensure that absolutely no tiny blank space remains on the line. Chazal did not create a rule under which such ordinary conduct routinely exposes a person to fabricated debts. But where there is already ordinary document space above the signature, or a full line and a half of writing before the signature, the situation is different. A person who signs in that irregular place has ignored the safeguards Chazal themselves prescribed and bears the consequence. This answers the authorities' concern about a witness leaving half a line blank. Since there is ordinarily a blank line between the document and the signatures from which we do not derive substantive content, someone could cut the earlier text and produce a configuration containing enough written space above the signature to look like a genuine document. That is a real danger to the witness. Accordingly, in practice, if a purported handwritten debt consists solely of the debtor's signature and the debt language written on the same first line at the top of the page, one should be cautious about extracting money against Ribam. On the analysis above, the later authorities do not clearly disagree, and the possessor can say kim li like Ribam.
