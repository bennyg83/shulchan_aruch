(Shach, seif katan 12) For the Beit Yosef wrote that this is surprising. Note: See Semag, end of shoresh 2, letter 6, who proves from Rambam, beginning of Hilchot Edut chapter 6, where the Rambam writes that authentication of documents is rabbinic so as not to close the door before borrowers, that the intent is that believing the witnesses whose signatures appear in a document without their appearing orally before us is rabbinic. Perhaps the Semag also holds that the borrower's handwriting itself has no Torah efficacy, because a litigant's admission is derived from the verse, "when he says, this is it." Alternatively, he may hold that authentication by comparison with two other documents is only rabbinic, and that this is the Rambam's intent in saying that authentication is rabbinic. See Shitah Mekubetzet to Bava Metzia 13, passage beginning "a document without a property lien," which cites early authorities who plainly state that a borrower's own handwriting is not subject to the rule excluding written testimony, because that rule excludes only written testimony of witnesses, whereas a litigant's own writing is effective.
