Unencumbered property. A writing that lacks signed witnesses has no publicity, so purchasers do not know to protect themselves. Therefore the lender may not collect from property sold to others, even according to the rule that a debtor's property is subject to the debt on a Torah level even for an oral loan. Sema. The Shach notes that at the beginning of siman 39 he discussed the possibility that this lien is not Torah law.
Below. Hagahot Ashri in the name of Rivam states that if the words "I borrowed one maneh from so-and-so" are written at the beginning of a line and the borrower's signature appears at the end of that same line, it is ineffective. The Shach notes that Tosafot and many other authorities appear not to agree.
Signed. Perhaps he signed his name concerning some other matter and left blank space beneath it, and someone then cut out the signature together with the blank area and wrote whatever he wished above it. Shach, in the name of his father.
Liable. If he claims that he wrote it only as a sample form, the Ritva in the name of his teachers writes that he is believed. Partners are different, however, when that is their established practice. See Beit Yosef. Shach.
Ever. Meaning that he cannot say, "I intended this writing only as jest," or "I wrote it so that people would not think me wealthy." Such defenses are accepted, when applicable, only regarding an oral admission, not a written admission, as stated in siman 81, seif 17. Sema. The Taz explains that one who gives no plausible explanation for writing it is liable.
Even. This gloss belongs in seif 2 after the law of an oral loan. See Responsa Mabit II, 292. Shach.
