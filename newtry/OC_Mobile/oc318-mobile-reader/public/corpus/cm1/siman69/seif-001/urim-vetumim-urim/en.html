He collects from unencumbered property. Even according to the opinion that a monetary lien exists by Torah law even for an oral loan, since there is no publicity here, because there are no witnesses to the loan, the Sages enacted that he may not seize encumbered property. See what I wrote above in simanim 39 and 43 regarding whether a handwritten acknowledgment has priority like an oral loan.
And he signed below. If he wrote at the very top of the page, without leaving any blank line and immediately adjacent to the margin, "I, so and so, owe," and signed his name on that same line, and the entire text is not in his handwriting, I wrote in Tumim that one must be cautious and deliberate whether money may be extracted on this basis.
Perhaps he signed his name. At the top of a blank sheet. The Sema explains that he may have signed merely in order to exhibit his signature in beit din. The Shach writes that he may have signed beneath some writing, after which the upper writing was cut away and another person wrote whatever he wished after the signature. From all these explanations it follows that if a person wrote in the middle of the page, with nothing preceding it, "I, so and so son of so and so," and another hand afterward wrote, "borrowed one hundred from so and so," the document would be valid according to their reasons, because none of those concerns applies. I therefore consider the principal explanation to be that of the Ir Shushan, that the writer can say, "I wrote 'I, so and so' merely in jest, and the other person afterward wrote whatever he wished." All agree that even if he does not raise this claim himself, beit din raises it for him.
Even if he did not write his name. The Shach writes that he can say, "I wrote it merely as a form." See Knesset HaGedolah, who records a dispute among the authorities. According to all opinions, if it says, "I, so and so, the undersigned," but he did not sign, this itself shows that the matter was never completed, for he intended to sign and did not. Likewise, if there is no blank space at all at the end of the writing, it is invalid, because we say that he wrote a form for someone else to sign; another person did sign, and now the claimant has cut away that other signature and presents the remaining handwriting against him.
