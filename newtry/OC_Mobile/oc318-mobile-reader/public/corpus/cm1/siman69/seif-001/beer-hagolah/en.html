b) Tur. So too writes Baal HaTerumot, gate 13.
c) There in the name of the Ramban, and Baal HaTerumot likewise writes in his name. The proof is from the Mishnah concerning three gittin: one written in the husband's handwriting without witnesses is valid, Gittin 86a. The Yerushalmi explains that these three gittin permit collection from unencumbered property. The Mishnah's wording "he wrote" is parallel to the case of a scribe and means that he wrote but did not sign. So too writes the Ritva in the chapter HaNose in the Ramban's name.
d) From the rule that a person should not sign his name at the bottom of a blank sheet, etc., Bava Batra 167a.
e) There, and from the incident of that customs officer before Abaye there. Even if he did not raise the claim, we raise it for him, as is evident in seif 2 and as stated there.
f) The mamranot customarily used in Poland, signed on one side with the amount of the obligation on the other, have the full law of a handwritten obligation, with all customary clauses of trust and legal strength. They are treated as though they explicitly state that whoever finds the document has authority to collect it, and they are acquired by delivery without writing, because this practice was established by enactment of the leaders of the various lands and regions. The Bach discusses this at length.
