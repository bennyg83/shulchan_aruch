An injured party who seized it. Tosafot writes that in the sequence of several injured parties seizing an innocuous ox, later claimants can sometimes recover only the share held by the immediately preceding injured party, while earlier parties can retain their portions. Shiltei Gibborim and Maharsha ask why the newest injured party cannot collect also from the owner's remaining share and from the earlier injured parties, invoking Rabbi Natan's rule. The Shach answers that where an injured party seized the ox forcibly, the original owner was prevented from guarding it and can claim unavoidable circumstances, like an animal taken by robbers. I do not think that is always correct. The law of robbers shows that when someone actually takes the ox into his control, that person can enter the owner's place for purposes of guarding even in cases of horn damage. Nevertheless, the present case must involve circumstances where the owner's failure to prevent the seizure was itself negligent. Tosafot says that according to Rabbi Akiva the seizing injured party acquires only his own half and does not even become an unpaid custodian for the owner's other half. If the owner had been entirely blameless in losing control of that other half, he should be exempt for later damage, like an animal that escaped at night. Thus, where the owner negligently allowed the seizure, he remains liable for his half under Tosafot and Rosh. If the injured party seized the ox against the owner's will without any negligence by the owner, the owner is exempt, and then the seizing party may have to bear both his own share and the owner's share under Rabbi Natan because the owner is exempt by law. According to Ramban, however, Rabbi Natan applies only where each participant independently has sufficient causal force, and not where neither could have caused even part of the damage without the other. Under that approach the result can differ. Rashi and Tosafot in the first chapter of Bava Kamma appear broader, and according to them Rabbi Natan can apply here as well.
