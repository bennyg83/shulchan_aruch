They signed and testified, etc. Maggid Mishneh explains that the reason for requiring this formulation is publicity. Without it, the document may not generate the public notice needed for collection from encumbered property. It follows that in areas where publicity is irrelevant, such as a get or testimony used only against the obligor personally, this additional phrase is unnecessary. There is proof from Gittin. Rabban Gamliel enacted that witnesses sign a get because of tikkun olam, lest the delivery witnesses die or travel overseas. Before that enactment, delivery witnesses alone were sufficient. If the Geonic rule required every delivery witness to sign a written statement saying, "We signed and testified that it was delivered before us," then there would already have been permanent written proof of delivery and no concern that the witnesses later die or leave. Thus the special requirement clearly does not apply to a get. One might ask how ordinary monetary documents work according to the view that delivery witnesses are operative. If a signed document is assumed to have been delivered before witnesses, why do we not inspect whether those delivery witnesses also wrote that they witnessed delivery? The answer is that where the same people are both the signing witnesses and the delivery witnesses, there is no need for an additional written statement. They themselves generate the publicity. Requiring both would be redundant. This is also supported by Ketubot 94a, where Shmuel says two documents bearing the same date are subject to judicial discretion because delivery witnesses are operative. The documents themselves do not record the hour of delivery, yet the law does not say the delivery is defective merely because the witnesses did not write a separate delivery certification. Accordingly, the Geonic formulation is needed only in the particular case where the document relies upon a separate proof of delivery for publicity. It is not a universal requirement for the substantive acquisition.
