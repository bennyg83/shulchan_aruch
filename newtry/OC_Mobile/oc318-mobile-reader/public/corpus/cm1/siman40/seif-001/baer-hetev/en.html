Nothing. This means that the person knows he does not presently owe the money and voluntarily obligates himself, comparable to making a gift. But if he obligates himself because he mistakenly believes that he owes the money and later discovers the mistake, even if he delivered a written instrument before witnesses it is ineffective. This is obvious from siman 126, seif 13, and many other places, because every kinyan made in error is reversible. Shach.
One who says. The Sema writes that many authorities disagree and hold that an oral statement is effective only as an admission that he already owes the money. If he seeks to create a new obligation merely by speaking, it is ineffective unless he writes it in a document or says, "I owe you one maneh in a document." It is surprising that the Mechaber and Rama state the rule without mentioning dissent. See above, siman 39, seif katan 3.
That he wrote. The Bach writes that this requires the obligor's own handwriting. If he neither wrote it in his own hand nor signed beneath it, the Tur implies according to Rabbeinu Tam that even if he delivered the document before witnesses he cannot collect even from unencumbered property, because the obligor may say, "I was joking with you." The Shach disagrees. Since witnesses to delivery give the document legal force, it makes no difference that it is not in the obligor's handwriting. Delivery before witnesses creates publicity, and the obligee may collect even from encumbered property. In such circumstances a claim of joking is inapplicable. See the Shach at length.
The guarantor. The Sema explains: where, before the witnesses sign, the document states, "So-and-so borrowed from so-and-so, and so-and-so is guarantor," and the witnesses then sign, the guarantor becomes bound even without a kinyan. This applies even if the guarantee was added after the loan, because it was written in the document, as explained in siman 129. The Shach responds that this is imprecise, because Rambam, the Tur, and the Mechaber in siman 129, seif 4, require a kinyan for a guarantor whose obligation appears before the witnesses' signatures. Their intent here is only that just as a guarantor can create an obligation even though he did not previously owe the debt, so too a borrower can create a new obligation upon himself. In my humble opinion this criticism is not decisive. Although Rambam cannot be explained as the Sema suggests because he requires a kinyan even before the signatures, the Tur and Mechaber can be read that way. The Tur plainly writes in siman 129 that if "and so-and-so is guarantor" is written before the witnesses' signatures no kinyan is required. The Mechaber there records a dispute even regarding a guarantee written after the signatures, with some saying no kinyan is needed, and then adds that according to Rambam even one written before the signatures requires a kinyan. See there, seif 7, and the Sema and Taz.
When he writes. See below, siman 81, seif 29. The same applies if a creditor orally admits that he received payment of the debt stated in a document, or orally waives it; the waiver is effective. Thus the Sema. The Shach there, seif katan 72, writes that where a creditor waives the debt but still retains the document, the law remains doubtful.
