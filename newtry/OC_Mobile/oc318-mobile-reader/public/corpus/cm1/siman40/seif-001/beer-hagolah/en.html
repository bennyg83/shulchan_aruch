The Rambam, chapter 18 of Hilkhot Mekhirah. The Kesef Mishneh writes that this is learned from the statement, 'Let Issur admit that these monies belong to him,' etc., and acquire them by admission, Bava Batra 149a.
The Maggid Mishneh writes that he rules like Rabbi Yochanan and according to the Gemara's interpretation there, such as where he said before witnesses, 'I owe you one hundred by document,' Ketubot 110b. According to the Rambam's explanation, he seeks to obligate himself without a loan, and since he mentioned the word 'document' it is as though he came to obligate himself and said, 'You are my witnesses.' All the more so if he wrote in a document, 'I owe you one hundred,' and delivered it before witnesses, according to the view that witnesses of delivery effect acquisition. Alternatively, the document was in the obligor's own handwriting and he gave it privately, in which case collection is from unencumbered property. The Maggid Mishneh writes that the Rif's view appears to agree with the Mechaber.
