The Law of One Who Voluntarily Obligates Himself to Another. Two seifim. A person who unconditionally obligates himself to pay money to another is liable even though he previously owed him nothing. For example, if he says to witnesses, 'Be witnesses for me that I owe so-and-so one hundred zuz,' writes in a document, 'I owe you one hundred zuz,' even without witnesses, or says before witnesses, 'I owe you one hundred zuz by document,' then even if he did not say, 'You are my witnesses,' his reference to a document is equivalent to appointing them as witnesses. He must pay even if both parties admit, and the witnesses know, that he never owed anything, because he obligated himself just as a guarantor binds himself. {Rama: All the more so, if a person writes, 'I received such-and-such from you toward the debt you hold against me,' it constitutes a waiver even when we know that he received nothing (Beit Yosef, siman 73, in the name of the Ritva).}
