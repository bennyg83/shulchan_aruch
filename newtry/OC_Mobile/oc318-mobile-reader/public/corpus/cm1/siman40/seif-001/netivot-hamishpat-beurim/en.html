You are my witnesses. Many authorities disagree with Mechaber's two rules concerning the formula 'You are my witnesses' and a written statement, 'I owe you one hundred,' holding that a person cannot create a gratuitous obligation except through a complete deed or his own handwriting. This dispute concerns a case where he expressly reveals that he is not admitting a preexisting debt but is creating a new obligation now. If he simply says, 'You are my witnesses that I owe so-and-so one hundred,' the statement is ordinarily treated as an admission. If the claimant says the debt really preexisted, the speaker cannot later retract by claiming he merely intended to create a new gratuitous obligation and has changed his mind. Even if it is independently known that no prior debt existed, an unqualified formulation of admission can itself function as an *odita*, a recognized conveyancing admission, and all authorities agree that an *odita* can transfer rights. Shach's statement that if he admits no prior debt he is exempt refers to a case where he also says his admission was mistaken. Otherwise the unqualified admission stands. I also reject the suggestion that according to Rosh a handwritten obligation requires delivery witnesses in every case. In money, the party's own admission can supply the necessary force against himself even without delivery witnesses; only where the document is intended to prejudice third parties, such as purchasers, can lack of delivery witnesses prevent the document from operating against them. Ketzot HaChoshen's claim that an *odita* itself requires witnesses is therefore incorrect. Wherever a complete admission is effective against a plea of joking, it can likewise have conveyancing force.
