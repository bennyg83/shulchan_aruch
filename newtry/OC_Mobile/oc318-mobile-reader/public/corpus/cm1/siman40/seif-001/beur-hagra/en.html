(Collected note) One who obligates, etc. According to the Rif and Rabbeinu Tam, as the Rif proves there from the challenge from HaNoseh and from redemption of the firstborn, even though the son is not redeemed, and from a guarantor. Thus the case concerns one who creates an obligation in this way. This is the meaning of "just as a guarantor becomes obligated." (End of collected note.)
(Collected note) One who obligates, etc. According to the Rif, unlike Rashi, who explains it as a document of admission. The Rif proves it from the Gemara's statement, "we learned, one who marries, etc.; he wrote to the Kohen, etc.; like the condition of a guarantor, etc." In all these cases there was no prior liability. See Beer HaGolah and Tosafot's explanation there. Therefore the Rambam and Shulchan Aruch write "just as a guarantor becomes obligated." (End of collected note.)
(Collected note) How so, etc. There: "if he said, etc." (End of collected note.)
(Collected note) How so, one who says, etc. According to the Rif's explanation, he says orally, "I am obligated, etc." All the more so if he says, "be my witnesses, etc.," for even Reish Lakish concedes in such a case, as stated there. "Or he wrote, etc." is from the challenge, "come and hear, he wrote to the Kohen, etc." Ramban and Rashba, however, explain like Rabbeinu Tam that it is effective only if written in a document. Merely saying "I am obligated, etc." or "you are my witnesses" is nothing, because one does not create a new obligation through words alone, as stated in Bava Batra 173b regarding "by virtue of that benefit, etc." Unlike there, after the money was already given a guarantor does not become obligated through words alone but only through a kinyan; so too here, only through a kinyan or document. Likewise Bava Metzia 94a: "even if you say, etc., by virtue of that, etc." As for Ketubot there, "what are the circumstances? If, etc.," according to Rashi's approach it initially thought it was an admission. This explains why the Gemara asks, "what is Reish Lakish's reason?" According to the Rif, why would the law have seemed so obvious? The Rambam, however, was careful here: the analogy to a guarantor is imperfect because there the Gemara initially asks that it is an asmachta, and likewise a paid bailee's undertaking in Bava Metzia there is an asmachta, as stated on 66b, "every 'if,' etc." Therefore the Rambam specifies "without a condition" and writes that it is not an asmachta. (End of collected note.)
