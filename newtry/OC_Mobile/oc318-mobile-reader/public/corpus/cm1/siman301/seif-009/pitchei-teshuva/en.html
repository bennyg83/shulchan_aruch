Who deposited. See Baer Heitev. Shaar HaMishpat writes that one might infer from here that if a Jew deposits property with a gentile, the gentile is not subject under Jewish law to the obligations of custodians. He rejects that inference because other sugyot show that a gentile can be liable to a Jew for damages, overreaching, and custodial obligations. The reason for the particular rule here is rather that the Torah imposed a special penalty only upon the party specified. Shaar HaMishpat further suggests that where a gentile owes a Jew for damages, overreaching, or custodial liability, and also owes another Jew an ordinary loan, but lacks funds for both, the loan debt may have priority, analogous to certain rules governing custodians. He leaves the practical ruling unresolved.
