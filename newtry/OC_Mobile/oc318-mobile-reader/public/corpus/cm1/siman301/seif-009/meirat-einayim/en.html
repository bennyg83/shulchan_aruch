Unless both its beginning and its end are in the hands of a Jew. We derive this from the fact that at the time of delivery the verse says, "If a man gives his fellow," and with respect to payment it says, "he shall pay double to his fellow." For the same reason, if the bailee was a non-Jew at the outset and later converted while the deposit remained in his possession, he does not have the law of a bailee. See the Perishah, where I wrote that in my opinion this is the correct reading in Rambam and in the Tur's citation of him.
Likewise, property that was unconsecrated and afterward consecrated. The Tur, in Rambam's name, adds: likewise, if one deposited consecrated property and afterward it was redeemed and became ordinary property while still in the bailee's possession. All these cases share the same reason, as explained above.
