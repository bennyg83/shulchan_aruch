And there are those, etc. Since the Gemara does not say that an unpaid guardian does not pay, some infer that negligence is comparable to direct damage and that he is liable regarding consecrated property by rabbinic law. See Tosafot, Bava Kamma 6b, s.v. 'an ox,' and the same applies to the other categories. The first opinion holds that negligence is not identical to damage, for negligence while the owner is present is exempt, unlike one who causes damage. See Bava Kamma 62a, 'one who gives, etc.' The Mishna mentions only liabilities expressly written in Scripture, and likewise a borrower, as explained in the chapter HaShoel, where 'and if a man borrows' adds to the preceding subject and is also derived through the rule of generalization and specification.
