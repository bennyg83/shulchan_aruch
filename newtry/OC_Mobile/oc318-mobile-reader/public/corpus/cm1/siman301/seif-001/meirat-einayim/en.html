The three laws stated in the Torah, etc. Meaning, an unpaid bailee, who swears concerning theft; a paid bailee, who is exempt only for an accident; and a borrower, who is exempt only where the animal dies because of the work for which it was borrowed. Otherwise they are liable.
They do not apply to land, etc. This was already explained at the end of siman 66 and the beginning of siman 95. We derive it from the verse in the section dealing with bailees, "If a man gives his fellow money or utensils to guard." Through the rule of generalization and specification, the guarded property must resemble the specified items, namely movable property whose substance has monetary value. Land is excluded because it is not movable; slaves are compared to land, according to all opinions in Torah-law matters, as stated above in siman 363, seif 3; promissory notes are excluded because their substance is not the money; and consecrated property and property of a non-Jew are excluded by the word "his fellow." See below, seif 6, concerning money belonging to the poor. These exclusions apply to everything mentioned in the section, namely liability for an oath and for payment.
And some obligate him for negligence. Their reason is that negligence is comparable to actively causing a loss. Those who exempt him hold that the two are not comparable, because where the owner is working together with the bailee, all four bailees are exempt even for negligence, whereas one who actively damages property is liable even when the owner is with him. So writes the Rosh.
Except for consecrated property. This means actual consecrated property, not property consecrated for the poor. So write the Beit Yosef and Maggid Mishneh.
One who borrows a house and it burns is exempt, etc. For this reason, even if the accident arose through the borrower, he is exempt. The Ir Shushan rules this way here, unlike what he himself wrote at the beginning of siman 340, that the borrower is exempt only if the accident came from an external cause and not through him. See there.
