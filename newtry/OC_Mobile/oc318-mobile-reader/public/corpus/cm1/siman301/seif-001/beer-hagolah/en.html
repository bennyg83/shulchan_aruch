Alef) This is the language of the Rambam at the beginning of chapter 2 of Hilkhot Sekhirut. The Maggid Mishneh cites the Mishnah in HaZahav, Bava Metzia 56a. A borrower is not mentioned explicitly in the Mishnah, but his law is the same as that of the other custodians, as Rambam writes regarding all four custodians. The Mishnah did not mention borrowing because borrowing is not applicable to consecrated property.
Bet) The Tur at the end of siman 95 in the name of Rav Yeshayah.
Gimel) Beit Yosef in the name of the Rosh's rulings there, and this is also implied by the Gemara.
Dalet) The Tur in the name of the Rif, Rosh, Raavad, Ramban, Rashba, and other poskim. They hold that just as the Torah excluded a paid custodian from payment concerning these categories, and not merely from the oath, so too it excluded an unpaid custodian from the basic liability for payment even where his negligence is known or admitted. A negligent custodian is not legally equated with a direct tortfeasor, for negligence while the owner is working for the custodian is exempt; if negligence were direct damage, why would the owner's presence create exemption?
Heh) Rambam there. The Maggid Mishneh cites some commentators who infer from the Mishnah's wording, "an unpaid custodian does not swear; a paid custodian does not pay." Since it does not say that an unpaid custodian does not pay, it implies only that he is not required to swear and may be believed that he was not negligent, but if it is known that he was negligent he must pay. Rashi's words also appear to support this.
Vav) The Maggid Mishneh there in the name of some authorities. Rambam cites a statement of Rabbi Elazar on Bava Metzia 58a.
See above siman 95, seif 2, where there are those who disagree and treat attached property as detached for this law, following the sugyot in Chullin. The Beit Yosef in Yoreh Deah siman 6, seif 2, rules like the Rif, Rashbam, and Rambam, and the Rama does not record the dissent there. See also siman 227, seif 32, where it is ruled that ona'ah does not apply to rental of houses, based on Rambam. In my humble opinion all this requires study, but this is not the place to elaborate.
