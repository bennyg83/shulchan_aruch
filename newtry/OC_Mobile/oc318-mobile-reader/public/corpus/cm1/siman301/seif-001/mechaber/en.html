The laws governing bailees with respect to slaves, land, consecrated property, and property of the poor. It contains ten seifim.
The three liabilities stated by the Torah concerning the four types of bailees do not apply to land, slaves, documents, property belonging to a non-Jew, or consecrated property, whether consecrated for the altar or for Temple maintenance. An unpaid bailee does not swear, and a paid bailee, renter, or borrower does not pay. Nevertheless, a paid bailee forfeits his wages unless he swears that he guarded properly. They are exempt even for negligence, and this is the accepted ruling, though some authorities impose liability for negligence. All of these, except consecrated property, are exempt even from the oath that the item is not in their possession. Rama: See above, siman 66, seif 39. A bailee who transferred custody to another bailee regarding these items is liable, because this is worse than negligence, though some disagree. One who borrowed a house that burned is exempt because a house is land.
