Seif 1. The three categories of custodians stated in the Torah, etc. Note: See the responsum of Mahara who questions whether those categories that are excluded from the Torah's custodial laws, but where the custodian nevertheless may be liable to pay in a particular circumstance, require an oath when he claims an unavoidable accident. See Ketzot HaChoshen. In my Chiddushim to Bava Kamma on the sugya of payment from the best quality land, I proved that he is exempt from such an oath. Whatever liability the Torah imposed upon him exists, and what it did not impose does not. Similarly, when he is obligated to pay, collection is not from superior land under the special rules of custodians, as is explicit in the Gemara and in Shach, siman 419; rather, he is like an ordinary debtor. Since the policy concern of discouraging lending is absent here, the Torah's baseline rule remains, and collection is only from inferior land. I demonstrated this there with a complete proof.
Seif 1. Mahara'sh also questions whether a gratuitous custodian who expressly undertakes the liabilities of a borrower thereby becomes obligated in the borrower's oath as well. See Shach and Ketzot HaChoshen here, and my Chiddushim to Bava Kamma, Perek Merubah, studied with my sharp and learned son, where I addressed this question.
