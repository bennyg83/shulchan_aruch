Netivot HaMishpat writes that this applies specifically where the custodian himself is the distributor, for then the charitable funds are money without an identifiable claimant. Rama therefore concludes that if the charity administrator told the custodian, "Guard it for me," the custodian is liable, because the depositor may distribute the funds to whomever he wishes and the money is therefore treated as having a claimant. The same applies to communal funds entrusted to administrators. Responsa Chavot Yair, siman 199, however, indicates that even where there is a charity administrator who controls distribution, the money is not thereby considered to have an identifiable claimant unless the recipients' shares are fixed. He nevertheless writes that where the charity belongs to the townspeople, they can appoint an authorized representative, since a group of partners should have no weaker standing than an individual claimant, even if each person's share is not fixed. Once there is an identifiable claimant, the custodian must pay. Responsa Shivat Tzion, cited below in seif katan 8, also bears on this. The practical law requires study. See Nachalat Tzvi.
