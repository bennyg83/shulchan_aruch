A custodian who transferred custody to another is liable in these matters, etc. The Darkei Moshe cites the Rosh as ruling that, although these classes of property are exempt even for negligence, a custodian who gives them to another is liable because it is as though he threw them into a river. He contrasts Maharik, who exempts a custodian who transferred consecrated property to another. I do not understand this. The Rosh’s responsum concerns one who gave the property absolutely to the second person, not one who transferred it for safekeeping. In an ordinary custodian-to-custodian transfer, the Rosh does not disagree with Maharik, and there is no reason to say it is worse than negligence. The Rosh himself rules that such a transfer is not even negligence. This is also established above, siman 291, seif 26: if the first custodian proves that an unavoidable accident occurred with the second, or can swear that he personally witnessed the accident, he is exempt. Were the transfer negligence, it would be a case whose beginning was negligent and whose end was accidental, and he would be liable. Rava’s accepted reason is that the owner can say, “You are credible to me under oath, but the second custodian is not.” According to this reason, the act of transfer itself is not negligence. Maharik called it negligence only according to the rejected rationale that an owner does not want his deposit in another’s hands. The law is therefore clear.
