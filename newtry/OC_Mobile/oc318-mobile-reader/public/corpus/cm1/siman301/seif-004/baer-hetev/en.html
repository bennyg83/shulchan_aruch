The Ran writes at the beginning of HaNose that according to the Rambam, chapter 11 of Hilchot Mechirah, the same applies if he says, "You are my witnesses," or obligates himself by a document. In my humble opinion, that applies only there, where he obligates himself immediately and unconditionally. Here he does not become liable immediately, but only if the item is lost, which resembles a guarantor. The Ran himself writes there that a guarantor resembles asmachta, and saying "You are my witnesses" is ineffective without a kinyan. The Rambam's wording is also precise, for he speaks of one who obligates himself in money "without a condition." The Sema writes that without a kinyan he does not become liable merely through speech. Although an unpaid custodian may stipulate to have the liability of a borrower and becomes liable through speech alone, as stated in siman 291, seif 27, there he is at least within the class of custodians and therefore can increase his liability by speech beyond what the law would otherwise impose. These persons are not custodians at all. The Sema attributes this to the Maggid Mishneh, but it is not in the Maggid Mishneh; it is in Nimukei Yosef, as may be seen in Beit Yosef. Tosafot in Bava Metzia 58a also write similarly. In my view, the reason is that one who stipulates to be liable like a borrower is as though he expressly says, "I hereby borrow," comparable to saying, "I regard it as though I have received it." Here, however, even an actual borrower would be exempt because the laws of custodians do not apply at all. Shach.
