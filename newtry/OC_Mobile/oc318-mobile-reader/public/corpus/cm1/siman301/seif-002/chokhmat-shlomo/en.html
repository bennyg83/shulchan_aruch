Seif 4. All of these, if they made a formal acquisition obligating themselves in responsibility, are liable. Note: Ketzot HaChoshen asks why an act of acquisition is needed for consecrated property, since a verbal undertaking to Heaven is equivalent to delivery to an ordinary person. In my view this is not difficult. Although speech to Heaven has the force of delivery, one can seek annulment of a consecratory vow, and even bodily consecration can be annulled, because it was created by speech and speech can undo speech, as the Shach writes. Thus, if the custodian merely undertook responsibility verbally toward consecrated property, he could later obtain annulment and retract. Moreover, as long as his obligation rests only on the verbal consecratory commitment, where he claims an unavoidable accident and would otherwise be exempt from payment, he should not require a new oath: first, his liability derives from a vow and one oath does not take effect upon another like obligation; second, he has a migo that he could seek annulment, and such a migo is effective according to all views. He would therefore lack the full status of a custodian for all purposes. The Shulchan Aruch consequently states that they made a formal acquisition, which binds him absolutely both to financial responsibility and, where appropriate, to the oath. It is not discussing mere consecratory speech.
There: All of these, if they made a formal acquisition. The source is Bava Metzia 58. See my fourth edition on Choshen Mishpat, responsum 191, where I discuss the Gemara's formulation 'if they made an acquisition.'
