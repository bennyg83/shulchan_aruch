Nevertheless, a paid custodian forfeits his wages. Responsa Beit David, Choshen Mishpat, siman 1, writes that if one entrusted ten documents to a custodian for a total guarding fee of ten gold coins and one document was lost through the custodian's negligence, causing forfeiture of his compensation, he forfeits the entire fee and receives nothing even for the documents he guarded properly. His proof is from Avodah Zarah 65a, where one who contracts to transport one hundred barrels for one hundred perutot does not earn anything until the entire job is completed. When compensation is bundled across multiple objects, failure regarding one prevents earning the aggregate fee. By contrast, Beit David argues that bundling across time is different. If one was hired to guard the documents for ten days for ten gold coins and negligently lost them on the tenth day, he does not forfeit the wages earned for the preceding days. He derives this from the discussion in Bava Metzia 58a concerning one hired to guard a child or a cow over an extended period. Beit David distinguishes between aggregation of objects and aggregation of days. This is difficult, however, because Tumim, siman 66, seif katan 68, states expressly that when one is hired for several days as one aggregate engagement, negligence can cause forfeiture of the wages for earlier days as well; only where he is hired for separate days are the wages of the other days preserved. Rabbi Yeshaya Strashun, in his glosses to the Vilna editions of the Gemara, likewise explains that a weekly or monthly hire is an aggregate engagement, comparable to transporting one hundred barrels for one hundred perutot, whereas daily hire is separate for each day.
