(Shach, seif katan 4) I did not understand his reasoning. Note: See Tumim, siman 66, seif katan 70. In my humble opinion the incident in the Rosh is not at all comparable to a guardian who transfers property to another guardian. The document was not in the scribe's hand for safekeeping as a deposit. His duty was to deliver it to the purchaser. Instead of delivering it to the purchaser, he gave it to the purchaser's father, and for that reason he was negligent. Therefore, if the son had authorized his father over his property and the father told the scribe that he came as the son's agent, the scribe would not be negligent in delivering it to him. This is what the Rosh explains when he compares the case to a guardian who transfers to another guardian: just as there is no negligence when property is delivered to one with whom the owner regularly deposits, so too here. But the underlying legal categories are not identical.
