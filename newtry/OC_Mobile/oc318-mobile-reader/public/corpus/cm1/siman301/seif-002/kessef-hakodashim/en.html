A paid custodian. A household servant who is alone in the house may bear responsibility for guarding everything there and perhaps has the status of a paid custodian, even though his wages were not expressly paid for guarding. This resembles a gift sent on Rosh Hashanah that creates paid-custodian status. Yet there the sender presumably transmitted it for the recipient's benefit in reliance on superior guarding, whereas ordinary wages paid for labor do not necessarily include compensation for custodial responsibility. In the case of craftsmen, the Sages expressly grounded paid-custodian status in the benefit they receive. Still, a resident servant may be more than one who merely says, 'Place it before me,' and may become a custodian without an express undertaking. This requires further study.
