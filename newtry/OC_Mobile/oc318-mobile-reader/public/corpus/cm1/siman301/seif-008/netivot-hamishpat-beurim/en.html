And it is forbidden to use them. There is room to question whether public funds held by a collector or communal administrator may be used by him temporarily. This is unlike unsealed money entrusted to an individual, where giving the money unsealed is itself treated as permission to use it. Here the funds were collected from individuals who never personally granted such permission. On the other hand, perhaps they contribute with the understanding that the money belongs to the community, and they deliver it unsealed in order that the person holding it become responsible for loss. Yet when the funds remain in the hands of communal administrators, I do not know whether they may use them. In their case one certainly cannot say that the individuals delivered unsealed money with the intention that the administrators use it and thereby assume liability. Nor can we simply say that the contributors presumably prefer to permit use so that the administrators will become liable, for the same argument could then be made regarding money found as lost property.
