Money belonging to orphans has the same law, etc. This also teaches us not to say that the recipient has no status of a bailee, since one does not swear on the claim of a minor, and the money was delivered to him by beit din, whose own money it is not and who are not personally seeking its return. The ruling therefore teaches that because the minors will claim it from him when they become adults, this is considered money with claimants, and he bears the law of their bailee.
And it is prohibited to use it. The Tur also writes this here, even though this does not appear to be the natural place for the law, and the Tur and Mechaber already wrote it at the end of siman 267. It appears that he repeats it here incidentally to the statement that orphan money has the same law as anyone else's money. Thus an unpaid bailee is exempt from theft and loss, and a paid bailee is exempt from accidents. Had he been permitted to use the money, he would be liable even for accidents, as the Tur and Mechaber explain there and in siman 292. One might have thought that he should be allowed to use it so that he would assume responsibility for it, as with money from a found object. The ruling therefore teaches that such use was permitted only regarding a found object because the finder conferred a benefit by returning it. See siman 267.
