Money of the poor, etc., he is exempt. Even though money of the poor does not have the law of consecrated property, as the Tur and Mechaber write at the end of siman 95 and in siman 212, the custodian is exempt because Scripture says "to guard," not "to distribute." The Torah imposed custodial liability only where an object comes into his hands to be guarded for a time and later returned to the person who gave it. Here the one who hands him the poor funds does not intend that he later return them. One might ask that he should then be the custodian of the poor recipients. The answer is that the funds have no particular claimant, because they were not designated for specific poor people. Therefore, if the money was deposited with him for distribution to named poor people, each to receive a fixed amount, he is considered their custodian and is liable, since such funds are not consecrated property. For the same reason Rama concludes that if the charity collector says, "Guard it for me," he is liable. The collector has made clear that he is to serve as custodian for the collector and later return the money to him for distribution.
