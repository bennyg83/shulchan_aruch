One with whom funds of the poor were deposited. Shiltei Gibborim writes that if ordinary money was first deposited and only afterward the owner vowed it to the poor, the custodian remains liable for negligence because it originally came into his hand under ordinary custody. This is difficult. The rule that an object once received for custody does not change merely through later words applies where the underlying ownership remains the same. Here the owner later dedicated the money to the poor. The legal character of the property changed against the custodian's will. Rambam expressly rules that property which was ordinary property and was afterward consecrated while in a custodian's hands becomes exempt from the ordinary custodial laws. Rama states the same. Therefore the prior receipt under ordinary custody does not preserve liability after the property is validly dedicated to the poor. Shiltei Gibborim's ruling is difficult.
