If they made a kinyan from them, etc. The Ran writes that according to the Rambam, a person could also obligate himself by saying, “You are my witnesses,” or through a written obligation. In my view, that applies when he assumes an immediate, unconditional monetary liability. Here he becomes liable only if the property is later lost, which resembles a guarantor’s conditional undertaking and is an asmachta. Saying “You are my witnesses” is therefore insufficient; a kinyan is required. This is precise in the Rambam’s wording, “one who obligates himself in money without a condition.” The Sema explains that a gratuitous custodian can condition himself to be like a borrower by speech alone because he is already within the laws of custodians. The source is not the Maggid Mishneh but the Nimukei Yosef and Tosafot. The better explanation is that saying, “I undertake to be like a borrower,” is equivalent to accepting the status immediately, whereas here even an actual borrower would be exempt because the property is outside the laws of custodians altogether.
