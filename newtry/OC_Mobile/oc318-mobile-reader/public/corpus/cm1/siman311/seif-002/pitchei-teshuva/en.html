Because there is substantial grounds for objection, etc. See Shach, who writes that where one said simply 'wine' and specified 'this ship,' it is possible that even if the wine was not lost the renter may refuse another ship, and he rules accordingly. Mishneh LaMelech and Ketzot HaChoshen agree, explaining that the dissenting view in the Tur concerns substitution of one load for another, not one donkey or ship for another. Rabbi Akiva Eiger, however, disagrees. He proves from the Tur in siman 310 that even where 'this donkey' was specified, if another donkey can be provided the renter cannot hire one independently for his own benefit and must accept the replacement. Thus the special concern in the case of the sunken ship is only where both ship and cargo were lost and neither party can fully perform. If only one side's item was lost and replacement causes no loss, that party may not profit merely from the technical wording. In his view this is clear, unlike the Shach and Sema. See Hagahot Ashri.
