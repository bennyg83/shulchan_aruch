There is great insistence concerning a ship. Tur cites Rabbi Meir HaLevi Abulafia that one who rents a donkey or ship for a specified load cannot substitute another load even if it is no heavier, and a lessor who promised this donkey or this ship cannot substitute another. Other authorities distinguish: ordinarily one load can be substituted for an equivalent one because there is no substantive difference, but where the original subject was lost and one party now has a reason to insist on the precise terms, that insistence is recognized. Sema explains that if the substitute load is present, compelling acceptance is merely preventing conduct like the measure of Sodom. If the substitute is not present and the contractor gains by resting, he can insist on the original terms. This is difficult from the law of a day laborer who finishes early. If comparable work remains, the employer can assign it to him even though the worker would prefer to rest. Hagahot Ashri attempts to distinguish a contractor from a day laborer. In my view the Shach's approach is primary. Two elements are needed before the party can insist on the precise wording: the originally specified object is gone, and the other side's corresponding object or performance is also no longer immediately available. Thus, where the ship remains but the specified wine was lost, the owner can require equivalent wine to be loaded. The worker analogy then fits: since the worker remains available, equivalent work can be assigned. Maharik likewise rules that one who swore to pay in a particular city may be required to bring payment to the creditor's nearby new location if there is no additional burden. This shows that equivalent performance can be substituted even where the exact original circumstance has changed. Regarding "this ship," however, substitution is different. Rental gives the renter an actual proprietary rental right in that specific ship for the term. The lessor cannot force him to accept another ship merely because it is equivalent. The same is true of "this donkey." The dispute among the authorities concerns substitution of the load or task, which is only a personal obligation. It does not permit substitution of the specific rented asset itself. This is clear from Tur's wording.
