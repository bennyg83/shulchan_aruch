Because there is greater insistence regarding a ship. The Sema unnecessarily lengthens the discussion and criticizes the Ir Shushan without justification. The Ir Shushan means only that the party who refuses performance must have a reason for his insistence. Otherwise, why may he refuse in the case of “this ship” but not in the case of an unspecified ship, and similarly regarding “this wine”? The Ramban's intent is the same. The Beit Yosef's reference to the Rambam is a printing error and should read Ramban. Two factors are required. Where the owner's specified ship sank and the renter's specified wine is no longer available, the owner may say: since I already lost my ship, I need not obtain another ship when you cannot supply the very wine specified. If only the wine sank but the ship remains, the renter may supply other wine even though the contract said “this wine” and “an unspecified ship,” because the shipowner has no greater reason to object than in any other case of substitution. Conversely, where the contract was for unspecified wine but this particular ship, the owner may be able to insist upon his ship and refuse a replacement even though the wine still exists. The Sema's wording should be corrected accordingly.
