Meaning, if the owners' practice with this field is to sow it one year and leave it fallow the following year so that its strength will be greater when sown in the third year, and they regularly alternate in this manner, the renter may not change that practice. The Tur gives an additional practical difference: a renter may not sublet, whereas a purchaser may transfer to another everything he acquired. The Mechaber omitted this because Beit Yosef challenged it from Rambam's ruling that with land a renter may sublet. It appears to me that there is a distinction between renting a house and renting a field. With a house, when one receives it intact he must return it intact, and any damage is apparent. A field, by contrast, can be weakened through continuous sowing or by planting crops that exhaust the soil, and it may not be evident that it was weakened. Sema. Taz writes that Rambam implies that only movable property may not be sublet, while immovable property is governed alike, whether a field or a house. It appears to me that the intent is that a renter may not sublet to one whose household contains more occupants than his own, as stated in siman 316, whereas a purchaser may transfer even to many purchasers. Taz.
