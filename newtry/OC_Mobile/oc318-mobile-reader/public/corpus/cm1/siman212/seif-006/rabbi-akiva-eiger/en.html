[Shulchan Aruch] He specified that he retained it for himself. Note: Regarding the airspace above a ruin or courtyard, even without this formulation it is effective for him to retain it for himself, since he did not sell it. It therefore remains with the seller, as stated in Responsa Rashba, cited by the Beit Yosef, siman 214. See Sema there, seif katan 26. See also the Rashba's novellae to Gittin 42a, regarding one who sells his slave with respect to the fine, where he writes that the rule concerning one who sells a ruin while retaining its airspace is because when one retains, he retains generously, and it is as though he retained the ruin for its airspace. This requires study.
