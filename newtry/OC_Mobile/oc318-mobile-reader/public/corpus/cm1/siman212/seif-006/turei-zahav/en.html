(Seif 6.) “A renter is not so.” Rambam’s wording is: “A renter may not rent it to another, whereas a purchaser may transfer to others what he purchased.” The Beit Yosef is astonished, because Rambam writes in chapter 5 of Laws of Rental that we say a renter may not sublet only regarding movables, lest the second renter steal them, but not regarding land. For this reason the Beit Yosef did not cite this language here. The Sema answers that with a field there is concern for damage, since the subtenant may fail to fertilize it, unlike a house, where one can deliver before witnesses a description of the house and its condition. With a field, its condition cannot be known. Yet if one examines Rambam there, one sees that he states the restriction specifically regarding movables. Things that are not movable have the same law, whether a field or a house. It therefore appears to me that Rambam means that the renter may not sublet to one who has more household members than the original renter, as stated in siman 315, whereas a purchaser may transfer the property even to many purchasers. One might ask: If so, there is indeed a difference between rental and purchase, so why was it not counted? The answer is that Rambam is in fact stating that this is the relevant difference and that this is the case under discussion.
