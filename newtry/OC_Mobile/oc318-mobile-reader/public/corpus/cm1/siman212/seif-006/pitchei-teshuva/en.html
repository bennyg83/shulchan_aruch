But a lessee is not so. See Baer Heitev and the Taz. The intent appears to be that a lessee may not sublease to someone whose household is substantially larger. Darkei Moshe and the Bach explain the passage similarly. Mishneh LaMelech, Hilchot Mechirah 23:8, offers another reconciliation based on Responsa Maharam Alshakar, siman 208, namely that the Rambam's rule allowing a lessee of land to sublease applies only where the original lessor has not yet received the rent. According to that approach, our case could involve a lessor who already received the rent. Mishneh LaMelech himself expresses reservations about that distinction. Indeed, the Sema, Darkei Moshe, Bach, and Taz did not answer in that manner, indicating that they do not accept Maharam Alshakar's limitation. In their view, even after the lessor has received the rent, the lessee may sublease to a tenant whose household is not larger. This is also stated explicitly by the Mordechai in the chapter HaUmanin, siman 345.
