(Seif 9: and similarly if he said, etc.) This is the Rosh's position mentioned above, distinguishing between "it will be" and "I will give it." It appears to me that the Mechaber did not intend here to rule independently like the Rosh. Rather, he is continuing the "there is an opinion" stated above, and this entire seif and the following one explain that opinion, which disagrees with the opening of seif 7 where no distinction is made between the formulations. The "there is an opinion" does distinguish, except that the Mechaber inserted between them the rule of purchasing land in seif 8 because it relates to the preceding point that even a firm internal resolve can resemble a vow. This resolves the Rama's objection that the Mechaber's words are incorrect. The Sema, seif katan 22, offers another distinction, but what I have written appears primary. On the contrary, at the beginning of Perek HaMafkid it appears that Rabbi Meir distinguishes regarding property not yet in existence between palm fruits, which are expected to come, and other matters. This implies that according to the Sages there is no such distinction. If so, from where can we derive a distinction concerning the phrase "when I acquire it"?
