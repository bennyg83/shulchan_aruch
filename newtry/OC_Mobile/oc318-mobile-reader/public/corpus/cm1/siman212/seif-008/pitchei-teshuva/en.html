And specifically if the person who made the vow is still alive, etc. See Responsa Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat, siman 111, concerning a man whose children had died. When another son was born, he symbolically "sold" the child to the chevra kadisha for five gold coins, stipulating that when the son reached marriage the father would pay the chevra kadisha ten gold coins and "buy" his son back. The father also vowed to give two small coins each week to the chevra kadisha until the son's marriage. Twenty-seven years passed without any payment. The father died, the son wished to marry, and the chevra kadisha sought to prevent the marriage until all sums were paid. The father left no assets beyond what was required for his wife's ketubah, so the children inherited nothing. Chatam Sofer rules that had the father left assets, the issue of the weekly vow would depend on the dispute whether heirs must fulfill their father's charitable vow from inherited assets. But where he left nothing, there is plainly no basis to require the children personally to pay his vow. As for the so-called sale of the child, a father has no power to sell or consecrate his son. The custom of "selling" a child after prior children have died is not a true sale of the body, but a symbolic practice motivated by concern that a decree may have been directed against this father's children. By assigning the child to another, they symbolically declare that the child will henceforth be regarded as the other's son and no longer called by the father's name. The condition that the father later "buy him back" at the time of marriage merely restores the paternal designation and the privilege of arranging his son's marriage. Accordingly, even if the father never buys him back, or is no longer alive to do so, and the chevra kadisha continues symbolically to hold him as a son, it cannot prevent his marriage. A father himself has no right to prevent an adult son's marriage, as explained in Yoreh De'ah at the end of siman 240, and it would therefore be a transgression for the chevra kadisha to delay the marriage even briefly. Nevertheless, since the son experienced divine salvation in connection with this custom, it is proper for him voluntarily to undertake, after his marriage and according to his means, to give the chevra kadisha something from time to time.
