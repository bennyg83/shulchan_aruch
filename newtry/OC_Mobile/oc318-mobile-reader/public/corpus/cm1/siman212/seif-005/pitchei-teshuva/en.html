He asks that the Ramban's own wording, cited in the preceding seif katan, does not present this as an obvious a fortiori case. Rather, the Ramban treats the repeated kinyan as an additional novelty. Rabbi Akiva Eiger suggests that the Rama's "all the more so" refers specifically to a deed that first states, "I hereby give," and then says that a kinyan was made. In such a case the later kinyan certainly supports the interpretation that the substance of the property was transferred. But where the deed first says, "Make a kinyan from me that I am giving the right to reside," the first kinyan itself is directed toward the intangible right. If the deed later repeats the kinyan, the effectiveness of the repetition is more doubtful, and the Rama may not have definitively ruled like the Ramban on that point. The practical distinctions emerging from Rabbi Akiva Eiger are as follows. If the deed says, "Make a kinyan from him that he gives the right to reside," and there is no repeated kinyan at the end, it is ineffective. If it says, "He gave the right to reside, and a kinyan was made from him," it is effective, and certainly so if the kinyan is repeated at the end. If it says, "Make a kinyan from him that he gives the right to reside," and then repeats the kinyan at the end, the matter is a doubt in law. If no kinyan is mentioned at the beginning, but the grant of residence is stated and only at the end the deed says, "and we made a kinyan from him," then according to Shai LaMora it is ineffective, while according to Rabbi Akiva Eiger it is effective. Finally, if the deed states that he gives future palm fruits or rental proceeds and a kinyan is made from him, R. Yaakov of Lisa regards it as effective, while Rabbi Akiva Eiger considers it a doubt in law.
