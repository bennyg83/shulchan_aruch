(Seif 4.) The expression “and likewise one who transfers it for its produce” necessarily indicates some point of comparison, since the text asks, “What is the difference,” rather than simply stating the law. It therefore appears to me that even where one transfers a field for its produce, there is a fixed term, namely until the first crop is gathered. If this were not so, and the expression granted the produce forever, then even Rav Nachman in seif 1 would agree that it means perpetually. This is also implied by the Tur’s wording nearby, “He may sow and plant it as long as he wishes,” which indicates that a transfer of a field for its produce is likewise perpetual. The same applies to one who purchases the produce of a beehive in siman 220, meaning all produce that will ever emerge, as Rashbam explains in the chapter HaSefinah, page 85, regarding the word “produce.” Rashbam’s statement that the produce of a beehive means that of one year implies that one who says simply “the produce of a dovecote” means forever. Hagahot Maimoniyot at the end of chapter 5 of Laws of Sale likewise writes, “and also to their descendants forever.” The reason in all these cases appears to be that if he had transferred the body itself, it would certainly belong to the purchaser absolutely. Similarly, transferring the produce of a beehive or dovecote is regarded as explicitly selling the dovecote or beehive for the produce that will emerge, as stated in siman 213. One may therefore properly distinguish this case from siman 60, where one who undertakes without specification to support another, according to the Rosh, need support him for only one year.
