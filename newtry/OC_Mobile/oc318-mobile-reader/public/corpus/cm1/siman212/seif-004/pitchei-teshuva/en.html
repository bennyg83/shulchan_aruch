See Beit Yosef at the end of siman 195 and Darkei Moshe here, which reproduce the Ramban's responsum at length. The Ramban adds that, were he not concerned that the witnesses might not know this law, he would have said that even where the deed first records one kinyan and then, at the end, states again, "and we made a kinyan from so-and-so concerning all that is written and explained above," the second kinyan would itself suffice to convey the substance of the property for the right of residence. The repetition of two kinyanim is not written for nothing, and may be understood as strengthening the beneficiary's rights. At first glance this is difficult, for if a kinyan written at the end of the deed is independently effective, why did the Ramban then need to reason from the fact that two kinyanim were written? Responsa Rabbi Akiva Eiger, siman 141, cites Responsa Shai LaMora, siman 29, which answers that a kinyan is regarded as coming "afterward" only when it immediately follows the language of transfer. A general "and we made a kinyan" written only at the end of the entire deed is not automatically treated as such. Therefore the Ramban needed the additional inference from the presence of two kinyanim. Rabbi Akiva Eiger himself suggests another explanation: a later kinyan is always effective, whether immediately after the grant or at the end of the deed, but only when no earlier kinyan had already been stated. If the deed began, "Make a kinyan from me that I am giving him the right to reside," that first kinyan indicates an attempted kinyan on the intangible right of residence rather than on the house itself. A second kinyan at the end might then be understood in the same limited fashion, unless the repetition itself indicates that it was added to strengthen the transaction. Rabbi Akiva Eiger further discusses a will made by a healthy person directing that thirty Reichsthaler each year be paid from the rental income of houses, with a concluding clause, "and we made a kinyan from him." He raises several difficulties. First, future rental proceeds are something not yet in existence. Second, the rental money is currency, which is not acquired through a sudar kinyan. The questioner, R. Yaakov of Lisa, argued that the concluding kinyan should cure these defects just as, under the Rama in siman 212, when one gives another the right to reside in his house and a kinyan is made, we interpret it as a kinyan on the substance of the house itself. Rabbi Akiva Eiger doubts this. According to Shai LaMora, a kinyan written only at the end of the instrument, with no second kinyan and not immediately following the dispositive words, is ineffective. More fundamentally, he questions whether the Ramban's principle extends from an intangible right to an object not yet in existence. The Ramban's foundation is the Gemara's rule that where a kinyan cannot meaningfully attach to the stated right itself, such as a bare waiver of claims or a right of residence, we may interpret the kinyan as attaching to the underlying property. But where the transfer concerns a defined asset and kinyan is conceptually applicable, yet the law invalidates it because the asset is not yet in existence, perhaps we do not reinterpret the kinyan as one on the underlying source property. He notes additional wording in that will showing that the testator apparently assumed ineffective forms of transfer were effective, which weakens the argument that the clause should be reconstructed into a valid transaction. He concludes that because the thirty Reichsthaler clause is doubtful, there may also be a question concerning the rest of the will if all provisions were included in a single kinyan. See there.
