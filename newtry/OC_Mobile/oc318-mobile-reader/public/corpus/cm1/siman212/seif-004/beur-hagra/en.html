One who sells, etc., it is sold. This is derived from Bava Kamma 68a, which needed a special exclusion for property transferred for thirty days. That exclusion is necessary there because the law requires something analogous to slaughter, which is irreversible; ordinary property rules do not require such permanence.
And he uses, etc., and eats, etc. From Arachin 31a, where such an arrangement is described as a form of, etc. There the seller has an immediate right of redemption. By contrast, regarding an ancestral field, 29b, there is not even an appearance of interest.
