It is as though he expressly stated it, etc. Because regarding a reservation for oneself we say that a person reserves generously for himself, as the Tur and Mechaber write in siman 209:7.
On condition that the upper story remain mine, etc. This law is explained with its reasoning in siman 214:11. The Tur writes there that the condition appears superfluous because, even without it, the upper story would not ordinarily be included in the sale of the house. We therefore interpret the extra language as intended to accomplish some additional legal effect, namely that the seller may extend projections from that upper story into the courtyard even if he sold the courtyard together with the house. This also shows that when the Tur and Mechaber here say merely that he sold "a house," they mean the house together with the courtyard. They mention the house because the upper story is physically above it.
And he also reserved for another person, we say, etc. This is not comparable to Rama's ruling in siman 203:10, where one transfers in a single kinyan an item that can be acquired together with something intangible, and Rama records a dispute. Here both reservations are of the same legal type. In each case one would need a reservation of a physical place to support the right of dwelling. Regarding oneself, we presume that he reserved such a place even without explicit words. Therefore, when the reservations for himself and the other person are made in a single act, we say that just as he reserved the place for himself, he did so for the other person as well. In siman 203, by contrast, the same kinyan sudar can acquire the movables but cannot acquire the money. Likewise in siman 209:4 Rama rules that when one transfers something not yet in existence together with something already in existence, the future item is not acquired, because the kinyan is inherently effective for the existing item but not for the future one. There is no basis to infer that he intended an additional reservation of place or other legal mechanism for the item that cannot be acquired.
