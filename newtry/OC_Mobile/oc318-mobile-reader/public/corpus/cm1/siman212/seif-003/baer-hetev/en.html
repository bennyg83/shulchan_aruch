Sema writes: This is not comparable to Rama's ruling in siman 203:10, where one acquires in a single transaction something for which an acquisition is effective together with something that has no substance, a matter subject to dispute. Here, by contrast, it appears that there is no dispute. One may answer that here the two are equivalent, because in either case it is necessary that he retain for himself a place for residence. With respect to himself, we assume that he implicitly retained the place. Therefore, since the transfer was effected at one time, just as he retained the place for himself, so too we say that he retained it for the other person. This differs from the case there, where movable property is acquired by barter but money is not. For the same reason Rama ruled in siman 209:4 that when one transfers something not yet in existence together with something already in existence, the former is not acquired. Since the act of acquisition is ineffective for the nonexistent item but effective for the existing item as it stands, what basis is there to say that he intended to reserve the place and thereby transfer the nonexistent item? Shach challenges Rama's ruling here and writes that the inference drawn from Ramban is inaccurate and is not implied there. See there. See Taz, who asks that here it appears to be effective only because the place is deemed reserved, but if 'on condition that' is language of a condition, it should not be effective. According to our ruling that 'on condition that' is indeed language of a condition, as Beit Yosef writes in siman 209, this is difficult for the Tur, who rules that 'on condition that' constitutes a reservation, whereas at the end of siman 209 he writes that it is a condition. The same difficulty arises from Shulchan Aruch and Tur in Yoreh Deah 61 regarding one who says, 'on condition that the cheeks are mine,' where it is treated as a condition. Taz leaves the matter difficult. Chacham Tzvi, in his glosses there, resolves these questions and responds to Shach and Pri Chadash in Yoreh Deah there, at length.
