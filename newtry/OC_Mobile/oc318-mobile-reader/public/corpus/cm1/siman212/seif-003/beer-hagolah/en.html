A statement of Reish Lakish, 'this teaches,' etc., there, 63a.
According to the Rashbam's explanation there, and likewise the Tur, seif 5. Tosafot there in the name of Rabbenu Tam object that the case is not about selling a courtyard but a house, and one who sells a house does not thereby sell the courtyard. Rabbenu Tam therefore explains that the reservation permits the seller to project beams from a structure he builds in the courtyard and rest them upon the upper story, even though this adds weight to the wall. Rambam likewise writes so in chapter 24 of Hilkhot Mekhirah, law 16, as the Maggid Mishneh explains there.
As Rav Zevid explains there at the beginning of 63b. Rambam also rules like Rav Pappa. Tosafot explain, s.v. if he wishes, that it does not mean literally upon the roof but upon pillars, as Rabbenu Tam explains there, 64a. The Maggid Mishneh likewise explains it this way and writes that we rule like Rav Zevid, and certainly like Rav Pappa, since Rav Zevid grants even more than Rav Pappa, as the Rif writes. I do not understand why the Mechaber did not write this as Rambam states it and as Tosafot, the Maggid Mishneh, the Rif, Beit Yosef, and siman 214 all explain and establish. Perhaps he holds that the Tur's law is also correct where the house and boundary were sold, as the Tur explains in siman 214, seif 11. Nevertheless, in my humble opinion the Mechaber should also have clarified here that the case is where he sold the house and courtyard.
