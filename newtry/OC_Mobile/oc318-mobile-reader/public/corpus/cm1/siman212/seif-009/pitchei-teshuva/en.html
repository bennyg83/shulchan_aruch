See Baer Heitev, which implies that the discussed exemption applies where the donor died before the beneficiary or object came into existence, and that if he died afterward his heirs might be required to fulfill the undertaking. Responsa Rama, siman 48, question 3, records that this was indeed the view of the Beit Yosef in a responsum, also printed in Avkat Rochel, siman 83: one who vows to give to the poor and dies leaves an obligation that his heirs must fulfill. The Rama strongly disagrees. He argues that unless the poor acquired through an actual kinyan, the heirs are not compelled to fulfill the deceased's vow. Rivash, simanim 328 and 335, similarly rules that although a person who swears concerning an item not yet in existence must personally keep his oath, his heirs need not do so after his death. The same applies to a charitable vow, whose force during his lifetime derives from the law of vows. The Rama analyzes Maharam's words in the Mordechai, chapter Mi SheMet, which might imply that if the vow had already taken effect during the deceased's lifetime the heirs would be bound. He explains that Maharam merely spoke according to the facts before him and did not intend to establish such a distinction. The Rama's position is also explicit below in siman 252:2, where he rules that one who swore or vowed to give a sum to a particular person and died leaves heirs who are exempt. This naturally includes a charitable vow. Responsa Panim Meirot II, siman 134, likewise explains that the rule equating an oral charitable declaration with delivery applies in its strongest form to consecrated property, whereas in ordinary charity the obligation is one of vow. Maharit, Choshen Mishpat, siman 3, also argues at length that heirs are not bound by a deceased person's vow, and Ketzot HaChoshen in siman 252:5 states this plainly. In siman 290:2, however, Ketzot attempts to justify the Beit Yosef. He suggests that because Scripture treats a charitable vow as a binding verbal undertaking, the obligation may create a lien on the donor's property no weaker than an ordinary debt, so that heirs who inherit the property would be bound. Ketzot nevertheless concludes that the authorities appear to follow the Rama, namely that a charitable vow does not create a property lien.
