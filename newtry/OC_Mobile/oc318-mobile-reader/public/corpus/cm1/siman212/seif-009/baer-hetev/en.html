The Mechaber and Sema write that there is a sound distinction. The Rambam holds that where one vows concerning 'what my cow will give birth to' or 'what this tree will produce,' matters that arise automatically, the vow takes effect and must be fulfilled. In contrast, acquisition of a house is something not within his control and will not arise automatically. This also resolves the words of the Kesef Mishneh and Beit Yosef, who write that the Rosh agrees with the Rambam in the foregoing law. According to the Rosh as well, it is specifically collection of a debt, which likewise does not arise automatically, concerning which he writes that the vow does not take effect unless he says, 'when it comes into my possession...' See there. Thus far the Sema. The Shach agrees. In his gloss there, however, he writes that the Rama's words are correct and that the Sema's distinction cannot be maintained, because the Rambam in chapter 6 of Hilchot Arachin explicitly indicates that no such distinction is made. See there and the Taz.
