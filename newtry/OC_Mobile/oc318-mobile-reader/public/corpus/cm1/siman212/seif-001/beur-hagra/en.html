Therefore, etc. According to Rav Hai Gaon and the Rambam, even the phrase “he shall eat the produce” fails because it concerns something intangible. This is why the Gemara speaks of “he shall eat, etc.” even where the produce already exists.
One who says, “So-and-so shall reside,” etc. Rav Nachshon Gaon explains the Gemara’s phrase “So-and-so shall reside, etc.” as ineffective because no duration was fixed, so even one hour could satisfy the wording. This differs from one who says, “Give this house, etc.” The Rama’s words are difficult, because according to our ruling he does not acquire in any event, as explained above. Shach and Taz likewise challenge this.
A document, etc., and they made a kinyan, etc. Whenever a formal kinyan was made, even if a mere verbal declaration would not create liability, we interpret the kinyan as taken from the substance of the land, because a kinyan is a strong legal act that perfects the matter. Thus at the beginning of Kesubos chapter 7, 83a, Rav Nachman says, “from the substance of the land, etc.” See Rashi there, s.v. “what is the law, etc.” We rule like Rav Nachman. Likewise Kesubos 95a and Bava Batra 43a establish cases where a kinyan was made. Rabbeinu Yonah explains that this applies specifically when the kinyan comes after the disclaimer: he first says “I have no claim or dealings” and then a kinyan is made from him. But if he says only “make a kinyan from me that I have no claim or dealings,” the kinyan is only on those words and does not transfer the underlying right. This is the meaning of the case of Reuven, etc.; but if not, etc.
All the more so if, etc. Whenever the later wording was added to strengthen the recipient’s rights, we interpret it as such, as stated in Bava Batra 136a concerning “whether he transferred ownership, etc.” See Rashbam there, s.v. “he transferred ownership, etc.”
