Seif 1. Something without tangible substance. It is written in the holy works, especially Bnei Chayei, that acquisitions of chazakot and leasing rights known as arendash in these countries are effective by virtue of dina demalchuta dina, even though they are classified as matters lacking tangible substance, property not in one's possession, and something not yet in existence. This is not changed by the imperial decree in these countries that a Jew may not lease such village concessions. One cannot say that this decree removes the force of dina demalchuta, because with respect to non-Jews the civil law still recognizes acquisition of such concessions, and when a non-Jew leases one to another non-Jew the transaction is valid under dina demalchuta. The decree therefore does not alter the property law governing the acquisition itself. Certainly where a Jew leases the right only in order to sublease it to a non-Jew and profit, without himself residing in the village, the decree may not apply at all, and presumably the acquisition should remain fully effective so that he may sublease it. The rule of 'acquire, you and a donkey' may also apply to such a concession because it contains many components that already exist and are in the seller's control. Likewise, the doctrine of 'if he seizes and consumes it' may have application.
