Tangible. See Responsa Maharashdam, simanim 271 and 322; Responsa R. Moshe Alshich, siman 87; Responsa Mabit, part 2, siman 329; Responsa Mahari ben Lev, part 1, siman 59, folio 82, and part 4, folio 70b.
Produce. The Sema explains that even though the fruit itself exists, the abstract right of eating is something intangible. The Taz writes that if the fruit already exists and he says, 'He shall eat the fruit of this palm tree,' it appears effective according to all opinions. See there. One who says, 'I make a kinyan that I grant the choice to so-and-so,' has attempted to transfer an intangible right and may retract. R. Shlomo HaLevi, Choshen Mishpat, siman 47. Bnei Chayei.
He shall dwell. Hagahot Mordechai in the name of Rav Nachshon Gaon states that for this reason the Gemara says that one who says, 'So-and-so shall dwell in this house,' has accomplished nothing, because the expression implies only a temporary stay. According to this, when he transfers the body of the house itself, which the Gemara explicitly says is effective, we do not interpret it as only one hour. The phrase 'shall dwell' is treated differently because it denotes an occasional act. Thus Rav Nachshon Gaon disagrees with the Rambam, who explains the deficiency as intangibility. It nevertheless requires study why the Rama did not cite this view. Certainly if the body of the house is transferred it is effective even according to Rav Nachshon, since this is explicit in the Gemara. I do not understand the Sema's statement that when one transfers a house for dwelling it implies only one hour. It appears to me that when one says simply 'a house to dwell in,' it means permanently, just as I explained, with the help of Heaven, regarding a field for its produce. Taz. The Shach's gloss likewise writes this. If he was already dwelling in the house, Maharashdam, siman 323, rules that the body of the house was given to him. Maharashach, siman 176, and Mahari Adarbi, siman 295, disagree. See R. Shlomo Yonah, siman 32. Bnei Chayei.
An hour. The Rama writes in siman 60, seif 3, that according to some, if one undertakes without specification to support his fellow or to give him one hundred gold coins per year, he satisfies his vow after one year. This implies that he must at least support him for a year. I wrote there, proving from Responsa Rosh, that this applies specifically there because he revealed his intent to support him for at least one year by ending his statement, 'or I will give him one hundred gold coins per year.' But if he simply undertook to support him, even one day suffices, because the burden of proof rests on the claimant. See there also what I wrote to reconcile the Rama at the end of siman 163, where someone exempted from taxes without specification may be understood to have been exempted for only one year, since that passage does not discuss every type of tax. To explain. The Ramban writes there that a kinyan is a strong legal act. When the kinyan language appears at the end, we interpret that he did not give only an intangible right of dwelling, but made a kinyan in the body of the land itself. This is unlike where the deed places the kinyan language before the dwelling clause, as the ruling concludes. Ibid.
Effective. One should not object from siman 211, seif 6, which requires proof that the money existed, because there the witnesses specified the mode of acquisition by writing 'agav land.' It is possible that the witnesses merely recorded the facts and did not intend to attest that the acquisition was legally effective. Here, however, when they write 'and we made a kinyan from him concerning this,' they imply that a legally sufficient kinyan occurred, but they did not wish to spell out its details. Regarding an admission by a litigant, the Rama already writes in siman 60, seif 6, that we do not scrutinize afterward, because we presume it was done in an effective manner. Taz.
