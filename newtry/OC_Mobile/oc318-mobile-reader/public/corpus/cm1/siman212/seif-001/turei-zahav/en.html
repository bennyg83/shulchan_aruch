(Seif 1: The consumption of the produce.) The same law applies if he said, “He shall consume the produce,” for that is the formulation in the Gemara, in the chapter Mi Shemet: if a person on his deathbed said, “So-and-so shall dwell in this house,” or, “He shall consume the produce of this palm tree,” he has said nothing. Rambam refers to this ruling and explains that the reason is that the subject has no substance. Rashbam and Tosafot, however, explain that the reason is that a person cannot transfer something not yet in existence. This is why the Tur did not cite the ruling concerning consumption of produce. It appears to me that if the produce already exists and he says, “He shall consume the produce of the palm tree,” the transfer is effective according to all authorities. According to Rashbam and Tosafot, who explain the defect as something not yet in existence, this is obvious, since here the produce exists. Even Rambam, who explains the defect as lack of substance, agrees in this case. The proof is that in siman 253, seif 4, the Tur writes in the name of the Ramah that if a person on his deathbed said, “He shall consume the produce of a palm tree,” it is effective. If this were something without substance, it would not be effective even for a person on his deathbed, as stated nearby. We certainly rule accordingly, since the Tur cites no dissent. One might object that the Tur there records a dispute concerning a healthy person who makes an acquisition, whether expressions that work for a person on his deathbed are effective, and the Shulchan Aruch there does not decide the issue. We should therefore not extract money by saying that an acquisition by a healthy person is effective. This is not so. There the reason is not that the matter lacks substance. The Beit Yosef writes that Rambam and his teacher, Ri Migash, agree with the Ramah that an acquisition by a healthy person is ineffective with these expressions because they connote a future transfer. Accordingly, if a healthy person says with an act of acquisition, “He shall consume the produce of the palm tree today,” it is plainly effective. Even regarding the expression, “I will give you,” siman 245, seif 3, states that it is effective when accompanied by “from now.” This appears correct.
