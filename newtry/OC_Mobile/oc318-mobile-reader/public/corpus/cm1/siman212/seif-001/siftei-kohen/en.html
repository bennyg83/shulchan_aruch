One who says, 'So-and-so shall dwell,' etc., for one hour, etc. The gloss appears to mean that even if he transferred the substance of the house for purposes of residence, the grant means only one hour. The Ir Shushan and Sema, seif katan 3, explain it this way. In truth, however, Hagahot Mordechai clearly implies that if he transfers the house itself for residence, the recipient acquires the right to dwell there forever according to all opinions. Rav Nachshon explains the first clause, 'So-and-so shall dwell in this house,' as ineffective because it may mean only one hour. This implies that in the latter clause, 'Give this house to him and he shall dwell in it,' he acquires the right permanently. The Baal HaItur, second discourse, Kinyan, page 5a, likewise implies this. The reason is compelling: once the substance of the house is acquired for residence, the house remains his for that purpose indefinitely. The Rama's words and those who follow him therefore require study. I presented this matter to my father-in-law, the Gaon, and he approved.
