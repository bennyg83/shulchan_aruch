(In the gloss there: Only for one hour.) The Hagahot Mordekhai there, in the name of Rav Nachshon Gaon, writes that this is why the Gemara says that one who says, “So-and-so shall dwell in this house,” has said nothing, because the expression implies only one hour. According to this, one must say that if he transfers the house itself, which the Gemara there explicitly says is effective, it is not limited to one hour. Only the expression “shall dwell” is understood that way, because it denotes a temporary act. It follows that Rav Nachshon Gaon disagrees with Rambam, who explains that the statement is ineffective because its subject has no substance. Nevertheless, it requires study why the Rama did not cite this view. Certainly, where he transfers the house itself, the transfer is effective even according to Rav Nachshon, for this is an explicit Gemara. I have not understood the Sema’s conclusion when he writes that one who transfers a house for dwelling implies only one hour. In any event, it appears to me that when he says simply, “a house in which to dwell,” he means permanently, as is evident in seif 4 regarding a field for its produce. (See there: And they made an acquisition from him regarding this.) One cannot object from siman 211, seif 6, where the Mechaber writes that proof must be brought that the money existed, and so forth. There the witnesses specified the manner of acquisition, writing that it was by means of movables together with land. It may therefore be that the witnesses meant only to say that there was an acquisition through land, and recorded the events as they occurred, without intending to testify that it was legally effective. Here, however, where they wrote, “and they made an acquisition from him regarding this,” the implication is that they testify that there was an acquisition sufficient for the matter, but did not wish to elaborate on how it was performed. Regarding the language of a litigant’s admission, the Rama already wrote in siman 60, seif 6, that we do not scrutinize it in this way, because it was certainly done in an effective manner.
