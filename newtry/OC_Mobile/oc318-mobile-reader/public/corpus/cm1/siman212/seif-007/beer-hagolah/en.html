Samekh) Rambam likewise writes in chapter 6 of Hilkhot Arakhin VaCharamin, halachah 32, and brings proof from Yaakov Avinu's words, "Of all that You give me, I will surely tithe to You," and from the verse, "where you vowed a vow to Me." The Raavad writes in his gloss that the proof from Yaakov is indeed a valid proof, and the Maggid Mishneh cites him there.
Ayin) The Maggid Mishneh writes that the first part is Rambam's own view, as explained above, and that some commentators likewise wrote it. Rambam adds that even with a gift of a deathly ill person, although the person who made the vow is no longer alive, there is a mitzvah to fulfill the words of the deceased just as he himself would have been obligated were he alive. The Kesef Mishneh asks why the poor should acquire the property, since the deceased is no longer in the world, the heirs did not vow, and the deceased's vow did not devolve upon them. He explains that this is the Raavad's objection. In his view, Rambam holds that because the deceased said, "Whatever this tree produces shall go to the poor," and the heirs heard and remained silent, they accepted the obligation and it is as though they themselves vowed. The Bach offers a stronger explanation: since the words of a deathly ill person are regarded as written and delivered so that his mind not become distressed, all the more so where he vowed property to the poor do we fear distress even though the subject had not yet come into existence. He further writes that once the vow took effect upon the deceased and there is a mitzvah to fulfill his words, the obligation attaches to his estate; the poor acquired a claim during his lifetime and the heirs must pay.
