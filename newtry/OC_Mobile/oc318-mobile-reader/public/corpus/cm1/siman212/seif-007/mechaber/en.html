The law of consecrated property, charity for the poor, and vows does not follow the ordinary law of acquisition between private persons. If one says, "Everything my animal will give birth to shall be consecrated for the upkeep of the Beit HaMikdash," or, "It shall be forbidden to me," or, "I will give it to charity," then although the object does not become consecrated because it does not yet exist, he must fulfill his word, as it is stated, "Whatever proceeds from his mouth he shall do." Accordingly, if a person on his deathbed instructs, "Everything this tree produces shall go to the poor," or, "All the rent from this house shall go to the poor," the poor acquire those proceeds. Rama: Others say that neither consecrated property nor charity acquires anything, because a person cannot consecrate something that has not yet come into existence, and his words are therefore ineffective. But if he says, "When my animal gives birth," or, "When this tree produces fruit," and the like, "I will give it to consecrated use or to the poor," he must fulfill his words because of the vow. Tur in the name of Rosh. This applies only while the person who made the vow is alive. If he has died, nothing is binding, because he is no longer present to fulfill his vow. Mordechai, chapter Mi Shemet. The latter view is primary and should be followed. See below, siman 252, seif 2. If he swore to uphold a sale, see above, siman 209, seif 4.
