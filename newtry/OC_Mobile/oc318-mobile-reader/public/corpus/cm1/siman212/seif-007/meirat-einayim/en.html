Or I will give it to charity. The Tur, citing the Rambam, reads, “or you shall give it to charity.” It is specifically in that formulation that they disagree with him, as Rama concludes. But if he says, “I will give it,” all agree that he is obligated to fulfill it by force of his vow. See the Derishah for what I wrote on this.
If a person gave instructions while on his deathbed, etc. The Beit Yosef writes that this refers to a case where his sons were present when he gave the instruction and remained silent, for then we say that they accepted it upon themselves. Otherwise, since the one who made the vow died and the sons themselves made no vow, they are not obligated to fulfill it. In the Perishah I wrote that in my view the Rambam's reason is that whenever he speaks this way, it is as though he transferred the house to the poor until they receive the rent, and likewise the tree with respect to its fruit. His sons therefore do not inherit that property until they first fulfill the vow.
When my animal gives birth or my tree produces, etc. In these cases even the Rosh agrees that he need not add, “when it comes into my possession I will give it,” because immediately when the animal gives birth or the tree produces fruit, it is treated as having come into his possession. Only regarding a debt owed to him by a particular person did the Rosh require him to say, “when it comes from his hand into mine.” See there and the Perishah.
