Some say, etc. They disagree with Rambam in two respects. First, only when he said, 'I shall give it,' is he obligated to fulfill his vow, as with Jacob's vow, 'of all that You give me I shall surely tithe.' If he attempts an immediate consecration by saying 'it shall be consecrated,' the consecration does not take effect and he need not give it. This follows Bava Kamma 36b and Rif, though Rambam can be defended according to Rabbenu Hananel. Rambam himself writes that although one cannot consecrate future property, one who says, 'I undertake to consecrate it,' must do so when it comes into existence because of his vow, citing Jacob. Similar examples include Israel's vow and Hannah's vow concerning Samuel, and the discussions in Ketubot and Nedarim. Second, they dispute Rambam's ruling that in the case of a person on his deathbed the poor acquire. According to their view, only the speaker is bound by his promise, so the heirs are exempt. See Sema, and siman 258.
