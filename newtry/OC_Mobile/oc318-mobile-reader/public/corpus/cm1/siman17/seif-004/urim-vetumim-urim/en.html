The defendant too should not bring his friends beside him. The reason is concern that they will signal to him with facial expressions or eye movements and coach false responses. Sema says this is not left to the preferences of the plaintiff and defendant; beit din itself should compel both parties to appear without such entourages. He therefore reads the Shulchan Aruch's text accordingly. Bach, however, implies that the matter does depend on the parties' objection. If neither litigant minds the other's companions, what reason does beit din have to intervene? See Tumim, where I conclude that Bach's view is correct.
