The law is with him; therefore the partners, etc. The correct text should include 'therefore.' Mordechai records a responsum of Maharam: if the defendant says he wants relatives seated beside him, while the claimant's partners object that they do not want their arguments disclosed before those relatives lest they signal the defendant how to plead, each side has a legitimate concern. Therefore the partners too should not all sit there; only the person actually presenting their claim sits. Beit din, not either litigant, imposes the arrangement.
Or each one may plead for himself. The choice belongs to the partners. This is what the Mechaber later means in siman 176, seif 35, when he says a defendant cannot force two or three brothers who have a claim against him to send only one representative. They may each present their own case if they wish; the point is not that they can all speak simultaneously against the defendant's will.
