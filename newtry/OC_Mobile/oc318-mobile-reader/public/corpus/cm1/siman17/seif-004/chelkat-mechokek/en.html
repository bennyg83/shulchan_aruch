In our time, when brazenness and immorality have increased. Bach writes that today this creates only an initial prohibition against marrying, but if she already remarried we do not remove her on this basis. He brings proof from Responsa Rosh, rule 44, siman 20, where a woman remarried while her first husband was in the city and the Rosh permitted her to the second husband because she married him in the first husband's presence. Since Rosh lived long after the Ramah who advanced the concern that brazenness had increased, Bach infers that the Ramah meant only initially. This is no proof. Rosh does not cite the Ramah at all, and indeed in the same rule, siman 1, he rules that she is believed even initially to remarry. That is contrary to the Ramah entirely. Once we choose to take the Ramah's concern into account, there is no source for distinguishing initially from after the fact.
