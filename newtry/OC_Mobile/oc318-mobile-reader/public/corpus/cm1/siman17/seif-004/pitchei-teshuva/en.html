If the beit din wished to seat them. Birkei Yosef cites Rav Uri, who quotes Tashbetz, perhaps the reference should be Rashbash, siman 117, as saying that the litigants themselves may not sit on their own initiative. If they did so, beit din must tell them to stand, and afterward, if the judges wish to seat them, they may do so. This resolves Tosafot's question in Sanhedrin 19 and Shevuot 30 from the incident of Shimon ben Shetach and King Yannai: Yannai sat on his own, and therefore Shimon ben Shetach had to tell him to stand. Beit Yosef, siman 28, however, cites the Mordechai to the opposite effect. Birkei Yosef adds that this resolution of Tosafot's question is also given by Rabbeinu Yonah in his manuscript novellae to Sanhedrin, by Rashba in his novellae to Shevuot, and by Ran in his novellae to Sanhedrin. The cited Mordechai is also brought by Bach and Knesset HaGedolah in this siman. See Tosafot in Shevuot, Responsa Emunat Shmuel, siman 15, and Tzon Kodashim to Zevachim 6, fourth column.
