Even if she is not presently her mother-in-law. The reason given in Yevamot 117a is that she anticipates that perhaps the husband will die and the woman will fall before her son for yibbum, thereby becoming her daughter-in-law. According to this reason, if the woman cannot enter yibbum with her son because she is an ervah to him or for another reason, the mother-in-law may testify for her. This question is raised in the Gemara there. According to the Beit Yosef we rule leniently, since the matter is one that is likely to become known. It is also possible that in these countries, where yibbum is not practiced and the stated concern does not apply, one may rely on the Beit Yosef and permit her testimony.
