The judge should not act as an advocate, etc. Sema explains that while a litigant is speaking before beit din, the judge should not supply arguments on his behalf. He should let the litigant state whatever he sees as his own defense and remain silent. Kesef Mishneh explains differently: when announcing the ruling, the judge should not say, "This party argued intelligently and that party foolishly," and the like. He should simply state the judgment as he understands it and no more.
We do not impose monetary liability on the basis of one witness. Sema writes that the Tur and Rosh disagree and permit the judge to tell the litigant this, because it falls under the duty to "open your mouth for the mute," helping a party who does not know how to present a valid legal point. Ir Shushan holds that all agree it is forbidden. See Tumim, where I conclude that Ir Shushan's position appears correct.
