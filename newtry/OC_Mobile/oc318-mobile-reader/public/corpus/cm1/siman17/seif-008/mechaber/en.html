If a judge issued a ruling but his conscience troubles him that he may have erred, he must not cling to his words and seek proofs to uphold them because he is ashamed to retract. Rather, he must examine every aspect so that the true judgment emerges. A judge must not serve as an advocate for a litigant's words. He should state what appears correct to him and then remain silent, and he must not teach either litigant how to argue. Even if one litigant brought a single witness, the judge should not tell him, 'Money cannot be collected on the testimony of one witness.' Instead, he says to the defendant, 'This person testified against you,' and waits for the defendant himself to argue, 'One witness is not believed against me.' The same applies in all similar cases.
