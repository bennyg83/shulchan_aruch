It is forbidden for a judge to hear one litigant alone. Knesset HaGedolah, letter 13, writes that Radach, responsum Beit 26, and Maharashdam, Yoreh Deah 153 in the name of Rav Avraham Formon, regard this prohibition as biblical. Raanach, volume 1, siman 4, writes that there is no distinction between a lone judge, an ordinary beit din, or a panel chosen by the parties. Knesset HaGedolah, letter 14, adds that the prohibition applies initially, but after the fact the judge is not disqualified, citing Maharibal, volume 3, siman 98, Maharashdam, Choshen Mishpat, siman 2, and the Sema. He questions whether these authorities disagree with Radach, for if the prohibition is biblical, perhaps even after the fact the judge should be disqualified. Eretz DeRabbanan, letter 183, infers simply from Maharibal that he indeed disagrees with Radach and regards the prohibition as rabbinic, since a biblical prohibition ordinarily does not distinguish between initially and after the fact. Afra DeAra counters that Radach's view is plainly biblical because it is derived from a verse, while Maharibal proves nothing: judging at night is biblically prohibited, yet many authorities hold that a judgment rendered at night remains valid, and similarly a convert's immersion at night may be effective although conversion is called 'judgment.' Birkei Yosef, letter 4, discusses whether biblical prohibitions can distinguish between initially and after the fact. In letter 8 he strongly rebukes judges who disregard this rule and hear one litigant alone in their homes. He cites the Zohar, Vayeshev 149b, that a judge who accepts one person's words before the other arrives is as though he accepts another deity as trustworthy. He laments that he has seen such conduct among decisors and cites the saying that a breach not originating with the great is no breach, together with Shemlah Chadashah at the end of Sotah concerning improper judicial practices. He further writes that if one litigant wishes to tell the judge both his own claims and those of his opponent, the judge may not hear him. Two verses are needed for these two forms of the prohibition, 'Keep far from a false matter' and 'Hear between your brothers.' Edut BiYehosef, volume 2, siman 1, and the law is clear and true.
