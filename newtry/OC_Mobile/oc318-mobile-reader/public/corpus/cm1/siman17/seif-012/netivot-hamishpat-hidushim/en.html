The Shach writes that this applies only where the claimant made no further claim and we do not know whether he refrained from claiming more because he waived it or because he erred in the law. In that situation we attribute his silence to waiver. But where we know that he erred in the law, the waiver was mistaken and the judge must award him more, unlike the Sema. See Beurim, seif katan 1, that the principal rule applies specifically to the case of one who surrounds his neighbor's property with a fence. If he claims half the fencing expenses, then upon payment his neighbor acquires half of the fence. If he claims only the fee for guarding, the neighbor acquires no share in the fence. Thus, by claiming only a guarding fee there is no money presently held wrongfully by the defendant, even if the plaintiff is mistaken in the law. Since the plaintiff did not intend to convey half the fence, there is presently no obligation at all under the claim he actually made. The judge merely sees that if he would claim more, namely half the fencing expenses, the defendant would then become obligated to reimburse half and acquire half the fence. Since no such claim has yet been made, the judge may not award more. By contrast, where the judge sees that according to law the defendant is already holding money that rightfully belongs to the plaintiff, and the plaintiff claims less only because he is mistaken in the law, the judge must inform the plaintiff that the defendant is obligated to return it, because it is wrongfully in his possession. We attribute the short claim to error rather than waiver, just as where an overpayment is discovered and the discrepancy is of a kind that a person might reasonably make by mistake, the excess must be returned. It is an error of law. The Shach writes that this applies only if the money has not yet been extracted. If it has already been extracted, the ruling is not reversed. See Beurim, seif katan 2, that if the money was already extracted, then even if the other party did not initially say that he claimed too little because of a mistake, the ruling does not automatically return. But if he subsequently claims that he demanded too little because he misunderstood the law, he is believed. Even before the money was extracted, if he makes that claim the judge awards the larger amount. This is according to the Shach.
