Seif 4: “If there are many claimants” The Sema’s explanation is unclear, especially his implication that beit din must object even if both sides consent. Mordechai’s case is that multiple claimants wished to sit together as one party and objected to the defendant bringing silent supporters, fearing hints. The defendant may insist that only one representative plead for all, or that they plead sequentially. Yet he cannot use that right to permit all claimants to sit while surrounding himself with relatives or friends. If the partners remain silent and consent to his friends’ presence, beit din need not intervene. Each side may therefore prevent the other’s friends from sitting nearby even silently; the contrary custom is foolish.
