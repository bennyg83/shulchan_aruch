They seat them. Regarding witnesses and litigants, however, there is a mitzvah to fulfill "the two men shall stand." Bach says they should answer the judges: "We will stand before Hashem and before you, for judgment belongs to God." Shiltei Gibborim writes that this applies specifically to monetary law. In a Torah-prohibition matter, such as testimony permitting or prohibiting a woman to her husband, the Sages cannot validate testimony given while seated if sitting truly disqualifies the testimony. He cites Ri'az. This assumes that standing is indispensable to valid testimony. According to the authorities who hold that testimony given while seated is valid after the fact and that the standing requirement is only an asmachta, there is no distinction between monetary and ritual matters. The testimony remains legally valid in either context. Shiltei Gibborim's statement that witnesses who testified while seated may repeat their testimony suggests that he indeed holds their first testimony invalid. That is why his distinction is internally consistent.
