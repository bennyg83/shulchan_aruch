A Torah scholar who comes to court. The Ran explains in Shevuot 32a that Rav Nachman said, 'Spread a cloak for her,' only because she was the wife of a scholar. For a Torah scholar himself, however, the rule is different.
(Anthology) A Torah scholar who comes, etc. In the second chapter of Ketubot we find that Rav Nachman removed the litigation of orphans from before him to hear the case of a scholar's relative. See siman 15:1, which requires precedence even in such a case. There is no concern that the other litigant's arguments will become confused, because this is the law. Only regarding the widow of a scholar, after his death, might ordinary people fail to understand the legal basis for the honor shown her.
