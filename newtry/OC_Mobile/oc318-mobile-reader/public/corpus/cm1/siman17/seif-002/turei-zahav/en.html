Seif 1, in the gloss “Standing by leaning is considered standing.” The Sema disagrees, while my revered father-in-law permits it, counting it as standing for one purpose and sitting for another. In my view Rama’s premise is incorrect. Responsa Rivash 266 states the reverse: one who would fall if the support were removed is not standing, as Zevachim 19 rules. Rivash only derives by an a fortiori argument that such posture may count as sitting. Thus leaning is not standing at all, though it may count as sitting. The proposed distinction between Torah-level service and rabbinic courtroom standing lacks basis, because the very derivation of courtroom posture would then fail. An appended defense of Rama argues that Rivash’s final reason, that judicial sitting is only rabbinic, permits leaning even initially, and the same applies to witnesses. Nonetheless, the primary conclusion here is that leaning is not standing.
