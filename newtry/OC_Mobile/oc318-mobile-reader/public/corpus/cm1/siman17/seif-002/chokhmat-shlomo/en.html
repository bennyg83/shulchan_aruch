Seif 8. “Even if a litigant produced one witness, the judge should not say, ‘One witness is not believed.’” Note: The later authorities cite a dispute regarding this. I saw in the Yerushalmi, Rosh Hashanah chapter 3, halachah 1, an incident in which Rav Huna knew testimony concerning a certain person and that person came to litigate before him. The litigant denied the claim. Rabbi Shmuel bar Rabbi Yitzchak said to him: “Because you know that Rav Huna is a great man, do you deny before him? What if he goes and testifies against you in another beit din?” Rav Huna asked whether one may speak in this manner, and he was told that one may. Rav Huna then removed himself from the case and testified in another court. Korban HaEdah struggles to explain Rav Huna’s question, “May one do this?” In my view there is no need for the difficulty. Rav Huna was asking why Rabbi Shmuel had given the opposing litigant an opening to refuse to be judged before him, for this might seem like acting as an advocate for a litigant. This is directly analogous to our case, where the judge says that one witness is not believed. The answer was that such a statement is permitted and is not considered improper advocacy. The Yerushalmi therefore supports the opinion that a judge may say, “One witness is not believed.”
