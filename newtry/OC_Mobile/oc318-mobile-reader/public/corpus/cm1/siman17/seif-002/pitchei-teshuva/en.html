They say to the distinguished litigant. See Baer HaGolah in the name of Maharshal, who writes that we do not have the power to compel him in this manner, but we say to him, 'Do not fear,' and so forth. This implies that, in any event, one must say to the other litigant, 'Do not fear.' This is also explicit in the Bach and Knesset HaGedolah. The Shach, however, implies that there is no need to say this, and Urim VeTumim likewise writes that for this reason the practice is not to say anything at all. See Responsa Maharashdam, Choshen Mishpat, siman 2.
