He hears through an interpreter. The Tur and Mechaber rule similarly below in siman 28:7 concerning testimony. The Gemara derives this from the verse, "by the mouth of two witnesses." The rule concerning litigants is learned from witnesses, because the reason is the same: truth can be clarified more effectively when the judge hears directly from the litigants and witnesses themselves. Therefore, if the judge understands their language but is not fluent enough to converse with them, an interpreter may stand between them. The interpreter speaks to them in the judge's presence, conveying the questions, examinations, and ruling that the judge places in his mouth, while the judge himself understands their responses and can evaluate their words. In Perishah I inferred from Rambam that even where the judge does not need an interpreter because he can respond adequately, the litigants may still use an interpreter to express and clarify their own words to the judge after they themselves first present their claims before the judges. I also distinguished between a case where the litigant gives the interpreter legal authority and power, so that he becomes like the litigant's representative and is permitted, and a case where no such authority is given, which is prohibited. The current practice of adjudicating foreign-language speakers through their authorized representatives rests on the principle that anyone who appears before established judges is deemed to have accepted their procedural practice. After such acceptance there is no further objection. See Perishah for more.
