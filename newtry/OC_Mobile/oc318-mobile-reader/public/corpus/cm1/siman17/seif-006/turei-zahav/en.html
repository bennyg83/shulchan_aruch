In the Tur, seif 14 “This is not implied by the Yerushalmi,” etc. Tur and Rambam differ in interpreting Rav Huna’s two statements. Rambam reconciles them by distinguishing whether the litigant had begun to articulate the defense. Tur instead explains that the judge should first confront the litigant with the single witness, perhaps eliciting an admission, and should not himself immediately teach, “One witness is not believed.” If the litigant does not answer, then during judicial deliberation the judge must raise that defense for him under “open your mouth for the mute.” Thus the two Yerushalmi passages are consistent.
