And they hold that it must literally be in her husband's presence. In Maharik, from whom Rama derived this rule, the wording actually suggests the opposite: he writes that the phrase 'in his presence' does not necessarily prove literal physical presence. Rivash, siman 127, likewise discusses a woman who claims that her husband lacks male potency, and writes that she must come before beit din and state this in her husband's presence. But where a woman accepts kiddushin while her husband is in the city, even if we say that receiving kiddushin outside his presence proves nothing, once the husband comes and she maintains that kiddushin and says he divorced her, why should she not be believed? She knew when she accepted the kiddushin that her husband would shortly come home and that she would necessarily have to maintain the claim to his face. Unless the case is where, when her husband returns, she retracts and says he never divorced her. Perhaps in that case Rosh would not disagree. Where no explicit dispute is found, what basis is there to create one?
