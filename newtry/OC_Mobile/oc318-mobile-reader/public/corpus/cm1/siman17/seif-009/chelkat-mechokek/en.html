Even if the witness is a slave or maidservant. The Gemara states in several places that ordinary slaves are presumed to be lax regarding transgressions, as when it says that if a slave is found to be a thief or a gambler the purchaser has no claim, and as Rambam writes in chapter 15 of Hilchot Mechirah. Accordingly, the Tur writes in Yoreh Deah, siman 1, that an ordinary slave is not believed regarding shechitah. This is difficult, for just as a robber disqualified by Torah law is invalid for testimony concerning a woman's husband's death, why should a slave be valid? Perhaps an Israelite who commits transgressions is worse than a slave, even though the slave too is presumed to be a transgressor. See the Bach, who does not write this.
