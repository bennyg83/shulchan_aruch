To hear the words of a litigant. See Responsa Zichron Yosef, siman 2, entry 6, who writes that a judge is certainly permitted to hear, by way of a general complaint, the basic substance and general character of the claim. He may not, however, hear its detailed form, quality, and precise substance at the center of the dispute. For example, if one person complained before someone whom he knows will serve as judge in the matter and said, 'So and so cheated me,' the prospective judge may not investigate and ask him how he cheated him, with what, when, before whom, and similar details. Nevertheless, from the wording of the Maharil cited in Darkei Moshe, seif katan 4, it is somewhat implied that even a general complaint may not be heard. End of quote. See there.
