Or he became confused because of foolishness. He is nevertheless not a complete incompetent. If he were wholly incompetent, beit din would plead on his behalf every defense he could have made while competent. Thus, if he was a bailee, we would plead that he returned the item or that it was lost through unavoidable circumstances, as we plead for an orphan and similar persons. The Gemara in Elu Na'arot likewise says that we plead unavoidable circumstances for a deaf mute woman or incompetent woman, even to enable her to collect a ketubah. Bach, and this is obvious.
And to make him understand the beginning of the matter. Tur and Rosh disagree and hold that the judge may formulate the party's entire valid legal argument from beginning to end. Why should he be limited to merely explaining the beginning? Rama omits the Tur and Rosh here and likewise in siman 42, seif 3, and I do not know why, since this is not his usual method.
