However, if the judge has already been consulted and has written a ruling for one of the parties, and his opinion is thereby committed to that party, he may no longer adjudicate the case because he is considered interested in the matter. So writes Rashdam in Responsa, siman 2, and Rabbi Levi ibn Chaviv in his epistle concerning semikhah. Shach.
And a student. The Beit Yosef writes that the text specifically mentions a student because there is a suspicion that, out of affection, he will arrange his claims before his teacher. I wrote that perhaps "student" is stated to teach a greater novelty: even though one could say that he entered merely to study, it is nevertheless forbidden. All the more so another person who has no ordinary business with the Torah scholar, whose entering alone plainly gives rise to suspicion. Thus concludes the Sema.
