Seif 12, in the gloss “More than he claimed,” etc. Darkei Moshe derives this from Ravina’s case and cites Rivash 227. But that case suggests the opposite. Rava said that by strict law the defendant owed according to Rav Huna, while Ravina voluntarily accepted only guarding compensation. Ravina knew the law and merely wished to avoid litigation. Thus a judge may not remain silent when a claimant is plainly unaware that more is due. Rama’s rule applies only when the judge sees that the claimant knows the law and intentionally waives the excess, or where circumstances ordinarily show knowledge and waiver. If the judge is uncertain whether it is waiver or error, he should remain silent; if it is clear error, he must tell the claimant the truth under “open your mouth for the mute.”
