It is permitted to serve. See Baer Heitev on the discussion whether a sage who has already been asked for a ruling is considered personally interested. Rabbi Akiva Eiger notes Responsa Chacham Tzvi, siman 131, and Maharit, volume 2, Choshen Mishpat, siman 79. See also below, siman 33, seif 12, Shach seif katan 9. Birkei Yosef, letter 11, discusses the matter at length and observes that the Shach writes simply that if the sage was already consulted, and so forth, without noting that Maharibal, in the very responsum previously cited by Shach, disagrees. Shach's statement that Rabbeinu Levi ben Chaviv agrees is based on Maharashdam, yet Edut BeYaakov, siman 42, gives grounds to say that Rabbeinu Levi ben Chaviv agrees with Maharibal in this case. Birkei Yosef concludes that the halachah follows Maharibal, as agreed by Maharashach and Dina DeChayei; this also appears to be Maharit's view in siman 79, and is the tendency of Bnei Moshe and Edut BeYaakov. The proofs brought by Be'er Sheva, siman 74, do not establish that the sage is forbidden to judge. He further writes that even according to Maharashdam, who forbids a sage who authored a ruling on a case from judging that case, if he nevertheless judged the very litigants, his judgment is valid after the fact, as Maharam Uziel ruled in a manuscript responsum cited by Divrei Moshe, siman 3. See Knesset HaGedolah, Urim VeTumim, and below, siman 231, seif 28, seif katan 6.
