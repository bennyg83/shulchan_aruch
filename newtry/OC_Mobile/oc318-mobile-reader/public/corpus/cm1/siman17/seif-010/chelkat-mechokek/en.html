Shiltei HaGibborim, chapter HaIshah Shalom, cites Ri'az that male relatives corresponding to these women, such as her father-in-law, her husband's son, her yavam, and likewise her husband, are believed to testify for her. A yavam is believed only where she is not bound to him; to permit her to himself he is not believed. At first glance 'her husband's son' and 'her yavam' may overlap. These words derive from the Yerushalmi. I discussed at length in a responsum whether a yavam is believed to say his brother died in order to perform chalitzah rather than yibum. See also Mahari's rulings, simanim 220 and 221: the five women who are not believed to permit her are likewise not believed to prohibit her. See below, seif 46. In another note, the author writes that there is a serious defect where the witness is the deceased man's brother, since the mishnah teaches that a man is not believed to say, 'my brother died,' in order to perform yibum with his wife. Even if in the present case the woman may have children and there is therefore no yibum, and even though today we do not perform yibum, it may be argued that since he is not believed for yibum he is likewise not believed for chalitzah. I was once asked by the Gaon of Lublin, Rabbi Heshel, about such a case and answered him at length. Nevertheless, where she has children there is strong reason to permit her. Still, I have always been cautious in issuing rulings and distance myself from cases of agunah not sitting before my own beit din, even from serving merely as a supporting opinion, though Heaven forbid that I should withdraw entirely from helping. The responsum itself was with me after the author's passing, and alas for what is lost and cannot be recovered.
