And standing by leaning. The Sema writes: “Nevertheless, it appears to me that if both the judges and the witnesses leaned during the same case at the same time, it is forbidden to do so, because whichever way one looks at it, one of them is not acting in accordance with the law.” The Bach disputes him and brings proof from the Gemara in Shevuot, in the chapter Shevuat HaDayanim, from the description that he sat “like one loosening his shoes.” Rashi explains that he was neither fully standing nor fully sitting. We learn that, for one person, bending is considered both standing and sitting: regarding the requirement that a judge stand before a Torah scholar, it is regarded as standing, while regarding the judge himself, who must adjudicate while seated, it is regarded as sitting. He thereby fulfills both requirements.
