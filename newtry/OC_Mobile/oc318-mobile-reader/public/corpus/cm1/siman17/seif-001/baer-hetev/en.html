His needs. The Sema writes that this implies that even if the judge did not tell one litigant, "Say everything you need," but merely told the other, "Be brief," this is also forbidden. The Bach writes that the judge may tell both parties, "Be brief in your words," and this is obvious. Shach.
Disgraceful. This implies that if his clothing is not disgraceful, no concern exists even though it is not as expensive as the other litigant's clothing. Sema.
Leaning. At the end of siman 28, regarding judges who must sit while receiving testimony, the Mechaber rules that leaning in this manner is considered sitting. The Sema writes that one must say we apply the leniency in both directions. Nevertheless, if in one proceeding both the judges and the witnesses were leaning at the same time, it appears forbidden, because inevitably one group is not acting according to law. This resembles the cases discussed below in siman 197 concerning acquisition involving a donkey and camel, in siman 200 regarding acquisition using the buyer's or seller's utensil, and in siman 350. The Bach disagrees and brings proof for his position. See the Shach.
