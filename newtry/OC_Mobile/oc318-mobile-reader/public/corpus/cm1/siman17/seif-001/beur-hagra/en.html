And leaning, from Megillah 21a.
(Collected note) And leaning, etc. See below, end of siman 28, where he rules that leaning is regarded as standing, and see Beer HaGolah there. But the principal law is that leaning is not called standing, nor is it called sitting. This is stated in Zevachim there: there is a dispute whether standing from the side is considered standing, and we rule like the first Tanna. There it says, "let him sit, etc.," and Tosafot there explain this as leaning, because there is no sitting, etc. Although in Yoma 25, s.v. "Ein," Tosafot resolve it differently, that for the needs of the Temple service it is permitted, nevertheless according to the Sages it is not called standing, as above. Likewise in the Yerushalmi regarding Torah reading, as Tosafot write there, s.v. "Amidah, etc.," and the Rosh in chapter 3 of Megillah. Nor is it called sitting, from the Tosafot cited above, s.v. "veLeitiv, etc." So too Tosafot write in Sotah 42, s.v. "veHa'amar," in the name of the Yerushalmi, and at the beginning of Midrash Shocher Tov: Rabbi Chiyya taught, there was no sitting, etc. Rabbi Ami said: even for the kings of the House of David there was no sitting. Resolve it that he leaned against the wall and sat. Yet is it not written, "King David came and sat before Hashem"? Rabbi Aivu bar Nagri said: he composed himself in prayer. Thus, leaning is not sitting with respect to the prohibition of sitting in the Temple courtyard. They also challenged the verse "and he sat," showing that it is neither sitting nor standing. There is no proof from the Megillah passage. The Rivash himself did not rely on that proof, but wrote that since after the fact it is valid in this manner, which is neither true sitting nor true standing, it is also permitted initially. See Orach Chayyim, end of siman 141, and Even HaEzer, siman 8, seif 45 in the gloss. (End of collected note.)
