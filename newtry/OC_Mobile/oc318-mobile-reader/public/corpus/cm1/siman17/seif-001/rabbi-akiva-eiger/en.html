[Shulchan Aruch] Both of them standing. Note: If a litigant is a Torah scholar, he may sit, just as a Torah scholar who is a witness may sit, as stated below in siman 28, seif 5. If the litigant is the wife of a Torah scholar after her husband's death, she is not seated at the time of the final judgment, according to Tosafot and the Ran. According to the first answer of Tosafot concerning the wife of Rav Huna, even a Torah scholar should not be seated at the time of the final judgment, because even then there is concern that the other litigant's arguments will be stifled; and seating the other litigant as well is improper at the time of final judgment, since he is not a Torah scholar. See Maharsha. In any event, if both litigants are Torah scholars, they may sit at the time of final judgment. According to this answer, in such a case even the wife of a Torah scholar after her husband's death may sit at the time of final judgment.
