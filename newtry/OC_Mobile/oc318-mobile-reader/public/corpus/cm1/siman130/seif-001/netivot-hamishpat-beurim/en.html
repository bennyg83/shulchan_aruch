If he paid before it became known. See Sema, seif katan 2, in the gloss concerning Reuven and a non Jew who borrowed jointly from Shimon, while Levi became guarantor for Reuven. Levi then paid Shimon half the debt, after which the non Jew paid Shimon the entire debt, and Sema rules that what was paid remains paid. This is difficult. Since Reuven and the non Jew borrowed jointly from Shimon, they became guarantors for one another, as explained in siman 77. The situation is therefore equivalent to Levi and the non Jew having jointly guaranteed the fifty that Reuven owed, as is explicit in Beit Yosef and Tur. Once Levi paid the entire fifty for which Reuven was liable, the non Jew was obligated to reimburse Levi for his share, as explained in siman 77 regarding three joint guarantors. Shimon, who then accepted the full amount from the non Jew while claiming that he had not yet been paid, is as though he robbed the non Jew and would have to return the money if the non Jew sued him before us. Shimon should therefore become obligated to Levi through the lien of Rabbi Natan, since the non Jew owes Levi and Reuven owes the non Jew. The lien of Rabbi Natan applies even where a non Jew is involved, as stated in the responsum of Maharam of Rothenburg, Prague edition, siman 253, cited by Tumim in siman 86. Although Tumim there questions its application to a non Jew's loan because one may sometimes retain a debt owed to a non Jew, here the matter resembles actual robbery and is exactly parallel to Maharam's case. Moreover, Tumim himself writes in siman 126, seif katan 27, that even regarding a non Jew's loan the permission to retain the debt applies only where one expressly intends to do so. This is clear. Where such conduct could cause chillul Hashem, it is forbidden in any event, and the lien of Rabbi Natan applies, as Ketzot HaChoshen writes. It therefore appears that the case must be one in which Levi paid before informing the borrower. In that situation, even against the borrower himself the guarantor cannot recover if the borrower later also paid, as Shach writes in seif katan 4. For the same reason, he cannot recover from the other guarantor. But if Levi paid pursuant to beit din, for example because the borrower was overseas, Shimon must return half.
