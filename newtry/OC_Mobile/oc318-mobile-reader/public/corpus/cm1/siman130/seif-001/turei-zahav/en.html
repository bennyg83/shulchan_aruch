(Seif 1) That he had already paid him. This means an oral debt, not one evidenced by a note, as Sema writes. Even if the borrower holds a receipt, the guarantor remained liable while the note was in the lender's possession and could not defer payment by suggesting that perhaps a receipt existed, as Beit Yosef writes in siman 128, seif 15. The borrower must therefore reimburse the guarantor. This is clear, notwithstanding a contrary view. The text then says that he is not liable to the guarantor. Although a kablan had to pay the lender even when the borrower said, “I paid you,” because the borrower is not believed against the lender, as Rama rules in siman 129, seif 6, the borrower may tell the kablan: “You were negligent. You should have stipulated with the lender that you would not have to pay if I claimed that I had already paid.”
