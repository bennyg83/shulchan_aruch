The Sema explains that even if the borrower claims that he later paid the creditor himself, one might have thought that even according to the view in siman 128 that one who pays another person's debt may recover from him, here the borrower is exempt because the guarantor paid without his knowledge and the borrower later paid as well. The ruling therefore teaches that a guarantor is different, because his payment is treated as having been made with the borrower's authorization, as explained in seif 2. The Shach writes that if this was an oral loan and the borrower says, “I later paid because I did not know that you had paid,” the borrower is plainly exempt, even if the guarantor proves that he paid the creditor and proves that at that time the creditor had not yet been paid. This is true even where the borrower had said, “Become my guarantor.” Although the guarantor was authorized to pay and is not treated as a stranger paying without the debtor's knowledge, he was still required to notify the borrower that he had paid. Why should the borrower have anticipated it? By failing to notify him, the guarantor caused his own loss. The Shach then suggests that where the borrower had already been found liable in court, he should have anticipated that the creditor would proceed against the guarantor and should not have paid again without first notifying the guarantor.
