Nothing. See Sema seif katan 4 in his resolution from above, siman 159 seif 15, and see what the Shach writes in seif katan 3 that the Sema's answer is insufficient. In truth, the Sema follows his own position, for he already wrote above that although a kablan must pay the creditor even when the borrower cries out that he already paid, nevertheless the borrower is exempt from reimbursing the kablan because the kablan should have stipulated and brought the loss upon himself. The Taz writes likewise. The Sema's difficulty is only from the Tur's language that the borrower did not authorize him to pay, implying that liability depends on authorization and that absent authorization he had no duty to pay. To this the Sema answers that the Tur did not give this reason regarding a kablan, since the borrower did not tell a kablan, Pay, but merely, Become a kablan for me. We therefore say, as above, that the kablan should have stipulated. With an ordinary guarantor, however, where the borrower must specifically say, Pay, he has in fact authorized payment. One might therefore think that the borrower must reimburse him. The Tur teaches that even then the borrower is exempt because he did not authorize payment in this manner, namely where the guarantor did not ascertain whether the borrower had already paid. Thus the Sema's words are correct according to his own approach. The law itself, however, is as the Shach writes and as I explained above, that the kablan is not obligated to pay in such a case.
Everything that he paid, etc. See Sema seif katan 6, who explains that the novelty is that even if the borrower claims that he later paid the creditor, he must still reimburse the guarantor. The Shach in seif katan 4 disputes this, arguing that there was nothing the borrower could have done, and therefore adopts a strained interpretation. In truth, the Sema's explanation is compelled. If, as the Shach holds, the borrower is exempt even if he paid the creditor afterward, then why do Baal HaTerumot, Tur, and Mechaber specifically state that if he paid before the guarantor paid, he is exempt? Even where he paid afterward he would be exempt, and certainly where he paid earlier while the guarantor paid without first informing him and determining whether payment had already been made. They should have taught the broader case, from which the earlier payment case would follow all the more so. Necessarily, the Sema is correct. The Shach's question, what was the borrower supposed to do, is no difficulty. A borrower is servant to the lender, and likewise toward a guarantor who undertook the guaranty for his benefit. Since the borrower knows that another entered the guaranty at his request and was authorized to pay, he must consider that the guarantor may already have paid. He should not pay the creditor until ascertaining that the guarantor has not done so. This is comparable to one who tells his agent, Go and pay, for an agent is presumed to carry out his agency. Why should the agent lose because the principal failed to ask him and paid a second time? The principal caused his own loss. The same is true even of an ordinary guarantor, for by saying, Guarantee for me, the borrower effectively sent him for this purpose. The Sema's words are therefore correct and clear.
