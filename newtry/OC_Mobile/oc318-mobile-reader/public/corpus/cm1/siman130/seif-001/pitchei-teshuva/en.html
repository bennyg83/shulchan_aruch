Even if the debt document is in his possession. See Responsa Avodat HaGershuni, siman 70, concerning Reuven, who entered a business arrangement with Shimon. Shimon undertook to supply certain merchandise to Reuven, and Reuven undertook to accept it and pay for it. Reuven appointed Levi as a kablan guarantor toward Shimon to pay for the merchandise, and Reuven gave Levi an indemnity document obligating himself to reimburse Levi and his representatives so that they would suffer no loss or indirect damage. The indemnity document also contained the customary clause of trustworthiness: so long as the document remains in force and no receipt is written upon it, Reuven is not believed to say that Levi was reimbursed, released, paid, waived the claim, that the transaction involved interest, or any other claim; rather, Levi and his representatives are believed, and the holder of the document has the stronger hand. Levi proved conclusively that he had paid Shimon for the merchandise delivered to Reuven and therefore demanded reimbursement from Reuven. Reuven answered that before Levi paid Shimon, he himself had given the money to Levi to deliver to Shimon. Reuven also claimed that after making the indemnity obligation he discovered that Levi had been Shimon's partner in the merchandise. Levi answered: what difference does it make whether I was a partner or not? The questioning rabbi assumed that Levi should prevail. Another scholar disagreed and argued that the indemnity document was ineffective because the law limits clauses of trustworthiness to repayment and not to other matters, as in siman 71, seif 3. Therefore, he argued, Reuven should be believed to say that he gave the money to Levi to deliver to Shimon. Avodat HaGershuni answered that the questioning rabbi was correct and Levi prevails. The other scholar's argument concerning the limitation of trustworthiness might show only that Levi is not believed without an oath; it offers no basis to annul the indemnity document entirely. Nor can one say that just as trustworthiness is limited to repayment, the entire indemnity was intended to apply only if it were established that Levi paid Shimon from his own money, while disputes over whether Levi paid from his own pocket would fall outside the document. The very essence of an indemnity is to protect Levi so that Shimon will not extract money from him. The word "indemnify" denotes rescue and protection. Thus Reuven obligated himself to save Levi from Shimon's claim, and the clause of trustworthiness applies to that obligation. Avodat HaGershuni notes that he is aware of Responsa Maharshal, siman 93, concerning two people who borrowed under one document, where one later paid the document, obtained it, and sued the other for his share. The other said, "I already paid you my portion so that you could pay the entire debt." Maharshal ruled that the defendant swears and is exempt. One might therefore think the borrower here is also believed to say he already paid the guarantor. In truth, however, that case proves the opposite. Maharshal's reasoning was that the debtor could not say, "Why is your document in my opponent's hand?" First, the single debt document had until then remained in the creditor's possession. Second, even if the document were in the co borrower's possession, it did not create a lien by one co borrower against the other; it merely recorded that both were jointly obligated. Here, neither reason applies. The document is in the guarantor's possession, and it is itself a lien from the borrower to the guarantor. Therefore the borrower is certainly not believed to say he had paid earlier. Beyond this, Avodat HaGershuni raises additional difficulties in Maharshal's proof from Baal HaTerumot. See the Sema, siman 77, seif katan 27, and what is written there. In the present case, even Maharshal agrees that Reuven is not believed to say he paid previously. As to Reuven's further claim that the indemnity should be void because Levi had been Shimon's partner, Avodat HaGershuni says he cannot rule either way because Reuven never explained why this partnership should annul the indemnity. What benefit or difference would it create if Levi had not been Shimon's partner? These are his words. See there. See also Noda B'Yehudah, second edition, siman 13, concerning this.
