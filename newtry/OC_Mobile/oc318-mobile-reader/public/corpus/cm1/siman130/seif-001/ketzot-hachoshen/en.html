The borrower must reimburse the guarantor. Sema says this remains true even if the borrower later claims that he himself paid the creditor. The Shach disagrees where the borrower actually paid after the guarantor had already paid, reasoning that the guarantor should have notified him and prevented the double payment. Tumim sides with Sema. The proper distinction depends on how the guarantor entered the transaction. If he became guarantor on his own initiative, without being appointed by the borrower, the borrower never made him an agent to pay. The guarantor's reimbursement right arises only because the borrower benefited when his debt was discharged. Although one who pays another's debt is ordinarily not reimbursed, a guarantor is different under the special rules explained in siman 128. If the guarantor failed to notify the borrower and the borrower then paid the creditor himself, the borrower received no benefit from the guarantor's payment. He therefore need not reimburse him. But if the borrower said, "Become my guarantor and pay," or even simply, "Become my guarantor," in a setting where that wording also authorizes payment, the guarantor acts as the borrower's agent for payment. Once he performed the authorized act, the borrower must reimburse him even if the borrower later paid the creditor again. In that setting Sema's ruling is correct.
