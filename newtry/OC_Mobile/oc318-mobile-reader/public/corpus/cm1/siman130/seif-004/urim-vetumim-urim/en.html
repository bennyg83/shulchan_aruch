I have received payment. The Rama in Darchei Moshe holds that a receipt is effective only if given at the time of payment, not afterward. The Sema, seif katan 15, argues at length that it is effective even afterward. The Shach cites the Bach, who agrees with the Darchei Moshe without giving a reason. In Tumim I concluded that the issue depends on the earlier dispute between Rashbam and Ri. According to Rashbam, a receipt gives the guarantor the standing of one holding a written loan, and therefore the Sema's argument does not apply. According to Ri, however, the Sema is correct. Accordingly, the Rama in Darchei Moshe stated his view only according to Rashbam, but in practical law he too would agree with the Sema. See there for the reasoning.
And there are those who say that even though, etc. The Shach, seif katan 11, writes that this is also the view of the Baal HaTerumot and the majority of authorities, and is primary. All the more so according to what he concluded, that the borrower can claim, 'I repaid you,' it is obvious that the guarantor cannot collect from purchasers. See Tumim, where I too concluded that practical law follows this latter opinion, though not for the Shach's reason. I proved that the first opinion is Rashbam's, who follows his own view that documents are acquired by delivery alone. Since we rule that acquisition of a document requires both writing and delivery, the guarantor certainly cannot collect from purchasers merely on the basis of a receipt. Therefore the latter opinion cited by the Mechaber is clearly correct as practical law.
Who is not believed to say, 'I paid.' See Tumim for my answer to Gidulei Terumah's difficulty on the Rosh and Tur, namely that the guarantor has a migo to deny the guaranty itself. They follow their own view that one who pays another person's debt is entitled to reimbursement, so there is no difficulty. As for the Mechaber, who holds that such a payer is ordinarily exempt, I offered several resolutions, but the strongest is that the borrower had already admitted to the guarantor that the guaranty was undertaken. Any migo would therefore be retroactive. Gidulei Terumah also takes this view, correctly.
If the borrower wrote, etc. See Tumim. Tosafot in Bava Batra imply that if the borrower wrote such language to the guarantor in a document, the guarantor has the status of a kablan and the creditor may claim from him first. As practical law, however, the matter requires study. Tosafot may have been compelled to interpret it that way only to explain the language of the Tosefta. According to the Ramban, and according to how I explained Rabbeinu Yonah, the baraita has a different interpretation, and the proposed law remains doubtful.
And from adult orphans. The Tur also writes this. See Tumim, where I showed that it supports what I wrote in seif katan 11: even if one witness testifies that payment was made to the creditor, the borrower is nevertheless believed to say, 'I paid,' contrary to the Sema, seif katan 14, with whom the Shach agreed.
