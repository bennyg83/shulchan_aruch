Tumim writes that if the borrower wrote to the guarantor within the document that he had received the guarantor's undertaking, the guarantor thereby becomes a kablan whom the creditor may sue first. This is astonishing. Why should something the borrower writes to the guarantor increase the guarantor's liability to the creditor? The guarantor can tell the creditor, "What concern is it of yours what the borrower wrote to me? I obligated myself to you only as an ordinary guarantor, not as a kablan." Tosafot's words mean something else. They explain the expression in the Tosefta that if the debtor wrote the relevant language, the creditor had instructed him to do so because the creditor wanted the guarantor to become a full kablan. The guarantor himself would not accept kablan liability unless the borrower first wrote the corresponding undertaking to him. Thus the creditor told the borrower to write it so that the guarantor would then expressly undertake kablan liability toward the creditor. If the guarantor never undertook toward the creditor as a kablan and obligated himself only as an ordinary guarantor, the borrower's private writing cannot change that status. This is clear.
