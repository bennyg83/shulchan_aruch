(Seif 3) If the lender wrote. Sema writes that when witnesses saw the guarantor pay the lender, no written receipt is required. This is difficult. Even if witnesses prove payment to the lender, why should the borrower not be believed to say that he later repaid the guarantor, since the guarantor has no document establishing that debt? Moreover, these words come from Sefer HaTerumot, gate 35, which expressly says that even after the guarantor proves payment to the lender, if the borrower claims, “I paid you,” the borrower is believed. The original note in the guarantor's hands does not turn his reimbursement claim into a documented loan. The Tur's wording does not support Sema's reading. The Tur first says that without witnesses the guarantor collects nothing, even absent any claim of repayment. He then states a separate rule: even if the note is in the guarantor's hands, whether witnesses establish his payment and the borrower now claims repayment, or no witnesses exist and the borrower says that the lender was never paid, the guarantor does not collect unless the lender wrote a receipt. The Shulchan Aruch intends only to teach that possession of the original note does not itself prove that the guarantor paid.
