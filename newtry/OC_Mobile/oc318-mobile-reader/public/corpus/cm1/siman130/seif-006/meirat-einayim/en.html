He returns and collects from the heirs, etc. The Tur concludes that if none of the three stated conditions exists, he cannot recover from the heirs. The Mechaber follows the Rambam and abbreviates because he had already written that even while the borrower is alive, if the guarantor pays without the borrower's instruction, the borrower need not reimburse him. All the more so after the borrower's death, the guarantor cannot collect unless one of the three stated circumstances is present. This law is stated in the Gemara. The Rambam understands it to apply to both adult and minor heirs. If one of the three circumstances is present, collection may be made even from minors and even when the guarantor does not hold the promissory note, as explained above in simanim 108 and 110. If none of the three circumstances is present, collection is not made even from adult heirs, even where the note is in the guarantor's hands. Since there is a defect in the claim, namely that the guarantor should not have paid without first notifying the heirs, we assume that the creditor had placed funds or security in the borrower's hands when the due date arrived. Rama therefore cites an opinion disagreeing with respect to adult heirs. See the Derishah, where I discuss this at length.
