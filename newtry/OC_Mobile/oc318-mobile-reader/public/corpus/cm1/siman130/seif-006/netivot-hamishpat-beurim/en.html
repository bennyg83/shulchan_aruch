The Rosh in Bava Batra 32 writes that if the borrower admits that the claimant had been his guarantor, and the instrument contains a written acknowledgment of receipt, the borrower is not believed to say, 'I repaid you.' Gidulei Terumah asks that the borrower should be believed through a migo, since he could have said that he never instructed the guarantor to guarantee for him and that the guarantor acted voluntarily. He answers that this is not a proper migo. A migo applies where, instead of the present claim, he could have made an entirely different claim. Here, even if he falsely said that he never instructed him to become guarantor, he would still need to say, 'I repaid you,' because according to the opinion that a guarantor who pays another's debt is entitled to reimbursement, that additional claim is still necessary. According to the opinion that the borrower is exempt where he asserts with certainty that he never authorized the guaranty, however, he would indeed be believed through this migo.
