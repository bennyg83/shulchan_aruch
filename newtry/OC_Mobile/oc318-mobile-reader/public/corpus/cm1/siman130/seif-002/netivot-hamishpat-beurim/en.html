He owes the guarantor nothing. See Shach, seif katan 3, who argues that the contractor or guarantor should be exempt from reimbursement: if the guarantor was liable to pay the creditor, why should the borrower not be liable to reimburse him? The guarantor can say that even on the borrower's account he was required to pay. It appears, however, that the case is one where the guarantor paid without being compelled by beit din. The borrower can then say that the creditor would have been required to swear before collecting, as explained in siman 129 and Sema, seif katan 28, comparable to collection in the debtor's absence, which requires an oath. Since the creditor had a right to collect only upon taking that oath, the guarantor caused his own loss by paying without requiring it, for perhaps the creditor would not have dared to swear falsely. This can apply where the creditor now refuses to swear or has gone abroad. Shach, seif katan 1, citing the Bach, writes similarly that if there was a note and the borrower possessed a receipt proving payment, the borrower is exempt from reimbursing the guarantor, because the guarantor paid without requiring the creditor's oath and the creditor can no longer swear in light of the receipt. Otherwise, there would be no reason to exempt the borrower, since the guarantor was compelled to pay, as the Taz explains.
