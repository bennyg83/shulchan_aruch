Become my guarantor. This is also Rabbeinu Yerucham's view, who writes that Rav Saadiah Gaon held likewise. See Responsa Maharit, siman 122, Responsa Maharshdam, siman 118, and Responsa Mahara Sasson, siman 123, cited by the Shach.
And if he paid voluntarily, he may return and collect from the borrower. The Sema writes that this is the Raavad's view as explained by the Maggid Mishneh. The Baal HaTerumot and Tur, however, did not explain the Raavad this way. In their view, reimbursement applies specifically when the borrower said, 'Become my guarantor'; then the borrower is liable even if the guarantor paid on his own before the creditor brought the borrower to judgment. The Perishah and Shach understand it this way as well. According to them, it is difficult to understand why the Mechaber entirely omitted the Baal HaTerumot's and Tur's interpretation. In Tumim I therefore suggested that the Baal HaTerumot's and Tur's interpretation of the Raavad and the Maggid Mishneh's interpretation may actually be one approach. The entire dispute here may concern the Sema's question: if the borrower afterward also paid, must he nevertheless reimburse the guarantor? It can then be said that the Tur holds that if the borrower did not say, 'Become my guarantor,' there was no reason for the borrower to notify or consult the guarantor before paying, because he never authorized him to become guarantor. If he did say, 'Become my guarantor,' however, then according to the Raavad the borrower bears responsibility to ask the guarantor whether he already paid, and if he fails to do so he causes his own loss. As to the general rule that one who pays another's debt without authorization ordinarily cannot collect from the debtor, the Tur agrees with the Maggid Mishneh that guarantorship is different. See there for the reasoning. The Maggid Mishneh in turn agrees with the Tur that where one became guarantor entirely on his own, the borrower has no duty to consult him and remains exempt. Thus they do not actually disagree. This is preferable, because it minimizes disputes among the Rishonim. See there also my defense of the Raavad against Gidulei Terumah's difficulty.
