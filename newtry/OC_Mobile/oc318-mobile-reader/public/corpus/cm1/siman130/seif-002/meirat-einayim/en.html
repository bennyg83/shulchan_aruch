"And he did not pay him to his mother," he said, "but he did not pay him to his father's house, for he would not be divided between the mountain to pay or ask him to be paid and paid:
And no property was found for him. This refers to the case where the creditor had not first litigated with the borrower. It teaches not only that if the creditor had already taken the borrower to court, the court ordered him to pay, and no property was found, then the borrower must reimburse even an ordinary guarantor who paid, because once judgment had been entered there is no argument that the guarantor should have waited for the creditor to litigate further. Rather, even where the creditor did not first sue the borrower to determine whether he had property, a case in which an ordinary guarantor who paid would not necessarily be reimbursed, an absolute guarantor is different. Since the creditor is entitled to sue the absolute guarantor immediately, the borrower must reimburse him even if he paid of his own accord.
And if he paid before they stood in judgment, etc. The Maggid Mishneh explains that this is because he is like one who pays another person's debt without that person's knowledge. This follows the Rambam and the Mechaber, who rule in siman 128 that such a payer is exempt from reimbursement. In the Perishah I wrote that even according to those who ordinarily require reimbursement, the borrower is exempt here if he claims that afterward he himself repaid the creditor.
Who entered into the guaranty on his own, etc. The Raavad states his view without explanation, and the Maggid Mishneh interprets it as the Mechaber does. The Baal HaTerumot and the Tur, however, do not explain him this way. They distinguish specifically where the borrower said to him, "Become guarantor for me." In that case the borrower must reimburse him even if the guarantor paid voluntarily before the creditor litigated with the borrower. See the Perishah.
For the guarantor becomes obligated only, etc. See Rama's gloss to siman 129:8 and the Sema there. This applies specifically where the borrower too has become legally liable but the creditor cannot actually collect from him through legal process. See there.
