See Ketzot HaChoshen, who writes that in the case of an oral loan, when the guarantor paid without witnesses and the creditor then denies receiving payment from him, both the creditor and the guarantor swear and collect, under the rule governing a shopkeeper on his ledger. All of his words there are astonishing. It is explicit in seif 3 that the guarantor cannot collect reimbursement until he produces witnesses. This is not comparable at all to the shopkeeper on his ledger, because there the employer appointed him as an agent to make the payment. Here, by contrast, the guarantor did not pay as the borrower's agent, but by reason of his own guarantee, and the principle, "I relied upon your trustworthiness," does not apply, as explained in Bava Metzia 3. This is the reason for the Shach's ruling in seif katan 12 that the guarantor cannot extract payment. Although simanim 58 and 121 show that where one instructed another to give away his money, the latter may recover without a document, this case is different because the guarantor did not give the money pursuant to agency. He therefore cannot say, "I relied upon your trustworthiness," and cannot extract reimbursement without proof.
