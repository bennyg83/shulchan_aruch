To the guarantor: "I have received payment," etc. Sema explains that this applies even if the borrower claims that he paid the guarantor, or alternatively where the lender wrote the guarantor, "I have received payment." The Shach, seif katan 8, agrees that this is Tur's meaning but holds that most authorities disagree, including Rosh in the chapter *Chezkas HaBatim* in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah, Baal HaTerumot in gate 35 part 2, and Nimukei Yosef. See Tumim. I concluded that the Shach's interpretation of Rosh in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah is untenable. He reads "he wrote him, 'I have received payment'" as meaning that the lender also transferred the debt document to the guarantor through writing and delivery. But if so, what need is there for the receipt language at all? Writing and delivery alone transfer the document. I brought additional objections and proved that Rosh and Rabbeinu Yonah actually agree with Tur. I likewise rejected the Shach's reading of Nimukei Yosef and concluded that Nimukei Yosef means that by writing "I have received payment" together with delivery of the debt document, the lender gives the guarantor standing in his place. These are the words of Rashbam and Rabbeinu Chananel cited by Tosafot in Bava Batra. Yet even Nimukei Yosef agrees with Tur. I also proved from Baal HaTerumot, gate 35 part 1, that he agrees with Tur, and explained the passage in part 2 according to Gidulei Terumah as presenting alternative cases. Thus Rosh, Rabbeinu Yonah, Nimukei Yosef, Baal HaTerumot, and Rivash all agree. This is the practical law. I further explained the source from which Rabbeinu Yonah derived his rule, showed that Rabbeinu Chananel agrees, and thereby resolved Tosafot and Maharsha. One should not depart from Rabbeinu Yonah's ruling. See Tumim at length.
"I have received payment." If the guarantor has witnesses that he paid the lender and the lender's debt document is now in the guarantor's possession, even though the lender did not write, "I have received payment," Sema holds that the guarantor is believed and stands in the lender's place, because it is known that he was a guarantor. The Shach, seif katan 9, agrees with Sema and adds a proof from siman 132:4. See Tumim. My view is otherwise. Baal HaTerumot explicitly implies the opposite, and siman 132 provides no proof.
