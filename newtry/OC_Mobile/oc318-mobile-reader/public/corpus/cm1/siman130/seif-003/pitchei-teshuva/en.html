Responsa Avodat HaGershuni, siman 70, discusses a business arrangement in which Shimon undertook to supply merchandise to Reuven, Reuven undertook to accept it and pay, and Reuven appointed Levi as an arev kablan for the price. Reuven gave Levi a comprehensive indemnity deed containing the usual strong ne'emanut clause, stating that so long as the deed remained uncancelled Reuven would not be believed to claim that Levi had been indemnified, released, paid, forgiven, or to raise any other defense, while Levi and his representatives would be believed and the holder of the deed would have the upper hand. Levi later proved conclusively that he had paid Shimon for the merchandise delivered to Reuven and demanded reimbursement. Reuven answered that before Levi paid Shimon he had already given Levi the money to transmit, and also claimed that he later discovered that Levi had been Shimon's partner in the merchandise. The responsum analyzes whether the ne'emanut clause is limited to a claim of payment or extends to these defenses, and whether Reuven can be believed against the indemnity deed. Its conclusion is that the strong language of the deed and the circumstances of the proven payment give Levi the superior claim, subject to the detailed rules of ne'emanut and the precise wording of the instrument.
