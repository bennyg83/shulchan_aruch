Although the passage discusses heirs, the same applies to the borrower himself, for any claim advanced on behalf of heirs would certainly be available to the borrower. The Gemara places the case among heirs because it states simply that he cannot collect, whereas for the borrower himself beit din does not raise a claim on his behalf. Certainly according to the explanation where the borrower admits the debt, there is no distinction between the borrower and heirs. See Tosafos there, s.v.
