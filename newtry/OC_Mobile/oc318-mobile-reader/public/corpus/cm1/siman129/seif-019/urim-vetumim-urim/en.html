He waived, etc. Sema explains that where it was not yet clearly known that the borrower had no assets, Rabbi Natan's lien had not yet attached to the person indebted to him, as stated in siman 86:2 and 5. The Shach objects that this is incorrect. Nimukei Yosef follows the first answer of Tosafot in the second chapter of Ketubot, and Beit Yosef's citation of Rashba, who was uncertain whether a guarantor's waiver is effective, follows Rashba's own view that there is room to consider whether waiver works where it harms another creditor. We, however, rule like Tosafot's latter answer, as explained in siman 86, and as I proved there at length. Accordingly a kablan guarantor cannot waive the debt, unlike Sema. See Tumim. I concluded that the Shach correctly criticized Sema insofar as siman 86 explicitly teaches that he cannot waive. But what the Shach writes, that Nimukei Yosef holds even under Rabbi Natan's lien that waiver remains possible, is incorrect and creates difficulty with the Shulchan Aruch's earlier ruling that waiver does not work. More fundamentally, Baal HaTerumot and Maggid Mishneh are themselves difficult to understand. In Tumim I offered an elegant resolution: if the kablan waived, the lender has no power to collect, whichever way one analyzes it. Either Rabbi Natan's lien never attached, or the waiver preceded the moment at which it attached. See there carefully.
The same applies to a guarantor. Meaning a guarantor who actually took and handed over the money. In that case the lender has no remaining claim against the borrower. Sema.
