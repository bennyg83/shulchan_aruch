If one became guarantor for a non-Jew, and under their law the non-Jewish debtor may direct the creditor first to the guarantor, Bach writes that the creditor may indeed sue the guarantor first. The Shach questions this from Rama in seif 8, citing Sefer HaTerumot, that if the non-Jew claims to have paid, both he and the guarantor are discharged. According to Bach, the guarantor would have the status of a kablan, yet with a kablan the borrower is not believed to say that he paid, as Rama writes in seif 15 and Bach himself states there. Sefer HaTerumot, however, holds that even with a kablan the borrower is believed to say that he paid, as explained above in seif katan 34. One might therefore say that Rama speaks where non-Jewish law follows our law, in which event the guarantor did not become a kablan for him, but this is forced. The practical halachah requires study. See Rashba's responsum cited by Beit Yosef in seif 16. The Sema notes five general rules governing collection from a guarantor and sets them out there.
