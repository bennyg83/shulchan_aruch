But if the borrower did not speak, etc. The Bach writes that where a Jew becomes guarantor for a non-Jew, if their law permits the non-Jewish borrower to redirect the creditor to the guarantor, the creditor may claim first from the guarantor. His ruling is difficult. The Rama writes in seif 8, in the name of the Baal HaTerumot, and the Bach himself cites it at the end of seif 3, that if the non-Jew claims he paid, both he and the guarantor are exempt. According to the Bach, however, the guarantor has the status of a kablan, and a borrower is not believed to say that he paid where there is a kablan, as stated in the gloss to seif 15 and by the Bach there. The Baal HaTerumot himself indeed holds that even with a kablan the borrower is believed to say that he paid, as cited above in seif 15. One would have to say that the Rama speaks where the non-Jewish courts apply our law, so that the guarantor did not become a kablan, but this is strained. The practical ruling requires study. See the Rashba’s responsum cited by the Beit Yosef in seif 16.
