Some say that the laws of an ordinary or unconditional guarantor apply when the borrower asked the lender for a loan and the guarantor joined the transaction using the language of an ordinary or unconditional guarantee. But if the borrower said nothing to the lender, and Reuven instead told Shimon, 'Lend Levi money, and I will pay,' 'Lend him, and I will give you the money,' or, 'Lend him, and I am liable to you,' Shimon has no claim at all against Levi, even if Shimon handed the money directly to him. His claim is only against Reuven, who told him to lend it and under whose agency he gave the money.
