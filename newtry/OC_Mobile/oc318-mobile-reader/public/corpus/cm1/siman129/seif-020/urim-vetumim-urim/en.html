For guaranty, etc. Bach writes that where a Jew lends to a gentile and another Jew becomes guarantor, if gentile law permits the creditor to proceed first against the guarantor, then he may indeed sue the guarantor first. The Shach, seif katan 39, cites Bach but questions this and leaves the matter requiring study. See Tumim. I concluded that there is no basis for hesitation. This is correct practical law. I showed that Rosh in the chapter *Eizehu Neshech* and other decisors agree, and I also proved it from Shulchan Aruch, Yoreh Deah 170. The matter is clear. See there.
