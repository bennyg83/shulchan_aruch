There is an opinion that guaranty, etc. See Shach seif katan 39, who cites the Bach that where a Jew lent to a non Jew and another Jew became guarantor in a jurisdiction whose law permits the creditor to proceed first against the guarantor, the Jewish creditor may indeed demand from the guarantor first. The Shach questions this and concludes that the law requires study. I do not understand the doubt. This is explicit in the Rosh and other decisors in chapter Eizehu Neshech and in Yoreh Deah siman 170. They rule that where a Jew lends to a non Jew and another Jew guarantees the loan, the arrangement is forbidden because of ribbit unless the creditor stipulated with the non Jew that the non Jew may not direct him to the Jewish guarantor. If the Jewish creditor had no right to demand from the Jewish guarantor until first proceeding against the non Jew, there would be no ribbit concern, since the Jewish guarantor would not be receiving interest from the lender. Indeed, it would be even more clearly permitted than where the non Jew expressly stipulated such a condition. Necessarily, the Jewish guarantor is treated as a full kablan. This is simple.
