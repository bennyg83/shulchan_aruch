Some say regarding guaranty, etc. Examine the Tur and the Mechaber carefully and you will find that, according to the Rosh and those who follow him, there are five general forms of collection from a guarantor. First, with an ordinary guarantor, the creditor may not collect from the guarantor first, even if no known assets of the borrower are available. Second, if the guarantor said, "Collect from whichever of us you wish," then if no known assets of the borrower are available, the creditor may proceed first against the guarantor. Third, if he expressly said, "Collect first from whichever of us you wish," the creditor may proceed against the guarantor first even if the borrower has known assets. Fourth, if the guarantor received the money from the creditor and handed it to the borrower, the creditor's legal claim is against the guarantor alone, not against the borrower, except by virtue of Rabbi Natan's rule when the guarantor has no assets. Fifth, if the borrower did not negotiate with the creditor at all and only the guarantor asked the creditor to lend to the borrower, then even if the creditor physically handed the money directly to the borrower, his claim is nevertheless against the guarantor alone. It appears that here too, if the guarantor has no assets, the creditor may return against the borrower by virtue of Rabbi Natan's rule. With an absolute guarantor the first two distinctions do not apply, but the final three do. An ordinary absolute guarantor is treated as though the creditor had stipulated, "If I wish, I may collect from you first.".
