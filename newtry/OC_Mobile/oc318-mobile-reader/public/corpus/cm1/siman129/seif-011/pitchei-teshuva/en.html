And there are those who disagree with this. See Responsa Rishmei She'elah, siman 51, who writes that the Tur makes clear that even according to the dissenting view, namely Rabbeinu Tam at the end of the chapter Get Pashut, the dissent applies only where the borrower has known real property. If the borrower has no known land, the creditor may proceed against the guarantor and need not first pursue the borrower in the hope of collecting movable property. This is the effect of a stipulation, "I may collect from whichever of you I wish": in a doubtful case we do not burden the creditor to pursue the borrower first. According to this, the first view in the Shulchan Aruch, that the creditor may collect from the guarantor first even if the borrower has assets, must refer to land, and the dissent likewise concerns land specifically. He further writes that in his impression the general practice is to rule in accordance with the first view cited in the Shulchan Aruch, permitting collection from the guarantor first even where the borrower has assets, contrary to the dissenting view. See Darkei Moshe, seif katan 8. He found the same in the Shulchan Aruch of the author of Yismach Yisrael, who copied only the first view, except in a place with an established custom to follow the dissenting view and therefore require the word "first" to be written explicitly. He heard that such a custom was newly established in the community of Posen. Where there is no fixed custom, general practice follows the first view. See there, and see Netivot HaMishpat on this matter.
