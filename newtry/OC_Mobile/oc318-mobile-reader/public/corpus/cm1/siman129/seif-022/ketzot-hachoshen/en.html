If a guarantor received a kinyan to be compensated excessively for entering the guaranty, the kinyan is effective. Rosh compares the case to one who says, "Take a dinar and ferry me across," where an excessive promise can be dismissed as jest absent a kinyan. This does not conflict with Rashba's ruling in the case of chalitzah conditioned on payment of two hundred zuz. Rashba says even a document or kinyan may not bind where the man was already obligated to perform chalitzah without charge. Nimukei Yosef explains that such a person is effectively coerced: the mitzvah rests upon the brother in law, he loses nothing by performing chalitzah, and therefore the woman is like one forced to promise an excessive payment. In that setting Rashba requires stronger delivery, such as depositing the money. Guaranty is different. No one is inherently obligated to become a guarantor. There is no coercive element. Therefore, once a proper kinyan is made, even an excessive promised fee is binding. The laborer cases also do not contradict this. If ordinary workers take three and the employer promises particular workers four, he must pay four even without a kinyan. Labor has no absolutely fixed market price, because workers differ in quality and wages vary with time and circumstances. A worker also bears actual toil. Thus the employer must pay what he promised. The rule, "He receives only his ordinary fee," applies more readily to a broker, matchmaker, or physician where the service has a recognized fee and the excess looks like a joking promise. With a kinyan, however, even those professionals can bind the principal to the full promised amount.
Rama says that without a kinyan the guarantor need not receive any fee. Tumim tried to explain that this means only an excessive amount beyond what beit din considers proper, while a reasonable fee remains payable even without a kinyan. That is not Rosh's ruling. Rosh was asked about Reuven, who claimed one fourth of a lease because Shimon had promised him that share in exchange for becoming guarantor. Rosh ruled that Reuven wins only if a sudar kinyan was made. Without a kinyan he receives nothing, because guaranty is not a service for which people ordinarily take a fee. Rosh compares it directly to the case, "Take a dinar and ferry me across," where the promisor can say he was joking as to the excess over the ordinary fee. Here there is no ordinary fee for guaranty at all. Therefore the whole promised compensation is treated as excess over the normal price. Absent a kinyan the guarantor receives nothing. Rama's unqualified wording is therefore exact.
