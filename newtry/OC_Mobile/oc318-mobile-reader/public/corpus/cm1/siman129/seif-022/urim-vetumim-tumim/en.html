If he made a kinyan, etc. He is liable according to the amount he fixed, but if he made no kinyan he need not pay him the stipulated fee. This means that he need not pay more than the amount beit din determines to be the ordinary compensation for one who undertakes such a guaranty. He certainly must pay the customary reasonable fee, for the amount depends on the nature of the guaranty, whether the principal is financially sound, whether the term is long or short, and whether the amount is large or small, as beit din assesses. Only an amount beyond the reasonable value is ineffective without a kinyan. The Rosh, in the responsum from which this law derives, compares it to the case of one who says, Take a dinar and ferry me across, where the ferryman receives only his proper wage. See below siman 264, Even HaEzer siman 169, and what I wrote above in siman 81. Here too he receives only the proper fee and no more. The essential point is that a kinyan obligates him to pay the full stipulated amount. The Rama's language is somewhat obscure, for reasonable compensation is certainly due. It further appears from the Rosh that with a kinyan even an amount beyond the reasonable value is binding. If so, the baraita concerning Take a dinar and ferry me, where the person may say that he was merely deceiving the ferryman and owes only the proper fee, applies only without a kinyan. With a kinyan he would owe the stipulated amount. However, in Even HaEzer siman 169 concerning one who says, Perform chalitzah on condition that I give you two hundred zuz, which the Gemara in chapter Mitzvat Chalitzah compares to Take a dinar and ferry me, the Beit Shmuel cites the Rashba that even a kinyan and a document do not help. Yam Shel Shlomo writes likewise in chapter Mitzvat Chalitzah. They did not note that the Rosh in this responsum disagrees and holds that a kinyan does obligate the stipulated dinar. The cases share one rule and one source. It is therefore clear that whether a kinyan is effective is a dispute among the Rishonim, the Rosh against the Rashba, and the law remains doubtful. The burden of proof rests upon the claimant.
