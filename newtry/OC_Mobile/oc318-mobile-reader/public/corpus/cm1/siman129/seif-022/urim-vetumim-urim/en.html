He need not give, etc. See Tumim. I concluded that the meaning is as follows: if he stipulated wages greater than what beit din considers reasonably due, a kinyan is needed. But he must certainly pay the reasonable amount even without a kinyan. Rama's language is obscure here. His source is Responsa Rosh, which compares the case to one who says, "Take a dinar and ferry me across." See below, siman 264; above, siman 81; Even HaEzer 169; and what I wrote in those places. See Tumim. I further concluded that where a kinyan was made, whether it validates whatever amount was stipulated, whether large or small, depends on the dispute between Rosh and Rashba. It is therefore an unresolved monetary doubt, and the burden of proof rests on the party seeking to extract. See there for the reasoning.
