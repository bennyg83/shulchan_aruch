And some say that where a kinyan was made from him, etc. From here I brought proof that even the Rosh, Nimukei Yosef, and Tur agree that if a kinyan was made from the borrower, the creditor may collect from encumbered property even if no document was written at all, provided the witnesses to the kinyan are before us and remember its date. Their dispute here concerns only a guarantor. See the Perishah here, and above at the beginning of siman 39 and siman 43:16. See also siman 39:4 concerning the rule that omission of warranty is treated as a scribal error with respect to a guarantor.
An acknowledgment before beit din is like a document. Meaning that it is like a document accompanied by a kinyan, because a guarantor who undertakes liability before beit din does not require a kinyan, as stated above in seif 2. The Tur writes this in the name of the Ramah.
