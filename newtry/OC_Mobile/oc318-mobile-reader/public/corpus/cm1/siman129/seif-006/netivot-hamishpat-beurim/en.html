He collects only from unencumbered property. See Sema, seif katan 20. Tumim writes that according to Ramban as well, a guarantor whose undertaking appears before the signatures is effective only against unencumbered property, because collection from encumbered property requires both a kinyan and a document. The underlying reason is as Knesset HaGedolah records in the name of a certain sage, cited by Tumim in siman 116, seif katan 5: whenever the kinyan is needed to create the very obligation, it does not also render the obligation collectible from encumbered property. But if the document states, 'And so and so is guarantor,' with the conjunctive wording joining it to the body of the document, it is effective even against encumbered property, for the reason given by Sema in seif katan 21.
