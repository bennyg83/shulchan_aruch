Some say that the law is with the kablan. The reason is that the creditor's position has been weakened because he no longer has the original document. There is room for a difficulty. In the first chapter of Bava Metzia, when a promissory note with property warranty is found, it is not returned because we fear repayment and collusion. If it contains no warranty, Rabbi Meir permits return because he does not hold that omitted warranty is automatically included. But even in that case why do we not fear that the lender had a kablan or guarantor on this loan through a separate document or kinyan, that the borrower already repaid, and that he was therefore careless about the original note? The lender could now collude with the borrower, recover the note, and use it to collect from the guarantor or kablan, whereas without the note he could not collect from them. The Sages feared even distant possibilities in cases of found documents. Perhaps the answer is that when a debt has been paid, the note should ordinarily be torn. We say only that sometimes the borrower trusts the lender and leaves the paid note with him because of the scribe's fee. This can occur when the lender originally trusted the borrower enough to lend without additional security, and the borrower therefore reciprocally trusts him. But where the lender did not trust the borrower without a guarantor, the borrower in turn would not trust the lender enough to leave a paid note intact. Therefore there is no realistic concern of prior payment in such a case. This is somewhat strained and requires study.
