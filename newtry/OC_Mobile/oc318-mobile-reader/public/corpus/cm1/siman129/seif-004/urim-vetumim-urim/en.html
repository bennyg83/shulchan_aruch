Some say he becomes obligated. Sema explains the dispute from Tur and Perishah. Raavad and his supporters compare it to one who writes in a document, 'I owe you one hundred,' which can create liability even without a kinyan, as above in siman 40. Rabbi Meir HaLevi Abulafia and his supporters hold that since he undertook specifically as a guarantor, and guaranty involves asmachta, it is weaker than a present unconditional personal obligation. Rambam likewise requires a kinyan for a guarantor whose undertaking appears before the signatures on the document, even if the document says, 'and so-and-so is guarantor.' Without a kinyan, Rambam holds that one cannot even collect from unencumbered property. Maggid Mishneh states this. Sema seems to infer that according to Raavad and Ramban a written document always substitutes for a kinyan. See Tumim. I proved this is incorrect. Otherwise Ramban's own objection to other authorities would apply equally against himself. I discussed the Gemara at length and concluded unlike Sema: a kinyan remains necessary.
And according to Rambam, even a guarantor, etc. Lechem Mishneh raises Ramban's difficulty against Rambam. The Shach seeks to answer it. See Tumim. I gave two answers, both correct in practical law.
