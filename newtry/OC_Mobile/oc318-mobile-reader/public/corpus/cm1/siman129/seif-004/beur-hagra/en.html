Some say that he becomes obligated... See Beer HaGolah. Their reasoning is from Rabbi Yishmael there: the guarantor is liable by virtue of his signature, just as one who writes, "I owe you one hundred," in a document obligates himself.
Others say... From the Gemara's statement, "all the others require a kinyan." A guarantor who signs after the witnesses' signatures is included, since the Mishnah begins with that case. The comparison in Kesubos to the dispute of Tannaim means that just as Rabbi Yishmael allows a guarantor to obligate himself though he owed nothing and the loan was not advanced in reliance on him, so too one who says "I owe..." can obligate himself. Ramban writes that even according to the Rif's reading, "all the others require a kinyan," it can be explained as referring only to a guarantor after the money was given and to a guarantor for a kesubah, though this is strained. See Tosafos there, s.v. "and the halachah," and the citation from Rabbeinu Chananel.
According to the Rambam... Because the Gemara says "all of them," and the discussion there includes even a guarantor who signed before the witnesses' signatures. The explanation of Rabbeinu Chananel cited by Tosafos also implies this. According to the second view, the Gemara discusses only the cases in that particular clause, not the cases of Rav and Rabbi Yishmael. According to what the Ramah and Shulchan Aruch write in seif 6, "some say that where..." one must necessarily follow the first view.
