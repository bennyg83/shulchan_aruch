Yehudah and Ben Nanas applies both according to the opinion that asmachta is effective and according to the opinion that asmachta is not effective. At first glance, this resolves only why the Gemara says that R. Yehudah cannot agree with Ben Nanas. According to Shach, Ben Nanas holds that even according to the opinion that asmachta is effective, a guarantor whose undertaking appears after the signatures on the document is exempt, and therefore he cannot create liability through such an inferior document, in accordance with Reish Lakish. Yet a difficulty remains. R. Yochanan explicitly rules that even according to the opinion that asmachta is not effective, an undertaking in a document is effective. Bava Batra rules both according to R. Yehudah and according to R. Yochanan, while Ketubot states that R. Yochanan follows R. Yishmael. Why then does Rambam rule that the document is ineffective? Rambam may be explained according to Nimukei Yosef at the end of Bava Batra, who writes that the Mishnah concerning a guarantor whose undertaking appears after the signatures deals with a kinyan. The comparison in Ketubot between R. Yochanan and Reish Lakish and the dispute of R. Yehudah and Ben Nanas would otherwise be difficult, because the latter dispute concerns a kinyan while the former does not. The Gemara must mean that the Mishnah concerns a document that says after the signatures, 'I am guarantor through a kinyan sudar,' meaning that the document itself contains an admission that a kinyan was made. This is an admission in an inferior document, as Tosafot explain. R. Yehudah therefore proves that an admission in such a document is effective, and consequently an obligation created through such a document is likewise effective, for the two issues are interdependent. One could still ask that perhaps Ben Nanas disputes only because he holds that a kinyan does not validate guaranty undertaken after the money was given, as several Tannaim hold, while wherever the kinyan is effective such a document may also be effective. Shach's words answer this. The dispute applies both according to the opinion that asmachta is effective, in which case no kinyan need be recorded in the document, and according to the opinion that asmachta is ineffective, in which case the document necessarily records that a kinyan was made. R. Yehudah holds that in both situations such an inferior document is effective, and therefore one can create an obligation through such a document just as Rambam holds that one can obligate himself by saying 'You are my witnesses.' Ben Nanas, by contrast, holds that even where asmachta is otherwise effective such a document does not suffice, and likewise where the document records a kinyan it does not establish the admission. Rambam therefore rules in accordance with the relevant conclusions requiring a kinyan when guaranty is undertaken after the money is given. Tumim offers another explanation, that a kinyan removes asmachta only where the kinyan is not itself needed to create the underlying obligation. Thus, according to R. Yochanan, who allows one to obligate himself by a document, the kinyan can remove the asmachta, whereas according to Reish Lakish, who requires the kinyan to create the obligation itself, that same kinyan cannot also remove the asmachta. This distinction is not indicated by the poskim. A kinyan certainly removes asmachta even where it is also needed for the substance of the transaction, and there is no basis for attributing such a novel rule to Rambam.
