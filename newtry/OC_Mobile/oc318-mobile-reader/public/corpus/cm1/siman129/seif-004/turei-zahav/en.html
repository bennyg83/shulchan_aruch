(Seif 5) To fulfill his oath. Sema, seif katan 16, rejects Levush and explains that there is a practical difference concerning release from the oath. This also answers the objection that the case does not resemble paying for another: even if the guarantor gave his solemn pledge, he could seek release, and when he saw that the debtor would not pay he should have done so. This appears to be the principal law where release is available. Yet in the newly printed responsa of Maharam, siman 72, I saw that a solemn hand-pledge cannot be released except with the consent of the person to whom it was given, as Rabbenu Chananel writes. Beit Yosef and Rama in Yoreh Deah 239 did not see these responsa. Accordingly, if the guarantor merely swore at the debtor's request, he cannot later compel the debtor to reimburse him, as Levush writes. If, however, he gave a binding hand-pledge, it is as though he paid on the debtor's behalf, in accordance with Sema.
