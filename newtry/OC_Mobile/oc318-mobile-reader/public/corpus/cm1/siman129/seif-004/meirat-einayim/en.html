Or witnesses testify concerning him. The definite article in "the witnesses" appears to be a scribal error; it should simply read "or witnesses," meaning ordinary witnesses.
Some say he becomes obligated without a kinyan. The dispute is explained by the Tur. Raavad and those with him compare a written guaranty to a person who writes, "I owe you one hundred," which creates an obligation even without a kinyan. Ramah and those with him distinguish: here he expressly obligated himself only in the form of guaranty, which is affected by asmachta, and this is weaker than one who now creates an absolute direct debt. Rambam likewise requires a kinyan even where the guaranty appears before the witnesses' signatures in the loan document. Without a kinyan, according to him, it cannot be collected even from unencumbered property. I explained that the Tur's view accords with Rambam.
