According to the Rambam, etc., a kinyan is required. The Lechem Mishneh, chapter 11 of Hilkhot Mekhirah, challenges this from the ruling at the beginning of siman 40 that a person may obligate himself without a kinyan, for the Gemara at the beginning of HaNose links these matters. This is also the proof brought by the Ramban in his novellae at the end of Bava Batra. It appears to me that the Gemara compares them because the dispute between Rabbi Yishmael and Ben Nanas in the Mishna applies even according to the view that an asmakhta is effective. Immediately earlier, at the end of Get Pashut, Rabbi Yehudah and Rabbi Yose dispute whether an asmakhta is effective. According to our ruling, however, an asmakhta is not effective unless a kinyan was made, and a guarantor’s undertaking resembles an asmakhta. It therefore takes effect only through a kinyan. It appears that this is the intent of the Ran at the beginning of HaNose. Although the Lechem Mishneh cites the Ran and objects that the Gemara’s comparison implies that the guarantor case concerns an undertaking without a kinyan, in my humble opinion the Gemara’s discussion accords with the view that an asmakhta is effective. The Ran therefore writes that a guarantor is bound only through a kinyan because his undertaking resembles an asmakhta.
