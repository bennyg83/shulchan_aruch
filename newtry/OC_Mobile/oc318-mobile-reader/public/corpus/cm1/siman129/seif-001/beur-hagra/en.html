One who lends... or demanded... See Rashbam at the end of Bava Basra 176b, s.v. "a guarantor appointed by beit din." Rashbam does not have this reading, but the Rif and Rosh do.
Even if he said... The Gemara there speaks of a guarantor appointed by beit din. The Rambam explains that beit din itself made him guarantor, as in seif 2 and as indicated by the Gemara's reason, "in consideration of that benefit." That rationale does not apply here. Therefore the text above says, "after he had lent him," corresponding to "after the money was given." It also says, "or demanded..." based on the law of a guarantor appointed by beit din, which is a case where the creditor had already made demand. Even so, liability arises specifically where he left the debtor alone at beit din's direction.
However, if... at the debtor's instruction. Meaning that the debtor said, "Become my guarantor." So explains the Shach. The Hagahos Maimoniyos likewise says that the requirement of a kinyan is only for obligating the guarantor toward the creditor, but for obligating the debtor toward the guarantor no kinyan is needed, since it is as though the debtor said, "Pay my debt." The rule that one who pays another's debt without authorization loses his money applies only when he paid without the debtor's knowledge. See above siman 126, seif 2. Nevertheless, if he gave him...
Whether... This is included in the Gemara's statement, "all the others," as above. See Tosafos there.
