This also follows explicitly from Responsa Rashba cited by Beit Yosef in siman 126. See Responsa Maharashdam siman 88 and Responsa Rama simanim 33 and 72. Shach.

Between them. Sema explains that this applies even without witnesses, because witnesses are created only to resolve false claims, as stated in siman 189. He asks why a formal acquisition should work without an important beit din when guaranty is ordinarily an asmachta. Only a guarantor at the time money is advanced is said not to involve asmachta because the lender relies on him and advances money on his account. Sema answers that even here there is some benefit of reliance, just as a guarantor before beit din becomes obligated even after the money was advanced and without a formal acquisition because beit din trusted him. Shach answers that there is no difficulty, based on Beit Yosef citing Baal HaTerumot that guaranty is not a complete asmachta, and on Beit Yosef in siman 207 that where performance depends on another person's conduct it is not asmachta. A day. Rashba's words appear to imply that if at the time money was advanced, or later with a formal acquisition, one guaranteed payment for a single day, the guaranty is valid. Taz rules likewise. See Bach; the practical law requires study. If one writes, whoever volunteers to lend a certain sum to so-and-so, I enter as guarantor and kablan for him, see Responsa Maharashdam siman 38, who tends to say this is not a guaranty because the writer did not use an imperative such as give him, and it resembles saying whoever feeds him will not lose. He further writes that if the borrower repaid once, the guarantor is released and is not bound for a second loan. This is straightforward. See Responsa Mahara Sasson simanim 122 and 178, Responsa Raanach siman 60, and Responsa Maharit, Choshen Mishpat siman 21. Shach.
