But if an act of acquisition was made from him. The Sema asks why an acquisition should help without an eminent beit din, since guaranty involves asmachta. The Beit Yosef, citing the Baal HaTerumot, answers that guaranty is not a complete asmachta. Another explanation is that because the guarantor’s obligation depends on another person’s conduct, it is not treated as ordinary asmachta.

And I am your guarantor for one day. The Rashba explains that this language is ambiguous. It may mean that the guarantor undertakes that the debtor will travel on the lender’s mission for one day, or merely that the debtor will not depart for one day. If he expressly undertakes, at the time the money is given, or later with an act of acquisition, to pay for one day, it may constitute a valid guaranty, though the practical ruling requires study. A handwritten declaration that ‘whoever volunteers to lend so-and-so a certain sum, I enter as guarantor for him’ may not be a guaranty because it lacks a direct instruction to lend, and resembles the statement ‘whoever supports her will not lose.’ If the debtor repaid the first loan, the guaranty ends and does not extend to a second loan.
