For one day. See Shach, seif katan 6, through his comparison to one who says, 'Whoever feeds him will not lose.' Tumim asks that Shach contradicts his own ruling in Yoreh Deah, siman 235, where he rules that one who says 'Whoever feeds him will not lose' is liable to pay. Tumim concludes that although Taz is correct there that such language creates no liability, if one says, 'Whoever lends him money, I will pay him, I am his guarantor,' he certainly becomes liable, just as if he said, 'Whoever feeds him, I will pay him.' This requires study. Ketzot HaChoshen writes that here he is exempt because guarantor liability requires language of agency, such as 'Give him and I will pay,' whereas saying only 'I am a guarantor' is insufficient. This is certainly incorrect. There are Tannaim who hold that even a guarantor who undertakes liability after the money was given is liable. In such a case he clearly did not say 'Give him,' but only accepted guaranty. Even those who exempt him do so because the lender did not originally lend in reliance upon him, as explained at the end of Bava Batra. Therefore, where the guaranty is undertaken when the money is given and the lender relies upon him, even if he merely says 'I am a guarantor' without language of agency, he is liable. This is evident in many places and is clear in my view.
