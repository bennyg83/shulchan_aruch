And so it appears to me. The Mordechai writes at the end of chapter Get Pashut that the same applies in a vernacular formulation. Whenever the language mentions neither a loan nor repayment, it is treated as the language of an absolute guarantor.
And a document in which it is written, etc. This excludes the view of the Baal HaTerumot, who writes that in such a case the holder of the document has the weaker hand, because perhaps the guarantor said, Lend him and I will be an absolute guarantor, which according to that view gives him only the status of an ordinary guarantor. The Tur cites this opinion.
