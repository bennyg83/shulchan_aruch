If he says, 'Lend him, and I am the guarantor,' 'Lend him, and I will pay,' 'Lend him, and I am liable,' 'Lend him, and I will give,' 'Lend him, and I am an unconditional guarantor,' 'Give him, and I am the guarantor,' 'Give him, and I will pay,' 'Give him, and I am liable to give you,' or even, 'Give him, and I am an unconditional guarantor,' all these are expressions of an ordinary guarantee. {Rama: Some say that, 'Give him, and I am an unconditional guarantor,' creates an unconditional guarantee. This appears proper to rule. If a document states, 'So-and-so is an unconditional guarantor,' he has the law of an unconditional guarantor, because the document certainly refers to a legally effective expression.}
