["And he and the guarantor are released, etc. See Sema seif katan 22, and see also what I wrote above in simanim 15 and 16. See responsa of Rabbi Shmuel Kohen II, siman 27, Maharit siman 112, and Maharshal siman 39."]
["But if the borrower said, etc. The Beit Yosef refers to a non Jewish borrower. See my work and the Bach. The Bach's explanation is correct: perhaps the guarantor undertook only that if the non Jew failed to pay he would pay, but not that he would bear liability if the non Jew simply denied the debt."]
["The guarantor must pay, etc. The Bach writes that in practice money should not be extracted from the guarantor unless a kinyan was made, even if he became guarantor at the time the object was borrowed. His words appear primary. See responsa of Rabbi Shmuel Kohen II, siman 11. Sema seif katan 25 seeks to reconcile this with the rule above in siman 49 concerning a guarantor for two people with the same name. In my view the difficulty from siman 49 remains, as is evident from Bechorot 48a. The Bach likewise holds this, and his ruling is primary."]
["The borrower sold land, and the lender himself signed as a witness, etc. See Sema seif katan 26. The Bach's explanation of this matter is forced and far removed from the plain meaning."]
