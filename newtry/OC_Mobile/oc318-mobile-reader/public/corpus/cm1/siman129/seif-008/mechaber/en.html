If a person lends another money through a guarantor, then although the guarantor becomes obligated to the lender, the lender may not initially demand payment from the guarantor. He must first demand payment from the borrower. Even if the borrower owns only inferior land, the lender may not collect from the guarantor. Even if the borrower owns no known property, such as land, while the guarantor owns known property, the lender may not collect from the guarantor without first pursuing the borrower, who may own movable property. {Rama: If the borrower says, 'I paid you,' regarding an oral loan, he takes a heses oath and both he and the guarantor are exempt. Even if the borrower is a non-Jew and says he paid, the guarantor is exempt. But if the borrower says, 'I never borrowed,' the guarantor must pay. If a person borrowed an object and said, 'If I do not return it by a specified time, I will give you such-and-such sum,' and provided a guarantor for that sum, then although the borrower's undertaking is an asmachta and he is exempt, the guarantor must pay, as stated in Responsa Rashba 1090. See below at the end of siman 131, seif 12. If the borrower sold land and the lender himself signed as a witness to the sale, the guarantor is exempt because the lender caused his own loss. In the corresponding case of an unconditional guarantor, however, the guarantor must pay, as stated in Responsa Rashba 892. See notes 4, 40, and 111.}
