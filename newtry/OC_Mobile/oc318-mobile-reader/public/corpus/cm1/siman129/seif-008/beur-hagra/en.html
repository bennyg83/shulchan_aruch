Even if there is no... See Rashbam 47a, s.v. "and they say."
Even if there is no... Tosafos there, s.v. "the text is defective," see there.
If he said... Bava Basra 174a, "Perhaps he gave her bundles of money." This shows that had the father himself raised such a claim, the guarantor would have been exempt. See below seif 12.
But if... For one who says, "I never borrowed," is treated as admitting that he did not repay, while the guarantor admits the loan.
One who borrowed... This is analogous to "Give one hundred to so-and-so and I will become betrothed to you," where even though the recipient does not owe repayment, there is no asmachta in the guarantee. As the Raavad writes, the essence of every guarantee contains an element of asmachta, yet the guarantor nevertheless becomes obligated.
If the borrower sold... A guarantor has the law of purchasers where the borrower has property, from whom one does not collect first. Kesubos 95a teaches that where the loss arose through the claimant's own negligence, by signing, he cannot collect from purchasers. Here too, had the guarantor not signed, the creditor could have collected from the encumbered properties before turning to the guarantor, for a person's property is itself a guarantor for his debt and precedes the personal guarantor, as reflected in the rule that the guarantor is pursued only where the borrower has no property. Gittin 49b proves that specifically where the land was destroyed, but if it was sold one may collect from the guarantor. See Rashi there, s.v. "what..." and seif 21.
On behalf of... Sema explains that he signed and thereby consented to the sale, like the case in that Gemara. See above siman 111, seif 17. This does not appear correct to me, for Rashba disagrees, and the passage implies otherwise. He holds like the Sages regarding one who contests a sale, where we rule that an act does not necessarily constitute admission, as stated in Bava Basra 30b.
But with a kablan... Since even if the property were still unencumbered the creditor could have collected directly from the kablan, we cannot say that the kablan caused his own loss. See siman 118, seif 1, "but if they wrote to the first..." There necessarily had to be some practical consequence for the first party, since he could have collected from him once they wrote it.
