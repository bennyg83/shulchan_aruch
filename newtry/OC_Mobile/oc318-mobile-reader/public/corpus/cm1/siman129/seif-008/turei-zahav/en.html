(Seif 12) The borrower claimed. The source of this ruling is Maharam in Responsa Maimoniyot, and it follows his position that a borrower who pays the lender's wife is discharged, as Rama cites at the end of siman 120. Although it appears at the end of a responsum of Rashba, examination of the sequence shows that it is Maharam's responsum copied by a student. Responsa Maimoniyot itself first states the law of returning money to the lender's wife and then says that the same reason applies when a borrower gives payment to the guarantor. According to what I wrote in siman 120 in the name of Maharshal, we do not accept Maharam's exemption of the borrower, but follow the Rosh who disagrees in siman 72. It follows certainly that the present responsum should not be followed. Even according to Rama, who rules like Maharam there, the borrower is discharged only because the lender's wife is legally competent and has the rest of his property in her possession, as Sema explains there, lest one object from siman 340. That is not true of a guarantor, who does not possess the lender's other property. The fact that this particular money was delivered to him as guarantor does not show that the lender entrusted him with everything. Two custodians are better than one, and the borrower remains liable until the money reaches the lender. Levush and Olat Shabbat reject this ruling on their own reasoning. Sema disagrees with them, but in my view Levush correctly identified the law. Although he did not trace the source, he could have supported it from the Rosh. This is the principal law, and here all authorities should agree.
