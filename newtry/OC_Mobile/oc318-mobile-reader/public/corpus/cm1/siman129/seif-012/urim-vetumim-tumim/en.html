He takes a heset oath. See Shach seif katan 30, who cites the Maharshal and Ir Shushan as disputing this ruling. In truth, in Hagahot Maimoniyot this ruling appears merely as an addition and is not found in the Prague edition of the responsa of Maharam of Rothenburg. Perhaps it speaks of a guarantor who is a kablan. If so, he may properly be compared to an agent appointed before witnesses, since from the outset the lender relied upon him and accepted him as the person from whom payment would be received. The borrower therefore acted properly in delivering payment to him, for the creditor had stated that payment would be accepted from him. He is plainly like an agent appointed before witnesses. With an ordinary guarantor, however, the principal obligation of payment remains upon the borrower, who must first be sued. What connection is there to an agent appointed before witnesses? The Hagahot Maimoniyot may have used the general term guarantor while referring to a kablan. Regarding a kablan, in my humble opinion, even the Levush, Yam Shel Shlomo, and Shach would agree. This is simple.
