And he is exempt through a migo. Sema writes that Hagahot Maimoniyot gives an additional rationale: it is no worse than an agent whose appointment was made before witnesses. Ir Shushan challenges Rama and says one should not rule this way, because the lender can say, "The guarantor is not yet my opposing litigant until I sue him, and you had no right to pay the guarantor." Ir Shushan apparently thought these were Rama's own words, but Rama merely copied the ruling from Hagahot Maimoniyot, and it has a rationale. We likewise find concerning custodians that if property is transferred to someone whom the owner trusts, the second person stands in the first one's place. Sema. The Shach writes that Ir Shushan's objection is correct and that the analogy to an agent appointed before witnesses does not apply here. Maharshal likewise disagrees and rules that the borrower is not exempt merely because he paid the guarantor. This is the principal law according to the Shach. See Tumim. In truth this is not a responsum of Maimoniyot but only an added gloss. I concluded that Rama speaks of a kablan guarantor, and the word "guarantor" is merely routine shorthand. With an ordinary guarantor, Ir Shushan, Maharshal, Shach, and Taz are correct. With a kablan guarantor, however, I concluded that all of them agree with Rama. See there.
