By a migo. The Sema writes that the responsa of Maimonidean scholars give an additional reason: the guarantor is no worse than an agent appointed in the presence of witnesses. The Levush questions Rama and writes that one should not rule this way, because the creditor can say, “The guarantor was not yet my litigant until I sued him, and you had no right to give my money to him. If you did, let him return it.” The Levush apparently thought this was Rama's own ruling, but Rama merely copied the responsum, which also has a reasoned basis. We similarly find regarding a borrower and other custodians that when an item is entrusted to one whom the owner trusts, he stands in the owner's place. The Shach, however, writes that the Levush's objection is correct, because an agent appointed before witnesses has no bearing on this case. Maharshal likewise disputes that responsum and rules that the borrower is not discharged by paying the guarantor, and this is the primary view. The Taz agrees and brings proof for the Levush's view that here all agree the guarantor's receipt does not discharge the borrower.
