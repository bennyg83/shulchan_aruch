The rule that when the borrower... See siman 106, seif 2, in Beer HaGolah, that even from the borrower himself collection is not made in his absence in such a case, since beit din advances a person's defense in his absence. Therefore an oath is required even for a loan evidenced by a document, and just as collection is not made from the borrower, so too not from the guarantor, as stated in Bechoros 48a, "Is there such a case..."
Unless... and the same applies... As stated above in siman 108, seif 1.
If the borrower claims... for... In that responsum the same ruling appears at the end of siman 120 in the gloss regarding one who paid the borrower's wife, and the gloss there says "the same applies." This is the proof for the present ruling, for the borrower trusted the guarantor more than himself, like the gardeners in Bava Metzia. Furthermore, the guarantor is no worse than an agent appointed before witnesses. Yet according to what is stated there, that Rashba and the Rosh disagree with that ruling, all the more so here. Even according to the view there that payment to the wife exempts him because her hand is like his, that rationale does not apply here. The additional comparison to an agent is difficult to understand. See below siman 121, seif 2, where even when the creditor directs him to a particular messenger, he is not thereby discharged from responsibility. See Taz and Shach.
