He takes a hesset oath and is exempt through a migo, etc. Teshuvot Maimoniyot gives an additional reason: the guarantor is no worse than an agent whom the borrower appointed before witnesses. Levush objects to Rama's ruling, reasoning that the lender can say, "The guarantor was not yet my litigant when you paid him. You should have paid me, and if you paid the guarantor, recover your money from him." But Rama did not invent the ruling; he copied Teshuvot Maimoniyot, which gives a reason. In laws of borrowing and bailment we likewise find that delivery to someone whom the owner trusts can be treated as delivery to the owner. Therefore the earlier authority's ruling should not be rejected merely by unsupported reasoning.
