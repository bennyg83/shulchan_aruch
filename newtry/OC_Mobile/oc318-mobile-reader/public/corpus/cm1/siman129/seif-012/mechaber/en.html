The rule that, when the borrower is overseas, collection may be made from the guarantor without notifying the borrower applies only to a documented loan. With an oral loan, collection can never be made from the guarantor without the borrower's admission, because we are concerned that the borrower may have paid, unless the loan is still within its term. Similarly, if the borrower died, an oral loan is not collected from the guarantor unless it is still within its term, the borrower admitted it when he died, or he was placed under a ban for failing to pay this debt and died while under that ban. {Rama: If the borrower claims that he gave the repayment money to the guarantor to deliver to the lender, he takes a heses oath and is exempt. Through a migo that he could have said, 'I paid you,' he is also believed to say that he paid the guarantor, because the lender trusted that guarantor. If the lender denies that he appointed this person as guarantor, the borrower swears that the lender appointed him and that he paid him, and is exempt.}
