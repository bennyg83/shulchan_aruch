The distinction between stipulating, "Collect from whomever I choose," and making no such stipulation must be explained according to the dissenting authorities who hold that even with such a stipulation the creditor cannot collect from the guarantor when the borrower has assets. Tosafot hold that without the stipulation, even if the borrower has movable property, the creditor cannot sue the guarantor. With the stipulation, however, only land in the borrower's possession, which is the primary property subject to collection, prevents resort to the guarantor. If the borrower has only movable property and no land, the creditor need not pursue those movables and may collect from the guarantor. Nimukei Yosef distinguishes instead between known and unknown assets. Without the stipulation, the creditor cannot sue the guarantor until it has been established, through the borrower's oath or otherwise, that the borrower has no assets. With the stipulation, the creditor may collect from the guarantor unless the borrower is known to possess assets. The Mechaber stated the matter briefly. The halachah follows Tosafot. There are textual errors in the Sema. In seif katan 39 it should read, "whenever he mentions neither the language of a loan nor the language of repayment." In seif katan 41 it should read, "he collects from the borrower and from unencumbered assets, and if there are no unencumbered assets, from encumbered assets."
