He may collect from the guarantor first even if the borrower has assets, etc. This is the view of Ri Migash. He explains the Gemara's statement, One who lends to another may not collect first from the guarantor, but if he stipulated, From whomever I wish I may collect, he may collect from the guarantor, followed by Rabbi Yochanan's statement that this applies only when the borrower has no assets, but if the borrower has assets he may not collect from the guarantor. According to Ri Migash, Rabbi Yochanan does not refer to the clause containing the express condition From whomever I wish I may collect. In that case it is obvious that even if the borrower has assets the creditor may collect from the guarantor. Rather, Rabbi Yochanan refers to the implication of the first clause: ordinarily the creditor may not collect first from the guarantor, implying that if the borrower fails to pay he may then collect from the guarantor. Rabbi Yochanan teaches that this is true only where the borrower has no assets; if he has assets, the creditor still cannot turn to the guarantor and must collect from the borrower's property. This is Ri Migash's explanation as cited by Baal HaTerumot, gate 35. Gidulei Terumah asks: if so, why does the Gemara emend the Mishnah by saying that text is missing because of the inference from the concluding view of Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, who says that if the borrower has assets, in either case one may not collect from the guarantor, implying that the first Tanna disagrees in both cases? Why not say that Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel refers specifically to the express condition From whomever I wish I may collect, while the first Tanna allows collection from the guarantor even where the borrower has assets? He answers with difficulty that the inference is from the phrase in either case. This is very strained, for such language is common throughout the Mishnah and creates no real difficulty. The plain explanation is that according to Ri Migash the reconstructed text must read: If he stipulated, On condition that I may collect from whomever I wish, he may collect from the guarantor; when does this apply, when the borrower has no assets, etc. The phrase When does this apply must refer to the implication of the first clause, not to the express condition, for under the condition one may always collect from the guarantor. If so, why does the Gemara later need to state that a kablan may be collected from even when the borrower has assets? This was already taught regarding the condition From whomever I wish I may collect, and certainly a kablan should be no worse. We must say according to this interpretation that a kablan is in one respect weaker than an express condition. A kablan did not explicitly state that the creditor may collect from whomever he wishes; his wording merely carries that legal implication. Where the creditor explicitly stipulated From whomever I wish I may collect, however, there is no room to disregard the condition, since monetary conditions are effective. Therefore, even after teaching the express condition, the Mishnah still needs to teach kablanut. On this basis, the Gemara may have assumed as obvious that Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel could dispute only ordinary guaranty or, according to the conclusion, ordinary kablanut, but not an express stipulation From whomever I wish I may collect. Such a condition is plainly binding, and there is no reason for him to dispute it. The Gemara's question is therefore sound, and its answer properly adds the clause concerning a kablan so that Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel's ruling applies to kablanut, not to an express condition. This also resolves the question raised against the Rosh's approach: why add a new clause concerning a kablan? It could simply say that if the creditor stipulated, From whomever I wish I may collect first, he may collect from the guarantor, and Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel could then respond to that. According to what I have written, even the Rosh would agree that Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel would not dispute an explicit condition that the creditor may collect first from whomever he wishes. The condition is explicit and valid. This is correct. Examine it carefully.
