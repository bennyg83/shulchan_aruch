And there are those who disagree. Responsa Rishmei Sheelah, siman 51, writes that even according to the dissenting view, namely Rabbeinu Tam at the end of Perek Get Pashut, the disagreement applies only where the borrower has known landed property. If the borrower has no known land, the creditor may proceed directly against the guarantor and need not pursue the borrower merely to see whether he has movable property. This is the benefit of stipulating, 'I may collect from whichever of you I choose': in a doubtful situation we do not burden the creditor to chase the borrower. Accordingly, the first opinion in the Shulchan Aruch, which permits collection from the guarantor first even where the borrower has property, is speaking of land; the dissent also concerns land. He further writes that ordinary practice appears to follow the first opinion, permitting collection from the guarantor first even where the borrower has assets, unless there is an established local custom to follow the dissenters and to require the word 'first' in the stipulation. He heard that such a custom had recently been established in Posen. See also Netivot HaMishpat.
