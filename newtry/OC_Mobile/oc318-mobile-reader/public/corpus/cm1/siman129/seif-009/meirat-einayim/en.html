And he must include in his oath that the debt is still owed. In the Perishah I wrote that this appears to concern an oral loan, and therefore the oath is imposed upon the borrower. If the borrower does not swear that the debt is still owed, the guarantor is discharged. If, however, the loan were documented by a promissory note, the borrower would not be believed even with an oath to say that he already paid. In such a case, when the creditor seeks payment from the guarantor, the creditor himself must swear that the debt remains unpaid, not the borrower, just as the Tur and Mechaber rule above in siman 114 regarding a creditor who seeks to collect from purchasers.
