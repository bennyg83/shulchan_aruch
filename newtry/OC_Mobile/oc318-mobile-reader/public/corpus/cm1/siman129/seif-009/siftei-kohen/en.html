["And he must include in his oath that the debt is still outstanding. In my commentary I wrote that this concerns an oral loan, which is why the oath is imposed on the borrower. With a written loan the borrower would not be believed even with an oath to say that he already paid it. So writes the Sema. The Bach gives another reason, but his explanation does not appear correct as practical law."]
["And he cannot collect from the guarantor, etc. The Bach explains that this applies only where the guarantor lacks cash or movables, as in siman 100 regarding a borrower, and the Maggid Mishneh may imply this. In my view the case applies even where the guarantor has cash. Otherwise the text should have stated that the guarantor claims he has nothing. The Rambam and Baal HaTerumot, who cite this in the name of the teachers, imply more strongly that the rule itself is novel. Its point is that the guarantor's present position should not be worse than that of a borrower, for an unspecified loan is due only after thirty days. Since the guarantor's liability has arisen only now, it is as though he borrowed now and need not pay for thirty days. This is why the Ramban, Baal HaTerumot, and Tur say thirty days from the day the guarantor became obligated to pay. According to the Maggid Mishneh's reading they should have said thirty days from when he requests time, even if that occurs well after the liability arose. Thus my explanation is primary."]
