To include it in his oath. See Baer Heitev citing Sema, who writes that with a loan documented by a deed the lender must swear. Responsa VeShav HaKohen, siman 43, explains that this is specifically with an ordinary guarantor. Since the lender must first pursue the borrower, if the borrower claims payment and demands an oath, the lender must swear. Therefore, when he seeks payment from the guarantor, the same rule applies, even though from the guarantor's standpoint the claim is like 'I do not know whether I repaid you.' The lender cannot collect from the guarantor unless he could collect from the borrower. A kablan is different, because the lender may collect from him first; there is therefore room to say that the kablan's uncertainty is simply 'I do not know whether I repaid you,' and the lender need not swear. The Sema, however, says that the lender must swear against the guarantor as one who collects from purchasers. According to that reasoning the same might apply to a kablan, and the oath should be a Mishnaic oath like one collecting from encumbered property. VeShav HaKohen questions the source for this. He further infers from the Rama in seif 16, who writes, 'It appears to me that the lender swears and collects,' that where the lender comes to collect a documented debt from a kablan and the borrower is absent, the borrower having claimed payment, the lender need not swear unless the borrower himself asserted payment. If every lender collecting from a kablan had to swear as one collecting from purchasers, the Rama would not have needed to single out that case or introduce it as his own inference. See there, and the discussion below in seif 15, as well as Tumim and Netivot HaMishpat. See also Ketunet Passim, which appears not to have seen the Sema and Tumim on this point.
