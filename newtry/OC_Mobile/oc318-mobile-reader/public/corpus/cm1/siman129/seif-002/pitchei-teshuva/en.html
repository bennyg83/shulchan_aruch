Or collateral. See Responsa Radbaz, part 3, siman 609. If the collateral is worth less than the amount of the loan, there is room to ask whether the guarantor can say that with respect to the amount exceeding the value of the collateral he is like a guarantor who undertook liability only after the loan was made and therefore did not become obligated, because the lender did not originally lend in reliance on him. Radbaz writes that it is reasonable to say otherwise. Since the borrower was trying to redeem the collateral from the lender, this shows that he did not want it sold, perhaps because it was precious to him, did not belong to him, or for some other reason. It is therefore treated as though the collateral were worth the entire debt. When the guarantor then said to the lender, "Return the collateral to him and I will pay the debt," his obligation is treated as having been undertaken at the time the money was advanced, and he is liable for the entire debt. See there. Shaar Mishpat cites this and writes that it is difficult to extract money based on this rationale alone. In his view the case may be compared to siman 356, seif 7. Indeed, he is uncertain in the opposite direction and suggests that perhaps in such a case the person is not considered a guarantor even with respect to the value of the collateral. See there.
