Explicitly. Shach writes that Yam Shel Shlomo rules that the adviser is exempt unless the creditor told him, see, I am relying upon you. Even authorities who hold in siman 306 seif 7, regarding showing a coin to a moneychanger, that this formula need not be stated, agree here because the creditor was not compelled to act on the advice. Liable. A person should not advise another to extend credit based merely on an impression of wealth, because one cannot truly know what belongs to another, even between parent and child. Even if one sees much gold, silver, and coin, perhaps it belongs to others or is offset by debts. Liability therefore applies where the adviser is a brother or partner who clearly knows the debtor has substantial unencumbered assets. If afterward an unforeseen event causes the debtor to lose everything and the adviser did not know of it, the adviser is considered under unavoidable circumstances and is exempt even if the creditor said, see, I am relying upon you. He need not take even a rabbinic oath that he lacked knowledge, because the claim against him is only doubtful. Maharshal. All the more so, if the debtor was solvent when the advice was given and only later suffered financial ruin, the adviser is exempt. Sema agrees. See Shiltei HaGibborim for additional laws. Maharam Galanti siman 61 rules that one who says, I will act with Levi so that he fulfills whatever you decree, has not thereby become a guarantor. Knesset HaGedolah.
