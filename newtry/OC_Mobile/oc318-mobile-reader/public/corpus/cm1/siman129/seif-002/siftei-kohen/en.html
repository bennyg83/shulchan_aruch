["And even if he did not become a guarantor, etc. Yam Shel Shlomo, Bava Kamma chapter HaGozel Etzim, siman 24, rules that he is exempt unless he expressly said, see, I rely upon you. Even according to the opinion below, siman 306 seif 7, that one who shows a coin to a money changer need not say, see, I rely upon you, here all agree that such language is required because the claimant was not compelled to act on the advice. Mahari Weil writes that this case is no worse than showing a coin to a money changer. This is difficult for the Rama, who below in siman 306 rules that the words, see, I rely upon you, are necessary. He should likewise have required them here, especially according to Maharshal, who says that all agree here. Mordechai and Hagahot Ashri support Maharshal. Mahari Weil's case evidently involved circumstances proving actual reliance. The Rama too must mean that the phrase, he acted on his advice, describes a case where it is evident that he relied upon him and that the adviser knew of that reliance. As the Rif, Rambam, and Mechaber explain in siman 306, where the circumstances prove reliance it is equivalent to an express statement, see, I rely upon you. The Bach writes that the case actually involved an express statement, but that is not the implication of the Rama or Mahari Weil. If, however, the adviser is genuinely expert in the person's affairs, such as his brother or partner, knows the amount of his capital, and knows that he owes nothing to others, he is exempt even if expressly told, see, I rely upon you, as follows from the Gemara there."]
["Because he is solvent, etc., he must pay, etc. He should not have given such advice, because one person generally cannot know what another truly owns. This is so even between father and son, and even if he sees large quantities of gold, silver, and cash, for perhaps they are not his or are encumbered by debts. Only in a case such as brothers who inherited a substantial estate, where he knows they owed no one before the inheritance, or former partners where he knows the other's substantial share and knows clearly that he owes nothing to anyone, and then an unforeseen event later caused him to lose all his assets, is the adviser considered compelled by circumstances and exempt. He is exempt even if told, see, I rely upon you, and he need not take a heset oath that he did not know, because the claim against him is only uncertain. So rules Maharshal. All the more so, if the person was solvent when the advice was given and later became insolvent, the adviser is exempt, as Sema seif katan 7 also writes. See Shiltei Giborim in Bava Batra, chapter Get Pashut."]
