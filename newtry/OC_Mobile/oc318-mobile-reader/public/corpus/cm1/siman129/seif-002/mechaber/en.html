If he says when the money is given, 'Lend him, and I am the guarantor,' the guarantor becomes obligated without a kinyan. {Rama: Even if he did not expressly guarantee the debt but told the lender to lend to the borrower because the borrower was trustworthy, and the lender acted on his word but the representation was false, he must reimburse the lender, because it is as though he guaranteed the debt, as stated in Mahariv 80. Similarly, if beit din made him a guarantor, he becomes obligated even without a kinyan. For example, beit din was about to collect from the borrower, and he said, 'Leave him alone, and I am your guarantor.' Since he benefits from beit din's trust in him, he obligates himself through that benefit.}
