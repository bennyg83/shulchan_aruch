And he is not joined with the borrower and lender, etc. See Sema, seif katan 21. He writes that according to the view that a kinyan alone permits collection from encumbered property, where the document reads ‘Ploni’ without the conjunction ‘and,’ the guarantee is inferior. It appears to me that this view understands the Gemara’s distinction in Get Pashut as referring to a guarantor at the time the money is advanced. If the document states ‘and Ploni,’ the guarantee becomes public and one may collect from encumbered property. If it says only ‘Ploni,’ the witnesses’ signatures do not relate to the guarantee. This also appears from Rashbam. According to my explanation of that view, if it is established that a kinyan was made and the witnesses testify before us, then even when the guarantor’s name is written without the conjunction, one may collect from encumbered property according to the Ramah. This is the primary ruling, unlike the implication of the Sema.
