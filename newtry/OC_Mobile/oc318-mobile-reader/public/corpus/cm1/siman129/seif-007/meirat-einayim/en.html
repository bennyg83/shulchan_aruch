He collects only from unencumbered property. This is specifically where a kinyan was made, as explained in seif 5.
He is not joined together with the borrower and lender. Therefore, even if a kinyan was made before witnesses, if the guarantor's kinyan is not recorded in the document the creditor cannot collect from encumbered property. When the document says, "and so-and-so is guarantor," with the connecting letter, the wording joins the guarantor to the borrower's obligation and reveals that his kinyan was intended to bind him within the same written lien. But if it says merely, "so-and-so is guarantor," without the connecting formulation, his own wording reveals that he did not intend the kinyan and writing to create more than a claim against his unencumbered property. According to the view that an independent kinyan alone can create a lien on encumbered property, one must say that this bare written formulation is actually worse than silence, because it reveals an intention not to create the full lien through the kinyan.
