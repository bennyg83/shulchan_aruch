Only where the text says “and so-and-so is guarantor,” with the conjunction “and,” does it add and join the guarantor to the borrower's lien, so that just as the creditor may collect the debt from encumbered property, the guarantor too is bound to that extent. If the name is written without the conjunction, he revealed that his intent in the kinyan and writing was only to permit collection from unencumbered property. According to the opinion above that a kinyan alone permits collection from encumbered property, one must say that writing the guarantor's name without the conjunction is worse than writing nothing at all. This is the Sema's position. See the Shach and Taz there. Knesset HaGedolah cites Maharshal Yonah, who extensively discusses the views of Ramah and Tosafot and concludes that one may collect from encumbered property only with both a kinyan and a document, or where the guarantee was undertaken before the signatures together with a kinyan, or at the time the money was given and before the signatures, and only where the conjunction “and” is used. Without it, one may not collect from encumbered property.
