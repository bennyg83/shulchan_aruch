(See there, gloss: And the lender himself signed as a witness). Sema writes that Rama was imprecise. Rama's words are correct. If the lender signed for the purchaser, he can ordinarily still seize on the alternative claim that the second purchaser is more convenient to him, as explained in siman 111. Here, however, the lender does not make that claim. He expressly says that this purchaser was as satisfactory as the other and that he willingly waived the lien because he relied on the guarantor. The guarantor is therefore discharged, because according to the lender's own account he caused his own loss. A similar principle appears in siman 146, where a party's express denial alters the result. This is simple and clear.
