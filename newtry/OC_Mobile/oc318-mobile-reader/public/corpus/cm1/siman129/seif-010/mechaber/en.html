If the borrower is absent, he must be notified when he is close enough for a messenger to travel to him and return within thirty days. If he does not pay then, the lender may sue the guarantor. If the borrower is overseas and cannot be notified, and owns no property here, or if he is a violent person who does not obey judgment, the lender may sue the guarantor and collect from him. The guarantor then proceeds against the borrower and collects what he paid on the borrower's behalf, or has the borrower placed under a ban until he repays him. {Rama: This appears to be the primary rule, although some disagree and hold that even if the borrower is violent and does not obey judgment, collection cannot be made from the guarantor until beit din compels the borrower to submit to judgment. If the guarantor says, 'Give me time, and I will bring the borrower to beit din,' then if the borrower's location is known, he is granted enough time to bring him. If the location is unknown, he is given thirty days. Once he brings the borrower to beit din, the guarantor is exempt until the borrower swears that he has no means to pay. If the guarantor brought him at twilight before Shabbat and he fled after Shabbat, the guarantor is not exempt. See notes 2 and 40.}
