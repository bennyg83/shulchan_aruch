["Within thirty days. See Sema seif katan 29, who divides the law into three categories. In my work I established three different categories, and they are primary. If the borrower's location is known and he is within a thirty day distance, the lender must trouble himself to pursue him. If his location is unknown, the guarantor must make the effort and is given thirty days. If it is known that the borrower is farther away than thirty days, the guarantor is given the time reasonably needed. This follows from the Tur, seif 11 and 12, and resolves the otherwise conflicting implications."]
["And he has no property here. If he had property here, beit din would collect from the borrower's assets even in his absence. So writes the Sema. Nevertheless, if he is close enough to be notified within thirty days, the lender must wait those thirty days, as stated above in siman 106. Maharshal likewise rules this, and see Tosafos and the authorities in Bava Batra, chapter Get Pashut."]
["Or if he is a violent person. The Sema cites Rabbenu Yerucham, who distinguishes a case where the borrower was already violent before the loan. In such a case we say the lender extended the loan with knowledge of that condition. In my view Rabbenu Yerucham's distinction is primary. But if beit din already imposed coercive measures and the borrower persists in his defiance, or if he is close to the authorities and there is concern of serious harm, all agree that collection may be made from the guarantor. So rules the Bach."]
["Once he brings him to beit din, the guarantor is released. See Sema seif katan 33, who explains that if the borrower then flees beit din before swearing that he lacks means, this is the case addressed by the continuation concerning bringing him on Friday. The Sema contrasts this with the implication of the Maggid Mishneh. In fact the Maggid Mishneh agrees, as I showed in my work, and this reading is compelled by Baal HaTerumot. The Beit Yosef was imprecise when he transferred the Maggid Mishneh's words to the Tur. The same difficulty applies to the Bach. See Lechem Mishneh at the end of chapter 25."]
