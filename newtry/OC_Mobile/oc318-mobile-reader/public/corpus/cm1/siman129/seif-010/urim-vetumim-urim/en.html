And to come within thirty days. Beit din sends notice to the borrower, and the lender initially pays the messenger's fee, later recovering it from the borrower or guarantor, similar to the rule above in siman 106, seif 1, concerning collection from a borrower who is absent. This law has several parts. The Sema explains that the full thirty day period is given only if the guarantor asks beit din to send notice to the borrower, perhaps the borrower will pay voluntarily. Beit din on its own, however, sends a messenger only where he can go and return within three days, provided it is known where the borrower is and notice can reach him within that period. If he cannot be notified or his whereabouts are unknown, beit din need not send at all. But if the guarantor wishes to make the effort to seek him and requests time, he is given whatever period is reasonably needed. See Sema. In Tumim I wrote that two aspects of the Sema's ruling are correct: a messenger may be given up to thirty days, and if the guarantor himself wishes to go, beit din gives him the time it deems necessary. But the Sema's distinction that beit din on its own sends only where the trip is within three days is very strained. The Sema was driven to it by a difficulty in the Derishah: the Tur gives a limit of thirty days, while the Rosh in Bava Batra refers to the rule in chapter HaGozel, where only three days are mentioned. I rejected the Sema's forced distinctions and showed that they do not adequately resolve the Tur. I therefore concluded with a satisfactory explanation and as practical law that thirty days are always given, just as in collection from an absent debtor, where siman 106 states a thirty day period. Although the Perishah cites Rabbeinu Yerucham as giving only three days, I explained in Tumim that Rabbeinu Yerucham follows his own approach. We rule thirty days.
And he has no property here, etc. If he did have property here, beit din would descend to the borrower's property even in his absence. See Sema. Nevertheless, if he is close enough to be notified within thirty days, the lender must wait thirty days, as in siman 106. So writes Maharshal. See Tosafot and the authorities in chapter Get Pashut, cited by Shach.
A violent person. Rabbeinu Yerucham distinguishes that if the borrower was already violent before the loan, we say the creditor lent with that situation in mind. The Beit Yosef and Darchei Moshe cite the Rashba that there is no distinction, because perhaps the creditor lent from the outset only in reliance on the guarantor. Even where there was another guarantor, we see that he was not satisfied until this one too entered the obligation by kinyan. The Sema concludes that Rabbeinu Yerucham's words appear primary, because otherwise the creditor should have required a kablan guarantor rather than accepting an ordinary guarantor. See what I wrote in Tumim to siman 111, seif katan 6. Here in Tumim I brought a semblance of proof for the Rashba from Yehudah's statement to Yaakov, 'I will guarantee him,' and concluded that the law follows the Rashba, Beit Yosef, and Darchei Moshe. I also gave a correct and satisfactory answer to the Tosafot Yom Tov's difficulty on the Beit Yosef's use of that verse, despite the Gemara's rejection of it. See there.
And once he brings him to beit din, etc. Meaning, if the borrower fled from beit din without paying and without swearing that he has no assets, the creditor still cannot collect from the guarantor until he first returns against the borrower and requires that oath. The Mechaber then says that if the borrower was brought on Friday afternoon, when beit din could not adjudicate with him and administer the oath, it is as though he was never properly brought before beit din, and the guarantor must bring him again. This is clear and is also written in the Darchei Moshe. See Sema.
