He must notify him. See Sema, seif katan 29. The correct reading is: 'But beit din themselves do not send unless the messenger can go and return within three days.' This is also the reading in Perishah. The point is that beit din may not proceed against the guarantor until they first make demand upon the borrower. If the borrower is within a three day journey, he is treated as present and the guarantor cannot be sued at all. If he is farther away, they may proceed against the guarantor unless the guarantor asks for time. If the borrower is within thirty days' travel, beit din send notice to him. Sema's concluding phrase, that they give him according to his need, refers to the case he described in which this cannot be done within the regular period, in which case beit din grants time according to its judgment. If they do not know where the borrower is, they give the guarantor more than thirty days, and the burden of locating him is upon the guarantor.
