The creditor swears and collects. Tumim argues that this proves Rosh agrees that absent a certain plea of payment from the borrower the creditor would not need to swear, and that the case is therefore unlike ordinary collection from encumbered property. I have already written that the law follows Sema. The novelty here is simply that the creditor swears and collects from the guarantor. The same oath is required even if the borrower himself is not presently before us.
