And the borrower is not, etc. For the kablan should have stipulated. These are the words of Maharam in Mordechai, chapter Get Pashut. There he writes that an ordinary guarantor is exempt because, since the borrower is exempt, the guarantor is likewise exempt. He brings proof from chapter Yesh Bechor, concerning two sons one of whom is a firstborn, where the Gemara says, Can there be a case where the borrower is exempt and the guarantor liable? See there. According to Maharam, that statement must apply specifically to an ordinary guarantor, while a kablan remains liable even where the borrower is exempt because the kablan is himself the creditor's direct litigant. If so, the inference drawn in Yesh Bechor from the case of a guarantor for two persons named Yosef ben Shimon, cited by the Shulchan Aruch above at the end of siman 49, would likewise apply only to an ordinary guarantor. A kablan for two such persons would actually be liable. In truth, however, Maharam's ruling requires study, as the Shach writes in seif katan 32, and the Shulchan Aruch itself several times appears to contradict it. Above in siman 108, regarding a borrower who died during the creditor's lifetime and a guarantor, the Tur and Shulchan Aruch state that the rule of Rav and Shmuel applies equally to a kablan. Yet if the kablan is the creditor's own direct litigant, and the kablan is alive, what relevance is there to the borrower having died in the creditor's lifetime? The Shach there in siman 108 already alludes to this difficulty. Therefore the Rama's ruling here requires study. The principal view appears to be that of the Maggid Mishneh and those who agree with him, while Maharam stands alone against all these authorities.
It appears to me that the creditor swears and collects. This itself proves that the Rama agrees that if the borrower did not make a definite claim that the debt was paid, the creditor would not need to swear. Thus this is not comparable to one who comes to collect from encumbered property, who must swear even where the borrower makes no claim and admits that he did not pay. Otherwise, why distinguish this case? In every case where one comes to collect he would have to swear. This therefore disagrees with the Sema above in seif katan 28. See what I wrote there.
