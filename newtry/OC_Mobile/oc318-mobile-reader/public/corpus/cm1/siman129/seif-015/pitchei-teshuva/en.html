For the kablan to stipulate. Baer Heitev quotes the Shach incompletely. The Shach expressly writes, following the Maggid Mishneh, that even from a kablan one may not collect without clear proof unless he received and transferred the money himself, and he cites Responsa Rabbi Eliyahu ibn Chaim and Sefer HaTerumot. Thus the Shach understands Sefer HaTerumot to exempt the kablan not only where the borrower claims payment, but even where the borrower is absent. Responsa VeShav HaKohen, siman 43, analyzes Sefer HaTerumot at length and argues that its position, together with that of the Raavad, is narrower: if the borrower claims payment, or if the borrower died and his heirs are legally exempt, the creditor cannot collect from the kablan, because the same debt cannot be extinguished as against the borrower yet remain collectible from the kablan. If the borrower is merely absent, however, his liability has not been legally extinguished, so the kablan cannot say, 'Perhaps he paid you'; his uncertainty is like 'I do not know whether I repaid you.' On this reading there are three positions. Maharam in the Mordechai holds that even where the borrower claims payment the kablan remains liable. Sefer HaTerumot and the Raavad exempt the kablan where the borrower claims payment or has died, but not merely because he is absent. The Maggid Mishneh exempts him even when the borrower is absent. VeShav HaKohen argues that where Maharam is certainly stringent and the interpretation of Sefer HaTerumot and the Raavad is doubtful, we do not abandon Maharam's clear rule because of that doubt. The Maggid Mishneh would then stand as a lone opinion on the absent-borrower case. He adds that even if the kablan says the borrower told him that he paid the lender, the kablan is not thereby exempt. Responsa Chacham Tzvi, siman 36 in the addenda, discusses a case where Reuven asked whether he should extend merchandise on credit to Levi, and Shimon became a kablan for whatever Reuven supplied. Levi later claimed that Reuven had deceived him with debased silver. Chacham Tzvi rules that Reuven must first litigate with Levi; whatever beit din determines Levi owes, Shimon must pay, but before Levi's liability is established the kablan need not pay. He compares this to the rule that a borrower may claim payment and thereby exempt the kablan according to several authorities, and therefore may also deny receipt by a migo of payment. Pitchei Teshuva remarks that in such a case even Maharam may agree, and leaves the matter requiring study.
