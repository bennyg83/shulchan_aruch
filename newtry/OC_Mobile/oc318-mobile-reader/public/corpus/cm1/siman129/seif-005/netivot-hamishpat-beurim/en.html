Whenever a kinyan is required. See Shach, seif katan 12, through his discussion of profit and a guarantor who undertakes liability after the money was given. Ketzot HaChoshen writes that it is considered guaranty at the time the money is given in an iska arrangement, which is half loan and half deposit, where the investor leaves the profit with the recipient in reliance upon the guarantor. This is not correct. When the profit comes into existence, it is already in the recipient's possession, so it is not comparable to money being released in reliance upon the guarantor. Nor is it comparable to the law at the end of siman 81 concerning two people who jointly accepted an iska and become guarantors for the profit as well. There, once they later received the profit and continued the enterprise jointly, they became like two people who accepted a deposit together and are guarantors for one another. Here, however, the guarantor is not present when the recipient acquires the profit. What Shach writes about a guarantor whom the investor appointed over the profit, that it is treated like guaranty at the time the money is given because the guarantor accepted responsibility to exert himself with respect to the profit, would likewise apply to the rental of movable property. Nevertheless, a guarantor to a non Jew for interest is treated only as a guarantor who undertakes liability after the money is given, even though compensation for waiting with money is comparable to rental of movable property, as Rama explains in Yoreh Deah, siman 170. See my Chavat Da'at there, where I distinguished these cases. See Shach, seif katan 15, where there is a scribal error. It should read: 'Where he caused him a loss, even though he performed a mitzvah, and likewise where no mitzvah was involved, he becomes obligated.'
