Just as... This is inferred from Shmuel's statement there, "One who was being strangled, and they made a kinyan from him..." which refers to the Mishnah's case of one being strangled. There the wording is, "and I will give," which according to the other Amoraim is the formulation of a kablan. All the more so where he said, "Leave him and I will pay." See Even HaEzer 102:6. The Gemara continues, "This implies that an ordinary guarantor..." If so, one could not prove the law of an ordinary guarantor from a kablan. Moreover, the Mishnah says, "What is a guarantor?" and uses language of kablanus according to the other Amoraim, while Ben Nanas does not dispute the first Mishnah's law that an ordinary guarantor is liable even though he is not a kablan. Thus, there is no distinction... The Mordechai in the name of Raavyah explains that "all the others require a kinyan" comes to include even a kablan.
Rather... Bava Basra 174b.
Or a dowry... For the same reason stated there regarding a kesubah. See Even HaEzer there, where some disagree, and Sefer HaTerumos, which argues that here she actually caused him a loss, since against the assessed dowry he adds a fifth and obligates himself to her corresponding to the dowry. See Mordechai there.
Whenever... This is the Tur's language, and he follows the Rambam in seif 4 that "all of them" includes even a guarantor whose undertaking is in writing. See Perishah and Tur.
Even if he gave... if... As stated in Bava Metzia 74a concerning situmta, and according to Rabbeinu Chananel there.
Nevertheless... As Rabbeinu Tam derives from Tzidkiyahu and the verse, "and behold, he gave his hand." See above siman 73, seif 8, in the gloss, concerning one who swore, even though...
One who gave... some say... Tosafos in Gittin 50a, s.v. "mitzvah," writes that both factors are required. With only one of them, he is like an ordinary guarantor. The same applies here.
Others disagree. Even if he becomes obligated, it does not follow that his status is the same as one who became guarantor at the time the money was given, since here the consideration of "that benefit" is absent.
