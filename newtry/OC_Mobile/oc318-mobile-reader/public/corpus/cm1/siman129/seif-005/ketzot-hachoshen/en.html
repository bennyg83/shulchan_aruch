Meisharim writes that one who invests money under an iska arrangement and obtains a guarantor for the expected profit can bind the guarantor without a kinyan when the guaranty is created at the time the capital is delivered. The Sema and Shach discuss who arranged the guaranty. The Shach says that if the recipient of the iska produced the guarantor, then regarding future profit the guarantor resembles one who became guarantor after the loan and would need a kinyan. In my view the profit arising from the deposit half of the iska is literally the investor's property. The recipient is working with the investor's half, and when the investor leaves his share of the profit in the recipient's hands, he is effectively extending that later amount to him in reliance on the guarantor's direction. This resembles a guarantor who says, in advance, "If you lend him later, I guarantee repayment." Even though the future profit does not exist yet, when it later comes into the recipient's hands it does so under the original guaranty arrangement. The law cited by Beit Yosef from Mordechai, that a guarantor for interest requires a kinyan because it is like guaranty after the time of the loan, concerns ordinary gentile interest that never belonged to the lender as principal until it became payable. It is not the same as the investor's own profit share generated from the deposit half of an iska. Therefore, where it is known that profit was actually earned in the iska, the guarantor is liable for the investor's profit share that reached the recipient's hands even without a kinyan.
