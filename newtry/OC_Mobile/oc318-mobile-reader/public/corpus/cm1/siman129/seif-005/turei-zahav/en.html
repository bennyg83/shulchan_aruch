(Seif 7) And he is not joined together with the borrower and lender. Rambam writes that an acquisition was made from the guarantor; the Mechaber omits this because he already wrote in seif 4 that according to Rambam even a guaranty written before the witnesses' signatures requires an acquisition. On this basis, the words “so-and-so is guarantor” without a connecting vav are not helped by the earlier acquisition. Rambam's rule is that after the witnesses have signed, nothing creates a lien, even against free property, except a separate acquisition; and no acquisition then creates a lien against purchasers. To bind purchasers, the guaranty must be written before the signatures as part of the instrument, “and so-and-so is guarantor,” so that the witnesses attest to the guarantor's obligation as they do to the borrower's. If it says merely “so-and-so is guarantor” without a vav before the signatures, the witnesses' attestation refers only to the preceding note and the guaranty is like one added after signature. With a separate acquisition it may be collected only from free property. The Tur's own position cannot be explained this way, because he expressly says that the acquisition extends to the entire document. If the document instead begins a new statement, “so-and-so is guarantor,” it does not relate back to the earlier acquisition and is a written guaranty without acquisition. Ramah, as cited by the Tur, says that after signature a guaranty without acquisition cannot even be collected from free property. It is difficult, because the Mishnah says that a guaranty written after signature is collected from free property, which would then have to mean with an acquisition; yet Ramah elsewhere says that an acquisition without writing is collectible even from purchasers. One must say that in Ramah's view an ordinary acquisition is intended for writing and therefore has the force of a proper instrument, while writing added after the signatures is inferior because it visibly appears subordinate and later than the loan, and therefore reaches only free property. The first occurrence of “so-and-so” in the Tur's quotation of Ramah is a scribal error.
