(Seif 2) The guarantor became obligated. Beit Yosef writes that the Gemara derives this from Judah's words, “I will guarantee him.” Rav Hisda objects that Judah was a kablan, since he said, “Place him in my hand,” and the Gemara ultimately derives ordinary guaranty from “My son, if you guaranteed your fellow.” Many ask why Beit Yosef did not cite the conclusion. They ask from lack of attention. Rav Huna derives guaranty from Judah, consistent with his later view that one who says, “Lend him and I will give,” is only an ordinary guarantor. Rabbi Yitzhak likewise holds this. Earlier Rabbi Yitzhak answered only according to Rav Hisda, who maintained that Judah was a kablan. Beit Yosef therefore properly cited Judah, because Rava may also follow that view. Had Sema considered this, he would not have cited “if you guaranteed your fellow.” The true derivation is properly from Judah, who was only an ordinary guarantor, in accordance with the ruling later in this siman.
