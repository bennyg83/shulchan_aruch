Released. The Sema explains that this means the creditor told the borrower, "From now on I will have no dealings with you, only with the guarantor." Therefore the guarantor becomes obligated even after the money was advanced. A similar rule appears in the Rama's gloss to siman 126:2. See Responsa Rama 72.
Money. The Shach disputes this ruling and writes that Mahari Weil, its source, never intended such a distinction. What difference does it make that the guarantor holds the borrower's money? No such distinction appears in the Gemara or authorities. Responsa Rosh, the Ir Shushan, and the Sema in the gloss to siman 126:2 indicate the contrary. Mahari Weil himself likewise implies that the guarantor is liable specifically where the very money for which he guaranteed was in his possession, but otherwise not. This is the primary ruling. Shach.
