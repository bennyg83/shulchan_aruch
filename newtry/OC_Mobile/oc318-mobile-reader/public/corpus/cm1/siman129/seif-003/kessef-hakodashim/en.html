Seif 2 in the gloss. (For he is reliable. See Shach, seif katan 7.) The rule in the laws of guaranty is that merely telling a person that another party is reliable does not create a guaranty. There may be support for this from the Gemara, which states that where a person appoints a messenger before witnesses he may nevertheless explain that his statement meant only, "He is trustworthy; if you wish to send through him, send through him." If such words themselves created guaranty liability, that explanation would not help. Yet the Gemara may concern only unavoidable loss. If the messenger is not formally appointed to stand in the principal's place, a statement that he is trustworthy does not make the speaker a guarantor against every accident, because the statement of trustworthiness was not false. The matter requires further study. A guaranty for an employee's performance may also present difficulty. The ordinary reliance benefit supporting guaranty is weaker because a worker can retract in the middle of the day. Even a kinyan may involve asmachta unless made before an important beit din and in a presently effective form. If a person says that he guarantees that a certain individual will conduct business with another, meaning that the other may trust him with goods and money, and the parties then conduct repeated transactions over time, ambiguous wording should be construed narrowly against imposing continuing guaranty. Even if the language broadly refers to ongoing business, it may mean only one transaction or one ordinary commercial cycle of advances and repayments. Even language such as "you will never lose through this" does not necessarily create a guaranty for an unlimited period unless the duration and nature of the guaranty are expressed clearly. Documentary ambiguity is construed against the party seeking to enforce the instrument.
