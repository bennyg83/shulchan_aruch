Likewise, if the lender released the borrower, etc. This also appears from the responsum of the Rashba cited by the Beit Yosef in siman 126, section 8. See Responsa Rama, siman 72.
Some say that if he had, etc. It appears that the Rama states the first ruling without qualification and introduces this ruling as ‘some say’ in order to indicate that it is disputed. This accords with what he wrote in Darkei Moshe concerning Mahari Weil, namely that this distinction is not evident in the words of Maharam in Mordechai, chapter Eizehu Neshekh, regarding the official to whom the community undertook to pay Reuven’s tax. The reasoning there does not depend on whether the guarantor already held money of the borrower, but on the fact that Reuven was exempt toward the ruler, so that the guarantee was given at the time the money was advanced. In truth, Maharam’s responsum does not imply the proposed distinction, and Mahari Weil himself cites Maharam there. In my humble opinion, Mahari Weil never intended such a rule. What difference does it make that the guarantor holds other money belonging to the borrower? No such distinction appears in the Gemara or the authorities. The responsum of the Rosh, the Levush, and the Sema above, siman 126, seif 3, seif katan 11, expressly imply that even where the guarantor holds money of the borrower, he remains a guarantor who undertook liability after the money was advanced. Mahari Weil ruled only where the very money lent was in the guarantor’s hands. Since she admitted that the loan money came into her possession and undertook to pay, she became obligated. His words are: ‘When she said, I wish to pay you, she became obligated. Although a guarantor who undertakes after the money is advanced is not bound, that applies to an ordinary guarantor who does not hold the money for which she guaranteed. This woman, however, while the money remains in her hands, is no worse than a guarantor.’ Thus Mahari Weil implies the opposite: only where the very money for which the guarantee was given was in the guarantor’s possession is the guarantor liable; otherwise not. This is the correct ruling.
