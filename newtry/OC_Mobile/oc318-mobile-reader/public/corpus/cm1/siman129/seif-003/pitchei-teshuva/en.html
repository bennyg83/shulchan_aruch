Or a pledge. Responsa Radbaz, part 3, siman 609, discusses a pledge worth less than the debt. One may ask whether the guarantor can say that as to the unsecured balance his guarantee was undertaken only after the money was advanced and therefore does not bind him, since the lender did not lend that portion in reliance on the guarantor. Radbaz inclines to liability for the entire debt. Because the borrower sought to redeem the pledge rather than allow it to remain with the lender, this indicates that he did not regard it as ordinary property for sale, perhaps because it was precious to him, belonged to someone else, or for some other reason. It is therefore treated as though it secured the whole debt. When the guarantor said, "Return the pledge to him and I will pay the debt," his undertaking is treated as made at the time of the advance, and he is liable for all of it. Shaar Mishpat cites this but writes that it is difficult to extract money on the strength of that reasoning. He suggests comparison to siman 356, seif 7, and even raises the opposite doubt, that perhaps in such a case the guarantor is not liable even up to the value of the pledge. See there.
