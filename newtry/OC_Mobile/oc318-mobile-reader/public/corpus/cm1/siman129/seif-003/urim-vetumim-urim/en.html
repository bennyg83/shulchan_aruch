Likewise, if he released the borrower orally, etc. Sema explains that the creditor said, 'From now on I have no dealings with you, only with the guarantor.' He therefore becomes bound even though the guaranty was not made when the money was originally delivered. Rama writes similarly above in siman 126:2. See Responsa Rama 72, cited by the Shach. See also the Sema and Shach above in siman 126 concerning whether a kinyan is required. I discussed that issue there at length.
And some say that if the money was still in his hands at the time, etc. The Shach says Rama uses 'some say' because Maharam in Mordechai disagrees with Mahari Weil, the source of this rule. He himself concludes that even Mahari Weil did not state Rama's law, but distinguished only whether the loan money was still intact in the guarantor's hands. Responsa Rosh cited by Sema likewise implies this. The Shach therefore rules unlike Rama. See Tumim. I showed that Maharam does not disprove Rama, although I nevertheless proved from Responsa Rosh, rule 69, and from Ritva in Kiddushin that Rama's rule is not generally correct. As to Mahari Weil, he did not mean the Shach's distinction, because a loan is given for expenditure and the law should not depend on whether the same coins remain. Rather, I distinguished a case where the borrower had no other assets. Then the money in the guarantor's hands is subject to Rabbi Natan's lien. This is the correct understanding. I concluded that where Rabbi Natan's lien does not apply, Rama's rule has no firm legal basis.
