The same rule applies to two who became responsible for one another, etc. This follows the Rambam, who holds that where two people guaranteed one debtor, the creditor may collect the entire debt from either one. The Tur writes, "two who became responsible for one another," which is correct even according to the Raavad and those who follow him, who hold that where two people merely guaranteed one debtor, only half may be collected from each. When each became an absolute guarantor for the other, however, all agree that the creditor may collect the entire debt from whichever one he chooses. See siman 132:3, where this dispute is stated, and siman 107:9 regarding two brothers who inherited and a creditor sought to collect from property sold by one of them.
But an absolute guarantor who received and delivered, etc. The same applies to an ordinary guarantor according to the opinion cited by the Mechaber above in seif 19. The Mechaber writes that the creditor cannot collect from purchasers, following the Tur and the Baal HaTerumot. This wording is not exact, because he cannot even collect from unencumbered property of the borrower once the guarantor himself received and delivered the funds, as the Mechaber wrote in seif 19. The difference is that with unencumbered property there is no practical consequence, because after the creditor collects from the absolute guarantor, the guarantor may return against the borrower and collect from him. He cannot similarly return against purchasers unless the creditor transfers the promissory note to him through a valid transfer of the note, as the Mechaber writes in seif 22 and in siman 130.
