They cannot defer him, etc. See Tumim. Gidulei Terumah and Knesset HaGedolah were troubled that the Rashba's responsa appear self contradictory, and they suggest that the Rashba retracted because of a difficulty from the Gemara in Gittin. I rejected their explanation with sound reasoning, discussed the Rashba's responsum at some length, and ultimately left the matter requiring study. See there at length.
They are responsible for one another, etc. When each one becomes a kablan guarantor for the other, the creditor may collect the entire debt from whichever one he wishes, according to all opinions. See below siman 132, seif 3. At the end of siman 106, the Tur and Mechaber write that where brothers inherited and a creditor comes to collect from property one of them sold, the creditor may do so. See Sema, and see what I wrote there at length.
But a kablan. The same applies to an ordinary guarantor according to the alternative opinion in seif 19. The Mechaber's reference to purchasers is not precise, for even from unencumbered property he cannot collect, since the kablan directly received and transferred the money. With unencumbered property belonging to the borrower there is no practical difference, because after the creditor collects from the kablan, the kablan returns and collects from the borrower. With purchasers, however, the kablan cannot recover from them until the creditor transfers the promissory note to him through the legal mode for acquiring documents, as the Mechaber writes immediately afterward and in siman 130. See Sema.
