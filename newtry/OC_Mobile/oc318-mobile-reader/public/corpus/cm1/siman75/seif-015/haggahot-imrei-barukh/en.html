(Shach, seif katan 40) And so it is explicit in the Ramban's novellae. Note: The Shach's statement that the Ramban and Ran wrote exactly like the answer of the Rivam requires study. According to Tosafot in Bava Batra in the name of the Rivam, the core principle is that once the Torah obligates a person to swear, if he does not swear he must pay, and even a migo cannot help him. The Rosh and Ri explain why one is nevertheless believed to say "I returned it" by a migo that he could have claimed unavoidable accident, despite the oath required on the latter claim: the obligation of oath does not begin until he actually claims that the item was lost through accident. The Ramban and Ran, by contrast, answer differently. Their foundation is that one witness has the force of two when the defendant does not swear against him. According to them, this special rule applies only where one witness is present, unlike the case of "I returned it" with a migo of unavoidable accident. If the Rosh and Ran had understood the Rivam's answer as resting on the same principle, namely that one witness is as two when not contradicted by oath, they would not have needed their separate explanation that before the claim of accident no oath obligation has yet arisen. Rather, they could have said simply that a migo generally applies even where the alternative claim would require an oath, and the case of the seized ingot is different only because the one witness stands as two. It follows that they understood the Rivam to hold that wherever the rule of "obligated to swear but unable" applies, a migo does not help, regardless of whether the oath arose through one witness. That is why they had difficulty with the case of "I returned it" and needed their distinction. Thus the Rivam's answer cannot simply be equated with the Ramban and Ran. Nevertheless, according to the Rashba's novellae cited in Shitah Mekubetzet to Bava Batra in the sugya of the seized ingot, in the name of the Rivam, his view can be explained like the Ramban and Ran. The Sema and Derishah also appear not to have understood the Rivam in the way the Rosh and Ri did. From the Rashba's novellae cited in Shitah Mekubetzet to Ketubot 85, where he says, "and this is also the Rivam's view regarding the witnesses of Rabbi Abba's ingot," it appears clear that he understood the Rivam like the Ramban and Ran: one witness who is not contradicted by oath is treated as two.
