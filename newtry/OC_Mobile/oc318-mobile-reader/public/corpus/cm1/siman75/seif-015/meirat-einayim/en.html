Whether he could not swear on the principal oath, etc., rather he shall swear, etc. The Tur explains: if one claims one hundred and the defendant says, 'I owe you only fifty, and regarding the other fifty I do not know,' and the claimant adds other matters by gilgul shevuah, to which the defendant answers, 'I owe you nothing on those matters,' then although he cannot swear regarding the principal oath and the rule is that one obligated to swear who cannot swear must pay, that rule is not applied to the gilgul. Rather, if he wishes he swears regarding the gilgul, or he may reverse that oath upon his opponent. Likewise, if one claims one hundred, the defendant admits fifty and denies fifty, and when he is about to swear the claimant adds other matters by gilgul, to which the defendant says, 'I do not know,' then although he is considered unable to swear concerning the gilgul, because the partial admission obligates him to swear one oath that he owes nothing beyond what he admitted and the gilgul prevents him from taking that oath, nevertheless he takes a Torah oath concerning the fifty he denied and a hesset oath concerning the gilgul that he does not know.
Rama alludes to both forms by writing, 'rather he shall swear, etc., concerning the gilgul,' meaning that sometimes he swears a definite denial regarding the gilgul and sometimes he swears that he does not know.We properly apply to this the rule, etc. The Tur explains that since gilgul shevuah is a Torah principle, where the defendant is already obligated in a Torah oath on the principal claim, he must include everything in that oath. If he cannot swear concerning the gilgul, he pays. But where the principal claim itself is, 'fifty I know and fifty I do not know,' so that he must already pay and there is no Torah oath on the principal claim, all agree that he may take only a hesset oath concerning the gilgul and thereby be exempt.
