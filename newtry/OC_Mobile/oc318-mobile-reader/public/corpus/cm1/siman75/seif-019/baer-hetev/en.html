Assessed as able. The Shach leaves open a case where one claims one hundred from a defendant known to possess only ten gold coins or the like. Is the defendant still treated as one who cannot swear and therefore must pay at least the ten that he plausibly could have had, or does the plaintiff's exaggeration make him a false claimant and eliminate the claim entirely? The Shach inclines that the defendant remains liable under the inability-to-swear rule for the amount he was plausibly able to possess. See also the end of siman 90.
For behold. The Shach writes that this rule is clear from the Ramban, Ran, Rivash, apparently the Rosh, the Mechaber in siman 292, the Rambam and Tur at the beginning of siman 298, siman 388, and many other places. He regards this as the principal law and questions why the Tur presents two opinions here. Concerning whether an admission of fruit worth less than a perutah counts as an admission, see siman 292 and the Shach's earlier critique in siman 72.
