For in any event the claimant demanded at least two silver ma'ah, unlike the preceding seif, where he did not demand a definite amount but only made a general claim, so perhaps the denied portion does not equal two silver ma'ah. We do not say here as well that perhaps he admitted more than a perutah, in which case the denied portion might still be less than two silver ma'ah, because his admission was compelled by the circumstances and we do not interpret such an admission as exceeding a perutah. Sema and Shach:
A matter that he is estimated to possess. But if he claims, "I lent you one thousand dinars," and the defendant is estimated to possess only one hundred, we do not obligate him to pay the entire thousand, for the claim appears false. It seems, however, that he must at least pay the hundred that he is estimated to possess. We do not say that since part of the claim is untenable the entire claim is untenable, because claiming an amount beyond his apparent means is not certainly false. It is possible that one appears poor while possessing great wealth. We simply do not believe the claimant beyond the amount the defendant is estimated to possess. At least as to the amount within that estimate, he should be believed. This is clear. The Shach expresses doubt, but in my humble opinion there is no doubt, since he is not certainly lying.
