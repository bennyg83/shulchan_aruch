His chest was broken open. See Responsa Panim Me’irot, part II, siman 155, by my ancestor, where he was asked about a person who found his chest broken open and a large sum of money stolen from it, while several strong presumptions indicated that one of his servants had committed the act. Was it permissible to hand the servant over to the secular authorities to be tortured until he confessed? He discusses the matter at length and brings proof from the incidents in Bava Batra 167a, where they bound a person and he confessed. Since, on the basis of an assessment of the circumstances, they saw that there had been a forgery and the person refused to admit it, they bound him and he confessed. “They bound him” there means corporal punishment, as in Chullin 110b regarding a man who failed to honor his father, where Rashi explains that they bound him to the post in order to flog him. See also the Rosh in the chapter Elu Metziot concerning Mar Zutra Chasida, whose silver cup was stolen. The Rosh explains that they bound the suspect with lashes until he confessed. He further cites the Tur and the Gemara, above siman 42 seif 3, that if it is necessary to coerce and strike the holder of a document until he admits the truth, this may be done. He also records that in the days of the gaon Rabbi Heshel, the Shach deposited a gold chain with him, which was stolen by a servant, and they agreed to afflict the servant somewhat until he confessed. He therefore concludes that when, after investigation by God-fearing persons, it is clear that there is legitimate reason to suspect the person, they may subject him to limited coercion so that he admit the truth. It is nevertheless improper to hand him over to the secular authorities. He concludes that this should be done only with the agreement of three Torah scholars who fear Hashem and despise gain.
