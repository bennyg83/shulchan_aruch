And some say, etc. Baal HaTerumot, gate 38, part 3, concludes in the name of Ramban that no further inquiry is required, because an admission is presumed to involve at least a perutah. Ramban derives this from Rabbi Yosef HaLevi and the Rambam in siman 72, seif 12, seif katan 48, concerning one who says, “I know the pledge was worth more, but I do not know how much,” who is treated as one obligated to swear but unable to do so, and therefore pays. The same is evident from Ramban, Ran, and Rivash cited there, from the Rosh in Shevuat HaDayanim, from the Rambam and Mechaber below in siman 292, seif 14, and from the Rambam and Tur at the beginning of siman 298 and in siman 388, end of seif 1, as well as many other places. This appears to be the principal ruling. It is therefore somewhat difficult that the Tur presents two opinions here.
For his admission cannot be for less than a perutah. Concerning produce, however, the Rosh writes in Shevuat HaDayanim that even produce worth less than a perutah is still called “produce,” and therefore an unspecified admission concerning produce is not necessarily worth a perutah.
