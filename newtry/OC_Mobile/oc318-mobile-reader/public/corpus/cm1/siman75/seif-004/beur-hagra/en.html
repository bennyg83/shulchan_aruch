And some say, specifically... As stated there, 4b: "but two, he is exempt..." So write the Rif and Rosh there, as stated above in siman 88, seifim 28 and 33.
A document is considered "here it is," and there is an opinion that disagrees. Their dispute depends on the text of the Gemara there concerning "the document that he admits..." as Rashi explains there, s.v. "this is the correct reading, and the document..." Certainly no one disputes the general principle that a document is considered "here it is," as stated in the Gemara there. They dispute only a case of this kind, where he does not admit the document. Yet this requires study, because in any event he should be exempt since the claim is secured by land, and according to all opinions here, even if he denied it, he is not liable because one does not swear concerning denial of an obligation secured by land, as stated there and by the Rif, and below in seif 28, "not only..." See the Shach, who also distinguishes here between admitting and denying it, such as where the debtor has no land. This requires study.
(Collected note) And there is an opinion that disagrees. In the Yerushalmi at the beginning of chapter 2 of Kesubos it states explicitly that he is exempt even with a document. (End of note.)
