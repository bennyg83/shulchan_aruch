Even though he stipulated with her, etc. The Ran writes: 'In the Galil, they do not compel her to leave.' Meaning, even if the husband is from Yehudah and may take her from a large city in the Galil to a city in Yehudah, if his residence in Yehudah was in a city he cannot argue that since he can take her from a large city in the Galil to a city in Yehudah, all the more so he can compel her to move from a large city in the Galil to a city in the Galil. Rather, she consented only to that which she specifically accepted. There is room to question whether he may move her within the Galil to an equivalent location, or whether, if he wishes to leave her where she is and she says, 'I married you on the understanding that you would bring me to your place, because your place appealed to me,' this is treated as though she had expressly stipulated that he bring her there. Alternatively, perhaps the choice remains his, and what we say, that he married her on this understanding, means only that he has the option to take her to his place, not that he is obligated to do so.
