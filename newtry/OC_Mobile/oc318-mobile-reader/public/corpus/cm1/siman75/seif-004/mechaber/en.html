If the defendant denies the entire claim and witnesses testify that he owes fifty, he pays the fifty and takes a Torah oath concerning the remainder, so that an admission from his own mouth should not have greater force than the testimony of witnesses. {Rama: Some say this applies only when the witnesses testify orally and there was no kinyan. If they wrote a document, or if there was a kinyan, their testimony does not obligate him to swear, because a document is treated like an immediate tender of payment. Others disagree. See below, the beginning of siman 87.} When does this apply? When the claimant demands repayment of a loan. But if he claims deposited objects worth one hundred and the defendant says, 'You have nothing of mine in your possession,' while witnesses testify that, when sued, they saw in his possession some of those objects worth fifty, he is established as a denier; the claimant swears and collects.
