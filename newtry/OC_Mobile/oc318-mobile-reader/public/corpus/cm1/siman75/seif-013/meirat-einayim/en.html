The ruling continues: if the single witness testifies that he did not repay, he is treated as one obligated to swear who cannot swear.
This is difficult.
Even if the witness says that he never moved his hand from the defendant, the defendant should be able to swear against the witness and say that the witness is lying and that he did repay, and thereby be believed through the oath that contradicts one witness. The Maggid Mishneh likewise writes at the end of chapter 4 of Hilchot To'en that this is specifically where the defendant first said, 'It never happened,' and only after the witness testified did he say he repaid.
In that case he cannot now swear that he repaid, because his own first statement admitted, in effect, that no payment occurred.
But if from the outset he said, 'I borrowed and repaid,' he can swear against the witness.
One must therefore establish the present ruling in a case where the defendant's own prior words prevent him from taking the oath that would otherwise contradict the witness, so that the rule of one obligated to swear who cannot swear must pay applies.
