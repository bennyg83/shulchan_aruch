If one claims, 'I lent you one hundred, and here is one witness,' and the defendant says, 'That is true, but I paid you,' or, 'You owe me an offsetting one hundred,' then if the witness testifies that he did not repay that one hundred, such as where the witness never left him, or testifies that the loan is still within its term, the defendant is obligated to swear but cannot swear, and therefore pays. The same applies if he first claimed, 'This never occurred,' but after one witness was produced against him changed his claim to, 'I borrowed and repaid.' {Rama: Where he could have claimed, 'I paid,' or, 'I returned it,' however, we do not say that he is obligated to swear but cannot swear, because he is believed through a migo. This is the consensus of the later authorities, as stated at the end of Responsa Rivash 392.} If one claims, 'You snatched an object from me,' and has one witness that he snatched it, and the defendant says, 'I certainly snatched it, but it is mine,' he is obligated to swear but cannot swear, and therefore pays. {Rama: Some say this applies specifically where the witness testifies that the object did not come into his possession as collateral. If the witness testifies that it came into his possession as collateral but does not remember how much was lent against it, the lender is believed upon taking an oath as to the amount lent, and we do not say he is obligated to swear but cannot swear, since it came into his possession lawfully. See Responsa Rashba 1040 and 998. Others disagree; see Responsa Ramban 84 and note 111, and what was written above in siman 72, seif 18.} If one claims, 'You have one hundred of mine,' and the defendant answers, 'I owe you only fifty, and regarding the rest I do not know,' then because he cannot swear he must pay, and the claimant need not swear. The borrower may, however, impose an unspecified ban upon anyone who takes his money unlawfully. But if he answers, 'I borrowed fifty from you and repaid it, and regarding the other fifty I do not know whether I borrowed it,' he takes a heses oath that he repaid the fifty and does not know whether he borrowed the other fifty.
