(See Shach, seif katan 33.) The Shach writes that at first glance the Sema appears correct, that he cannot later claim, "I was mistaken." In my humble opinion his intent is that otherwise the Gemara in the chapter Yesh Nochalin should have established the case as one where he later claims that he erred, rather than being forced to establish it where he says, "I do not know," and then to ask according to the opinion that a certain claim is superior. See there. The same is also evident from the case of one who claimed wheat, for otherwise the Gemara at the end of the chapter HaManiach could have explained that the reason the Mishnah does not teach that he is exempt is that he can claim, "I was mistaken." See there carefully. The reason the Tur gives here, namely waiver, requires study. At first glance, in the Gemara Yesh Nochalin it could have been established as a case where he retracted immediately upon the brother's death. Waiver would not be effective there, since it concerns something not yet in existence. See siman 209. This requires study.
