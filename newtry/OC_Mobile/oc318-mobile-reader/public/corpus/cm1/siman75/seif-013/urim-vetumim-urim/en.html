The same rule applies to deposited money if witnesses testify that they saw the money in the defendant's possession at the time of his denial, because he then cannot excuse the denial by saying that he was merely delaying until he could return it. The Shach limits this to money tied and sealed in a manner that prohibits the custodian from using it. If the money was unrestricted and he was permitted to use it, the Shach argues that even though it began as a deposit he may have spent it and merely delayed until he could obtain replacement funds. This follows his view that even unrestricted deposited money still physically in the custodian's hands may be exchanged for other money. See what I wrote above in siman 73, where it appears that this ruling is incorrect. If so, the same law applies even to unrestricted money.
