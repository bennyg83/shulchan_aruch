But I repaid you. This refers to one who claims that the claimant waived the debt, or that he has property of the claimant in his possession as an offset. He is believed only through a migo that he could have claimed payment. Certainly, if one witness testifies that he did not pay, this is a migo used to contradict a witness, and he is one who cannot swear and therefore must pay. But if he claims actual payment, he may swear to contradict the witness and is exempt. Sema and Shach:
Where he could have claimed, "I repaid," means where the witness does not testify that he did not repay:
He is one who cannot swear and therefore must pay. Nevertheless, he may afterward impose a hesset oath upon the claimant. Shach:
He claimed, "You snatched it." The same applies to a handwritten acknowledgment that contains no clause of credibility: afterward he may require a hesset oath. If the handwritten acknowledgment grants credibility, there is one witness, and he claims that he paid, since he is believed only through a migo that he could have claimed forgery, he is one who cannot swear and therefore must pay.
