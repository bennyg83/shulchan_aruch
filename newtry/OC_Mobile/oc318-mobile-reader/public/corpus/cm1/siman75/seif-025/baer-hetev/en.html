Witnesses. The distinction is that with a written instrument a person knows whether he has such a document in his house, and the defendant may therefore say, 'Sue me on the document or nullify it.' With witnesses, however, the claimant may have witnesses and not remember them. This is the language of Sefer HaTerumot. It follows that if the claimant identifies particular witnesses, he must produce them immediately or renounce reliance upon them before the defendant swears. This is also implied by the Levush and the Sema in siman 87, seif 33. Shach. The Taz writes: It appears to me that if a creditor produces an unverified document and the borrower claims repayment, and the creditor seeks to have him swear, while the borrower refuses to swear until the creditor voids or tears up the document, and the creditor says that he may yet find witnesses through whom to verify it, the law favors the creditor. See there. The Taz further records a question concerning one who repaid his fellow money, after which the creditor produced a counterfeit coin and said it had been received as part of the payment. The debtor answered, 'I know nothing about this, and when I paid you I was already discharged on the assumption that the coins were good.' The Taz ruled that this is like one who says, 'I do not know whether I owe you,' because it is a new claim. The debtor takes a hesset oath that he does not know and is exempt. See there for his proof.
