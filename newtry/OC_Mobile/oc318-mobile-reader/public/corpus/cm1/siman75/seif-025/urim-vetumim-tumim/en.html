I had a document and it was lost. It appears that if witnesses establish that the document was actually lost, the claimant need not formally cancel the document. The rule was learned from the case of who swears first, where we fear that one party will swear and the other will afterward produce the entrusted object. Yet with a pledge itself, if witnesses establish that it was lost, even through negligence, the borrower may swear to its value. We do not follow the view that even after a proven loss we still fear that the holder will exert himself and later recover it. If the pledge is later found and turns out to be worth less than the borrower's sworn valuation, the creditor can of course recover the excess previously paid. The same should apply to the lost document. Once its loss is proved, we do not require the claimant to surrender or nullify a right that may later reappear merely because of a remote concern that it will be recovered.
