And he has a wife there. One who studies the Beit Yosef's citation of Tashbetz will see that the statement that the husband already has a wife in the other place is unnecessary to Tashbetz's reasoning. Even without that fact, whenever the husband's established residence is in another country the wife must follow him, even from a pleasant place to an inferior place and from a city to a large town, because it is as though she agreed when marrying him that she would follow him there. The same case and rule appear in Responsa Rivash, siman 177, but Rivash rules for the woman, and the Rama follows Rivash shortly afterward. I therefore do not know why the Beit Yosef quoted Tashbetz here, since it was already ruled above that a wife must go to her husband's country. If the intended novelty is that even Rabbeinu Tam, who disagrees and says the husband must follow the wife, would concede where he has another wife elsewhere, the source is unclear. Tashbetz ruled according to the other authorities, not according to Rabbeinu Tam.
