See Shach, seif katan 30, who writes that one who says, "I do not know whether I returned it to you," is exempt even if the other party then responds, "I know with certainty that you never repaid me," provided that the claimant himself did not previously know of the loan. Tumim is astonished at this. If one asserts an uncertain claim based on a witness and the defendant says, "I do not know whether I repaid you," the defendant is liable. Here, since the uncertain claim arose from the defendant's own statement, and an admission of a litigant is equivalent to one hundred witnesses, it should be like an uncertain claim supported by witnesses. Tumim suggests that because the borrower's uncertain statement preceded the claimant's definite assertion, the claimant's later certainty is weak, since after hearing the defendant say "I do not know," he may simply be asserting certainty. Tumim himself rejects this. He need not have brought a distant proof, because the issue is decided from this very law. The Shach agrees that if the claimant says, "I knew of the matter," and makes a definite claim both concerning the loan and concerning nonpayment, the defendant is liable, even though the defendant's uncertain statement preceded the claimant's definite one. Nevertheless, since the Shach and Taz agree on the ruling, it appears to be correct. The reason is as follows. The rule that one who says, "I do not know whether I repaid you," is liable rests on the principle that a definite claim is stronger than an uncertain claim, as Mishneh LaMelech and Tumim write, and as I wrote above. Where the defendant had no reason to know, he is exempt because his uncertainty is not a weak claim. Tumim, citing Tosafot, explains that the central factor is whether the uncertain claim is weak. One must explain why a weak uncertainty matters. Do we fine someone merely because he should have known but did not? Rather, an assertion of "I do not know" in circumstances where he should know is itself a weak claim, and we suspect that he is lying when he pleads uncertainty. If beit din knew with certainty that he truly did not know, he would be exempt, no worse than one who had no reason to know. The only reason for liability is that we suspect him of falsely claiming uncertainty. One cannot say that he should be believed that he does not know through a migo that he could have made a definite claim, because a person prefers to make an uncertain claim rather than a definite false claim, as Sema writes in seif katan 23. Nor is he believed by a migo that he could have said, "I do not know whether I ever became obligated to you," because he may fear that witnesses exist to the loan. Moreover, where his uncertain claim is intrinsically weak, an ordinary migo does not validate it, except where the very mouth that created the prohibition is the mouth that permitted it. Therefore, where no one had claimed against him, the lender did not know of the loan, and the defendant himself volunteered, "I do not know whether I repaid you," he is certainly believed that he genuinely does not know, through the migo that he could simply have remained silent. A migo based on the ability to remain silent is stronger than all ordinary forms of migo, as the Gemara explains in many places. The case is therefore as though we know with certainty that he truly does not know, and he is exempt, just as in a case where he had no reason to know. But if the lender says, "I knew of the loan and intended to claim it from you," we suspect the defendant of lying and evading payment. This also removes the difficulty from a bailee who says, "I do not know whether I returned the deposit," who is liable even though the depositor does not make a definite assertion that it was not returned, since perhaps it was lost through unavoidable circumstances and the only basis to know otherwise is the bailee's own admission. There, once the depositor has made a claim against him, we do not believe the bailee's statement that he does not know by a migo. His assertion of uncertainty can be believed only through the stronger migo that he could have remained silent, and that migo no longer exists once a claim has been made.
