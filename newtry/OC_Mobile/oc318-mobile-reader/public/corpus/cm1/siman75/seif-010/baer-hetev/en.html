I returned it. Even if the other party then responds, 'I know with certainty that you never repaid me, but I do not know whether I ever lent you,' the defendant is exempt. This follows from the authorities, who did not distinguish such a case, and it can also be proved from the Gemara in Bava Kamma, chapter HaGozel Batra. Nevertheless, it appears that he must take a hesset oath. This is unlike siman 88, seif 32, where, because the defendant claims with certainty, 'I repaid you,' he has a migo and is treated as one returning a lost item. Here, by contrast, he says, 'I do not know.' See what I wrote there further on this matter. Shach. The Taz writes likewise. See there.
