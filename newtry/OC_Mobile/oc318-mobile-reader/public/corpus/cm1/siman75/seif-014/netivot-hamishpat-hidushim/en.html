Where the liability is rabbinic. For example, land and debt instruments. Likewise, where even if he admitted the witness's statement the claimant still could not collect without an oath, as in the case of a worker whose hiring is proved by one witness and who claims nonpayment. The employer is believed through a migo that he could have denied the hiring, and he is not treated as one obligated to swear but unable to swear, because even with two witnesses the worker would still have to swear. He instead takes a serious oath that he paid. Similarly, where a shopkeeper claims that the defendant instructed him to pay the workers and one witness supports that fact, while the defendant says, "I do not know," the Shach holds that he is exempt by hesset, because even if he admitted the shopkeeper's claim, the shopkeeper could collect only with an oath.
