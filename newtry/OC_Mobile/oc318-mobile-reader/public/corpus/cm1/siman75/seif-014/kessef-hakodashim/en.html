(See Sema, seif katan 40.) At first glance, the reason for the Sema's statement that in a case of "fifty, I do not know" arising through gilgul shevuah everyone agrees that he is exempt from a Torah oath and takes only a shevuat heset would seem to be as follows. According to our ruling, we employ a migo to exempt from an oath. Here he has a migo, for he could have admitted the entire claim and paid everything, exactly as he must pay now, and he would then have been exempt from the Torah oath. So too now he should be exempt with only a shevuat heset. According to this, if he says, "I do not know," regarding the matter introduced through gilgul shevuah, according to the second opinion he would have to pay, because that opinion holds that one who is obligated to swear but cannot swear must pay even regarding gilgul shevuah, and a migo does not apply to a claim of "I do not know," as the Shach writes in seifim 9 and 10. See there. In truth, however, this is not so. Everyone exempts him from a Torah oath in such a case because there is no Torah oath here at all. He was never obligated in an oath, only in payment. This appears to me straightforward.
