Even to heirs. The Shach writes that if the claimant says with certainty, "You know that your father owed me a maneh," and the heir says, "Fifty I know and fifty I do not know," the heir incurs a Torah partial admission oath. This seems inconsistent with the Shach's own discussion of the verse, "The oath of Hashem shall be between the two of them and not between the heirs," and with Tosafot, which indicates that one does not take a Torah oath on a response of uncertainty. The Torah oath ordinarily requires a certain responsive denial. The correct distinction is this. If the claimant says, "The event occurred in your presence," and the heir says with certainty, "It did not occur in my presence," that is a definite contradiction and can create the Torah oath. But if the heir says, "I do not remember whether it occurred before me," that is an uncertain response. The Torah oath is not administered merely because the claimant says the heir should know. Where the heir's answer remains genuinely uncertain, the special exclusion of heirs applies and no Torah oath arises.
