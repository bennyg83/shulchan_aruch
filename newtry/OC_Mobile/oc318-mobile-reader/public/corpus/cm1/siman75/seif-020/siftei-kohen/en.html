Whether he sued the heirs. The Tur writes that there is no distinction between the debtor and his heirs except where the heir answers, “I know about fifty and do not know about fifty.” The Bedek HaBayit objects that this does not accord with those who hold that heirs need not swear to exempt themselves; and even according to those who require an oath, only the special heirs’ oath is required, not another oath. This objection is not difficult, because here the heir advances a definite claim, as in seif 16 above. The Sema explains it similarly.
It is further proved from siman 88, seif 20, that if the claimant says, “You know with certainty that your father owes me,” an heir who denies everything must take a heset oath that he does not know. Accordingly, if he admits part, where the claimant says, “You know with certainty that your father owed me one hundred,” and the heir responds, “I definitely know about fifty, but I do not know about the other fifty,” he must take a Torah oath as one who admits part. The claimant definitely asserts that the heir knows about the full hundred, and the heir contradicts him; therefore a Torah oath is required. Nevertheless, we do not say that he is one obligated to swear who cannot swear and must therefore pay. Since the heir says he does not know, he may swear that he does not know, because he was not expected to know. Nor is it difficult that a Torah oath generally cannot be taken with the answer “I do not know.” That rule applies where the matter was one he should have known. Here he swears definitely regarding the claimant’s allegation that he knows, declaring that allegation false. His ignorance concerns a matter he was not obligated to know.
