To his heirs. The Shach writes that from siman 88, seif 20, it follows that if the claimant says, 'You know with certainty that your father owed me,' then if the heir denies everything he must take a hesset oath that he does not know with certainty. Accordingly, if he admits part, saying, 'Regarding fifty I know with certainty, and regarding fifty I do not know,' he must take a Torah oath as a partial admitter. We do not apply the rule that one who cannot swear must pay, because an heir is not expected to know. One might ask that a Torah oath cannot be taken on an 'I do not know' response. The answer is that this limitation also applies where the person was expected to know. Here, however, the heir's lack of knowledge does not arise from the claim and he is under no duty to know his father's affairs. Shach.
