Because he is not believed in his words. The Shach writes that the principal rule is as follows. If liability emerges from his own words, for example, he claims, "I paid interest on your behalf," thinking this was permitted, and beit din sees from his statement that he is liable, beit din is equivalent to witnesses, and there is no testimony stronger than this. But if he is liable only because he is not believed in his words, for example, regarding part of the claim he is one who cannot swear and therefore must pay, this is not considered testimony of witnesses. If, however, he becomes liable for part because he says, "I do not know whether I repaid you," he must take a Torah oath regarding the remainder. Where the claimant swears and collects part, then after he has actually sworn on that part the defendant is deemed one who admits part regarding the remainder.
