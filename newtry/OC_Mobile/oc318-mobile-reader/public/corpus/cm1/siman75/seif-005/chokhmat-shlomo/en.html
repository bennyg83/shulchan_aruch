Seif 9: “You have a maneh of mine in your possession that I lent you,” etc., and “I do not know whether I repaid you or not,” he is obligated to pay, etc. Note: See Shach, seif katan 27, who writes that even where the uncertainty about repayment preceded the loan, he is obligated. The Urim VeTumim writes that this is compelled by the Gemara in the first chapter of Bava Metzia, which says that perhaps he has an old outstanding loan against him. I have heard that many questioned this proof, since it is Abaye who says this there, and Abaye follows his own view in the chapter Yesh Nochalin that even where one says, “I do not know whether I borrowed from you,” he is liable. I do not understand the objection. My principal proof was according to the halacha as we rule it, for otherwise one ruling would contradict another. We rule in accordance with the opinion that there may be an old outstanding loan, as stated in Shulchan Aruch siman 92, in the Sema there, and in siman 87; yet we also rule that where one says, “I do not know whether I borrowed from you,” he is exempt. The two rulings would conflict unless we say that where the uncertainty concerning repayment existed before the later loan, he is nevertheless liable. This is simple. One might instead say that Abaye obligates only where the claimant has a strong certain claim and the defendant has a weak uncertain claim, and that in the case of uncertain prior repayment he is likewise liable only in such a situation. But then the proof from the Gemara would still be lacking, because the concern for an old loan is only a possibility. One could say that the concern is specifically for an old loan regarding which the claimant has a strong certain claim and the other party a weak uncertain claim, in which case Abaye would obligate him. Indeed, perhaps this is why the Gemara specifically speaks of an “old loan.” The very fact that it is old and has not been demanded until now may itself strengthen the claimant’s certain assertion and weaken the other party’s uncertainty, similar to what I wrote in siman 61, where such a circumstance can even serve as evidence sufficient to defeat a document. Thus, if the case were merely an old loan with a strong certain claim and weak uncertainty, Abaye would simply be following his own view. It necessarily follows that the proof must be understood as I stated, namely according to our halachic ruling. Examine this carefully. This is what I answered to the distinguished scholar Rabbi Aharon Zev Wolf Frenkel of Krakow. See also the Urim VeTumim, Ostroh edition, where the glossator challenges the Shach’s ruling. His words have no substance. The Shach spoke only where the latter party received the money as a loan, or even seized it himself, rather than where his fellow voluntarily paid it to him specifically on account of an uncertain earlier loan. In the former situation, it resembles “I do not know whether I repaid you,” because he certainly became obligated through the later loan or through his seizure, and the only uncertainty is whether an earlier counterclaim existed against him. But where the borrower voluntarily pays him expressly on account of the uncertain earlier loan, there is certainly no case of “I do not know whether I repaid you.” Rather, it is “I do not know whether I was ever obligated,” and indeed it is even stronger than that, because he himself gave the money as payment of that uncertain original loan. In such a case the recipient is certainly fully exempt. This is clear and simple in my eyes.
