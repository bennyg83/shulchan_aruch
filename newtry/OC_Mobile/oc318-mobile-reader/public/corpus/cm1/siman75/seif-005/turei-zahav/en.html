(Seif 11) “I know with certainty that you do not owe me.” The Sema says the result would differ without the word “certainly.” On examination this is incorrect. The source is the Mordechai in the name of Maharam, derived from the case “You are not my brother” and from one who claims wheat while the defendant admits barley. Rashi explains that by claiming only wheat the claimant waived the barley. Thus “certainly” is not essential. The source’s phrase means that the statement “you do not owe me” is ordinarily made as a definite waiver. Even if he later says, “I remembered that you do owe me; I erred before,” he cannot retract, just as the wheat claimant cannot revive the barley claim after waiving it.
