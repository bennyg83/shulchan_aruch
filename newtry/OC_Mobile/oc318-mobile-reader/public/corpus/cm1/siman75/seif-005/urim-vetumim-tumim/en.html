Or one witness testifies that he robbed him. According to Rivash above in siman 34, if the litigant himself says the defendant robbed him and one witness supports him, the claimant can join with the witness for certain purposes of disqualification, because we divide the claimant's statement and do not treat his financial interest as destroying every aspect. If so, why may the defendant swear here after one witness says he robbed? The case must be where the claimant does not personally know the facts and sues only on the witness's report. Then the defendant has not been disqualified through the claimant's own testimony plus the witness. According to Rama above, who does not allow the interested claimant to join because he is personally affected, the question is easier. One witness alone does not disqualify the defendant from taking an oath.
