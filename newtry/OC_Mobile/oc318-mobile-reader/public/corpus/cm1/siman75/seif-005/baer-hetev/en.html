Became liable. The Shach writes that from the Sema in siman 87 it appears that he understood the distinction between these two rulings as follows. In the first case, the defendant's own words indicate that according to the calculation he still owes a certain amount. In the latter case, his liability results from the fact that we do not believe his assertion, or because beit din imposed liability upon him. An example is one who says, 'I paid interest on your behalf,' and beit din determines that the lender is not obligated to reimburse the interest because it was fixed interest, and similar cases. In my opinion, however, if he became liable by a ruling of beit din, even though he admitted nothing, he is obligated in a Torah oath. How could liability established by beit din be weaker than testimony of witnesses? Everywhere beit din has greater force than witnesses. The Shach brings proofs for this at length.
