One hundred belonging to your father was in my possession, etc., he is exempt, etc. The reason is that he is comparable to one returning a lost object, since the heir did not make a claim against him and did not know of the matter. One who returns a lost object is exempt from an oath, as the Tur and Mechaber state below in siman 267. The reason the formulation says "belonging to your father" should not be understood to mean that if he said the same thing to the claimant himself, the claimant could respond, "I knew of this and intended to demand the entire amount from you." In siman 88:7 the Tur distinguishes in a comparable case according to whether the defendant appears to have rushed forward strategically or not, and if that distinction were relevant here he should likewise have stated it. Rather, "your father" is merely the ordinary case, because a lender or depositor generally demands repayment of his own loan or deposit, whereas when his father has died the heir may not know of it. Tosafot write similarly according to their approach. Yet according to this explanation, in seif 10 below the text should also have said, "Your father lent me," etc., while there the Tur's language is, "You lent me," etc. See the Derishah.
