If he says, "I owe you one maneh," and the claimant responds, "You reminded me," he is likewise exempt even from hesset, because he is like one returning a lost object. The claimant admits that without the defendant's statement he would not have remembered. But if the claimant says, "I already knew about it and intended to sue you," the defendant must take hesset according to everyone. Regarding a Torah oath, the Sema distinguishes between one who acts deceptively and one who genuinely admits. The Shach holds that even where he acts deceptively there is reason to exempt him because the admission preceded the claim. If beit din cannot determine whether the admission was deceptive or genuine, he is certainly exempt.
