Shevuat heset. The Sema explains that he resembles one returning a lost item, who is exempt from an oath, as stated in siman 267. The wording "to your father" may merely reflect the ordinary case. If he made the same admission directly to his living creditor, the creditor could say that he knew of the admission and intended to claim the full amount, and siman 88 distinguishes whether the defendant preemptively admitted in a manipulative manner. The Sema therefore suggests that the text uses a father because ordinarily a lender or depositor knows and claims his own property, whereas an heir may not know what his father had. Tosafot likewise speak this way. The gloss notes that the Shach found the Sema's wording obscure and interpreted it differently, but the annotator suggests that the reference to seif 6 is simply a scribal error and should read seif 10, where the same linguistic issue arises.
