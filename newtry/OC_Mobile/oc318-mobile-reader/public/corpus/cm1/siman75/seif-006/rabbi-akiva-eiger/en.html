[Shach, in the continuation] Just as he could have claimed, “I paid you.” Note: In my humble opinion, the wording is awkward, for it implies that there too the reason he may compel the other party to swear is because of a migo, since he could have compelled an oath by claiming, “I paid.” See Beit Shmuel, Even HaEzer, siman 1, seif katan 40, that we do not employ a migo in order to impose an oath. In truth, this is not so. There, the Rama rules that even where the document contains a trustworthiness clause preventing him from compelling an oath concerning payment, he can still compel the creditor to swear regarding the claim, “You waived the debt for me.” As to the fundamental law, although that is itself a weak claim, the claimant can nevertheless compel an oath upon it.
