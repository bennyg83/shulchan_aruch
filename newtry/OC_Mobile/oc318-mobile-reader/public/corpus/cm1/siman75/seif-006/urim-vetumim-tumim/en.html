Shach writes that even if the doubtful repayment preceded the later definite seizure, the possessor cannot retain the later property because of the earlier doubt. For example, Shimon once held Reuven's deposit and negligently lost it, but does not know whether it was worth fifty or one hundred. Later Reuven's one hundred comes definitely into Shimon's hands. Shimon cannot retain the full hundred merely because of the earlier uncertainty. The second possession is definite, while the first monetary claim is doubtful. This is not merely inferred from the poskim. It follows from the Gemara's explanation why a person suspected of monetary wrongdoing is not automatically suspected of a false oath. Perhaps he thinks he has an old doubtful claim and therefore temporarily withholds money, yet people withdraw from a doubtful oath more than from doubtful money. If a doubtful old claim actually entitled him to retain the other person's property as a matter of law, there would be no wrongdoing at all and no reason to discuss his moral hesitation. The Gemara therefore assumes that a doubtful old claim does not justify definite retention of another's money. Accordingly, Shach's rule is correct.
