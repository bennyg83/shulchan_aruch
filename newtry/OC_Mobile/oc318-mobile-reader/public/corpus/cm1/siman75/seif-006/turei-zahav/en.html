(Seif 12) “Or the defendant says, ‘I do not know whether I paid.’” Many ask why one witness is needed, since one who admits the loan but is uncertain about payment must pay even without a witness. The Tur deliberately presents two clauses. In an ordinary case there is a distinction between “I do not know whether I borrowed,” which is exempt, and “I do not know whether I paid,” which is liable because the claimant’s certain assertion is stronger once the original debt is admitted. Where one witness supports the claimant, however, even uncertainty whether the loan occurred becomes a weak doubtful claim. The witness creates an oath obligation; because the defendant cannot swear, he pays. The Tur therefore says that with one witness there is no distinction between uncertainty about borrowing and uncertainty about payment. The latter category demonstrates that a certain claim can prevail even to extract money when the defendant’s uncertainty is especially weak; the witness gives the former uncertainty the same weakness.
