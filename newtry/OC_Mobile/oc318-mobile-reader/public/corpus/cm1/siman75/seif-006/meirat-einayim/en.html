And if he says, "Here it is," he is exempt, etc. Since he has given the claimant what he admits owing, with respect to the remainder he is one who denies the entire claim. I have already explained why one who denies an entire claim is not liable for a Torah oath: there is a presumption that a person does not brazenly deny everything. The Sages nevertheless enacted that he take a lighter oath, without holding a sacred object, accepting upon himself that he owes the claimant nothing. This oath is called in the Gemara and the poskim an oath of hesset. The term derives from the sense of placing or imposing, because the Sages imposed this oath upon him. See the Perishah for what I wrote further concerning this.
