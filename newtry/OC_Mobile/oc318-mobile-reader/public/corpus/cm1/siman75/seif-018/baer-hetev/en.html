Responsa Maharashdam, siman 1, cites a sage who ruled this way, but Maharashdam disagrees and rules that where there is a written instrument and both parties are uncertain, the borrower must pay. He brings proof from siman 59. There was no need for his strained proof, since this is explicit in siman 82 at the end of seif 2. With an oral loan he need not pay, although one who wishes to satisfy Heaven must do so. Maharashdam brings proof from seif 17 above, which speaks of an obligation to satisfy Heaven where the defendant does not admit the claim. He infers that if the defendant also says 'perhaps,' he is obligated in order to satisfy Heaven. The Shach writes that this is no proof. The first clause discusses a defendant who makes a definite denial, where it is obvious that according to all opinions he has no obligation even to satisfy Heaven. The text then explains that when both parties are uncertain there is a dispute, as the Tur and Shulchan Aruch write in this seif. Shach.
