(Shach, seif katan 82) The incident there concerned one who sued his fellow, etc. Note: I do not understand what grounds for suspicion existed in that incident. The intention of Rabbeinu Baruch appears to be that he distinguishes between a case where the claimant should himself know whether the defendant owes him. If he admits that he himself does not know and only heard it from another, he cannot impose an oath, because if the report were true he should himself know it as well. By contrast, in the incident there, where others told him that property stolen from him had come into the defendant's possession without his knowledge, he had no reason to know the facts personally, and it is therefore reasonable for him to be uncertain.
