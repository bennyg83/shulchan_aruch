And the defendant answered, etc.; we tell the lender, etc. She’erit Yosef asks from the end of siman 87, seif 31, where one claims a debt evidenced by a kinyan or a lost note, the defendant says he paid, takes an oath, and afterward witnesses or the note appear. There the note is collected even after the oath. Why, then, must it be nullified before the oath here? He answers that here the claimant did not initially say that a note existed, but mentioned it only after the defendant spoke, creating suspicion that he may fabricate something and cast aspersion on the oath. In siman 87, by contrast, he claimed from the outset that a note had existed and was lost. This explanation does not appear correct. In both places, if the defendant says that he will not swear until the claimant nullifies the note, the note must be nullified; otherwise he need not swear. Siman 87 speaks of a borrower who swore voluntarily without requesting nullification. The Sema and Bach explain it this way there, and this is the principal ruling. See also my comments at the beginning of siman 82 and in siman 87.
And afterward you will produce the paid note, etc. Even though production of the note would not necessarily prove that the defendant swore falsely, because the note may still exist although it was paid, and the statement “I have nothing of yours” may mean “I repaid you,” as explained in siman 79, seif 10, nevertheless the defendant may say: “I will not swear while your note remains capable of creating a needless oath.” Although the note may ultimately be collectible, the oath itself could prove unnecessary. This is comparable to siman 72, seif 15, where Rabbeinu Chananel explains that an oath is avoided so that it not be taken in vain. The Maggid Mishneh and Baal HaTerumot, gate 24, write similarly.
Or I had a note and it was lost, etc. Gedulei Terumah asks, according to Baal HaTerumot’s view in siman 65, seif 16, that if the borrower later proves he already paid he is exempt, why must the lender nullify the note? This is no difficulty. There, another person found the note and returned it improperly. Here we must be concerned that the lender himself will find it in his house, or that he falsely claims it was lost while it is actually in his possession. Gedulei Terumah further raises an objection from siman 20, but his discussion does not appear well founded and need not be prolonged.
