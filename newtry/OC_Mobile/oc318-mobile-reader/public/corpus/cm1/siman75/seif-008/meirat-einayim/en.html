I'm exaggerating. “If he was taken from the interest rate and examined by Dr. C. X.C. and C. C. C. C. C. and C. C.C., he would be divided into the interest of whether he claims to take the permit and whether we have given him or later:
We administer an oath to him, etc. Although the claimant suspects him of robbery, and one witness has even testified that he robbed him, as the Mechaber concludes, he is not disqualified by the testimony of one witness. We say that perhaps he took the money because of an old loan that he is uncertain the claimant still owes him, and that if, after reviewing his accounts, he discovers that the claimant owes him nothing, he intends to return what he took. For this reason the Sages imposed an oath upon him, because he certainly will not swear on the basis of a doubt when he may later discover that the claimant owed him nothing and that his oath was false. The students of the Rashba explicitly give this reason, and this rationale will likewise be explained in siman 92 in the words of the Tur and Mechaber. This is unlike the reason stated by the Ir Shushan here and in siman 87:1, that a person may be suspected regarding money without being suspected regarding an oath. See there. I later saw that in siman 92:3 the Ir Shushan combines the two reasons, but this does not appear correct to me. In any event, if his view here and in siman 87 is as stated, he should not have written without qualification. He should have explained that only one who is suspected regarding doubtful money is not thereby suspected regarding an oath, whereas one certainly guilty concerning money is different, as stated in siman 92. All this applies specifically where one witness says that he robbed. With two witnesses, he cannot escape by such a claim, as stated in siman 92. See there.
