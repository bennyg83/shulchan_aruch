(Seif 14) “But if he is exempt by Torah law.” The Tur, citing the Ramah, concludes that where two witnesses would impose payment only after the claimant’s oath, a single witness does not generate a Torah oath and the rule “unable to swear, must pay” does not apply. The Sema cites this, and it is surprising that the Mechaber omitted an undisputed rule. Siman 90, seif 3, concerning one witness who saw a person enter another’s house to seize property, supports it. The Rashba may disagree where he treats the pledge-holder as obligated through one witness, but that depends on whether two witnesses would themselves shift possession. In any event, doubtful seizure cannot ordinarily extract money. The Mordechai further rules that a purchaser holding land with a deed need not swear when the seller later claims a resale within three years; had there been a resale, the original deed would normally have been returned. Heset was enacted for common oral claims, not for an implausible land transaction that ordinarily generates a document. We learn that heset is not imposed on an inherently uncommon claim.
