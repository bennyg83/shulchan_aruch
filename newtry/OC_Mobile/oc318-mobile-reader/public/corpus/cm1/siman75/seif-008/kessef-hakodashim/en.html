And I do not know whether I returned it to you. Urim VeTumim writes that in an ordinary commercial transaction we do not apply the rule of 'I do not know whether I repaid you,' even if the buyer took the merchandise and later became uncertain whether payment was made. Perhaps this applies only while they are still engaged in the same transaction, as though the merchandise has not fully left the seller's control. Once they have separated, the buyer is under a presumption of obligation. If he did not physically take the goods and they remain in the seller's possession, even if title passed, perhaps there is no fixed presumption of debt, because if the seller refuses to deliver he need not pay. Still, the rule that each party collects from the other inclines toward treating it as an established obligation. If, when taking the goods, he stated that payment would be through a debt owed him by the seller's partner and the seller remained silent, the seller cannot later retract even without ma'amad sheloshtan. He delivered the goods only on that condition, and waiver requires no kinyan, so the monetary obligation was extinguished.
