Shach writes that even if the lender later says definitely, "I know you did not repay me," while the borrower says only, "I do not know whether I borrowed, but I certainly did not repay," the borrower remains exempt. Shach infers this from the silence of the poskim and from Bava Kamma 118. I do not agree. If a claimant may make a definite claim based on one witness, then certainly he can make a definite claim based on the defendant's own admission, which is stronger than one hundred witnesses. The borrower admits facts from which the lender can say with certainty: "According to your own words, you once owed me, and I know you never repaid." The resulting structure is a definite claim against a doubtful defense. The Gemara in Bava Metzia similarly treats the lender's claim as definite when it rests on the borrower's own admissions concerning the collateral. Shach's proof from Bava Kamma does not work. The Gemara asks why the uncertain-loan clause is exempt according to the view that definite claim defeats doubt. It could not simply establish the first clause as a case where the lender later remembered nonpayment, because then the second clause could likewise involve a lender who later remembered that the original loan definitely occurred. The two clauses must be read symmetrically. Perhaps Shach thought the lender's later certainty is weak because he first heard the borrower express uncertainty and can now safely assert a claim. But the borrower's doubt is itself poor: a debtor should remember whether he repaid, and he may still later remember. The lender therefore does not have the kind of riskless certainty that would undermine the claim. Accordingly, where the lender makes a definite nonpayment claim grounded in the borrower's own admission that a debt existed, the borrower who says "I do not know whether I repaid" must pay. Taz also reads the law as involving a later definite lender claim, but his suggestion that without such a claim the debtor would not even have a heavenly obligation is difficult. In ordinary doubtful repayment, the poskim generally require satisfying the obligation at least to fulfill one's duty toward Heaven. The later seif concerning doubtful original loan is different.
