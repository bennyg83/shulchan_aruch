Because he can say, "I was jesting with you." Shach asks: the defense of jesting is not pleaded by beit din unless the defendant himself raises it. Why then is the defendant exempt from an oath here? Shach distinguishes between a claimant asserting an independent definite debt and one relying only on the defendant's prior admission. There is an even simpler answer. If the defendant admits making the statement and simply fails to explain it, beit din does not invent "I was jesting" for him. But here he denies ever making the admission at all. We cannot impose an oath on his denial as though the prior admission were already a definite monetary obligation, because even if he later admitted making the words, he might still have the legal defense that he was jesting. The present denial therefore is not yet a clear denial of money. Without a definite underlying monetary claim, no oath arises. This is likely Shach's underlying intent, though his wording elsewhere requires qualification.
