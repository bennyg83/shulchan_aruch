Rather, he places a general ban. See Responsa Rishmei She'eilah, end of siman 49, which writes that this applies specifically here because it is still possible for the heir to discover the facts. Generally, however, where the defendant could not have been expected to know, just as he is not required to swear, so too the claimant cannot impose a ban upon him. This explains why in siman 93, seif 13, and siman 8, seif 1, the Shulchan Aruch does not say that a general ban is imposed. There is also something of a proof from siman 158, seif 1, where the Shulchan Aruch does not say that after assessment at the lowest estimate the builder should impose a ban upon the surrounded party who does not believe his statement of expenses. Rather, since we are witnesses, so to speak, that he could not have known, why should he be made to accept a needless ban? See there.
