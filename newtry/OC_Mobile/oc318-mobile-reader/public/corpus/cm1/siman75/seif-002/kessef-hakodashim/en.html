Seif 2. Some say that this exempts him. A person bought a crate on condition that it contained a specified quantity of merchandise, transported it elsewhere, and upon opening found less than the agreed quantity. He produced testimony to that effect. Where the goods are window glass and the crate bears recognizable signs showing that it was not opened during transport, such visual recognition can support exemption from paying for the missing amount, just as lost property may be returned through reliable visual identification. If there is no formal testimony and the buyer himself says that by visual recognition he knows nothing was stolen en route, while at the place of manufacture an appointed worker packed the glass and is the person whose act creates the factual uncertainty, the buyer need swear only that he himself did not take any glass and that he recognizes that no one else opened and removed goods from the crate. If the first buyer purchased on condition that the crate contain a fixed quantity and immediately resold it to a second buyer, who opened it elsewhere, the second buyer is financially interested and therefore cannot testify to exempt the first. Nevertheless, the first buyer need not swear that he does not know whether he became liable for the full quantity, because the original seller knows that the first buyer himself lacks that knowledge. This is not comparable to the counterfeit coin case at the end of siman 75. The buyer never entered an unconditional established debt; he bought only on condition that the crate contain the stated quantity. Nor is this a case of one who is obligated to swear but cannot swear and therefore pays. Even if a single witness would otherwise create an oath and even if the buyer might have been expected to know, the rule does not apply where the claimant knows that the defendant in fact does not know. Here, moreover, he had no reason to know because he immediately resold the closed crate in the seller's presence for transport and opening elsewhere. If before coming to beit din he already said, "here is payment for the glass that was actually in the crate," he is plainly exempt as to the balance. Suppose the first buyer sold to someone who would transport the goods elsewhere, a third person became an unconditional guarantor, and that guarantor executed a promissory note for the entire stated sum. Since the note was given only as security and was conditioned on the crate containing the stated quantity, if the original seller claimed personal knowledge that the full quantity was packed, he could in principle swear against one contrary witness and collect, assuming the note has the formal status of a document collectible from encumbered property. The second buyer is interested and cannot serve as a disinterested witness. But where the seller himself knows only from the testimony of the worker who packed the glass, even if that worker is not interested, the defendant would have been able to swear and defeat the note had he possessed personal knowledge. Where he does not know and the seller knows that he does not know, no Torah oath is imposed merely because the note exists. One might argue that the note makes the case resemble "I do not know whether I repaid," because the written obligation stands until the shortage is proved. That is not certain, since perhaps the note was given only because the seller trusted the packer, so with respect to the uncertain condition it resembles a document of trust rather than a final debt. Even if it is treated as a case of uncertain payment, one witness can support the defendant. There is then a witness on each side and the defendant's possession of the money stands against the presumptive force of the written obligation, leaving him exempt. If the promissory note is payable to bearer, beit din may give the defendant a written record that the claimant admitted that the note arose from a conditional sale requiring the specified quantity and that he cannot presently collect because one witness establishes the shortage. A migo of the claimant does not permit extraction of money against the opposing witness, all the more where the claimant himself knows the facts only from the packer. Thus the defendant is exempt.
