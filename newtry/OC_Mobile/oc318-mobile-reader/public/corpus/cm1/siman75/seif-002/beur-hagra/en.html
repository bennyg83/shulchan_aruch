Some say that it exempts him. As stated in Shevuot 41a: 'for every iniquity,' etc., but one witness establishes liability for an oath. Just as two witnesses establish monetary liability, whether to impose payment or to exempt, so too one witness does regarding an oath. There is also an a fortiori argument from the greater power, etc. Another proof is Bava Metzia 4a, where witnesses impose an oath by an a fortiori argument from one's own admission and one witness. Were this not so, one could object that one's own admission and one witness cannot exempt from an oath. A further proof is the statement there that a document supports him; all the more so when one witness supports him.
(Anthology) Some say, etc. This is also ruled in siman 87:6.
And some say that it does not exempt him. This follows Bava Metzia 35b concerning one who rents a cow, etc. If the first opinion were correct, the borrower supports the renter, so why must the renter swear? It is also derived from Shevuot 45a, where Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel says, 'If there are witnesses,' implying that one witness does not suffice. Likewise, one who received a deposit under a document must return it with witnesses. Rabbeinu Tam explains there, s.v. 'under a document,' that this means he cannot be exempted by an oath, implying that one witness would not exempt him. Semag cites proof from the Yerushalmi: when is an unpaid guardian liable for an oath? When there are no witnesses, etc.
