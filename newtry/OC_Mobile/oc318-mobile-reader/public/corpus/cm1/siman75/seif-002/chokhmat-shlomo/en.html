Seif 2: if there is one witness supporting him, some say that witness exempts him from an oath. Regarding whether a supporting witness exempts from an oath of hesset, see Baal HaTerumot, gate 21, who brings a proof that he does not, and Giddulei Terumah who questions that proof. I discussed the matter in the addenda to my Nedarim Zerizin, part 2, in a responsum to the community of Yanov in Poland. I saw in Responsa Brit Avraham a question why a statistical majority does not at least impose an oath, since a majority is stronger than one witness in certain laws, such as lost property. If one witness obligates an oath, why should a majority not do so all the more? In another responsum I answered that one witness has the special quality that his testimony can combine with another witness, but there is a more fundamental answer. In monetary law we generally do not extract money on the basis of majority alone, whereas in prohibitory law we do follow the majority. Therefore a majority cannot directly compel payment. But neither can we readily impose an oath, because from the standpoint of prohibitory law the majority already establishes that the contrary oath would likely be false. We cannot say that the defendant must pay because he is unable to swear, since he makes a definite claim and is prepared to swear. Nor can we simply enact that the claimant swear and collect. In the case of a person who made himself suspect regarding oaths, the Sages shifted the oath because his own misconduct caused the problem. Here the defendant did nothing wrong. Why should the Sages create a new rule to his detriment when the underlying monetary law leaves the money with him? This seems clear and correct in my opinion.
