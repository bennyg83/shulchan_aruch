And if he married a woman in another land, she is compelled to go with him. Maharam of Rothenburg, in his explanation of the Yerushalmi cited by the Beit Yosef, writes that since the marriage occurred in Galil she cannot be compelled to follow him to Yehudah. But the Tur cites Maharam as saying that if he is from Yehudah and betrothed her in Galil, even if he married her not in Galil but in Ever HaYarden, she must follow him to Yehudah, which implies all the more so if the marriage itself occurred in Galil. Darkei Moshe emends the Tur to read, "even if he did not marry her in Yehudah." On that reading Maharam disputes Rambam and Rif, who hold that if a man marries a woman in another country she must follow him. The Bach labors to reconcile them by distinguishing whether he wishes to return home immediately after the wedding or remained for some time in her place. The Ran writes that she is compelled to leave, meaning even though he is presently standing in Galil. For practice there is no need to lengthen the matter. We should rule according to the plain language of Rif, Rambam, Ran, Maggid Mishneh, and Rivash.
