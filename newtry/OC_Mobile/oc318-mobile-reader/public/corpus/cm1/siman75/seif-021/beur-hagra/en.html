One who says to his fellow, 'I was told,' etc., some say he is exempt. As stated there, since he did not say, 'I know with certainty.' Even saying, 'I was told,' is not a definite claim, as stated in Bava Batra 135a concerning one who says, 'You owe me a maneh,' for that case is different because it is equivalent to a direct monetary claim.
And some say, etc. They explain the passage in Shevuot 47a as consistent with both the Sages and Rabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov, and maintain that their dispute concerns only one who says, 'My father told me.' The Yerushalmi in Gittin states that where one says, 'You owe my father a maneh,' and the defendant admits fifty, he is exempt as one returning a lost object, but if the claimant says, 'I stood beside my father when you admitted,' the enactment does not apply. This discussion follows the Sages. According to this reading, Rif's proof is not decisive. Sefer HaTerumot cites Raavad that the halachah follows the Sages and also adduces the Yerushalmi. Maggid Mishneh writes that Rambam's ruling is correct either way, while Raavad identifies it as the dispute of Rabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov and the Sages and says Rambam ruled like the former.
