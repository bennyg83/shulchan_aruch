Above, siman 72, the Mechaber rules without qualification like the opinion that beit din need not ask the defendant to quantify the amount, while here a dissenting position appears. The cases can be distinguished. Here the defendant may have received less than one perutah. He nevertheless admits that some of his fellow's money is in his hands because he cannot honestly deny that it is another person's property, and even less than a full measure has a prohibition, as explained below. He can therefore say, "I owe him something, but it may not be worth a perutah." With a pledge, by contrast, where one dinar was lent and the defendant says, "I know the pledge was worth more than a dinar but I do not know how much more," it is not reasonable to say that the excess was positive but less than a perutah. Valuation of an object cannot be exact to that degree. Saying that it was "worth one dinar" already means approximately one dinar, because precise valuation is impossible, as the Gemara states. Therefore, when he admits that it was worth more, his admission necessarily refers to at least a perutah of excess. This is why the Mechaber there could rule without requiring further questioning. The Shach treated the two cases as identical, but in my view they are distinguishable.
