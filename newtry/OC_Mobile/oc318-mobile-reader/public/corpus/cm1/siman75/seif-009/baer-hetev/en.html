He must pay. The reason is that his fellow claims with certainty, while he himself is not certain that he is not liable. So writes the Sema. The Rivash, siman 392, writes in the name of Ramban that if the claimant seized property in the presence of witnesses, we remove it from him, because seizure does not help where his only right is an obligation to pay in order to satisfy Heaven. The reference to seizure before witnesses is necessary only because there the claimant himself asserts certainty. The same applies in seif 10, where one says on his own, 'I robbed you and do not know whether I returned it,' and in siman 76, seif 1, and similar cases where he is obligated only to satisfy Heaven. There seizure is ineffective even without witnesses, since the claimant himself does not assert certainty. Shach.

Liable. It appears that this is so even where the doubt whether payment occurred arose before the loan. For example, the creditor had earlier held a deposit belonging to the borrower, and after it was lost through the creditor's negligence, the borrower lent him one hundred gold coins. The custodian says, 'I know the deposit was worth only fifty,' while the depositor, who is now the borrower, says, 'I do not know what it was worth, and perhaps it was worth one hundred. If so, I was never indebted to you at all, because the one hundred you lent me was offset by the one hundred owed for my deposit.' Even so, the borrower must pay fifty, because the obligation is certain and the exemption is doubtful. In such a case, a definite claim prevails over a doubtful one. This is also implied by the unqualified language of the authorities. Shach.
