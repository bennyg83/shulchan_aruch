And one witness, etc. The Shach writes that although the defendant has a migo that he could have said, "I paid you," once he says, "I do not know," he is a person obligated to swear who cannot swear and therefore must pay. This still requires explanation. If, had he contradicted the witness, he would have incurred a Torah oath, then inability to swear can produce payment. But according to those who say that a migo can exempt even from an oath, why should he have had a Torah oath at all? If he had said, "I never borrowed," he could claim the migo that he had paid and thereby avoid the oath. The answer must be that just as in monetary liability we do not say migo against two witnesses, with respect to an oath we do not say migo against one witness. The witness is the Torah's direct basis for the oath, and a hypothetical alternative claim cannot displace that testimony. See what I wrote in the general rules of migo.
