I have property of your father in my hands, etc. The general rule is similar if one says, 'I owe you one hundred and have already paid fifty,' and the other responds, 'I had forgotten the entire matter, but now that you reminded me, you did not repay me at all.' The first person is still treated as one returning a lost claim and is exempt even from hesset. There is no issue of appearing evasive, because the claimant himself admits that without the other's reminder he remembered nothing. But if he says, 'I knew about the debt and merely had not yet chosen to sue you, and you paid me nothing,' then a hesset oath is required according to everyone. Regarding a Torah oath, Sema says beit din evaluates whether the admission was strategically made before the claim. If it was evasive, the Torah oath may apply. Shach holds that even a calculated prior admission remains exempt from a Torah oath because the admission preceded the claim.
