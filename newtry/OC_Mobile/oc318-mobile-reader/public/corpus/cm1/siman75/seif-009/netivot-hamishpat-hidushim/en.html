To satisfy the laws of Heaven. If he seized without witnesses, he is believed through a migo that he could have denied the seizure entirely, and he may claim that the other party owes him. But if he seized in the presence of witnesses, the property is removed from him even though the other party is obligated toward him under the laws of Heaven:
He is obligated to pay. Doubt regarding payment does not remove a definite obligation. This applies even where the doubtful payment preceded the loan, such as when the debtor had a deposit belonging to the lender, it was lost before the loan, and he is uncertain of its value. Nevertheless, he must pay. See the Beurim. Where the matter was not one that he should have known, even if he claims, "I do not know whether I repaid you," he is exempt, unless his lack of knowledge resulted from his own negligence. One who paid another and later found a counterfeit coin is treated as one who says, "I do not know whether I ever became obligated to you," since he had already been discharged through an act of payment:
And the claimant need not. For no oath is administered on a doubtful claim:
And if he later retracted. A person may forget and subsequently remember, so he is believed even if he has no migo. The same applies wherever he became liable because he said that he did not know, such as where he says, "I know about fifty," and similar cases: he may later claim that he remembered. But if one witness testifies against him and he says that he does not know, making him one who cannot swear and therefore must pay, he cannot later claim that he remembered. See the Beurim:
