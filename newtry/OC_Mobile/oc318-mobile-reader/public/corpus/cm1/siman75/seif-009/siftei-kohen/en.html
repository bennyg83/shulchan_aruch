That I lent you, etc. The same applies if he says, “You robbed me.” There is no distinction among robbery, deposit, and loan regarding these laws. See siman 365.
To fulfill his obligation before Heaven, he shall pay, etc. The Rivash, siman 392, writes in the name of the Ramban that if the claimant seized property before witnesses, it is removed from him, because seizure does not help where the claimant has no enforceable right and payment is required only to satisfy the obligation before Heaven. In my opinion, the Rivash specifies seizure before witnesses because there the claimant asserts certainty. The same rule applies in seif 10, where one says voluntarily, “I robbed you, but I do not know whether I returned it,” and in similar cases. Since the claimant does not assert certainty, seizure is ineffective even if not witnessed.
He is obligated to pay. Since the debt is certain and repayment is doubtful, the certain claim prevails over the doubtful defense. It appears that this applies even when the doubt concerning payment preceded the loan. For example, a deposit was lost through the custodian's negligence, and afterward the owner lent him one hundred gold coins. The custodian says, “I know that the deposit was worth only fifty,” while the lender says, “I do not know its value; perhaps it was worth one hundred, in which case the loan was entirely offset by the deposit.” Even so, the borrower must pay fifty, because the obligation is certain and the exemption is doubtful. This follows from the unqualified language of the authorities.
And the prosecutor should not swear. It is said that if he does not swear to him, he will say that it is not the case of Hashem
And if he later returns, etc., he is believed upon oath. Maharshal writes that this ruling applies only according to the Rif, who holds that when one says, “I do not know whether I repaid you,” the claimant must swear. The defendant has then not yet become absolutely liable, because perhaps the claimant will not swear. According to our ruling that the claimant collects without an oath, however, the defendant cannot retract. Although this is not a classic retraction, since at first he was uncertain whether he repaid and later remembered, once the law required immediate payment without an oath there is no greater retraction than this. See siman 79, seif 9, and my comments there.
