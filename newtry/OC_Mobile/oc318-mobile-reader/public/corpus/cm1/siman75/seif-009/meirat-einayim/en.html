To fulfill his obligation toward Heaven he should pay, since his fellow makes a definite claim and he cannot state with certainty that he does not owe him.He later claimed, 'I do not know whether you lent me.' The Tur explains that he says this in order to lighten his oath and exempt himself with an oath that he does not know.
Such an oath is easier in his eyes, because even if he truly owes the money, he is not swearing falsely if he genuinely does not know.
Moreover, even if the claimant later produces witnesses that he was liable, he was truthful in swearing only to his lack of knowledge.
Nevertheless, once he originally made a definite denial or another claim inconsistent with this later uncertainty, he cannot necessarily retract into an 'I do not know' claim merely to improve his oath position.
The effect depends on whether the new claim contradicts the substance of his first statement or merely explains it.
