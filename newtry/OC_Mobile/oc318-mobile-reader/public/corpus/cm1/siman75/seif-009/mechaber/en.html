If one claims, 'You have one hundred of mine in your possession, which I lent you or deposited with you,' and the other says, 'I do not know whether you lent me anything or deposited anything with me,' he takes a heses oath that he does not know and is exempt. If he wishes to fulfill his obligation toward Heaven, he pays. This applies even if he first made the definite claim, 'I have nothing of yours,' and, after he was required to take a heses oath, changed his claim and said, 'I do not know whether you lent me anything or deposited anything with me.' He takes a heses oath that he does not know and is exempt. If one claims, 'You have one hundred of mine in your possession, which I lent you or deposited with you,' and the other says, 'I know that you lent me one hundred or deposited it with me, but I do not know whether I returned it,' he must pay, and the claimant need not swear, even a heses oath. The defendant may, however, impose an unspecified ban upon anyone who takes his money unlawfully. {Rama: If he afterward says, 'I remembered that I paid you,' he is believed upon taking an oath.}
