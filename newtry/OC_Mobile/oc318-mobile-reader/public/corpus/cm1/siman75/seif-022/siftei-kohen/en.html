There is one who says that we require him to take a heset oath. The Sema explains that this refers to one who denies everything. Consequently, one who admits part must take a Torah oath, because this opinion follows the view in the preceding seif that requires a Torah oath when the claimant says, “My father told me,” and the same applies where he says that he found the debt recorded in his father’s ledger. This is not compelled. These authorities may follow Rabbeinu Baruch in the Mordechai, Shevuat HaDayanim, cited below in seif katan 82. Baal HaTerumot, gate 38, also implies this, for he does not place the words of Rav Hai Gaon after those of Raavad. Although the Tur cites Rav Hai after Raavad, his purpose is only to say that Rav Hai agrees with Raavad regarding the obligation of a heset oath.
