He said, 'I found,' etc., and there is one who says, etc. This opinion follows Raavad in the preceding seif. Sefer HaTerumot, responsa of Rav Hai, and Tur likewise discuss one who says that his ancestor told him, or that he found an entry in his ledger, that the defendant owes him. Raavad disputes this. (Anthology) He said, etc. This accords with the later opinion in the preceding seif. According to the first opinion he is certainly exempt. Torat HaBayit states explicitly that even a written entry in the father's ledger is not a definite claim. Rabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov's case is where the claimant stood beside his father when the admission was made, as stated in the Yerushalmi. Even testimony from many persons does not itself transform the heir's claim into a definite personal claim.
