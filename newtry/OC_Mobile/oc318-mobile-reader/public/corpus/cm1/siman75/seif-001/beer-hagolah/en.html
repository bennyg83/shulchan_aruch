The wording of the Tur in the name of the Rambam at the beginning of chapter 6 of Hilkhot To'en VeNit'an. The Maggid Mishneh writes that these matters are self-evident and have support in the Gemara from the statement that when one administers an oath, he says, "not according to your understanding," etc., Shevuot 29a, and from the Mishnah in the chapter Zeh Borer, "How do you know that this person owes him?" etc., Sanhedrin 29a. The concern there is that witnesses may mistakenly think the defendant owes money based upon what they saw or knew, when legally he does not. All the more so must we be concerned regarding the litigants themselves, since a person is close to himself.
If the defendant demands that the claimant clarify his words and the claimant refuses, no oath should be imposed upon the defendant, because shevuat heset is a rabbinic enactment and is administered only upon a clearly articulated claim. Responsa Rosh, rule 71, siman 5, from siman 61.
The Nimukei Yosef writes in the chapter HaMokher Et HaBayit that we do not scrutinize a person's words against him unless he actually stated them precisely; ordinarily we say that he spoke casually or foolishly. Sema.
