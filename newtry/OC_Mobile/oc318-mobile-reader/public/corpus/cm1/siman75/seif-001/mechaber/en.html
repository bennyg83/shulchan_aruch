Whether the lender and borrower must clarify their claims, the laws of those obligated in Torah or rabbinic oaths, one who cannot swear, and definite and doubtful claims. This siman contains 25 seifim.
When a claimant says to his fellow in beit din, "You have one hundred of mine," beit din tells him to clarify his words and explain the basis of the obligation: whether he lent him the money, deposited it with him, or whether the defendant damaged his property. This is because the claimant may think the defendant owes him when in fact he does not. Likewise, if the defendant answers, "You have nothing in my possession," or "I owe you nothing," he must clarify his response, because perhaps he is mistaken in thinking that he is not liable when he actually is. Even if he is a great scholar, they tell him, "You lose nothing by answering the claim and explaining why you do not owe him." Rama: If he refuses to clarify, then if the judge perceives deceit in the matter he may lose his case. If there is no indication of deceit, but it appears to the judge that he simply cannot clarify his position or the like, he is not held liable merely because he did not clarify it. The instruction to clarify one's words exists only to enable the court to establish the true law because deceivers are common. See also what was written in siman 72, seif 17.
