Clarify your words. It is forbidden to claim two hundred from another when he owes only one hundred, in order to obligate him in an oath and then impose upon him, by extension, an oath concerning another matter. Likewise, the defendant may not say: if I admit part I will become liable to an oath, so I will deny everything and privately give him the portion I truly owe. Similarly, if one owes three people, one may not act as claimant while the other two testify, as it is stated, "Distance yourself from a false matter." Gemara, Shach:
You deposited with him. If the defendant is holding property on account of another matter, we do not say that it should first be deposited with beit din; rather, that other matter is adjudicated first:
And likewise the defendant. This is when the claimant clarifies his claim. If the claimant does not clarify it, the defendant also need not clarify his response:
He must clarify. We do not scrutinize a person's words so as to use them against him unless he actually said the matter. We say that he may have spoken foolishly. Sema, citing Nimukei Yosef to Bava Batra 70:
And if it appears. If it does not appear to beit din that there is deceit, he is exempt from an oath on that account, though a Torah oath is administered. Shach:
