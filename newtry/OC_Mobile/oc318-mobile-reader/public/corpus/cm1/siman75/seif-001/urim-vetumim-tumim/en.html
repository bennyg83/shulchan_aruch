And witnesses testify. Below in siman 87, Sema and Shach dispute what kind of testimony R. Chiyya's rule requires. Sema says the witnesses must know the fifty remain unpaid, for example because they never lost sight of the parties or because the debt is still within its term. If they testify only that fifty were lent and the defendant says, "I never borrowed," then although his denial legally includes "I never repaid," the witnesses do not know whether repayment later occurred. Sema therefore does not treat this as the classic case of witnesses imposing a partial-admission oath. Shach cites Ran, Nimukei Yosef, and Ritva against him. They explain that the witnesses need know only of the loan. The defendant's own statement, "I never borrowed," is legally an admission that he never paid. The witnesses and his admission together establish the unpaid fifty. Those authorities are explicit, but there is still room for doubt. They themselves acknowledge that the Gemara's language sounds as though the witnesses know positively that fifty are still owed. Otherwise it would more naturally say, "witnesses testify that he lent him fifty." They depart from that plain reading because they are troubled by the rule that a claim secured by land does not generate the ordinary partial-admission oath. If witnesses establish an unpaid loan, the debt may carry a land lien. Their answer is that the witnesses alone do not establish nonpayment. Only the debtor's own admission does, and his admission cannot prejudice purchasers. Therefore the claim is not treated as one secured by land. This follows Ramban's view that a partial admission not collectible from purchasers is not classified as a land-lien claim. Rosh disagrees with Ramban. According to Rosh, even where purchasers are protected because of possible collusion, the underlying debt can still be a land-lien obligation for oath classification. Tosafot similarly avoid the problem by establishing the case where the debtor never owned land or expressly waived the lien. According to Rosh, there is therefore no need to force the Gemara away from its simple wording. We can say the witnesses themselves know the fifty are still unpaid, provided there was no land lien. There is another reason. R. Chiyya derives the rule by kal vachomer from partial admission and from one witness. If witnesses merely prove the loan while the defendant's own denial supplies the nonpayment element, the evidentiary structures are not fully parallel. The inference is cleaner when the witnesses themselves establish a definite unpaid fifty. Rambam's wording also says that the witnesses testify he "still has fifty," which supports Sema. It is possible that Rambam states the stronger case only for clarity and would agree with Ran under current rabbinic collection rules, but his wording is noteworthy. Accordingly, Sema and Shach reflect a genuine dispute among major authorities. The matter remains a safek din.
