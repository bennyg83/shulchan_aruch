Clarify your words as to why he is liable, etc. Here, at the beginning of the laws of claims, the Tur discusses whether a litigant must clarify the beginning and basis of his claim.
Above in siman 70, by contrast, the parties had already clarified the original claim, and the defendant merely asserted additionally that he had witnesses that he repaid; the issue there is whether he must bring those witnesses and what happens if they later contradict him.
The law here derives from the Gemara's rule that witnesses who come to testify that one person owes another must each clarify the substance of what occurred and how the obligation arose. The same applies to a litigant himself when the court sees reason to require clarification.
If his stated details are later contradicted, the effect depends on whether he was obligated to clarify those details in the first place and whether the contradiction concerns the essence of the claim or only an incidental matter.
A person is not generally bound by superfluous explanations of matters he was not required to state, but when the clarification is necessary to make the claim intelligible and legally cognizable, he is bound by it. This is the distinction between this siman and siman 70.
