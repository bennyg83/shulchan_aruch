Seif 1: they tell him, clarify your words. The Gemara in the fourth chapter of Shevuot derives from Scripture that a person should not make a false monetary claim merely in order to impose an oath upon another. The Shach wonders why the Tur and Mechaber omitted these laws. In my opinion the omission follows from the geonic enactment, codified by the Rambam and Mechaber in siman 87, that anyone who seeks to impose an oath on another must first accept a ban affirming that he is not asserting a false claim merely to force an oath. In the time of the Gemara, before that enactment, there was a distinct practical need to state that such strategic false claims are forbidden because of “distance yourself from falsehood.” After the geonic enactment, the practical consequences are largely absorbed into the oath procedure itself. A claimant who asserts a false claim would first have to accept a severe ban falsely. If he is willing to do that, an additional admonition against falsehood is unlikely to restrain him. Likewise, defendants are now subject to the oath of hesset even on complete denial, so manipulating the form of the pleading ordinarily does not create the same procedural advantage. This explains why the later codes did not separately reproduce every rule stated in that Gemara.
