He is one who is obligated to swear and is unable to do so, etc. Even though the witness does not testify that the defendant has not yet repaid, or that the debt is still within its term, nevertheless the claimant asserts with certainty that the defendant still owes him, while the defendant answers, "Perhaps I never borrowed from you," and thus does not contradict the witness who testifies that he borrowed. He must therefore pay, because a definite claim prevails over an uncertain response. Although it was already explained that when the claimant asserts with certainty and the defendant answers, "I do not know whether I borrowed from you," the defendant takes an oath of hesset that he does not know and is exempt, this case is different because there is at least one witness that he borrowed. The Torah gave one witness the credibility of two so long as the person against whom he testifies does not swear to contradict him. Here the defendant does not contradict him, but merely says that he does not know. Thus, when the Tur and Mechaber say that he is unable to swear, they mean that he makes no claim upon which he can swear in contradiction of the witness. Therefore the claimant's definite assertion joins with the testimony of the witness to impose liability. This must be the explanation of the Tur's words, as I wrote in the Perishah. See there.
