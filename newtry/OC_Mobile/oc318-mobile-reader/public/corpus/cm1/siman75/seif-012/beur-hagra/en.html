You have one hundred of mine, etc. See Tosafot to Bava Kamma 41b, s.v. 'he admits,' beginning 'It appears,' etc. Earlier authorities resolved the case by saying that the owner does not know, but according to Tosafot there, 46a, s.v. 'even,' beginning 'It may further be answered,' that resolution does not stand.
(Anthology) You have one hundred of mine, etc. Bava Kamma 41b says, 'By the owner's own admission,' etc. See Tosafot there and Sefer HaTerumot, who answer that he said, 'I do not know.' See also 46a, s.v. 'even,' beginning 'It may further be answered.' Their reasoning is refuted by Tosafot to Ketubot 12b, s.v. 'Rav Huna,' beginning 'It may be answered that even according to the one who says,' etc. This supports what is written in seif 23 regarding a doubtful claim. Hence the text says, 'or the defendant said, I do not know.' Shach explains that the claimant too knows only from the witness. This is precisely Tosafot's case: 'I do not know whether I repaid' is equivalent to 'I do not know whether I became liable' according to Rav Huna and Rabbi Yehudah, and even if the witness too does not know whether payment was made, it is a definite claim against an uncertain one, and in such a case he is liable.
