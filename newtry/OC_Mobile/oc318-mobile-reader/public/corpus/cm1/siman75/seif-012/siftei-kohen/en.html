And one witness testifies that he lent him. This applies even if the witness does not testify that the debt remains unpaid or that it is still within its term. Although the defendant has a migo, because he presently says that he does not know and a Torah oath cannot be taken on an assertion of ignorance, he is one who is obligated to swear but cannot swear, and therefore pays. This appears from Responsa Maharashdam, contrary to Responsa Mabit. The rule applies only when the witness definitely testifies to a loan, not merely that he saw the claimant count money out to the defendant.
I do not know whether I repaid you, and one witness testifies that he did not repay. Several authorities struggle with this because a defendant who says he does not know whether he repaid is liable even without one witness. In my view the principal point is missing from the printed wording. The case concerns a claim made on the basis of another person’s information, as discussed below in seif 23.
