When he says, "I was told." See Shach, seif katan 83, who writes that one may learn from this regarding another person who is in possession, for example one holding money, but only through a power of attorney. Tumim questions this. Every power of attorney makes the attorney only an agent, and we rule that he is merely an agent. How then can the agent's migo help the principal? This is even worse than a migo used to extract property. Tumim is correct. Even if the principal sought to transfer the claim to him through a full kinyan, he could not do so while uncertain, for how can one transfer another person's property when one does not know that it belongs to him? With collateral as well, he cannot transfer it to another because delivery to another would constitute negligence, as explained in siman 58. Tumim also correctly rules in the following case. Reuven holds a promissory note against Shimon but does not know whether it was paid. Levi knows that it was not paid and is in possession of money belonging to Shimon. Reuven may sell the note itself to Levi, and Levi may then retain Shimon's money, because Reuven has not acted upon Levi's money at all; he has merely transferred his own note. Ketzot HaChoshen understood Tumim differently and therefore raised objections that do not apply. See there.
