He told me. Meaning, "My father told me." But if he says merely, "Others told me," everyone agrees this does not create a Torah oath, unless that other person is competent to testify and actually testifies in beit din.
Who says that it was told to him. The Shach disagrees. He also writes that if the claimant himself does not know but can give authorization to another person who does know, that authorized person may impose an oath based on a certain claim. See Beurim: this is not correct unless the actual debt instrument is genuinely transferred to him and he himself has a true certain claim. Likewise, if he actually holds property and there is a genuine unresolved legal doubt in the document, he may say, "I rely upon the authority supporting me," because he is then a true litigant.
He is not interested in the testimony. For example, Reuven sent money through Shimon to Levi to buy produce. Shimon says he gave the money to Levi, and Levi says he received nothing. Reuven cannot make Levi swear on the basis of Shimon's statement, because Shimon is financially interested: perhaps he kept the money himself. This applies where Levi makes no claim against Reuven, because Reuven owes Levi nothing and is merely suing Levi. But if Levi sues Reuven for money owed to him, the case resembles the rule of a householder who tells a shopkeeper, "Pay my workers," where the workers swear and collect.
