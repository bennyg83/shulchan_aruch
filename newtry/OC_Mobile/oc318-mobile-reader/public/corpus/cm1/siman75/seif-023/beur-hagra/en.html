He made an uncertain claim... As stated in Shevuos 41a from the verse, "one witness shall not arise..." He may not establish liability, but he may establish, etc. A pair of witnesses creates liability even where the claimant himself knows nothing, as stated in Bava Kamma 35b: Rabbi Chiya bar Abba said, "This teaches..." The first clause is where the injured party is certain, etc., yet the Gemara says that the burden of proof rests on the claimant. Thus with evidence he prevails. Likewise there on 46a: "But the Sages say, this is a great principle..." even where the injured party, etc.
(Collected note) He made an uncertain claim... Many disagree with this, based on the statement at the beginning of Bava Metzia, "the common feature is that through a claim..." and at the beginning of chapter HaDayanim, "this was taught only regarding the lender's claim..." Rashba likewise writes in Kiddushin 28a. See Tosafos there, s.v. "safek..." and Rashi there. Ramban, Rashba, and Ran, however, question this because the oath of bailees is taken on an uncertain claim. See there, where they labor to resolve the matter. Rashba writes there, "The correct view in my eyes is like the poskim cited above," and therefore it is impossible to derive it from sotah by a kal vachomer. This is very sound. He also cites the incident of Avimi in Kesubos 85a. This refers to Tosafos' question in Bava Basra 34a at the end of s.v. "havei," and one must say regarding what is stated in HaKosev, etc. Although Tosafos' question can be answered as they write there on 30a at the end of s.v. "lav": "and even though this purchaser knows..." Here too it is denial of an obligation secured by land, as stated in HaKosev. Tosafos at the beginning of Bava Metzia likewise write that it concerns only a rabbinic oath, as stated in seif 14. Nevertheless, many poskim disagree with this, as stated there. (End of note.)
But if there is no... As stated in Bava Basra 135a. That case is different because it is like a maneh claimed for another person, as above.
And some say that every... As above in seif 21, "My father told me," according to the Raavad. So too the Sema explains. The first opinion in the Rama, which is the Tur's view in the name of the Raavad, must likewise hold so. When it says, "even his relative," it means as above, "my father told me."
