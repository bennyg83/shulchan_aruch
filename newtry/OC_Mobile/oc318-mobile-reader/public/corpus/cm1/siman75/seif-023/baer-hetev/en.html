That he was told. The Shach discusses this law at length and concludes: If one claims, 'My father or predecessor told me that you owe him one hundred,' then even if the defendant admits part, he is exempt even from hesset. Certainly the same applies if the claimant says that some other person told him, unless there are grounds supporting the claim. If the informant himself appears before beit din, then if he is a valid witness his testimony can require a Torah oath. If he is a relative, his testimony may require a hesset oath when given before beit din although some disagree, as stated above. But if the witness has a personal interest in the matter, according to all opinions no hesset oath is imposed on his testimony. Shach.
A relative. The Shach writes that this law requires study even where the relative testifies before us, because I have not found this novelty in any authority. I also found a responsum of Rabbeinu Tam that clearly implies the opposite, namely that a relative can cause a hesset oath only where there are independent grounds supporting the claim. Terumat HaDeshen, siman 308, however, implies that Mordechai holds that testimony of a relative requires a hesset oath even without such grounds. This requires study. I later found that Maharshal also cites Rabbeinu Tam without qualification. One may learn from this that if a person lent another money against a pledge and forgot how much he lent, while his relative knows the amount, he can grant the relative a power of attorney, the relative can swear, and the creditor can collect. Perhaps this proof can be rejected because with a pledge the creditor has only a lien, as stated in siman 72, seif 14 in the gloss. Nevertheless, the principle can be learned for other cases of possession, such as where money is held. This works specifically through a power of attorney. Without it, one cannot say that the relative should swear and the claimant collect, because the relative is not the litigant, leaving the claimant with only a doubtful claim, and money cannot be extracted on a doubtful claim. Shach.
