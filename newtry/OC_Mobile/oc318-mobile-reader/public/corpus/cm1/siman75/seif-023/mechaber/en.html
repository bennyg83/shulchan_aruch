If a person makes an uncertain claim based upon a witness, such as, 'So-and-so told me that you took one hundred of mine,' and the defendant denies it, then if he produces that witness, the witness obligates the defendant to take a Torah oath as though the claimant had made a definite claim. {Rama: If the witness is not before us to testify, the claimant's statement that he heard it from others is not considered a definite claim, and no oath is administered upon it because it is an uncertain claim. Some say that whenever he says he was told by a trustworthy person, even by his relative, the defendant is required to take a heses oath. This is how I consider it proper to rule, provided that the person whose report he cites has no personal interest in the testimony. See Terumat HaDeshen 308.}
