(Seif 13) “Since it came into his possession lawfully.” This means that the creditor acquired a proprietary interest through his money. The Rashba explains that even where witnesses saw the pledge and the creditor has no migo, a dispute about the loan amount may still be treated as a claim against a possessor, with the borrower considered the one extracting money. The Rashba elsewhere records a dispute whether the creditor has ownership or only a lien. This dispute also determines whether one witness who saw the loan but not its amount makes the creditor one obligated to swear who cannot swear. If two witnesses would leave the creditor in possession, one witness cannot create monetary liability. The Sema’s explanation that the creditor prevails because the witness does not contradict the amount implies that contradiction would defeat him, which is incorrect; the creditor can swear against one witness. The decisive issue is possession, not migo. The Rama and Sema appear not to have connected the Rashba’s two responsa. The practical ruling will be discussed below.
