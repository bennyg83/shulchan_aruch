'I have a claim against you.' Baal HaMaor, in the first chapter of Bava Metzia, holds that he is not believed through migo. His proof is from the law of two people holding a garment, where the Gemara raises the possibility that one seized because of an old loan. If a person could simply say, 'I have an old loan against you,' and be believed through the migo that he could have claimed the entire garment, why does the Gemara not formulate the matter directly that way? He therefore concludes that such a migo is ineffective. See what I wrote in my Sefer Shev Shemateta, section 6, chapter 14, resolving this difficulty.
