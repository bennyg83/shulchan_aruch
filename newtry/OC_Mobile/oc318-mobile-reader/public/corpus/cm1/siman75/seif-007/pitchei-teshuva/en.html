Or you gave it. See Baer Heitev and the Shach, who implies that a defendant is not believed to say, “I have an offsetting claim against you,” or “you waived it,” except where he has a migo. Responsa Neot Deshe, siman 61, writes that although the Shach’s discussion concerns a claim that the plaintiff has clothing or utensils belonging to the defendant, one cannot distinguish and say that this is only because such a claim is unusual and weak, whereas an offsetting money claim arising from ordinary business dealings and accounts should itself be as strong as a plea of payment. The proofs showing that a claim to clothing in the other party’s possession is weak also demonstrate that an offsetting money claim is weak. Otherwise a debtor could claim payment before the due date by a migo that he had an offsetting monetary claim, and debts could not be collected from heirs within the term. It follows that even an offsetting claim for money is not believed without a migo. See there.
