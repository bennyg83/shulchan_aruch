Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe write that if the defendant claims that the plaintiff holds clothing or utensils belonging to him as a deposit, he cannot set them off through an oath unless the plaintiff denies holding them. If the plaintiff says that they were lost by unavoidable accident, however, the plaintiff is exempt by the Torah oath of a custodian and the defendant cannot swear that he has an offsetting claim. The Sema notes from siman 87 that even after a defendant was exempted by heset or a Torah oath, if the plaintiff later seizes property without witnesses and claims that the defendant swore falsely, the plaintiff is believed through a migo that he could have denied the seizure and may swear to the value seized. The Shach infers that a claim of setoff or waiver is believed only through a migo of complete denial or payment. The Rama at the end of siman 70, Mordechai, and Ramban indicate this, contrary to Responsa Maharanach 21. The opinion in siman 82 allowing a borrower to claim waiver despite the lender's possession of a document applies only to requiring the lender to swear, as with a claim of payment.
