If one claims, 'You have one hundred of mine in your possession,' and the other replies, 'I have nothing of yours,' or claims, 'I have clothing or utensils of yours in my possession as an offset,' or says, 'It is true that you once had money in my possession, but you forgave it or gave it to me as a gift,' he denies the entire present claim. He is therefore exempt from a Torah oath and takes a heses oath. Even if two witnesses testify that the claimant lent him the money but do not know whether he repaid it, he is believed upon taking a heses oath to say, 'I hold an offsetting claim against you,' or, 'You forgave it.' See below, siman 87, for all the distinctions governing a heses oath.
