I have clothing and utensils of yours in my possession corresponding to it, etc. The Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe write that if the defendant claims that the claimant holds clothing and utensils of his as a deposit, he cannot swear to offset the claim unless the claimant denies possession. If the claimant says that the objects were lost through unavoidable accident, the claimant is exempt upon a Torah oath, and the defendant cannot swear that the claimant holds property corresponding to the debt. The Sema states this, and the Rama records it in siman 72, seif 17. See what I write there.
But you waived it, etc. This implies that a defendant is not believed to say, “I have a corresponding claim against you,” “you waived it,” or “you gave it to me,” except through a migo that he could have denied the transaction entirely or claimed repayment. This also appears from the Rama in siman 70, at the end of seif 1, and from the Mordechai in the first chapter of Bava Batra in the name of Maharam, who writes that the Ramban agrees. A responsum of Rabbi Eliyahu ibn Chaim challenges this from the Rambam, who writes at the beginning of Hilchot To'en that one who claims movable property from another, and the defendant denies everything, admits part and immediately tenders it, or says, “It is true that you once had property with me, but you waived it, gave it to me, sold it to me, or I returned it,” takes a heset oath and is exempt. He infers that waiver is independently effective, like repayment or return, even without a migo. I do not understand this objection. The Rambam also lists “you gave it to me” and “you sold it to me,” and it is clear from many passages of the Gemara and agreed by all authorities that such claims are believed only through a migo of complete denial or return. The Rambam himself says this repeatedly in chapter 9 of Hilchot To'en. Thus, “you gave it to me” or “you sold it to me” are not comparable to “I returned it.” Since the Rambam groups waiver with gift and sale, waiver too is believed only through a migo. This is also why the Rama rules at the beginning of siman 78 that one is not believed to claim waiver during the term of the loan, for the Mordechai's reason that waiver is believed only through a migo. Although in siman 82, seif 11, some hold that a borrower may allege waiver even when the lender holds a note, that ruling concerns only the ability to require the lender to swear, just as when the borrower claims repayment.
