Every doubtful claim. No oath is imposed on a doubtful claim except in the cases listed in the mishna, 'These swear even without a definite claim,' and only those cases. So Rashi in Bava Metzia. See Tosafot to Bava Batra 33a, s.v. 'migo,' beginning 'It may be answered,' etc. Even a hesset oath is not imposed. See the glosses to Bava Metzia 26b, where Rav Nachman says, 'One who saw a sela,' etc., and Rashi there, 'If I seize him,' etc. See also 42b: 'Let us tell his mother, Go pay'; she says, etc.; 'and his mother shall swear,' etc. We do not administer an oath to the mother because he did not tell her that it was not his, proving that no oath is imposed on a doubtful claim, even through extension, as Tosafot write in HaShoel 97a and the Shulchan Aruch rules below in siman 94:2. A doubtful claim is added only where there are grounds for suspicion. See Mordechai there, chapter 3.

And some say that we administer an oath, etc. This is like the oath of partners and the oath in chapter 3 of Bava Metzia that the item is not in his possession. In all the cases listed in the mishna, the reason is that there are grounds for suspicion. The listed cases are therefore not exclusive; every analogous case is included. Tosafot in HaShoel 97a similarly distinguish, regarding adding a doubtful claim by extension, between a case with grounds for suspicion and one without them. Since without grounds he does not swear even by extension, all the more he does not swear independently. Mordechai there proves this from the case of one who asserts a doubtful claim inherited from his father, where the defendant is treated as returning a lost object. Otherwise, he would in any event not swear on a doubtful claim. A clear proof is Bava Metzia 26b, 'If two found it, he is liable,' etc. See Rashi there, 'I will seize him,' etc. Shach's rejection, that the claimant mistakenly thinks the law is so, is not persuasive. Why should he err here more than in the case of three who found it, where he knows that no oath is imposed on a doubtful claim, as Rashi explains? Sefer HaTerumot, which Shach cites, entirely rejects Rashi's view and cites Rabbeinu Chananel that with two finders it is a definite claim, as the gloss here states.
