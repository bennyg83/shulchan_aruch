No oath at all is administered upon an uncertain claim. For example, if one says, 'It seems to me that you have one hundred of mine,' or, 'It seems to me that you did not repay the one hundred I lent you,' the defendant is exempt even from a heses oath. He is not obligated even to fulfill his duty toward Heaven, since the claimant speaks uncertainly and the defendant makes no admission but responds with certainty. Similarly, if one tells another, 'You owe me one hundred because you admitted to me that you took my property,' but the admission was not made before witnesses, this is not a definite claim. The claimant knows of the debt only through the defendant's own admission, and an admission not made before witnesses, or even one made before witnesses without saying, 'You are my witnesses,' has no effect, for the defendant may say, 'I was jesting with you.' But if he claims, 'You admitted on your own that you owe me such-and-such, and I told the witnesses, or you told them, You are my witnesses, but the witnesses are not here,' this is a definite claim. He may require the defendant to take a heses oath that he did not say to the witnesses, 'You are my witnesses.' If the defendant reverses the oath to the claimant, the claimant swears that he admitted before them after designating them as witnesses and collects. {Rama: Some say a heses oath is administered upon an uncertain claim when circumstances support it. For example, Shimon was in Reuven's house, Reuven later found his chest broken open and its contents taken, and he suspects Shimon. Reuven may require him to take a heses oath. This is how I consider it proper to rule. This requires examination, however, because the cited responsum concerns a claimant who comes to collect, as explained below in siman 91, seif 3, in the Rama, and is not comparable to this case.}
