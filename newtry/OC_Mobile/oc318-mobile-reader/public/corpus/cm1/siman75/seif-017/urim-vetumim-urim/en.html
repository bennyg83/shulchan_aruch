Because he is not believed in his explanation, it is not called an admission, etc. See Tumim. If the defendant becomes liable from his own words because the legal system rejects his explanation, this can still be treated as partial admission. For example, if he says, 'I never borrowed,' which is legally equivalent to saying, 'I never repaid,' and witnesses establish the loan, or if he claims an entire cloak is his while we effectively witness that the portion held by the other party belongs to that party, he is a partial admitter. But if he says, 'I paid interest on your behalf,' thinking the interest was legally collectible, and beit din rules that taking that interest was prohibited and therefore obligates him for fifty, this is not a partial admission and creates no Torah oath. This follows Sema below in siman 87, not Shach. Likewise, if he says, 'I repaid you,' while witnesses establish only that he borrowed fifty and that the lender had required payment before witnesses, even though he now must repay, this is not a partial admission because it remains possible that he did in fact repay. There is no verbal admission at all. See siman 364 for the analogous case of contradicting one witness concerning fifty, where Rambam and the Shulchan Aruch do not treat the resulting payment obligation as partial admission.
