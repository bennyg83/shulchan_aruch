Five bundles. Rambam explains the Gemara's case of one who increased the damaging load through bundles and rules that if it is not known whose bundle caused the damage, all participants pay equally. This is difficult. Each person can say, "My bundle did not cause the damage; perhaps only the final bundle did," while the final participant can say that the earlier bundles already caused it. How can money be extracted from everyone on a doubt? Rambam appears to have a source from Bava Metzia concerning two people handling a load where it is uncertain whose negligence caused the damage. Rashi explains that because both should have exercised exceptional care and even a slight deviation by either could have caused the breakage, the matter is uncertain and both pay. This suggests that where two people act together and the causal uncertainty lies between them, both can be required to pay equally. Tosafot rejects Rashi's explanation. We rule like the Sages rather than Sumchus, and in an ordinary monetary doubt the burden of proof is on the claimant. If the case were merely uncertain, both would be exempt. Tosafot therefore explains that both actually acted negligently. Under Tosafot's approach, if the bundles case contains only causal uncertainty and no established negligence by all, all should be exempt. Even under Rashi, the bundles case is not necessarily governed by the same division. Although there are places where the Sages specifically instituted division in unresolved monetary disputes, Tosafot notes that such division applies only where the Gemara explicitly indicates it. Otherwise the general rule is that the claimant bears the burden of proof. One might say that once Rashi's case establishes a general pattern that two jointly acting tortfeasors divide when causal responsibility cannot be determined, the same principle extends to the bundles. But since Tosafot expressly rejects that reading and would exempt them, it is difficult that Tur and the Shulchan Aruch adopt Rambam's equal-payment rule without qualification. See also siman 388.
