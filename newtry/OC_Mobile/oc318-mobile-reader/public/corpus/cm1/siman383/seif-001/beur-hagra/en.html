When he is bound. Tosafot, Bava Kamma 22b, unlike Rashi there. See Rashi on 27a and 61b. Tosafot does not accept Rashi's approach. See Sema. Yet in siman 418 Shulchan Aruch rules without qualification like Rashi, and Rambam also follows Rashi. See 418.
Or he pushed it, etc. Meaning that although one who pushes a person into a place from which he can climb out is exempt, one who does so to an animal is liable, like placing a coal upon it, even if it is not bound. Shulchan Aruch's wording is therefore difficult, for it inserts 'bound' into Rambam's ruling. Rambam writes that a person's slave is like his body and his animal like his property: if one places a coal on the slave's heart or pushes him into the sea or fire while he can escape, he is exempt, but if he does so to another's animal, he is liable.
