When it is bound. Specifically where the ox is tied. If it is not tied, the person who placed the coal can say, "I thought the ox would shake it off in the ordinary way when it felt the heat; the owner's bad fortune caused what happened." If one places a coal upon another person's slave, even if the slave is bound the person who placed it is exempt, because he can say, "I assumed the slave's master would remove the coal and would not allow him to burn, since the master is obligated in mitzvot." This differs from an ox, where one can say that the owner relied upon receiving monetary payment, since what difference is there to him between having the ox and receiving its value? The life of a slave who is obligated in mitzvot is different. The Tur writes these laws briefly here, while their main discussion appears below in the laws of fire, siman 418, seif 18. The Mechaber likewise did not elaborate here, though there too he abbreviated and did not mention the condition of being bound either for the ox or slave. One must say that he relied upon what he wrote here. It cannot be said that he followed Rashi's interpretation there, which speaks even when not bound, because here he explicitly rules that the case is one where it is bound, following Tosafos, Rosh, Tur, and Rashbam. See what I wrote further in the Derishah and Sema to siman 418.
