And he purchased it from you. This means that the possessor is not yet claiming with certainty that the seller purchased it from the claimant, such as where he says, 'Presumably that seller purchased it from you,' or, 'The seller told me that he purchased it from you.' If, however, he says, 'He purchased it from you in my presence,' he is believed. Even if he initially said only, 'So-and-so sold it to me, and he purchased it from you,' and afterward explains, 'He purchased it from you in my presence, and this was my original intent when I said that he purchased it from you,' he is believed. The Rosh likewise writes this. The Sema, seif katan 34, writes that Tosafot disagree, referring to Tosafot on page 30a. In my view, however, Tosafot mean only that when he said, 'I purchased it from so-and-so, who purchased it from you,' in the sense that he merely assumed this or that the seller told him so, he cannot later claim that the purchase occurred in his presence, because we do not recognize a migo retroactively after he has already admitted that the property had belonged to the claimant. The Gemara there expressly concerns such a case and Tosafot merely abbreviated their wording. This is also evident from the incident involving Rabbi Chiya on page 41, where the same expression necessarily means, 'He told me that he purchased it from you,' as the Rosh and Rashbam explain. But when the possessor originally said without qualification that the seller purchased it from the claimant, his later explanation is not a retroactive migo. He says that this was his intent from the outset, namely, that he knew the seller had purchased it from the claimant. His original admission that the property had belonged to the claimant thus came together with its answer: it was yours, but I purchased it from one whom I know purchased it from you. It also appears to me that even after leaving beit din he may explain that his earlier words meant he was certain of the seller's purchase, provided that his original wording did not itself indicate doubt. He had not yet become liable through the first formulation, since beit din itself was required to construe his statement as a claim of certainty, such as that the purchase occurred in his presence or that the claimant admitted having sold it to the seller. This differs from siman 80 and from seif 24 below, where leaving beit din completed the adverse consequence of the first claim. Here, the second formulation is itself the meaning of the first. It is also comparable to the Tur and Mechaber below, seif 24, who construe a statement that the property belonged to his ancestors as meaning that his ancestors purchased it from the other party when that construction is possible. The Sema's statement in seif katan 35, limiting this to before he leaves beit din, applies only where he initially said merely, 'I purchased it from so-and-so,' in accordance with the Mechaber's wording. Examine this carefully.
