Knows. The Tur concludes in the name of the Rosh that even if the possessor knows this only from the seller, who told him so, it is considered knowledge. His own ownership is certain, while the seller's acquisition from the challenger is doubtful, and a doubt does not displace a certainty. The Sema writes that seif 15 below shows that the Mechaber also agrees. The Shach writes that the Rambam and the other authorities are primary: the possessor must say that he knows the matter independently and clearly, without relying on the seller's statement. Otherwise it remains doubtful. See there.
Purchased it. The Shach explains that he still does not make a definite claim, but says, "Presumably that seller purchased it from you," or, "The seller told me that he purchased it from you." If, however, he says, "He purchased it from you in my presence," he is believed. Even if he initially said only, "So-and-so sold it to me, and he purchased it from you," and afterward clarifies, "He purchased it from you in my presence, and that is what I meant originally," he is believed. The Rosh writes likewise. The Sema says Tosafot disagree, but the Shach believes Tosafot also agree and merely abbreviated their wording. See there.
