Take from you. According to the seller, he told me that she had taken from you, and that he did not know it in any way, and that it was proven in the report:
And the possessor cannot recover, etc., had you not admitted to him that it was his, etc. The Tur writes in seif 25 in the name of Rabbeinu Yonah that the possessor cannot return against the seller, because he caused his own loss through negligence. Had he said merely, 'I purchased it from so and so,' and nothing more, the challenger could not have removed it from him even if the possessor had not yet held it for three years, since the challenger had no witnesses that it had ever belonged to him and the possessor had not admitted that it had. The seller can therefore say to him: Your own mouth caused you to lose by admitting that the property had belonged to the challenger. Had he come to litigate with me, I would have exempted myself by claiming that the property was yours and that I purchased it from you, through a migo that I could have said it had never belonged to you at all. Although you yourself cannot now be exempt through the claim you made against him, since you possessed a claim that could have exempted you and through excessive speech caused yourself to become liable, I should not suffer that loss. This is the Tur's language.
