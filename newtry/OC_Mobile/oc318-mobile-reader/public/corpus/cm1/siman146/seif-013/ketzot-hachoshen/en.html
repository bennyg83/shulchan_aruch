The Shach says that if the possessor originally said, "I bought from so and so, who bought from you," he may later add, "I was present when he bought from you," and explain that this was his meaning from the beginning. Sema reads Tosafot as disagreeing, because a later claim that he personally witnessed the sale would be a retroactive migo. The Tosafot passage is best understood where the original statement meant only, "He told me he bought from you." In that case later adding, "I personally saw it," truly changes the evidentiary basis and relies on a migo that existed earlier. But where the original words were simply, "I bought from so and so, who bought from you," without explaining whether this knowledge came from the seller or from personal observation, later clarification can be accepted if it naturally fits the original words. The episode of Rabbi Chiya likewise can be read as one where the possessor had said the seller told him of the purchase. That is why the Gemara says he would have been believed had he originally said, "He bought from you in my presence." The later addition was not automatically available. This resolves the Tosafot language without proving that every later clarification is barred.
