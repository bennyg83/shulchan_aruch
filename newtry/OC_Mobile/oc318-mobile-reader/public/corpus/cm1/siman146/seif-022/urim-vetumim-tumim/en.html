We leave the property in their hands, with the stronger party prevailing. It is reasonable that this seif and the following seif refer to a case where one set of witnesses testifies that in a particular year Reuven's ancestors were using this field, while another set testifies that in that same year Shimon's ancestors were using it. The testimonies directly contradict each other as to that year, but it may still be possible for either party to clarify through additional witnesses that before or after that period his ancestors occupied the land. If such proof appears, it can establish ownership without necessarily contradicting the original sets, which testified only about one specified year. Since further clarification is possible, the rule remains that the stronger party prevails. But suppose one set testifies that Reuven's ancestors had always lived there, as described above in seif 24, while Shimon produces a contradictory set saying that Shimon's ancestors had always lived there. Such a dispute can no longer be clarified by additional ordinary testimony. Even if a thousand witnesses come for Reuven or Shimon, the original two remain contradictory, and two witnesses are legally equivalent to a thousand. Since no further factual clarification is possible, we do not apply the rule that the stronger party prevails. Instead the court must use judicial discretion or divide the property, as Ramban writes. Although the Tur appears to disagree with Ramban, the disagreement concerns Ramban's treatment of the ordinary statement that the property belonged to one's ancestors. In that ordinary case, further clarification may still be possible. The Nimukei Yosef explains Rashbam, whose ruling accords with the Tur, by saying that the party who truly has the right will investigate and find witnesses exposing the other's falsehood. But where the witnesses explicitly say "always," there is no possible clarification by proving an earlier or later period. It would be strained to answer that because one of the two original sets necessarily testified falsely, it may eventually be possible to disqualify that set through proof of theft or through hazamah, since truth endures while falsehood does not, thereby leaving the other set controlling. Such a theory is highly speculative and is nowhere mentioned by the authorities. The distinction stated above therefore appears correct.
