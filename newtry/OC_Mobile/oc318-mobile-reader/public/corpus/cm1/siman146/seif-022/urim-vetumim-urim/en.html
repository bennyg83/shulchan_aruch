He used it even one day. And the son then possesses for three years. See Tumim. The case is where the son has no independent proof that he is truly the heir, and therefore the father's single day does not combine with the son's period; the son needs a full three years of his own. If he has proof of inheritance, the father's day can combine. Mahara Sasson 41 says the same. I further wrote that if only the decedent had three years and the heir himself did not, the heir needs proof that he is truly the heir, unlike Bach, who thought one day of the heir suffices without proof. Responsa Mahara Sasson also rejects Bach. Rosh initially thought the heir must say, 'My father told me he bought it from you,' but later retracted, which is why Tur and Mechaber omit that requirement.
