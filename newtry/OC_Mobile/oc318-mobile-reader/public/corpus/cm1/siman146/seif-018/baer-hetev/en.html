This is like what the Sages said in the first chapter of Bava Metzia regarding one who knowingly purchased property that did not belong to the seller, and regarding one who betrothed his sister, where Rav and Shmuel disagree and the halachah is said to follow Shmuel. These are the words of the Rashba's responsum cited by the Beit Yosef. This is difficult, for Rava concludes there that the purchaser is entitled to recover his money, which is contrary to Shmuel, and all the authorities rule accordingly, as stated in siman 373. Although regarding one who betroths his sister we rule that the money is not returned, as stated in Even HaEzer siman 50, based on the Maggid Mishneh's explanation that we follow Shmuel in monetary law, that case is different. The Rosh likewise writes in the first chapter of Bava Metzia that Rava's ruling that the purchaser recovers his money does not prove that the halachah follows Rav regarding one who betroths his sister, because there may be a distinction between his sister and an unrelated woman. Similar distinctions appear in the Gemara there. It is strained to say that here, since he knew the property was not the seller's but his own, even Rava agrees. Moreover, if so the Rashba would not have needed to say that the halachah follows Shmuel, because if Rava agrees then Rav would agree as well. This requires study. Shach.
