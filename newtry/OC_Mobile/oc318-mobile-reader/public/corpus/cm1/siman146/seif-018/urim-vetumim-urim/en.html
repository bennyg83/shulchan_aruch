If he brings witnesses, it returns. Tur says the claimant swears and takes. Mechaber follows Rambam, who does not mention an oath. Above at the beginning of siman 140 Mechaber did require an oath in the ordinary case where a possessor claims purchase without three years' possession. Sema suggests Mechaber relied here on that earlier rule, though he also notes a distinction. The Shach says the claimant is like one who extracts land and therefore should swear even on an uncertain defense, but this is not convincing. See Tumim. In my view even Tur agrees no oath is needed merely to recover the land. The oath concerns produce the claimant seeks to recover, because perhaps he had sold the land to another person and may be liable to repay the produce again, as explained above in siman 145:7. This is clearer than Sema and Shach.
