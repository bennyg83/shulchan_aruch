As a gift. The Rashba's responsum cited by the Beit Yosef compares this to the cases in the first chapter of Bava Metzia where one knowingly purchases property that is not the seller's, and where one betroths his sister. Rav and Shmuel dispute those cases, and the responsum states that the law follows Shmuel. This is difficult, because Rava concludes in Bava Metzia that the purchaser recovers his money, contrary to Shmuel, and all the authorities rule accordingly, as stated below in siman 373. Although in the case of one who betroths his sister we rule that the money is not returned, as Rambam and the Mechaber state in Even HaEzer, siman 50, Maggid Mishneh explains that this follows Shmuel only in that distinct context. The Rosh likewise writes that Rava's ruling does not establish the law as Rav in the case of betrothing one's sister, because there may be a distinction between one's sister and an unrelated woman. The Gemara itself draws such distinctions. It is difficult to say that here, because he knew the property was not the seller's but his own, even Rava would agree, for then the Rashba would not have needed to invoke the ruling like Shmuel, and Rav too would agree. This requires study. Citation references: 100, 370.
