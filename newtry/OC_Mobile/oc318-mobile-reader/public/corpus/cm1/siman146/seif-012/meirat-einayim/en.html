Specifically when he mentions, 'today,' etc. It appears that 'today' is not literal. The same applies whenever he identifies a particular time, such as a certain month or a period in the summer. He must then prove that he was in that country at least once during that time. But if he merely says, 'I bought it more than three years ago,' and does not know exactly when, we do not compel him to specify a date unless he himself first mentions one. Once he does, he must prove it.
Even if the claimant himself says, etc. Meaning, not only do we not advance this argument for him; even if the claimant himself raises it, we do not listen to him.
Some say this applies specifically in a time of emergency, etc. Meaning, at the particular time when he claims the sale occurred there was danger and caravans were not then available. During the following three years, however, caravans did become available. Otherwise it is obvious there would be no chazakah, because the claimant could say, 'I did not know that he was occupying my property during the three years,' as explained at the beginning of siman 143. Likewise, protest is impossible where caravans are unavailable, as explained at the beginning of this siman, and whenever one cannot protest the other cannot establish chazakah. See the Perishah.
Perhaps he sold it through an agent. Even if he says, 'I sold it personally and not through an agent,' he is believed through a migo. The Tur writes in the name of Ramah: outside a time of emergency, even if the possessor says, 'I bought it from you on such-and-such day,' he need not prove it because he has a migo that he could have said, 'I was with you on another day,' or 'you sent me instructions.' From the phrase 'I was with you on another day' it appears that even where witnesses testify that he was not here at all, he can say that he was in the place where the claimant was and purchased it there.
