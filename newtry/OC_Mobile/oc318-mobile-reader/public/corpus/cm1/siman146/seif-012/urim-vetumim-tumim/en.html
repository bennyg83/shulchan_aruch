And some say specifically when he identifies the day, etc. The Sema in seif katan 24 understands that the authority holding this view does not explain the Gemara's phrase "I was in the inner residences" like the Rif and Rambam, namely that the owner claims he was in distant places at the time the possessor says he bought the property. Rather, he understands it like Rashbam and the Rosh. But according to the Shulchan Aruch's ordering, after this view it cites, "and some say, specifically in a time of emergency." This proves that the present view itself explains the Gemara like the Rif. If this authority followed Rashbam's explanation, it would be obvious that the possessor need not bring proof except in an emergency. From where else could there be any basis to require proof? It could not be imposed merely by logic, for Ramban and the Rosh themselves struggled with the Rif's explanation as to why the possessor should need to prove his claim. All the more so, if the Gemara's "inner residences" is interpreted like Rashbam, there would be no source at all for requiring proof outside an emergency. What then would the Mechaber mean by introducing the next view as "and some say"? It is therefore clear that the present authority follows the Rif's interpretation of the Gemara. He merely holds that even the Gemara's case requiring the possessor to clarify his consumption applies only where the possessor identifies a specific day on which the purchase allegedly occurred. If he does not identify a day, he need not bring proof. But the case does not depend on an emergency at all. This is clear and correct, contrary to the Sema.
And some say, specifically in a time of emergency, etc. It appears that this refers to a case where it was known that the seller was in distant lands. If it was not an emergency, we say that the parties could have communicated through a messenger or similar means. In an emergency, however, caravans are not commonly available. But where it is not known at all that the seller was far away, what difference does an emergency make? The possessor says, "I was with you in the same country." An emergency does not prevent two people in the same country from meeting. This is explicit in the Rosh's rulings. This also resolves the Tur's citation of the Ramah, who writes that outside a time of emergency, even if the possessor says, "I purchased from you on such and such day," he need not bring proof, because he has a migo that he could have said, "on another day I was with you." The Perishah asks what kind of migo this is. If he identifies another day, he will simply have to prove that day as well, and he can never escape the requirement of proof. According to what I have written, however, the answer is clear. The entire discussion refers to a case where it is known that the seller was in distant lands. Otherwise the challenger's claim is obviously meaningless, as the Rosh writes. Even where the possessor identifies a particular date on which it is known that the challenger was outside the country, if it was not a time of emergency he need not prove his claim, because he has a migo to say that he purchased the property on some earlier day long before the challenger traveled far away. The challenger was not always in distant lands, and regarding an earlier date there would be no need for proof according to anyone. The possessor is therefore believed even to say, "at that very time when you were far away, I was with you and bought it from you," as the Sema writes in seif katan 28. But where caravans are not common and the challenger was known to be far away on the date the possessor identifies, the possessor is not believed to say, "I was with you that day," by migo that he could have claimed an earlier purchase. Such a migo runs against a chazakah, because in an emergency caravans are unavailable and there is a strong presumption that he was not with the challenger. He must therefore clarify his claim. The matter is straightforward and correct.
