Because it is testimony from writing. Meaning that a proper protest document should be written by two witnesses as a formal document, not merely as written testimony sent to beit din. Tur cites Rashi and Rambam above in siman 28 that written testimony sent to court is generally ineffective. Only formal deed language has documentary force. Sema attempts to connect this to Rabbeinu Tam's view allowing capable witnesses to submit written testimony. See Tumim. I rejected the comparison. A protest record does not depend on Rabbeinu Tam's rule, because without proper formalization it is not a deed whose testimony is treated as already examined. I explained instead why Tur and Mechaber omitted Nimukei Yosef's stricter wording: Tosafot and Rosh understand the ability to write a protest broadly as a rabbinic enactment made to protect the oppressed. Therefore, after the fact, other wording can also work. Rama's preferred wording is an initial drafting rule, not an absolute condition. This is unlike Maharit, who would invalidate it after the fact. I also rejected Maharit's further view that once three years have passed and protest is no longer possible, the witnesses can no longer write a record of a protest that was timely made. They can still record that earlier protest. Only after the challenger has already brought the matter to beit din should they no longer create the protest document; at that point they should testify orally in court.
