Since he could have said, etc. Although one witness would then have contradicted him, this remains a valid migo because he would not have been required to take a Torah oath. See above, siman 93, regarding what I wrote against the Sema in seif katan 9, and Responsa Rabbi Yosef of the House of Levi, siman 22, page 117.
Some disagree, etc. They hold that even where there is no witness at all, once he admits that a protest was made, the migo is ineffective. Rabbeinu Yerucham, Netiv 11, end of part 3, rules accordingly.
