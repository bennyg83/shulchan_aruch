And the law. See Baer Heitev, who writes that the purchaser or heir must bring proof that he is an heir, etc., through his citation of the Maggid Mishneh, chapter 14 of Hilchot To'en. See Tumim and Netivot HaMishpat, who explain that this applies specifically where the heir or purchaser possessed the property for three years and the decedent or seller possessed it for one day. In that case he must bring proof that he is an heir or purchaser, since his migo is that he could have said, "I purchased it from you." But in the reverse case, where the seller or decedent possessed it for three years and the heir or purchaser for one day, and they do not have the migo that they purchased it from the challenger, they must bring proof that they are an heir or purchaser. Otherwise the challenger can claim that the seller or decedent admitted to him that he had placed him there only to take the produce, or the like. This is also evident from the responsum of the Rashba cited by the Beit Yosef, section 14. See there. See Lechem Mishneh there, who explains that according to the Ramban, although an heir need not make a claim, he must bring proof that the property came to him as an inheritance. He therefore understands the requirement to prove that he is an heir as proof not merely that he is a relative fit to inherit, but that the decedent actually left this property to him by way of inheritance. Yet even according to the Ramban, where the decedent possessed it for three years and the heir for one day, he agrees with the Ramah as explained above. Thus, according to Lechem Mishneh, proof that he is merely a relative fit to inherit is insufficient. Responsa Beit David, Choshen Mishpat siman 2, discusses this at length and strongly rejects the Lechem Mishneh. Shitah Mekubetzet, chapter Chezkas HaBatim, 41b, likewise indicates otherwise, namely that even according to the view cited as "some explain," it is enough to prove that he is a relative fit to inherit, at which point we return to the general rule that beit din advances claims on behalf of an heir. See there. As for Baer Heitev's citation of Responsa Mabit, siman 211, that we do not advance claims for a renter, see Netivot HaMishpat and Shaar Mishpat on this point.
