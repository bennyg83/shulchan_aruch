Ramban in Perek Chezkas HaBatim, cited by Ketzot HaChoshen, Maharit, and Tumim, was understood by some to disagree with the Mechaber's ruling, but the primary law follows the Mechaber. The reason is as Ketzot explains: once the challenger protested against the seller, the seller's one day residence is known to have been in the capacity of a wrongful possessor and therefore is not treated as the one day residence that can support the purchaser's chazakah. Sema, seif katan 17, writes that where the challenger is not confident that the purchaser knows of the prior protest, he should protest again. This is sound practical advice, lest the purchaser later advance the relevant claim. But so long as the purchaser does not make such a claim, his possession does not establish chazakah.
