But if he said to them, 'So and so who is using my courtyard is a robber,' this is not a protest, etc., until: perhaps he merely insulted him. This is only according to those who hold that he must conclude by saying, 'Tomorrow I will sue him,' and the like. But according to the alternative opinion, in such a case it is a valid protest, for they maintain that the Gemara's reasoning that the possessor can say, 'I thought he was merely insulting me,' was stated only where the protester said, 'So and so is a robber,' as in the wording of the Tur. This is straightforward. I wrote this to exclude the view of some who wish to distinguish and say that the alternative opinion disputes only where the protester says in his protest, 'So and so is using my courtyard as a robber,' which is as though he said, 'So and so is using my courtyard by robbery.' But here, where he says, 'So and so who is using it is a robber,' they agree that he must say, 'Tomorrow I will sue him,' for otherwise the possessor can say, 'I thought, etc.' According to that view, the words 'who is using it' were stated only as an identifying description of which person he meant. This distinction is incorrect. See Sema.
