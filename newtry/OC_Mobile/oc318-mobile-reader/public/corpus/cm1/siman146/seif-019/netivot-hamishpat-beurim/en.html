That he consumed it for seven years. See Sema, seif katan 46, through his statement that so long as the claimant does not prove that the seller lived there for one day. This wording is not precise and is abbreviated. Since Reuven already possessed the property for three years earlier, the land is treated as known to have belonged to Reuven. The only remaining possibility is that Reuven later sold it to Shimon. Shimon must therefore have lived there for one day, and the purchaser from him must possess it for three years. This is what Sema means, as is clear from his concluding words concerning interruption.
