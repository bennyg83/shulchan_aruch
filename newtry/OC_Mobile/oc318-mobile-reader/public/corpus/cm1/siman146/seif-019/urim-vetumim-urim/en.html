He need not make a claim. The Shach writes that he need not even prove that he is an heir or purchaser. If he possessed for three years and says he is an heir or purchaser, he is believed. Tur cites Ramban and Rosh, and Maggid Mishneh agrees. See Tumim. Rashba, however, holds that if the present possessor did not himself possess for three years but relies on the fact that the seller or decedent did, then when he claims through purchase he may need proof that he indeed comes through that predecessor, because perhaps the predecessor would have admitted he held only for produce. I concluded that Rashba's ruling should be accepted, since I found no true dissent from it.
