Witnesses that he consumed it for seven years. This is the language of the Gemara and the Tur. In the Perishah and Derishah I wrote that if he brings witnesses that he consumed the property for three years before the four years indicated by the challenger's purchase document, that alone is sufficient, so long as the challenger does not bring proof that the seller lived there for one day after those three years. If such proof existed, we would advance on the challenger's behalf the claim that the seller reacquired the property from the possessor and then sold it to the challenger. The reason the authorities mention seven years is that at the outset of the dispute the possessor had already brought witnesses that he consumed it for the most recent three years. They teach that this is insufficient. He must bring witnesses extending backward seven years from the present, so that his three years of possession began before the challenger's purchase and there was no intervening day in which the seller resided there between those years and the challenger's acquisition.
