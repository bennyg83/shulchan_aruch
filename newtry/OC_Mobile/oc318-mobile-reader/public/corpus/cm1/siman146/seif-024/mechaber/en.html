If each party says, 'It belonged to my ancestors,' and one brings witnesses that it always belonged to his ancestors while the other brings witnesses that he consumed its produce for the years establishing possession, the property is returned to the one who proved ancestral ownership, and the possessor returns the produce he consumed. This applies even if the only proof of his consumption is his own admission, because he made no valid claim, and possession without a claim against the owner has no effect. {Rama: If the witnesses testified only without qualification that it belonged to the claimant's ancestors, but did not testify that they never sold it, and the possessor's statement that it belonged to his ancestors can be understood to mean that his ancestors purchased it, the possessor is heard. But if he initially claimed that it had always belonged to his ancestors, he cannot change his claim. If the claimant says, 'It belonged to my ancestors,' and the possessor says, 'It belonged to my ancestors,' implying that it did not belong to the claimant's ancestors, we do not reinterpret the possessor's words for him unless he explains them himself. If he has witnesses that his ancestors lived there for one day, however, we make the appropriate claim for him even if he did not explain his words.} If the possessor then says, 'Yes, it belonged to your ancestors, but you sold it to me. When I first said it belonged to my ancestors, I meant that I rely upon it and it is mine as securely as ancestral property,' or says, 'It belonged to my ancestors, who purchased it from your ancestors,' this is a valid claim because he explained his first words. He is maintained in possession, provided he makes the claim while still before beit din. If he left beit din and later returned with the new claim, he is not heard, because others may have taught him to make a false claim. If he initially said, 'It belonged to my ancestors and not to yours,' this other claim is not heard even while he remains before beit din. When does the restriction on changing a claim apply? When the first claim was made before beit din. A statement made outside beit din may be replaced by another claim in beit din. Some say that even if he said outside beit din, 'It belonged to my ancestors and not to yours,' he may say in beit din, 'It belonged to my ancestors, who purchased it from your ancestors.' Others disagree.
