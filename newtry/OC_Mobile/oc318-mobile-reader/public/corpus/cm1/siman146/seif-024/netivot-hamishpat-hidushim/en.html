One cannot say that we plead for him that he knows his ancestors purchased it in his presence, because then with a purchaser who says, "I bought it from so and so who bought it from you," we should likewise plead that the intermediate seller purchased it in his presence. It belonged to my ancestors, who purchased it from your ancestors. This refers to one who initially said only, "It belonged to my ancestors." If he initially said, "It always belonged to my ancestors," he cannot retract and offer this new claim. The reason the text does not say, "My ancestors purchased it from you," is that in such a case the challenger makes a definite claim that they never purchased it from him, and then witnesses that the predecessor lived there one day are specifically required, as above in seif 14. But if he says they purchased it from the challenger's ancestors, the challenger cannot make a definite claim that they did not buy it from those ancestors, and therefore it helps if the possessor says, "In my presence he lived there one day."
