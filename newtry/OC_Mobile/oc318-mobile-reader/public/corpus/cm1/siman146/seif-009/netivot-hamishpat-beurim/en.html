We do not remove it from his possession until this challenger brings proof. At first glance this is difficult. Siman 139 states that when one says, "It belonged to my ancestors," and another says the same, if a third person comes and seizes the property he must return it to one of the litigants, even though neither has witnesses. Why, then, is the possessor here not required to return the property to the challenger until the challenger proves ownership? The distinction is that here the property has already come into the possessor's hands. When another person then appears and says, "It is mine," the law is analogous to a lost object that has already been found. One does not return a lost object to anyone who merely claims it without witnesses or identifying signs. Although a finder of a lost object may not consume its fruits, here the possessor may use the land because the owner is absent and is not attempting to rent it. It is therefore comparable to a courtyard that is not ordinarily rented, where use is permitted, as explained in siman 363, seif 6. By contrast, if a lost object has not yet come into anyone's possession and one person steps forward with a definite claim of ownership, beit din need not prevent him from taking it, because a definite claim prevails over uncertainty where no opposing monetary possession exists. Only after property is already in someone's possession must the claimant prove his right before taking it. Thus, in siman 139, where the parties disputed the land or movables before a stranger obtained possession, the property is regarded as being in their sphere rather than in that of the rest of the world, who have only uncertain claims. Here, once the land has entered another person's possession, it is not returned to any claimant without proof.
The field returns to him. See Sema, who writes that the claimant swears and takes. See Shach, seif katan 8. The reason he is treated as one who takes property is that, if he is not the owner, the possessor is permitted to consume the produce as explained above. Recovering the field therefore has the character of taking from the current possessor.
