If he brings witnesses, the property returns, etc. The Tur writes, 'he takes an oath and receives it.' The Mechaber copies the Rambam's language, which does not mention an oath here. Likewise, in the law at the beginning of siman 140, where a challenger disputes a possessor who claims, 'I purchased it,' but the possessor has not yet maintained possession for three years, the Rambam does not mention that the challenger must swear. There the Mechaber nevertheless writes that an oath is required. Perhaps he relies here upon what he already wrote there. There is, however, room to distinguish. In the earlier case the possessor claims that the land itself belongs to him because he purchased it or inherited it from his ancestors. The present case is different. See the Perishah, where I explained the reason for requiring the oath there.
And they remove from him all the produce. The Rambam writes this as well. The reason is straightforward, because the possessor has no valid claim either to the land or to its produce. In the Perishah and Derishah I explained that although the Tur writes in seif 14 concerning this case, 'The law of the produce he consumed I explained above, when he must return it,' the word 'when' does not imply that there are cases here in which he need not return it. In this situation, where he has no valid claim at all, he must always return the produce. I explained there the meaning of the Tur's wording 'when.' See there.
