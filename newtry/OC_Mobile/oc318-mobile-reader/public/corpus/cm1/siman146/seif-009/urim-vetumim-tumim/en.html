If he brought witnesses, the field returns to him, etc. See Sema and Shach regarding the Tur's statement that the challenger must swear. Why should any oath be required? The witnesses establish that the field is his, and the possessor did not claim that he purchased it. The Shach says that the oath is imposed because of a doubt, but I do not understand what doubt exists here. The witnesses establish ownership. It therefore appears clear that even according to the Tur no oath is required concerning the land itself, because the challenger is not taking property from another's established possession in the ordinary sense. The land stands in his prior ownership. Rather, the oath concerns recovery of all the produce. The possessor may have a legitimate concern: since you allowed me to consume this field for such a long time without making a claim, perhaps you sold the land to someone else, and today or tomorrow that purchaser will come and I will have to pay the produce a second time, as in siman 145 seif 7 regarding a lessor. I would then suffer a loss. Concerning such a reasonable doubt, the Tur may require the challenger to swear, because he is now collecting the produce from the possessor and there are grounds for suspicion arising from his prolonged silence. The possessor can say: how could you simply leave the land without protest, when perhaps I would claim that I purchased it from you? The natural inference is that you sold it to another by deed, and therefore neither you nor that purchaser was concerned to protest, as explained above in seif 6. There is thus a concrete concern that the possessor may later suffer a second collection. The challenger should therefore swear. In my view, this is clearly what the Tur intended: the oath concerns the produce, not the land itself. Consider this carefully.
