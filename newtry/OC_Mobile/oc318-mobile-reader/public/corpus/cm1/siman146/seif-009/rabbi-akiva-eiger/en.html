[Shulchan Aruch] Beit din reverses its ruling. Note: If Levi was in possession of a field, Shimon produced witnesses that it belonged to his ancestors, and beit din delivered the field to Shimon; afterward Reuven produced witnesses that it belonged to his ancestors. Since when beit din placed Shimon in possession Reuven had made no challenge at all, Shimon is now a complete possessor, and Reuven cannot remove the field from him. Rashbam writes this in Bava Batra 42b, and Shitah Mekubetzet there cites Rabbeinu Yonah that in such a case, where the field had previously been in Levi's possession, even if Reuven immediately protested but had not yet produced witnesses, and beit din gave the field to Shimon, when Reuven later produces witnesses we do not remove it from Shimon. The present case is different only where no one had been in possession of the land. But when Levi had previously possessed it, and Shimon came with witnesses, nullified Levi's possession, and lawfully removed it from him, Shimon enters Levi's place with respect to possession, and Reuven's later protest is ineffective. See there. He further writes that if, while the field was initially in Levi's hands, Yehudah challenged with witnesses of ancestral ownership and Levi produced witnesses that it was not Yehudah's, and afterward Shimon produced witnesses of ancestral ownership, we place the land in Shimon's hands. Yehudah cannot remove Levi by saying, "According to my witnesses the land is mine, and according to your witnesses Shimon's witnesses are thereby sustained, so in either event the land is not yours and the dispute is between Shimon and me." Levi can respond that since according to Yehudah's own position Shimon's witnesses are false, Yehudah cannot rely upon them to oust Levi. Shimon, however, can remove Levi by the argument that in either event the land is not Levi's, because even Levi's own witnesses assist Shimon. The land therefore remains in Shimon's possession. See there.
