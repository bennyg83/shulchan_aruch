Sema says witnesses writing a protest should use language of agency, such as, "So and so instructed us to testify and write that he protested." He understands this as a document form that avoids the rule against written testimony. In my view the point is more precise. According to Rashi and Baal HaMaor, a document escapes the prohibition of testimony from writing because it is created with the authorization of the person against whom or for whom the legal writing operates. Thus, for a protest, agency language makes the written protest equivalent to the challenger's own written declaration. According to Ramban, however, ordinary documents are valid because they possess a formal law of a document whose testimony is treated as though investigated in beit din. A protest or notice of coercion has no ordinary document status. Tosafot therefore had to say that the Sages specially relaxed the written testimony rule for protests, because possession is easily defeated, and for notices of coercion, to protect the coerced party. It follows that under Tosafot and Ramban, agency language is not essential. The special rabbinic dispensation validates the written protest regardless of whether it is phrased as formal agency. The Tur's failure to specify a formula therefore makes sense. Rama's requirement of agency language follows the line of Rashi and Baal HaMaor. This also explains why the parallel issue must be examined in the laws of notices of coercion.
Maharit rules that a protest may be written as a memorandum only if it is written while the protest is still legally effective, meaning within the three year period. After three years, witnesses cannot create a written protest record even if the protest actually occurred earlier. At that point they are no longer carrying out the challenger's present act. They are testifying after the fact that a protest occurred, and ordinary written testimony rules apply. Tumim objects that if written protest was allowed to protect the oppressed owner, why should timing matter. In my view Maharit is correct. Under Nimukei Yosef's agency theory, the written instrument works because the matter is still within the challenger's power. During the three years, he can still protest now, so the witnesses' writing functions as his declaration. After the three years, he can no longer create a protest, and the writing becomes mere testimony from writing. Even according to Tosafot's special dispensation, the same distinction is sensible. During the three years the possessor's legal presumption is not yet complete, and the Sages were lenient in allowing written protest to prevent the presumption from arising. After three years, the possession is already established, and we do not uproot it by accepting written testimony that would ordinarily be invalid. The dispensation was to prevent acquisition of the presumption, not to undo an already completed one.
