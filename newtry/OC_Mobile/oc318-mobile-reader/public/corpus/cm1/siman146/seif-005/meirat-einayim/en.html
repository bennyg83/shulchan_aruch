They write it even though he did not tell them, 'Write.' The reason is that writing benefits the claimant, and one may acquire a benefit for a person in his absence. Tosafos write that although ordinary documents may not be written without the consent of the person who would be disadvantaged, this is a special enactment, so that even slight evidence will be treated as testimony sufficient to defeat chazakah. It is also an enactment benefiting the possessor, for when the protest becomes known he will preserve his deed.
They should write it in the language of agency: 'So-and-so caused us to testify,' etc. The word 'caused us to testify' is language of agency, meaning that he designated us to testify to this matter. Although he did not expressly command them to write, once he designated witnesses to the matter and acted so that it would become public, we presume he intended them to write, because a written document creates publicity. Nevertheless, they should not write, 'So-and-so sent us' or 'appointed us as agents,' because in truth they are not actual agents; if they wish they may remain silent, and the protest is nevertheless valid, as stated in seif 3.
For otherwise it is testimony from their writing. The Torah says 'by the mouth of two witnesses,' not from their written statement, as the Tur writes in the name of Rashi and Rambam in siman 28, and the Mechaber rules like them there in seif 11. Only in the language 'he caused us to testify' may they write it, because that is the language of a formal document, as the Tur and Mechaber write above in siman 61:10. All agree that testimony in a valid document is effective because of the needs of society, as explained in siman 29. This differs from witnesses who merely write their testimony in their own hand and send it to beit din, which the Mechaber rules there is ineffective. The same problem would arise if they wrote merely, 'We heard.' According to Rabbeinu Tam, however, whom the Tur cites in siman 28, any witnesses fit to testify, provided they are not mute, may initially send their testimony in writing to beit din. According to him they may also write the protest in the language 'we heard.' Perhaps this is why the Tur simply writes that they should record the protest without specifying the wording, because in siman 28 he cites Rabbeinu Tam last, implying agreement. Although the Mechaber in siman 28 rules like Rambam, and here too simply says they write the protest, this is because he copied Rambam's formulation, which in turn follows the Gemara's general language without entering these distinctions.
Once he protested in the first year. This is also the Tur's wording. In the Perishah I wrote that 'the first year' is not literal. The same rule applies if he did not protest until the end of the third year; he need not make another protest until nearly another three years have passed. There is nevertheless a novelty in stating the first year: although he showed his concern by protesting immediately, we do not say that because he then remained silent for so long he must have retracted the protest.
If he waited, etc., the chazakah is not established, etc. The reason is that the possessor can explain: when I saw that the claimant remained silent for three years after his protest, I stopped guarding my deed because I assumed he had withdrawn the protest.
