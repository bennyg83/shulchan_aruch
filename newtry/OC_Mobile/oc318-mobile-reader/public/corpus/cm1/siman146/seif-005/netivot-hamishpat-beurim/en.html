To write for him that he protested. Maharit, responsum 26, writes that this applies specifically within the three years, but after three years the witnesses may no longer write the protest. Tumim concludes that there is no distinction, and this is reasonable. Since the writing is permitted by an enactment for the benefit of purchasers, what difference is there whether it is within three years or afterward? Only when the litigation is actually before beit din, and the witnesses can testify orally, are they forbidden to write it, because the enactment for purchasers does not apply when the witnesses are present. From their writing. See Sema, seif katan 11, who writes that according to Rabbeinu Tam a written statement is effective in every form. Tumim correctly questions this, because the present case concerns writing that is intended to remain effective even if the witnesses later die, when they are no longer capable of oral testimony. They must therefore write, "We testified," even according to Rabbeinu Tam. I have written elsewhere that even according to Rabbeinu Tam a writing is not regarded as testimony until it reaches beit din, and at that time the witnesses must still have been capable of oral testimony. Thus, even according to Rabbeinu Tam a protest is written only because of the enactment for purchasers, and the language, "We testified," must be used. Tumim writes that this requirement applies only initially, while after the fact any wording is valid. According to the poskim who require the formal language of a document so that it not be disqualified as testimony from writing, however, there is a substantive requirement to write it in documentary form, using the language, "We testified."
