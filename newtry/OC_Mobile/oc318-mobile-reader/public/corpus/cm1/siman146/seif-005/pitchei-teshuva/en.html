He lived there even for one day. See Baer Heitev. Tiferet Shmuel, in the glosses of the Gaon Maharshak to the Tur on this siman, seif 20, writes that this is where the seller supports the possessor's claim, or is not before us to be asked. But if the seller says that he never purchased it from Levi, this does not help, for the seller himself contradicts the possessor. He cites Rosh, rule 99, seif 4, and Mahari ben Lev, part 2, siman 70. See Ketonet Passim on Nimukei Yosef, chapter Chezkas HaBatim, 168b, who cites this and challenges it from the Tosefta brought by the Rosh there, siman 8: one who purchases from a purchaser, even if the first seller says that the property was stolen while in his possession, does not have the power to destroy the right of this purchaser. The responsum of the Rosh there likewise indicates the opposite of Tiferet Shmuel's ruling. This was cited above in seif 10 in the gloss; the Beit Yosef at the end of this siman also cites Rabbeinu Yerucham, and above in siman 140, section 8, cites the Rashba bringing the same Tosefta. Mahari ben Lev initially expressed doubt, but ultimately ruled that we are concerned for collusion and brought many proofs that collusion is a concern. He then cited this law of the Tosefta. In any event, the law is certainly that even if the seller contradicts him, the chazakah remains effective, and the words of Maharshak require great study.
