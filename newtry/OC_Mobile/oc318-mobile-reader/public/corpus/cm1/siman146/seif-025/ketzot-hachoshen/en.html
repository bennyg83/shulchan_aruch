The Gemara rules like Rabbah concerning land where a person produced a defective document but also had a migo, while concerning money Rav Yosef can prevail. Ramban explains that the dispute is only whether beit din should validate the defective document enough to use the migo. If the court actually went ahead and validated it, even improperly, the factual basis needed for the migo would then be established. This answers Tosafot's difficulties. The document itself remains legally invalid. The court is not converting a void document into a valid one. Rather, validation reveals that the defendant truly could have presented a stronger document based argument had the document been legally sufficient. Once the factual capacity behind the migo has been established, the migo can support the independent substantive claim. Thus an improper act of validation can still supply the factual predicate for credibility even though it does not make the document itself enforceable.
The same point explains the monetary branch. Rav Yosef says the defective document is mere pottery and the claimant cannot extract money on its strength. But if the court already validated the signatures and thereby established the alternative claim on which the migo rests, the credibility analysis changes. The underlying debt is still not collected because the invalid paper became valid, but because the claimant's independent plea is now supported by a demonstrated alternative. Ramban's interpretation therefore preserves the distinction between document validity and evidentiary migo.
