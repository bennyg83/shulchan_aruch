The claimant brought witnesses, etc. It appears to me that the Rif and Rabbeinu Chananel also agree to this as a matter of law. They interpreted the case as one of a trust document only because it is uncommon to establish the authenticity of a forged document. Otherwise, there would be no reason to distinguish between a document issued in trust and a forged document. This is my view, although the Tur and later authorities do not appear to understand them this way.
And he produced an authenticated document, etc. The Tur states an even greater novelty: the document need not yet be authenticated, provided that he can authenticate it afterward. This is necessarily the view of Ramban and Rashba, cited at the end of siman 83, who interpret the passage as involving a document not yet authenticated that will later be authenticated. Although the Tur and Rambam there interpret the case as an authenticated document, they evidently hold that the rule is the same whether it was already authenticated or is authenticated afterward. The Rosh in Perek Chazakat HaBatim also implies this. I further concluded at the end of siman 83 that if the judge already acted and placed the claimant in possession, or the claimant himself took possession of the land, then even if the document was authenticated we do not remove the property from the claimant. See there.
Since, had he wished, he could have maintained his document, etc. This applies even though he did not hold the land for three years. The Sema writes that the Tur states this at the end of seif 10 in the name of Rashbam and the Rosh, but it is not found there in their names. They do, however, explain expressly that the case concerns one without chazakah, in their discussion beginning, 'The document is forged.' Tosafot likewise states this explicitly. The Bach rules like Ri Avi Ezri, but that isolated view is nullified before Rashbam, Tosafot, the Rosh, and the Tur. It is astonishing that he relied upon it against all these authorities. His reasoning is also incorrect for several reasons. Examine the Gemara and Ramban's novellae, which likewise prove that the case concerns one who had not established chazakah.
