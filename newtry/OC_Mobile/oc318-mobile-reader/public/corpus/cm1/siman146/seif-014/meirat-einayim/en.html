And Durk there is. This G-d is here, Dr. A.M., who connects at the beginning of the clause that the holder brings witnesses to which the seller is named. And he is simple:
But if after he claimed, 'I purchased it,' etc., through, 'we do not listen to him.' The reason is that if he initially said, 'The seller told me that he purchased it,' this wording implies that the seller merely told him so and that the possessor himself had no direct knowledge of the sale. How can he then return and say, 'He purchased it from you in my presence'? This differs from a case where he initially said simply, 'I purchased it from so and so,' as the Mechaber concludes. All the more so, if he initially said, 'I purchased it from so and so, who purchased it from you,' he need add only the word, 'in my presence.' This is the Rosh's view. Tosafot disagree and hold that even in such a case he may not revise his statement in that manner. See the Derishah, where the issue is explained. The Mechaber abbreviated greatly, while the Tur discusses these distinctions at length. In the Perishah and Derishah I explained them fully with God's help.
We listen to him. This applies specifically before he leaves beit din. Once he has left beit din, we do not listen to a revised claim in any form, as the Tur writes in this siman, seif 41, and the Mechaber below in seif 24. See there.
