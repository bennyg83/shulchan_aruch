If the seller affirmatively says that he did not buy it from Levi, the possessor's claim should not help because the seller himself contradicts it. Ketunet Passim challenges this from the Tosefta cited by the Rosh: one who purchased from a purchaser does not lose his right merely because his seller later says that the property had been stolen. This is also contrary to the responsum of the Rosh and to the rulings cited by the Beit Yosef and Rabbeinu Yerucham. Mahari ben Lev initially expressed doubt, but ultimately ruled that we suspect collusion and cited numerous proofs. He too brings the Tosefta. The law is therefore clear that even if the seller contradicts him, the possessor's chazakah remains effective, and the words of Tiferet Shmuel require substantial study.
