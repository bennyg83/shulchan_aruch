The Shach writes that even if witnesses told the possessor that the challenger had protested, if they also report that the challenger instructed them not to let the matter leave their mouths, the protest is ineffective. It appears from his wording that if they told the possessor only that a protest occurred, without mentioning the instruction of secrecy, the possessor must preserve his deed. In my view the final legal fact controls. If no valid protest ever existed, because the challenger said it should not be publicized, then the possession remains valid even if the possessor mistakenly believed a protest had been made. This is analogous to a case where witnesses falsely tell the possessor there was a protest and it is later discovered that no protest occurred. The possessor's subjective fear does not create a protest the challenger never made. Therefore if it is later established that the challenger ordered secrecy, the possession is valid.
