Since he could, etc. Meaning, even if there was one witness to the protest, we do not say in such a case that he is one who is obligated to swear but cannot swear, and therefore must pay. In matters of testimony concerning land, one witness does not obligate him to take a Torah oath, and the rule that one who is obligated to swear but cannot swear must pay is said only regarding a Torah oath, as the Tur and Mechaber wrote in siman 75, seif 14; see Sema. The Shach wrote that even though at that time one witness contradicted him, it is still considered a migo because he did not need to take a Torah oath. See above what he wrote regarding the Sema, seif katan 9 in siman 93. See what I wrote there on this matter. Here in Tumim I concluded that, at first glance, according to the reason given by the Mechaber, namely migo, if he admitted before witnesses that he had protested against him but did not then claim, 'It is purchased in my possession,' and afterward came to beit din and made that claim, he would not be believed, because this would be a retroactive migo. However, I wrote that the Mechaber's wording that this is because of migo is imprecise. Its source is the Ramban, and the Ramban explained the law even without a migo. I also wrote that the reason is as the Rashba stated: since he protested against him without witnesses, I thought that he was merely mocking me in this matter. See there. I further wrote that the Ramban's underlying ruling is difficult for me to understand, and I left it requiring study, for if he claims the produce that the possessor consumed during three years, he himself thereby acknowledges the chazakah. This requires study.
