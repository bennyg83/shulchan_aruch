The next is a gift of inheritance and there is no need for the occupants, and it is not for the man who is not in his face:
The law of a purchaser. The same applies to a creditor. He need not prove that he is an heir or purchaser, because three years of chazakah suffice. Shach. But in the reverse situation, where the decedent or seller held the land for three years and the heir or purchaser held it for only one day, so that the successor lacks the migo of "I purchased it from you," proof that he is truly an heir or purchaser is required. Regarding a creditor, we advance claims for him like a purchaser only after he has already collected the land. If he is still seeking to collect the land from the borrower, the borrower is believed to say the land is not his, even if the borrower himself had possessed it for three years. Beurim.

See Beurim: this rationale applies only to a woman selling for her ketubah outside beit din, where she is believed even without holding the ketubah, because the heirs certainly would not have allowed her to sell publicly unless the ketubah was in fact due.
