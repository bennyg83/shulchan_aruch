Maharit said the reason a purchaser must prove that the seller lived in the property for one day is merely to establish that he is truly a purchaser rather than a robber inventing a seller. I disagree. Rashbam states that the one day residence is required to substantiate the purchaser's claim that the seller himself bought from the original owner. Once the seller is shown to have resided there even briefly without challenge, there is at least some objective indication of ownership, and beit din can advance the seller's claim for the purchaser. Thus even if the purchaser possesses a written deed showing he bought from the seller, he still must show that the seller occupied the property for a day. The deed proves only that he bought from that seller. It does not prove that the seller owned the property. Rashbam's wording is explicit: the one day occupation is needed to make the purchaser's assertion about the seller's purchase plausible. This is the proper understanding.
Maharit further argued that if Levi produces a deed showing that he bought from Yehudah, this should be even stronger than proof that Yehudah lived in the property for one day. I am astonished. Rashbam expressly says that even where the purchaser's own deed from the seller is still in his hand, he must also have witnesses that the seller occupied the property for at least one day. The deed proves Levi bought from Yehudah, but not that Yehudah owned the land. The true function of one day residence is to give some factual basis that the seller may have bought from the prior owner. Although one day is far too little for a legal presumption, it is still an objective indication. Once that indication exists, beit din advances the seller's possible purchase claim for the purchaser. A bare deed from a seller who never possessed the property provides no such basis.
