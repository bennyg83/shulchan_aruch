And the law of a purchaser and a creditor, etc. It appears that the Mechaber means as follows. Just as we advance a claim for an heir where his father lived on the land for one day and the heir himself held it for three years, or the reverse, by saying that the father or heir purchased it from the challenger, so too we advance a claim for a purchaser. This applies where the seller lived there for three years and the purchaser himself has not yet maintained any possession, or where the purchaser held it for three years and the seller had lived there for one day. The same applies to a creditor who collected the land from his borrower and then held it for three years. The text does not separately say that we advance claims for a creditor as we do for a purchaser because a lender who collects land from the borrower for his debt is himself legally treated as a purchaser. The ruling concludes that even if the borrower or seller later says that he never purchased the land from the challenger, he is not believed by an admission that would now prejudice the person presently possessing the land.
