The reason is that only in the preceding case, where the rule is that whoever is stronger prevails, do we say that once one party has prevailed we do not remove the property from him unless there is clear testimony that it is not his. Here, however, beit din placed one party in possession, and it later became clear that they should not have done so because the other party has equally strong witnesses. We therefore say that the beit din that placed him in possession must remove him, restoring the parties to their original equal status, and the rule again becomes whoever is stronger prevails. The Tur writes likewise in the name of the Rosh, contrary to Rabbeinu Chananel and the Ramban. See there. Ibid.
