It is nothing. See Baer Heitev and Responsa Raanach, part 2, siman 43. Reuven possessed a certain house for many years. Shimon challenged him, and Reuven answered, 'I purchased it from your father, and here is my deed.' Shimon produced an earlier deed showing that his father had sold the same right to him, so it appeared that the father later sold Reuven property that was no longer his. Reuven then brought witnesses that eighteen years earlier he had in fact purchased the right from Shimon's father and had possessed it since then, but the written deed had been executed only five years later. There is no direct contradiction between the deed and his claim, since the deed could have been written later. The question is whether his later assertion is nevertheless considered a retraction from his first claim, because perhaps his first statement implied purchase only as of the date written in the deed. Raanach inclines to the possessor. According to the Rashba's view, this is not a prohibited retraction because the possessor merely mentioned the deed as compelling evidence and did not intend to specify the acquisition date. More fundamentally, he did not say, 'I bought it from your father eighteen years ago'; he said only, 'I bought it from your father, and here is the deed.' Those words do not limit the time of purchase. They mean that he purchased it at some earlier time and has a deed evidencing the acquisition. This resembles one who says, 'I consumed it for the years of chazakah,' where people may use that expression for many years. Some authorities explain the Gemara's case similarly even without witnesses to seven years of possession. Raanach therefore concludes that the later clarification is not necessarily a retraction, though the matter requires study.
