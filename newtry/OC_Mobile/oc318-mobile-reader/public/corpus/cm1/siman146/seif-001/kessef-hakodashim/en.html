Seif 10. (He does not need another claim.) A person challenged another's ownership of a house. According to the challenger, many years earlier he and his father were present when his father's sister, who was an ancestor of the present possessor, occupied the house and said that she would pay rent. About twenty years later, he says, he and his father were again present while the present possessor's father occupied the house, they protested, and he refused to obey judgment. The present possessor, however, inherited from his father, and his father had exchanged another house for this one with his brother in law approximately fifty years ago. There was later a testamentary division among the father's children, followed by a formal judicial record awarding this house to the present possessor. The challenger also objected to synagogue places that had belonged to the earlier ancestor. The possessor stated that he had seen a writing signed by several heirs of that ancestor awarding the places to his father, and those places were also included in the later judicial record. I found no basis to impose any burden upon the present possessor. One who comes by inheritance does not need to state the original purchaser's detailed claim, as ruled in siman 146, seif 10. There is likewise no compelling concern that close relatives would not ordinarily protest one another. The matter depends upon the circumstances as seen by beit din, and here even the challenger's own narrative indicates that objections did occur. Yet no valid protest was maintained during each required three year period, certainly not throughout the lifetime of the elderly woman. Moreover, there were at least two public transfers, including a formal judicial act. Such an act is plainly public. Urim VeTumim explains that the required publicity is not limited to an announcement in the synagogue. In a region where travel between towns is common, information can be considered public much as if the interested party were physically present. By the challenger's own account, he was already an adult, knew of the asserted claim, and nevertheless failed to make the legally required protest.
