It is nothing. See Baer Heitev and Responsa Raanach, part 2, siman 43, concerning Reuven, who had possessed a house for many years. Shimon challenged him, asking by what right he occupied it. Reuven answered, "I purchased it from your father, and here is my deed." Shimon then produced a deed earlier than Reuven's showing that his father had sold that right to him, so that apparently the father later sold Reuven property that was no longer his. Reuven then produced witnesses that eighteen years earlier he had purchased the right from Shimon's father and had possessed it from then on, but delayed writing the deed for five years. Thus Reuven's acquisition actually preceded Shimon's acquisition. The deed does not necessarily contradict Reuven, since it may indeed have been written later. The question is whether his later assertion constitutes changing his claim. Perhaps his original claim, coupled with the deed, amounted to saying only that he purchased it as of the date of the deed and not earlier. If so, even witnesses would not help, because a litigant's admission is equivalent to one hundred witnesses and he may not retract and make a different claim. Raanach writes that at first glance the law favors the possessor. First, according to the Rashba, which is the "some say" in this seif, this is not considered changing his claim, since he had no reason to mention anything beyond the deed that furnished him with conclusive proof. Second, fundamentally, he did not explicitly say, "I purchased it from your father eighteen years ago." He merely said, "I purchased it from your father, and here is my deed." His words contain no specific date that would make his later statement a changed claim. Rather, he said that he had purchased it at some prior time and produced a deed as evidence of the purchase. This is closely comparable to saying, "I consumed it for the years of chazakah," since people call many years "the years of chazakah," according to the Rashbam's explanation. In addition, some authorities explain that the case justified by the expression that people call many years "the years of chazakah" is specifically where he did not bring witnesses to seven years of use.
