The challenger may say: you were more convenient for me, etc. See Sema, seif katan 39, who writes that nevertheless the challenger acted improperly. The Rambam's formulation is that the challenger told the purchaser, "go buy the field, it is a good field," which appears to mean that he advised him regarding the quality of the field, whether fertile or poor. But if the advice concerned whether the seller's title was secure, and the challenger told him affirmatively that the field belonged to the seller, so that the purchaser bought in reliance on that statement, it appears that the challenger may be liable. This resembles one who shows a coin to a money changer and says, "I rely on you," as discussed above in siman 129 seif 2 and what I wrote there. Maharit, Choshen Mishpat responsum 18, writes similarly. Maharit also gives the reason that an admission can be treated as an act. There is room to doubt that latter ground, but the reliance principle of showing a coin to a money changer provides a strong basis for liability where the purchaser expressly relied on him. Maharit supports his proposition that an admission is considered an act from Tosafot in Ketubot 109, who discuss the rule that Reuven cannot testify for Levi because the second litigant is more convenient for him. Tosafot say that this can accord even with the Rabbis there because the statement at issue is not an admission, implying that if it were an admission the Rabbis would agree that it creates legal consequences. Maharit infers that an admission is treated as an act. This proof can be rejected. There the person actually testifies in beit din. Testimony in beit din is plainly a legal act. A witness can even receive lashes for false testimony, demonstrating that his testimony is treated as operative conduct. Thus, where he testifies because the second litigant is more convenient for him, that testimony itself is an act. Tosafot therefore need to say that the particular statement is not an admission. But an informal admission made privately to the other party outside beit din may not have the same status. Tosafot therefore provide no proof on that point.
