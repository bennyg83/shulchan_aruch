Responsa Raanach, part 2, siman 43, writes that mere verbal advice does not cause the challenger to lose his rights, but if the challenger actually served as broker between the parties he does lose them, for this is an affirmative act comparable to signing as a witness on the transaction. This applies where there are witnesses that he acted as broker. If the possessor merely claims that the challenger was the broker and the challenger denies it, Raanach says the possessor is not believed by a migo that he could have said, 'I purchased it from you,' because the brokerage claim itself does not create title; it is only evidence that the challenger has no right, since otherwise he would not have assisted in the acquisition, and evidence cannot be created solely from the litigant's own statement. Shaar Mishpat disagrees: once the possessor's version that the challenger acted as broker would ordinarily show that the challenger sold the property to the possessor's seller, why should a migo not validate that account? He compares it to the case where one comes through a non-Jew and says, 'He purchased it from you in my presence,' or has a deed, and is believed by a migo that he himself purchased from the challenger. He later found that Responsa Mabit expressly supports this view.
