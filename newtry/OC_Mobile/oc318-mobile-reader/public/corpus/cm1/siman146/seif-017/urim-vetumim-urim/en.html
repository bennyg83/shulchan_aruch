He need not protest. Sema cites the source: the later purchaser does not claim that he bought from the challenger, but only that he acquired through the prior possessor. This suggests that if the challenger is not confident the later purchaser will admit that chain, prudence favors making a protest. See Tumim. This is advice, not an absolute legal requirement. If the challenger did not protest and the later purchaser never claims purchase directly from him, the challenger's right is not lost because he did protest against the first possessor and the later purchaser admits he did not buy directly from him. This is unlike Maharit, who misunderstood Sema as making nonprotest an automatic forfeiture.
