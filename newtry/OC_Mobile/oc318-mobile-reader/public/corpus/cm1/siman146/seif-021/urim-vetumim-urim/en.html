The seller or borrower is not believed to say the land was not theirs. See Tumim. Rosh's responsum and rulings speak primarily of a purchaser. Rama extends the same principle to a borrower whose creditor collected. Maharshal disagrees and says the borrower can admit that the land was not his. I initially thought to prove Maharshal from Bava Batra but rejected the proof. Nevertheless, if the creditor has not yet collected, then besides the fact that we do not make claims for him as above, the borrower's admission should indeed be effective, as Maharshal holds. Rama's language is best read as referring specifically to land already collected. Responsa Mahari Weil and Mechaber below in siman 245 also support Maharshal in the uncollected case.
