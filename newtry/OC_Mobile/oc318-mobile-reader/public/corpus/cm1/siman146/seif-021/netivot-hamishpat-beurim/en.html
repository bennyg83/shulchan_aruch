Tumim cites Ramban that if witnesses establish the possessor's consumption for all six years, he has no migo that he bought directly from the claimant, because then why did he consume during the earlier three years? If he says that at first he had only produce rights, he has no chazakah at all because his initial entry was for produce. See siman 103, seif 3, where the poskim dispute this. In my view, those authorities who do not distinguish here hold that because the property later came to the possessor through another person and did not reach him from that person as a rental, he can establish chazakah against the claimant. This is even stronger than the case of a changed name and circumstance described by Sema there, seif katan 4, regarding one who entered a married woman's property and she later divorced. Tumim writes that he is not believed through a migo that he bought both from the seller and separately from the claimant, on the theory that people sometimes pay to eliminate litigation, even if he did not know of the seller's document. That would be a weak migo because it is unusual to buy the same right twice. Here the case concerns one who only now learned of the seller's deed. If he admits that he already knew of it before the three years ended, it is like knowing of a protest and depends on the dispute cited in seif 2 whether a migo helps once he knew of the protest. See Sema there, seif katan 4.
