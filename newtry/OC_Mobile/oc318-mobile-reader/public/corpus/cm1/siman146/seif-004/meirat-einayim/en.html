And if he later claims against me that I sold it, etc. This is the Rambam's language. According to the alternative view Rama cited immediately before, which holds that a protest need not conclude with, 'I will sue him,' all the more so this additional phrase is unnecessary. Even in a case of alleged robbery there is more reason to require such a statement, so that listeners should not ask: If it is truly being held from him by robbery, why does he not bring the possessor to court? Certainly, then, where the protest says only that the property is held on loan or rental, there is no need to add, 'I will sue him,' since the protest is made only out of concern for the future. This is clear.
But if he said to them, 'So and so, who is using my courtyard, is a robber,' this is not a protest, etc., because perhaps he was merely insulting me. This is specifically according to those who require the challenger to conclude, 'Tomorrow I will sue him in court.' According to the alternative view cited above by Rama, that no such conclusion is required, a statement of this kind is a valid protest. They hold that the Gemara's rule that the possessor can say, 'I thought he was merely insulting me,' applies only where the challenger says nothing more than, 'So and so is a robber,' as the Tur writes in seif 27. This appears obvious to me. I write it only to exclude those who wish to distinguish the wording and say that the alternative view disagrees only when the challenger says, 'So and so is using my courtyard as a robber,' which is equivalent to saying that he is using it through robbery, but that where he says, 'So and so who is using my courtyard is a robber,' even that view would require him to add, 'Tomorrow I will sue him,' because otherwise the possessor can say, 'I thought he merely meant to insult me,' and the reference to the person using the courtyard was merely descriptive so that the listeners would know whom he meant. This distinction is incorrect. In the first clause too the text reads, 'So and so who is using my courtyard,' and the Rambam, whose words the Mechaber copies, states it explicitly. Nevertheless Rama writes that some say the challenger need not add, 'I will sue him.' There is therefore no meaningful difference between the formulations. This is clear.
