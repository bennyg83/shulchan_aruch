That he bring witnesses that the seller lived there. See Mishneh LaMelech, chapter 14 of Hilchot To'en, law 14, who cites Maharit, Choshen Mishpat siman 27, that even if he did not bring witnesses that the seller lived there for one day under a claim of ownership, it is enough that it became known through witnesses or a deed that the seller sold it to this possessor. For if it were not his, he certainly would not have sold it, and this is better evidence than witnesses who merely testify that he lived there for one day. Mishneh LaMelech disagrees and leaves the matter requiring study. Ketzot HaChoshen and Shaar Mishpat likewise disagree with Maharit and conclude that even where the purchaser has a deed of sale from the seller, it does not suffice until he brings proof that the seller lived there for one day, which supplies at least some evidence that it was his.
