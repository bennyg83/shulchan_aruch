His implication that according to the opinion in siman 145, seif 4, that a sale without a deed has no public voice, the case could also involve a sale before witnesses without a deed, is certainly incorrect. As I proved above in siman 145, when there is a sale before witnesses, the buyer's protest is effective because investigation will reveal the buyer's acquisition, and therefore the possessor should investigate. See there. One should not ask that if the case involves only a handwritten document it is ineffective in any event, because the possessor can say, "Perhaps you received this handwritten document only now after the three years and antedated it." The case can be one where the handwriting was authenticated by witnesses who confirmed the signature. Witnesses authenticating a signature need not read the document and therefore may have no knowledge of the protest or transaction during the three years. Beit Yosef in siman 250 cites Rashba concerning a claimant who says he did not protest because he had secretly given the land as a gift and did not know of the gift until now. For example, Reuven gave Shimon a house by deed, deposited the deed with a third party, and Shimon did not know of it. Reuven later died, and the heirs claim that their father Reuven possessed the house for years after the date of the deed and perhaps repurchased it from Shimon. Rashba rules that because Shimon did not know of the gift, Reuven's possession does not establish chazakah. This applies specifically when the heirs admit the relevant facts. If the heirs do not know, Shimon the claimant must prove the ones, namely that the deed did not reach him until now.
