What wording constitutes a protest? The owner says before two people, 'So-and-so, who is using my courtyard or field, is a robber, and I will sue him in the future.' {Rama: Some say he need not say, 'I will sue him.'} Similarly, if he says, 'The property is rented or mortgaged to him. If he claims that I sold or gave it to him, I will sue him,' or makes any comparable statement, it constitutes a protest. But if he says only, 'So-and-so who is using my courtyard is a robber,' this is not a protest. The possessor may say, 'When I heard this, I assumed he was merely insulting me, and I therefore did not safeguard my deed.' {Rama: Only the protest of a claimant publicly known to have owned the land is effective. If the land was secretly given to this claimant and his ownership never became known, his protest has no effect, because the possessor may say, 'I disregarded his protest because I did not know the land belonged to him.'}
