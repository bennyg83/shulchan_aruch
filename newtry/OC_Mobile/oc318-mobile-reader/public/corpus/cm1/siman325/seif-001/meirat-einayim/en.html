For a few years. Meaning fewer than seven years, because flax greatly weakens the soil, and in fewer than seven years the land does not return to the strength it had at the outset.
Sycamore beams. A sycamore is a non-fruit-bearing tree whose branches are cut for lumber. In fewer than seven years it does not return to the condition it had when the tenant received it. The Tur concludes: even regarding the enhancement by which the branches increased while the field was in his possession, he receives nothing. Meaning, we do not appraise the thickening of the branches and beams during the period he held the field and pay him the value of that enhancement when he leaves.
And not the enhancement of the trees that grew, etc.; and some disagree, etc. The Tur writes: if one accepted a field for cultivation and trees grew there on their own, then if they arose in a place unsuitable for sowing, such as on the boundary, he has no share in them. If they arose in a place suitable for sowing and he says that he prefers the trees and that, had they not grown on their own, he would have planted them, the trees are appraised as though he planted them and he is paid their value when he leaves the field. Although this siman concerns a sharecropper, who ordinarily may not change the crop, one can say that the trees do not weaken the land more than the crop he was supposed to sow, or that the case is in Babylonia where, according to the dissenting view above, such a change is permitted. If he says that he would have preferred sowing the place occupied by the trees, we inspect the trees. If they are fit to be uprooted and replanted elsewhere, the owner must pay him the value of seedlings standing ready for planting, which is less than the value of an established tree. It is not enough to pay him merely the value of the seed he would have sown, because he may say that he would have planted a valuable crop such as saffron in the place of the tree. If the trees cannot be replanted, the owner pays him only their value as wood. End of the Tur's words. This explains the Mechaber's ruling and the dissenting view cited by Rama.
He sowed it in the first year, etc. Meaning, the first year after he entered the field. The Tur explains more fully: whether he entered at the end of the seven years following the previous cutting, or he entered in the third or fourth year, he cuts the sycamore when the full seven-year cycle is completed, because by the time he leaves the field the trees will regrow to the condition in which they were when he entered. End of his words.
