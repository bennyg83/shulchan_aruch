a) The language of the Mishnah, Bava Metzia 109a, and the Rambam, chapter 8 of Hilkhot Sekhirut, law 3, who writes 'the renter or receiver.' The Maggid Mishneh derives this from Rav Pappa's statement regarding the first two Mishnayot, Bava Metzia 104a. Rashi explains that the case is fixed-rent tenancy, because under sharecropping what concern is there over what he plants when the owners share in the produce? The Tur likewise writes this. The Ramban challenges Rashi and explains the opposite, because the owner may say that he needs grain specifically. The Rambam applies the Mishnah both to fixed-rent tenancy and sharecropping, which is somewhat difficult. Perhaps he follows the Rashba, who writes that Rav Pappa was not speaking of the substantive law in every case, but only that some rulings needed to be taught specifically in one form of tenancy even though the law itself does not change. The Mechaber did not choose either position, but copied the wording of the Mishnah. 'Less than seven years' means that flax weakens the soil and its damage remains evident for six years, and likewise a sycamore that is cut does not regrow into usable beams until seven years.
