One who sells a courtyard, etc. The term "courtyard" includes everything built within it and designated for use by the residents of the courtyard, excluding a bathhouse and olive press, which have independent names. Many courtyards contain neither a bathhouse nor an olive press. The Tur and Mechaber in the previous siman, seifim 4 and 5, cite differing opinions whether one who sells a courtyard acquires its airspace, while here it appears obvious that he does, since he even acquires all the houses, cistern, reservoir, and other listed items. In the Perishah I wrote that the earlier case concerns one who did not simply say, "I sell you the courtyard," but rather, "I sell you such-and-such an area of land for a courtyard." In that case reason suggests that only what is expressly specified is included. See the Derishah for further discussion.
The outer and inner houses. In the Perishah I wrote that Ran explains "inner houses" as those with no opening directly to the courtyard and whose access to the courtyard passes through the outer house into which they open.
And the shops that open into it. Meaning, shops designated for the residents of the courtyard to purchase their needs.
That are not included in the sale of a house. Meaning, the movable items that are not ordinarily included in a sale of the house, as explained in the previous siman, are likewise not included automatically in the sale of the courtyard.
Except wheat and barley. Those are foodstuffs intended for his sustenance, which a person ordinarily takes with him wherever he goes.
He sold everything. As we said above, etc. Meaning, above in siman 214, seif 2, Rama concludes in the gloss: "Some disagree if he wrote, 'I have retained nothing,' etc." Here too the case is one where he marked the boundaries and also said, "everything within them." This gloss refers to what the Mechaber wrote immediately before. Saying "everything within them" has the same effect as writing "I have retained nothing in this sale." Nimukei Yosef writes this. Rama likewise writes it in the gloss to siman 214, seif 11. See what I wrote there further.
Some disagree regarding a cistern and reservoir, and the same applies here. Meaning, just as they disagree there regarding a cistern and reservoir, as the Mechaber writes in siman 214, seif 2, so they disagree here regarding a bathhouse and olive press. But with respect to the cistern and reservoir themselves it is obvious that they are sold with an ordinary courtyard, as the Tur and Mechaber state plainly at the beginning of this seif; this is an explicit Mishnah and Gemara. See what I wrote in the previous siman, seif 2, that if he marks the boundaries and writes that he sells everything within them, it appears everyone agrees that everything is sold. This language is stronger than writing, "I have retained nothing in this sale," because in the latter case one can say that the cistern and reservoir in a house sale, or bathhouse and olive press in a courtyard sale, are not included in "this sale" at all and do not fall within the ordinary category of a house or courtyard. By contrast, when he writes explicitly "everything within the boundaries," the language includes them. It is possible that saying "everything within the boundaries" is even stronger than saying, "I sell you the courtyard and everything within it," followed by marking the boundaries. This requires study.
