The houses. This is not comparable to what is stated in siman 214:4 and 5, where there are differing views whether one who sells a courtyard transfers the airspace. Here it appears obvious that he does, since even all the houses, the pit and cistern, and the other items listed are acquired. There, however, the case is not where he sold him a courtyard without qualification, but where he said, 'I sell you a tract of land of such-and-such dimensions for a courtyard.' In that case it is reasonable that only what is expressly specified is included. Sema. Taz writes that there the discussion concerns the airspace above, in which the seller retains the right to build higher while leaving the courtyard its necessary airspace. Here, by contrast, the purchaser acquires what lies within the courtyard's walls, both the houses and the airspace, but not the airspace beyond the courtyard walls.
