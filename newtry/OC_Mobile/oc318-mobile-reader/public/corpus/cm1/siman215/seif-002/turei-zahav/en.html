(Seif 2.) The large stone. The Beit Yosef raised several objections against Rabbeinu here, as explained in his comments. It appears to me that Rabbeinu listed the implements in the order of their use. First the olives are crushed in the crushing apparatus, afterward they are placed in the millstones, and then in the olive press. He therefore first mentioned the large stone, which is the meaning of the term used for the crushing apparatus in the Gemara. Although Rabbeinu did not expressly mention the crushing apparatus, it is included in the stone upon which he said the olives are ground, for initially they are crushed with that apparatus. He then mentioned the basin. This is also the “basin” listed in the Mishna and the “press-vats” listed in the baraita, for they are one and the same, unlike the Rambam who treated them as separate items. This resolves the Beit Yosef's question why Rabbeinu did not mention the crushing apparatuses. The Beit Yosef also asked why Rabbeinu did not mention the press-beams, meaning the objects used to press the olives, and answered that they are included in Rabbeinu's reference to the upper millstone. This is difficult, for there is a major distinction between them. The baraita in the Gemara teaches that if the seller said, “it and everything within it,” all the implements are sold with respect to the upper millstone, but that wording does not suffice for the pressing implements. Although according to the tanna of the Mishna it does suffice, we nevertheless see that the two are distinct. It appears to me that Rabbeinu had no need to mention the pressing implements, because their law follows by precise inference. Since he expressly stated that the boards, meaning the walls placed around the press, are sold, the status of the other implements follows automatically. The baraita, by contrast, first stated only in general that the boards were sold, and therefore had to clarify afterward that the pressing implements were not sold, as the Rashbam explains. The Tur, however, first expressly identified the boards that are sold. From the affirmative one therefore hears the negative; otherwise he could simply have written “boards” without specification. The Beit Yosef also asked why Rabbeinu did not state that the wheel is not sold. It appears that this follows by an a fortiori inference from the beam. Both are detached implements, and the wheel is subordinate to the beam. Since the buyer does not acquire the beam, he certainly does not acquire the wheel. The Mishna listed it only in the ordinary sequence of the implements. Rabbeinu likewise did not state that the phrase “it and everything within it” is effective for the pressing implements, because this is included in his statement that everything is sold except the liquids, and the like. It follows that all remaining implements are sold. This, it appears to me, is Rabbeinu's intent.
