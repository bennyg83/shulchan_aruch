One who sells an olive press, etc. The Mechaber copies Rambam's wording. The Tur's wording differs in some details, following Rashbam's explanation, and in the Derishah I wrote that the Tur's version is more correct. See there.
And they are the mefarech, etc. Meaning, these are the items called "mefarkheset" in the Gemara, and this is the wording in Maimoniyot. This is unlike the Levush, who writes "and the mefarkhot," implying that these are additional utensils. He followed Beit Yosef, who wondered why the Tur did not also mention the mefarkhot. I already wrote in the Derishah that there is no difficulty. The vessels into which crushed olives are placed are called mefarkhot because crushed olives are placed inside them.
These are sold. Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe also express surprise at Ramban's statement that whether in one case or the other they are not sold. This is unlike the Levush, who simply follows the Mechaber and omits Rama's gloss that some say these items are sold and that Rama considers that the primary view.
The sacks. These are made from goat hair, while martzofim are likewise sacks but made of leather.
If he said to him, "the olive press," etc. The phrase "all are sold" appears to refer also to the sacks and leather bags. Rambam and Beit Yosef say this explicitly. In the Perishah I wrote that the Tur should be emended accordingly. See what I wrote in the Derishah concerning this.
Upon which they spread olives, etc. Meaning, even though these shops are also used to sell wine and bread, which is why they are called shops.
If he marked the outer boundaries. The Gemara and Tur state that two elements are required: he must mark the boundaries and say, "I sell you the olive press and all its appurtenances." See there. The Mechaber agrees; this refers back to what he wrote immediately before.
