In a sale. The Sema explains: Although we say that a seller sells generously, that principle means, for example, when he sells a house while retaining the pit and cistern for himself, that he does not also retain a path to them. Since he expressly sold the house, we say he sold that house in full. But the pit, cistern, and roof were not sold because they are not included in the designation 'house.' Since we presume the seller sold only because he needed money, whatever he sold he sold, and whatever he did not sell remains his. The burden of proof rests upon the claimant. See Responsa Mabit, part 2, simanim 46 and 66.
