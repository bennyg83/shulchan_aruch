When does this apply? In a sale, etc. The reason is that although we say a seller sells generously, that principle applies, for example, where he sells a house but retains the cistern and reservoir. We say he did not retain even a right of passage to the cistern and reservoir, because once he expressly sold the house, he sold that house in full. But the cistern, reservoir, and roof are not included in the term "house," and therefore we say he did not sell them. Since ordinarily he sells only because monetary need compelled him, what he sold is sold and what he did not sell remains his; the burden of proof rests upon the claimant.
But one who gives a gift. A donor is under no financial pressure forcing him to give. Since he gives generously and willingly, we say that he gives everything connected to and fixed within the property. Nevertheless, matters outside it, such as a side structure, inner room, and the like, are not included if he did not mention them at all, as Rama writes.
The general rule is that one who gives land. Specifically regarding land is there a distinction between sale and gift for this purpose, not regarding movable property, as the Tur and Rama write below at the end of siman 220. See there.
Everything attached to it, etc. Rashbam writes that if money or detached grain that does not need the land is present, or any item so plainly outside the meaning of a house, courtyard, or field that the Mishnah did not need to mention that it is excluded from a sale, the recipient of a gift certainly does not acquire it either. The Mechaber agrees. From this we can infer that whenever we say that language such as "everything within it is sold to you" includes even detached property, all the more so such detached property is included in an outright gift. This is clear.
