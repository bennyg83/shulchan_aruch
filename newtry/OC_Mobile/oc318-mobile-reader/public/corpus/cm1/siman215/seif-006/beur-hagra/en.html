(Collected note) Because, etc. According to Rabbeinu Chananel as cited by Rashbam there, s.v. “this, etc.” (End of note.)
Whether, etc. Meaning that the Mishnah’s phrase “When does this apply?” refers to the entire chapter.
(Collected note) Whether, etc. Meaning all the laws in chapter 4, which are the rules codified in this siman. Rashbam writes likewise on 73a, s.v. “saddlebags, etc.” See the end of siman 220. (End of note.)
(Collected note) Everything attached, etc. Rashbam likewise writes on 71a, s.v. “but, etc.” Beer HaGolah seif katan 90 writes that even grain still needing the ground is included. Rashbam, however, says specifically grain that no longer needs the ground, and his words are compelling because the entire chapter concerns that category. Ramban later writes similarly that perhaps a gift is stronger than a sale only for things attached to the land within it, while detached items such as stones not needed by the land and harvested grain are the same in both. This also appears to be the Rambam’s view. The Gra nevertheless finds Rashbam’s view strongest from 79b, where according to the first Tanna consecration includes even water, although a sale of a pit does not include its water. Rashbam explains there that the Tanna follows his father, etc. A gift is comparable to consecration. (End of note.)
(Collected note) Seif 5, see Beer HaGolah seif katan 90. As explained above, Rashbam disagrees, and Ramban supports him from 79b. According to the first Tanna the water is consecrated even though the case concerns collected water, which is detached. See Rashbam there. (End of note.)
Specifically, etc. See Tosafos there, s.v. “the brothers.” Rabbeinu Meir answers that a right of way, because it lies outside the property itself, follows the same rule in a sale and in the other cases. Ramban cites the Yerushalmi as support. Rabbi Elazar asks there whether the qualification “When does this apply?” was stated concerning the entire Mishnah or only its first rule, meaning whether it also refers to the right of way. The Yerushalmi resolves from the case of brothers who divided property and retain rights of way against one another, supporting Rabbeinu Meir. Another version derives the same point from one who takes possession of a convert’s property. Since acquiring the field of a deceased convert certainly does not automatically acquire a right of way lying outside it, the same is true for the other cases, including a gift according to the Sages.
(Collected note) Specifically, etc. See Rashbam there, s.v. “he acquired all of it, etc.” Although Shmuel disagrees in one case because he follows Rabbi Akiva, all agree that brothers who divide have the status of purchasers. The Yerushalmi cited by Ramban likewise resolves the question concerning the right of way from brothers who divided and from acquisition of a convert’s property. (End of note.)
(Collected note) Specifically, etc. Ramban writes that one cannot challenge this from 65a, where when both properties leave the seller’s possession we say that he favored one recipient more generously. Rashbam’s reason there is that the seller cannot say “I will dwell, etc.” His explanation follows his textual reading. Rabbeinu Chananel and Tosafos read the passage differently and treat the cases as following their respective principles, with an additional necessity for each. Even according to Rashbam, one would otherwise have expected the reverse, that brothers’ division resembles a gift. (End of note.)
