One was higher than the other. Rama abbreviates the rule stated by Nimukei Yosef at the beginning of HaBayit VeHaAliyah. Where one party's structure was larger, he receives proportionately more of the broken stones. This is not treated like an ordinary case of doubtful money divided equally, because the uncertainty is not equal. If the upper story contained more stones, then even where the collapse came by impact and upper stones were more likely to break, there would still be more intact stones from the upper portion simply because it contained more stones. One therefore first calculates what amount of breakage is attributable with certainty to each side according to size and the manner of collapse. The residual uncertainty, where the probabilities are equal, is divided equally. Thus the rule does not assume that all the broken stones came from one level, but only that one level is statistically more likely to contribute more breakage. The gloss printed in Nimukei Yosef implying that in one type of fall all the broken stones belonged to one party appears mistaken.
They divide the stones. See Sema, seif katan 10. Even if the stones lie in the domain of one party, they are divided according to the rule of the Mishnah. The Gemara indicates that this is true even where both parties make definite claims and even if the stones lie in the public domain. One should not invoke Tosafot's rule that where neither party is in possession the stronger party prevails. Here there is a genuine monetary uncertainty that exists independently of their claims. In such a case the rule is division even without joint possession. It also makes no difference that the division may not correspond to the actual historical truth. In cases of genuine uncertainty, such as the exchange of a cow for a donkey, division may be ordered even where the object could not actually have belonged half to each. As for the explanation that partners are not particular with one another's use of place and it is as though each lent the other space, that differs from an ordinary deposit. In an ordinary deposit, even if a place was lent, the depositor's property remains identified as his. Among partners, the license to use the shared place continues throughout the partnership and is broader, like a rental for a period, which has the quality of a temporary sale.
