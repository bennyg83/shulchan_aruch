He compels him. This means by placing him under cherem or by descending to his property. Only in seif 5 below, where the entire house and upper story fell, can the lower owner not be compelled to rebuild. Here, by contrast, the structure has not fallen; only the walls have deteriorated and require repair. Sema. One may answer, based on what Nimukei Yosef writes in Rambam's name, that seif 5 deals with a case where the lower owner traveled overseas. See the Beit Yosef, Maggid Mishneh, and Kesef Mishneh. He wishes. The Sema writes that the matter is made dependent on the upper owner's wishes because the owner of the house certainly does not object if the upper-story walls remain unrepaired. On the contrary, he prefers their deterioration so that the upper owner cannot live there and burden him. If there is danger that the ceiling of the lower house may collapse because of the weight of the upper-story structure and break, it appears to me that the upper owner is obligated to repair it. Taz. The ruling therefore teaches only that the upper owner cannot compel the house owner to repair the upper walls for him, or even to build them jointly with him. If he wishes, the upper owner must repair them himself. Sema.
To repair it. The Tur concludes with the following reason: We presume that when the upper owner acquired or entered the upper story, he did so with the understanding that the lower owner would not repair its ceiling, since the lower owner is unconcerned about that ceiling because the roof protects him. Even if the upper owner wishes to remove the roof so that the house owner will be forced to repair the ceiling, we prevent him, because the roof is also pledged to the house owner. Even when there is a ceiling, everyone knows that if rain falls directly on it, the water will pass through into the house. Their original division was therefore made on the understanding that the upper owner would maintain his roof so that rainwater would not descend to the lower owner. But the lower owner has no obligation at all to provide support for the upper owner. The Tur's reasoning explains Rama's gloss.
