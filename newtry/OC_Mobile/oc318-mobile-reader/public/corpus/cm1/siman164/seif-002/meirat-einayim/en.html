The house beams sagged and descended into the airspace of the house; if they descended within ten tefachim, etc. This is Rambam's wording and shows that the upper and lower walls remain as before, while only the ceiling sagged downward. When he writes that the lower owner demolishes and rebuilds, Rambam follows his view that the lower house owner is responsible for the ceiling. The Tur says that where the entire ceiling sank downward and reached within ten tefachim of the lower floor, both parties must rebuild. It appears that he too speaks where the walls remain standing and only the ceiling descended, since he mentions no change in the walls. His requirement that both rebuild follows his earlier view that ordinarily the upper owner repairs the ceiling. Here, because the lower dwelling has become unusable, the lower owner must assist. Rama, who says the ceiling sank because the house walls settled into the ground, follows Rashi's explanation of the Gemara. It is somewhat difficult that he does not mention that the same law applies if the ceiling alone sags, especially after citing the Tur's approach in the preceding gloss.
Within ten tefachim, etc. Within ten tefachim the lower space cannot be used at all, and the lower owner can tell the upper owner, 'You have no right to drive me out of my domain.'
He demolishes and rebuilds, etc. Maggid Mishneh explains Rambam's wording: the lower house owner rebuilds the ceiling, and the upper owner provides the plastering, in accordance with the allocation stated above.
The owner of the upper story can prevent him. The Tur writes that even if the lower owner says, 'I will rebuild everything at my own expense and provide you another place to live until completion,' the upper owner can say, 'I do not wish to trouble myself by moving.'
