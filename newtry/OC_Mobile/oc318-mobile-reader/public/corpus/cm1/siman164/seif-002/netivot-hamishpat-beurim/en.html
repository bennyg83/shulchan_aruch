The beams of the house sagged. See Sema, seif katan 5. In my view Tur speaks specifically where the walls themselves sank and thereby lowered the ceiling. If only the ceiling sagged, with no defect in the walls, Tur would place the repair on the upper owner. Since the ceiling has descended into the lower owner's space below ten handbreadths, the lower owner may remove it from his domain, and the upper owner must then restore it, as in seif 1. But where the walls sank into the ground, the cost of raising them falls on both. The defect is in walls that serve both, and the lower owner can say to the upper owner, "You are now occupying space that is not yours and must help raise yourself back to the proper level." Since the defect harms both, the expense is shared.
