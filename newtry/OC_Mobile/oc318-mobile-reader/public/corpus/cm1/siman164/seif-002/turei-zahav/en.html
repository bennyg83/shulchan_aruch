(See there in the gloss: Some say that the homeowner has no obligation, etc.) The Sema, seif katan 4, attempts to reconcile two statements of Rashi. Yet one may ask: if the owner of the lower story has no servitude to the owner of the upper story, beyond the obligation to construct the ceiling for his own benefit, why may he not alter the ceiling as he wishes? One may answer that at the outset they obligated themselves to maintain this arrangement. The lower owner will certainly repair what he needs, and presumably must restore it in its existing form. I am nevertheless greatly troubled by the Rosh’s discussion in the second chapter of Bava Metzia. The Rosh asks how the rule that the upper owner must repair the ceiling can be reconciled with the law that when the lower owner wishes to replace a cedar roof with sycamore beams, the upper owner must accept the change, which is the law stated by the Mechaber in seif 6 and implies that the lower owner bears responsibility for the ceiling. The Rosh answers that the latter case concerns a lower owner who also has another upper story, meaning two upper stories one above the other. But why was this difficult? The case of changing the roofing concerns a collapsed building, as Rashi explains on 114. This requires extensive study. The Ran understands Rashi to mean that among partners, both the ceiling and the plastered floor above it are the lower owner’s responsibility, unlike the view of the Rosh and the Tur in their explanation of Rashi.
