Until he gives him all of his expenditures. See Panim Meirot, part I, siman 62. Two partners purchased a ruin. One spent several hundred from his own funds repairing and building it into a habitable house. At the beginning they had agreed that each partner would occupy it for three years at a time, with the partner who supervised the construction receiving the first three years. In practice he remained for five consecutive years. The other partner demanded rent for the two extra years, while the occupant argued that since he alone had advanced the construction costs, he could remain without rent until reimbursed. Panim Meirot understands the original agreement as granting priority because of the labor and lost time involved in supervising the building, not as an agreement that one partner alone should fund the construction. Ordinarily each was to bear half the expense. The case therefore resembles the Mishna in Bava Metzia 117 concerning a house and upper story that collapsed: where the owner of the lower structure refuses to rebuild, the upper owner may rebuild the lower structure and occupy it without rent until reimbursed. Here too, since they bought the ruin to build it and there was no practical division by which each could build only on his own part, each partner's share was subject to the other's right that the common property be made usable. The partner who advanced all the construction costs may therefore occupy the house without rent until the other pays his share. Once reimbursement is made, however, the second partner is entitled only to his original three-year occupancy, not to an additional number of years equal to however long the first partner remained. His right to his three years was merely suspended while the first partner's reimbursement claim prevented enjoyment of the property. Even if the first partner remained ten years, once reimbursed the second receives his agreed three-year term, after which the rotation continues as originally arranged.
