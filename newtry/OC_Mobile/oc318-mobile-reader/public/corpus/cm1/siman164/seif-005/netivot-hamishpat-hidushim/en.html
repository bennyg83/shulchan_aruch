The owner of the upper story builds. The house owner cannot be compelled to rebuild because he can live below without the upper story. See the Beurim. The same applies in reverse: if the house owner wishes to rebuild but the upper owner does not wish to rebuild the upper story and roof, the upper owner cannot be compelled. The house owner may build the house together with an upper enclosure and roof and live there until the upper owner reimburses him:
And he lives in it. This is not considered interest, because the land is also encumbered to the upper story, and the house owner would not otherwise be living there. Sema. See the Beurim. He may likewise rent it to others, or even rent it to the house owner himself and charge rent, without a concern of interest:
One third of the land, because an upper story reduces the durability of the house by one third. If a house without an upper story would stand for ninety years, with an upper story it stands only sixty. Sema:
With the consent of both. The house owner cannot force a sale against the upper owner's will and simply give him one third, because the upper owner can say, "I prefer land itself." If the house owner wishes to sell subject to a condition that the purchaser rebuild the upper story, he may do so, for it makes no difference whether the upper story stands over this person's house or another's.
