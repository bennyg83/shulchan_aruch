And concerning some of them he says, "I do not know." See Sema, seif katan 11, through his discussion of the rule that one who is obligated to swear but cannot swear must pay. It must therefore be speaking where the item concerning which he admits negligence was broken, or where he originally took it by way of robbery, so that it is not a case of "here it is," as I wrote above in siman 87. Otherwise it would indeed be "here it is," and the rule that one who cannot swear must pay would not apply.
And the second party takes a corresponding quantity of whole stones. In my humble opinion, this appears to be the explanation of the Yerushalmi at the beginning of HaBayit VeHaAliyah: Rabbi Hoshaya said, "This teaches that possession controls from one side." Meaning, there is a possessor whom we judge only as possessing from one aspect, not as a complete possessor, for the other party receives whole stones corresponding to what he takes. We do not say that he is a complete possessor and that they should divide the remainder. The commentator there gives a strained explanation; see there.
