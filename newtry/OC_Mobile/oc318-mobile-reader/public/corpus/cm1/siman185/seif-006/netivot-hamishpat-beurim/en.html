The seller said, "I did not wish to sell at this price." This case necessarily concerns one where the agent was overreached. If there was no ona'ah, the seller cannot simply say, "I do not wish to sell at this price." Siman 182 establishes that where the agent sells without ona'ah, the principal cannot void the sale merely because the agent failed to obtain the hoped-for amount. Beit Yosef explains that the present case assumes the principal authorized the agent to sell. It is strained to distinguish by saying that siman 182 concerns a principal who said, "Sell at its market value," while here he said only, "Sell," intending that the agent should negotiate but leave the final price for the principal's own approval. If that were the case, the agent would never have been appointed with power to finalize a price at all. The sale would then be ineffective even where the agency was undisclosed, and the buyer too could retract. Yet Beit Yosef, cited by Sema, writes that the buyer cannot retract. Rather, the case is one where the agent was actually overreached and the principal had said, "Sell for whatever you can obtain." Even then, where the agent disclosed the agency, the sale can be void because the principal's broad instruction is not treated as authorizing a sale under ona'ah. It is not equivalent to saying explicitly, "Whether for improvement or detriment." Raavad disagrees and holds that such an instruction does include authority to accept even a disadvantageous price.
The buyer returns it. See Sema, seif katan 15, who writes that although the seller can retract, the buyer cannot retract. This proves that even where the agent was actually overreached and the principal may void the transaction because the agent lacked authority to impair, the buyer cannot argue: "Since the principal may void it, the agent must have had no authority to sell, making this equivalent to a sale of another person's property, from which I too may withdraw." The case necessarily involves ona'ah, as explained above, and nevertheless Beit Yosef rules that the buyer cannot retract. The buyer's acquisition therefore remains binding unless and until the principal exercises the special right to void because of the agent's unauthorized impairment.
