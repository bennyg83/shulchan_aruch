Knew. He must know specifically at the time of sale that the object belongs to Reuven. This also appears from siman 182, seif 2, which states, specifically where he informed him that it belongs to so-and-so. The reason is that because the buyer knows the principal, he must return the item, since he should have considered that the principal might object. If he does not know who the principal is, however, then even if the seller tells him that he is an agent, the buyer can say, I assumed that because you did not identify the principal, the principal certainly did not object and would ratify your act. This also appears from the Rosh in the chapter Get Pashut, where the essential requirement is to inform him that he is specifically the agent of a named person, and similarly from the Ran in the chapter HaIsh Mekadesh. He must return it. Although the agent contradicts the seller and says that the principal instructed him to sell it for fifty, the agent is not treated as a single witness because he has a personal interest in the matter. The seller's claim is therefore definite and the opposing claim uncertain, and an uncertainty does not overcome a certainty. Sema. The Shach writes that the ruling in siman 182, seif 4, that the burden of proof rests upon the claimant, presents no difficulty. Here the specific object is clearly the principal's property and the buyer knew this, so he must return it. There, however, the principal is the buyer and seeks to recover money from the seller, so the burden of proof rests upon the claimant. This is unlike the strained resolution of the Sema. See there. The Bach makes a different distinction and therefore rules like the Rambam, but his conclusion does not appear correct to me, for his words are strained.
