I swear. If: or The priest and the confession:
And if he knew. The Yerushalmi there, cited by Tosafot, s.v. almanah, distinguishes whether he knew. This is as stated above, siman 182, seif 2.
(Supplement) And if he knew, etc. If he did not know, the sale remains valid, as stated above, siman 182, seif 2, regarding a seller and a broker. If he knew, the sale is invalid, as stated there: if it was known that the agent violated his instructions, the transaction would be reversed. Here the seller makes a definite claim, while the purchaser does not know. This resembles the view in Bava Batra that the seller may say, 'You are not my litigant,' etc. The purchaser can impose only a ban, not an oath. The broker cannot testify in support of the purchaser, because if the sale remains valid the seller's claim would then lie against the broker, who would be required to swear. One who is subject to an oath concerning the matter cannot testify, as stated in Kiddushin 43b. (End of supplement.)
