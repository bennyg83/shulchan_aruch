The broker is liable. Rashba writes that he is a paid bailee, and a paid bailee is liable for matters approaching unavoidable accident so long as they are not a complete unavoidable accident. Thus we say in chapter HaPoalim that even if he entered the city at the time people ordinarily enter and slept at the time people ordinarily sleep, he is liable, etc. The Tur and the Mechaber cite this below in siman 303 in the laws of a paid bailee; see there.
A matchmaker has the status of a broker, etc. See what Rama further writes concerning matchmaking above in siman 87 and below in siman 264.
If they retracted, etc. The Levush questions this and writes: Is it not our custom to write a penalty in all engagement conditions, and the practice is that the party who performs receives the penalty from the party who breaches? The matchmaker brought about the match, and based upon his efforts the conditions were written and the parties bound themselves with a penalty. His brokerage therefore produced a benefit, since one side receives the penalty. Why should he lose his fee entirely? Even if the parties later retracted and waived claims against each other, the matchmaker can say, "I do not wish to waive my share." See the Levush for what he writes further. The complaint, however, is not against Rama but against the rulings of Mahari, who writes this explicitly in siman 85: "Ordinarily, meaning where they did not expressly stipulate that the fee was due when the parties became reconciled, in our regions it is not customary to collect any matchmaking fee unless the marriage is completed. In Upper Galilee they customarily collect it immediately after the penalty has been fixed, unlike in our regions." Since he concludes that in Upper Galilee they collect immediately after fixing the penalty, unlike in our regions, it follows that Mahari holds that even in a place where the parties make a penalty clause, we nevertheless follow the local custom not to collect the matchmaking fee until after the marriage. It is also clear from his words that where there is no established custom, unless the matchmaker expressly stipulated that he be paid immediately when the parties became reconciled, he has no right to demand the matchmaking fee until after the marriage, and consequently if they retract he collects nothing. The Levush's objection is also not compelling. Tosafos and the poskim write that the penalty is collected because the young woman or young man and their family have been embarrassed, and they would have preferred that the match never have been made rather than receive the penalty. Thus the matchmaker has not truly produced a profit through collection of the penalty, since whatever financial gain resulted is offset by the loss and embarrassment that his arrangement caused. Examine this carefully. Parenthetical gloss: Hagahot Mordechai to Kiddushin writes that hiring a worker does not require a kinyan, and see above at the beginning of siman 182. Therefore, if Reuven says to Shimon, "I will give you two gold coins if you speak on my behalf to the official," he must keep his word. Reuven's claim that he promised only out of fear that Shimon might otherwise damage his interests with the official is merely an unexpressed intention, and unexpressed intentions have no legal effect. Darkei Moshe cites this at the end of this siman.
