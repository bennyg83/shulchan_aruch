And it was canceled. The Levush questions this ruling. Our custom is to include a penalty in every set of engagement conditions and to collect it from the party who breaches them. The matchmaker has therefore produced a benefit, since the other party receives the penalty. Why should the matchmaker be entirely deprived of his fee? Even if the parties later mutually waive the penalty, the matchmaker can say, I do not waive my share. The Sema answers that the recipient has not actually profited from collecting the penalty, because, as Tosafot and the poskim explain, the penalty is compensation for humiliation. His gain is therefore offset by his loss. See there. Maharshal writes that an ordinary matchmaker is deemed to have stipulated that his fee depends upon the match continuing until the binding kinyan for which the penalty is made. Nevertheless, if one party breaches and the other cannot collect the penalty and thereby suffers a loss, the matchmaker must return to that party his share of the matchmaking fee. See there. Editor's note: The Taz does not write this. It appears obvious that even if both parties agree to dissolve the match, the matchmaker does not lose his fee in a place where the fee is collected after the engagement conditions are signed. This is the language of the Taz. Regarding a broker in a comparable case, where the sale was completed and the parties later mutually waived their rights, see Responsa Shaar Ephraim, siman 150, and Bnei Chayei to the Beit Yosef, gloss 3.
