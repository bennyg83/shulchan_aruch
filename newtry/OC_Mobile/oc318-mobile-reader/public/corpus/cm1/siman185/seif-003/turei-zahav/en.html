(Seif 10, in the gloss: and in a place where there is no custom.) Maharshal writes regarding these words of Mahari Chayun: in my view, ordinary matchmaking is as though the parties stipulated that the matchmaker's fee becomes due once the penalized engagement conditions are executed, because the matter has become publicly established and it is as though he completed his work. Nevertheless, if one party later breaches the penalty and is too powerful for the other party to collect it from him, the matchmaker is not penalized on that account. This is the end of his words. It appears obvious that even if both parties mutually agree to cancel the match after the engagement conditions were written, the matchmaker does not lose his fee in a place where matchmaking fees are collected after the writing of the conditions.
