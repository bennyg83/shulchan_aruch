And afterward he sold it to Levi. If a broker completed a transaction and another broker then caused it to be undone so that he himself could broker the replacement transaction and collect the fee from both sides, the second broker may be liable to the first. Once the first transaction was effectively concluded, destroying the first broker's earned right can resemble burning another person's promissory note, a form of direct consequential damage. Perhaps the second broker is liable even for the fee that the first broker would have received from the opposite side, although that side never actually paid because the transaction was not ultimately performed and instead paid the second broker. That too may be grama of the more direct type known as garmi rather than mere remote causation. If the second broker's ongoing relationship with the party who changed the deal enabled him deliberately to induce cancellation so that he could replace the first broker, the case may also resemble the law of one who interferes with another person's established fishing opportunity. The authorities cited in Mishnah Berurah concerning post-Pesach redemption of chametz contain several considerations that may support a claim by the first broker against the second.
