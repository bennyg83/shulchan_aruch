The broker is an agent. The Shach cites the Gemara concerning one who says, "Take this get to my wife," and the wife says, "Be my agent to receive it," where there is doubt whether the original delivery agent can become her receiving agent because he cannot first return and notify the husband. The Shach is therefore uncertain in a monetary case where one gives an item to another to sell and a prospective purchaser says to that same intermediary, "Be my agent to acquire it from him," whether the intermediary can acquire on behalf of the purchaser. See Beurim: in monetary law it appears that once the intermediary tells the purchaser, "Go, pull and acquire," his original agency to sell has been completed. The purchaser can then appoint that intermediary as his own agent to perform the acquisition. This differs from a get, where saying, "Take your get from the ground," is invalid and the husband's agency is not complete until actual delivery into the wife's hand. Therefore one person cannot simultaneously remain the husband's giving agent and become the wife's receiving agent.
