The broker is an agent, etc. The Gemara in HaMekabel, 63b, discusses one who says, 'Bring this bill of divorce to my wife,' and the wife says to the messenger, 'Become my agent to receive it.' It remains doubtful whether he may become her agent because he did not return and inform the original principal. All the authorities rule accordingly, as explained in Even HaEzer, siman 141. Although this generally has no monetary consequence regarding an instruction to deliver property to another, because delivery of a loan or deposit is equivalent to acquisition for the recipient even without the debtor's or recipient's appointment of the messenger, while delivery of a gift is not equivalent to acquisition even if the recipient appointed the messenger, as explained in siman 125, there is nevertheless a practical consequence. If one gave an article to a person to sell and another person said to that intermediary, 'Become my agent to purchase it from him,' it is uncertain whether the intermediary's acquisition is effective. This is therefore an unresolved legal doubt, and the burden of proof rests upon the claimant. See the beginning of siman 185 in the Tur, the responsum of the Rosh, and Beit Yosef there. See also Responsa R. Shlomo Kohen, part II, siman 46, though the matter requires study, and Responsa R. Moshe Alshich, siman 19, p. 72.
