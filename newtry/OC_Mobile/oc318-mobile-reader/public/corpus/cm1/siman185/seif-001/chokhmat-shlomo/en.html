Seif 1. Note: The Shach writes that just as in the laws of gittin agency fails where the agency does not return to the principal, so too a comparable defect invalidates agency in sales. I heard that Torat Gittin and Netivot HaMishpat disagree, arguing that the special defect of “take your get from the ground” applies only to a get. I have not seen those works themselves, but in my opinion the Shach is correct. In ordinary property law, “take this object from the ground” can work because the object remains owned by the transferor until the recipient acquires it. At the moment of acquisition there is therefore an owner actively conferring title. But where the seller's agent is supposed first to cease being the seller's agent and then become the buyer's agent, there is an unavoidable dilemma. If he remains the seller's agent until the instant of acquisition, the agency has not returned to the principal and is defective. If his agency for the seller already ended before he became the buyer's agent, then at the instant of acquisition there is no one with authority to confer title. The seller is unaware of the transfer and the former agent no longer represents him. The property is not ownerless, so acquisition cannot occur without a competent transferor. Thus the Shach's ruling is correct. Ibid. I was asked about Reuven, who gave an object to Shimon to sell and expressly instructed him to sell only for cash. Shimon ordinarily takes brokerage fees, but because of friendship with Reuven would probably not charge him. Shimon sold the object, took only a deposit, and before the balance was paid the purchaser fled. The agent must pay. First, an agent who deviates from the principal's instructions is negligent and liable even if acting without pay. Second, here he is in law a paid custodian, because no agreement was made that he would act gratuitously. Even a friend who performs professional work may demand payment unless he expressly waives it, and the fact that he could have demanded brokerage renders him a paid custodian. Cases in which a mere gift is required to make a custodian a paid custodian concern simple safekeeping, where people often act gratuitously. An active broker or worker is different, because ordinary labor is not presumed free. Seif 5: If he retracted after I purchased.
