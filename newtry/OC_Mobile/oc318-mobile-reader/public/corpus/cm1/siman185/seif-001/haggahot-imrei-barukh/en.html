(Mechaber, seif 2) An agent cannot complete the purchase for himself. Note: The Ritva in his novellae, cited in Shitah Mekubetzet to Ketubot 98, leaves this law in doubt according to the Geonim who explain the Gemara's phrase "who appraised it for you" to mean perhaps you did not appraise accurately. See also the Ran, who explains the rule on the ground that a sale is an act of transferring an object from one domain to another, whereas here the object never left the principal's domain because the agent stands in the principal's place. See Responsa Rivash, siman 356. Responsa Maharit, siman 93, writes that even a sale to oneself should not necessarily be void merely because no third party acquired on the agent's behalf, citing the case of Yochanan Chakuka'ah in Pesachim 13a, where the reason given is "you shall be innocent before Hashem and Israel," implying that absent suspicion the sale could stand. It appears, however, that even according to those who hold an agent cannot acquire the item for himself, he is certainly not considered to have wrongfully used another's property if he takes it for himself at the very price authorized by the principal. The problem is only that the purchase does not give him a full acquisition while the object remains intact, because there was no transfer from one domain to another. Rashi's language in Pesachim is therefore precise: "let him take it for himself, eat it, and pay its value to the owner; why trouble to go to another market once he has authority to sell?" The word "eat it" would otherwise be unnecessary. Rashi's point is that although the agent cannot technically buy for himself, he may consume the chametz and pay its value because doing so is not wrongdoing once sale is necessary and he has authority to sell. This resembles the Rosh's ruling below, siman 359:2, that one may take an item that is standing for sale without the owner's knowledge if he immediately gives something better in exchange and causes the owner to acquire it through another, based on the principle that one may act beneficially for another. Even there, if the owner protests immediately upon learning of the act, the transaction is void, as with other acquisitions based on benefit. Nevertheless, until the owner's position is known, one may deal with the item as his own. All the more so with chametz during the fifth hour, when sale is necessary, there is no reasonable objection if the bailee consumes it and pays its value.
