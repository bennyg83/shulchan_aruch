He sold it to Reuven for two hundred. Although the broker was told to sell it for one hundred, we do not say that the excess belongs to the broker. His law is like that of an agent who sells an item without a fixed market price, where the excess belongs to the principal, as explained in siman 183:6. Here too the item has no fixed price, as shown by the fact that the owner had to tell him to sell it for one hundred. Even though the owner revealed his view and instructed him to sell it for one hundred, we do not say that he thereby waived the excess to the broker. Rather, we say that he thought it was not worth more. The Tur concludes that even if he knew it was worth more but, because of financial pressure, instructed the broker to sell cheaply, we still do not say that he intended to transfer the excess to the broker.
