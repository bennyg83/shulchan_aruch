Seif 5: He must return the object to its owner. This seems difficult in light of siman 182, which says that unless the principal proves he appointed the agent only to improve and not to impair, we presume a general agency. Why not presume here too that the agent did not violate his instructions? The Sema answers that here the principal expressly said not to sell below fifty, and the agent did not improve matters. But that answer makes the agent a deliberate wrongdoer, whereas it is more reasonable to suppose he erred and thought the principal would approve. The Sema also distinguishes between an unpaid agent and a broker. It appears instead that in siman 182 there is no contradiction between principal and purchaser concerning what the agent actually did. The question is only how broadly the agency was framed, and ordinary practice supports a general mandate. Here, however, the principal and agent necessarily contradict one another about the authority given. The Rambam therefore prefers the principal's claim, and the purchaser is treated as though he received the object directly from the principal without valid authority from the agent.
