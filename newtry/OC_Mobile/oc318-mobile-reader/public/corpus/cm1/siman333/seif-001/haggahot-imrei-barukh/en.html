Rama, seif 3: for this reason it is prohibited even for a worker, even a teacher. Note: This is Maharam's view, and he follows his own position that even for a worker or teacher, the labor obligation is not waived merely when the employer says, 'Go away from me.' He compares it to the law of a Hebrew servant, as he explains at length in the responsum cited below by the Shach, seif katan 47. One who studies the responsum itself will see that Maharam held that even a worker has at least a partial bodily acquisition in the form of an obligation to perform the work. We do not rule like him on this point, as is evident from Rama in the gloss to seif 8 and Shach there. According to Tosafos in Kiddushin 17a and Bava Metzia 10 and 78, one can say that since a worker may withdraw and his body is not acquired, there is no prohibition even where he hires himself for a long period. It is possible, however, that a teacher or scribe, whose work is regarded as a matter of loss and who therefore cannot freely withdraw, is subject to a greater prohibition than an ordinary worker. Rif's wording seems to imply the opposite, for he writes, 'it is prohibited for a worker, even a teacher,' etc. See Maharam's responsum in the cited Mordechai. Some of the points above were already noted by the Rosh cited by the Shach, seif katan 17.
