Beginning work also for the second year. A worker was hired for a fixed period in a manner that did not permit retraction. During that term the parties discussed employing him for another year afterward, and in reliance on that discussion the employer did not hire another available worker, creating a potential irretrievable loss. It appears that this is not yet a binding employment contract for the second year. During the first term, mere words concerning the future do not create a present lease of the worker's person, because his current work does not itself demonstrate a kinyan for the later period. Had the parties performed some act intended to extend the employment, the later term could become binding. This differs from leasing a house for a future period while it is currently leased to another, because a Jewish worker's body is not literally acquired by the employer through employment. With mere words alone, no binding second-year employment appears to arise.
