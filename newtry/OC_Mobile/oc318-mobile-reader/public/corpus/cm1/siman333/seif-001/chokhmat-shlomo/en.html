Seif 3. If a worker began the labor and withdrew in the middle of the day, etc. Note: It appears to me that the rule allowing a worker to withdraw mid-day applies only when he is ceasing to be hired labor, or even if he might seek work elsewhere tomorrow or another day, since we do not say that his wish for freedom today was invalid merely because circumstances later changed. But if, at the very same moment, he leaves the first employer in order to hire himself immediately to another, he is still choosing the status of a hired worker and is making himself subject to another employer. In that case he may not withdraw from the first. The fact that a worker is not deemed a slave rests on his ability to cease being a worker altogether. I further believe that since an independent contractor cannot withdraw, the same applies where one hires a teacher for only two hours each day without specifying which two hours. Because the teacher may choose whichever two hours he wishes, he resembles an independent contractor rather than a day laborer. An ordinary teacher, who is expected to teach throughout the day like other teachers, has the status of a worker. But one hired for only two hours, with discretion to select those hours, has the law of a contractor and may not withdraw. In any event, a teacher is generally treated as a case of irretrievable loss, as the Rama states in siman 333. I further believe that if a teacher entered into a formal kinyan, he certainly cannot withdraw. The Shach cites early authorities holding that any worker bound by kinyan cannot retract, and although he also cites dissenters, it appears that in a case of irretrievable loss all should agree that a kinyan prevents withdrawal. Even where an ordinary case of irretrievable loss might permit some withdrawal, a formal acquisition should bind completely, and it is preferable to minimize dispute. Thus, where the employer gave the teacher earnest money at the time of hiring, this can itself function as an acquisition, comparable to the acquisition of an indentured servant by money, as reflected in siman 267. This does not contradict the Mechaber's rule that even if the employer has already paid all wages the worker may withdraw. There the payment was made after the hiring, as payment of wages rather than as an act of acquisition. But where money is given at the outset of the hiring, it is given as a kinyan. This also explains why the Shach, who argues that kinyan does not help, did not prove his position from the Mechaber's ruling about advance payment. The cases differ. The Shach's additional reason, that such a kinyan is merely an acquisition of words, is also difficult in my view. Here the acquisition is in the worker's own service commitment, analogous to acquiring the labor of an indentured servant, and is not merely words. This appears correct to me.
