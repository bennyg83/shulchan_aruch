Only a grievance. The Tur writes that whichever party wishes to retract has legal permission to do so. The employer can tell the worker, "Hire yourself elsewhere," and the worker can tell the employer, "Go hire another worker." The retracting party is nevertheless subject to grievance because of the inconvenience caused. In the Perishah I wrote that the Tur's phrase "he has permission to retract" implies that there is not even a problem of being one who lacks good faith. The Tur appears to hold that the criticism of lacking good faith applies when one retracts from a promise without any relevant change or benefit, whereas if market circumstances change there is no such criticism, as the Tur and Rama write at the end of siman 204. Here too, if the employer retracts because he no longer needs the workers or finds cheaper workers, or the worker has a corresponding reason, the same principle applies. According to the authorities there who disagree and consider retraction blameworthy even after a market change, the same would apply here. Thus "they have only a grievance" means there is no monetary claim, though according to that view the retractor may still be morally classified as lacking good faith. The absence of a monetary claim is explained in the next seif and applies specifically where the retraction caused no financial loss to the other party.
If the employer pulled the artisan's tools, etc. Even if after the meshichah he returned the tools to the worker, the earlier meshichah still counts as an acquisition and neither side may ordinarily retract. The exception is a laborer. The Sages gave a laborer the right to retract even in the middle of the work in order to fulfill the verse, "For the children of Israel are servants to Me," meaning they are Hashem's servants and not servants to servants. The Tur and Mechaber state this below in seif 3. Rama then concludes that even with a laborer the employer may hold onto the worker's tools, etc. That means where the worker's tools are still in the employer's possession. This differs from the opening case, where after the meshichah the tools had already been returned to the worker.
The donkey drivers, and they found nothing, etc. Meaning: where the worker was hired as a donkey driver to bring produce and went but found none, or where the worker was hired to plow the employer's field and went there but found it too wet and unfit for plowing.

He gives them their wages. Since the employer was negligent and the workers' travel is treated as the beginning of the work, the employer can no longer retract without liability. If he does retract he must pay them like idle workers. This is so even though the workers suffered no independent monetary loss through the retraction, for even had he not hired them they would not have found other employment. The Tur states this explicitly in the Rosh's name. This is unlike the Ir Shushan, who writes that the employer is liable because he caused them loss by preventing them from hiring themselves out elsewhere, under the law of indirect damage. That is incorrect. His own words in the following seif contradict it, where he explicitly writes that once they traveled, the employer is liable even in a case where no other employment was available.
