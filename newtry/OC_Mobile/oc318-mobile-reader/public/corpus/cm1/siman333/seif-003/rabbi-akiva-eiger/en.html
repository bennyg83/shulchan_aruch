[Shulchan Aruch] This is treated like a matter that will be lost. Note: See Terumat HaDeshen, siman 300, Bnei Chayei, siman 386, and Responsa Kol Ben Levi in the discourses at the end of his book, page 76. Their discussions are all built on the premise that liability here arises from garmi, as the Sema writes in seif katan 8. In truth, that is the view of the Rosh, but the Nimukei Yosef writes in the name of Ramban that liability is because this is a matter that will be lost, and the wording of the Shulchan Aruch implies this. See Tosafot, Bava Kamma 20a, and Maadanei Yom Tov. Hagahot Ashri asks against the Rosh: if this is garmi, why in a matter that will be lost may he hire replacements only up to the amount of their wages, rather than recover the entire loss under the law of garmi? It answers that a matter that will be lost includes even a case without an actual monetary loss, such as where he could not have found other workers except for these. Thus, from the fact that he does not recover the entire loss it follows that this is not classified as garmi. See Shach, seif katan 28, who rules like Hagahot Ashri. I find it difficult, however, because the Shulchan Aruch here treats an employer's retraction toward workers as a matter that will be lost. If so, even where they could not find other employment, the employer should be liable. Examine carefully.
