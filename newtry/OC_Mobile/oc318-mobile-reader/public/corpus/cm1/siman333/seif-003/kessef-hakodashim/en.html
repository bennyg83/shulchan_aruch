See Ketzot HaChoshen, seif katan 7. Ketzot HaChoshen discusses Chavot Yair's statement that the rule, 'They are My servants and not servants to servants,' applies to a teacher, even though the teacher may receive wages for supervision and for teaching punctuation or cantillation, because those paid elements are not themselves service of Hashem. Ketzot argues that even if compensation is permitted only because the obligation is rabbinic, the work remains service of Hashem. Without Chavot Yair before me, it appears that with respect to supervision his statement is certainly defensible, since supervision is ordinary labor and the worker may retract. Even regarding payment for cantillation, Chavot Yair may mean that once the Sages did not require him to teach another's child without pay, his service to that child is not necessarily the same as his own direct service of Hashem. He could study those matters for himself, or teach other children. A prayer leader differs. Once the congregation asks one who is fit to lead the prayers, halachah limits his right to refuse, and a regular prayer leader who has accepted the position may be obligated out of honor for Heaven and for the congregation. Even if the detailed rule is rabbinic, performing the communal prayer under the enactment of the Sages is service of Hashem through the mitzvah of obeying rabbinic authority. The customary melodies may also fall within honoring Hashem, especially fixed melodies that the congregation regards as part of the established service. Thus the 'servants to servants' principle may not apply to the same extent. This requires careful study.
