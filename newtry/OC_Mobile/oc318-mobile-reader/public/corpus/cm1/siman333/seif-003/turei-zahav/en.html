(He may withdraw even if he accepted, etc.) The Beit Yosef cites the Ritva that a worker may withdraw only when he hired himself by words alone, but if he bound himself by a formal acquisition, that acquisition was added precisely to prevent withdrawal. This is difficult. If the employer pulls the worker's tools, pulling acquires in the case of a contractor, yet with an employee it cannot override the Torah's principle that Jews are servants of God and not servants of servants. Why should another form of acquisition be stronger? Terumat HaDeshen discusses an employer and worker who made a formal acquisition and suggests that perhaps the worker intended to bind himself only through a written instrument. His reasoning assumes that an acquisition by itself does not prevent withdrawal; otherwise there would be no reason to say the writing was necessary for that purpose. This indicates that he does not accept the Ritva's view, and that is the correct ruling.
