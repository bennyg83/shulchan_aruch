To pay. Maharik's language is: "and the money is not in his hand to pay." It is possible that this is precise, meaning that the money is not presently available, but he nevertheless has property from which he can pay. If he has no ability to pay at all, perhaps he cannot retract because this is like a matter that will be lost, below, seif 5. The Mechaber, however, appears to hold that even if he has no means to pay at all, he may retract. This differs from a matter that will be lost, where the employer may hire others at his expense or deceive him, but still cannot compel him to labor. Alternatively, even if he cannot presently pay, the work is not considered lost merely for that reason. Although regarding testimony we do not say, "perhaps he will become wealthy," as stated in siman 37, seif 10, that does not mean the work is presently classified as lost, since it remains possible that he will later obtain funds. Shach.
Forbidden. It seems to me this applies specifically when he has food and clothing, for in such a case it is forbidden to sell oneself as a Hebrew servant, as stated in Torat Kohanim and in the Rambam and Semag at the beginning of Hilkhot Avadim. But if he is extremely poor and lacks even clothing, where the Rambam and Semag write that he may sell himself, it is obvious that he may do so even for many years, as stated in Kiddushin 14b and in the Rambam and Semag. If so, he may certainly hire himself for a fixed period longer than three years, for this is no worse than selling himself. So it appears to me. Ibid.
Three. This is how it appears in Darkei Moshe at the beginning of siman 331. Sefer Be'er Sheva, p. 112b, and Sefer Imrei Emet, p. 107d, write that it is a scribal error and should read "more than three years," as in Hagahot Mordechai. Their words are correct, for it is explicit there. See also Ibn Ezra to Parashat Re'eh. The Ir Shushan and Sema did not notice this and wrote that three years itself is forbidden because he acquires the status of a hired worker. On the contrary, Hagahot Mordechai makes clear that a period like the years of a hired worker is permitted, and only more than three years is forbidden because he leaves the category of an ordinary hired worker. Be'er Sheva further writes that Tosafot, Bava Metzia 10a, implies that even more than three years is permitted. Tosafot writes that the verse "For the Children of Israel are servants to Me" nevertheless permits a person to hire himself, because only a Hebrew servant who cannot retract and does not leave before his term except by a deed of release violates the principle that they are My servants. The Mordechai, chapter HaUmanin, likewise writes as Tosafot. Shach.
