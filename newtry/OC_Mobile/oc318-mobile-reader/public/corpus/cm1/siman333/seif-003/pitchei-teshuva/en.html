He is considered compelled and is exempt. Responsa Mayim Chaim, Choshen Mishpat 6, discusses a small community that appointed a shochet without a fixed term and paid him from each slaughter. Later the community leased its meat tax, and as part of that arrangement was required to receive kosher meat slaughtered in a neighboring city, eliminating the shochet's livelihood. If the community was truly compelled by taxation, it is exempt under siman 333. But if it could have conditioned with the lessee that slaughter remain local, its failure to do so may make it liable to pay the shochet as an idle worker, perhaps with only a small deduction because a shochet's work resembles the position of a communal rabbi. Although no term was fixed, the author inclines that such an appointment is not merely for one year. Even where strict law exempts the community, it is proper to act beyond the letter of the law for a poor shochet with dependents. Shaar Mishpat cites Responsa Rama 50 concerning a rabbi whose community withdrew before he began. A public communal appointment is ordinarily not freely retractable, though genuine epidemic or other force majeure may permit retraction.
