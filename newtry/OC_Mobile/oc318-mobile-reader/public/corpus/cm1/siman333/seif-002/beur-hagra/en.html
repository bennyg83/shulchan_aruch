When does this apply, etc. As with an employer, for with a matter involving irretrievable loss he may hire replacements at the workers' expense or mislead them, even if they never began the work. The Rosh writes this in the name of Ra'avad, since the Mishnah concerns a case where they did not begin, as it says "they have no, etc.," and the latter clause teaches "he hires, etc." Moreover, we rule like Rabbi Akiva regarding dina d'garmi. Tosafos there, s.v. "ein, etc.," and other poskim. Where they actually traveled to the worksite, by contrast, even without irretrievable loss they become liable because that constitutes beginning the labor. Just as other property is acquired through a kinyan, employment is bound by beginning the work. This is the meaning of the gloss, "specifically, etc."
And this is where now, etc. As stated there 79b: "what are the circumstances? If workers are available to hire, etc." The Tosefta, chapter 7, teaches: one who hires a worker and the plowing ends at midday, and he shows him his fellow's field, the worker may say, "either provide me work from your own property or pay me for what I did." This shows he can claim only for the work already done. Nimukei Yosef.
They have no, etc. Maggid Mishneh, as stated there: "when does this apply? In a place where, etc.; but if there are, etc." This is not comparable to the ship case 79b, where if another ship is available there is not even a grievance. See Shach, seif katan 1.
