The rule that workers have only a grievance against the employer when he cancels applies, where they did not begin traveling, specifically when they could not have hired themselves out the previous evening when this employer hired them. If they could have been hired then but now cannot find any work at all, the employer's cancellation is treated as causing them a direct loss, and he pays them as idle workers. If they can now obtain work only for less, he pays the difference. If they actually traveled specifically if the workers themselves traveled, not merely their agent. Maggid Mishneh, chapter 9, in their name. then even if they could not have hired themselves out the previous evening, he pays them as idle workers if they now cannot obtain any work. But if they can find work at the same wage, they have only a grievance. Some say that even if they find only heavier work, if the new employer is willing to increase their wage they must accept the other work. Mordechai, chapter HaUmanin, in the name of Maharam. Others disagree. Beit Yosef in the name of the students of Rashba. If they can find work only for less, the first employer pays the difference. All these laws apply when the employer failed through his own negligence to inspect the work the previous evening. If the employer was not negligent at all, he is considered compelled by circumstances and is exempt, as explained above. Rama: See below, siman 334, seif 2. A teacher who hired himself for two years and began the first year is considered to have begun the second year as well, and the same applies to any worker. Hagahot Maimoniyot, chapter HaUmanin.
