[Shach, letter 4] To perform the work if the employer wishes. Note: In my humble opinion this is indeed compelled by the sources. From the Rama it appears that the distinction is between an employee and an independent contractor. With a contractor, once he has taken the tools of his trade, even if he returned them afterward, he may no longer retract, as the Shach writes in seif katan 3. If taking the tools had only the status of beginning the work, as the Derishah seems repeatedly to assume, so that upon retraction his hand is on the lower side, then even after work has begun he need not pay out of pocket. According to the Derishah, following his own view in Sema seif katan 16, there could be a difference where the employer seized money, since after the tools were taken it is as though work began and he may hire replacement workers from the seized funds. But according to the Shach, seif katan 21, who holds that he may not hire replacements from money seized, what practical consequence remains here if work never began and the tools were returned, merely from saying his hand is on the lower side? It must therefore be that taking the tools has a stronger effect, namely that if a beit din is available it compels him not to retract. Nevertheless, if no beit din was available to compel him and the employer hired others, the worker need not reimburse him. This is explicit in Shach, seif katan 34. Examine carefully.
