Since a litigant's admission is equivalent to one hundred witnesses, there was an effective kinyan, and the contractor could no longer retract. Shach, siman 333, seif katan 2, rules principally that once the employer has taken possession of the contractor's tools the contractor may not retract, and seif katan 4 indicates the same for kinyan sudar. Seif katan 14 even implies that kinyan sudar is stronger than taking tools. According to Ritva, after kinyan sudar even an ordinary employee may not retract. Furthermore, even without a kinyan, once a written contract has been executed a contractor may no longer retract, as Rama writes in siman 320, seif 2. There the ruling necessarily concerns a document without an additional kinyan. A signed handwritten document of the obligated party is no weaker than a witnessed document except with respect to a plea of repayment and collection from encumbered property. For other matters, the force of a signed writing is equivalent. Noda BiYehuda adds in paragraph 9 that where taking the tools or kinyan sudar prevents retraction, beit din compels the contractor to perform so long as performance remains possible. If he nevertheless refuses until the opportunity has passed and the damage can no longer be repaired, liability is ordinarily only for preventing the employer's profit, unless the employer suffered an actual loss. Rabbi Akiva Eiger writes that according to Shach, the contractor cannot simply retract and accept the usual disadvantageous accounting. Beit din should compel performance. If no beit din was available, the contractor withdrew against the employer's will, and the employer hired others at a higher price, it would seem that the contractor must reimburse the added cost because those replacement workers act in effect as his agents to perform the work he remained obligated to do. Yet Shach later, seif katan 34, explicitly writes that if the employer hires others against him, the contractor need not reimburse the excess. Rabbi Akiva Eiger therefore leaves the point requiring study.
