Shach, seif katan 47: if he says to him, 'Go,' because his body is acquired. Note: From Maharam's responsum in Teshuvot Maimoniyot, Hilchot Kinyan, siman 31, and Hagahot Maimoniyot, Bava Metzia, chapter HaOmanin, it is clear that Maharam derives the law of a worker by an a fortiori argument from a Hebrew servant, whose body is acquired. It follows necessarily that in his view a worker's body is not acquired by the employer in the same way. One must say that a Hebrew servant is acquired bodily in a fuller sense, whereas an ordinary worker is not acquired, but his body is obligated to perform the work he accepted upon himself, and with respect to that obligation the employer has a form of acquisition in his person. See Ritva's novellae to Kiddushin 13.
