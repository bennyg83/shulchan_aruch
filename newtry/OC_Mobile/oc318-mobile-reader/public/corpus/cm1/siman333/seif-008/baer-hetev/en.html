Liable. The Sema infers from the Rosh's wording that the craftsman must undertake the effort of selling the article, while the employer need only cover the loss. It seems this applies specifically where the craftsman supplied his own materials. If the employer supplied the materials, then since we hold that a craftsman does not acquire the improvement in the article, as stated in siman 306, seif 2, the article belongs to the employer and the burden of selling it is not upon the craftsman. So it seems to me. Shach.
He hired himself. Meaning, he hired himself without qualification for another year. We therefore say that because he made no new condition, he hired himself on the original conditions. On this basis the Rivash's proofs are sound. But if he rehired himself and they fixed a new amount of wages without mentioning the prior condition, we certainly say that the new wage agreement is a new arrangement and not subject to the old condition. The Rivash's proofs do not apply to such a case. This is unlike the Ir Shushan, who writes that in the second year, whether they lowered or increased the wages, the prior condition continues. I do not know his source. Ibid.
Second. The Sema writes that this is all the more true where he continues with the original managers, who know the first condition. So too in Rivash, siman 506. See there further. Ibid.
By silence. The Shach writes that this rule requires study, because the Rama's derivation from the Rivash and Maharik is not compelling. See his lengthy discussion.
He is agreeable. The Ramban's responsum states: since he knew of the father's condition when the price was fixed and entered the work, he presumably agreed to what the father had done, since he did not protest. It seems that the Rama likewise refers to a case where he knew of the condition. Accordingly, "father" is not literal; the same applies to any other person who hired him, if he knew that person's arrangement. It also seems unnecessary that he know the precise amount; it is enough that he knows the father or another person fixed terms with him and he then entered the work. He is presumed to accept whatever terms were fixed. So it appears to me. Shach.
And to retain him. Responsa Rashdam, siman 119, distinguishes between oral statement and writing, holding that writing is effective and waives the lien. I do not find his words correct. The Gemara at the beginning of chapter HaKotev and the authorities there imply no such distinction. Ibid.
With other. Although Responsa Maharam, Responsa Rashba, and the Mordechai imply that even with an ordinary worker, telling him "go" is ineffective because his person is acquired like a Hebrew servant, we do not appear to rule so. A worker is treated like a Hebrew servant only leniently, in that he may retract, not because his body is owned. Tosafot, Kiddushin 17a, likewise implies this. Maharam himself appears not to rely primarily on that rationale but only to add it as support; his main reason is that one cannot waive the boy's lien. This is unlike what appears from the aforementioned Responsa Rashdam, and the former view is primary. Ibid.
Two. It seems that two witnesses are not specifically required. The language merely follows Kiddushin 16a. As Tosafot writes there, the same applies if he said it privately and later admits it, because witnesses were created only to prevent falsehood. I later found Rivash, siman 476, citing the commentators likewise. Ibid.
Exempt. Even if he already received all the wages, he need not return anything. (See what I wrote in siman 207, seif katan 40.) Ibid.
