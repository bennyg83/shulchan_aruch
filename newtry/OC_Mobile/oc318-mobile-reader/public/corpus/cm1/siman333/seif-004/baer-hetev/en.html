The increase in price. For example, he hired a worker for eight dinar for the day and the worker completed half the day. Even if labor became more expensive so that another worker must be paid six dinar for the remaining half-day, the first worker still receives four dinar for the half-day he completed. We do not reduce him to two merely so that the employer can finish the entire day's work for the agreed eight. The Tur further writes that if the worker was hired for eight and labor became cheaper so that the remaining half-day can be completed for two, the first worker receives six and the employer retains only two. The Shach writes that he found no other posek who states this latter rule and that it is difficult: why should the first worker receive more than the four dinar agreed for half a day's work? The Beit Yosef says that the rule for a fall in price follows simply from the rule for an increase, but the Shach rejects the analogy. When prices rise, the first worker receives exactly half the wage originally agreed, as though he had been hired for half a day at four. When prices fall, there is no basis to increase his earned wage. Tosafot, chapter HaOmanin 77a, together with Rashi and Tosafot in the first chapter of Bava Metzia 10a, support this distinction. The Shach later found an old parchment manuscript of the Tur that omits the law concerning a fall in price.
