If he retracts in the middle of the day, he may retract. Chavot Yair 103 considers whether a worker's right to retract applies only when he leaves employment entirely, or even when he leaves one employer to work for another. He inclines that no distinction was stated by the classical authorities. Yet the Tur and Rama rule that if a worker retracts merely because wages have risen, he is not heard, and Bach explains that since he is still willing to remain a worker for higher pay, the reason based on 'the children of Israel are servants to Me' does not assist him. Some later authorities therefore hold that one who leaves solely to work for another likewise may not retract. Others, including Mayim Chaim and Knesset Yechezkel, distinguish: a worker is never bodily acquired and may leave one employer for another, although in some circumstances his hand is on the lower side and he forfeits the special advantage ordinarily given to a worker who retracts. The matter remains in need of practical study. Chavot Yair also discusses a cantor who bound himself to a community for ten years by a handshake and the community's reciprocal undertaking, and rejects the claim that such a term is automatically forbidden merely because ordinary workers may retract; the validity depends on the nature of the office, the form of undertaking, and the authorities discussed there.
