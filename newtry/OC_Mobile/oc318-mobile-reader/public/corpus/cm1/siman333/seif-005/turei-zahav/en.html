(Seif 8: A communal prayer leader), etc. The Rama's ruling is very difficult. The Rivash ruled that a prayer leader whose original engagement included exemption from communal tax, and who was later reengaged without specifying terms, continues under the original conditions. He compared this to Rav Hai Gaon's ruling that a tenant who remains after the term without a new agreement pays the former rent. The Rivash distinguishes the case of a convert and his wife, where the additional ketubah does not revive through silence, because that additional obligation had ceased and there was no ongoing obligation to infer from silence. Where an ongoing employment or lease is renewed, silence indicates continuation of the former terms unless a change is stated. This applies even if the prayer leader was not formally reappointed but simply continued to serve after the first year; continuing the service is no different from remaining in a house. Maharik's case of partners is different. There one partner had received one hundred ducats for a prior period only through a compromise, not through an underlying obligation, and therefore the gratuitous payment did not continue into a later year without a new stipulation. The governing distinction is whether the earlier term was part of the binding compensation or merely a gift or compromise. A prayer leader's tax exemption was part of the wage for which he agreed to serve. It therefore continues together with the salary, even without a formal new appointment. The Rama's suggestion that only a renewed hiring preserves the condition, while mere continuation preserves the salary but not the tax exemption, has no basis. In practice communal prayer leaders in major communities are not reappointed each year; they remain in office with all their rights until the community expressly objects. This is clear and straightforward.
