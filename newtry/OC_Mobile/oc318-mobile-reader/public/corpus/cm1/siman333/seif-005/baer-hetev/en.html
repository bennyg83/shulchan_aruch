Others. The Beit Yosef cites Tosafot Rabbenu Peretz in the name of the Ramban that even if the employer could not have found other workers when he originally hired these workers, if they later retract the employer may hire replacements at their expense or mislead them. The Shach does not know why the authors of the Shulchan Aruch omitted this view. Perhaps they relied on the Tur, Maggid Mishneh, and Nimukei Yosef, who cite the Rashba without qualification. Yet the matter requires study, because Hagahot Ashri also explicitly accords with Tosafot Rabbenu Peretz. It explains that the Gemara can concern a case where the workers caused no actual loss, such as where the flax was already soaking and, at the time these workers were hired, no others were available. Even so, once they accepted the work they must perform it, or the employer may hire replacements at their expense or mislead them. Shach.

That he stipulated. The Shach writes that perhaps this applies specifically where the worker knows that another worker is available and tells the employer, "Here is another person whom you can hire." That is equivalent to supplying a substitute. On this basis the Rosh's rulings in his Piskei HaRosh and responsa need not conflict, and the Rosh need not disagree with the later authorities cited by Nimukei Yosef and the Rama in siman 332, seif 5, who rule that where withdrawal is permitted only subject to a grievance, the first wage agreement remains controlling. Here too there is at least a grievance, as stated below in seif 7, and it is difficult to distinguish between an employer who retracts and a contractor who retracts. The case is different because the contractor specifically told the employer to hire one of the available replacements. However, the Tur and Rama do not state that he pointed out replacements, implying that they see no distinction. The Tur therefore writes in seif 4, based on the Rosh's responsum, that the employer may mislead the contractor when he cannot find another worker. The Tur apparently understands that the Rosh's responsum does not follow his Piskei HaRosh and follows the Piskei HaRosh as authoritative. Beit Yosef in Bedek HaBayit similarly explains that the Tur's seif 5 is based on the Rosh's Piskei HaRosh. If one assumes that the Rosh does not disagree with the later authorities cited by Nimukei Yosef, one can distinguish further: here the employer first misled the workers by saying, "Take two," whereas if the workers themselves begin by demanding more, the employer need pay only the original wage. This explains why the Rama in siman 332 records the distinguishing opinion there while citing the Rosh here without qualification. Shach.
