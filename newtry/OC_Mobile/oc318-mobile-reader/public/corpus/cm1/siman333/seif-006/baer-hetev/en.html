That he add. Meaning, for example, he hired them for two sela'im and paid them nothing yet; they retract, and he hires replacements for four sela'im. He may recover two sela'im from the first workers. This is clearly the view of the Rambam and Semag, and also of the Rosh and Tur. Rabbeinu Yerucham writes that some say "up to their wages" means only up to the amount of their wages that the employer is holding, following Rashi, while others say twice their wages; he prefers the first view. I am surprised that he rules against his teacher the Rosh, especially since the Rosh's view is primary and is also evident from the Yerushalmi. Shach.
In his hand. This implies that even if the money was not given as collateral and no formal kinyan was made, the rule applies. So it appears from the Tur and authorities and explicitly from Tosafot, Kiddushin 8b, and Piskei Tosafot there, as well as from the Mordechai. This is unlike Semag in Hilkhot Kiddushin, and the former view is primary. It also appears that even other property belonging to them, not specifically their tools, suffices. The Tur says, "if he has in his hand property belonging to them." Rabbi Baruch in Sefer HaChochmah, cited by the Mordechai, likewise implies this. It seems that Rashi too agrees, because he explains "their bundle came into his hand" as meaning that he possesses substantial property belonging to them, since craftsmen commonly bring their tools to the employer's house. Thus Rashi's mention of a bundle is not specific; he uses the ordinary example of craftsmen bringing tools. Maharam in the Mordechai disagrees and requires their actual bundle, and this is the dissenting opinion cited by the Rama. In my view he is a lone opinion against all these authorities. Ibid.
A worker. Up to the amount employers normally pay at the time necessary to prevent the work from being lost. So write the Ramban and Nimukei Yosef. Therefore the stated amounts of forty or fifty are not literal; the amount depends on customary hiring costs and on the circumstances of the loss, and may be more or less. This also appears to be the Rambam and Mechaber's intent, unlike the Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe. Sema.
