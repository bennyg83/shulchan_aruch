For a fixed term of three years. Baer Heitev notes a textual correction: the concern is employment for more than three years. Hagahot Mordechai cautions a teacher, scribe, or other worker against binding himself to live permanently in the employer's home and at his table without interruption for more than three years. Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat 172, applies the concern also to a communal rabbi: the community collectively hires him, provides his residence and maintenance, and he is not free to live elsewhere, which can constitute being supported at their table. He further writes that if a rabbinic contract extending beyond three years also requires the rabbi, upon leaving, to repay the community's expenses in a manner that effectively prevents retraction, the arrangement resembles servitude even more. A permissible formulation is to leave him legally free to retract at any time, while requiring repayment only if he leaves to replace this community with another. In Orach Chaim 206 Chatam Sofer explains the varying custom of writing rabbinic contracts for three or five years. The three-year concern is derived by some from 'three years, like the years of a hired worker,' though Rashi offers other interpretations that may indicate only a six-year term is objectionable. The stated term is primarily for the rabbi's benefit, enabling him to leave after it expires. The community may not remove him merely because the stated term ended unless local custom permits it. In these lands, he writes, removing a rabbi from his established office was never the accepted practice.
