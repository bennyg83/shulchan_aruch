How he hires them, he says, “How does he stand before himself and I will tell you how he is doing and interpreting them as he is,” and he says, “How is it that he has already commanded him to pay for him and that he will be able to hire them.”
Up to the amount of the first workers' wages. This is the language of the Gemara and Rambam. Rashi explains it literally. If the first worker was hired for two dinars for the day and retracts in the middle, the employer may hire another worker for an additional two dinars. The Rosh and Tur interpret the phrase to mean up to double the first wage. In the Perishah I explained that the amount the first workers were originally to receive becomes, in effect, a fund they accepted could be used against them if they retracted in a matter of irretrievable loss and the employer had to hire replacements. This resembles the law of earnest money, where one party says, "Let my deposit acquire," and the seller says, "If I retract I will double your earnest money." The Tur discusses that in siman 190. The Rosh challenges Rashi's explanation: if the employer had already paid the first workers, or if they had not yet begun the work, there would be no penalty at all under Rashi's reading, even in a case of irretrievable loss. In the Derishah I explained that even the Rambam and Mechaber, though they copy the Gemara's wording, understand it like the Rosh and Tur. Their later statement, "whatever he adds over this amount he collects from the first workers," implies collection beyond money still owed to them, even from their own property. They also do not distinguish whether the first wage was already paid or the work had not yet begun.
Up to forty or fifty. Ramban and Nimukei Yosef explain that this means the amount householders commonly spend in urgent circumstances to prevent their work from being lost. Forty or fifty is not an exact maximum. Depending on the employer's ordinary conduct and the degree of potential loss, he may add more or less. It appears the Rambam and Mechaber agree, unlike the implication of the Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe.
