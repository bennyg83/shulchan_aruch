For a person to make a guardrail. The Rambam writes in Hilchot Berachot, chapter 11, that one must recite the blessing, “Who sanctified us with His commandments and commanded us to make a guardrail,” and he also writes there that one recites Shehecheyanu. However, Nishmat Adam, klal 15, writes that the Rambam follows his own view that Shehecheyanu is also recited over tzitzit and tefillin. According to our ruling in Orach Chaim, siman 22, regarding tzitzit, that Shehecheyanu is not recited, the same applies to a guardrail: one recites only the blessing for making the guardrail. See there. Chayei Adam there, din 24, writes that this blessing is recited specifically when one makes a guardrail for his roof. If one makes a raised rim or cover for a pit, as discussed below in seif 7, no blessing is recited. See there. See Machaneh Ephraim, Hilchot Sheluchin, siman 11, concerning a person who wishes to construct a guardrail through a non-Jewish craftsman, whether the owner may recite the blessing. He inclines to the view that the owner may indeed recite it, because the hand of a worker is regarded as the hand of the employer. See what I wrote above in siman 192, seif 1, seif katan 2. His language there implies a distinction: if the work is done by a craftsman engaged as an independent contractor, then even where the craftsman is Jewish the owner does not recite the blessing. According to Knesset HaGedolah in the name of his teachers, the craftsman who installs the guardrail recites it. But if the craftsman is hired as a day laborer, then even if he is a non-Jew the owner may properly recite the blessing, because the work is regarded as though he himself performed it. See there at length.
A guardrail for his roof. See above, siman 314, seif 2, that one who rents a house from another is obligated to make the guardrail; see there. See also Eretz DeRabbanan, entry 403, who cites Knesset HaGedolah as ruling that a tenant is obligated in the mitzvah of a guardrail only by rabbinic law, whereas he himself writes that the tenant is obligated even by Torah law; see there.
