It is a positive commandment for a person to make a parapet. This entire siman does not appear in the Tur. The Mechaber copied it from the Rambam, who writes these laws in chapter 11 of Hilchot Rotzeach uShmirat Nefesh. Because the preceding simanim also discuss preservation of life and laws related to homicide, the Rambam placed these rules in the same set of laws, and the Mechaber followed his arrangement here.
Provided it is a dwelling house. Seif 3 below explains that the Torah's phrase "your roof" excludes buildings not made for residence. The Kesef Mishneh notes that the Sifrei includes certain such structures in the obligation of parapet, and Semag likewise writes that they can be obligated. The Rambam, however, follows the view that exempts those structures from mezuzah and holds that the same definition applies to parapet because they are not called "your house." The Sifrei that obligates them follows the opinion that obligates them in mezuzah, which is not the halachah according to the Rambam. This creates a difficulty with the Mechaber. In Yoreh Deah siman 286 he rules like the Rif and Rosh that cattle sheds, poultry coops, and storehouses for oil and wine require mezuzah, unlike the Rambam. Here regarding parapet he rules that such buildings are exempt, even though the Kesef Mishneh linked the two laws. It appears that the Mechaber distinguished them. Regarding parapet, people do not ordinarily use the roofs of cattle sheds, storehouses, and wood houses as residential or frequent use areas. Even if a person occasionally stays there, people do not regularly enter and leave the roof in a manner creating significant danger of falling. Regarding mezuzah, however, the Torah imposed the mitzvah upon the person who dwells or uses the entrance, so that the mitzvah of Hashem should be before his eyes when entering and leaving. Therefore a person who uses such a structure can still be obligated to affix a mezuzah.
