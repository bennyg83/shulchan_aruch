A dwelling. It is surprising that regarding mezuzah, in Yoreh Deah at the beginning of siman 286, the Mechaber rules without qualification that a cattle barn, chicken coop, and storehouses for oil and wine require a mezuzah, following the Rif and Rosh and unlike the Rambam, as explained there. Here, regarding a parapet, he rules without qualification that these places do not require one, even though the law of parapet is derived from the law of mezuzah, as the Kesef Mishneh writes. It appears that the Mechaber distinguished as follows. Regarding a parapet, people do not ordinarily use the roof of a cattle barn, storehouse, or woodshed as a living area. Therefore, even if on occasion someone lives there, people do not enter and leave so frequently that one must fear a fall and require a parapet. Mezuzah is different, because the Torah obligates the resident so that the mitzvah of Hashem will be before his eyes when he enters and leaves. Therefore the Mechaber rules that one who lives there must affix a mezuzah to that entrance. Sema. See Responsa Mabit, part 1, siman 110.
