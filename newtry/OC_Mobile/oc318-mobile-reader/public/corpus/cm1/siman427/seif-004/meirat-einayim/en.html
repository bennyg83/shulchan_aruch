The public domain is higher, etc. Meaning, there is concern that people may fall from the public domain onto the roof.
He is not obligated to build a parapet. Meaning, do not say that he must build a parapet on his roof from below upward until it stands ten handbreadths higher than the public domain. Since the users of the public domain are like partners in a common pot, it is obvious that the roof owner is not obligated to build a parapet for the public domain.
As it is stated, "for the one who falls may fall from it." Chazal expound: "from it," but not "into it." This is somewhat difficult. Why is such an exclusion necessary? Even without it the homeowner could say: had my house not been there, all the more so the person would have been bruised, injured, or killed by falling from the height of the public domain to the ground. With some difficulty one can answer that one might otherwise have thought that the homeowner is obligated to fill the area of his house with earth until its lower level is even with the public domain.
