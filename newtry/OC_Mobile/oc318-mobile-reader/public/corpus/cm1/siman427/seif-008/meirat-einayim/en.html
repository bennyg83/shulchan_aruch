And he violated "You shall not place blood." Darkei Moshe to siman 426 cites the Rambam in Hilchot Avel: Escorting guests is a rabbinic positive commandment. The Sages said that one who fails to escort is as though he sheds blood, and beit din can compel escort just as it compels charity. Even escorting another person four cubits earns great reward. The required measure of escort is: a teacher escorts a student to the outskirts of the city, one companion escorts another to the Shabbat boundary, a student escorts his teacher one parsah, and a student escorts his primary teacher three parsangs. Yalkut Maharash writes that today the custom is not for a student to escort his teacher a full parsah because teachers waive that honor. One should nevertheless walk with him or with a companion to the gate, or at least four cubits. The Rambam further writes that acts of bodily kindness have no fixed measure, including visiting the sick, comforting mourners, attending to the dead, bringing a bride to marriage, and escorting guests. Although many of these are rabbinic commandments, they are included in "You shall love your fellow as yourself": whatever you would want others to do for you, do for your fellow in Torah and mitzvot. The reward for escorting guests is especially great. This is part of the path of kindness established by Avraham Avinu, who fed travelers, gave them drink, and escorted them. Welcoming guests is greater than receiving the Divine Presence, and escorting them is greater than merely welcoming them.
