Rambam writes in chapter 14 of Hilkhot Avel that there is a rabbinic positive commandment to visit the sick, as explained in Yoreh Deah siman 335; comfort mourners, as explained there in siman 376; accompany the dead, as explained in siman 361; escort a bride, as explained in Even HaEzer siman 65; accompany guests; and attend to all burial needs. These are acts of kindness performed with one's body and have no fixed measure. Although each of these mitzvot is rabbinic, they are included in 'You shall love your fellow as yourself': whatever you wish others to do for you, do for your brother in Torah and mitzvot. The reward for accompanying a traveler is greater than all the rest. This is the statute established by Avraham Avinu and the path of kindness he practiced: he fed travelers, gave them drink, and accompanied them. Hospitality is greater than receiving the Divine Presence, as Scripture says, 'He saw, and behold, three men,' etc. Accompanying guests is greater than welcoming them. The Sages said that one who does not accompany a traveler is as though he sheds blood, Sotah chapter 9, 46b. People may be compelled to provide accompaniment just as they may be compelled to give charity. Beit din would appoint agents to accompany a person traveling from place to place, and if they were negligent in this it is accounted to them as though they shed blood. Even one who accompanies his fellow only four cubits receives great reward. The Gemara explains the required measure of accompaniment, such as from a teacher for a student, etc. In a compilation of Maharshal I found that nowadays the custom is not to accompany a student with his teacher for a full parsah, because in our time teachers waive that honor. Nevertheless, one should walk with him, or with one's fellow, to the gate, or at least four cubits. Sema cites this in the name of Darkhei Moshe.
