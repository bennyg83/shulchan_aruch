The height, etc. Bava Batra teaches that a roof is excluded from the sale when it has a parapet, and the Tosefta, Bava Kamma chapter 6, states that one who dug a pit and made a rim ten handbreadths high is exempt, whereas if the rim was lower than ten handbreadths he is liable. This rejects Semag, as explained in seif 7.
(Supplement) The parapet, etc. Raavad writes that ten handbreadths are required specifically at the place where people tread, the lowest point, while three handbreadths suffice on the other sides. He bases this on Sifrei: 'How much is the place of its circuit? Three handbreadths; a place people tread, ten.' Semag instead explains that a pit requires only three handbreadths, whereas a house requires ten. Rambam does not accept that interpretation, as shown by the Tosefta: if he dug properly, covered properly, and made a rim ten handbreadths high, he is exempt; if the rim was lower, he is liable. See the Mishna at the beginning of Bava Batra chapter 4.
(Supplement) The height of the parapet, etc. Sifrei in Ki Tetzei states: 'How much is the place of its circuit? Three handbreadths; a place people tread, ten.' Semag explains that a pit requires three and a house ten. Raavad explains that the place where people tread, the lowest point, requires ten, while the other sides require three. Rambam makes no distinction. It is possible that he accepts Raavad's reading of Sifrei but holds that the Gemara disagrees, because the opening Mishna of Bava Batra chapter 4 speaks simply of a roof that has a parapet, and the Yerushalmi likewise gives no distinction. See siman 50.
