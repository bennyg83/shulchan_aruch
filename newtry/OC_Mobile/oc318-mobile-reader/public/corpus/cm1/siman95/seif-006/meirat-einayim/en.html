He must pay. The reason is that he damaged the property directly. As explained in seif 1, the exemption for negligence applies only where the item was lost because of the negligence, not where he physically damaged it.
This is like any other monetary claim. Maggid Mishneh writes that Rambam does not agree. Since the claimant cannot compel the defendant to refill the pits, but if he wishes the defendant can pay the monetary value of the damage, there is no distinction between a demand for payment and a demand that the pits be refilled. Either way it is a monetary claim arising from land, and nevertheless Rambam exempts him from a Torah oath. This dispute parallels the dispute stated by Rama in seif 2 concerning grapes standing ready to be harvested. The first view there holds that a monetary claim arising from land retains the law of a land claim. The Mechaber follows that view here, ruling that even if he claims the monetary depreciation he is exempt from a Torah oath. This is also why in seif 2 he states the grape case without qualification, treating it as applicable even where their value is claimed. Rama's second view there follows the "some say" brought here. Beit Yosef explains this in seif 12. See also Nimukei Yosef in the sugya of the grapes, who explains the case of digging pits differently in Rashba's name.
