Specifically where he claimed that he should fill them in. See Shach, seif katan 18, through his proof from the case where one says, “You injured me twice,” following the Raavad. The primary explanation is that of the Tumim. Specifically with pits dug in land, the damager has the option to fill the pits rather than pay money, and therefore the claim is treated as a claim concerning land. By contrast, bodily injury is a wrong that cannot be repaired physically and can be compensated only with money, so it is treated as a monetary claim. It also appears that only slaves were compared halachically to land, whereas free persons were not compared to land. This is correct.
