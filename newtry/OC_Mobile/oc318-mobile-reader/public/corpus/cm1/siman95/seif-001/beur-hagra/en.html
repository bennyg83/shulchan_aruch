By Torah law. Rabbinically, oaths are administered, as stated in Perek HaKosev. See Tosafos, Bava Basra 33a.
Even property outside Eretz Yisrael. Although Erchin 29a says that land outside Eretz Yisrael is like movable property of Eretz Yisrael, that statement concerns consecrated property and matters dependent on Yovel, learned from ancestral fields. It does not govern oaths or ona'ah. Baal HaTerumos, sha'ar 7.
Likewise property of a non-Jew. So taught in the Mechilta, Mishpatim: "If a man gives to his fellow..." excluding consecrated property and others.
Whether... whether the oath of bailees. The Mishnah in Shevuos exempts these categories from the oaths of judges and of bailees. The same applies to an oath generated by one witness. The Yerushalmi states that wherever two witnesses would obligate payment, one witness obligates an oath, but land is different because no oath is taken on land. Partial admission is likewise excluded, as seen in Bava Metzia and Perek HaShoel, and both principles appear in Perek HaKosev.
And even... whether... At the end of Perek HaChovel, wherever Scripture excluded these categories, even a paid bailee is exempt from liability for negligence. This follows because according to Rav Yosef a finder is a paid bailee due to the incidental benefit of exemption from charity, and the same reasoning extends to a borrower. See Beer HaGolah, the end of siman 66, and the beginning of siman 301 for dissenting views.
And if he stipulated... Only if a kinyan was made from him, as stated in Bava Metzia 58a and Tosafos there. So too in siman 66 and siman 301.
But... As above.
Likewise... See Beer HaGolah. The same applies to slaves, as stated in Bava Metzia 100b regarding a claim for a slave and its garment, and similarly to all the excluded categories. The Yerushalmi in Kiddushin likewise derives gilgul for matters upon which no direct oath is imposed, as explained in the preceding siman.
And consecrated property... but... From the end of Perek HaChovel, which derives from "to guard" an exclusion for property designated for distribution to the poor because it is money with no specific claimant. The Gemara does not exempt it merely because of consecrated status.
And some say that detached produce... Compare Chullin 16a regarding idolatry, susceptibility to impurity, and shechitah. Although Rava has a doubt there regarding shechitah, we do not rule like him, as the Rif and Rambam state, and as ruled in Yoreh Deah siman 7. The same principles apply to structures and fixtures, with distinctions between a built wall and a natural cave wall, and between articles that are actually rooted and those merely attached. Various sugyos in Bava Basra, Chullin, and Avodah Zarah are reconciled on that basis.
And some disagree... They maintain that here all attached property is treated as land, as the Rabbis say, "Anything attached to the ground is like the ground." The inclusive wording indicates no distinction. Bava Basra 65b supports this, because land was excluded through a general-specific-general hermeneutic requiring an item movable like money and vessels. Anything attached to land is not movable in that manner. See seif 3.
