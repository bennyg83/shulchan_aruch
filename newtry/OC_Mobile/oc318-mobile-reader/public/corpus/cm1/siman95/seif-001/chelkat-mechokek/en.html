Since the heirs are obligated to support the widow. See Bach, who carefully analyzes the Tur from Ketubot 95b and the Rosh. According to his reading, among the people of Yehudah, where the heirs may discharge the widow by paying her ketubah against her will and thus cease supporting her, her handiwork does not belong to them. Among the people of Galil, where she is supported against the heirs' will, her handiwork belongs to them. He therefore concludes that in a place with a custom like Yehudah, her handiwork likewise does not belong to the heirs. I have serious doubt about this. Baal HaMaor writes that the Gemara's question was not resolved and that the conclusion, "we learned, she who is supported," was stated only as a rejection. Ramban agrees. How then can she extract support from the heirs while withholding her handiwork? The heirs can say that even if the text reads "she who is supported," her handiwork still belongs to them. In particular, Rif and the Geonim did not understand the Gemara's question this way at all, and in their view her handiwork is always in exchange for support. Even Tosafot, who explain the question as above, can agree that the final statement was only a rejection, so that even according to the reading "she who is supported," her handiwork remains theirs. There is therefore no need to create an unsupported dispute between Tosafot and Rosh on one side and the other authorities. Nor is there any inference from the Tur's use of the word "obligated" rather than "give," because even among the people of Yehudah they are obligated to support her by the ketubah condition until they actually pay her ketubah.
