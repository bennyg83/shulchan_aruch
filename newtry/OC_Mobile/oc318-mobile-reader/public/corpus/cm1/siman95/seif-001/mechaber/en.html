The law governing which oaths apply to land and the law of consecrated property in the present era. This siman contains 6 seifim.
The following are matters concerning which a Torah oath is not administered: land, even land outside Eretz Yisrael; slaves; documents; consecrated property; and likewise property of non Jews. This applies whether the defendant admits part of the claim, denies it all and is contradicted by one witness, or is liable under the laws of bailees. Even if he was negligent with such property and it was lost, he is exempt from payment, whether he was an unpaid bailee, a paid bailee, or a borrower. If he expressly stipulated that he would pay, the law follows his stipulation. See above, siman 66, seif 40. A heset oath, however, is administered even concerning these matters, and an oath concerning them may also be imposed by gilgul. The consecrated property referred to above means property consecrated to Heaven. Property dedicated to the poor, to a synagogue, to a Torah scroll, or similar purposes is treated like ordinary private property for purposes of oaths. Rama: Some say that movable property that was later attached to land is not treated as land, while others disagree. Therefore one who borrowed a house and the house burned is exempt from payment. See below, siman 301.
