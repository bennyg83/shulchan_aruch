In the Rambam the text reads, "he takes a shevuat heset," and the Tur copies this wording. The Mechaber likewise writes without qualification above in siman 66, seif 39, at the beginning of this siman, and in siman 301, seif 3. In my humble opinion, it appears obvious that there is a scribal error in some texts and the wording should be: "If he claims, 'There never was such a matter,' he takes a shevuat heset; but if he claims it was lost," etc. I hesitated to emend the text because the Sema did not wish to do so and instead forced an interpretation at length. See there. The Bach likewise upholds the version before us, explaining that one who says, "There never was such a matter," is exempt even from shevuat heset through a migo that he could have said, "You did deposit it with me, but it was lost," in which case he would be exempt even from shevuat heset. The Bach ultimately leaves the matter requiring study in light of what the Mechaber writes at the beginning of this siman and in siman 301. In my humble opinion, I searched and found no such ruling among the early or later poskim. I did see that the Mordechai in the chapter HaDayanim writes that Rashbat brought proof that one does not take shevuat heset for total denial concerning servants, documents, and land from the incident of Rabbah bar Sharshom, where a rumor went out against him, etc., Bava Batra 33a, and the Rashbam explains likewise. But this proof is rejected, as Tosafot there write, because in that case the claimant did not make a definite claim; there was merely a rumor concerning him.
