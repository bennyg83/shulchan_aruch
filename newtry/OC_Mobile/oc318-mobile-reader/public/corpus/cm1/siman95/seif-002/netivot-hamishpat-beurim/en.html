He claimed two months, and so forth. See Shach, seif katan 12, who writes that if the time for payment has not yet arrived, the rental obligation is treated like land. Tumim explains that although we rule that rental accrues continuously from beginning to end, so that each day already creates a debt for that day's rent, payment is not due until the end. The reason this still remains connected to the land is that if, during the rental period, a violent person evicts the tenant from the house, the landlord must provide another house, as explained below in siman 312 seif 2. Thus, if the rent already paid is insufficient for the substitute premises, the landlord may have to supplement it from the rental amount. This is correct, and it is likewise the reason for Tosafot's statement in Bava Kamma 99 that before the due date rental is not considered an ordinary loan for purposes of kiddushin. Even according to the view that kiddushin made with a loan is effective, kiddushin made with accrued rental before its due date is not effective, for the reason given by Tumim. Ketzot HaChoshen cites this discussion as well.
