He claimed, etc.; and it was, etc.; for everything, etc. Rambam explains that the Sages' ruling applies specifically where the produce was entrusted for safekeeping. In sale, overreaching, and partial admission, where the original claim was not based on safekeeping, produce ready to be harvested is treated as movable property when it no longer needs the soil. This follows Ketubot and Gittin. Rambam nevertheless treats it as land for bailees, robbery, and certain creditor rules because it was delivered in attached form or because the creditor relied upon the land. Ramah and Tur dispute parts of this classification. Collection from heirs and damages follow their own rules. See citations 100, 115, 193, and 301.
Some say the case concerns, etc.; and others disagree, etc. Ran asks that the admitted portion should be 'here it is' and answers that the grapes were harvested. Although the Sages would then seemingly have to impose an oath, the original deposit was land and remains exempt even after harvesting. Rambam follows this view. Rashba holds that a claim for damage to land or payment for grapes already harvested is a movable-property claim. The dispute here parallels the dispute later in seif 6.
