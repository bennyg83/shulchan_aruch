Grapes standing ready to be harvested. Rambam's words in chapter 2 of Hilchot Sechirut are difficult. He writes that one who entrusts another with guarding something attached to the ground is exempt from the laws of bailees, even grapes standing ready to be harvested, because they remain legally like land for bailee law. Shulchan Aruch rules likewise in siman 301, apparently even if they no longer need the ground. See Sema in siman 193 and the extended discussion in Shulchan Aruch. It appears that Rambam follows his own position that bailee liability requires meshichah, as he writes in chapter 2 of Hilchot Sechirut. Grapes ready to be harvested cannot be acquired through meshichah while still attached. The chazakah acquisition used for land does not help because, as movable property for this purpose, they cannot be acquired through land chazakah. The only possible route is acquisition together with the land. Rambam in chapter 23 of Hilchot Mechirah writes that flax standing in the ground, when fully dry and no longer needing the earth, is acquired together with land like other movable property. See Maggid Mishneh there. But because bailee liability does not attach to the land itself, neither does it attach to the movable property acquired only together with that land. This is like Bava Kamma 117, where a cow was lying in another's field. According to the Rabbis, who hold that land cannot be stolen, the robber is not liable for the cow through the land either, as Rashi explains, because he did not perform meshichah on the cow and acquired it only together with the land. So too here, where the guardian's only mode of acquisition is together with land and no bailee liability attaches to land, no bailee liability attaches to the grapes either.
