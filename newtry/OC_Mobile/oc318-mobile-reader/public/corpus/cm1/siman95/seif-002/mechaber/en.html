If one claims grapes ready to be picked or dry grain ready to be harvested, and the defendant admits part and denies part, he swears concerning them as he would concerning other movable property, provided they no longer need the ground. Anything ready to be picked is regarded as already picked for purposes of denial and admission. If they still need the ground, they are regarded as land in every respect. {Rama: Some say this refers to a claim for the grapes' monetary value. If he claimed the grapes themselves and the defendant admitted some of them, then even if they are ready to be picked, the admission is considered an immediate tender of payment. Others disagree. They hold that if he claimed the grapes' value, they are regarded as movable property in every case. Rather, the case is where he claimed the grapes themselves, and the defendant admits some but says that he picked and ate them, so there is no immediate tender. See below, seif 6 and note 12.}
