(Seif 4) “The matter never occurred.” The Mechaber’s words are difficult, for he rules contrary to Rambam and the Tur, as Sema notes. The text should be emended: if he claims, “It never occurred,” he is liable to take hesset; if he claims that the document was lost, he is exempt. Maggid Mishneh explains that he is exempt even from hesset because the claimant cannot assert with certainty that the document remains in his possession. According to this, if the claimant states with certainty that it is still in his possession, hesset is certainly required, as stated in siman 301, seif 3. This also resolves a difficulty according to the Rosh’s view in siman 93 that a migo can exempt from an oath. Why is one who says, “It never occurred,” not exempt by virtue of a migo that he could say, “It was lost”? According to what I have written, the answer is that the defendant fears that the claimant may say, “I know with certainty that the document is still in your possession.” There is therefore no migo. When the Tur writes here that Raavad’s view does not appear correct, he means only that an uncertain claim does not impose the ordinary oath of bailees.
