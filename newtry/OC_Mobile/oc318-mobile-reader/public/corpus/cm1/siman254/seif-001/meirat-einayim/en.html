Even on Shabbat. Ordinarily making a kinyan sudar on Shabbat is prohibited because it resembles commercial activity, as explained in simanim 195 and 235 and in Orach Chaim siman 338. Here it is permitted because the kinyan itself is legally unnecessary, since the words of a dangerously ill person are already treated as written and delivered. It is done only to calm the donor's mind.
They make a kinyan on Shabbat. The reason is concern that the dangerously ill person's mind may become agitated, so the Sages permitted it. According to the first view, this applies specifically when he distributes all his property in the ordinary manner of a deathbed will among heirs, wife, and others, because it is then evident that his action is driven by fear of death. If he gives only part of his property, which healthy people also do, the gift actually requires a kinyan and the first view does not permit making that kinyan on Shabbat. The alternative view cited by Rama permits a kinyan even in a partial gift. Since the commercial prohibition is only rabbinic, the Sages were lenient for a dangerously ill person even where the kinyan is legally necessary.
