It is the animal, etc. The Rambam and Tur rule like Rabbi Yaakov, as the Rambam explains in Sotah chapter 3, because Rabbi Yaakov’s statement is presented last in Eruvin chapter 1. See there. Likewise in the Yerushalmi at the beginning of Bava Batra chapter 3, this is the latter version, “and some say a large goat.”
He sold him a flock, etc. From the statement that this applies specifically to the leader of the herd, showing that another animal does not effect acquisition of the entire herd. This is why the ruling says “specifically, etc.,” as Rashi explains there.
If he did not deliver, etc., he did not acquire, etc. As above.
Only. This is a textual error. See Sema.
If he delivered, etc. According to the first version in Kiddushin 27b. The second version differs because he said “acquire this one,” but ordinarily it agrees. See Tosafos there, s.v. “make.” It appears that this applies specifically, etc. The statement that he delivered them with one halter means even if he held only the hoof or hair of one, as Tosafos explains there and Rabbeinu Yerucham likewise writes. This is specifically mesirah, which is an act of holding analogous to chazakah in ten fields, as stated in the Gemara. Other modes of acquisition do not acquire the remainder through one item, as stated there. Chazakah is stronger, etc. The same applies to movable property by meshichah.
