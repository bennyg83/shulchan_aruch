Seif 6: You will acquire in the future. The Beit Yosef questions Rabbeinu, but his words can be reconciled. Rabbeinu first says that the Rambam interprets the phrase as future acquisition. The Gemara's question, “Who said to him, acquire?” therefore treats “and you will acquire” and “in order to acquire” similarly. According to the Rambam, saying “Draw this object in order to acquire it now” could be effective. Rashi, however, would invalidate even that language because “in order to acquire it” implies, “you may choose to acquire, but I am not presently conveying it.” Rabbeinu then cites the Rosh that when one says, “Draw this object in order to acquire it,” the acquisition is effective. The Rosh is unconcerned both with the Rambam's future-tense issue and with Rashi's concern that the seller has not conveyed. He limits Rashi's concern to “Draw this animal in order to acquire the vessels upon it,” because the thing drawn is not itself being acquired together with the vessels. In contrast, “Draw this object in order to acquire it” means simply “acquire it.” Rabbeinu then cites the Raavad as disagreeing with the Rambam regarding the wording “and you will acquire.” Rabbeinu was uncertain whether the Raavad rejects the Rambam's entire future-tense analysis and follows Rashi, or whether he accepts that concern but holds that “and you will acquire” means immediately after the drawing, while only “in order to acquire” sounds future. This explains Rabbeinu's cautious wording that the Raavad “also objected,” rather than saying that he agreed with the other view.
