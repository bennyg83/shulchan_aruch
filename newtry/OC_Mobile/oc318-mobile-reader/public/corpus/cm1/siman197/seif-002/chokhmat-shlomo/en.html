Seif 4, gloss: He sold one book, etc. Note: The Shulchan Aruch, Rama, and the poskim generally imply that pulling part of a sale does not complete acquisition of the remaining part. Yet Rashi in Avodah Zarah, citing a responsum of the Geonim, writes that if one sells a kor of wheat and sends a kav to the buyer's house, once the buyer takes possession of that kav the sale is completed. This appears to imply that when a kor is sold as a single unit, pulling one kav acquires the entire transaction. Tosafot accept Rashi's explanation, although their own wording could perhaps be read differently. The Gemara's language elsewhere seems to require pulling each item, contrary to this implication. One possible reconciliation is that separate animals sold together remain separate units, so pulling one does not acquire the others, whereas a kor sold as one collective quantity is a single sale and pulling part may acquire all. Still, the language of the poskim appears to reject such a distinction and to require acquisition of each portion in every case. It requires study why Rashi's apparent view was not cited.
