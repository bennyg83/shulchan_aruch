[Shulchan Aruch] Or he rode it. Note: This plainly implies that riding acquires by the law of meshichah, because the animal moves somewhat as a result of his action, as stated in the discussion that compares it to the prohibition of diverse species. This also follows according to the Rosh, who holds that riding alone does not acquire unless the rider also directs the animal with his feet. It therefore appears that the entire basis is meshichah. If so, riding should not acquire in a public domain, as is also indicated by the language of the Shulchan Aruch. At the beginning of seif 2 it states that meshichah acquires only in a side alley and not in a public domain, and then in seif 3 it states, "How does one acquire through meshichah? By riding it," implying that riding is effective only in a side alley. According to this, the ruling in seif 5 is difficult, where it says, "And if in a city, in a public domain," based on the Gemara, implying that riding does acquire in a public domain. According to the Rosh, that entire discussion concerns one who directs the animal with his feet. How then does he acquire in a public domain, where meshichah does not acquire? This requires study.
