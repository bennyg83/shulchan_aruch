Or he rode upon it. All the commentators ask on Rambam, who rules that riding alone acquires, although R. Yehudah in the name of Shmuel concludes that riding alone does not acquire and answers all the objections. In my view Rambam follows Ritzba, cited by Shitah Mekubetzet, that the Gemara says riding does not acquire only where the rider sits on the animal while it remains standing. If the animal actually walks because of the rider, he certainly acquires, because that is meshichah. The Gemara's unresolved issue of a rider where another person is leading concerns a different question: when the animal is seen moving, perhaps one person's action cancels the other's causal force, so it cannot be determined whether it moves because of the rider or because of the leader. In my novellae I discussed the sugya at length. I also wrote there that even according to the view in siman 200, seif 1, that four cubits can acquire in a commercial transaction, once the person rides he reveals that he wishes to acquire by riding and not by the rabbinic acquisition of four cubits. Even those who disagree in siman 268 and say that falling on an object does not reveal such intent speak specifically of falling, which is no kinyan at all. According to the opinion that riding itself is a valid d'oraisa-type kinyan, beginning that superior kinyan shows that he does not wish to rely on the inferior rabbinic one. Even among d'oraisa kinyanim, when one begins the stronger form we say he revealed intent not to acquire through the weaker, as with mesirah and meshichah in siman 198, seif 12. All the more so a rabbinic kinyan yields to the acquisition he deliberately began. This is why the Gemara asks about rider versus leader rather than simply awarding the animal to whichever entered the four cubits first. But according to the view that riding is not a kinyan at all, there is no such revelation, just as with falling, and the rider can acquire through four cubits according to those who recognize that acquisition even in sale.
