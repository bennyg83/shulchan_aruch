Who rode. This is the view of the Rif and Rambam, who hold that riding alone effects acquisition, unlike the Rosh and Tur, who hold that riding does not acquire until he causes the animal to move with his legs. See the Tur, seif 5. Ibid.
Who led it. Since the Mechaber and Rama rule without qualification here, it appears that they hold that pulling and leading acquire all animals. The Beit Yosef explicitly writes that the halachah follows the Rif and Ramah, who hold this way, and not the Rosh and Tur, who write that with a donkey and camel one of these methods does not acquire. The Rambam's view is cited by the Mechaber in siman 271, where he also holds that both methods acquire all animals, whether by sale or gift or from ownerless property. He writes, however, that this is specifically where one person seeks to acquire the animal. If two people simultaneously seek to acquire a camel and one pulls while the other leads, then leading does not count in the presence of pulling. See the Sema there.
And it ran. It appears that this refers to striking it with a stick. If he leads it by voice, it need not run before him, because the very fact that he led it suffices. It would therefore appear appropriate to reverse the wording of the gloss so that 'he led it by voice' follows 'it ran before him.' Ibid.
