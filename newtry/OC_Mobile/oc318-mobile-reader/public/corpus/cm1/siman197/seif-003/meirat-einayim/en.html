Or if he rode upon it. This is the view of Rif and Rambam, who hold that riding alone effects acquisition, unlike the Rosh and Tur, who hold that riding does not acquire until he also moves the animal with his feet. See the Tur, seif 5.
Or if he led it by voice, etc. Since the Mechaber and Rama state the rule here without qualification, it implies that they hold that meshichah and leading acquire all animals. Beit Yosef explicitly writes that the halacha follows Rif and Ramah, who hold this way, and not the view of the Rosh and Tur in this siman, who write concerning a donkey and camel that one of these acts does not acquire. The Mechaber writes Rambam's view below in siman 271, and Rambam likewise holds that both methods acquire all animals, whether in a sale, gift, or ownerless property. He writes, however, that this applies only where one person comes to acquire. If two people simultaneously come to acquire a camel and one pulls it while the other leads it, leading does nothing in a case where it competes with meshichah. See the Sema there.
And it ran before him. It appears that this refers to striking it with a stick. With leading by voice there is no need for the animal to run before him, since he has already led it. It would therefore seem preferable to place the gloss concerning leading with a loud voice after the words "and it ran before him."
See below siman 271 concerning these laws. Meaning, in Shulchan Aruch siman 271 the law of acquisition is discussed as explained above, but it does not state there that he must say to him, etc. See also siman 192, seif 2, where this is discussed regarding chazakah of land.
