It need not be stated if, etc. This is the opening Mishna of Bava Metzia concerning one riding and one leading. See Sema, seif katan 5 and 6. The discussion there follows the initial assumption that riding does not acquire. See Tosafot there, s.v. if so, who ask why the cases are not combined. See below, siman 271, seif 1. Rambam and Shulchan Aruch there follow the passage's conclusion, but explain that the Gemara's phrase 'there is one aspect' applies specifically where one person pulls and the other leads, so that one does not acquire in the place of the other's act. They rule like the second version, following their general practice, that leading a camel does not acquire where another pulls it, although in other circumstances it does. Rosh writes that riding alone does not acquire, following Shmuel, but in Hilkhot Kilayim he explains that Rif omitted Shmuel because the Gemara's resolutions are strained. Rambam likewise rejects Shmuel and follows the anonymous Mishna that one seated in a wagon incurs lashes. See Beit Yosef here and in Yoreh Deah.
