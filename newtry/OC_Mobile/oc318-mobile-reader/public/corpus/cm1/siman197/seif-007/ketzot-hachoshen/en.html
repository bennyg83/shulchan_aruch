After thirty days. Ran rules that if one says, "Pull this cow, but do not acquire it until after thirty days," the original meshichah can operate retroactively if at the end of the thirty days the animal stands in a place such as a meadow where acquisition can be completed, even without the word "from now." This appears difficult from the opening of Perek HaMafkid. There one version treats the custodian as though the owner said, "My cow is acquired to you from now when it is stolen and you choose to pay," while another treats the transfer as beginning only shortly before the theft. The Gemara says that one practical difference is whether the cow is then standing in a meadow. According to Ran, however, even the second version should work in a meadow through the original meshichah. Shitah Mekubetzet resolves the point. If the acquisition is understood as beginning with the original meshichah but finishing only near the theft, the animal would have to remain continuously in a place suitable for meshichah throughout the interval. If at any point it enters the public domain, the original possessory act is interrupted. Since an animal ordinarily moves about, that is unrealistic. By contrast, where the transfer is stated as taking effect from the original time, later movement into the public domain does not matter because the acquisition already took effect. This also clarifies Ran's otherwise difficult statement that where "from now" was said, a meadow is not essential and even the public domain or the transferor's domain can suffice. The proof is that under the first version of Perek HaMafkid the cow can wander during the entire interval and the acquisition still stands. If the transfer were only being completed at the later time through the original meshichah, continual presence in an acquisition domain would be needed. Tosafot in Yevamot, however, understand Rabbi Yochanan's "from now and after thirty days" as a partial acquisition beginning now and completing at the end. According to them it is enough that the animal be in a meadow at the final moment; it need not remain there throughout. This is why they do not give Shitah Mekubetzet's answer to the custodial case. The better reading of the second version in Perek HaMafkid is therefore that the implied statement itself is made only shortly before the theft, not at the time of the original meshichah. If so, when the cow is standing in the meadow at that later moment there is no new meshichah at all. This preserves Ran's analysis and accords with the interpretation I defended at the end of siman 61.
Standing in a meadow. Sema says the same certainly applies in the purchaser's domain or in a domain belonging to both parties, but not in the public domain or the seller's domain. Shach objects and says Maggid Mishneh and Ran treat the meadow as only an ordinary example, and that even the public domain can work where the acquisition was stated "from now." In fact Ran's discussion shows that a meadow is specifically needed when the acquisition is not fully effective from the original time. The Gemara's dispute whether the side of the public domain is like a meadow proves that the location matters. Rambam, who does not let the original meshichah operate where the transfer is only after thirty days, must therefore be understood as discussing "from now and after thirty days." According to Rabbi Yochanan, that language leaves part of the acquisition to be completed at the end, and the animal must then stand in a domain capable of supporting completion. Thus a meadow is indeed specific. The Shach's proof from siman 210 points the other way. There the Sema and Tur say that the unborn animal must be in the recipient's domain or in a meadow when it is born. They do not mention the public domain. A difficulty remains: an unborn animal is future property, and ordinarily saying "when it is born" resembles "acquire only later," where an original meshichah should not suffice even if the animal is in a meadow. The answer is that a fetus is partially treated as already in existence. Rashbam in Perek Mi SheMet explains that the defect is mainly in the language, and saying "when it is born" allows the acquisition to complete once it becomes fully fit for acquisition. If no aspect of acquisition existed now, a present act could never create acquisition later. Since the fetus is partially in the world, a partial acquisition can begin now and complete at birth when the animal is in the recipient's domain or a meadow. Mishneh LaMelech raised a different question against Rambam: why does "from now and after thirty days" not mean the body from now and fruits after thirty days, as with a father writing property to his son from now and after death? Tosafot in Yevamot already answer that "from now and after death" naturally indicates a present transfer of the body because the whole purpose is to remove the property from later heirs. That special context does not apply to an ordinary delayed sale.
