In the public domain. This requires study, for according to the Rama the Mechaber's words appear contradictory. At the beginning he writes that even in the public domain there is no acquisition, because it is not the normal manner to acquire there, except for an important person, while at the end he writes "or if they were in the public domain," implying that in the public domain anyone acquires because the crowd presses there. It appears that the Rosh and Tur had a different text of the Gemara. The Mechaber copied Rambam according to Rambam's text, while the Rama added the Tur's ruling according to his text, and the two are opposites in the same case. It may perhaps be reconciled with difficulty, but requires study. Sema. The Taz writes that the Sema correctly questions the Rama, but wonders why he did not also question the first gloss, "or in an alley," since that too cannot fit the Mechaber's language. According to Rambam, the essential distinction between an important person and another person is specifically in an alley, whereas the Rama rules categorically that in an alley every person acquires.
