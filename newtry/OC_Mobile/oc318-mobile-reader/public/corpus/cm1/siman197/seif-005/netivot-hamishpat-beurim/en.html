One possible answer follows Tosafot in Bava Metzia 9, who imply that if riding acquires, it does so through a combination of mesirah and the animal's movement caused by the rider. Although Tosafot also require that it move somewhat because of him, they can hold that mesirah and this partial meshichah combine. Shitah Mekubetzet says similarly, except that with a found object mesirah cannot combine because no one delivered it to him. In a sale, however, mesirah is relevant. Since mesirah itself belongs in the public domain while meshichah ordinarily belongs in a side alley, but the riding acquisition is completed by combining both, it is reasonable not to distinguish rigidly between the two domains. Alternatively, the case can be one where he rides from the public domain into a side alley. He need not move far inside the alley; once the whole animal has been drawn into it, acquisition occurs, as explained in siman 198, seif 14. In an ordinary public domain where people do not crowd, riding may fail because the earlier public-domain riding was not the normal commercial method and the later acquisition is not completed. But if he rode from the public domain into the alley and there completed full meshichah, bringing the entire animal into the alley, he acquires even if the public domain itself was not crowded. The improper manner at the beginning does not matter once he ultimately completes a full ordinary kinyan in the proper place.
