And if it is in a city, in the public domain. This requires study. According to Rama's gloss, which explains the Mechaber's language according to the Rambam as referring to the public domain, the Mechaber appears self contradictory. At the beginning he writes that even in the public domain riding does not acquire for an ordinary person, because riding there is not his normal mode, unless he is an important person, a woman, or otherwise unable to walk comfortably. Yet at the end he writes, "or they were in the public domain," implying that in the public domain riding acquires for anyone because passersby press between the rider and the animal and the animal may slip away. In the Derishah I wrote that the Rosh and Tur had a different version of the Gemara from Rashi, the Rambam, and the Mechaber. The Mechaber copied the Rambam according to his version, while Rama inserted the Tur's wording from a different version, and the two formulations conflict. It is possible, though strained, to reconcile them by saying that even the Tur agrees that in a truly crowded public domain, where many people press one another, riding acquires for any person. The Tur speaks of a public thoroughfare that has passersby but is not so crowded, where the rationale of people separating the rider from his animal does not apply. There an ordinary person is embarrassed to ride although people are present, while an important person, woman, or one otherwise unable to walk normally is not. The Ir Shushan appears to have understood Rama this way, because in the latter clause he added the word "place" and wrote, "in the public domain, in a place where many people press," indicating that within the public domain itself there are different situations. But this is strained, for Rama should have explained the distinction, and the Tur should likewise have distinguished one public domain from another. This requires study.
