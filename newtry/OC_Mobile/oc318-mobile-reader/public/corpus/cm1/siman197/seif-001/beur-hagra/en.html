An animal, etc. Kiddushin 25b, “That is what he said, etc.,” according to Tosafos’ explanation there that “by mesirah” means even by mesirah and certainly by meshichah. Likewise Bava Batra chapter 5 teaches: “A ship is acquired by mesirah, according to Rabbi; the Sages say it is not acquired until he pulls it.” There is no authority who says an animal cannot be acquired by meshichah, for the opening Mishnah of Bava Metzia discusses one riding and one leading an animal, and the Gemara asks, “Is there an authority who says leading does not acquire?” Rabbi Elazar, who says in Kiddushin that an animal is acquired by mesirah, himself rules there that leading in the city acquires. The case is one of sale. Shmuel’s question, “But did we not learn mesirah?” and the discussion in Bava Metzia, “I heard from Mar Shmuel, etc., leading alone, etc.; I say riding, etc.; let us examine, etc.,” show that Rav’s statement means specifically by meshichah.
(Collected note) An animal, etc. This also appears from there, 7b, “your cow by meshichah.” If mesirah were sufficient, the baraisa should have taught the simpler mode, mesirah, which would work according to all views. (End of note.)
Some say that even, etc. Following the anonymous Mishnah and the Sages.
Some say, etc. In accordance with Rav and the Sages of the baraisa, and according to Rashi, who explains that the Mishnah refers to mesirah and not meshichah, while Rav says that meshichah also works. Nearly all poskim agree with the first opinion.
(Collected note) Some say, etc. Rabbeinu Tam explains that mesirah is stronger than meshichah because it takes place before the seller and from hand to hand. Therefore mesirah acquires in the public domain and in a courtyard belonging to neither party, and certainly in a side alley and jointly owned courtyard. When it says here that an animal is acquired by meshichah, it means even by meshichah. Tosafos and the Rosh disagree, holding meshichah stronger. They also reject all the above requirements, maintaining that mesirah need not occur in the seller’s presence or hand to hand, as explained above, siman 138 seif 8, and that it does not acquire in a side alley or jointly owned courtyard. See Tosafos in Kiddushin there and at the beginning of chapter 5 of Bava Batra, and the Rosh. Their words are clear, as explained in siman 158 there, yet the Rama did not comment there. (End of note.)
