Seif 2: Lifting acquires in every place. Note: Ketzot HaChoshen writes that even where an object is lifted through the purchaser's force rather than literally held in his hand, acquisition works in the public domain and in the seller's domain, because lifting works everywhere and there is no reason to distinguish the manner of lifting. According to the Sema's explanation, however, one could distinguish. The reason lifting works everywhere is that an object held in one's hand is treated as located in one's domain. If the object is lifted only through his force and is not actually in his hand, that rationale is absent, and such lifting might resemble pulling or delivery, which work only in appropriate domains. Nevertheless, Tosafot in Kiddushin proves that lifting through one's force works even in the seller's domain. Likewise, Chullin indicates acquisition of the contents of a dovecote even where the dovecote itself remains the seller's. Since this form of lifting works in the seller's domain, where other modes do not, it is evidently superior to ordinary pulling and equivalent to actual lifting. There is therefore no reason to distinguish the public domain from the seller's domain. The practical ruling of Ketzot HaChoshen is correct.
