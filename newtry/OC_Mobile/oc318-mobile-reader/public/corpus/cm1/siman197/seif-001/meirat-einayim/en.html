It is not acquired by mesirah. The Beit Yosef writes that this is the view of Tosafot, the Rosh, and most authorities, and that the Tur erred. In the Derishah and Hagahot Darkei Moshe I demonstrated, in my view conclusively, that Tosafot, the Rosh, and most authorities agree with the Tur that mesirah does acquire in this situation when the animal stands in the public domain or a courtyard belonging to neither party, places where ordinary meshichah does not acquire. The Sages did not require him to drag the animal from the public domain into his own property or a side alley, but allowed mesirah. This applies specifically to an animal, like a ship, whose slight movement is comparable to meshichah because once moved it tends to continue by itself. Since the act has the character of meshichah, the Sages were lenient where full meshichah into an appropriate domain would be burdensome. Other movables cannot be acquired by mesirah even in the public domain, as the Tur writes in the name of Ri and the Rosh in siman 198:12. See the Derishah below.
