(It is like any other unavoidable accident) The same applies when one rents a house and a dangerous change of climate or epidemic, Heaven protect us, forces most inhabitants to flee. The loss falls upon the landlord. First, this is a regional calamity, because houses in the city cannot be inhabited. The Mordechai's ruling that a governmental prohibition on study was not a regional calamity rested on the fact that most residents did not flee; where most must flee, it certainly is. Second, even apart from that classification, it is an unavoidable accident unknown to both parties, and the landlord, who seeks to extract rent, bears the loss. I saw a printed ruling in an edition of the Rif requiring the tenant to pay all rent, based on a responsum of Maimonides, but the responsum provides no proof. The rule stated here appears correct.
