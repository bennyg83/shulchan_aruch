If it occurs in the middle of the day, he does not pay him, etc. Shaar Mishpat cites Responsa Rosh Yosef concerning Reuven, who prepaid Shimon twenty coins to clean stains from twenty garments. After Shimon completed half the work, Reuven said he had no more garments and demanded return of ten coins. The responsum held that Reuven loses the prepaid balance. Shaar Mishpat agrees with the result but explains why: had Reuven not prepaid, he could have stopped because he no longer needed the work, comparable to a worker prevented by rain in the middle of the day. But once the money was prepaid, the case resembles a chartered ship and specified cargo lost midway, where prepaid hire need not be returned, and Rama's rule concerning a prepaid house rental when the tenant dies. Since Shimon remains ready to fulfill the contract and Reuven lacks the garments, Reuven bears the loss.
