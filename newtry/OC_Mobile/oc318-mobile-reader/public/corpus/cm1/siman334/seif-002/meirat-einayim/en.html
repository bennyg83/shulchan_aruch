He gives him nothing. This too follows the general principle above. Most fields in the world, once sufficient rain has fallen, do not require additional watering. Therefore landlord and worker are equally positioned in their knowledge of the relevant possibility, and we say the burden of proof rests upon the claimant. This is so even if the landlord did not show the field to the worker beforehand. This differs from a case where rain failed and this particular field uniquely required extra digging or watering. Since most fields do not fail for that reason, a landowner who knows the character of his own field should have stipulated. Tosafot writes this.
But if the river came, he pays, etc. The reason the ruling does not distinguish here between whether the river usually comes or not, or whether the workers are local residents, as it does in seif 5 regarding a river that stopped, is that an ordinary river is expected to come. That is why channels are dug to carry its water through the field. The owner knows the layout of his own field and channels, whereas the workers, even if local, do not know whether the owner dug channels so the water would reach this field. But whether a river commonly stops is something all the townspeople know. Tosafot explains this in HaOmanin.
For he should have informed them. See what I wrote immediately above.
If the owner inspected it, etc. Meaning, even if he did not show the field to the workers when hiring them, since the owner himself saw it beforehand and found it fit to be dug, he is no longer considered to have caused the workers a loss. What else could he have done? But if he did not inspect it first, he should have considered that perhaps the ground was too wet to dig. By hiring workers without checking, he appears to have accepted responsibility to pay them in any event.
For a specified job, etc. From Rashi it also appears that he must hire them for a specified field. Otherwise the worker can say, 'Give me another field fit to dig. Who says you hired me specifically for this field?' At the beginning of siman 335 the Tur and Mechaber write that just as a worker may demand another comparable job when the original job ends, so too the owner may transfer him to another comparable job.
