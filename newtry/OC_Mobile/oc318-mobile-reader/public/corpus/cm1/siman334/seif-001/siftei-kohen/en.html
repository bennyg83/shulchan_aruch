And similarly with every unavoidable event that occurred, etc. See Responsa Rama, siman 19; Responsa Rashdam, simanim 295 and 415; and Mabit, part 1, siman 205.
Nevertheless, some disagree, etc. In Darchei Moshe the Rama cites the responsum of Maharam in Mordechai, Perek HaOmanin, and Responsa Rashba, siman 1108, in the name of the Raavad, which disagree. He then writes that Terumat HaDeshen, siman 329, rules like Maharam, but that it appears there that if the rent was paid in advance he need not return it. I do not understand this, because Maharam himself explicitly holds there that if he already received the entire rent he need not return it, except that he must refund the value of the benefit he receives from the house being vacant, calculated like an idle worker, since he is free to use it as he wishes. Terumat HaDeshen writes the same, namely that he need not return the rent itself, but must refund the value of his saved effort like an idle worker. Here in the gloss, however, one can say that he need not return even that amount, because Rashba disagrees and the burden of proof is on the claimant. But Darchei Moshe and the Sema, seif katan 4, clearly imply that the ruling was based on Terumat HaDeshen, and this is difficult. If the intention is merely that he need not return the rent already received for the period during which the tenant no longer wishes to reside there, while he must still return the idle worker value, there was no need to derive this from the dissenting opinions, for Maharam himself explicitly rules that if he already received the rent he need not return it. This requires study. As a matter of law, it appears to me that Maharam's view is primary. Although the Beit Yosef at the end of siman 312 rules like Rashba and not Maharam, the Bach there refutes his position. Rashba's main proof is that a rental for each day is considered a sale, but Tosafos in Perek HaZahav, Bava Metzia 56b, and at the beginning of Avodah Zarah 15a, write that the principle that a daily rental is a sale applies only to onaah, where Scripture included it, and not elsewhere. Terumat HaDeshen, siman 318, and Maharshal, Responsa siman 43, write likewise. Even if one were to say that a rental for each day is a sale, the heirs still need not pay, because one can say that each day is a separate sale. Furthermore, even in an actual sale, if an unavoidable event occurs in circumstances where the seller should have stipulated concerning it, would the sale not be void? Here too, since the reason is that the owner is treated like a worker and should have stipulated, the rental is void. Nevertheless, if he already received the rent he need not return it, because by paying him the tenant accepted that the money would be his even if an unavoidable event occurred, as Tosafos in Perek HaOmanin, page 79b, and the poskim and Terumat HaDeshen, siman 329, explain. The practical difference is that he must refund the amount corresponding to an idle worker for the benefit of having the house free for his own use. Examine carefully.
Because of a change, etc. Mahari Weil writes in that responsum that if one wishes to argue that this should not be considered an unavoidable event because the child would not have fled on his own had his father not fled, this is incorrect. See there. Responsa Maharam Padua, siman 26, implies that this applies only where a minority fled, but if, Heaven forbid, the epidemic is severe and everyone fled, it is a calamity affecting the entire region. This is similar to the Hagahot Ashri in Perek HaOmanin in the name of Or Zarua: if the interruption results from a decree of the ruler in the city and the teachers cannot teach, it is a calamity affecting the entire region and he receives his full wages. Maharam Padua further implies that even if a majority fled it is not yet considered a regional calamity. He writes that the gloss deliberately shifted from the illness of one child to a decree of the ruler, which affects all teachers; had illness of a majority been enough, it could have said that where illness is widespread, such as an epidemic or other seasonal disease, it is a regional calamity. I do not agree. The Gemara in Perek HaMekabel states that if most of the valley's fields were devastated, this is a regional calamity, and all the poskim agree. The Tur likewise writes this above at the beginning of siman 322. Rather, the Gemara mentions a governmental decree because that is a clear and fixed example, whereas with illness one must determine whether a majority are ill. Therefore, if it is known that the majority are ill, or that the majority fled the city, it is likewise a regional calamity. See the rulings of Maharam of Tiktin in Mordechai, Krakow edition, at the beginning of Nezikin, concerning one who rented a house, entered it, and afterward there was a change in the air and an epidemic, Heaven preserve us, forcing them to flee. He ruled that the tenant must pay. His words require study as practical law. See also Responsa Maharam Padua, siman 434, and Responsa Rabbi Eliyahu ibn Chaim.
