One who hires, etc.; and likewise in every, etc. This follows Rosh's interpretation of the Gemara.

(Likkut) And if, etc. See Hagahos Ashri, s.v. "and also," concerning if the cancellation, etc. End.
Who, etc.; because the employer, etc. Meaning, the landlord receives the rent and therefore has the legal position of a worker, who should have stipulated against the contingency. This differs from the case of a ship on 79a, which concerns an unspecified ship and this particular wine, as Tosafot explain. Nevertheless, payment is owed for the period of actual use, as the Gemara states concerning the donkey: the owner can say that had the renter wished, etc.
Nevertheless, etc. A lease is a temporary sale, as stated on 56b, and the landlord therefore cannot remove the tenant during the term, as explained at the beginning of siman 312.
Therefore, etc. Darkei Moshe cites Terumat HaDeshen, who rules like Maharam but implies that if rent was prepaid it need not be returned. Mordechai states this explicitly regarding heirs. Yet according to Rosh, the workers' loss rests on the claimant's burden of proof, while Rama understands the reason as failure to stipulate. On that basis even prepaid rent should be returned. The word 'therefore' means that because the matter is disputed, the tenant may retain what he possesses. Alternatively, Rama may mean that the heirs may place a comparable tenant in the property, as in the case of a worker who completes his task early, and if they do not, the landlord must deduct the value of having the property vacant. Rashba certainly agrees to that deduction. See simanim 5 and 316.
