The loss is the worker's, and the employer gives him nothing. Meaning not even payment as an idle worker. The Tur gives the general rule, which Rama immediately writes as well. The employer is liable only where he knew the relevant circumstance and the worker did not, because the employer should have disclosed it and stipulated accordingly. If both knew, or neither knew, the worker bears the loss, because the burden of proof rests upon the claimant and the worker is the party seeking payment.
If it was a regional calamity. See siman 321. Rama's cross reference to siman 321 rather than siman 322, where the principal rules of regional calamity in leasing fields are located, suggests that here he intends specifically to allude to what he wrote in siman 321:1, that in some cases of regional calamity the worker's hand is on the upper side. He cites Maharam there concerning a teacher. I wrote there at length that in my view Mordechai's Maharam does not mean what Rama attributes to him.
The landlord has the status of the worker, etc. Meaning, just as regarding a worker we say that the party seeking to extract money bears the burden of proof, so too here the landlord who seeks to collect from the successors of the deceased tenant is treated as the claimant. Those who disagree hold that rental of a house is different because rental for the term is treated like a sale of the house for that period. Rashba's responsum explicitly gives this reason.
