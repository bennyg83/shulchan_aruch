Some disagree. The Shach writes that as a matter of practical law the first opinion appears correct. The main proof of the dissenting authorities is the principle that "a lease for its term is a sale," but Tosafot in Bava Metzia 56b and Avodah Zarah 15a write that this principle is stated only in the law of onaah, where Scripture specifically includes rental, and not generally. Terumat HaDeshen, siman 318, and Responsa Maharshal, siman 43, also write so. Moreover, even if a lease for its term is treated as a sale, the heirs need not pay for the period lost to an unavoidable accident, because each day can be viewed as a separate temporary sale. Further, even in an actual sale, where an unavoidable event occurs in circumstances in which the seller should have stipulated about it, the sale may be void. Here too, since the rationale is that the employer is analogous to a worker and should have stipulated, the lease is canceled. However, if the lessor already received the rent, he need not return it. By paying in advance, the tenant demonstrated consent that the lessor keep it even if an unavoidable accident occurs, as Tosafot, Bava Metzia 79b, and the poskim write, and as Terumat HaDeshen states in siman 329. Even then, however, the lessor must return the amount corresponding to the benefit of having the house free for his own use, calculated as an idle-worker type reduction. Shach.
A change of circumstances. The responsum of Mahari Weil there states: If you would argue that this should not be considered an unavoidable circumstance, since he would not have fled on his own had his father not caused him to flee, it appears that this is incorrect; see there. From Responsa Maharam of Padua, siman 86, it appears that this is specifically when only a minority fled, but if the plague, Heaven spare us, is so severe that everyone fled, it is deemed a calamity affecting the entire region. This is as stated in Hagahot Ashri, chapter HaOmanin, in the name of Or Zarua: if the interruption resulted from a decree of the local ruler and the teachers cannot teach, it is a regional calamity and the teacher is paid his entire wage. See above, beginning of siman 321 in the gloss. It further appears there that even if the majority fled it is not a regional calamity. This does not appear correct to me, for the Gemara in chapter HaMekabel states that where most of the fields were blighted it is a regional calamity, and this is agreed upon by all the poskim; the Tur likewise rules so at the beginning of siman 322. Rather, it appears that he mentioned a decree of the ruler because that case is clear-cut, whereas with illness one must determine whether the majority are ill. But indeed, if it is known that the majority are ill, or that the majority fled the city, it is a regional calamity. This appears correct to me. See the ruling of Maharam of Tiktin in Mordechai at the beginning of Nezikin, concerning one who rented a house to another, the tenant entered it, and afterward the climate changed and a plague, Heaven spare us, arose, forcing them to flee. He ruled that the tenant must pay the landlord. His ruling requires study as a matter of practical law; see there. See also Responsa Maharam, Prague edition, siman 434, and Responsa Raanach, siman 53. Shach.
