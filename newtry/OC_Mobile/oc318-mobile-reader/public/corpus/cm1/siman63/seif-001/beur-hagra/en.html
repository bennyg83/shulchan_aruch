Some disagree. This is the view of the Ramah cited above, supported by Bava Batra 167a, "He said to him, 'This is my signature...'" Rambam regards such a case as uncommon. (Collected note) One who produces... through "always." And if the witnesses... All of this is from Rambam. His language is: "It appears to me that if the witnesses to the document come and themselves testify regarding their handwriting, collection may be made with it." He follows his own position that a witness may testify regarding his handwriting only if he remembers the underlying matter, unlike others, who testify merely to the handwriting. This resolves Beit Yosef's difficulty, and so it appears from the Shach. It seems that even those who disagree with Rambam concede that ordinarily the witnesses remember the matter, as implied by the Gemara in Kesubos: "When you analyze the matter..." The Mishnah states without qualification, "This one says, 'This is my handwriting...'" According to all opinions, whether Rambam or his disputants, we need not ask whether they remember, and no additional witness need join them. It follows that there is no practical dispute here: even Rambam would agree that mere recognition of the signatures does not suffice, as the gloss states, contrary to the Shulchan Aruch's interpretation of Rambam that it does. (End of note.)
