Or others testify about them that they saw, etc. This means specifically that they saw the witnesses sign this document. It is not sufficient for them merely to say that they recognize the signatures of those who signed, for, as stated immediately above, even if the document has been authenticated, collection cannot be made with it.
And if the witnesses to the document testify that these are their signatures, etc. In the Perishah I proved that this applies even if they do not remember signing this document, but merely say, "We recognize that these are our signatures." One may collect with it, because authentication by the witnesses themselves is stronger than authentication by other people, regarding whom it is possible to say that they erred. The Mechaber states these three clauses in ascending order of novelty. This also explains the Rama's concluding gloss that some disagree and hold that recognition of their signatures is ineffective. He means that in a case where the witnesses do not remember signing this document but merely recognize their signatures, such recognition is ineffective. Regarding the first two clauses, namely where they saw the loan or where others testify that they saw the witnesses sign, no one disagrees. Likewise, if the witnesses to the document themselves testify, "These are our signatures, and we remember signing this document," there is no basis to say that their testimony should be inferior to that of others who saw them sign. See also the end of siman 31, where the Mechaber states this law as well. The Rama does not mention the dissenting view there, relying on what he wrote here, which is the primary source of the law. The Tur records both opinions. See Darkei Moshe and its glosses there, where he cites the Rambam's view that witnesses to a document who come to authenticate it must remember the loan, as stated at the end of siman 46. Nevertheless, if they said that these were their signatures and then left, we presume that they also remembered the loan.
