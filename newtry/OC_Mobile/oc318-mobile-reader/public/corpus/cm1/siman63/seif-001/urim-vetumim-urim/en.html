This document. Meaning a document like this one, for the same amount and against the same person. Therefore not only are the holder's documents against other people unaffected and remain valid, but even if he has two documents against this same person, one of them valid, he may collect from encumbered property using the one with the later date, Bach and Shach, unlike Knesset HaGedolah, who questioned this.
We do not collect with it. But other documents may be collected, as I wrote. Meirat Einayim explains that we say this person is not generally a forger. Rather, he truly has an oral debt against the debtor and sought a forgery only to turn it into a documented loan, Sema. This wording is imprecise even though Rosh writes similarly. If that were the reason, then at least where the borrower claims, "There never was such a debt," the lender should be able to collect this document from unencumbered property, because according to the theory the underlying oral loan is real and the borrower is not claiming payment. Yet the law says that we do not collect with this document at all, implying that even a denial of the original debt can defeat it. The correct explanation is that we suspect perhaps the lender has some old and uncertain monetary claim against this person for the same amount. People do not always separate themselves from doubtful money, and he may therefore have sought to create a forged document for that amount. There is no clearly established debt, so this document cannot be collected. His other documents remain presumptively valid.
With it, etc. If witnesses saw this document in Nisan and have identifying marks proving that it is the same document now produced in beit din, and other witnesses testify that the holder only began seeking a forgery in Iyar, the document is not weakened retroactively. We do not presume a later defect existed earlier, Bach, Perishah, and Shach.
Ever. Even if the lender seized property, Sema holds that we remove it from him. See Tumim, where I wrote that this depends on the dispute between the Mechaber and Rama above in siman 58, seif 2. If a person once produced a document whose witnesses were found disqualified, even through a disqualification obvious to everyone, or brought witnesses before beit din who were found false, he may later produce another valid and properly validated document, and the later document is not thereby weakened, Knesset HaGedolah in the name of many authorities.
Who saw the loan. Even if they know nothing of this particular document, once the underlying loan is true and a document is now before us, we do not go so far as to fear that the loan they saw was merely oral and has already been paid, while the present document is forged. Beit Yosef, Sema, and Bach understand the law this way. Shach disagrees and holds that the document itself has only the status of an oral loan, so if the borrower says, "I paid," he is believed. Only if he says, "There never was such a debt," is he liable. Shach's reading does not appear correct, as I wrote, because then the ruling would teach little and the language does not support it.
If the witnesses to the document, etc. See Tumim. The case is not where they say, "We do not remember." Rather, they testify without qualification, and Rambam holds that we presume they remember the underlying event, because had they not remembered they would have said so. The document is therefore valid. If, however, they expressly say that they do not remember the transaction itself and know only that these are their signatures, everyone agrees the document is invalid. The dissent cited by Rama holds that unqualified testimony does not suffice in this case. The witnesses must expressly say, "We remember the substance of the testimony." But if they do say that, everyone agrees the document is valid. See there.
