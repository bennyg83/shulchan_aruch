If it was seen in his possession before the time when he began seeking forged documents, the document is not retroactively invalidated, because at that earlier time it had a presumption of validity. So writes the Bach. This means either that the document was authenticated when it was then seen, or that the witnesses know with certainty that the present document is the same one they saw before, such as where it has a distinctive identifying mark. Alternatively, if they say, ‘We saw the document in his possession previously and then recognized the signatures,’ their testimony helps where the debtor claims that he never borrowed. The debtor may nevertheless claim payment and say that this is another, forged document, unless it was already authenticated or has a reliable identifying mark. This appears correct.
They testify that they saw the loan, etc. The wording implies that even if the witnesses do not remember signing, and the transaction may have been only an oral loan, nevertheless, once others recognize the signatures of the witnesses appearing on the document, the forms of testimony combine and collection may be made with this document. The Sema explains it so, based on the Beit Yosef’s understanding of the Ramah, and the Bach agrees. This does not appear correct to me. Since we suspect forgery, how can the testimonies combine? There is even greater reason to fear that the creditor made an oral loan, was later repaid, and then forged a document so that the debtor could not plead payment or so that he could collect from purchasers. Rather, the Ramah should be understood as I explain below in seif katan 4 regarding the Ran. Recognition of the signatures is not effective in any manner when the witnesses themselves do not remember the execution and the document is under a specific suspicion of forgery. The fact that they remember the underlying loan does not authenticate this particular instrument. It proves at most that a loan once occurred. To collect by virtue of the document, there must be reliable proof that this is the instrument they executed for that loan, or independent authentication free of the present suspicion.
And if the witnesses to the document, etc. The Beit Yosef and Kesef Mishneh offer two explanations of the Rambam, cited above at the end of siman 31; the Bach cites them, and Mahari ibn Sasson, responsum 200, agrees. In my opinion, neither explanation is satisfactory. The first says that the case is one in which the witnesses’ signatures are authenticated from another source, for otherwise, since they do not remember the loan, their testimony is ineffective under the Rambam’s ruling in Hilchot Edut 8, explained above in siman 46:10. The Beit Yosef similarly understands the Ran. This is incorrect. The reason authentication from another source is effective in siman 46 is that once the signatures are independently established, we no longer require the witnesses’ testimony and do not heed their statement that they do not remember. Here, however, the particular document is suspected of forgery. Independent familiarity with the signatures does not remove that suspicion unless the witnesses identify this very document or remember signing it. The second explanation likewise does not resolve the difficulty. The correct approach is that the witnesses must be able to connect the instrument before us to the loan they witnessed, either by remembering that they signed it, by recognizing a distinctive feature, or through other reliable authentication. Mere testimony that a loan occurred, joined with other persons’ recognition of handwriting, is insufficient where the creditor has been seeking forged documents. Otherwise an oral loan that was repaid could be transformed into a written debt through forgery, depriving the debtor of defenses and harming purchasers. Accordingly, the Rambam and Ran should not be understood as allowing ordinary handwriting authentication in this exceptional case. The document may be enforced only when the suspicion attached to this particular instrument is removed. The authorities’ formulations must be read in that manner, and what I wrote in the preceding seif katan applies.
