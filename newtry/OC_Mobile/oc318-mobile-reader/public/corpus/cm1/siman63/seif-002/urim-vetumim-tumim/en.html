Sema explains that in the set-off case of siman 58, and in the case of witnesses who say they were minors in siman 46, we do not leave the document with a third party because in those cases seizure can be effective, whereas here seizure does not help. That follows Sema's own approach. But I already wrote in seif katan 2 that according to Rosh, even here seizure can be effective, and Bach says the same. Moreover, what would Sema say according to Mechaber, who holds that even in the set-off case seizure does not help? Bach forced an explanation, but it does not solve the case of siman 46. The correct explanation is different. The concern here is that the creditor might later sue the borrower's heirs, who know nothing of the matter, as Tur says. One might ask: beit din can simply write a ruling stating that this document may not be collected. If preserving such a ruling is necessary, we generally allow a receipt to be written. But here the holder is suspected of pursuing forgery. A ruling is not sufficient. He may alter the date or the amount so skillfully that the new form will no longer appear to be the same document covered by the earlier ruling, and then he can collect from the heirs. We are already concerned that he forged the witnesses' signatures so precisely that even the witnesses themselves might be mistaken. Certainly we must worry that he can alter an amount or date. That concern does not exist in the earlier cases. There, a court ruling that the document cannot be collected is enough, because the holder is not suspected of fabrication. This is why the document remains with the third party here.
