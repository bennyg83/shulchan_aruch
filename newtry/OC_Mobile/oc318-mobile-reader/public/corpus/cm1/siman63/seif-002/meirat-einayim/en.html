We do not leave it in his possession. The Tur writes: Nevertheless, we do not leave it in his possession, lest the borrower die and he then produce it against the orphans in another beit din, where it is not known that he had attempted forgery. Rather, we place it in the hands of a third party until the matter is clarified by witnesses. In the Perishah I wrote that specifically here, where there is an evident defect because he sought to obtain a forgery, seizure is ineffective, as stated above. Therefore they were concerned that he might collect in another beit din. This differs from the case above in siman 58 concerning the claim that a payment was for another debt, and from siman 46 concerning two witnesses who say that the document's witnesses were disqualified. Although there too we say that we neither collect with the document nor tear it up, we nevertheless leave it in his possession, because seizure is effective in those cases, as explained there. Therefore we are not concerned even if he should collect in another beit din, for there is no substantive difference between effective seizure and collection through another beit din.
All the documents of the person who sought to forge are impaired. The word "who sought" refers to the immediately preceding phrase, "the person," and not to "all his documents." The case is one where he asked them to forge only one document. However, because we do not know the name of the borrower or the amount, all of his documents are doubtful, and the burden of proof is upon the claimant. According to this, if he has two documents against one borrower, it would seem reasonable that he should be able to collect one of them, as the Tur and Mechaber write above in siman 31 concerning two groups of witnesses who contradict one another. Yet if so, the authorities should have explained this here. Moreover, the Tur indicates that even if he has two documents against one person, neither may be collected, as I wrote in the Derishah. I explained there that when he told them merely to forge an unspecified document, there is reason to suspect each individual document. This also appears from the words of the Rosh.
