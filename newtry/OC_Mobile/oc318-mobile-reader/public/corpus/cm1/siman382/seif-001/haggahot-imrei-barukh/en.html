(Shach, seif katan 4) And from the words of the Rosh that I cited. Note: See Tevuot Shor, siman 28, and Pleiti there; Darkei Moshe to Yoreh Deah 264; Abudraham, page 8; Pesachim 7b, "what of Pesach and sacred offerings," and Rashi there, who explains that "and he shall slaughter" refers to the owners. Rashi writes similarly in Yoma 27a. See also the beginning of chapter HaIsh Mekadesh. All the places I have cited show that Rashi holds the mitzvah of slaughter rests upon the owner, and nevertheless agency is effective. Why should circumcision differ merely because the mitzvah rests upon the person? It is not a mitzvah that must be performed with one's own body, like tefillin. The Rosh, however, requires study because he exempts one who circumcises where the father had already appointed someone else to do so. Why should the intruder not have to compensate the father? Had the appointed mohel acted as agent, the father would have fulfilled the mitzvah through the principle that a person's agent is like himself, whereas when another circumcised without his consent he did not. See Tosafot Yom Tov, Nedarim chapter 3, mishnah 11, which calls circumcision a mitzvah incumbent on the person's body. This requires study. If we say that agency applies only to the physical act of circumcision, so that it is as though the father himself performed the act, but not to the blessing recited by the agent, then the Rosh's ruling can be resolved. The penalty is only for loss of the blessing. Since the father did not wish to circumcise personally and delegated it to an agent, the right to the blessing belonged to the agent who would have recited it.
