One who cuts down another person's tree, and one who prevents another from performing a mitzvah. It contains one seif.
If an ox was designated for killing because it endangered people, or a tree was designated for cutting because it endangered the public, and another person preceded the owner and slaughtered the ox or cut down the tree without the owner's consent, he must compensate the owner in an amount determined by beit din, because he deprived the owner of the opportunity to perform the mitzvah. If he claims, 'You told me to kill it or cut it down,' he is exempt, since the object was already designated for that purpose. Rama: With respect to an ordinary act of damage, however, the damager is not believed to say that the owner authorized him, even where he has a migo that he could have denied causing the damage, because this is a migo against the presumption that a person does not permit another to damage his property. He is believed, however, to say, 'You instructed me,' where he has a migo that he could have claimed that the damaged object was his own rather than the claimant's. Likewise, if one slaughtered an undomesticated animal or bird and another person covered the blood without the slaughterer's consent, the latter must pay whatever beit din assesses. Some rule that the fixed penalty is ten gold coins. The same ruling applies whenever one prevents the owner from personally performing a positive mitzvah that was properly his to perform and another performs it first: the latter pays the owner ten gold coins. If a father had a son to circumcise and another circumcised him first, that person must pay ten gold coins; but if the father had already entrusted the circumcision to another person and a third party performed it first, the third party is exempt. Nowadays beit din does not collect this penalty, but if the claimant seized property, it is not taken from him.
