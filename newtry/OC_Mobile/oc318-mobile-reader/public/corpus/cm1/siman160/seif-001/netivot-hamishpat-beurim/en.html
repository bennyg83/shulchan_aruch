And the courtyard owner does not assist. He assists only according to his proportional obligation. See Sema, seif katan 3. The authorities dispute whether, according to Rambam, the courtyard owner participates in the cost of the first ten handbreadths, but all agree that the roof owner must build on his own side. Since the roof owner must continue the wall from ten handbreadths up to four cubits because he himself causes the privacy damage, the courtyard owner can object to building that upper section on his land, saying, "I do not wish my airspace reduced." Even if the first ten handbreadths could have stood on both properties, the roof owner must ultimately build the upper section on his own land. It is therefore better for both that he build the whole thickness on his side, while the courtyard owner contributes his share of the expense for the lower part and receives a written acknowledgment of ownership in half the stones, as in siman 159. The courtyard owner may also use the wall. This is not a gratuitous gift, since he paid half the cost of the lower portion and owns half of those stones. The fact that the wall stands on the roof owner's land for their mutual benefit does not deprive him of his ownership in the wall.
