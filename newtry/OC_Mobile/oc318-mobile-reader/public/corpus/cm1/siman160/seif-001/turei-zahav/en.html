This is difficult, for the Rambam himself writes that between one roof and another a partition ten handbreadths high is required, as the Mechaber quotes him in siman 159, seif 2. The Gemara clearly implies that adjacent roofs and a courtyard elevated above a roof are equivalent, and in both cases ten handbreadths are required. Beit Yosef sought to distinguish them, but his distinction is exceedingly forced against the implication of the sugya. It appears to me that here the Rambam discusses a courtyard already ten handbreadths higher than the roof. He did not need to state this explicitly, because he relied upon his earlier ruling that between two roofs ten handbreadths are required. The Gemara likewise distinguishes on this basis. The Rif also quotes the baraita simply, “if the courtyard is above his roof, he need not attend to ten handbreadths,” relying upon what he already wrote that between two roofs ten handbreadths are required. The same necessarily applies where one's courtyard is elevated above the other's roof. It therefore appears plain that there is no dispute on this point. Although the Mechaber afterward writes “and some say that this applies only,” and so forth, I have written what appears correct to me.
