And if the courtyard is much higher, etc. The Tur likewise writes this in Rabbi Yonah's name concerning two courtyards. I wrote in the Perishah that Rabbi Yonah may agree in the earlier case of a roof and courtyard that the roof owner must build four cubits even if the courtyard stands much higher and the roof is narrow. Since the courtyard owner is not considered a source of visual harm toward the roof owner, because the roof's use is not fixed, the courtyard owner bears no obligation to build four cubits. The entire burden therefore falls on the roof owner, who is the source of harm toward the frequently used courtyard. Two courtyards are different, because each owner causes visual harm to the other. The upper owner is in any event obligated to remove his own harm by constructing a four cubit barrier. Rabbi Yonah therefore holds that the lower owner need contribute only to the extent that ordinary people can actually see that his use creates harm for the upper one. It appears that the "some say" cited by the Mechaber in seif 1, the view of Rabbi Yosef ibn Migash, who holds that if a roof is four cubits below the courtyard the roof owner need build nothing even though the courtyard owner likewise builds nothing, would certainly hold the same in two adjoining courtyards where one is four cubits higher. The upper owner would build the four cubits required above his level, while the lower owner would contribute nothing. The Mechaber does not state that view here because the present discussion concerns a courtyard less than four cubits above the other. In that case, because the lower owner must at least build from below upward and the upper owner contributes below, the Mechaber agrees that the lower owner must also assist in the upper portion. Proof is that even in seif 1 the Mechaber records the dispute only when the courtyard is four cubits higher, but where it is less than four cubits higher he states without qualification that the lower owner builds from below until the partition rises four cubits above the courtyard. Rama, who speaks here of a much greater height, omits the other view because he rules like the Rosh, cited by the Tur both regarding roof and courtyard and regarding two courtyards, that visual damage remains a concern even with a height difference of four cubits.
