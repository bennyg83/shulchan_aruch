If it is a matter... they divide. Bava Basra 34b and 35. Where the authorities themselves dispute the law, there is likewise no way to determine conclusively which side is correct. Therefore the property is divided, or shuda d'dayanei would apply. In our time shuda d'dayanei is not used, as Rabbeinu Chananel derives from Kesubos, where Rav Nachman says that he is a qualified dayan and the other is not, implying that this requires an expert judge of Rav Nachman's stature. Consequently division remains the practical rule.
If not... Gittin 60b says that where no ruling was stated, whoever is stronger prevails. Terumas HaDeshen asks why division is not used and answers that division applies where the object is naturally divisible, whereas the watercourse there is not. He himself remains uncertain about this distinction. Tosafos at the beginning of HaMocher Es HaBayis also discusses the foundations of these rules. Since in our time shuda d'dayanei is unavailable, the Kesubos passage implies that where that remedy cannot be used, division is preferred.
