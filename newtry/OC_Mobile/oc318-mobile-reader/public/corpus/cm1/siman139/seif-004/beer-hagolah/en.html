The language of the Tur from the incident involving the relatives of Rav Idi bar Avin, Bava Batra 33a, according to Tosafot's version in the name of Rabbeinu Chananel, s.v. "This is the correct text, at the end," etc.
Rambam likewise writes in chapter 15, law 3, according to his textual version of the Gemara that this person was the closer relative. Maggid Mishneh writes that there is another version, but according to all versions Rambam's ruling is correct.
In accordance with Abaye and Rava there, who hold that since he admitted, his admission is binding. See also seif 8. Baal HaTerumot likewise writes there according to Rambam. If Reuven held land properly for three years or more and died, and before his death admitted that the land belonged to Shimon, and Reuven's creditor then came to collect from the land, Maharshal rules in Responsa siman 1 that Shimon prevails.
See there, where he writes that this follows the Talmudic ruling in Gittin 60b that where no halachah has been stated, "whoever is stronger prevails." He explains that there no practical division was possible, etc. Even if each could use the upper channel for half the day, there would still be a dispute over who begins, and perhaps in such a case both would suffer loss. This also resolves the responsum of Maharam in the first chapter of Bava Batra concerning partners who wished to force Reuven to take his share on the western side, where he ruled "whoever is stronger prevails." See Rabbeinu Yerucham, Netiv 3, part 5, who lists six categories of property in doubt and writes that "whoever is stronger prevails" applies only where the property is not currently in anyone's possession and the matter may eventually become ascertainable, such as where each says, "It belonged to my ancestors," as in the preceding seif. Maharik, root 194, likewise writes that seizure does not help in a dispute of law between authorities except concerning a bridegroom's dowry. See there, and Even HaEzer at the end of siman 53. I saw Sema write similarly in a comparable case.
