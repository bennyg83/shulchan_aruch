Mahari in Terumat HaDeshen siman 312 cites the Gemara in HaNizakin and the Mordechai at the beginning of Bava Metzia and at the beginning of HaMokher Et HaBayit, which apply the rule "whoever is stronger prevails" in disputes among major authorities where no ruling was established according to either side. The Rambam's language, however, implies that this rule is used only where it may eventually be possible to determine to whom the property belongs, for the reason explained above. A dispute among authorities is different, because who can testify or definitively determine which authority the halachah follows? To reconcile these sources, Mahari writes that division is certainly preferable wherever the truth cannot ultimately be determined, but where division is not a workable solution, the rule of seizure applies. Thus in the case in Gittin, even dividing usage into half days would still leave a dispute over who uses it first, and perhaps such an arrangement would produce certain loss to both parties. This also resolves Maharam's responsum in the first chapter of Bava Batra, where division was impossible. With this explanation Rama's words are clear and the Ir Shushan's objection falls away. He mistakenly assumed that Rama wrote this distinction on his own without consulting Mahari and the Mordechai.
