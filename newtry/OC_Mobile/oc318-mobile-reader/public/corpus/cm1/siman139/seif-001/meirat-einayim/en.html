And each one says, 'It is mine.' Rambam explains that the same law applies if each brings witnesses that it belongs to him, or belonged to his ancestors, or that he consumed it for the years of chazakah. The Tur likewise writes this in siman 146, seif 34, based on the Gemara in chapter Chazakat HaBatim.
Whoever is stronger prevails. Rosh explains in the first chapter of Bava Metzia that the Sages did not institute division here, lest we cause a loss to the one to whom the entire property truly belongs. Instead they relied on the assumption that the party whose claim is true will exert himself more strongly to retain what is his, while the other will not exert himself to the same degree, thinking, 'What do I gain by risking myself? Today or tomorrow the other will bring proof and take it from me.' Since each says the whole item is his, neither presently holds it, and there is no independent monetary basis supporting both sides, the Sages left the matter to these presumptions and did not prevent the party who prevailed from holding it. This differs from two people holding a garment or similar property. There, since each physically possesses part and whatever a person holds is presumed his, applying 'whoever is stronger prevails' would allow one to take what is already in the other's possession. Therefore the Sages instituted division, as explained in the preceding siman.
He must swear that it is his. This teaches that although he took possession with the permission of the Sages under the rule that whoever is stronger prevails, he does not retain it without an oath. See the Perishah.
Even if the other later becomes stronger. Rosh in chapter Chazakat HaBatim explains the reason: the Sages did not wish to establish a system that would leave them in perpetual quarrel, one prevailing today and the other tomorrow. They therefore relied upon the existing possession once established, as explained above.
