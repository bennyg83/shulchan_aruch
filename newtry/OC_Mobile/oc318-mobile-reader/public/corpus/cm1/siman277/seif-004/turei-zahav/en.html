(Seif 11, one concerning whom we are uncertain.) Beit Yosef cites a responsum of the Geonim. One person claimed against his brother, “I am the firstborn for inheritance,” and the other denied it. The claimant produced a writing stating, “So-and-so was born on the fourth of Nisan in year fourteen,” and produced his mother’s ketubah dated the second of Kislev in year thirteen. The responsum says that we do not rely upon the memorandum of the birth date together with the ketubah date, because the memorandum is not a testamentary declaration. The writer may have erred or may have wished to write something untrue. It appears to me that this concerns a writing made by an outsider who was not the father, and whose notation may not have been precise. But where a father records the birth dates of his children as a memorandum, we may rely on it concerning inheritance. We find in siman 91 that a person may swear based on what is recorded in his ledger. The second concern, that he wished to write falsely, also does not apply, because we see that he intended to record the birth date so that he would later know when the child becomes bar mitzvah and reaches legal majority for all matters. Moreover, immediately afterward the Mechaber writes that a father is believed to say, “This one is the firstborn,” even where he is not otherwise known as such. It therefore appears that a writing made by the father is effective concerning firstborn inheritance.
