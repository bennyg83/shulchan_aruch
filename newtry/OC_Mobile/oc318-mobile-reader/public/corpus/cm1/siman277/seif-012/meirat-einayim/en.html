Concerning one who had not been established as his son. Scripture says regarding the father, "he shall recognize the firstborn son of the hated wife." From "he shall recognize" we learn that the Torah gives the father credibility to identify him to others. The Tur and Mechaber carefully say "one who had not been established as his son," not one who had been established as not his son. In the latter case the father is not believed. How did that contrary presumption arise? If from the father's own earlier statements that he was not his son, the father cannot later reverse himself. If from witnesses who testify that the person was born from another woman or that the wife conceived from another man, he certainly cannot contradict witnesses. When the Tur and Mechaber later say that a father is believed to say that someone established as firstborn is not firstborn, there is no contradiction. There the witnesses do not testify directly that he is the firstborn son of this father. They merely testify that this child was born first to the wife, creating a presumption of firstborn status. The father then says the later child is the firstborn, which automatically renders the first child illegitimate. The Torah believed him regarding his son. This applies where the presumption came from witnesses, not where the father himself had previously established the older one as firstborn. Rama says this explicitly in the Ramban's name. The Tur adds that the father is believed so that this son takes a double portion even in property that falls to the father while he is dying. The Mechaber omits that detail, and Rama writes it at the beginning of siman 279 regarding an ordinary share. The same applies to the firstborn portion.
