7. P. Until the eighth day, the father is removed from the mother's permission to enter the covenant. Name:
Believed. The verse states, 'he shall recognize,' meaning that he may identify him to others. From here the Torah grants the father credibility concerning him. The Tur and Shulchan Aruch carefully write that this applies to one who had not previously been established in a contrary status, rather than saying that it applies even to one established as not being his son. They hold that in such a case he is not believed. For how did that prior status arise? If through the father, because he regularly said that the person was not his son, he can no longer later say that he is his son. If through witnesses who testify that he was born to another woman or that his wife bore him from another man, the father certainly is not believed to contradict witnesses. When the Tur and Shulchan Aruch later write, 'likewise, if he said regarding one who was presumed,' that case does not concern witnesses testifying that he is the firstborn. Rather, they testify that he was the first child born to the man's wife, thereby giving him the status of firstborn. The father then says of the second son, born afterward, that he is the firstborn. The first son is consequently disqualified as a mamzer, and the Torah gives the father credibility for this. This applies where the firstborn status was established by witnesses, but not where it was established by the father's own prior statement, as Rama writes in the gloss. Sema. See Responsa Maharashdam, siman 304, and Responsa Mahari ben Lev, volume 1, principle 11, siman 61.
