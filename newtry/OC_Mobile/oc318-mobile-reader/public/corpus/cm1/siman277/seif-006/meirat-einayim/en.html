Even though the head of the nonviable fetus emerged while alive, etc. Rashi explains the Gemara where a woman carries twins. One fetus put its head out alive and then withdrew it, and the second fetus came out first. The second child is nevertheless the firstborn for inheritance. Although even emergence of most of the forehead ordinarily counts as birth, that applies only to a viable child. A nonviable fetus is not considered born for firstborn inheritance, because Scripture calls the firstborn "the first of his strength," one over whose death the father would mourn. On this reasoning, even if the entire nonviable fetus was born, it appears that the next viable son, whether born in the same delivery or later, remains the firstborn for inheritance. Rashi mentioned a fetus that merely put out its head and withdrew it because the Mishnah also teaches that the next child is not firstborn for priestly redemption, and Rashi wished to teach that novelty even where the first fetus was not wholly born. In the Derishah I argued this at length against the responsum of Mahari Katz of Krakow, who ruled that if the nonviable fetus was born entirely, the next child is not firstborn for inheritance either.
