Only with respect to what he consumed. This does not mean that he acquires chazakah in the particular trees whose fruit he consumed and in the soil beneath them. Since the trees do not combine in such a case, and a tree whose fruit he ate one year was not eaten from the next year, how could there be a chazakah in any particular tree? Rather, the Sema explains that he has no chazakah in the land at all, only in the produce that he already took and consumed in prior years, and he may not take the produce of future years. The Shach writes that aside from the strained wording, the ruling itself is difficult, because below in siman 145 it is explained that wherever there is no chazakah in the land, the possessor must also return the produce. The Shach therefore explains that the phrase means he acquires only that portion which he consumed in the legally required manner, and teaches that even if he consumed from ten trees in one location for three consecutive years and afterward consumed from the other trees, he has no chazakah in the remaining trees beyond those he actually used. See Tumim. I agreed with the Shach's objection to the Sema, but his own explanation is also strained. In my opinion the proper explanation is this: in one seah of land, the remaining trees produced no fruit, and only the three trees from which he ate each year bore fruit. In the other two seah, other trees also produced fruit. Therefore he has no chazakah in those two seah, because he failed to consume from all the available producing trees; but in the third seah he does have chazakah, because each year he consumed from three trees in that seah while the others bore no fruit. This is correct.
The same applies if they were, etc. This is Rashbam's interpretation. The Tur questions this opinion in the name of Ri, because we rule that if he consumed the trees consecutively, it is a chazakah. The Perishah and Bach question the Tur. See Tumim, where I wrote that they followed Tosafot in Bava Batra, but Tosafot themselves require study and appear self contradictory. The Tur's position is correct. See there at length.
