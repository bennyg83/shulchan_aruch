He established possession only in what he consumed, etc. The Sema explains that this does not mean that he established chazakah in the trees from which he ate and in the land beneath them, because in such a case the trees do not combine, and the tree from which he ate this year was not eaten from in another year, so what chazakah could he have? Rather, the Sema says it means that he has no chazakah at all except regarding the produce that he plucked and ate in the previous year, and he may not take the produce in future years. This explanation is not satisfactory, and the ruling according to it is puzzling. Why should he not repay the produce he ate if he has no chazakah in the trees from which he ate? He then consumed them unlawfully, and as stated below in siman 145, whenever one has no chazakah he must return the produce as well. Rather, the meaning is that he established chazakah only in what he ate, provided he ate it in the manner that establishes chazakah. The ruling teaches that even if he ate from ten trees in one place for three consecutive years and afterward also ate from the others, he acquires chazakah only in what he actually ate. Examine carefully.
Although he did not gather all of their produce, etc. According to Rashbam, Raavad, Tur, and the other authorities, in every case he has no chazakah unless the trees had not produced. See there and examine carefully.
And if he ate them scattered, he did not establish chazakah in the land, etc. I do not know the source for this. Even if he ate from all of them for three consecutive years, he has no chazakah in the remaining land, as Rashbam and the Tur explicitly imply. It appears that this is also the Rama's meaning: he ate from all of them while they were scattered, meaning distant from one another, because they were planted at a density of more than a beit se'ah for ten trees. Examine carefully.
