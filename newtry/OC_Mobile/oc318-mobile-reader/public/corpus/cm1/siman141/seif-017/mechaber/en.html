If an orchard contained thirty trees within an area sufficient to sow three se'ah, and the possessor consumed the produce of ten trees in the first year, ten in the second, and ten in the third, he establishes presumptive ownership over the entire orchard. This applies provided the ten trees consumed each year were dispersed throughout the area of three se'ah and the remaining trees did not produce fruit that year. If the remaining trees produced fruit and he did not consume it, he established ownership only over the trees whose fruit he consumed. When does this apply? When he consumed only part of the fruit and other people plundered the rest. But if he left their fruit upon them, then because he consumed the fruit of trees distributed from one side of the field to the other, he establishes ownership over the entire field even though he did not gather all their fruit. {Rama: This applies only when ten trees are planted per area sufficient to sow one se'ah. If more than one se'ah is allotted to ten trees, then even though the trees he used were dispersed, he establishes ownership only over the ground needed by those trees. Some say the same applies when they are planted more densely than ten per se'ah. Others say that in such a case he establishes ownership unless the trees are within four cubits of one another.}
