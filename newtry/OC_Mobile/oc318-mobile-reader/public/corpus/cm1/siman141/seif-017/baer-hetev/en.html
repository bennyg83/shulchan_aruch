Thirty. It appears that the same applies where there are ten trees in a beit se'ah and each year he consumes the produce of three scattered trees. Even though in any given year he has not consumed a complete third, because the tenth tree is omitted each year, this nevertheless constitutes chazakah. Sema.
That he consumed. This cannot mean that he establishes chazakah in the particular trees whose produce he consumed and in their ground. Since the yearly consumptions do not combine in this case, and he did not consume from the same tree in the following year, what basis would there be for chazakah? The Sema therefore explains that he has no chazakah at all, but he keeps the produce he already plucked and consumed in the prior year, although he may not consume the produce in future years. The Shach rejects this interpretation. The law would be surprising, because if he has no chazakah in the trees he consumed, he ate their produce without right and should have to repay it, as stated in siman 145, where one who lacks chazakah must also return the produce. Rather, the phrase means that he does not establish chazakah in the remaining trees merely because he consumed produce in the manner required for chazakah. It teaches that even if he consumed produce for three consecutive years from ten trees in one location and only afterward consumed from the others, his chazakah extends only to what he actually consumed, not to the remaining trees. Shach.
Scattered. It appears that this means he consumed from all of them while the trees themselves were scattered, meaning that they are far apart because more than a beit se'ah is planted for every ten trees. Shach.
More. Meaning that he did not consume from all the trees each year, but only from ten. Sema.
