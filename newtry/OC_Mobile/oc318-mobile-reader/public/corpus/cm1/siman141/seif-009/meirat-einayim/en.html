And some disagree, etc. From the arrangement of the Mechaber's and Rama's language, it appears that the dissent is directed specifically to this rule, where plowing, sowing, and consuming the produce all occur within one year. With respect to the preceding seif, everyone would agree that since he did not derive benefit during the year in which he built, that year does not count toward the years of presumptive possession. In the Perishah, however, I wrote that in my view the dissent applies also to that preceding clause. Even if he built until near the end of the three years, they may hold that this establishes presumptive possession, because he is building in order to settle and reside there immediately when construction is completed. This resembles the case of slowly maturing figs in seif 16 below. Rama therefore did not need to give the reason that he ultimately consumes the produce, in a manner implying that he actually consumed it during that same year, except in the case of plowing. Plowing in the current year is not done to derive benefit from that same year's produce but for a later year, unlike construction. See there and examine carefully.
