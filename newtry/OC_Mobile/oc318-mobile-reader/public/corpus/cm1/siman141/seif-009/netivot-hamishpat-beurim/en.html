The period during which he plowed is not counted. At first glance this is difficult. The Rashba wrote this rule only according to the view that three complete years are required. On that approach it makes sense to say that a period of plowing is not counted toward the three years. But the Mechaber ruled in seif 1 that eating three crops is sufficient and three complete years are unnecessary. If one plowed, planted, and harvested three crops, what more could be required? It appears that the matter can be explained from the Rashba in Bava Batra 36, s.v. dekel na'arah. He writes that Rav and Shmuel disagree as follows: Shmuel requires only three acts of consuming the produce of that particular field, whereas Rav requires three years corresponding to the ordinary harvest cycles of the world and does not measure solely by this particular field. Accordingly, the rule that plowing time is not counted can apply where the ordinary practice is to plow after Rosh Hashanah but this possessor plowed before Rosh Hashanah. If plowing time counted, we would calculate three years from the beginning of his early plowing, since possession for three full years is effective in all circumstances. But if plowing time does not count and there are not yet three full years of qualifying possession, he must remain until the ordinary period for completing three annual crops has concluded according to common agricultural practice. Thus, if most people plow after Rosh Hashanah and complete the third harvest by the beginning of Elul, while his early plowing would make three calendar years end at the beginning of Av, he must nevertheless wait until the beginning of Elul, because the plowing days do not count toward the required period.
