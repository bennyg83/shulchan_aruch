If the tree sheds its fruit, etc. That this does not constitute a chazakah requires study. The Mechaber cites this rule, mentioned by the Tur, without qualification. Yet aside from the fact that according to Rabbenu Chananel it applies specifically to a tree that sheds its fruit before its proper time, as the Sema and Bach write, the Ramban in his novellae explains the Gemara in the opposite manner: according to Rav, a tree that sheds its fruit does not create a chazakah, whereas according to Shmuel, whose ruling we follow, even such a tree does create a chazakah. There is therefore a doubt in the law. In my view, however, according to the Mechaber's own approach this rule has no place. Regarding the distinction involving a young date palm, there are two explanations: one, a date palm that sheds its fruit; the other, a palm that produces twice a year. These two explanations have a practical difference. According to the first, it necessarily follows that even Shmuel requires three full years from day to day, for otherwise the Gemara should simply have said that the difference between Rav and Shmuel is early and late ripening crops, as the Rosh expressly writes. Why choose a distinction involving something uncommon? According to the second explanation, however, Shmuel may not require three years, but only three productions of fruit. Thus, the view that three harvests suffice for an orchard necessarily understands a young date palm as one producing twice a year. The Bach likewise writes explicitly that the Rambam follows the latter version according to Shmuel, meaning the latter explanation of Rabbenu Chananel that a young date palm produces fruit twice yearly. On that explanation there is no source whatever for saying that a tree whose fruit falls by itself without harvesting cannot create a chazakah, for we find no hint of such a rule in the Gemara, and who would invent it? It therefore requires study why the Mechaber, who in seif 1 rules simply like the Rambam that three harvests suffice and thus necessarily explains a young date palm as producing twice yearly, nevertheless rules here like the Tur that a tree shedding its fruit does not create a chazakah. The Tur follows the Rosh, who holds that even according to Shmuel three years are always required and therefore adopts the explanation that a young date palm means one that sheds its fruit, but that explanation is not the halachic interpretation according to the Rambam. It is difficult to say that the Mechaber, in his concise formulation, withdrew from what he wrote in Beit Yosef and understood that even the Rambam explains a young date palm as one that sheds its fruit. One could then explain the Gemara's question of the difference between Rav and Shmuel as follows. Certainly, according to Shmuel, who says that three harvests suffice, there is no need to discuss three years, for he expressly says, "until he harvests three harvests," and never mentions three years. The question is only on Rav, who requires three years from day to day: what relevance do the three years have, since a chazakah depends on consumption of produce, and mere residence in an idle field even for a thousand years is not a chazakah? The essential matter is consumption of produce, since a person does not see another eat the produce of his field three times and remain silent, as the Ramban writes. Why, then, did Rav mention three years rather than three consumptions of produce, and what is the difference between the two views? The answer is that everything indeed depends on consuming produce. Rav mentions three years only because ordinarily three crops are eaten in three years, as the Rosh writes. Thus even if the possessor performs no act, but eats fruit that fell by itself, once he has eaten three crops it is a chazakah. Shmuel, however, requires an act of harvesting, and if the fruit falls by itself there is no chazakah; therefore he says "until he harvests." We rule like Shmuel, but three consumptions constitute a chazakah according to all, even Rav. On this reading the Mechaber's ruling is understandable. Still, it requires study that the Mechaber would innovate an explanation not found in any earlier author. Perhaps he was troubled by the Tur's citation in the name of Rabbenu Chananel that three consumptions suffice, even though Rabbenu Tam in one version explains a young date palm as one that sheds its fruit. One must therefore say that even on that explanation three consumptions suffice, which accords with what I have written. Nevertheless, the matter requires study.
