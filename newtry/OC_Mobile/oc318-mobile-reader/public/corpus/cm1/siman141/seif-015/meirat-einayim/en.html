Even though they remained on the tree, etc. The Rosh, in the name of Rav Hai, derives this law from the 'young palm' mentioned in the Gemara. He explains it as a palm that sheds its fruit by itself, not like Rabbeinu Chananel, who explains that it sheds its fruit before the fruit is fully ripe. The Beit Yosef treats Rabbeinu Chananel's explanation as equivalent to Rav Hai's, but that is incorrect. According to Rav Hai, even if the tree sheds the fruit only after it has fully matured, so long as he did not pick it with his own hands there is no presumptive possession. This accords with the Shulchan Aruch's formulation here. The Ir Shushan's language also requires precision. He writes, 'even though now they remained on the tree.' Why say 'now'? Even if this is their ordinary pattern every year, there is no presumptive possession if they ultimately fall by themselves. See the Derishah at the beginning of this siman.
