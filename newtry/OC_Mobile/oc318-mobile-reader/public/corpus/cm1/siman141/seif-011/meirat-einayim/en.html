He consumed produce of orlah and Shemittah. This means, for example, that he transgressed by plowing and sowing during Shemittah, so that the resulting growth is prohibited. This is why the baraita and Rambam mention Shemittah in the middle of the list, to teach that the discussion is of prohibited produce, rather than to teach merely that Shemittah produce is ownerless, as the Rashbam explains. See the Perishah.
Unless he consumed the vine branches. Rama abbreviated and did not state the distinction among the three cases regarding consumption of the branches, which might imply that the branches are permitted in all three. That is incorrect. With orlah, Scripture says, 'you shall treat its fruit as orlah,' from which we learn that its fruit is prohibited but not its wood. A similar rule can be said regarding Shemittah. Kilayim is different. If prohibited growth increases the permitted plant by one part in two hundred, the whole becomes prohibited. Nevertheless, one can find a case where the branches remain permitted while the fruit is prohibited, such as where the vineyard had already been planted and afterward another species was sown alongside it. The fruit that develops after the prohibited sowing grows in prohibition, while the branches that had already grown permissibly remain permitted until the prohibited growth adds one part in two hundred to them. Tosafot write this. See the Perishah.
