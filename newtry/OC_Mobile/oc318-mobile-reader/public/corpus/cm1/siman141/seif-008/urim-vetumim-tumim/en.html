If a person rebuilt his ruin, etc. See the Sema, seif katan 14, who wishes to explain that this law follows everyone and gives a strained interpretation. We should not invent such distinctions. Ramban, at the end of the passage discussing a young palm, explicitly gives the source of the Tur's ruling that rebuilding a ruin does not count toward the three years. He compares it to plowing, which does not establish possession because the owner can say, "Let every plow enter it." The same reasoning applies to construction. Thus there is no special distinction. The law of construction follows the same dispute among the authorities as the law of plowing. The Sema apparently did not locate Ramban's words, because he searched in the earlier passage about whether plowing establishes possession, whereas Ramban's discussion appears later. Had he seen it, he would not have written his interpretation. It is therefore clear that construction is itself part of the general dispute.
And similarly if he improved the field through irrigation. This means that during one year he repaired the irrigation system so that in the second year he could plant and cause the field to produce, and he then did so in the second and third years. According to the view that construction does not count, that first year still does not join the years of chazakah. Although the irrigation work was essential to agricultural use, he did not actually derive produce from the field during that year. This explains why the Rama repeats the point even though the Mechaber already stated above in seif 5 that improvement without eating produce does not establish possession. There the person performed such work during all three years and never consumed produce at all. Here the novelty is that the first year's irrigation directly enabled the land to produce in the second year and he then consumed the produce, yet the first year still does not count because he himself had no produce during that year. There is therefore no need for the Sema's explanation.
