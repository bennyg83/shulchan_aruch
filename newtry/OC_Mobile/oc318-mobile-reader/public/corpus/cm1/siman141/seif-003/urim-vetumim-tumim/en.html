The Tur implies that if the field is so good that it does not require fallowing, and the people in the surrounding valley were also sowing their fields that year, so that the reason that one cannot guard a single field among all the fields of the valley does not apply, then this would not constitute a chazakah. See there. From the unqualified language of the Rambam and the Mechaber, however, it appears that there is no distinction. The reason is that the owner can say that even though the field is excellent, if it rests for a year it will become still better, and there is no limit to how much better it can become. This also appears from the Rif and the Rosh, who omit the Gemara's first answer, that one cannot guard a single field among all the fields of the valley, and cite only the latter answer, that it is preferable to the owner because the field will produce more afterward. According to the Tur, they should have cited the first answer as well, because there is a practical difference where the field is especially good. In such a case the latter reason would not apply, yet the first reason, that one cannot guard a single field, would still apply. It must therefore be that the Rif and Rosh hold that the owner can always make the latter claim, that it is preferable to him for the field to rest and thereby produce more. Consequently, there is no practical difference arising from the first answer, and for that reason they omitted it. The Tur therefore requires study, since he rules contrary to the view of his father, the Rosh.
