(Seif 11: orlah, sheviit.) When our master states his own view, he does not mention kilayim, and neither does the Gemara in the relevant formulation. It appears that this is because the distinction he draws is between the vines, which remain permitted, and the fruit, which is forbidden. That distinction applies only to orlah and sheviit, where during the period the fruit is prohibited the vines themselves remain permitted. With kilayim, however, the vines are no more permitted than the fruit. Their permitted status arises only because they existed before the growth increased by one two-hundredth, while the fruit became prohibited only afterward. Thus there is no greater novelty concerning the vines than the fruit. Since our master explicitly frames the rule as a distinction between fruit and vines, kilayim follows all the more readily, and chazakah applies during the permitted stage to both vines and fruit. The Gemara, which did not explicitly formulate the fruit-vine distinction, also mentions kilayim. Rambam, however, holds that consumption of forbidden produce can constitute chazakah, and therefore he counts kilayim as well.
