Some say that it constitutes a chazakah. This is the opinion of Ri Yeshayah cited by the Tur. He explains that all the more so the owner should have protested, because otherwise his land is being ruined. From this it is clear that Ri Yeshayah is speaking of a locale where everyone leaves fields fallow. If some people leave their fields fallow and others do not, it is obvious that continued cultivation constitutes a chazakah, because there is no defect at all in the possessor's conduct. He can simply say that he belongs to the group that does not leave fields fallow. Since there are differing practices, there is no need for an argument of "all the more so," and the concern that the land will be ruined likewise does not apply, for many people cultivate continuously and their land remains intact. We must therefore say that Ri Yeshayah speaks of a place where everyone leaves fields fallow because the soil there cannot continuously bear crops without a year of rest. He therefore gives the reason that all the more so this should constitute a chazakah, since the owner should have protested and protected his land from becoming ruined through excessive continuous cultivation. This is straightforward. The Bach writes that the Tur did not fully understand Ri Yeshayah's position, and that Ri Yeshayah was speaking of a locale where some leave fields fallow and some do not. His words are untenable for the reasons stated. Ri Yeshayah necessarily speaks of a place where everyone leaves fields fallow, and in that case the Tur disagrees with him. In a place where some leave fields fallow and others do not, everyone agrees that continued cultivation constitutes a chazakah. Now, from the unqualified ruling of the Rambam in chapter 15 of Hilchot To'en, law 2, cited by the Mechaber below in siman 145, where one witness says that the possessor consumed the produce in years one, three, and five and another says in years two, four, and six, and their testimony does not combine because the years to which one testifies are not those to which the other testifies, he does not limit the case to one in which they contradict each other, where one says that he did not consume in years two, four, and six and the other says that he did not consume in years one, three, and five. It follows that even absent express contradiction their testimony does not combine. This proves that the Rambam does not agree with Ri Yeshayah. For if he agreed with Ri Yeshayah, why should the testimony not combine? The case there concerns a place where fields are normally left fallow, as the Maggid Mishneh and the Sema explain below in siman 145. Each witness would therefore be testifying to a period sufficient for chazakah. It would be like one witness saying that in other fields the possessor consumed in years one, two, and three and another saying that he consumed in years four, five, and six, whose testimony does combine. What difference is there? It must therefore be that the Rambam agrees with the Tur. If we say that the witnesses do not contradict one another and both are telling the truth, then in a place where fields are left fallow the possessor cultivated for six consecutive years, and this is no chazakah because he did not possess the field in the ordinary manner of people. Thus there are not two compatible testimonies, as the Tur writes below in siman 145. This expressly proves against Ri Yeshayah. Accordingly, the Shulchan Aruch, which rules below in siman 145 in accordance with the Rambam, should not have cited Ri Yeshayah's opinion here, certainly not as the first opinion. The principal rule follows the Tur: in a locale where everyone leaves fields fallow, continuous cultivation does not constitute a chazakah.
