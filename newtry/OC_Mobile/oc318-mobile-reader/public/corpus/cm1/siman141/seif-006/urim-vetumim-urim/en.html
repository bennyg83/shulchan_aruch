But if he spent, etc. The Rashba concludes in his responsum that this is because the low return does not result from depletion of the land, for otherwise all chazakot in our times would be nullified, since because of our sins taxes have increased to the point that the produce does not cover the expenses. See Sema. In Tumim I wrote that the case appears to concern an occasional condition, such as wartime. If it persists regularly, the land becomes effectively unusable for profit, and chazakah should not apply, because the owner is content for someone else to bear expenses exceeding the gain. This also explains the Rashba's proof from orlah. See there. Nevertheless, I concluded that the Rama's ruling requires study. The Rashba's entire proof is from orlah, but according to the Rambam and other Geonim no proof can be drawn from that case. The Mechaber below in seif 11 follows that alternative interpretation, and for that reason omitted the Rashba's ruling here. This is especially so according to the Tur. It therefore requires study why the Rama presents the Rashba's rule here as undisputed, while below in seif 11 he leaves the Mechaber's ruling without comment.
