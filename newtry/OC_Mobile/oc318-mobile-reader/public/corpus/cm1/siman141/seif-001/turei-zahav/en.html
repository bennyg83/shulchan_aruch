(Seif 4) Some say that it is not prescriptive possession. This is the Tur's view: the owner did not trouble to protest because the possessor harvested continuously like a robber. It is difficult, because the Gemara says that Rav Huna requires three consecutive years but concedes that in a place where fields are left fallow, the years may alternate. The phrase “concedes” implies that alternating years suffice there, and Rashbam expressly explains so. It would follow all the more that consecutive years should suffice. One is forced to read the Gemara as saying that Rav Huna concedes that there specifically alternating years are required. But then why does the Gemara ask that this is obvious? Perhaps that itself is the novelty. The answer must be that if this were the central novelty the word “concedes” would be inappropriate, since its plain sense suggests the opposite, that consecutive possession certainly remains effective. See what I wrote in siman 149, seif 9. The Tur's view nevertheless appears primary.
