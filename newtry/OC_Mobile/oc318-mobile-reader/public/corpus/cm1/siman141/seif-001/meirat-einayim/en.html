And they sow in them, and take from them continually. In three printed editions of the Rambam in my possession the reading is 'and they plant in them,' which is difficult. According to that reading, the conclusion that with an orchard the years are not counted from day to day would be difficult, unless one says that because the property is irrigated land, which is watered continually, even the trees growing in it produce fruit more than once a year. But if so, the final clause should not have separately mentioned an orchard, because it would already be included in the phrase, 'but rain fed land,' which is the opposite of irrigated land. Perhaps 'they plant' there does not mean planting trees, but planting kinds of seed into the ground, for sowing too can be called planting. This is strained, and if so the order should have been reversed, 'they sow and plant in them,' so that the word 'in them' would plainly refer to both. It therefore appears that the Mechaber intentionally corrected the text to read 'and they take,' meaning that they continually take each growth as soon as it matures.
