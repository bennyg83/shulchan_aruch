Tosafot holds that one who establishes possession of trees acquires no ownership in the body of the soil beneath them, even with three trees. He acquires only the right necessary to maintain the trees and, if they die, to plant replacements. The person who established possession in the ground itself retains ownership of the soil and may dig beneath the trees so long as he does not damage them. However, where one person alone establishes possession of a single tree with a valid ownership claim, the rule is stronger. The Gemara says that one who buys a palm acquires from the surface down to the depths when he comes with a valid claim. In such a case he acquires the ground beneath the tree itself and the field owner may not dig there. Thus possession by a single claimant in the tree can be stronger as to the specific soil beneath it than a division where one person possesses the trees and another possesses the ground.
