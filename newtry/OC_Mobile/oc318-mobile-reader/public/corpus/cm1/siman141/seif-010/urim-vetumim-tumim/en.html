Likewise, if it came into his possession while the crop was still green fodder, etc. Even if in the third year he consumed only green fodder. See Sema, seif katan 15, who explains that the possessor consumed three full crops and that the green fodder was in the fourth year. He asks that according to the opinion above in seif 1 that three consumptions suffice, this teaches nothing new. He answers that it teaches the rule according to the opinion requiring three full years from day to day. See there. Yet this still does not answer the difficulty, as the Bach writes. Once he consumed three crops and the property remained in his possession for three years, what more could he possibly have done? Why should this not be a chazakah, and how long would he have to continue possessing the property? The Bach gives a strained answer. It appears that the Tur follows those who hold, as the Ramban writes in his novellae in the passage beginning "a major crop," that although Rabbi Yishmael and Rabbi Akiva measure chazakah by crop cycles, when the original owner sowed the field and the crop reached the stage ready for harvesting, and then another person entered and harvested it, that consumption does not count for the entrant. That year is still regarded as being in the possession of the first owner. We do not presume that he sold the standing crop. A qualifying consumption must include the possessor's own sowing and harvesting. See there. Accordingly, one might have thought that the Sages, although they require three years, also require three qualifying consumptions. Suppose the possessor entered in Tammuz while the prior owner's crop stood ready for harvest, and in Av he consumed it. That consumption would not count for him, because the principal plowing and sowing were performed while the property was in the prior owner's possession. His first countable consumption would therefore be the crop that he himself sowed after that Av and harvested the following Av. His second would be the crop harvested in Av of the third year. From that Av until Tammuz, which completes his third year of possession, there would be no mature crop, only green fodder. He would thus have only two full consumptions during three years. One might therefore have thought that there is no chazakah. The Tur teaches that there is nevertheless a chazakah, for two reasons. First, he did in fact possess the field for three years and consumed everything that was fit for him to consume during those three years, whether much or little. Second, there is a decisive either way argument. If the first consumption is disregarded because the principal plowing and sowing were done under the first owner's possession, then in the final year, where the plowing and sowing were done while the present possessor held the property, even though he had not yet harvested the mature crop, the green fodder must count as his consumption. He is therefore regarded as having possessed through three consumptions. This is what the Tur means when he writes that if it came into his possession while the crop was green fodder, and in the third year he likewise consumed it while still green fodder, it constitutes a chazakah. The conclusion follows from the either way reasoning just explained. Much is therefore taught by this ruling, and the questions of the Sema and Bach do not arise. This, in my view, is the clear intent of the Tur and the Rama.
