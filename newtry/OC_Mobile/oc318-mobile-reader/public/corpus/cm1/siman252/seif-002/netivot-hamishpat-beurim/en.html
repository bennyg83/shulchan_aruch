One who swore or vowed. Ketzot HaChoshen rules that if a person vowed to give a sum of money and, during his final illness, instructed that this same amount be given, the heirs are exempt. He reasons that the original vow does not bind the heirs, and the later instruction is not a shechiv mera gift because the donor commanded payment only because of his earlier vow. He compares this to Tosafot in Bava Batra 175 concerning one who says, "I owe one hundred to so-and-so; give it to him," where according to the opinion that an oral loan is not collected from heirs, the instruction does not become a shechiv mera gift but remains payment of the original debt. In my view the comparison is incorrect. In the debt case, the heirs themselves retain a mitzvah to pay their father's debt even where beit din cannot compel them. The donor can therefore be understood as merely instructing them to fulfill that preexisting mitzvah. With a vow, however, after the donor dies there is no mitzvah upon the heirs to fulfill his vow. If he nevertheless says during his illness, "Give this amount," the command must be understood as a shechiv mera gift. This is also evident from the rulings of the poskim, who collect from heirs in such a case.
