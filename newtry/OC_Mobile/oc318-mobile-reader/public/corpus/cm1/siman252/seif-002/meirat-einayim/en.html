The gift recipient may remove it from the purchaser, etc. This appears to concern land. If the gift consisted of movables and the heirs sold them first, the deathbed gift recipient cannot remove them from the purchaser because of the market protection enactment. Rabbeinu Yerucham says this explicitly. He further writes that according to the Rosh, market protection applies even if the heirs gave the movables as a gift. The Beit Yosef and Rama questioned him because they assumed that if the heirs themselves gave the assets away, the deathbed recipient could remove them. I am surprised that they overlooked what the Tur writes in siman 60, that a creditor does not collect movables that the borrower sold or gave away because of market protection. The Beit Yosef there observes that this implies that market protection applies even to a gift, and although Rashba disagrees, the common ruling follows the Rosh. Thus market protection applies to gifts as well. The heirs themselves are not personally liable merely because they transferred the assets, just as the Tur writes in siman 107 that if heirs sold movables they need not pay because a creditor's lien does not rest upon movables after transfer, even after the Geonic enactment. The Rosh holds similarly regarding land in some contexts. Likewise, in Even HaEzer siman 112 the Tur states explicitly that if heirs sold or gave away land, the widow and daughters do not collect maintenance from the transferees. Movables after the Geonic enactment are certainly no stronger than land before it. This is clear.
