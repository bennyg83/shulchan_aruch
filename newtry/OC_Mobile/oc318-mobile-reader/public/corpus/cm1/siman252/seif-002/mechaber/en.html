It is a mitzvah to fulfill the wishes of the deceased, even when a healthy person gave the instruction and then died, provided he now placed the property with a third party for that purpose. Rama: If it was already in the third party's hands for another purpose, or came into his hands only after the instruction, this principle does not apply. Property acquired by a deathly ill person's gift may be recovered by the recipient from purchasers if the heirs sold it first. Where the property was not given but only subject to the mitzvah of fulfilling the deceased's wishes, a prior sale by the heirs stands. If one swore or vowed to give something and died without giving it, the heirs are exempt and the mitzvah does not compel them. If one deposited money with another and said that after his death it should be used in the best manner, it is given to his heirs.
