To recover it. It appears that the ruling concerns a gift of land. If he gave movable property and the heirs preceded the recipient and sold it, the recipient cannot recover it from the purchasers. This is stated explicitly in Meisharim and cited in Darkei Moshe. The Sema explains that the reason is the market protection enactment. Meisharim further writes that even if the heirs gave the property as a gift to another, the Rosh holds that the market enactment applies to a gift as well. The Beit Yosef and Rama were surprised by Meisharim and labored to explain him, because they understood that if the heirs themselves gave the property to others, the original recipient may recover it from them. The Sema objects that the Tur already writes in siman 60, seif 4, that a creditor does not collect movable property that the debtor sold or gave away because of the market enactment, and the Beit Yosef there expressly notes that according to the Rosh the market enactment applies even to a gift. The heirs who gave the property away are themselves exempt from payment, just as where they sold it, as the Tur writes in siman 107, seif 8, even regarding land. Likewise Even HaEzer, siman 112, states explicitly that if the heirs sold or gave away land, a woman and daughters do not collect their support from it. This follows the Gemara in the chapter Metziat HaIshah and the Nimukei Yosef in the chapter Yesh Nochalin. Movables after the enactment of the Geonim cannot have greater collection rights than land before that enactment. The Shach, however, rejects the Sema's entire analysis and rules that even with movable property the recipient may recover from a purchaser or gift recipient. He explains that Meisharim was not speaking here because of the market enactment at all. Rather, Meisharim says that a ketubah and maintenance are not collected from movables, whether the father gave them away or the heirs gave them away, because the woman has only a lien on those movables, even after the enactment of the Geonim, and there is no law of priority among claims against movables. But here the recipient acquired the actual movables by a legally effective deathbed gift, so they are his property wherever found. All agree that he may therefore recover them from a purchaser or gift recipient, because the heirs sold or gave away property that was not theirs. If, however, the purchaser did not know that these movables had been given by deathbed gift and the market enactment therefore applies, the recipient must reimburse the purchaser for the money he paid, as explained in siman 356.
