For. Rashbam gives the reason. A ketubah and support obligations are rabbinic enactments collectible from property after death, and the Sages established that their liens attach to the assets immediately when the person becomes moribund and is no longer expected to live. By contrast, with a gift made by the person dangerously ill himself, or even by a healthy person who stipulates that another shall acquire after his death, a person values his own continued possession and definitively intends that these transfers not take effect until his soul actually departs. As long as life remains, he hopes that he may recover and the assets will remain his. Thus far the Sema. See Responsa Maharashdam, siman 41.
His heirs. Whatever remains in the heirs' hands is treated as though it remained in the giver father's own hands. Therefore we apply the rule that one does not collect from a recipient when the giver still has unencumbered assets. Sema.
He returned. Meaning, he did not recover from his illness in the interim and did not need to make a new gift. This was simply how the incident occurred. Ibid.
