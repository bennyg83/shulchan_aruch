For at his death, etc. I wrote in the Perishah, citing Rashbam, the rationale for the difference. Ketubah and support obligations are rabbinic enactments designed to be collected from the estate after death, and the Sages fixed their lien to attach while the person is in his final state, when he is already dying and no longer expected to survive. A gift given by the dangerously ill person, or even by a healthy person to take effect after his death, depends upon his own intent. A person is dear to himself. So long as his soul remains within him he hopes that he will recover and keep the property. He therefore does not fully intend the gift to take effect until after his life actually ends.
The property became obligated for the ketubah. Rivash, responsum 247, writes that a widow who made a kinyan with a dangerously ill husband to uphold his will concerning his property nevertheless collects her ketubah first and only afterward carries out the will from the remaining assets.
