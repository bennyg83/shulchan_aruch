For the ketubah of male children. See Sema, seif katan 1, who says that otherwise even the ordinary ketubah should not be collected from a shechiv mera gift because the recipient collected first. This is very difficult. Priority of collection has no relevance here. In Even HaEzer siman 93, seif 20, Rama cites two opinions whether, in contemporary practice, movable property is treated like land regarding collection from a shechiv mera gift made to others. Ketzot HaChoshen cites Bach concerning one who, while ill, handed money to another and said that if he recovered it should be returned to him, but if he died it should be given to a named person. Bach rules that the widow can collect her ketubah from that money. Ketzot objects that since the donor physically delivered the money, the case resembles siman 125: it is a present transfer subject to a condition, and upon death the recipient acquires retroactively, so the widow cannot collect from encumbered movable property. This objection is not correct. The donor expressly conditioned that the money remain with the third party and not be delivered to the recipient until after death. That differs from siman 125, where the donor removes the money from his own control in order that it be delivered immediately to the recipient, as explained above by Sema.
