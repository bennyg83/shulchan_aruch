Bet) The Maggid Mishneh explains Rambam's reference to a woman's ketubah as referring to ketubat benin dikhrin, which does not collect from ordinary encumbered property but does collect from a gift of a deathly ill person. It cannot mean the ordinary ketubah of a living woman, because that collects even from fully encumbered property, as explained in Even HaEzer siman 100. The Mechaber derives this law from the Gemara's reasoning there: if a widow's support, which is rooted in Torah inheritance law, is collected from the estate, then all the more so should it be collected from a rabbinic deathbed gift. This is the Rif's view. Rav Yosef ibn Migash further learns that an oral loan, since it can be collected from heirs, can certainly be collected from a deathbed gift where otherwise the creditor would lose his debt entirely.
