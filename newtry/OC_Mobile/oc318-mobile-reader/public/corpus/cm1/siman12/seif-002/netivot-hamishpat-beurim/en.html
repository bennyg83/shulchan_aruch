See Shach, who explains that the prohibition applies specifically to a compromise in which the litigants authorize the judges to determine a compromise according to their discretion. Once a party knows that strict judgment entitles him to more, any reduction imposed by the judges can look like distortion of the compromise process. But if the judges merely try to persuade the successful party voluntarily to forgive a certain amount, there is no prohibition. Why should it be forbidden to ask one person to waive a right against another? Indeed, the entire discussion of compromise before three must concern formal decisional compromise. Simple persuasion and voluntary forgiveness can certainly be accomplished even by one person.
