But after the judgment has been concluded, etc. See Responsa Rashdam, siman 106.
And he said, "So and so, etc." The Bach writes that one should be stringent in accordance with Tosafot. This does not appear correct, for it is a mitzvah to effect compromise, and the primary ruling follows the majority of the authorities.
"So and so, you are liable, etc." The words of Tosafot in Bava Kamma, at the beginning of chapter Merubah, 69a, and in Bava Metzia, at the beginning of chapter Shenayim Ochazin, 17a, state explicitly that as soon as the judge says, "So and so, you are liable to him," the judgment is concluded, even if he has not yet said, "Go and pay him." This also appears explicitly from Nimukei Yosef in the name of the commentators. Semag, positive commandment 105, page 189, third column, however, states explicitly that the judgment is not concluded until he says, "Go and pay him." See there, and see what I write below in siman 22.
He may not effect a compromise between them, etc. Shiltei Giborim, chapter 1 of Sanhedrin, at the end of page 74, writes: In my view, all these statements apply only where the judges seek to impose a compromise as they see fit, without the litigants' consent. If, however, they explain the nature of the compromise to them and persuade them until they willingly agree that one will waive his claim against the other, or that one will give the other a specified amount, it is proper to do so even after the judgment has been concluded, provided there is no element of compulsion, but only persuasion and entreaty. This is a great mitzvah, for it brings peace between one person and another.
Beit din may effect a compromise, etc. Here too the Bach writes that according to Tosafot they may not effect a compromise, and that one should be stringent in accordance with their view. His words are not correct. Moreover, this stringency itself can produce a leniency. The primary ruling is as the Tur writes.
There are those who disagree. See the Bach concerning this.
