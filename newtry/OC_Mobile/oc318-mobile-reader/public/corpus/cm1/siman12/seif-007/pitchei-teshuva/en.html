For beit din to waive. Responsa Chatam Sofer, Choshen Mishpat, end of siman 112, writes that the same rule applies to a guardian: he may waive part of a claim when doing so benefits the orphans. This is explicit in Responsa Rashba 1015, which makes no distinction, and Responsa Ralbach, siman 95, likewise writes so. Chacham Tzvi, in the additional responsa at the end of his work, siman 3, cites Ralbach as expressing some doubt, but explains that even Ralbach would agree in the case discussed there. Ralbach’s case involved paying out the orphans’ money contrary to Talmudic law, and he therefore questioned whether a guardian may pay from orphan assets merely because a creditor offers to reduce the debt, since when the orphans grow up they may discover a receipt or another defense. In the case before Chacham Tzvi the situation was the reverse: the orphans were seeking to collect money from others, and a debt outstanding on credit is inherently uncertain. According to the questioner, refusal to compromise created a serious risk that the entire debt would be lost. In such circumstances the guardian certainly has authority to compromise. Chacham Tzvi concludes that the proper course is to convene a distinguished beit din, present to them the entire substance and extent of the matter, and have the guardians act regarding the compromise according to that court’s direction. This should specifically be a reputable beit din recognized in its generation as knowledgeable and expert, as the Rosh writes in responsa.
