Who agreed. Even after the arbitrators have stated the proposed compromise, if no kinyan was made the parties may retract. So states Responsa Maimoniyot. See Responsa Rashdam at the end of siman 11. Shach.
They made a kinyan. This implies that even if the parties initially accepted the arbitrators as a beit din to judge them, and even if the claimant agreed to waive part of his claim, a kinyan is still required. Although an ordinary waiver requires no kinyan, there is a distinction: an ordinary waiver is made voluntarily, whereas in a compromise the waiver is made at the arbitrators' direction. To prevent a later claim that the arbitrators misled him, a kinyan is required. For the same reason, even after they have already stated the terms of the compromise, a kinyan is still required, including from the claimant. Darkei Moshe writes that if after hearing the compromise he explicitly says, "I waive it for you," then according to all opinions no kinyan is required. Sema.
With three. Meaning that even if three judges made the compromise, a kinyan is required. In ordinary adjudication three judges do not require a kinyan. Compromise is different because the parties are being asked to concede part of their strict legal entitlement, beyond the letter of the law. Sema.
With two. Nimukei Yosef writes that the Rif also holds that one arbitrator is sufficient. The Shach writes that arbitrators are believed to say that a kinyan was made. A compromise that the litigants make themselves requires no kinyan. So writes Maharam of Lublin in Responsa, siman 47. See Responsa R. Shlomo Kohen, siman 44.
A document. The Mechaber likewise writes this in seif 13. Rama teaches further that even before a party knows the amount that will be fixed by compromise, if he gives a written instrument obligating himself, he may not retract. He should write in the document, even without witnesses: "I hereby obligate myself to you up to such-and-such an amount, according to what the arbitrators determine between us." Through such wording he becomes obligated even in something he never previously owed, because the force of a written instrument is strong, as explained above in siman 40. Sema.
The object. Meaning that where the dispute concerns a specific object, the defendant must transfer an interest in that object by kinyan sudar according to whatever the arbitrators determine. If the dispute concerns money, which cannot itself be acquired through chalipin by kinyan sudar, he must instead obligate himself personally as explained above. Sema.
Words. The Sema writes that this appears to apply specifically to a defendant who merely makes a kinyan to give something in the future, which is only a kinyan on words, as explained at the beginning of siman 245. He therefore must transfer the object itself. For the claimant, however, it is sufficient to make a kinyan that he waives to the defendant whatever remains in his possession. See below, siman 73. If the claim is based on a document held by the claimant, he should make a kinyan sudar obligating himself to return the document as the arbitrators direct. But a kinyan merely to execute a document of waiver is a kinyan on words, as Rama writes in the gloss to siman 203, seif 1.
