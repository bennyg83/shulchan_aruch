So long as they did not make a kinyan. Ran writes that this is specifically where they made a sudar kinyan regarding the compromise and explicitly appointed named persons as arbitrators. But if they made a general kinyan accepting compromise without identifying the arbitrators, three persons cannot impose a compromise unless the parties subsequently accept them. If the parties came before two or three judges for litigation and then made a general kinyan accepting compromise, that is treated as explicit acceptance of those judges as the arbitrators. See Shach, who writes that a compromise made directly by the litigants themselves does not require a kinyan. This means, as Responsa Maharam of Lublin explains, that the claimant's forgiveness itself takes effect without a kinyan.
