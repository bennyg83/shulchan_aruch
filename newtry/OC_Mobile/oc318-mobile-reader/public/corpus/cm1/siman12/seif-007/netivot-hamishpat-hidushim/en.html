They may retract. Even the claimant may retract. We do not say that waiver requires no kinyan, because when the waiver was part of a compromise he may say, "The judges misled me with their words." But where the judges themselves formulate the compromise in a manner constituting an outright waiver, a kinyan is unnecessary for the waiver. See Biurim seif katan 4. Likewise, if after the compromise he explicitly says, "I forgive you," that is a valid waiver. Sema. Taz writes that if the waiver came only because the judges pressed him repeatedly, he may say, "My heart was not fully committed to the waiver. I said it only to escape their pressure." This is correct.

A kinyan on words. This refers to the defendant. For the claimant, if they made a kinyan that he waives, it is sufficient. But if they made a kinyan that he will later execute a document of waiver, that is a kinyan to give and is ineffective, according to Sema. Taz in siman 203 disagrees and holds that even a kinyan to waive is itself a kinyan to give. That is the primary view. Therefore he should explicitly say, "I hereby forgive you up to whatever amount the arbitrators determine." Regarding money, coin itself is not acquired by barter. Therefore when the defendant accepts a sudar kinyan, he should use language of present obligation: "I obligate myself up to the amount determined by the arbitrators." Sema.
