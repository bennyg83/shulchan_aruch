Even though they agreed, etc. The Tur, Tosafot, and Mordechai write that a compromise still requires a kinyan, excluding Raavyah and Raavan, who hold that if the parties remained silent after hearing the compromise, no kinyan is needed.
In a compromise before beit din, etc. This implies that even where the litigants initially accepted the panel to adjudicate the case, a later compromise requires a kinyan. This excludes Ramban and the Mordechai's cited view that distinguishes whether the judges were initially engaged for judgment or compromise. Darkei Moshe notes that the other poskim do not make this distinction.
They can retract. Even the claimant who agreed to waive part of his claim may retract. Although an ordinary waiver requires no kinyan, a waiver produced through a judicial compromise is different. Since he yielded because of the judges' persuasion, without a kinyan he could later say the judges misled him. Even after the judges announced the amount of the compromise, the claimant's waiver still requires a kinyan unless he expressly says afterward, 'I waive it,' in which case all agree that no kinyan is needed.
Even with three. Meaning even where three judges made the compromise. In ordinary adjudication three do not require a kinyan, but compromise seeks a concession beyond strict law and therefore does.
Even by one. Some say specifically two arbitrators are required. Nimukei Yosef writes that Rif too holds that one suffices; see Hagahot Darkei Moshe.
The same applies if he gave a document obligating himself. The Mechaber later writes that if the defendant accepted the arbitrators' decree and executed a document of admission or obligation, he cannot retract. Rama adds that even before the exact amount of the compromise is known, if he writes an obligation such as, 'I hereby owe you up to such-and-such amount, according to what the arbitrators decide,' it binds him because a written obligation is powerful enough to create liability even where none previously existed.
He must transfer the object. If the dispute concerns an object, the defendant must transfer by kinyan sudar the amount or portion the arbitrators will award. If the dispute concerns money, which cannot itself be acquired by chalipin, he must obligate himself personally to pay according to their award.
So that it not be a mere kinyan on words. This applies particularly to the defendant, because a kinyan merely to 'give' is only a kinyan on words. He must transfer the actual object or create an obligation. For the claimant it suffices to make a kinyan to waive what is in his hand. If the claim is based on a document, he can undertake by kinyan to return the document according to the arbitrators' decision. A kinyan merely to execute a future waiver document would again be only a kinyan on words.
