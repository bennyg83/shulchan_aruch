Seif 14. And afterwards he found witnesses. A meat-tax contractor in this country heard that a villager had been concealing taxable transactions. He traveled and investigated but did not then find proof, and thereafter reached an arrangement with the villager for a fixed amount. The villager later claimed that the contractor had said, "What happened in the past is past." It appears that this does not exempt him if the earlier concealment is later established face to face, even by one witness, and all the more where the contractor had sought proof from two. This resembles the Shach at the end of siman 25: one who waives an oath does not necessarily waive actual proof. Even if the phrase "what happened is past" was said, it is not an explicit waiver of money. Although the contractor had earlier heard about the concealment from an outside source, if that witness would not testify before beit din, the earlier settlement cannot reasonably be read as waiving proof that would later be established properly before beit din. If later circumstances create substantial support for the suspicion, a different compromise may perhaps be warranted. The contractor may also say that earlier he could not accept the rumor because the suspected party denied it, whereas now he has heard the matter from a relative or source he trusts. The issue requires careful study.
