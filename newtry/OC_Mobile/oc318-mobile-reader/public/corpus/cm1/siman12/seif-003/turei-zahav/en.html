Further, in seif 7 “Although they agreed,” etc. The Sema cites Darkei Moshe that if, after the compromise, one expressly says, “I forgive you,” it is effective. This is difficult from Mordechai in the name of Raavan, who requires an act of acquisition for forgiveness made through arbitrators, because otherwise the party may say he was merely putting them off under their pressure. Darkei Moshe must refer to a spontaneous declaration, not an answer extracted by the arbitrators’ urging, such as where authority to pay or forgive was placed in their hands and, after they determined the amount, he independently said, “This I forgive you.” Likewise, if the opposing litigant himself requests forgiveness by way of compromise and the claimant says, “I forgive you,” there is no claim of evasion, since he need not be embarrassed to refuse his opponent. This is supported by siman 6 and by Responsa Maharam of Lublin 47.
