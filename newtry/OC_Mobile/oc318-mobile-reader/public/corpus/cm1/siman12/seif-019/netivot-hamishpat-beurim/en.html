Unless they said, 'not as an asmachta.' See Shach, who leaves the matter requiring study. It is indeed very difficult. If the property itself was never transferred, merely giving a pledge does not create the underlying obligation: there is no maneh here and therefore no effective pledge, as explained in siman 207. If, on the other hand, he actually transferred ownership in the object to the other party to the extent the arbitrators would determine, there can be no greater acquisition than that. If a person gives money or an object and says, 'It shall be yours if the arbitrators say so,' the recipient acquires. The same is true through another person. A compromise does not require only a sudar kinyan; any normal method of acquisition works. Why then is it necessary to say 'not as an asmachta' or 'from now'? Ketzot HaChoshen attempts to answer Shach and Urim VeTumim by saying this is only a weak asmachta, but that does not answer the basic question. In Rashba's responsum the wording is, 'unless they made a kinyan and said not as an asmachta.' It appears the case is where the parties placed earnest money to secure compliance with the compromise and stipulated that if one refuses to perform, the earnest money becomes the other's. This resembles siman 207. Rashba means that they made a kinyan on that conditional forfeiture in a legally effective form removing the asmachta. The same formulation appears in siman 207: 'he shall acquire it by sudar not as an asmachta,' meaning in a way that is legally effective. Alternatively, saying 'from now' can cure the problem, as explained there. This resolves the text.
