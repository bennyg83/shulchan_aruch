And they placed collateral in the hands of the arbitrators. Rashba rules that a compromise can be retracted so long as no kinyan was made, even if the parties gave collateral to the arbitrators and said that if they fail to fulfill the compromise the collateral should belong to whichever side upholds it. Such an arrangement is asmachta and does not acquire unless they expressly say that it is not asmachta, or make it effective from now. All the more so where what was deposited was merely a debt instrument, which itself requires writing and delivery to transfer. It appears, however, that even saying "not as asmachta" is insufficient unless they say, "Acquire the body of the collateral itself for whichever of us is entitled." Merely saying, "Acquire it as collateral," does nothing, because where there is no underlying obligation there can be no collateral. Kiddushin teaches that if a man says, "Be betrothed to me for a maneh," but merely leaves collateral for that maneh, she is not betrothed, because there is no maneh and therefore no collateral. Tosafot and Rosh explain that property functions as guaranty only after a personal obligation exists. If one merely says, "I will give you a maneh," and leaves collateral, nothing is created. This is codified in siman 60. The same is true in engagement arrangements: the parties must transfer the body of the collateral. Therefore, here too, the language must be that the arbitrators acquire the body of the object for the party entitled under the compromise. Rashba's own view in Kiddushin is different. He limits the "no maneh, no collateral" rule to kiddushin, because collateral still remains tied to the giver and is therefore not the money of betrothal, while in an ordinary gift he allows collateral to work. Accordingly, Rashba could allow simple collateral in compromise. We, however, rule in engagement cases that collateral alone is ineffective until the body of the object is transferred, and the same must apply here. Maharshal HaLevi sought to prove from the law of "here it is" that collateral is treated like payment and should therefore make a compromise binding. That proof is incorrect. In the law of "here it is," the underlying debt already exists, and collateral can therefore secure it. Here the entire point is to make the compromise itself binding before an obligation exists. Without an underlying obligation the collateral has nothing upon which to rest. If, however, after the compromise is fixed the party says, "Acquire the body of this object," that certainly works, because it is no worse than actual payment. Before the compromise amount is fixed, however, it remains asmachta. The later authorities did not sufficiently distinguish these points.
Not as asmachta. Although we generally rule that merely stating "not as asmachta" does not cure asmachta, that applies to complete asmachta. A compromise cannot be complete asmachta, because if it were, even a kinyan would not make a compromise effective, while we know that a compromise made by kinyan is valid. Since the defect is only a lesser form of reliance, the language "not as asmachta" can demonstrate full intent according to everyone. The Shach leaves this ruling requiring study, and Tumim likewise questions the distinction, since the Gemara never expressly creates such a category. It appears to me that there is proof from the law of encumbering movables together with land. The Gemara requires language excluding asmachta, and Ramban explains that the arrangement is not complete asmachta but resembles a guarantor who becomes liable if the borrower does not pay. A guarantor likewise is not complete asmachta, since kinyan can make him liable. Ramban therefore says that the language "not as asmachta" demonstrates that his mind relies upon the undertaking. This is cited by Beit Yosef in siman 113. It explicitly proves that where the arrangement is not complete asmachta, such language is effective. A compromise that becomes valid through kinyan is necessarily not complete asmachta. Therefore the statement "not as asmachta" is effective there as well.
