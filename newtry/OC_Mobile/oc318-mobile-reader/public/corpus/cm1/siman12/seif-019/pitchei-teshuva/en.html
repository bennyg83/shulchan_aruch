A compromise without a kinyan, etc. Rabbi Akiva Eiger notes in his glosses that Responsa Masat Moshe, siman 62, quotes Responsa Maharashach, part II, siman 113: where creditors agreed to compromise with a debtor and one creditor refuses for various reasons, there are several grounds to compel the dissenter to join the compromise adopted by the other merchants, who constitute the majority. Even if the debtor had some assets, if the creditors saw fit to grant him time in the hope that Hashem might yet prosper him, commercial custom is to compel the minority creditor who refuses a compromise, and that custom has halachic support from the law of situmta. Birkei Yosef also cites Maharashach and notes that Peletat Beit Yehudah, simanim 21 and 22, relied on it. He further cites Maharashach, part III, siman 40, who addresses a creditor’s claim: “Granted that I signed the document without conditions, there was no kinyan, and we hold that compromise requires a kinyan.” Maharashach rejects the claim because a compromise of this commercial type is customarily made without kinyan. Knesset HaGedolah also cites this. Birkei Yosef finds it somewhat difficult that Masat Moshe, siman 62, cited Maharashach, part II, only for the rule that the majority may compel the minority, but did not also cite part III for the rule that this kind of compromise is valid without kinyan. He suggests that in Masat Moshe’s locale merchants did not customarily make such compromises without kinyan, unlike Maharashach’s locale, though they did customarily compel the minority to follow the majority. Therefore Masat Moshe relied on Maharashach only for that latter aspect.
