Not as an asmachta. Shach, seif katan 26, writes that this ruling requires study, and he is correct. Below, siman 207, Mechaber rules that merely stating "not as an asmachta" does not eliminate the law of asmachta. It is difficult to answer that compromise is not a full asmachta because some authorities hold that compromise does not require a kinyan at all. Would that mean the authorities are disagreeing over the general question whether an asmachta is binding? Moreover, even according to the view that compromise does require a kinyan, what does an ordinary kinyan accomplish according to those who hold that an asmachta remains ineffective unless the kinyan is made before an important beit din? We must therefore say that compromise is not a complete asmachta. If so, one could argue that everyone agrees an explicit statement "not as an asmachta" is sufficient in this weaker form of uncertainty. But from where did Mechaber derive such a distinction? Rashba, who wrote this in a responsum, follows his teacher Ramban, whose view is cited by Beit Yosef at the end of siman 207. The Gaon likewise holds that with every asmachta an explicit statement "not as an asmachta" is effective. According to our ruling following Rosh that such a declaration does not cure an asmachta, what proves that it should work here merely because this is not a complete asmachta? Since there is no clear source in the Gemara for distinguishing the two cases, the ruling requires study.
