Where the matter cannot be clarified. This is included in the concept of *shuda deDayanei*: where the judge faces a genuine unresolved uncertainty, he may decide according to his reasoned assessment for the sake of Heaven rather than allow the litigation to remain completely unresolved. He may do this even against the will of the parties. Darkei Moshe and Sema cite Rosh. This applies where the defendant's conduct prevents clarification and the judge perceives an element of deceit. The judge cannot simply rule on the ordinary presentation because of the suspected fraud, yet if he withdrew the defendant would profit from his wrongdoing. In that situation the judge may use a reasoned assessment. If, however, the indications of fraud lie with the plaintiff, the judge should not impose a compromise. He should withdraw from the matter entirely, and every Jewish judge should do likewise, for deceit should not succeed in his court. Rosh states this explicitly in his responsum, kelal 107, responsum 6.
