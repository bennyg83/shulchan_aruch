It is forbidden to seek stratagems, etc. See Tumim, where I discuss Tosafot in Bava Kamma at length. I conclude that if a person genuinely has an old monetary claim against his fellow but lacks proof, he may in strict law use procedural means to protect his own property, for before Heaven the truth is known. Even so, one should ideally be exceedingly careful, even where loss is involved, not to utter any falsehood at all. Truth endures. Shiltei Gibborim writes that if a person became obligated to swear but has no intention of swearing, he should not threaten the other party by saying, "I will swear," merely to induce compromise. All of this is a matter of ethical restraint. In strict law, if he knows that justice is with him, he may act to preserve what is truly his.
