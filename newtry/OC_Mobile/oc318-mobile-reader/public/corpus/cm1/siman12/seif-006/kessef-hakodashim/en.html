And they made a compromise. One who claims that he entered a compromise under duress, because otherwise his fellow would have taken much more from him unlawfully, and he delivered a protest, raises a difficult question. A compromise is analogous to a sale, and duress that comes upon a person through his own circumstances is generally not considered coercion that voids a sale. According to the defendant, moreover, he could have sworn to his version, so the claimant's pressure came through his own position. Nevertheless, the claimant's disclosure of intent may be effective, because according to his version the pressure was imposed by the other party. This requires further study. If, at or before the compromise, he stated before others that there remained between them a legal claim or another dispute, and there is room to understand this as disclosure that the compromise was only outwardly accepted, or that he referred to another dispute outside the compromise, the matter resembles a protest lacking a written date, where we presume that the protest remained in force. Still, there may be distinctions according to the reasons stated there, and the matter requires further study.
