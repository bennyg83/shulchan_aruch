Seif 18. For in compromise we do not follow the majority. Where two arbitrators participated and one says that a particular interpretation was his intent in the compromise, while he does not know the other arbitrator's intent and the other is himself uncertain, and neither side is more strongly in possession, the first arbitrator does not become a decisive witness merely by stating his own intent. The same is true even if several arbitrators support one interpretation, because a compromise is not determined by majority rule.
