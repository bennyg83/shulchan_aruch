Waiver does not require a kinyan. Where the creditor is holding a promissory note, see Urim, where I noted a dispute among the poskim. Accordingly, the rule that one who sells a promissory note to another person and afterward waives the underlying debt has effected a valid waiver should seemingly require a kinyan. Yet no authority states this. This is especially difficult in light of Tosafot in Bava Batra concerning the young daughter who was a relative of Rav Nachman. The Gemara suggests that she waive her ketubah note, and Tosafot explain that she was a minor. If a kinyan were required, a kinyan sudar performed by a minor would not be effective, as explained below in siman 235. It must therefore be that the problem exists specifically when the note itself remains in the creditor's possession. In that circumstance we say: if he truly intended a complete waiver, he would have returned the note. His retention of the document casts doubt on the sincerity of the waiver. But where the note is in someone else's possession, there is no reason to infer that his heart was not fully committed to the waiver. Therefore, after a promissory note has been sold to another, the original creditor can waive the debt without a kinyan because the note is no longer in his hands. The matter nevertheless requires further study.
