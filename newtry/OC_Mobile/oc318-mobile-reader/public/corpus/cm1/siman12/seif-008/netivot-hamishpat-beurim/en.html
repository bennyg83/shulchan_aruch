Forgiveness does not require a kinyan. Maharshal writes that if the claimant says he had already intended to forgive the debt but now wishes to sue merely out of revenge, he can no longer collect, because forgiveness in thought is effective. He brings proof from the woman who remained silent for twenty-five years and thereby waived her ketubah. Ketzot HaChoshen correctly objects from Maharit that we do not follow unexpressed intention except where there is an objectively compelling presumption recognized by everyone. It appears that this limitation applies to forgiveness of an already existing obligation. But where one says, 'Live with me,' or, 'Eat with me,' and afterward says he had intended at the time to waive payment, his intention can be effective, because that intention accompanied the very act that otherwise would create the obligation. In such a case it is like giving the benefit as a gift at the moment it was conferred.
