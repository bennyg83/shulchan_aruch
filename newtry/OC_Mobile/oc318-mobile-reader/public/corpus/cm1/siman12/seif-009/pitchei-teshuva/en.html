So long as they did not make a kinyan. Netivot HaMishpat cites Ran’s chiddushim that this applies specifically where the parties accepted the compromise by a kinyan sudar and expressly named particular persons as the arbitrators. If they merely made a kinyan accepting “a compromise” in general, three persons cannot impose a compromise unless those three were accepted by the parties. If, however, they came before three judges for adjudication and then made an unspecified kinyan to accept a compromise, that is treated as though they expressly named those three as the arbitrators. Responsa Bigdei Yesha, end of siman 2, notes that according to this, the rule below, siman 200, seif 7, that if one says “I sell to you at the price assessed by three” the sale is effective, would likewise require specification of who the appraisers are. The same difficulty would arise in our sale of chametz, according to the current practice of selling all items to the non-Jew without fixing a separate price for each item, but stipulating that each item’s price will be whatever three knowledgeable appraisers determine. The unqualified language of the Gemara and poskim does not imply such a requirement, and even Ran’s chiddushim do not necessarily mean this as a matter of law. The issue therefore requires study.
