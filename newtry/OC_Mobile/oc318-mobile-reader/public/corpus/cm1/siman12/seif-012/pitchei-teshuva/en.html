Specifically before two. Baer Heitev cites the Shach that the arbitrators are believed to say that a kinyan was made. See Tumim, Netivot HaMishpat, and Birkei Yosef on this. As for the statement that a compromise reached directly by the litigants themselves does not require a kinyan, attributed to Maharam Lublin, Responsa Beit Shmuel Acharon, siman 6, warns that many judges misunderstood the Shach to mean that any direct compromise between plaintiff and defendant requires no kinyan. That is incorrect. Maharam Lublin states explicitly that this is true only of a concession by the plaintiff to the defendant, because a waiver needs no kinyan. Netivot HaMishpat and Shaar Mishpat say likewise, as does Responsa Neot Deshe, end of siman 57. Beit Shmuel Acharon discusses a case where an agent negligently lost his principal’s documents. The principal held the agent’s signed undertaking accepting liability even for unavoidable accidents. The agent advanced several defenses, including that the principal had waived the claim by saying, “I forgive you, provided only that you swear the documents are not in your possession.” The principal admitted the statement but sought to retract because no kinyan had been made. The responsum rules that he may retract. At first glance this seems contrary to Maharam Lublin, since the plaintiff himself made the compromise without beit din. But where a waiver is conditional, as in “swear by the life of your head and be exempt” below, siman 22, seif 3, the waiver does not take effect until the condition is fulfilled. Therefore the plaintiff may retract before the oath. The same applies here, where he said, “I forgive you only if you swear that it is not in your possession.” See also siman 54 there and Shaar Mishpat below, siman 22, seif 3.
