And this one built a little and that one built a little, etc. The wording here implies that both must participate in the building. Below, in siman 157, seif 2, concerning partners who divided a courtyard that was too small to be subject to compulsory division, the Mechaber also cites the Rosh, who holds that once they agreed to divide and even one of them took possession of his allotted portion, he can no longer retract. The Ir Shushan and Bach therefore understand that according to the Rosh below, here too it should suffice if only one of them begins building. Sema answers, but his words are obscure. It appears that the distinction, and perhaps this is Sema's intent, is as follows. There, there is no dispute over ownership of the courtyard itself. It is clearly owned by both, only it is not large enough for compelled division. Once they agree to divide and one partner acquires his share, the division has been fixed and neither may retract. Here, however, they dispute who is obligated to build the wall. Each says that the other alone must build it. When they later compromise that both will build, so long as only one begins building there is no proof that the other accepted the compromise. According to the builder's original claim, the other was obligated to construct the entire wall anyway, so his own beginning to build does not demonstrate the other's consent. We therefore require both to participate, for only then is it clear that the compromise was accepted, since both, rather than one alone, are building. It follows from all this that the one who already began building certainly cannot retract even before the other begins, because he himself performed an act demonstrating his acceptance of the compromise.
