They cannot retract. Whether a party who made a kinyan sudar accepting compromise may retract before the arbitrators announce the compromise requires study. The Sema's language concerning one who delivered a protest against the kinyan suggests that after the claims were heard, but before the compromise was announced, ordinary retraction would not be effective and only the prior protest could be asserted. This is not conclusive, since the party there did not simply retract but relied upon a specific protest. The Tashbetz, Beit Yosef, and the parallel ruling in Yoreh Deah do not clearly establish the point, and the Sema's wording may not have been intended precisely enough to decide it. If retraction is possible before announcement, perhaps it depends on whether the arbitrators have already reached a final formulation comparable to "go out and give" or "you are liable." If the parties intended the compromise to be written, perhaps the writing itself is required. An analogy may be drawn from kiddushin and gittin where one says "from now and after thirty days," and the Gemara treats the legal effect as layered and doubtful. The matter therefore requires further study. A compromise made directly by the litigants without an intermediary is different. Where the parties themselves clearly agree upon a waiver or settlement, no kinyan sudar is required, nor must one expressly say "I waive." Their mutual agreement itself is effective.
