And he said, “So-and-so is liable,” etc. The Shach cites the Bach, who rules stringently like Tosafot in Sanhedrin 6b: where the judges have already thoroughly analyzed the case and the judgment is effectively complete, lacking only the declaration “So-and-so, you are liable,” they may no longer mislead the litigant into compromise because the law has already become clear. The Shach disagrees, arguing that compromise is a mitzvah and that the primary ruling follows the majority of authorities. Birkei Yosef questions the Shach’s argument, for according to Tosafot, once the law is fully clear it is forbidden to compromise. He concludes that the Bach’s stringent view is more persuasive, since Rabbeinu Yitzchak, Rashba, Or Zarua, Agudah, Rabbeinu Yonah, Ran, and Hagahot Maimoniyot all hold that once the law is clear and only formal pronouncement remains, compromise is forbidden. It is therefore proper to be stringent, as the Bach ruled. Perishah, letter 3, likewise records that Maharshal ruled this way.
