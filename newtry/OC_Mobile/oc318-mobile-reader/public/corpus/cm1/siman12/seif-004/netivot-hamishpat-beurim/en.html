To decree and place under ban. This implies that if beit din did not impose the ban or otherwise bind the arrangement, the minors may object when they grow up, similar to the rule concerning a minor convert. This applies where beit din compromises merely in order to quiet disputes. But where beit din or a guardian compromises with a litigant in exchange for an oath or similar right, the matter resembles buying and selling the oath. Beit din and guardians have authority to buy and sell within orphan property, and their transactions stand. The minors therefore cannot protest later. Proof is that they may sell slaves and emancipate them, and the emancipated slaves may marry free Jewish women. Likewise in siman 289 concerning division of land, the minors cannot later protest merely because another side paid an equalization amount, as Rashba explains in Gittin.
