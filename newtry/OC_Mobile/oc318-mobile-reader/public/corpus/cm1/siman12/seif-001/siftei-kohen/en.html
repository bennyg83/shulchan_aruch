"For years before you have come to trial," he said, "to thee," and to him, "and to thee shall be done."
Likewise, if both of them are powerful, etc. For if one pursues the judge, the other will come to his aid. So write Tosafot, as cited by the Sema. This is unlike Rashi's explanation, that they might pursue him to overturn the judgment; according to Tosafot, 'difficult' means quarrelsome and one who burdens the judges. See the Bach, who challenges the Rama and writes that Hagahot Ashri states this only according to Rashi's explanation, but according to Tosafot, even if both litigants are powerful the judge need not involve himself because of the danger to the judge. See there for his reasoning, and examine it carefully.
