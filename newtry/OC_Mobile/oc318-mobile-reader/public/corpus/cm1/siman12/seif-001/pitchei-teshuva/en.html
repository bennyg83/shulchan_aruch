Not to withdraw. Urim VeTumim writes that the permission to withdraw applies only before the judge has begun dealing with the case. Once he has undertaken the case, he may not withdraw because of fear. Netivot HaMishpat writes likewise. Responsa Shevut Yaakov, part I, siman 143, explicitly disputes the Bach and concludes that the Bach’s statements here are difficult. Once the judge knows toward which side the law inclines, he may not withdraw under any circumstances except danger to life. Urim VeTumim further cites Shevut Yaakov, part I, siman 138, that if one judge sees that the other two judges are corrupting the judgment, so that even if he states his opinion they will outvote him and an unjust ruling will result, it is preferable for him to say, “I do not know,” thereby requiring additional judges to be added. I cite those words at length below, siman 18, seif 1, seif katan 4, and there cite Birkei Yosef that this applies specifically when the other judges are ignorant and unqualified, as in the case addressed by Shevut Yaakov, but not where they too are God-fearing and competent.
