And you know toward which side the law inclines. The prohibition "You shall not fear any man" applies once the judge already knows toward which side the law inclines. The verse continues, "for the judgment is God's," and "judgment" refers to the final decision, meaning that the judge has already clarified the case. Sema explains it this way. Taz says a person is not yet considered the judge to whom this prohibition applies until he knows which way the law inclines. Both readings are somewhat forced. More naturally, the verse says, "You shall hear the small as well as the great; you shall not fear any man." This order teaches that after hearing, meaning after clarifying the matter, the judge may no longer withdraw out of fear. Before that point, he may withdraw. Yet the verse itself addresses appointed judges, as it says, "I commanded your judges." An appointed public judge may not withdraw even before he knows which way the law inclines, as Rambam and the Shulchan Aruch rule. Therefore the verse should not be read as specifically limiting the prohibition to the stage when the outcome is already known. Rather, the Sages stated as a matter of reason that an occasional judge who is not permanently appointed need not place himself in danger from the litigants before he has clarified the case. The verse itself addresses permanent judges and applies more broadly.
