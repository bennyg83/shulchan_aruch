If he married her, he need not divorce her. The Rama rules this according to Rambam, chapter 10 of Hilchot Gerushin. Maggid Mishneh writes that this is also Rashi's view and that of Rav Achai Gaon, and that Ramban conceded to them. Rosh also rules this explicitly in responsa. The Gemara's wording likewise supports it: in cases of mere rumor we do not remove a woman from her husband. Tosafot distinguish the case of the man suspected with a married woman, where if the suspicion were true she would certainly be forbidden to him, from the case of a sage who prohibited a woman by vow or one who delivered or verified a get, where even if the suspicion were true there may be no Torah prohibition. Although according to Tosafot one could argue that a get agent or witness should be treated like the suspected man if the allegation would make her a married woman, nevertheless there is room to distinguish. In those cases, even if the agent or witness lied, the get may in fact have been properly written or the husband may in fact have died. We therefore should not adopt the Bach's stringency, based on Rashba and the implication of Tosafot, to remove a wife from her husband merely because of rumor against the majority of authorities. In any event, the prohibition involved is rabbinic, so we rule leniently.
