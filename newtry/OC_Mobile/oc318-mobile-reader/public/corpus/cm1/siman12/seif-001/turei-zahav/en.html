In the Shulchan Aruch, seif katan 2. "As it is stated, you shall not be afraid," etc. The Sema explains that this is derived from the verse, "for the judgment," and that the term "judgment" means the conclusion of the case. This cannot be correct. First, the Gemara and our master the Tur should then have cited the words "for the judgment." Second, the Beit Yosef cites a responsum of Rashba concerning one who threatened a beit din, etc. Moreover, if the Sema were correct, anyone could act this way, yet the Torah states, "You shall not be afraid of any man." In Rashba's case the judge had not even begun to err, yet it says that he violates "You shall not be afraid of any man," apparently contrary to the Gemara's rule that this applies specifically after he knows toward whom the law inclines. It therefore appears that the Gemara's essential derivation is that the prohibition of "You shall not be afraid" applies only to one who bears the status of a judge. So long as he has not heard the case or does not know toward whom the law inclines, the title of judge does not yet apply to him for this case, and the prohibition does not apply. Once he knows the direction of the law, the status of judge rests upon him and he violates "You shall not be afraid." Therefore, in the responsum of Rashba cited above, which concerns a publicly recognized expert judge, as the Beit Yosef states, he already bears the status of judge, and the prohibition applies to him in every circumstance. This is correct.
