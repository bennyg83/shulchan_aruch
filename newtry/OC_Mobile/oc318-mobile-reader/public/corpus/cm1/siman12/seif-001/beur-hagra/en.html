One judge. So rules the Rif. This is likewise stated in the Yerushalmi at the beginning of Sanhedrin: "perhaps, etc., and the stronger litigant will become his enemy." The same appears in the Tosefta and in the Sifrei on the verse "you shall not fear," where it says: "Perhaps you will say, etc." There it also states, "before you hear, etc."
(Collected note) One judge. So it is in Yerushalmi chapter 1: "before you hear, etc.; perhaps, etc., and the stronger litigant will become his enemy." (End of collected note.)
(Collected note) And if he was, etc. Sifrei, Parashat Devarim, on "at that time": previously you were under your own authority, but now you are subordinated to the public. This refers to a case such as this. For if both litigants are weak or both are powerful, he must always attend to their case, as written in the gloss. There it also states on "you shall not fear any man": perhaps you will say, "I fear so and so, lest he kill my son, burn my stack, or cut down my plantings"; therefore Scripture says, "you shall not fear." Likewise concerning Yehoshafat it states, "he said to the judges, etc." This can be rejected, for perhaps it applies specifically after he has heard their claims and knows, etc. The statement "previously, etc." may refer to where both are, etc., while with a single dangerous litigant he need never accept the case. This also seems from there, since the opening uses the Gemara's wording, "you shall not fear any man, for if they come, etc., before you hear, etc." Moreover, they were appointed judges, and nevertheless it says "before, etc." (End of collected note.)
