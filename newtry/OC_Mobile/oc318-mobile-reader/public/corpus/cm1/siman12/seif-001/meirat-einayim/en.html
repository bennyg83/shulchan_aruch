And one of them is harsh. Meaning powerful and violent, one who does not listen to the voice of those who instruct him.
And you know toward whom the law inclines, etc. The verse says, 'Do not fear any man, for the judgment is God's.' The essential judicial act is the final ruling. Rashi likewise explains the verse, 'they shall judge the people at all times,' from which we derive that a final judgment may be rendered at night. As I wrote in siman 5, Tosafot also agree that this verse refers to the final ruling, and therefore 'for the judgment is God's' also applies at that stage. See what I write further at the end of siman 28; there is no need for Ir Shushan's lengthy discussion.
You are not permitted, etc. See below, siman 18, seif 4, regarding one judge who wishes to withdraw.
If both were powerful, etc. Because if one pursues the judge, the other can stand to assist him. So write Tosafot.
Nevertheless, nowadays the practice is, etc. Meaning, even when both litigants are powerful, nowadays judges still sometimes withdraw, because the concern that a litigant may inform to the authorities generally entails financial expense or danger even when the other litigant is also powerful. This appears from Rama's arrangement of the glosses. Darkei Moshe discusses both declining to protest sinners and the case of two powerful litigants.
