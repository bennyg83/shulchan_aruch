If arbitrators who are not formal judges reached a compromise because they thought one litigant's claim had substance, and afterward heard from judges that the claim had no legal basis, it appears that the compromise may be a mistaken compromise. Although the litigants did not submit the matter for a formal Torah-law ruling, the arbitrators' own judgment was based on error, and since their agreement to the compromise flowed from that mistake, the compromise may be invalid. On the other hand, perhaps because the parties deliberately relied on the arbitrators' practical judgment without requiring expertise in the law, the compromise remains effective. If the arbitrators themselves regret that they did not ask the law, and had they known it they would not have compromised as they did, this may weaken the effect of the parties' acceptance. Perhaps the distinctions between an error in an explicit Mishnah and an error in discretionary judgment apply here as well. If the mistake was not contrary to a clearly fixed halacha, it may not invalidate the compromise.
