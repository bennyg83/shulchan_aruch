A compromise made with a kinyan. See Baer Heitev, who writes regarding one who delivered a modaah before the compromise, concluding that Darkei Moshe in Yoreh Deah, siman 232, cites this from Tashbetz. In the Darkei Moshe editions before us I did not find it, though Beit Yosef there does cite Tashbetz, and Shulchan Aruch there, seif 18, rules accordingly. Ateret Tzvi cites Responsa Maharam Lublin concerning one who was compelled to compromise with his creditor for a reduced amount and gave him a solemn undertaking not to sue him further, but had previously delivered a modaah before witnesses regarding the compromise. Maharam Lublin ruled that the oath was taken under duress and the compromise is void. Ateret Tzvi observes that this seems contrary to what the Sema cites in the name of Darkei Moshe, but concludes that Maharam Lublin ruled correctly. A careful reading of Maharam Lublin, siman 11, shows that there is no disagreement. Maharam Lublin himself cites the above Tashbetz, the source of the Darkei Moshe’s ruling, and does not dispute it. See there carefully. One should also examine Tashbetz, part II, siman 62 and the gloss there, as well as Maharam Lublin, siman 23.
