Some say that in compromise we do not follow the majority. Kenesset HaGedolah asks why Rama presents this as "some say," when Tosafot, Mordechai, and Mahari Weil all state it explicitly and no dissenting authority is known. It appears that the reason lies in Tosafot on Sanhedrin 6, who cite the Yerushalmi explaining that compromise requires three because there must be a deciding vote. If two arbitrators disagree, one taking one position and the other another, the third decides between them, just as we say a beit din cannot have an even number. But if compromise required unanimous agreement of all arbitrators, what function would a deciding vote serve? Three would be no better than two or four, because without unanimity no result could issue. Necessarily, according to that Yerushalmi, R. Meir holds that even in compromise we follow the majority. Although the authorities who disagree with R. Meir concerning the number of arbitrators do not expressly state that they disagree with him on majority rule, the Yerushalmi at least implies the opposite of the cited ruling. Rama therefore properly writes "some say," because there is a meaningful indication of a contrary view.
