Even before three. The rule that even a compromise made before three persons requires a kinyan is not universally accepted, as appears from Hagahot Ashri at the beginning of Sanhedrin, Teshuvot Maimoniyot to Sefer Shoftim, siman 9, Responsa Tomat Yesharim, siman 128, and Responsa Maharshal, siman 4. Birkei Yosef discusses this at length and cites Masat Moshe, siman 302, who writes that since Riaz and the Baal Halachot Gedolot hold that the statement “compromise requires kinyan” applies only before two, while before three no kinyan is needed, and Mahari Tov Elem, Rabbeinu Simchah, and other poskim follow them, one who wishes to rely on them cannot readily be dispossessed. Birkei Yosef implies that had those views been truly isolated one could not plead kim li. I respectfully disagree. Even though they are not isolated, one still cannot in practice plead kim li here, because Tumim at the end of siman 25 records a received rule, which he himself applies, that one cannot plead kim li against a ruling of Shulchan Aruch and Rama where those authorities do not even mention the dissenting view. He cites Responsa Chavot Yair to the same effect. See what I write there, siman 25, seif 1, seif katan 2.
