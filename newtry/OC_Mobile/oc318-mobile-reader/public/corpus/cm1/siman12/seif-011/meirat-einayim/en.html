With a kinyan and cancellation of protests, etc. Even an oath can be ineffective against a claim of coercion if the oath was phrased in a manner that allowed him internally to nullify what he was uttering, as explained in Yoreh Deah regarding oaths to tax collectors. See also the end of siman 205, where Beit Yosef and Darkei Moshe cite this Responsa Rashba and conclude that this applies when it later becomes known that even the cancellation of protests was itself made under coercion. Darkei Moshe further cites a case where one first delivered a protest, then selected an arbitrator and appeared before the panel under a ban, and afterward sought to retract by claiming he had been coerced so that his opponent would not deny an admission made before the arbitrators. He could not retract, because such pressure was not sufficient coercion and he had accepted the procedure under a general ban.
