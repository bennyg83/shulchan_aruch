Sema says the plea, "My father counted out the money to you in my presence," helps only according to Rosh, who holds that even a robber's coerced sale can become valid if payment is seen and no notice of coercion was lodged. According to Rambam, where a robber need not lodge such a notice, seeing the payment would at most require return of the money. In my view, according to Rambam the claimant does not even recover the money on the strength of this plea. The son is believed only through the migo that the land was purchased. But once he admits the land is not his and claims only repayment of money, the migo is being used to extract. As the Shulchan Aruch rules in siman 150, one who has a migo concerning land is not believed merely to demand its monetary value when his present claim is not to the land itself. Therefore the Tur states the rule because he follows Rosh, under whom the payment supports validity of the sale and the son is claiming the body of the land. Rama likewise cites it because he follows the opinion in siman 151 that the sale can stand absent a notice of coercion. The Shulchan Aruch, following Rambam that the sale does not stand, appropriately omitted this rule entirely. The omission is not accidental.
