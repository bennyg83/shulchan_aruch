Their sons have, etc. Even the son of a robber, while his father is still alive, is not presumed to inspire fear of protest merely because of fear of his father the robber. So wrote the Darchei Moshe. See above, siman 134, Sema, and what I wrote there.
They sold it. The Tur writes: Even if he admits that it originally came into his father's possession in the capacity of a sharecropper, etc., but claims that he later purchased it from him, he has a chazakah; see Sema.
Who consumed it. Even if the son afterward also consumed it for the years required for chazakah, this does not help, because it is a chazakah unaccompanied by a valid claim; see Sema.
Who inherited it from his father has a chazakah. This is because beit din advances on behalf of an heir any claim that his father could have made. Had the father still been alive, he would have been believed to say that he purchased it, since he was not a robber and held it for three years, or lived in it for one day and his son, who stands in his place, lived in it for three years, as above in siman 146. This is also what the Mechaber intended by what he wrote in seif 7: 'And the grandson of the robber, even if he comes with the claim of his father, has a chazakah, but if, etc.' Accordingly, what the Rama writes here appears superfluous, since the Mechaber himself writes it immediately afterward, and the Rama wrote it here incidentally; see there. See Tumim, however, where I explained that the Rama teaches us that even according to the Rosh's interpretation of the Gemara, from which there is no proof for this ruling, the Rama nevertheless rules that the law itself is true. I also explained what further novelty the Rama teaches; see there.
And if he brought witnesses that the owners admitted, etc. See Tumim, where I showed that this is the wording of the Rambam and that this was his version of the Gemara. The Maggid Mishneh raised against him Tosafot's difficulty: Why is the case stated specifically regarding the son of a craftsman or sharecropper? The same should apply even to the craftsman or sharecropper himself. See what I wrote to resolve this; it is correct.
With a migo. It appears that this is only according to the Rosh's position cited by the Rama at the end of siman 151, that even regarding a robber, if witnesses saw the transfer and the seller did not deliver a moda'ah, the sale is valid. According to the Rambam, however, who holds that with a robber there is no need to deliver a moda'ah, he likewise has no chazakah; rather, he must return the money. See Sema.
And likewise. The text should read, 'And a son.' The word 'even' means that certainly if he comes with his own claim that the protester sold it to him, he acquired it; see Sema.
