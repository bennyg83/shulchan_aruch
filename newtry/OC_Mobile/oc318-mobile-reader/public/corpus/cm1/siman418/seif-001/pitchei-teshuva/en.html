And the sender is exempt. See Shach above, siman 32, seif katan 3, who writes that the Mishnah does not teach that the sender is liable toward Heaven because once the legally competent agent is liable to pay, there is no remaining heavenly liability upon the sender. Ritva in Kiddushin says likewise. Responsa Beit Shlomo Acharon, siman 12, asks why the Mishnah does not at least teach that the sender is liable toward Heaven when the agent has no assets or is violent and refuses to pay. He suggests that one who indirectly causes a definite loss to another may have a heavenly duty to pay, but here the sender did not definitely cause a loss at the time of the act, because the competent agent himself became fully liable. Only later did it turn out that the agent could not or would not pay. Since the loss was not definite when the damage occurred, the sender may be exempt even toward Heaven. He further raises a question where one tells another to damage a third party in circumstances where the acting agent is exempt in human court but liable toward Heaven. Is the sender also liable toward Heaven because the agent's human exemption makes the sender's causal role sufficiently certain, or does the agent's own heavenly liability still make the sender's causation insufficiently definite?
