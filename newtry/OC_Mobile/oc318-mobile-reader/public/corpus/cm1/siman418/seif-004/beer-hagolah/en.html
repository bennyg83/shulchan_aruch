tet) Ibid., and there in the Mishnah.
yud) Mishnah there on 60a. The Kesef Mishneh writes that since the Rambam does not mention the irrigation channel referred to by Shmuel, it appears he understands Rav and Shmuel to disagree concerning a broad river: according to Rav it interrupts the spread even without water, while according to Shmuel it interrupts only when it contains water. Shmuel calls it an irrigation channel only to indicate that it contains water.
kaf) The Maggid Mishneh cites the baraita there. Rabbi Yochanan explains that a shlulit is a water channel that distributes its flow to its banks. The Kesef Mishneh writes that the Rambam rules like Shmuel, who explains it as a place where rainwater gathers, as Rashi and the Arukh explain.
lamed) So appears in our Rambam.
mem) The Kesef Mishneh writes that the Rambam apparently holds that in every comparable case the person is exempt even when the fire is large.
nun) Explicit in the Gemara there, following Rashi and Tosafot.
samekh) Ibid., law 3. Neither the Maggid Mishneh nor Kesef Mishneh explains the source for the Rambam's distinction concerning the fence. In my humble opinion, he derived it from the baraita there stating that if the fire crossed a fence four cubits high, the person is liable. Although the Gemara explains that the Tanna counts from top to bottom, that is according to the first Tanna. We rule like Rabbi Shimon, who says everything depends on the height of the fire, and therefore the baraita is read literally where the flames were high.
