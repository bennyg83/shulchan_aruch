If one person brought the fire and another brought the wood, the one who brought the wood is liable. If one brought the wood and another brought the fire, the one who brought the fire is liable. If a third person then fanned the flame, the one who fanned it is liable. If an unusually occurring wind fanned it, all are exempt. If an ordinary wind was blowing, the final actor is liable. The same applies if an unusual wind was already blowing when the final actor came, because he acted in awareness of it. If he fanned and the wind also fanned, he is liable. Rama: Some say the one who fanned is liable only where his own fanning was sufficient to kindle the fire and the wind alone was not. If neither alone was sufficient, or if each alone was sufficient, he is exempt. Some also say that if two people fanned and neither one's fanning alone was sufficient, both are exempt.
