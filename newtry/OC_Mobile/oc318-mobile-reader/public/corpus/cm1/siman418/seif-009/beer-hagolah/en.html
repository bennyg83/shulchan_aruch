The baraita there, 60b. Raavad objects that Rambam did not distinguish as the baraita does: if his own fanning was sufficient to fan the fire, he is liable; if not, he is exempt. The Maggid Mishneh initially thought to answer that Rambam follows one of the Gemara's explanations under which all his words stand. But this would mean Rambam rules unlike Rav Ashi, who says indirect causation in damages is exempt, and Rambam himself rules like Rav Ashi in chapter 11 of Hilkhot Shekhenim, as explained in siman 155, seif 34. Kesef Mishneh resolves that there the damage does not come directly from the damager's own act, whereas here it comes from his own fanning, so we follow the other Amoraim who impose liability. In my humble opinion it is still difficult to rule unlike Rav Ashi, the later authority, and Rif cites only him. It appears to me that Rambam explains the baraita's phrase 'if his fanning was sufficient' as meaning together with an ordinary wind; if it was not sufficient except with an unusual wind, he is exempt. Tosafot similarly explain the discussion of winnowing with the wind assisting. Rava answers that winnowing is sufficiently accomplished even with an ordinary wind, and therefore one can be liable there even where an unusual wind assists. In damages, however, without intent beyond an ordinary wind there is exemption for an unusual wind. Rav Ashi explains that Shabbat differs because the Torah prohibited calculated labor, whereas damages require that the fanning be sufficient with an ordinary wind. Rambam therefore did not need to restate exemption for an unusual wind because he had already written it above. I later found this explanation in Nimukei Yosef on Rif and in Hagahot Ashri in Bava Batra chapter 2 in the name of Rabbenu Tam and Rivam. This appears to me clear and true according to Rambam.
