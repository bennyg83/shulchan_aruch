The one who brings the wood is liable, etc. The Tur's wording is: the last one who acted is always liable.
A wind that is not constantly common. Maggid Mishneh, chapter 4 of Hilchot Nizkei Mamon, writes that an uncommon wind means one by which the world is not normally governed on a constant basis, even if it occurs from time to time with considerable frequency. All the more so a storm wind, which blows only rarely.
The one who fans the flame is liable only, etc. Although with winnowing on Shabbos, where the wind assists him, he is liable, there the Torah prohibited intentional constructive labor and the normal manner of winnowing is specifically through the assistance of the wind. Here, however, his act is only indirect causation of damage, for which he is exempt. Rambam holds that he is liable because he himself fanned the fire and therefore personally participated in the resulting damage. This is unlike ordinary indirect causation, for which one is exempt. See Derishah.
