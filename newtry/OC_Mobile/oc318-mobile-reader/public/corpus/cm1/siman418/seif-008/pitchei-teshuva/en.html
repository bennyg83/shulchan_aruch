And the sender is exempt. See Shach above, siman 32, seif katan 3, who explains that the Mishnah does not say the sender is liable in the heavenly court because where the competent agent himself must pay, no additional heavenly liability rests on the sender. Ritva in the chapter HaIsh Mekadesh writes similarly. Responsa Beit Shlomo Acharon, siman 12, asks why the sender should not at least be liable in the heavenly court where the competent agent has no money or is violent and refuses to pay. He suggests that although one who causes a definite loss to another must satisfy the heavenly court, here the sender did not certainly cause a loss, because at the time of the damage the agent was legally obligated to pay. Only later did it turn out that he could not pay or would refuse. Since the sender did not cause certain loss at the moment of the damage, he is exempt even in the heavenly court. The responsum further raises a question where one instructs another to damage a third party in a manner for which the agent is exempt in earthly law but obligated in the heavenly court. Is the sender also obligated in the heavenly court because, since the agent has no earthly liability, the sender's act created certain loss? Or perhaps the agent's heavenly obligation itself means that the loss is not considered certain with respect to the sender. See there for the discussion.
