And likewise if the wind was, etc. See Shevut Yaakov, volume 1, siman 136, concerning Reuven and Shimon traveling together on a wagon loaded with Reuven's cotton. Shimon smoked tobacco and sparks from the pipe fell upon the wagon. Reuven objected and told Shimon to get off the wagon when smoking so that the merchandise would not burn, but Shimon ignored him. After some time an unusual wind arose, sparks fell into the cotton, and all of it burned. Shevut Yaakov rules that Shimon must pay. Although ordinary tobacco smoking is a less direct cause than the Talmudic case of smoldering material for which one is exempt, here there is another basis for liability. Since the unusual wind was already present, Shimon should have anticipated the danger and stopped smoking on the wagon, especially after Reuven expressly protested. Because he failed to do so, even without actively blowing the sparks, the case resembles one person bringing the fire, and Shimon is liable under the law of fire damage, payable from the best of his property.
