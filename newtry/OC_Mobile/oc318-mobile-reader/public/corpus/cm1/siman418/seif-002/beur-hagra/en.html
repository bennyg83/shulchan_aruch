It is written, etc. Bava Kamma 60b.
One who ignites, etc. Rashi there, s.v. "he opened," and as stated on 61b. See Tosafos there at the end of s.v. "rather," concerning Ravin when one lights within, etc.
Even if he did not, etc. The Mishna, 60a, and the Gemara there: if Scripture already wrote, etc., why is "field" needed, etc.
And all this, etc. This follows the Mishna's wording, "one who lights within his own property." The Rosh. It is also evident from the Rif, who asks why with an oven the Sages prescribe a distance yet liability remains if damage occurs, whereas here, etc. He answers that here the fire is lit only temporarily, and if it travels beyond the prescribed distance the owner is considered an ones, for what more could he have done? In a case of continuing negligence, by contrast, he is liable.
