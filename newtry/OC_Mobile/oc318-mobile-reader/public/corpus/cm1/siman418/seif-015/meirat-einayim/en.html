He gave permission to stack wheat and he stacked barley. One may ask: this seems obvious. Why should the custodian pay more than the damage he actually caused? The novelty is that we might otherwise have said he should at least be liable for agricultural implements hidden within the stack as though the outer stack were wheat, because hidden implements in a wheat stack can be compensable and he gave permission for wheat. The ruling teaches otherwise. See the Perishah.

And what the Tur and Mechaber write, "or he stacked barley and covered it with wheat." In the Perishah I cited Rashi, who explains that the owner gave him permission to stack barley. According to that explanation, the novelty is as follows. One might have thought that only in the first case, where he completely deviated from the owner's instruction by being given permission for barley and stacking wheat, is the custodian exempt from the value of wheat. Here, however, he did stack barley as authorized, so perhaps he should at least be liable for the wheat used as the covering. The ruling teaches that he is not.
