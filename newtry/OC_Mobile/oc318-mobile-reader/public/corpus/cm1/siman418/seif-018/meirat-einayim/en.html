A minor slave has the same law as an ox. In the Derishah I explained that Nimukei Yosef wrote this only according to Rashi. Rashi holds that if an ember is placed upon an ox, the person who placed it is liable even if the ox is not tied, because we do not say of an animal lacking reason that it should have fled. Nimukei Yosef therefore says the same of a minor slave who lacks reason. Tosafot and the Rosh, however, hold that liability for an ox applies specifically when it is tied. If it is untied, the person who placed the ember is exempt because the animal's normal manner is to flee from fire even without intellectual understanding. They also hold that if the ember is placed upon a slave's heart and the master is present, the person who placed it is exempt even when the slave is tied, because he may assume that the master will remove it, since a slave is obligated in mitzvot and has independent human status. According to that view, an untied minor slave is also exempt because he should flee like an ox. If he is tied and his master stands beside him, he resembles an adult slave, and the one who placed the ember cannot excuse himself by saying he relied upon the master unless the relevant conditions apply. Rama's statement that some say a minor slave is like an ox follows the Mechaber's abbreviated formulation, which did not mention tying. But according to Rama's later view that liability for an ox applies specifically when tied, a minor slave is not simply identical to an ox in every respect. The Mechaber himself must also be understood as speaking of a tied ox and slave, since at the beginning of siman 383 he expressly states the rule that way. He merely abbreviated here and relied on that earlier passage.
