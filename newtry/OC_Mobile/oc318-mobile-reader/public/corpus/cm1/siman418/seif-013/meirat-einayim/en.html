One is exempt for concealed property in a fire. At the beginning of this siman I wrote that this is derived from the extra phrase "or standing grain," which teaches that the damaged item must resemble standing grain, something exposed and visible. This phrase appears in the verse concerning fire that goes out from a person's domain. It is specifically there that we find the exemption for concealed property, unlike one who directly ignites another person's property, as explained below.
Something normally concealed in a stack. Meaning, he should have anticipated that such items were there and is therefore liable for burning them.
As though there had been wheat or barley there, etc. In Perishah I wrote that if the stack consists of wheat or barley together with their straw, as stacks ordinarily do, then farm implements concealed within it are also assessed as part of the wheat or barley and its straw. This too is included in the Tur and Mechaber's phrase, "according to the nature of the stack."
And he could have erected a barrier. For otherwise he would not be liable for the fire damage once the force of his original act had ceased, as explained above in seif 6.
He swears while holding a sacred object and collects. Meaning, even though there are no witnesses that precisely this amount of property was in the house. See Darkei Moshe, who cites Maggid Mishneh that if they dispute even about exposed property that burned, with the injured party saying it contained a certain amount and the damager asserting with certainty that it contained only less, the injured party nevertheless swears and collects. The Sages extended the enactment for one whose property was robbed to one whose property was burned. See siman 90 for the source of the rule that in such cases one swears and collects.
