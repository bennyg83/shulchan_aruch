For if he ignited, etc., when does this apply, etc. There, where they dispute two points, etc.
And we assess, etc. The baraisa there.
(Collected note) And his arrows ceased, etc. The Rambam and Semag omit this entire limitation, and their position is correct. All the sugyos indicate that liability for hidden property is exempt in every case. The same verse that makes one liable because fire is like one's arrows exempts hidden property. The Gemara's question, "since," etc., also becomes very difficult if Rabbi Yochanan's exemption for hidden property exists only where the wall fell. Rather, when the Gemara says "but according to the one who holds," it retracts the earlier assumption that the case must be one where the wall fell. The Torah's exemption for hidden property applies when one lights within one's own property, because while the fire was still in his domain he could yet have fenced it after ignition. If fire were always identical to shooting an arrow directly into another's property, every case would be like lighting in the other's domain and the exemption could never apply. The Gemara therefore explains that the case is one where he still had the ability to contain the fire. It then asks what practical difference remains between the theories and answers regarding the four categories of personal injury, where later ability to protect does not exempt the tortfeasor, just as one who shoots an arrow remains liable even if the victim could afterward protect himself. Hidden property is different only because Scripture specifically exempted it. This is also how the Rif's words are understood. The word "for example" is therefore not exact, and it appears preferable not to read it. The Rambam omitted the limitation for this reason, though he agrees to the rule stated in seif 6 because there containment is always possible. (End of note.)
That people ordinarily place in houses. The Mishna says, "for this is what people ordinarily do," indicating that only objects ordinarily stored there are included, just as regarding a stack the rule mentions implements normally placed there.
And everything, etc. There, where the Gemara says the Sages enacted, etc. Although the halacha does not follow Rabbi Yehuda, the enactment has practical effect according to the Rabbis in this circumstance. See Tosafos there, s.v. "according to Rabbi Yehuda."
