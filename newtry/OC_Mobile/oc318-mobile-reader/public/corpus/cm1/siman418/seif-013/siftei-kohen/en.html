He takes an oath while holding a sacred object. The Darkei Moshe and Beit Yosef cite the Maggid Mishneh that when the parties dispute the identity or value of the item consumed by the fire, the injured party swears and collects under the special ordinance enacted for one whose property was damaged by fire. The oath applies only to matters that the tortfeasor could reasonably have anticipated would be present. If the claimant alleges unusual valuables or an amount outside the normal use of the place, he must bring proof, or at least establish circumstances that make the claim credible. The rule also depends upon whether the tortfeasor admits that an item was present but disputes its value, or denies that any such item was there. Where the claimant's allegation is within the ordinary range, the rabbinic ordinance permits collection after an oath taken while holding a sacred object. Where the claim is extraordinary, the general rule that the burden of proof rests upon the claimant applies. The same distinction governs household goods, merchandise, money, and other property according to the ordinary character of the building and its use.
