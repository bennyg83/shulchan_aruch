To place. This implies that if it is not customary to place such an item there, he is exempt even when it is known that the item was present. This is difficult, because at the beginning of siman 388 it is explicit that where it is known to have been there, he is liable even if it is not normally placed there. One must distinguish that there the damage is caused directly by hand, whereas here, although the case speaks of one who lit the fire within another's property, it means negligent lighting rather than direct intentional damage. This also appears from Maharshal, HaKones, siman 32. There seems to be an omission in his text, and it should read that when the Sages exempt one who negligently lights within another's property for items that are not normally placed there, that is only where the lighting was negligent. If he directly set fire to them, he is plainly liable for everything. Shach.
And he collects. Meaning, even if there are no witnesses establishing exactly how much property he had in the house. See the Darchei Moshe, who writes in the name of the Maggid Mishneh that if they dispute property that was openly present and burned, with the injured party claiming a certain amount and the person who caused the fire asserting with certainty that there was only a smaller amount, the injured party nevertheless takes an oath and collects, because the enactment made for a robbed person was also applied to one whose property was burned. See there, and see above siman 90 for the source of the rule that one may swear and collect in these situations. So writes the Sema. The Shach writes that this case requires witnesses to the fire, as the Maggid Mishneh states explicitly. This resolves the apparent contradiction with the beginning of siman 388, where one who damages another's property and then says, 'I do not know,' is subject to the injured party's oath with a sacred object and payment, implying that if the damager asserted certainty the injured party would not collect on his oath. There, there are no witnesses that he caused the damage, so he is believed through a migo. All of this is necessary according to the Maggid Mishneh and Sema. In truth, however, it appears to me that the Rambam and Mechaber follow the alternative opinion cited by the Maggid Mishneh, that in a fire case, if the person who lit it makes a definite claim, he takes an oath and is exempt. Reason supports this, and Maharshal at the end of siman 33 states it explicitly. He also writes in siman 30 that the enactment for a robbed person regarding fire does not apply where the person who caused the fire denies the claim, because despite his wrongdoing he does not have the status of a wicked robber. Only a moser is properly comparable to a robber, not an ordinary damager, even one who acted maliciously. This is also explicit in the Ra'ah on HaKones. Shach.
