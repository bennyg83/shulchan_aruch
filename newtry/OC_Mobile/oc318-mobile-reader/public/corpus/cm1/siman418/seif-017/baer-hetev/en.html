And for his humiliation. Although siman 421 begins by explaining that one is not liable for humiliation unless he intended to humiliate, here, because the fire spread through an ordinary wind, it is considered as though he knew and intended the resulting injury. Rashi and Ran, however, write in Keitzad HaRegel that he is exempt from humiliation in such a case, citing the case of one who fell from a roof in siman 421, seif 11. According to the Mechaber, one must distinguish that falling from a roof is more nearly unavoidable than lighting a fire that escapes and injures a person. The Rosh and Tur state simply that he is liable for the four categories of damages. It appears that they too hold him liable for humiliation, and by 'four categories' they mean four in addition to ordinary damages, because for ordinary damages everyone agrees he is liable through negligence even if the liability is not based on the doctrine of one's arrows but rather on one's property.
