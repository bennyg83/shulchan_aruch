Three people who gave. At first glance it is difficult why, when their funds were mixed together, an explicit statement by the agent can separate one person's share, whereas when the money was given in separate bundles it does not help. The answer appears to be that where the funds are mixed, a partner may divide equal money without the other's knowledge where division is beneficial. The agent can likewise divide on a beneficiary's behalf where it is clearly to his advantage. Nevertheless, an explicit declaration is required, just as a partner who in a time of loss wishes to separate himself and save only for himself must state this expressly, as explained above in siman 181. Mere private intent is insufficient. Where the funds are in separate bundles, however, the agent cannot take one person's bundle to satisfy another. That would be robbery. He cannot become an agent to rob one participant for another, because there is no agency for a transgression and such conduct is not a benefit for those whose funds are taken. Tur gives a different emphasis. In his view, when the principals initially give the funds already mixed together, they show that they are satisfied with treating them as one pool, and the agent cannot later divide without their consent. But if they give separate bundles that later become mixed, the agent may separate equal funds because he is merely restoring each person's distinct property. In either reading, the legal effect depends on how the money entered the agent's hands and whether separation itself is a permitted beneficial act.
