[Rama] He expressly specified. Note: The Tur implies that even if those providing the funds mixed their money together, it is effective if the agent expressly stated that he was purchasing for one of them. See the Bach, who discusses the reverse case: each one gave a separate bundle of money, and the agent purchased using the money of one of them. In that case, even if he expressly stated that he was purchasing for all of them, only the person whose money was used acquires. See there. Even according to the one opinion cited in siman 187, seif 6, in the Rama, that one who purchases for himself with another person's money acquires it, that is specifically when he purchases for himself, not when he purchases for a third party. This is also explicit in Rashba's novellae to Bava Kamma 102b, s.v. Achiku b'Maarava. He explains that the seller's intent is only toward the purchaser or the owner of the money. But where the intended acquirer is neither the purchaser nor the owner of the money, but a third party who neither owns the money nor purchases from the seller, that third party does not acquire. However, Rashba's words there also indicate that even if the funds were mixed and the agent specified that he was purchasing for one of them, the specification is ineffective and all of them acquire, contrary to Rabbeinu Yeshayah.
