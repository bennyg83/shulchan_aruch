Seif 1: The money was mixed. Rabbeinu explains the Rambam to mean that even if each principal gave the agent a separate bundle, once the agent mixed the money it is considered mixed. This is indicated by the Rambam's wording, “if the money is mixed,” rather than “if it was mixed,” showing that the relevant time is not necessarily the original delivery. The Sema infers that if the principals gave mixed money and the agent later separated it into bundles, the agent's intent governs. This is not reasonable. If his declaration cannot allocate money that remains mixed, neither should his later bundling undo the original joint character. The Rambam's phrase that each person's money was bundled means that the bundles remained separate from the time of delivery until the purchase. Only then does the purchase belong to the person whose money was used, without regard to the agent's undisclosed intent. If the money was delivered mixed, it plainly belongs to all. Rabbeinu then adds the stronger case that even where the agent himself mixed originally separate bundles, the result is treated as though the principals had mixed it from the outset. There are not two different approaches in Rabbeinu, as the Beit Yosef thought. This appears clear.
