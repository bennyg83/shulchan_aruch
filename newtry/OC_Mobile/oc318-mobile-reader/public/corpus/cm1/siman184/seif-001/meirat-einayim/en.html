If the money was mixed together. The Mechaber copies the Rambam. The Tur explains that according to the Rambam, even if the three owners each gave the agent his money separately, bundled and sealed, if the agent later mixed all three sums and purchased with part of the mixture, the purchase belongs to all three, even though the agent intended it for only one of them. If the agent had not mixed them and instead bought with the separate money of one owner, the purchase would belong to that owner alone. However, if all three initially gave their money together in one mixture and the agent later separated and bundled each owner's amount, then bought with the money of one of them, I demonstrated in the Perishah and Derishah that in this case we do not follow the agent's intent, contrary to the implication of the Tur. The Tur first cites another opinion that does not follow the agent's later conduct at all but looks to how the owners delivered the money, whether mixed or separate. He then cites the Rambam, and says Rashi explains similarly, and therefore the Mechaber rules like the Rambam. According to what I have written, when the Mechaber later says that "the money of each was bundled separately," he means that it was separate both when delivered and when the purchase was made.
However, if he expressly specified, etc. In the Derishah I wrote that the Tur's language implies that the Rambam does not distinguish between an unexpressed intention and an explicit statement. If so, Rama should not have blended the positions together, but should have cited this distinction as an alternative opinion, namely the view of Rabbi Yeshayah, in the language of dispute.
