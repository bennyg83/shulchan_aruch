He cannot retract. See Taz, who strongly questions this because a sale is not inherently a benefit. Taz therefore explains that the agent used his own money and said to the seller, "Here is one hundred, and sell the field to so and so." Such a case requires the buyer's later consent, as explained in siman 190, seif 4. Taz then distinguishes between acquisition by money, which can become effective retroactively once the buyer consents, and acquisition by kinyan sudar, which he says cannot, because by the time the buyer consents the cloth has already returned to its owner. According to Taz, the buyer must have consented before the seller retracted. Otherwise, if the acquisition is incomplete until the buyer's consent, the seller can plainly retract before completion. Even with this clarification, Taz's distinction is difficult. Because the agent gave his own money not as a gift to the buyer but in order to acquire the field for him, the transaction is certainly a benefit for the buyer. Once the buyer later consents, the benefit can take effect retroactively. The same should apply to kinyan sudar. See what I wrote above in siman 190. Bach emends Rama and reads that the agent "bought with his own money." The intended case, as Ketzot HaChoshen explains, is that the agent initially bought for himself, but told the seller that he was buying for Levi. The agent thereby acquires, and the seller cannot retract merely because the agent named Levi. If Levi later claims that the purchase was actually made with Levi's own money and therefore belongs to him, that is a separate dispute between Levi and the agent.
