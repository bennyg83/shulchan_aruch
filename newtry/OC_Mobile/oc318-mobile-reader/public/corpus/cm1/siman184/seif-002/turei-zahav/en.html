Seif 2: And moreover. This is the view of the Rosh and Rabbeinu, but the Rambam omitted it, and the Beit Yosef was left in difficulty. It appears that the Rambam requires an initial disclosure that the buyer is not purchasing for himself. The Rif writes that if the buyer first tells the seller, “I am buying this field for myself and writing Levi's name only to conceal my ownership, and afterward you will write a deed in my name,” the seller is compelled to write the second deed. Even if he merely tells the witnesses in the seller's presence to write another deed, the seller is compelled. The latter case still includes the initial disclosure before the seller that he is not buying for Levi. Otherwise, if telling witnesses alone sufficed without disclosure, surely telling the seller directly would suffice without disclosure, and the Rif would not have required it in the first clause. This is also the Rambam's meaning when he says that the buyer stipulated with the seller. At minimum, he must disclose that he is not buying for the named person. Rashi also requires disclosure, though he may not require an express instruction to write a second deed. In my opinion, even the Rambam requires mention of the second deed, because otherwise the word “stipulated” is inappropriate. Thus both elements are required: disclosure that he is not buying for himself under the first name, and a stipulation for a second deed. The stipulation may be addressed only to the witnesses if made in the seller's presence. One cannot compel the seller to write a second deed against the Rif and Rambam without both elements.
