In Levi's name. Taz writes that this gloss is obscure, and the words of Hagahot Mordechai, citing Baal HaIttur, require explanation. The case is Reuven who bought land from Shimon while placing the transaction in Levi's name, although Levi had not authorized him, and the deed was written in Levi's name as a device. Shimon later sought to retract when Levi did not appear. Taz understood the Gemara, "One who buys a field in another person's name," as implying that the named person acquires, and therefore tried to explain how Levi could acquire without appointing Reuven as agent. That reading is mistaken. The Gemara asks whether the true buyer, who paid the money while using another person's name, acquires. The named person never acquires unless there is an actual agency or transfer to him. The concern is whether the seller intended to sell to the true buyer when the transaction was presented under another name. The Gemara establishes that where the seller was informed that the named person was merely being used for the documentation, the true buyer certainly acquires. According to our law, however, we do not follow the scholars of the West who require notification to the seller. The seller's intent ordinarily follows the owner of the purchase money. Therefore even if Reuven did not tell Shimon that the land was really for himself, Reuven acquires because he was the actual buyer and supplied the money. Shimon cannot retract merely because Levi was never an agent. The phrase that the deed was written in Levi's name "as a deception" is thus literal. Reuven bought for himself but used Levi's name, perhaps to discourage challengers or for another practical reason. The closing phrase, "If Levi comes and says, He bought with my money," concerns a new dispute between Reuven and Levi. If Levi claims he appointed Reuven and supplied the purchase funds, while Reuven denies it, the seller has already sold the land to one of them and cannot retract. The remaining dispute is between Reuven and Levi and is resolved by the ordinary rules of proof and oath. This, in my view, is the plain meaning of Baal HaIttur.
