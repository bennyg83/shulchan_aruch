Samekh) There, chapter 4 of Hilkhot Nachalot, halachah 6, from the Mishnah in the second chapter of Yevamot 22a: one who has a son, etc., except a son from a maidservant, etc. The Maggid Mishneh discusses the Rif's citation of a Gaon who was uncertain regarding a man's own maidservant, whether we apply the principle that a person does not ordinarily make his relations licentious and therefore presume that he first freed her. Rambam had no such doubt and treats the law as straightforward, making no distinction between one maidservant and another, just as there is no distinction between one gentile woman and another. Had such a distinction existed, the Gemara should have stated it. Rambam similarly writes in chapter 9 of Hilkhot Avadim. Regarding inheritance, however, there is a distinction involving a Torah scholar because the father has the power to give the property to this person as a gift even if he remains fully a slave. Since the father has a chazakah of proper conduct, we set that chazakah against the woman's chazakah of servile status, leaving room for the migo. Although the relations would still be improper if she had not been freed, that prohibition is not as severe, and people of established propriety distinguish between more and less severe prohibitions. With ordinary people, however, there is only a migo against the chazakah of servile status, and such a migo does not overcome that chazakah. Therefore property is not removed from the existing heirs. The Maggid Mishneh discusses this further at length.
