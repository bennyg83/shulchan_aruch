This also appears correct to me. See Responsa Mahari ibn Lev, who challenges the Gaon's position regarding inheritance from the case of an uncertain son and a yavam who come to divide the deceased brother's estate. His answer is correct, while the Bach's words in Even HaEzer, siman 156, are incorrect, as I wrote there. According to Mahari ibn Lev, where the deceased left only one son, the widow performs chalitzah, but regarding inheritance the Gaon holds that the parties divide the estate. The Gaon's statement that we follow the lenient possibility and that the son does not inherit refers to a case where the deceased left another son from a valid wife. The doubtful son is then comparable to an uncertain son and the sons of the yavam dividing the yavam's estate. This allows the Rama's words to be reconciled. Initially he cites the Gaon's view that the widow performs chalitzah. Regarding inheritance, however, the son sometimes inherits and sometimes does not, as explained. The Rama then writes that in monetary matters one may rely upon the Gaon and that the burden of proof rests upon the claimant, so that even against a brother the son of the maidservant does not inherit. The Rama does not refer there to yibbum at all, unlike the Sema. His meaning is that even when the father's presumptive status is valid, the alternative opinion cited by the Rambam disputes the Yerushalmi and holds that the son never inherits, whereas according to the Gaon he sometimes inherits, as explained. Alternatively, the Rama may mean as follows. The Gaon's words appear at first glance to indicate that the son never inherits, even where his opponent is the deceased's brother, who is likewise uncertain. The Rama therefore writes that some say that in monetary matters the burden of proof rests upon the claimant. Consequently, where both parties are uncertain, as in the case of the deceased's brother and the maidservant's son, the maidservant's son inherits because neither can remove the property from the other. But where the maidservant's son competes with a son from another wife, he does not inherit at all, because the other son is certainly an heir while he is only a doubtful heir. This appears to me the correct reconciliation of the Rama, unlike Sema, seif katan 17, through the words 'they do not sell him,' where he understands the father to be valid. See there. The father's wife requires yibbum, and the Rama disputes the Rambam regarding inheritance and sale. See also Sema, seif katan 18, through the words 'he does not accept that opinion.' This refers to inheritance even though the son is not presumed valid, and regarding yibbum the widow does not undergo yibbum. The Rambam, however, does not dispute this view concerning yibbum, as the Maggid Mishna states explicitly. Examine this carefully.
