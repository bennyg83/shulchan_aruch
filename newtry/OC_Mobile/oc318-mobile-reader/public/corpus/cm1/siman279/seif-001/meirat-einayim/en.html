Even though he acknowledged concerning people who were not established, etc. Above in siman 277:12 the Tur and Mechaber write the same rule regarding a firstborn: even if this person was not previously established at all as his son, the Torah believed the father to say that he is his son. Here the law is stated concerning an ordinary son. In the Perishah there I explained that the verse "he shall recognize" does not expressly teach this except where the person is known to be his son and the father identifies him as firstborn, thereby disqualifying as firstborn one older than him who had previously been regarded as such. Where the person was not known to be his son at all, the verse does not expressly say he is believed to establish him as a son. The Sages nevertheless found it reasonable to say so. If the rule rests on ordinary reasoning alone, one might have said it applies only where the person's parentage was unknown, such as people who arrived from a distant place, but not where the person had long been present and the alleged father had consistently distanced him completely. Yet perhaps the rule is not based on reasoning alone. The Sages may understand the verse "he shall recognize" as applying in every circumstance, even where it was not known at all that he was his son. If so, there is no basis for the distinction. The Ir Shushan writes, "just as the Torah believed a father to identify one established as his son as his firstborn." Perhaps his intent too is the first explanation, that the verse directly teaches only where the person is known to be his son, yet in truth even where he was not known at all as his son, the father is believed to identify him as his firstborn, as explained.
Even property that will fall to him, etc. The Tur's wording is "even property that will fall to him when he is a goses." This is a major novelty despite two defects. First, the property is not yet in existence for him, and ordinarily a person cannot transfer something not yet in existence. Second, when the property later comes to him while he is a goses, he is no longer legally capable of making acquisitions even by examination and gestures, for he is nearly dead. Nevertheless, through this earlier acknowledgment that the person is his son or heir, that person automatically inherits from now onward.
