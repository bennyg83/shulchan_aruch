And he retracted. This applies specifically after the period of immediate retraction. Within that period he is believed, for throughout the Torah an immediate retraction is treated as part of the original statement, except in the cases of idolatry, betrothal, divorce, and blasphemy. So writes the Tur. His credibility here within that period, and likewise below when passing a customs station, applies specifically where he says that the person is his son born from a maidservant and therefore is a slave. Otherwise he is not believed to disqualify him as a slave, because the Torah granted him credibility only concerning his son, as explained above. Ibid.
Serves him. This is also the language of the Rambam. It means that even if he serves him like a slave, although one might therefore say that he is certainly his slave and not his son, the father's statement is believed. The Tur writes: if he said, 'He is my slave,' and then said, 'He is my son,' he is believed, because when he called him his slave he meant that he serves him like a slave. He is believed even if the person does not actually perform slave-like service. It appears that the Rambam and Mechaber agree. Their concluding phrase, 'that he is like a slave to him,' means that he listens to him and readily attends to his needs and requests. Ibid.
