"He is not my brother," etc. He is not believed. Only with respect to his son did the Torah expressly grant credibility through "he shall recognize." We find no such grant concerning a brother or other relatives. Nor is he believed through a migo that he could have given his property to someone else, because this is a migo against an established presumption.
Although he is not believed regarding lineage, etc. The reason is that with lineage it is impossible to say that his son is disqualified for marrying a mamzeret while the son's child is valid. In inheritance, however, the grandson has no independent present share or right in the estate, and during his lifetime the grandfather could have removed the grandson entirely by saying, "All my property shall belong to so-and-so."
Since his son is no longer alive. He has no independent credibility to disqualify grandchildren except through the disqualification of his own son, concerning whom the Torah believed him, as explained.
