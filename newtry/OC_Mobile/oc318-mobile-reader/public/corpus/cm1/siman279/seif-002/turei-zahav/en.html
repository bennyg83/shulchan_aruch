(Seif 4, he was passing a customs station.) The Sema distinguishes between one who has a presumption of legitimate lineage and one who does not. Such a distinction cannot be made. Kiddushin 49 states: “What does ‘even if both acknowledge’ teach? It is formulated as ‘not only.’ Not only where he has a presumption of legitimacy is she not believed, but even regarding a fetus, which lacks such a presumption, they are not believed.” Thus according to the Sages there is no distinction, even regarding a grandson, between one with a presumption of legitimacy and one without it. The same necessarily applies according to Rabbi Yehuda regarding a stranger. Here the discussion concerns only inheritance, as stated immediately below. The Sema also explains Ramah’s words in the Tur, that the father is not believed to establish that the son was born from a maidservant, as meaning that if you say the son does not inherit, you must necessarily interpret the father’s statement as referring to a maidservant. That is the point of Ramah’s language.
