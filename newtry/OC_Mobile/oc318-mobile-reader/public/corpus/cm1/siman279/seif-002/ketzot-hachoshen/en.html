For their father was not his son. This refers to a case where the deceased father had already been established as the claimant's son. Therefore the claimant cannot now say that he was not his son, either through paternal recognition, which no longer applies once the son died, or through migo, because the assertion contradicts the established presumption. If there had been no established paternity and the matter remained genuinely uncertain, he could be believed to say that the deceased was not his son through the migo that he could give all his property elsewhere. This can reconcile Maharik's language implying some credibility even after the son's death where no contrary presumption had been established.
