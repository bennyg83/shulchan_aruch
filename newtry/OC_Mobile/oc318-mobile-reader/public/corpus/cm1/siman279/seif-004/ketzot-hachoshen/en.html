The mother was established as a Jewish woman. Sema explains that where the child's mother is known, the father cannot disqualify the child by saying the mother was a mamzeret or maidservant, because that contradicts her established presumption of fitness. He can, however, say that the child was not conceived from him and is therefore a mamzer. The distinction is clearer from Ritva. When the father says the child is not his, the mother's own status need not be ruined. She may have sinned unintentionally, and even if the father claims she acted knowingly we divide his statement: he is believed regarding the child but not to impose separate consequences upon the mother, such as loss of ketubah or disqualification of future children. By contrast, saying she herself is a mamzeret or maidservant directly contradicts her established personal status and cannot be accepted merely as a consequence of his credibility concerning the child.
