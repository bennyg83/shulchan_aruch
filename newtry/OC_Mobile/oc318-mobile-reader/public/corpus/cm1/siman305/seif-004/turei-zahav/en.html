Go tomorrow. Custody together with the owner must exist specifically at the time of the loan. The requirement that he say “tomorrow” applies also where one says, “Guard for me and I will lend to you,” or, “Lend to me and I will guard for you.” If he did not say “tomorrow,” it is custody together with the owner. The Tur likewise writes this in the name of Terumat HaDeshen. It appears to me that the objection of the Tur and Beit Yosef to the Ramah may be resolved as follows. In the case of “Guard for me and I will lend to you,” the owner is considered with him because the owner is already obligated to provide his utensil as a loan. Even if he does not physically hand it over, he must at least show where it lies and may not prevent the borrower from taking it. In the case of “Lend to me and I will guard for you,” it must be said that this loan has the status of a paid bailment, so that the borrower is exempt from unavoidable accidents. Rashi explains, regarding “Lend to me and I will lend to you,” that the borrower does not receive the entire benefit as in an ordinary loan. The same applies here, since he too is guarding the lender’s property. The borrower is therefore willing to serve as custodian, for thereby he is exempt from unavoidable accidents on the borrowed object. If the lender were unwilling to accept the borrower as his custodian, the borrower would lose this benefit. Since the lender undertakes custody of the borrower’s property and thereby benefits him, this is properly deemed a loan together with the owner. The Sema, seif katan 7, explains that the Ramah holds that it is unnecessary for the owner actually to be engaged in labor with him. I say that, were this the Ramah’s view, the entire discussion in chapter HaShoel would contradict him, for everywhere the Gemara speaks of the owner being with him in his labor. A verbal condition that the owner will later go does not itself create custody together with the owner. Otherwise every paid custodian would be regarded as guarded together with the owner, since the owner says: Guard for me on condition that I pay you and thereby make you my paid custodian. The correct explanation is therefore as I have written. Because the matter is doubtful, money should not be extracted in these cases against the views of the Rambam and the Ramah, even though other authorities disagree.
