To become liable even for unavoidable accidents. See Responsa Beit Ephraim, Choshen Mishpat, siman 21, cited above in siman 129, seif 1, s.v. "for one day." It is clear from his words that where the paid custodian was required to provide collateral, his stipulation to assume liability even for unavoidable accidents is ineffective without a formal act of acquisition. The reason a custodian can ordinarily undertake such liability through words alone is that, in return for the benefit that a reputation spreads that he is trustworthy, he resolves and obligates himself. Here that rationale does not apply, for the very requirement that he provide collateral demonstrates that the owner did not trust him. See there. In my humble opinion, I noted support for this from the Hagahot Ashri at the end of the chapter HaPo'alim. See there.
