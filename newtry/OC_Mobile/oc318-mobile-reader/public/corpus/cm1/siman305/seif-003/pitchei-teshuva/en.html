The owners were with him in his work. See Responsa Chut HaMeshulash, part 4 of Tashbetz, second column, siman 24. The case there was as follows: Reuven brought Shimon ten gold shekalim and asked him to fashion from them a bracelet, while Shimon assisted him in melting the gold. When Shimon later came to take the bracelet, it could not be found and no one knew what had happened to it. The question was whether Reuven was required to pay Shimon the value of the gold, and, if Reuven was exempt, whether Shimon was nevertheless required to pay Reuven for his labor. The responsum discusses the matter at length and concludes: Since Shimon was engaged with Reuven in the work, it appears clear that this was a case of guarding while the owner was in the custodian's service. Before Reuven received the gold and became obligated to guard it, Shimon was already in Reuven's service, for Reuven had received the crystal from him, Shimon sat with him and began assisting him, and only then did Reuven receive the gold from his hand. The manufacture of the article was entirely for Reuven's benefit. It is therefore plainly a case of negligence occurring while the owner was in the custodian's service, which exempts the custodian. Since we have established that Reuven is exempt, it also appears clear that Shimon must nevertheless pay him the wages for his labor, enforceable in beit din. See Ketzot HaChoshen, Netivot HaMishpat, and Responsa Beit David, Choshen Mishpat, siman 1, concerning this ruling. I copied their words in Nachalat Tzvi and there discussed them at length, including the practical conclusion.
