The owners were with him in his work. See Responsa Chut HaMeshulash, volume 4, from Tashbetz, second column, siman 24. Reuven brought Shimon ten gold shekalim and asked him to make a bracelet, while Shimon assisted him in melting the metal. When Shimon later came to take the bracelet, it was missing and no one knew what had happened to it. The question was whether Reuven must pay the value of the gold and, if he is exempt, whether Shimon must nevertheless pay Reuven for his labor. The responsum concludes that because Shimon worked together with Reuven in the work, the custody was a case of guarding while the owner was working for the custodian. Before Reuven received the gold and became responsible for guarding it, Shimon was already working for him by having delivered the crystal and then sitting with him and assisting. The making of the item was entirely for Reuven's benefit. It is therefore a clear case of negligence occurring while the owner was working for the custodian, from which the custodian is exempt. Since Reuven is exempt from the loss, Shimon must nevertheless pay him the agreed value of his labor under ordinary civil law. See Ketzot HaChoshen, Netivot HaMishpat, and Responsa Beit David, Choshen Mishpat, siman 1. I copied their words in Nachalat Tzvi and discussed them at length there, including the practical conclusion.
