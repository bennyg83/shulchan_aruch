Seif 4, gloss. If he said simply, 'on condition that I not be liable in responsibility,' etc. Note: See Sema and Shach, seif katan 3, who challenges him. In my view the Sema's intent can be explained in one of two ways. He may hold that in the laws of custodians, a statement made within the brief time ordinarily treated as part of the preceding statement does not automatically merge with the earlier acceptance. If custodial status arises through speech alone without an act of acquisition, the Sema may reason like Rambam and the Mechaber in siman 255 regarding consecrated property, that where legal effect is completed solely by speech we do not always apply the rule that a statement within that brief interval is treated as one continuous statement. Alternatively, he may hold that custodial status requires meshichah, as reflected in the dispute in siman 291. Once an acquisition has been performed in the body of the object, he could follow the Taz in Even HaEzer 38 that after a completed gift an immediate qualifying statement does not retroactively merge with the act. Beit Yosef there distinguishes where the two statements are linguistically one declaration, while Taz disagrees. The Sema can be understood as drawing a similar distinction here. If the custodian says 'on condition that I not accept responsibility,' the phrase itself shows that the qualification is part of the original acceptance. But if he accepts custody and only afterward adds an unconnected disclaimer, it is a separate statement. Thus, where he stipulates before accepting custody, he never accepted full responsibility in the first place. Where he first completed the effective acceptance, whether by speech according to one view or by meshichah according to the other, the acceptance is complete. A qualification framed as 'on condition' can still be viewed as part of the same declaration, whereas a later unconnected statement cannot undo it. This, I believe, is a sound defense of the Sema.
