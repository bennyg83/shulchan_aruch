(Seif 3, according to some texts seif 4) He said it without qualification. This means at the time he accepted the collateral. If he accepted the collateral without qualification, he already became an unpaid custodian, and so long as he has not returned it he has no power to terminate his custodial obligation. The Mordechai at the end of chapter HaOmanin, from whom the Rama derived this ruling, likewise states: “At the time he accepted the collateral he said that he would not undertake liability,” etc. The same appears in Teshuvot Maimoniyot, siman 11, in Sefer Mishpatim. See there. The Sema, seif katan 5, writes that after accepting custody of the collateral he must subsequently say, “on condition.” This is exceedingly difficult, for once he accepted custody without qualification he certainly cannot exempt himself even by using the language of a condition. The Rama’s use of “on condition” here is not precise, and the Mordechai itself does not use that phrase. I saw in the Darkei Moshe that, when copying the Mordechai, the Rama inserted the phrase “on condition,” but there too the case concerns what was said initially. It therefore appears clear to me that the words “on condition” add nothing: if stated at the outset he is exempt in every formulation, whereas afterward he cannot exempt himself in any formulation.
