This is custody while the owner is with him. A responsum discusses a goldsmith who received gold to make a bracelet while the owner assisted him with the melting. The bracelet was later lost. Since the owner participated in the work at the beginning of custody, the case was treated as custody with the owner present and the artisan was exempt even from negligence. The responsum adds that the owner must nevertheless pay the artisan's wage. That is correct where the artisan's payment was for the craftsmanship itself. He completed the work, and the later loss occurred in circumstances for which he is exempt. His paid-custodian status follows automatically from the benefit of his employment, but his wage was not specifically payment for guarding. This differs from one hired specifically to guard. If a paid guard fails to guard properly, even where the technical custodial damages are excluded for some reason, he can lose the wage because he did not perform the service for which he was hired. There is still room for study where the loss was caused by the artisan's actual negligence. Since the ordinary rule is that an artisan's wage becomes due only upon completion and return, perhaps negligent failure to return means the employment was not fully completed even though damages are excluded by the owner's presence. The question remains.
Even for one day each one becomes a paid custodian. Chavot Yair discusses the reciprocal formulas: "Guard for me and I will guard for you," "Lend to me and I will lend to you," and mixed forms of lending and guarding. He sought to limit which object carries paid-custodian status. In my view each side's benefit supplies payment for the other side's custody. If one lends an animal in return for the other's guarding, the borrower is treated as a renter regarding the borrowed animal because his benefit is not entirely free, and the one guarding is a paid custodian because the loan he receives is the compensation for his guarding. The same is true where one says, "Guard for me for pay today, and tomorrow I will guard for you for free." Chavot Yair suggested the free guarding tomorrow is not compensation. I disagree. The first party did not agree to today's paid guarding without the additional term that the other would guard tomorrow without charge. That later free guarding is part of the negotiated compensation. The custodian tomorrow is therefore also a paid custodian, because his supposedly free service is the price of the earlier arrangement.
