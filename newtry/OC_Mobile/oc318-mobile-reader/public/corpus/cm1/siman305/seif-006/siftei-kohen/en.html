It is difficult why the Rama presents it merely as “some say,” since the Mechaber himself appears to agree. Guard for me today, etc. The Tur and later authorities struggled with the Rambam and Ramah, but their reading of the Gemara is primary. Otherwise the Gemara’s repeated distinction between simultaneous service and “today and tomorrow” would be unnecessary. “Lend me and I will lend you” is naturally simultaneous, like “guard for me and I will guard for you.” While each is obligated to guard the other’s article, each owner is working for the borrower, and the exemption of custody while the owner is employed applies. “Guard for me and I will lend you” is also intelligible: once the first accepts the article for guarding, the other is obligated to make his own article available for lending and therefore has a present duty connected with it. Conversely, “lend me and I will guard for you” refers to a later transfer of the article to be guarded; if both articles were delivered immediately, the lender would no longer be working for the borrower. But where one says, “Guard for me today and I will lend you tomorrow,” the lender has no present duty to provide the article until tomorrow, and there is no custody while the owner is employed. Other commentators may agree with the practical distinction even if they construe the Gemara more narrowly.
