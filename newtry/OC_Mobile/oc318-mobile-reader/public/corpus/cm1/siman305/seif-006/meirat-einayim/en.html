Some say that the same applies to, "Lend me and I will lend you." The reason is that the lender is engaged in the borrower's service by guarding for him the item that was lent to him.
And I will guard, etc. The reason is that, as already explained, the owner must be with him at the beginning of the safeguarding of the item that was lost.
"Guard for me today and I will lend to you tomorrow," etc. This implies that if he said, "Guard for me today and I will lend to you immediately," or, "Lend to me and I will guard for you immediately," it is considered guarding while the owner is with him. The Tur writes this explicitly in the name of Ramah, and he questions it, which is the view cited by Rama here as the dissenting opinion. The Beit Yosef likewise questions the case of "Lend to me and I will guard for you immediately." In the Perishah I wrote that the reason for Ramah's ruling appears to be that he holds that the verse, "If its owner is not with him," does not necessarily mean that the owner must be physically working with him. Rather, it means that the owner of the borrowed item is not connected to the borrowing arrangement through another undertaking. Therefore, when one says, "Lend to me and I will lend to you," "Guard for me and I will lend to you," or "Lend to me and I will guard for you," all are considered cases in which "the owner is with him," because the owner tied another act of lending or guarding to this transaction as a reciprocal condition. But if he says, "Lend to me today and I will guard for you tomorrow," that is not considered "with him," because "with him" implies immediately, at the beginning of the borrowing. See further what I wrote concerning this in the Perishah.
