This is guarding while the owner is with him. Ketzot HaChoshen was uncertain whether an artisan working under shemirah b'baalim and whose item is stolen may still collect his craftsmanship fee. He compared land, where one may be exempt from custodial payment yet lose his wage. There is no real doubt. Bava Metzia 58 concerning communal shekalim says the worker loses his wage even though his payment was for transport, not for guarding. Therefore an artisan whose object is stolen before he completes and returns the job likewise loses his craftsmanship fee. We rule that wages are due only at the end, and if he cannot complete delivery because he failed to protect the object from theft, he has not completed the hired work.

And guard for me. See Shach, seif katan 6, who explains that this is shemirah b'baalim because the owner must guard the other person's property in order to lend him his item. I do not understand why there should be a difference between "I will lend it to you immediately" and "I will lend it tomorrow." Tomorrow as well, the lender must keep and preserve the item until he can lend it. In my view the distinction is readiness for labor. If he says, "Lend me yours and I will lend you mine immediately," he has presently readied himself for the service of handing over the borrowed object, comparable to "Give me water." If he says, "I will lend you tomorrow," he has not yet entered the other's service. Under all the explanations of Beit Yosef, Sema, Shach, and this approach, the shemirah b'baalim exemption applies only to the person guarding the first person's object. The borrower himself remains liable for the borrowed item. The owner of that item is not performing any service for the borrower merely because the borrower agreed to guard something else. Similarly, where a paid custodian hands the object back to the owner but remains legally liable for theft, we do not say the owner is now guarding while the custodian is "with him" merely because a personal liability still rests on the paid custodian. The paid custodian is not actually working for the owner at that time, and in any event he is not the owner of the object. Shach cites Ran concerning a pledge. That case must be one where the borrowing occurred first and only afterward the pledge was placed, so that the owner's service did not exist at the time the custodial relationship began. Tumim says similarly.
