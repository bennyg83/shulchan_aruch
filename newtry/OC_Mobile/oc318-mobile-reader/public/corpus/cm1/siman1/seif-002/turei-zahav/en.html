Acts of kindness, which are the attribute of kindness that causes, etc. At first glance, it is unclear what this explanation intends. According to what we have said, however, it is precise. The difficulty was that, since we were compelled to say that these three things existed at the time of creation, how could acts of kindness then exist? Such acts apply only after creation, when people perform kindness for one another, and perhaps there would never be any need for them, as was said regarding judgment. The answer is that the mishna refers to the divine attribute of kindness, which existed then. Should one ask why we do not likewise interpret judgment as referring to the attribute of judgment, the answer is that although the attribute of judgment also existed, Hashem did not desire to create the world through judgment. He desired kindness, and this attribute brought about the world's creation, as the Sages say: Kindness said, “Let it be created.” Thus, this attribute caused the desire before the Holy One, blessed is He, to create the world. He then said that, after the purpose of creation had been completed, there was a concern that the world might fall into ruin, and that Israel would be unable to engage in Torah and the Temple service because of quarrels, disputes, and falsehood prevailing in the world, as indeed falsehood continually increases, because of our many sins, especially in our time. The Holy One, blessed is He, therefore established truth, judgment, and peace, so that Israel could fulfill the three things for whose sake the world was created. This explanation resolves everything the Beit Yosef asked, as well as other textual difficulties that he did not mention.
