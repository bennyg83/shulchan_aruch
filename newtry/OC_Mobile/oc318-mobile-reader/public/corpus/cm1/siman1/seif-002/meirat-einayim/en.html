Damage, pain, and diminution, etc. Rambam and Tur do not include kofer in this general category, because kofer does not ordinarily apply to bodily injury. Ordinary bodily injury includes damage, pain, shame, and diminution. Since the Mechaber already wrote "damage," his later "diminution" must refer to rape and seduction, where it stands in place of full damages, as Rashi explains at the beginning of HaChovel. Kofer applies where an ox kills a person. The Mechaber shortened and grouped these matters together. As for why diminution is not collected nowadays even though the Gemara says its assessment is common and it involves monetary loss, the Gemara means only that the appraisal of the difference between a virgin and a non-virgin is familiar because people understand marriage values. The act of rape or seduction itself is uncommon, and therefore we do not act as agents of the earlier judges for it.

And I have not seen the practice, etc. Darkei Moshe, seif 4, writes this regarding the rulings of Mahari that one collects medical expenses and lost labor and also fines the assailant. Mahari's language implies that even nowadays this is done. Rama comments that he has not seen the practice to collect compensation for lost labor, but only to obligate the assailant to pay for treatment and to impose a fine as the judges deem appropriate. Here in the gloss he did not mention obligating him for treatment separately, but apparently included medical expenses within the sum imposed to secure appeasement. His statement, "I have not seen the practice," appears to refer to the Mechaber's ruling to collect both lost labor and medical expenses by formal assessment. Rama says instead that the practice is to compel the assailant to appease the victim.
